# Context-enrichment: SberMobile ranks second by net subscriber inflow via number portability
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
SberMobile (Sberbank's full-MVNO) says it ranked **#2 among operators by NET number-portability (MNP) inflow for 9M 2026**, and that its subscriber base grew to **7.5M** (vs ~6.3M at end-2025, itself +160% YoY). The lever named explicitly: a **September 2026 campaign** offering Sber salary-account clients **50 GB + 300 minutes with no monthly fee** if they port their number in. The "healthy competition benefits subscribers" line is pure PR boilerplate — ignore it.

De-PR'd: this is a **self-reported, carefully-framed** stat, not a regulator (Roskomnadzor/CNIIS) league table. Note the framing choices: (a) the metric is **net MNP inflow**, not total base or revenue — on total base (7.5M) SberMobile is a rounding error next to MTS (~83M), MegaFon (~79M), t2 (~49M), Beeline (~44M); (b) it's **#2, not #1** — in **February 2026** Sber claimed the **#1 positive MNP balance (12.3% share)**, so "#2" is actually a *step down* the PR glosses over by switching to a 9-month cumulative window; (c) the base is flattering — porting-in is cheap to buy with a free-tariff salary-client offer, so the "inflow" is partly bought churn, not organic preference. **Why framed this way:** MNP-balance is the one axis where an ecosystem MVNO using a zero-price promo can plausibly top the chart, so Sber anchors to it rather than to base or ARPU.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Bank MVNOs (the real peer set):** **T-Mobile (T-Bank/T2)** is the category leader by revenue — **RUB 22.1bn in 2025, +32.8%, ~27% of the MVNO market, growing ~28%/yr**. SberMobile's 2025 revenue was **RUB 9.3bn (+117.8%)** — smaller but growing far faster off a lower base. Gazprombank, VTB and marketplace players (the "second wave") are circling.
- **The MNOs being fed off:** MTS 83.4M, MegaFon 79.37M, t2 48.9M, Beeline 44.1M (Q4 2025). Full-MVNOs ride on host RANs (SberMobile historically on Tele2/t2; T-Mobile on T2+MTS).
- **Global fintech-MVNO analogues:** [[Nubank's NuCel surpasses 1 million mobile customers]], [[Revolut launches Dutch mobile network on KPN 5G]], [[Monzo opens waitlist for eSIM mobile plans with loyalty discounts]] — same playbook (bank/fintech bundles connectivity to deepen ecosystem lock-in), but Sber is far larger in absolute MVNO scale.
- **Position:** #2 among bank MVNOs by inflow; a distant also-ran on total base; the standout is *growth rate*, not scale. **Why:** the premium is ecosystem-distribution-specific (free SIM at the point of a salary/banking relationship), not a telecom-quality edge.

## [2] Company history / fit
SberMobile trajectory: ~86k subs (early days) → ~4M (mid-2025) → **6.3M end-2025 (+160%)** → **7.5M (Oct 2026)**; revenue RUB 9.3bn in 2025. It fits Sber's ecosystem logic precisely: connectivity is a **retention/lock-in tool**, not a standalone P&L — hence the willingness to give 50–100% subscription discounts to salary clients. **Why Sber acts this way:** a bank's marginal cost of a loyal, data-rich, low-churn customer inside its super-app is worth more than telecom margin; the SIM is a hook that raises switching costs across banking, investing (SberInvestments), devices (SberDevices) and media. Giving away 50GB is cheap customer-acquisition for the *banking* franchise.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Not novel.** Bank MVNOs porting on free-tariff promos is a 2024–2026 category trend, and Sber itself already claimed MNP leadership in Feb 2026 — this is a **re-report of ongoing traction**, not a new capability. Real traction *is* present and quantified (7.5M base, measurable net inflow), which keeps it above pure-PR. **Who captures the margin:** the **host MNO** still captures network economics; SberMobile captures the *ecosystem/data* value, not standalone telecom profit — so "subscriber inflow" overstates the standalone business. What breaks it: if a free-tariff war erupts (t2/Megafon defending), the "inflow" is bought and reverses once the promo ends; regulators could also scrutinize zero-price bundling. The durable asset is the Sber relationship, not the SIM.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Bank MVNOs are the structural story: **23.4M subs = ~12% of the Russian mobile market at end-2025** (vs 3.4% five years ago), market growing **~3.5x faster** than the mainstream, with projections toward **~43.6M SIMs by 2030**. Expect continued share-shift from MNOs to ecosystem MVNOs, more promo-driven MNP churn, and a likely **second wave from marketplaces** (Ozon/Wildberries-type). Risk/second-order: MNOs host their own disruptors, so hosts will push back on wholesale terms or launch retention offers; the "inflow" metric is promo-elastic and will compress once acquisition subsidies normalize. Net: real but low-weight incremental data point confirming a known trend.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russia's mobile market is a saturated ~257mn-SIM base (per Mordor Intelligence, via telecomlead, 2025) — SIM penetration is well above population, so net growth for any player is a zero-sum share grab via mobile number portability (MNP, live in Russia since 2013), not greenfield demand. The MVNO layer is the live growth pocket: per Telecompaper, the Russian MVNO market reached RUB 111bn in 2025 serving 40mn+ SIMs (35% M2M); the non-operator-affiliated MVNO base was 15.07mn. Mordor sizes the MVNO segment at ~USD 0.87bn / 23.47mn subs (2025) growing ~7.2% CAGR to 33.17mn by 2030 — roughly double the ~3.5% CAGR of the overall market, confirming MVNOs out-grow the host MNOs. "Why now": the structural driver is bank-ecosystem distribution — per www1.ru (Jul 2026), bank-owned operators (Sber, T-Bank, VTB, Gazprombank, PSB, Alfa) already hold ~10% of the market. Sber's lever is zero-fee bundling for payroll clients (Sep 2026: 50 GB + 300 min free if they port in), i.e. the SIM is subsidised as a retention/engagement hook for the banking relationship, not sold as a standalone telco P&L.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: subscriber base, net MNP inflow (the de-PR'd growth metric here), ARPU, and — for bank MVNOs — ecosystem attach / cross-sell rather than standalone telco margin. Host MNOs (basis = network + price): MTS ~81mn / ~31% share, MegaFon ~76mn / ~27%, Beeline 54.3mn / ~28%, Tele2 (T2) 45.9mn / ~14% (per telecomlead, 2025). MVNOs lease these networks; SberMobile reportedly rides the Tele2/T2 and MTS cores. Recent peer moves: SberMobile grew 160% in 2025 to 6.3mn subs (from 3.9mn end-2024; ~30% of 2025 adds came via MNP) per Telecompaper — the note's 7.5mn for 9M-2026 is consistent with continued ~2x-ish trajectory. T-Mobile (ex-Tinkoff, T-Bank) is the closest comp: 5mn paying subs by May 2026, ~7.5mn incl. 3-month active SIMs, 22% MVNO share, RUB 22.05bn 2025 revenue (+33% YoY). Position: SberMobile is **catching up but scaling fastest** — "#2 by net MNP inflow" is a flow metric (who's winning ports now), distinct from stock share where the MNOs still dominate. Moat (analysis): distribution + switching costs via Sber's ~100mn-client ecosystem (ID, payroll, SberPay, subscriptions) — a distribution/network-effect moat the standalone MVNOs can't match, not a telco-infrastructure one.

**Comps & multiples.** No market-cap multiple is computable: SberMobile (Sberbank-Telecom) is an unlisted subsidiary with no standalone equity value disclosed; T-Mobile is inside private T-Bank. So EV/Revenue, P/S → **no data** / `[UNSOURCED]`. Operational benchmark instead: T-Mobile RUB 22.05bn revenue / ~5mn paying subs ≈ **RUB ~4,400 revenue per sub / year** (≈ RUB 365/mo ARPU, analysis) — a useful read-across for where SberMobile's 7.5mn base could monetise, though Sber's free-bundle payroll model likely runs **lower** telco ARPU by design (economics captured in the bank, not the SIM). SberMobile's own standalone P&L is thin: per Tadviser it posted a RUB 1.75bn net loss for a recent year — consistent with the "SIM as a loss-leading banking product" thesis. Internal comps (corpus, grep fallback — semsearch 402): bank/fintech MVNO precedents — [[Revolut launches Dutch mobile network on KPN 5G]], [[Nubank's NuCel surpasses 1 million mobile customers]], [[Monzo opens waitlist for eSIM mobile plans with loyalty discounts]], [[Walmart-backed BAIT becomes Mexico's second-largest operator]], [[Nedbank readies public launch of MVNO platform]] — same playbook globally: a consumer-finance brand bolts on an MVNO as an engagement/retention layer. Distribution not computed (no ≥3 comparable valuations); qualitative comparison only.

**IR grounding (company/sber).** SberMobile is a non-material line inside Sber Group; the parent does not break it out, so IR confirms scale/funding capacity, not the SIM unit's economics. Per Sber IFRS 9M/Q3 2025 results (2025-10-28, drive_url https://www.sberbank.com/common/img/uploaded/files/info/ifrs_results_q3_4gpw5f9j_2025.pdf) and FY2025 statements (2026-02-26, https://www.sberbank.com/common/img/uploaded/files/info/reporting_q4_8a5ewlbo_2025_en.pdf); ecosystem/non-financial strategy framed at Sber Investor Day 2025 (2025-12-10, https://www.sberbank.com/investorday/en). Takeaway: the group's capital and ~100mn-client base let it subsidise MVNO bundles indefinitely — the MVNO is a moat-feeder, not a standalone earnings driver.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Loss-leader economics / no standalone profitability.** Free-bundle payroll offers + a reported RUB 1.75bn historical net loss mean subscriber growth ≠ telco profit; the value is captured upstream in the bank, so "#2 by inflow" overstates the business's intrinsic strength if the ecosystem cross-sell doesn't materialise.
2. **Host-network dependence & disintermediation.** As an MVNO, SberMobile leases capacity from the MNOs (Tele2/MTS). The hosts control the rails, wholesale pricing and quality, and are themselves building AI/bundle plays (MTS "More Than All") — a margin squeeze or wholesale-terms change from a host that is also a competitor is a structural vulnerability.
3. **Flow-vs-stock + regulatory/MNP framing.** "#2 by net MNP inflow" is a momentum metric that can reverse once the free-bundle promo normalises; and heavy bank-MVNO use of free SIMs tied to payroll invites regulatory/ FAS scrutiny of tied selling as banks approach ~10%+ share.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Watch whether bank MVNOs convert flow leadership into durable stock share and whether regulators treat subsidised SIMs as anticompetitive tying (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if SberMobile holds #1–2 net MNP inflow for 2–3 more quarters AND Sber starts citing SIM-driven ecosystem cross-sell (e.g. SberPay/subscription attach) in IR, the MVNO is a validated retention moat; trigger that breaks it: a host MNO repricing wholesale or FAS restricting payroll-tied free bundles — either collapses the growth driver.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (context-only — operating-metric print, not an earnings release).** This is a subscriber/MNP operating disclosure, not a financial report: SberMobile reached a 7.5M subscriber base and ranked #2 among operators by net MNP (number-portability) inflow over 9M 2026. No P&L is in the news. For earnings anchoring, the relevant standalone print is OOO Sberbank-Telecom (the SberMobile legal entity): FY2025 revenue RUB 9.3bn (+117.8% YoY) but net loss roughly doubled to RUB 3.33bn — profitless growth. At the group level, telecom sits inside Sber's loss-making non-financial segment (H1 2026 revenue RUB 273.5bn vs RUB 269.4bn; segment loss RUB 87.7bn vs RUB 76.1bn a year earlier), so this metric does not move Sber's consolidated results.

**Key figures (with growth).**
- Subscriber base: 7.5M (9M 2026) vs 6.3M end-2025 (+~19% YTD) vs 3.9M end-2024. 2025 growth was +160% YoY (2.6x). Trajectory: ~3.8M end-H1 2025 → 6.3M end-2025 → 7.5M now — still accelerating off the Sept-2026 promo.
- MNP net inflow rank: #2 among operators over 9M 2026 (positive MNP balance = more porting in than out); SberMobile had earlier been #1 by net balance in parts of the 2025–2026 cycle.
- Standalone financials (OOO Sberbank-Telecom, FY2025, not 9M 2026): revenue RUB 9.3bn, +117.8% YoY; net loss RUB 3.33bn (roughly doubled YoY) — operating-metric growth is NOT yet translating into profit.

**By segment / driver.** Single driver is explicit and recent: the Sept-2026 program giving Sber payroll clients free mobile service (50 GB + 300 min, no subscription fee) only if they port their number into SberMobile via MNP. This weaponizes ecosystem distribution — the bank's payroll base funnels into the MVNO — which is why bank-backed MVNOs are out-growing the traditional telecom market. The metric to watch is whether porting-in converts to ARPU/revenue rather than subsidized free lines (the free-service promo mechanically inflates net adds while pressuring per-sub economics).

**vs expectations / prior period.** No consensus exists for a subscriber-count disclosure; assessed vs prior period. Beat on volume/share (clear sequential and YoY acceleration, #2 MNP rank), but [UNSOURCED] on whether 9M-2026 revenue/margin followed: the only hard P&L is FY2025 (+117.8% revenue, widening RUB 3.33bn loss). Base trajectory corroborates acceleration: ~3.8M (H1 2025) → 6.3M (FY2025) → 7.5M (9M 2026).

**Guidance / forward.** No guidance — an operating-metric press item. Independent read (analysis): sub growth should stay strong through 2026 while the payroll-port promo runs, but the FY2025 doubling of the loss on 2.6x subscriber growth flags that monetization lags acquisition; a promo that gives free service to the incremental cohort likely deepens the loss before it narrows. Management stayed silent on SberMobile ARPU, revenue, and profitability in this disclosure — the de-PR tell: they lead with base size and MNP rank, not unit economics.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. Ecosystem distribution works as an acquisition engine (fact: #2 MNP, 7.5M base → why: payroll funnel + zero-fee hook → matters: structurally cheaper CAC than standalone telcos → second-order: pressures incumbents' retention, visible in operators "pivoting to retention").
2. Profitless growth risk (fact: FY2025 loss ~doubled to RUB 3.33bn on +117.8% revenue → why: free/subsidized lines + subscriber-grab pricing → matters: volume ≠ value yet → second-order: SberMobile keeps dragging Sber's non-financial segment, where H1 2026 loss already widened to RUB 87.7bn).
3. Immaterial to SBER consolidated (fact: RUB 9.3bn telecom revenue vs group's trillions → matters: a strategic/ecosystem signal, not a numbers mover for the stock thesis).
4. Promo-dependence (fact: the Sept-2026 payroll-port offer is the named sole driver → why: growth is engineered, not organic demand → matters: net-add quality and churn-after-promo are the real watch items).
