Pigment raises $250M at $2.3B valuation 
Series E raise is led by Scaleup Europe Fund managed by EQT, with participation from existing investors, WIL (World Innovation Lab), and a planned investment from NVIDIA. 
New capital accelerates Pigment’s move toward autonomous planning, powered by its proprietary Graphite engine.
‍
 Pigment , the AI business planning and performance management platform, today announced a $250 million Series E funding round at a $2.3 billion valuation. The round was led by Scaleup Europe Fund , managed by EQT , with participation from WIL (World Innovation Lab), existing investors including Greenoaks, ICONIQ , IVP , and Meritech , and a planned investment from NVIDIA.   This capital will accelerate Pigment’s mission to move fast toward autonomous planning for global enterprises.
The investment comes on the heels of a milestone year for Pigment, which surpassed $100 million in Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) earlier this year, once again doubling year-over-year. Adoption of Pigment’s AI is also accelerating: today, 85% of new metrics in Pigment are created by an Agent. The fundraise puts Pigment in a position to build on this momentum and expand its presence and growth in new regions.
Pigment has rapidly cemented itself as the enterprise-ready platform of choice for large enterprises like Danone , DirectTV, Siemens , Snowflake, ServiceNow, and Unilever displacing legacy incumbents. And, instead of building internal solutions, the world’s most AI-savvy and engineering-dense organizations, including Anthropic, ElevenLabs, and Uber , trust Pigment to serve as the reasoning runtime for their enterprise data. 
A commitment to technical superiority and continuous innovation is why some of the largest organizations trust Pigment to replace their legacy systems. It also ensures that Pigment has the capacity to seamlessly support massive global deployments, such as its recent selection by L’Oréal, who is replacing its salary planning tool for their 95,000+ employees globally by deploying Pigment.
On the raise, Pigment Co-Founders Eléonore Crespo and Romain Niccoli said, 
‍ "Enterprise planning will change more in the next few years than it has in the last thirty. Legacy tools are being left behind, and the world's most advanced AI companies now run their planning on Pigment. With the backing of our investors, we are moving fast to make autonomous planning a reality for every enterprise. With AI agents running planning workflows end to end and turning decisions into action, companies can respond to change as fast as it happens."  
Enterprise-ready scale and engineering
This $250 million injection will significantly accelerate Pigment’s R&D and fuel the expansion of Pigment's engineering team, focused on its proprietary, patent-pending compute engine, Graphite, and the AI agents that run on it.
AI agents are only as good as the engine underneath them. Graphite already lets thousands of AI agents and people work on the same governed model at once, scaling to hundreds of billions of data points with deterministic, fully traceable results. That is what makes autonomous planning possible: agents can only be trusted to run planning workflows and act on the results when every number they produce is accurate and every action can be traced. With this funding, Pigment will keep advancing Graphite to handle even larger and more complex workloads, while extending its agents from building models and analyzing data to running planning workflows end to end.
“In most companies, the richest picture of how a business actually runs sits in its plans. Pigment has built that foundation with a single platform connecting finance, sales and workforce planning - and put AI agents to work on it, helping teams model, analyse and act in hours rather than months. Eléonore and Romain have built a global category leader from Paris, and that is exactly the kind of company the Scaleup Europe Fund was created to back.” Shannee Braun, Managing Director, EQT and Scaleup Europe Fund

**[0] TL;DR.** On 2026-10-20/21 Pigment, the Paris-founded AI business-planning & performance-management platform (co-founders Eléonore Crespo, Romain Niccoli), announced a **$250M Series E at a $2.3B valuation**, led by EQT's **Scaleup Europe Fund** (a ~€5B public-private vehicle targeting European tech), with WIL (World Innovation Lab), existing backers Greenoaks, ICONIQ, IVP and Meritech, and a **planned investment from NVIDIA** (Jensen Huang flagged the stake while in France). Pigment says it **surpassed $100M ARR** earlier in 2026 (doubling YoY for a third year) and that **85% of new metrics are now created by an AI agent**. Capital funds R&D and engineering on **Graphite**, its proprietary patent-pending compute engine, and the agents running on it, pushing toward "autonomous planning."

**[1] Why it matters.** This is a ~2.3x valuation step-up in ~2.5 years ($1B at the Apr-2024 Series D → $2.3B now) and reframes Pigment from an Anaplan-style FP&A challenger into an **"autonomous enterprise planning" platform** explicitly pitched (per Bloomberg) as taking on **Microsoft Excel/PowerPoint** and legacy planning incumbents. The NVIDIA stake + Graphite compute narrative tie Pigment to the agentic-AI capex cycle. It is also a flagship European-scaleup bet: EQT's new Scaleup Europe Fund using Pigment as a marquee "global category leader built from Paris" deal.

**[2] Context / background — funding trajectory.** Prior rounds (per stockanalysis/getlatka/Tracxn, treat as approximate): Series A ~$25M (2020) → Series B ~$73M (2021) + ~$65M extension (2022) → **Series C ~$88M at ~$850M post (May 2023, ICONIQ-led)** → **Series D ~$145M at ~$1B (Apr 2024, ICONIQ-led)** → Series E $250M at $2.3B (Oct 2026). Cumulative raised now ~$650M+. Enterprise logos cited by the company: Danone, DirecTV, Siemens, Snowflake, ServiceNow, Unilever, plus AI-native users Anthropic, ElevenLabs, Uber; a recent L'Oréal win replacing a salary-planning tool for 95,000+ employees.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. ARR >$100M "doubling YoY for a third year" is company-stated and unaudited. Is it true ARR (contracted recurring) or includes services/one-time? No net revenue retention or gross-margin disclosure.
2. ~$2.3B / >$100M ARR ≈ a ~23x ARR multiple with no stated profitability — is this an AI-premium top, and what does it imply if 2027 ARR growth decelerates below 2x?
3. "85% of new metrics are created by an Agent" — is this a meaningful adoption metric or a vanity stat (auto-generated metrics ≠ decisions acted on by customers)?
4. NVIDIA's investment is "planned," not closed. If it falls through or shrinks, how much of the headline (and the compute/agentic narrative) is load-bearing?
5. Named logos (Anthropic, Unilever, L'Oréal, Siemens) — are these full deployments or pilots/single-module wins (e.g. L'Oréal is specifically a salary-planning tool replacement)?
6. Against Microsoft (Excel/PowerPoint + Copilot embedding agents into the default toolset) and Anaplan/Workday with far larger installed bases and ARR, is Pigment's moat durable, or does bundling by incumbents commoditize it?
7. Graphite "scaling to hundreds of billions of data points with deterministic, fully traceable results" — zero independent benchmark. Is "autonomous planning" shipped, roadmap ("Planner" agent is roadmap), or aspiration?
8. EQT's Scaleup Europe Fund is a public-private, policy-flavored vehicle ("global leader built from Paris"). Is pricing partly strategic/political rather than a pure growth-equity mark?
9. Burn/runway: $250M raised — what is the cash-burn profile funding a large R&D/engineering expansion, and is this a growth round or a runway-extension at a managed-up mark?
10. Crowded, fragmenting field (Anaplan, Workday, Planful, OneStream, Vena, Cube, Abacum, Datarails, plus ERP-native agents from SAP/Oracle) — does "autonomous planning" differentiate durably, or is every vendor now shipping "FP&Agents"?
11. Single press-release source / one channel in-DB — coverage is announcement-driven; where is independent verification of the financials?
12. Does displacing "legacy incumbents" at large enterprises face multi-year switching costs (Anaplan/Workday stickiness) that cap near-term net-new-logo velocity?

**Importance: 4/5 — rationale.** A large ($250M), high-profile Series E more than doubling Pigment's valuation to $2.3B, with a marquee lead (EQT's Scaleup Europe Fund) and a planned NVIDIA stake, in a secularly growing, strategically central category (agentic planning for the office of the CFO) — a genuine European-scaleup and competitive-landscape marker vs. Anaplan/Workday/Microsoft. Held below 5/5 because all financials are company-stated and unverified, the NVIDIA money is not yet closed, "autonomous planning" is partly roadmap, and single-source/one-channel coverage; it is a significant funding event, not (yet) a proven market-structure shift.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Pigment sits in FP&A / enterprise planning (EPM/xP&A) software. Market size: per Stratistics-style secondary estimates (via marketresearch.com / openpr aggregators), the FP&A software market is ~$6.8bn in 2026 growing to ~$23.5bn by 2034 at ~16.8% CAGR; narrower "cloud FP&A among public-company buyers" estimates run higher (~$8.5bn 2026, ~28% 3yr CAGR). Treat the exact TAM as soft — figures come from secondary market-research vendors, not primary filings. Structure: consolidating, not fragmented — the two largest pure-plays went private in a two-year window (Anaplan → Thoma Bravo 2022; OneStream → Hg/General Atlantic/Tidemark Jan 2026), leaving a short list of scaled independents (OneStream, Board, Vena, Prophix, Planful) plus suite incumbents (Workday Adaptive, SAP/CCH Tagetik, Oracle, Microsoft/Excel). Barriers: deep switching costs (planning models embed a company's operating logic), governance/audit requirements, and enterprise distribution. Why now: the secular driver is a tech shift — AI agents moving planning from human-built models to "autonomous planning." Pigment reports 85% of new metrics now created by an agent (company PR, 2026-10-21) — the de-PR'd read: AI is both the growth story and the reason NVIDIA is taking a stake (compute/agent tie-in), and the reason incumbents (Microsoft Copilot in Excel) are the real competitive front, not legacy EPM.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: ARR, ARR growth / net-new ARR, NRR, Rule of 40 (SaaS); for this vintage, ARR-doubling cadence is the headline metric. Key players + basis of competition: (a) scaled independents — OneStream (~$605M LTM rev), Board, Vena, Planful — competing on depth/consolidation; (b) suite incumbents — Workday Adaptive, Oracle EPM, SAP/CCH Tagetik — competing on bundling/distribution; (c) the real disruption front is Microsoft (Excel + Copilot), which Bloomberg framed as Pigment's target ("challenge Microsoft"). Recent peer moves: OneStream taken private at $6.4bn (Jan 2026); Anaplan taken private at ~$10.4bn (closed mid-2022). Protagonist position: catching up on absolute scale (OneStream/Anaplan are ~6x Pigment's ARR) but **ahead on growth and AI-native architecture** `(analysis)` — $100M ARR after ~3 years of YoY doubling, proprietary Graphite compute engine, marquee logos (Unilever, Snowflake, Anthropic, L'Oréal). Moat: switching costs + the Graphite engine as an intangible/technical moat `(analysis)`; weaker on distribution vs suite incumbents. NRR / CAC payback / gross margin not disclosed → `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Comps & multiples.** Pigment round: **$2.3bn / ~$100M ARR = ~23x ARR** (round post-money, not market cap). Peer take-private multiples (trailing revenue):
- Anaplan (Thoma Bravo, 2022): ~$10.4bn / $592M = **~17.6x** (headline often cited 18x on trailing sales).
- OneStream (Hg/GA/Tidemark, Jan 2026): ~$6.4bn / ~$595M = **~10.8x**.
- Pigment prior round (Series D, 2024): $145M at ~$1.0bn post-money → this round is a ~2.3x valuation step-up `(analysis)`.
Internal comps (adjacent AI-native finance software, from corpus): [[Campfire raises $65 million Series B for AI ERP]], [[DualEntry raises $90 million Series A for AI ERP]] — same "AI-native displaces legacy finance stack" thesis, earlier stage (no public multiples). Read: Pigment's ~23x ARR is **rich vs the 10.8–18x trailing-revenue take-private marks** of larger, slower peers — but those are private-equity entries on mature, ~20-23% growers, whereas Pigment is a ~100% YoY grower; on a growth-adjusted basis the premium is defensible for the vintage, not an automatic red flag. Clean EV/EBITDA, P/E, NTM → no data (private, likely unprofitable).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Valuation vs comps execution risk** — ~23x ARR prices in continued ~100% growth; the moment doubling slows toward peer-level ~20%, the multiple compresses toward the 10–18x band and the paper valuation resets `(analysis)`.
2. **Incumbent disintermediation (Microsoft)** — the explicit target is Excel/Copilot; if Microsoft folds agentic planning into Office, Pigment's AI-native edge narrows at the point of distribution, not product. Second-order: pricing/CAC pressure.
3. **NVIDIA dependency / "planned" capital** — the NVIDIA stake is announced as *planned*, not closed, and ties the autonomous-planning story to a specific compute partner; a slip or strings-attached terms would dent both the narrative and the agent roadmap.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a re-rating of EPM toward "AI-native autonomous planning" and signals the next consolidation wave is now AI-led, not PE-led `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: if Pigment's ARR-doubling holds through 2027 and agent-created-metric share keeps climbing, it becomes the first AI-native name to credibly contest the OneStream/Anaplan tier; trigger to watch — a disclosed NRR or the NVIDIA stake closing. What breaks it — a Microsoft Copilot planning GA that commoditizes the agent layer, or a growth deceleration print.
