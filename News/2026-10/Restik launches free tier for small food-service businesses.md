## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened (2026-10-22):** Restik, a Russian SaaS platform for restaurant/cafe/coffee-shop automation, introduced a free tier aimed at small and early-stage food-service projects (coffee shops, bakeries) still validating their business model or preparing to launch. On the free plan, operators can build an operational platform from scratch on a smartphone with no outlay for extra software: compose menus, calculate dish cost (food cost), prepare tech cards (техкарты), run procurement, and track stock/inventory. The free tier includes Restik's in-system AI assistant, which the company says now aggregates more data and conversation detail and can handle both single calculations and multi-step tasks — e.g. ABC analysis, nutritional (КБЖУ) calculation, flagging ingredients that have risen in price, or creating a supply/delivery from a photo.

**What Restik is:** A Russian cloud HoReCa-automation platform — POS cash register, inventory/warehouse accounting, menu & tech cards, food-cost/costing, guest base/CRM & loyalty, a delivery-site builder, QR-menu, and an embedded AI assistant. Positioned for restaurants, cafes, coffee shops, bars, fast food and bakeries; markets a ~15-minute self-onboarding with no staff training. Its standard offer is a single unified subscription (all features, no add-ons) from ~₽2,499/mo on annual billing (₽3,490/mo monthly), historically with a 14-day full-feature trial. The new free tier replaces/augments the time-limited trial with a permanently-free entry plan restricted to back-office functions (menu, tech cards, costing, stock, guest base/bonuses, AI assistant) — i.e. the core operational tooling minus the full paid feature set. Sources: [restik.com](https://restik.com/), [prices](https://restik.com/prices/), [picktech.ru review 2026](https://picktech.ru/product/restik/), [toolfox.ru](https://toolfox.ru/services/s/restik).

**Market context — Russian restaurant-tech / POS:** The Russian HoReCa IT market was roughly ₽9.9bn in 2025, growing ~22% YoY, per Russian HoReCa trend coverage ([kb-horeca.site](https://kb-horeca.site/trends/market-2026/)). The POS/automation segment is dominated by two incumbents — **iiko** (~35-40% share, 60k+ establishments) and **r_keeper** (~30%, 60k+ establishments) — both historically heavier, often on-prem/full-stack deployments. A cloud/SMB-focused cohort — **Poster**, **Quick Resto**, **SBIS Presto** (Tensor) — competes for small independents; Poster lists from ~$29/mo, SBIS Presto from ~₽9,000/yr. Key 2025-26 trends: shift from perpetual licenses to subscription SaaS, AI/predictive analytics, and dark-kitchen IT stacks. Sources: [HoReCa market 2026](https://kb-horeca.site/trends/market-2026/), [iiko](https://www.selecthub.com/p/restaurant-pos-systems/iiko/), [TAdviser restaurant market](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Article:Restaurant_market_of_Russia).

**Why a free tier / freemium read:** A permanently-free, smartphone-first back-office plan is a classic SMB land-grab: it lowers acquisition friction for pre-revenue founders (coffee-shop/bakery openers) and seeds the funnel before they need POS, delivery site, or full loyalty — the paywalled, higher-value modules. The AI assistant being included free is the differentiator the release leads with, aligning Restik with the "AI in HoReCa" trend while the incumbents (iiko/r_keeper) and Poster/Quick Resto still largely gate automation behind paid tiers. Strategic goal is top-of-funnel against both heavyweight incumbents and other cloud SMB players.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Restaurant-tech / POS-SaaS for Russian food-service (общепит). Market context, not Restik's own numbers: Russian cafe/restaurant turnover reached ~2.66 trn rub in 2025, +14.1% y/y (per TAdviser, as of 2025). But the *count* of outlets fell for the first time since 2022 — ~115,000 establishments in Nov 2025, -3.1% y/y (~-3,700 venues), cafes -2.8% to ~76,300, restaurants -1.7% to ~24,800 (per The Moscow Times / TAdviser, Dec 2025). Structure: moderately consolidated software layer on top of a fragmented, shrinking SMB operator base. Legacy on-prem leaders iiko (~66k venues) and r_keeper (~65k) dominate; cloud/SMB challengers (Poster, Restik) compete on price and smartphone-first onboarding. Barriers: switching costs (menus, tech-cards, inventory history locked in a vendor), hardware/fiscal-register integration, and distribution/channel. Why now: operator counts are contracting, so vendors can no longer grow by riding new-venue formation — they must capture share earlier (pre-launch founders) and expand ARPU via AI up-sell. A free tier is a classic freemium land-grab aimed at locking in operators *before* they open, when switching cost is zero. (analysis)

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: venues/active locations, ARR/ARPU per venue, free-to-paid conversion, net revenue retention (churn is high in a closing-venue market). Restik is already freemium — its public pricing shows a free tier for menus/inventory plus a 14-day full trial, paid from ~2,499 rub/mo annual / 3,490 rub/mo monthly (restik.com, as of 2026). The news is an *expansion* of that free tier: the AI-assistant (ABC-analysis, dish cost/KBZHU calc, supply-by-photo, finding price-inflated ingredients) is now included free and targeted explicitly at pre-launch / model-testing founders (coffee shops, bakeries). Peer freemium posture: iiko has no free version, entry ~$79/mo/user (itqlick, 2026); Poster's cheapest "Mini — Coffee Shop" is ~$29/mo (softwareadvice, 2026). So Restik's move pushes the price floor to zero where the incumbents sit at $29–79/mo — it is competing on *distribution and acquisition cost*, not product depth. Position: niche SMB/early-stage challenger, chasing share below the iiko/r_keeper tier; the AI-in-free-tier is the differentiator vs Poster. Moat: switching costs if founders build their whole operational stack (menu, tech-cards, inventory) on-platform before opening — thin today, accrues with each locked-in venue. (analysis)

**Risk flags.**
1. Shrinking addressable base: food-service outlet count is *falling* (-3.1% y/y) — a free tier aimed at new-venue founders is fishing in a pool that is contracting, so free sign-ups may not convert into a growing paid base.
2. Monetization/cannibalization: giving away the AI-assistant — the likely premium up-sell lever — for free risks eroding the paid-tier value proposition; conversion economics are undisclosed, so the land-grab could inflate free MAU without ARPU (why: freemium only works if a credible paywall remains above the free line).
3. Incumbent retaliation + scale gap: iiko/r_keeper each serve ~65k venues vs Restik's niche base; a well-capitalized incumbent can match a free tier and out-distribute on brand/integrations, compressing Restik's only edge (price/acquisition).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Reads as a defensive/offensive acquisition play in a stagnating market: capture founders pre-launch at zero cost and bank on switching-cost lock-in to convert later. Falsifiable thesis: if Restik's move pressures Poster/iiko to launch or deepen their own free AI tiers within ~6–12 months, the SMB POS segment re-rates toward zero-floor freemium and competition shifts to AI-feature depth + conversion. Watch: Restik disclosing free→paid conversion or venue counts (would validate land-grab); a Poster/iiko free-AI response (would confirm the floor reset). (analysis)
