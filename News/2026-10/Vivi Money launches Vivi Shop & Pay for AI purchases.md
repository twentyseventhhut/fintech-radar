Vivi Money launches Vivi Shop & Pay, letting AI shop for you without the keys to your wallet
Byron Bay fintech Vivi Money is bringing shopping and payments into one conversation with Vivi Shop & Pay, an AI assistant that finds products, compares options and completes purchases with customer approval, without ever handing over your existing card details or control of your money.
 Vivi has already completed real AI initiated purchases through Visa ‘s payment network. “Right now the agent says, here’s your shirt, go and get it. You still have to do it yourself,” says founder and CEO Will Cormack (pictured above). “We wanted to close that gap. Find it, then pay for it.”
Two videos on Vivi Money ‘s LinkedIn page show Vivi Shop & Pay in action, from purchasing an Afends T-shirt to completing a flight booking involving seat selection, baggage and payment, with both transactions successfully settled through Visa ‘s payment network.
As the world’s biggest AI companies race to make their assistants capable of shopping, Vivi has taken a different approach to payments. With Vivi Shop & Pay customers can ask Vivi’s AI Assistant to find something within a budget and deadline, compare the options and approve a purchase. With their details already securely stored in Vivi, there’s no need to fill out checkout forms or enter payment information again. Customers simply review and approve the purchase inside the Vivi app, and Vivi handles the payment through Visa, the AI never sees the card number used for purchase.
 The Vivi Account: AI that actually knows your money 
Most AI assistants start from scratch, asking who you are, where to deliver and how you’ll pay. Vivi already has a head start. “Our customers are identity verified, we know their details and account balance, and through Open Banking we can see the other accounts they choose to connect,” says Cormack. “So when you ask Vivi to buy something, it already understands your finances and what you can afford.”
That means one conversation can do what used to take four apps. Ask “how much is in my account” and then “I need a black tee, medium, under $100, here by Friday”, and Vivi handles both. Banking and shopping sit in one place, with the same context.
Vivi can also search across retailers and compare prices. Ask for “a black tee with pink writing” and it looks at product images to find a match. And when booking flights, Vivi goes far beyond comparing airfares, handling everything from choosing airlines and fares to selecting seats, adding baggage or even a surfboard, before completing the purchase with the customer’s approval.
 Text it the way you text a friend 
Cormack believes people shouldn’t have to open a banking app just to chat. Customers can message Vivi in the app or through iMessage, with conversations synced across devices. “You tell Vivi what you need, it texts you back when it’s found it,” he says.
The conversation can happen anywhere, but payments stay inside Vivi. When it’s time to buy, customers return to the secure app, where Face ID and the full purchase details let them review exactly what they’re approving before any money moves.
 Security first: Hold, don’t tap 
Security is built into every Vivi Shop & Pay transaction. Customers are identity verified and can review the merchant, price and delivery details before approving a purchase inside the secure Vivi app. And approval isn’t a quick tap. “You press and hold, so there’s no accidentally authorising a payment,” says Cormack. “The AI can find something and ask to pay, but the customer stays in control.”
Only after approval does Vivi generate a single-use Visa card, restricted to the authorised merchant and amount and deactivated after use. The AI never receives card details or access to the customer’s money. “We’ve deliberately separated intelligence from control of the money,” Cormack says. “The AI doesn’t get the keys to your wallet.”
 Your AI, powered by Vivi Money 
Vivi has its own AI assistant, but its payment technology will also let customers connect their preferred assistant, such as ChatGPT or Claude to their Vivi Account. Vivi handles payments securely, with every purchase requiring approval through the Vivi Money app.
“Vivi doesn’t have to be the AI you choose,” says Cormack. “Your agent pays with Vivi.” The company aims to provide the identity-verified accounts and payment infrastructure that enable AI assistants to transact.
 Vivi Money: live now, with much more on the way 
Vivi Money is already live with its in-app AI Personal Assistant, Visa card, Open Banking and Australia’s lowest FX rates. Through Open Banking, Vivi can also monitor accounts customers choose to connect from other banks, helping identify opportunities for better savings and mortgage rates.
Vivi Shop & Pay will be available to all Premium Metal customers from November, with the waitlist now open. Customers will be able to find, compare and purchase products using AI while retaining control of every payment. Premium metal cards and Vivi’s Boost Saver high interest savings product are also planned for rollout next month.
The developments build on Vivi’s earlier AI initiated payments over Australia’s New Payments Platform and Snap & Pay technology which turns invoice photos into ready-to-approve payments.
For Cormack, it all comes back to one idea: bringing banking, payments, shopping and financial management together in one place. “The end goal isn’t an AI that talks about your money. It’s one that can do something about it.”

# Context-enrichment: Vivi Money launches Vivi Shop & Pay for AI purchases
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Byron Bay (Australia) neobank Vivi Money announced **Vivi Shop & Pay**, an in-app feature where the AI assistant finds a product, compares options, and — on explicit customer approval — completes the purchase over Visa's network. The mechanism: after the user "press-and-holds" to approve inside the app (Face ID), Vivi mints a **single-use Visa card locked to the specific merchant and amount, deactivated after use**; "the AI never sees the card number." It also books flights (fare, seat, baggage selection) end-to-end.
- **Announced, not broadly live.** Availability is a **waitlist**; rollout limited to **Premium Metal customers from November 2026**. The only proof-points are **two LinkedIn demo videos** (an Afends T-shirt purchase and a flight booking) that Vivi says settled on Visa's network. No GMV, user count, merchant count, conversion, or fraud-liability terms disclosed.
- **Why framed this way:** the "AI doesn't get the keys to your wallet" / single-use-token narrative is the de-facto industry pattern (see [1]), not a Vivi invention. Positioning it as a differentiator is marketing — the genuine delta is bundling it into a consumer neobank app with identity-verified + Open-Banking-enriched context, plus the explicit press-and-hold UX. (analysis)
- Stated roadmap: lets customers connect third-party assistants ("ChatGPT or Claude") to a Vivi account so "your agent pays with Vivi" — i.e., Vivi wants to be the identity + payment layer under other agents. This is a **stated aim, not a shipped integration**.

## [1] Competitors / peers
The single-use/tokenized-card-for-agents mechanism is already the market standard:
- **Visa Intelligent Commerce** (launched Apr 2025) + **Trusted Agent Protocol** ([[Visa launches Trusted Agent Protocol for AI commerce]], [[Nuvei supports Visa Trusted Agent Protocol]]) — Vivi runs *on top of* this, not beside it. Visa's Apr-2026 "Intelligent Commerce Connect" gives merchants one integration across four agent protocols.
- **OpenAI Instant Checkout + Agentic Commerce Protocol** (Stripe-co-developed, Sept/Oct 2025): uses **Shared Payment Tokens** — merchant-and-amount-bound, single-use — the same security primitive Vivi describes. ([[Stripe enables payments in ChatGPT with OpenAI]], [[PayPal adopts Agentic Commerce Protocol, expands in ChatGPT]], [[Walmart partners OpenAI for ChatGPT checkout]]).
- **Mastercard Agent Pay / Agentic Tokens** ([[Mastercard and PayPal partner on agentic commerce]], [[PayPal integrates Mastercard Agent Pay into wallet]]); **Coinbase x402 / Payments MCP** ([[Coinbase launches Payments MCP for AI agents]]); **Google AP2** ([[Affirm to support Google's Agent Payments Protocol]]).
- **Position:** catching-up / parity on mechanism; the global rails and protocols are owned by Visa/Mastercard/Stripe/OpenAI. Vivi's only edge is distribution-level (a specific consumer app in one country). **Why the landscape is this way:** the margin-capturing layers (network tokenization, the agent protocol, the chat surface) are held by incumbents; a sub-scale neobank is a *consumer of* those rails, so its defensibility is the end-user relationship, not the plumbing. (analysis)

## [2] Company history / fit
Consistent, incremental trajectory:
- **Mar 2026** — launched its AI-native platform on **Pismo** infra + Visa (debit card, FX, savings, Open Banking) ([[Vivi Money launches AI financial platform with Visa in Australia]]).
- **Jun 2026** — demoed "Australia's first AI-initiated NPP payment" (pay *people/bills* via Open Banking + New Payments Platform) ([[Vivi Money demos Australia's first AI-initiated NPP payment]]).
- **Oct 2026** — Vivi Shop & Pay extends AI-initiated payments from P2P/bills (NPP) to **merchant purchases** (Visa) + adds product discovery; plus prior "Snap & Pay" (invoice-photo → payment).
- **Fit:** logical. Vivi's thesis is "one conversation for banking + shopping + payments," leveraging the fact it already holds verified identity, balance, and Open-Banking context. **Why:** a small neobank can't out-scale incumbents on rails, so it competes on *context ownership* — knowing the customer's finances well enough to shop within budget. (analysis)

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty is modest.** The payment security model (single-use merchant-bound token, AI never sees PAN) mirrors Visa Intelligent Commerce / ACP Shared Payment Tokens that predate this by ~6–12 months. The incremental novelty: packaging agentic *shopping* into a retail neobank with pre-loaded identity/Open-Banking context, plus the press-and-hold approval UX. "Australia's first" framing (for consumer AI shopping) is plausible but unverified.
- **Traction = effectively zero.** Evidence is two demo videos; the product is waitlist-only, Premium-Metal-gated, from November. No customers, GMV, or merchant integrations cited.
- **Who captures the margin:** Visa takes network/tokenization economics; merchants/ACP surfaces capture discovery; Vivi captures only the interchange on its own card + subscription (Premium Metal). **The real question isn't "does Vivi have an AI story" — it's whether a sub-scale single-market neobank can retain the customer once OpenAI/ChatGPT, PayPal, and Visa's own agent directory offer the same checkout natively.** On that, the value-add is unproven. (analysis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Next:** Nov 2026 rollout to Premium Metal tier; waitlist open; Boost Saver savings product also due. Stated ambition to be the identity+payment rail under third-party agents (ChatGPT/Claude) — watch whether any such integration actually ships.
- **Sentiment/backdrop:** agentic commerce is the dominant 2025–26 payments theme; Visa predicts millions using AI agents to shop by the 2026 holiday season. That tailwind helps distribution but also means **commoditization** — the security pattern Vivi markets is becoming table stakes.
- **Risks:** (1) fraud-liability/chargeback ownership for AI-initiated purchases is unaddressed; (2) scale — Australian-only, premium-tier gating limits reach; (3) disintermediation — if ChatGPT/Visa/PayPal own both the chat surface and the rails, Vivi risks being a thin wrapper. **Second-order:** marketing a security model that incumbents are standardizing is a weak moat; Vivi's durable asset is the verified-identity + Open-Banking customer graph, not the "AI pays" feature itself. (hypothesis)

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Vivi sits in agentic commerce / AI-initiated payments — the subvertical where an AI agent discovers, compares and completes a purchase, settling over card rails. Sizing is early and dispersed: the global agentic-commerce market was put at ~$18.4bn in 2025, with 2030 forecasts spanning a wide range (IDC ~$187bn / 59% CAGR; Morgan Stanley $190–385bn of US e-commerce / 10–20% share; Bain $300–500bn / 15–25% of e-com; McKinsey ~$1tn US) — the spread itself signals a not-yet-formed TAM, treat as directional, not firm (per Morgan Stanley/Bain/IDC/McKinsey, via secondary citations, as of 2025–2026). Structure: fragmented and forming. Value is being contested across three layers — (a) the agent/LLM (OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity), (b) the payment-authorization/tokenization layer (Visa Intelligent Commerce, Mastercard Agent Pay, PayPal ACP), and (c) consumer-facing wallets/neobanks like Vivi. Entry barriers are highest at the rails layer (network effects, tokenization, fraud liability) and lowest at the app layer, where Vivi plays. Why now: Visa cited a ~4,700% surge in AI-driven traffic to US retail sites ([[Visa launches Trusted Agent Protocol for AI commerce]], 2025-10-17), and the networks spent 2025–2026 standing up agent plumbing — the app layer is now racing to consume it.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs (payments/wallet): TPV/GPV, take rate, attach rate, approved-vs-declined (agent fraud is the live problem), active users. Vivi discloses none of these — all `[UNSOURCED]`. Key players & basis of competition: at the rails layer Visa (Intelligent Commerce, Trusted Agent Protocol, Agentic Directory/Agent Score, named partners incl. Anthropic, OpenAI, Microsoft, Perplexity — Visa Payments Forum, June 2026) and Mastercard (Agent Pay) compete on trust/tokenization/fraud; at the checkout layer PayPal (adopted the Agentic Commerce Protocol, OpenAI Instant Checkout in ChatGPT — [[PayPal adopts Agentic Commerce Protocol, expands in ChatGPT]], 2025-10-28). Vivi competes at the consumer-wallet layer on product/UX — its stated differentiator is "separating intelligence from control of the money": the agent never sees the card, Vivi mints a single-use, merchant-and-amount-scoped Visa token only after press-and-hold human approval. Protagonist position: **niche / early** (analysis). Vivi is a Byron Bay, Australia neobank; Shop & Pay is gated to Premium Metal customers from November (waitlist) — announced and partially demoed (Afends tee, a flight booking over Visa), not yet broadly live. Moat: thin today — the identity-verified-account + open-banking context is a switching-cost play, but the actual security primitives (scoped single-use tokens, agent verification) are Visa's rails, not Vivi's IP (analysis).

**Risk flags.**
1. *Disintermediation / value captured upstream.* The security that Vivi markets (scoped single-use tokens, agent verification, fraud scoring) is Visa Intelligent Commerce / TAP functionality; if Visa exposes the same consumer-approval UX to any wallet or to the LLMs directly, Vivi's app-layer differentiation erodes. Vivi is a consumer of someone else's rails, not an owner of them.
2. *Dependence on Visa's rails + the LLMs.* Vivi explicitly plans to let customers connect ChatGPT/Claude as the agent — so it is sandwiched between the model layer (OpenAI/Anthropic, who are integrating payments directly) and the network layer (Visa). A squeeze from either side compresses whatever take Vivi can keep.
3. *Execution / "announced not live."* Shop & Pay is waitlist-gated to Premium Metal customers from November 2026 with only demo transactions shown; agent-initiated fraud/mis-purchase liability and scale are unproven. De-PR: no economics (take rate, who bears fraud loss), no user numbers, no TPV disclosed.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Agentic commerce is re-rating the consumer-wallet layer around one question: who holds the trusted, identity-verified account the agent pays from (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: Vivi's "your agent pays with Vivi" positioning only becomes a moat if third-party agents (ChatGPT/Claude) actually route payments through Vivi rather than through the networks' own agent integrations — watch for a named LLM or merchant integration beyond the November waitlist launch. Trigger that breaks it: Visa/Mastercard or the LLMs shipping a direct, wallet-agnostic consumer-approval flow, leaving Vivi as a thin front-end.
