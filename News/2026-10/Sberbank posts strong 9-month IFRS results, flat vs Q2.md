### [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)

On 2026-10-28 Sberbank released its **9M / Q3 2026 IFRS** results. Headline numbers (corroborated across Investing.com Q3-slides, Reuters/TradingView, and the bank's IR deck):

- **9M 2026 net profit: RUB 1,529.9bn, +17.0% YoY**; **ROE 24.0%** (+0.3pp YoY).
- **Q3 2026 net profit: RUB 510.8bn, +13.9% YoY.**
- **Net interest income: 9M RUB 3,159bn (+23.0% YoY); Q3 RUB 1,108bn (+24.0% YoY).**
- **NIM 6.65% in Q3, down ~4bp QoQ.**
- **Cost of risk 1.1%** (9M and Q3), improved from 1.4% in Q3 2025; corporate CoR 0.7%, retail CoR ~1.7%.
- **Loan book:** corporate RUB 34.2tn (+5.7% QoQ, +9.6% YtD); retail RUB 20.9tn (+2.9% QoQ), mortgages RUB 13.6tn.
- **Equity RUB 9.0tn** (+7.8% YtD); H20.0 capital adequacy ~15.1% vs 13.3% internal floor (as of H1).

**The real story the channel flagged: profit was essentially FLAT quarter-on-quarter.** Q3 net profit (RUB 510.8bn) was marginally *below* the ~RUB 520–525bn implied for Q2 (H1 profit RUB 1,019bn − Q1). The Финтехно note nails the mechanism: Sber earned **+RUB 41bn more in net interest income** QoQ, but this was **fully absorbed by higher operating expenses and "other negative results"** (likely trading/FX/one-off provisioning swings). So the top-line NII engine is still revving (+23–24% YoY), yet it no longer *converts* into incremental bottom-line growth sequentially.

**Why this framing matters:** the PR/headline anchors to the flattering YoY frame (+17%), which is backward-looking and benefits from an easy 2025 base. The *forward*-looking signal is the QoQ plateau — the first quarter in this cycle where Sber's record NII stopped lifting profit. That is the de-PR'd substance.

### [1] Competitors / peers

- **VTB, T-Bank (Tinkoff), Alfa:** Per the parallel CB September review note [[Финтехно CB September review shows record bank profits]], the Russian sector earned RUB 3.7tn in 9M 2026 (> all of FY2025's 3.5tn). **Sber alone is ~3.6x VTB and ~10x T-Bank by absolute profit** — a category of one. Top-5 banks took ~2/3 of sector profit (RUB 2.47tn), i.e. heavy concentration.
- **Growth-rate leader is T-Bank**, but off a low 2025 base (RUB 48.1bn) — not comparable in quality.
- **Peer read-across (non-RU):** the same QoQ dynamic — strong NII but flat/compressing sequential profit — is visible at Asian banks reporting the same window, e.g. [[OCBC posts S$2 billion Q3 net profit, up 9% sequentially]] (there profit *rose* QoQ) and [[CIMB Group Q3 2025 net profit rises, non-interest income up 20.3%]]. Unlike those, Sber's sequential print went sideways despite NII growth.

**Why the lay of the land is this way:** Russian banks are riding an abnormally wide NIM because deposit rates are falling faster than loan yields as the CBR eases from peak. That is a *transient* margin tailwind, not structural — second order, it means the sector's record profits are partly a rate-cycle artifact that normalizes as the gap closes.

### [2] Company history / fit

Sber's 2026 trajectory: Q1 ROE 24%, H1 RAS profit RUB 995bn (+20.4% YoY, [[Sberbank H1 2026 net profit rises 20.4% to RUB 995bn]]), H1 IFRS RUB 1,019bn at ROE 24.2%. The 9M IFRS +17% is a *deceleration* from the +20%/+21% YoY prints earlier in the year — consistent with a maturing rate cycle. Guidance: FY2026 net profit ~RUB 2tn, ROE target ≥22% (already beating it), 50% IFRS payout dividend policy. This result fits the pattern: still beating its own target, but the YoY growth rate is fading and the QoQ plateau confirms it.

**Why Sber acts/reports this way:** as a state-controlled systemic bank with a 50% dividend commitment, it is structurally motivated to signal profit *stability* and dividend coverage (capital buffer 15.1% vs 13.3% floor) rather than chase growth. The flat QoQ print is therefore tolerable to management — the binding constraint is maintaining ROE >20% and the dividend, both intact.

### [3] Novelty / value-add / traction

**Novelty for the corpus: this is a genuinely NEW reporting period** — the first full 9M/Q3 2026 IFRS disclosure, distinct from the H1 RAS note and from the sector-level CB review. It is FRESH.

**But the analytical value-add is the inflection, not the beat.** Traction of the franchise is real (NII +23–24% YoY, loans +9.6% YtD, CoR improving to 1.1%). What's *new in substance* is that the record NII no longer drops to the bottom line QoQ — cost growth and "other negative results" ate the RUB 41bn NII gain. Who captures the margin: right now the deposit-vs-loan spread does; as CBR cuts continue, that spread compresses and the question becomes whether fee/transaction income and the Sber tech/AI ecosystem can backfill. That is the structural swing factor, not the quarterly headline.

### [4] What's next / market sentiment

- **Guidance:** FY2026 profit ~RUB 2tn, implying a stronger-than-Q3 Q4 to hit it; ROE ≥22% target comfortably held; dividends ~45–48 RUB/share possible at 50% payout.
- **Macro/CBR backdrop:** the sector's record profit is NIM-driven as the CBR eases; the tailwind is self-limiting as deposit repricing catches up with loan yields. Corporate credit still growing, retail slowing, asset-quality pockets deteriorating (per CB review).
- **Sentiment:** analysts frame it as a "beat" on the +17% YoY, but the QoQ plateau is the counter-signal. **Counterintuitive second order:** record sector concentration (top-5 = 2/3 of profit) makes Sber's dominance look safe, yet it also means Sber *is* the Russian banking cycle — a NIM normalization or asset-quality shock hits it disproportionately, not less.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Importance: 3/5** — Sber is the single most important Russian bank and this is a genuinely new (9M/Q3 2026 IFRS) reporting period, so it is fresh and material. But the headline "+17% strong results" is a backward-looking YoY beat on an easy base; the only *new* analytical signal is the QoQ profit plateau, which is incremental rather than a regime change. Not a 4–5 (no strategic shift, no surprise, guidance intact).

1. **Is "strong" accurate or PR?** 9M +17% YoY IFRS, ROE 24% — genuinely strong vs its own ≥22% target. But QoQ profit was flat/slightly down (Q3 510.8bn vs ~Q2 ~520–525bn). "Strong" is a YoY-frame; the news is the sequential plateau.

2. **Why did profit stay flat QoQ despite +RUB 41bn more NII?** The channel says higher opex + "other negative results" absorbed it. Open: exact split between opex, provisioning, trading/FX one-offs — IFRS deck needed to confirm.

3. **IFRS vs RAS — same thing?** No. akm.ru reports RAS 9M +18.2%; H1 note was RAS (+20.4%). This note is IFRS (+17%). Different basis → NOT a duplicate of the RAS prints.

4. **Is it a duplicate of the CB September sector review?** No — that note is sector-level (RUB 3.7tn, concentration). This is Sber's own IFRS statement. Related, not duplicate.

5. **Is the +17% flattered by base effects?** Partly — 2025 comparables were weaker; YoY growth is decelerating from +20–21% earlier in 2026.

6. **How durable is the NIM?** 6.65%, -4bp QoQ. It rests on deposits repricing down faster than loans as CBR eases — a transient rate-cycle spread, not structural. Open: management's NIM guidance into 2027.

7. **Asset quality — real or masked?** CoR improved to 1.1% from 1.4% YoY (corporate 0.7%, retail 1.7%). But the CB review flags deteriorating quality in parts of the book. Is the low CoR sustainable or under-provisioned? Open.

8. **Can Sber hit FY RUB 2tn guidance?** 9M at 1,529.9bn implies Q4 ~470bn — below Q3's run-rate, so guidance looks conservatively reachable. Low risk.

9. **Dividend safety?** 50% IFRS payout; capital 15.1% vs 13.3% floor → buffer intact. ~45–48 RUB/share plausible. No red flag.

10. **Who captures the margin as rates fall?** Currently the deposit-loan spread. Second order: if CBR keeps cutting, spread compresses — does fee income + the AI/tech ecosystem (GigaChat etc.) backfill? Unproven. Key forward question.

11. **Loan growth quality?** Corporate +5.7% QoQ (strong) vs retail +2.9% (slowing) — matches sector pattern (corporate up, retail cooling). Consistent, not anomalous.

12. **Concentration risk framing.** Top-5 = 2/3 of sector profit; Sber ~3.6x VTB, ~10x T-Bank. Dominance looks safe but means Sber IS the cycle — a shock hits it disproportionately. Counterintuitive.

13. **Source quality?** Note's own source is e-disclosure.ru (filings portal) — no extracted text. Numbers cross-checked vs Investing.com Q3-slides, Reuters/TradingView, marketscreener guidance. One stray figure (interfax "1.307tn 9M / 448bn Q3") appears to be a stale/different-date item; the 1,529.9bn / 510.8bn set is the consistent 2026 print. Flag for reviewer.

14. **Does the title overclaim?** "Strong, flat vs Q2" is accurate and appropriately hedged — rare honesty in an earnings headline.

15. **What would make this a 4–5?** A guidance cut, a NIM collapse, an asset-quality surprise, or a dividend-policy change. None present — hence 3/5.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russian banking is an oligopoly dominated by state-linked giants: the ten largest banks capture ~75–76% of total sector profit, so Sber's result is close to a read on the whole sector (per CBR, via Interfax, 2026). The CBR raised its 2026 sector net-profit forecast to RUB 3.9–4.4tn, citing persistently high interest margins (CBR sector NIM guidance lifted to 4.9–5.1% from 4.4–4.6%), with Gref flagging ~RUB 4tn (per CBR/Gref, via Interfax/TASS, 2026). "Why now": the CBR held its key rate at 14.00% on 23 Oct 2026 (down from the 2025 peak) while raising its 2026 inflation forecast to 7.1–7.6% — a still-restrictive but easing cycle that keeps asset yields high yet signals margin peak is near. Barriers: capital, deposit-franchise scale and regulation (sanctions wall off foreign competition), entrenching the incumbents.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for banks: ROE, net interest margin (NIM), cost of risk (COR), cost-to-income (CIR). Key players: Sberbank (dominant, ~half of retail deposits), VTB (#2, state), T-Bank (digital-native), Alfa-Bank (largest private), plus Gazprombank/Rosselkhozbank. Basis of competition: deposit-funding cost, digital distribution and scale rather than price — foreign rivals are sanctioned out. Recent peer moves: VTB reported 8M 2026 IFRS net profit RUB 314.8bn, **down 4% YoY**, ROE 16.7%, though NIM recovered to 2.6% from ~1% a year earlier (per VTB, via TASS, Sep 2026). Sber's position: clearly ahead — its 9M 2026 ROE of 24.0% is ~7pp above VTB's 16.7%, reflecting a structurally cheaper deposit base and higher NIM (6.65% vs VTB's 2.6%). Moat: deposit-franchise scale + the dominant retail-tech platform (switching costs, network effects) `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Sber 9M 2026 IFRS (reported 28 Oct 2026): net profit RUB 1,529.9bn (+17.0% YoY); Q3 RUB 510.8bn (+13.9% YoY); ROE 24.0%; NII RUB 3,159bn (+23.0% YoY); NIM 6.65% (−4bp QoQ). The headline "flat vs Q2" checks out: Q2 2026 net profit was RUB 511.2bn vs Q3's RUB 510.8bn — effectively flat QoQ, i.e. ~RUB 0.4bn lower, consistent with the note's "profit did not grow vs Q2" and "+RUB 41bn more interest income offset by higher costs/other negatives" (per Sber Q2/Q3 2026 IFRS, via Investing.com/Interfax).
- Internal / prior-year base (IR, year-stale): Sber 9M 2025 operating income before provisions RUB 3,078.7bn vs 2,622.7bn, +17.4%; NII RUB 2,567.8bn, +18.0% — showing the same ~17% growth cadence a year earlier (Sber 9M/Q3 2025 IFRS, drive_url https://drive.google.com/file/d/1dgQJ8PCYhwk5SjQDa5katvsdt-VjWGJN/view). FY2024 ROE 24.0%, NIM 5.9%, CIR 30.3% (Sber AR 2024, drive_url https://drive.google.com/file/d/11RQ_7D9_QI8dPMWK_MNYA2WyMuhRJNtA/view).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Margin peak / rate-cut sensitivity.** NIM already slipped 4bp QoQ to 6.65% and Q3 profit went flat; as the CBR eases from 14%, deposit repricing lags asset repricing, so NIM — and thus the sector's record profit — likely compresses into 2027. Second-order: Sber's ~17% growth cadence is late-cycle, not structural.
2. **Cost-of-risk / credit-cycle.** 9M 2025 IR already showed net credit-quality charges jumping +81.6% YoY (RUB 504.0bn vs 277.6bn); a high-rate economy with a slowing economy ("economic slowdown" per Q3 slides) raises provisioning risk that can absorb the extra interest income — exactly the "other negative results" the note cites.
3. **Concentration / sanctions & capital.** Sber is a single-geography, sanctioned entity; sector profit is hyper-concentrated (top-10 = ~75% of profit) while ~a quarter of Russian credit institutions turned unprofitable in 2026 — the headline hides a bifurcated system, and VTB's CEO flags capital-raising as the sector's main 2026 challenge.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Flat QoQ profit at peak NIM is the first visible sign that Russia's rate-driven earnings supercycle is topping out; the story to watch is NIM trajectory and cost of risk, not headline profit `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: if the CBR cuts further and Q4 2026/Q1 2027 NIM holds above ~6.5% with COR stable, Sber clears ~RUB 2tn FY2026 as guided; the thesis breaks if COR keeps climbing (as the +81.6% YoY provisioning trend hints), in which case flat-QoQ becomes outright decline. Trigger: Q4 2026 IFRS + any CBR rate move.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (headline read).** BEAT / solid in-line-to-beat · 9M 2026 net profit RUB 1,529.9bn (+17.0% YoY), Q3 RUB 510.8bn (+13.9% YoY) · 9M ROE 24.0% (Q3 23.5%) · driven by net interest income +23.0% YoY (9M) and a falling cost of risk (1.1% vs 1.4% a year ago), partly offset by sliding fee income and rising opex · FY2026 guidance reaffirmed around 22% ROE and ~RUB 2trn profit. Shares rose on the print; Tsifra Broker called Q3 "practically in line with expectations." Note the "flat vs Q2" framing in the Финтехно headline is not supported by the reported figures — Q3 net profit actually rose ~+13.9% QoQ (RUB 510.8bn vs Q2 RUB 448.3bn); the "flat" characterization refers only to the fact that a +~RUB 41bn QoQ lift in net interest income was partly eroded by higher costs/other lines, not to bottom-line profit.

**Key figures (with growth).**
- Net profit: 9M RUB 1,529.9bn (+17.0% YoY); Q3 RUB 510.8bn (+13.9% YoY); Q2 2026 was RUB 448.3bn, so Q3 +~13.9% QoQ.
- ROE: 9M 24.0% (+0.3pp YoY); Q3 23.5%.
- Net interest income: 9M RUB 3,158.8bn (+23.0% YoY); Q3 RUB 1,108bn (+24.0% YoY). NIM guided above 6.2%.
- Net fee & commission income: 9M RUB 632.1bn (+2.8% YoY); Q3 RUB 212.3bn (−1.9% YoY) — a drag, now flat-to-declining.
- Operating expenses: 9M RUB 991bn (+14.7% YoY); cost-to-income 9M 28.4% (+0.4pp), Q3 CIR 29.4%.
- Cost of risk: 1.1% (both Q3 and 9M), down from 1.4% in Q3 2025; 9M net provision charges RUB 443bn (−12.2% YoY), Q3 provisions RUB 145.0bn (−14.2%), helped by updated (more benign) macro forecasts.
- Capital: total equity RUB 9,001bn (+7.8% YTD); N20.0 group capital adequacy 13.7% (−1.4pp QoQ after the record RUB 850bn August dividend); IFRS CET1 12.9%, Tier 1 13.2%, total capital 14.0%. Stage-3 loan share 5.3% with 97.6% coverage.
- Loan book: corporate RUB 34.2trn (+5.7% QoQ, +9.6% YTD, 32.7% share); retail RUB 20.9trn (+2.9% QoQ, 51.1% share). Retail clients 110.7mn; SberBank Online MAU 87.6mn.

**By segment / driver.** The engine is net interest income (+23–24% YoY), compounding on book growth and a still-wide margin, plus a lower cost of risk (1.1% vs 1.4%) as reserve charges fell ~12–14% on improved macro assumptions. Working against the print: fee income has turned negative YoY in Q3 (−1.9%) — management's own FY guide is ~0% fee growth, confirming this is structural, not a blip — and opex grew +14.7%, nudging CIR up. The de-PR read on the "flat vs Q2" story: the +~RUB 41bn QoQ gain in NII was largely offset at the operating line by higher expenses and weaker non-interest/other results, but the bottom line still rose ~14% QoQ, partly flattered by the falling provision charge rather than by core revenue acceleration.

**vs expectations / prior period.** No clean public sell-side consensus number was located for Q3 net profit; marking the explicit beat/miss delta [UNSOURCED]. Qualitatively: Q3 RUB 510.8bn is +13.9% YoY and +~13.9% QoQ vs Q2 RUB 448.3bn — momentum intact, not decelerating. Tsifra Broker (Цифра брокер) reportedly judged Q3 "practically in line" with its expectations and rated the results positively; T-Investments reiterated a Buy with a RUB 388 target. Sber-group IFRS context in the corpus runs through FY2025 (RUB net profit under the 12M 2025 statements, `data/sber/ir/annual_report/reporting_q4_8a5ewlbo_2025_en.pdf`) and 9M/Q3 2025 (`data/sber/ir/quarterly_results/ifrs_results_q3_4gpw5f9j_2025.pdf`), confirming the YoY base; the irdb does not yet hold the 9M 2026 filing (lags ~3+ months).

**Guidance / forward.** FY2026 guidance effectively reaffirmed/updated at the print: ROE ~22%, NIM above 6.2%, cost of risk below 1.4%, CIR 30–32%, fee income ~0% growth, N20.0 ~13.3%; macro assumptions nudged up (GDP +0.5–1.0% from 0–0.5%; inflation 6.5–7.5%). Management targets ~RUB 2trn FY2026 net profit — a record — which the 9M RUB 1,529.9bn run-rate (implied Q4 ~RUB 470bn) makes plausible. Management tone: confident on profitability but openly flagging an economic slowdown (B2B turnover growth collapsed to 1.2% in Q3 from 10.2% in Q2; household income growth eased to 10.3%); heavy emphasis on tech (GigaChat 3.5 "reasoning" model). What they lean away from: the fee-income softness and the capital draw-down from the dividend.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. Capital headroom is thinning — N20.0 13.7% (−1.4pp QoQ) after an RUB 850bn dividend, guided to ~13.3% — so the record payout and the record profit target now compete for the same capital; a sharper slowdown or a COR back toward the sub-1.4% ceiling would squeeze the dividend-vs-growth trade-off (second-order: dividend sustainability becomes the key equity-story swing factor).
2. Earnings quality is tilting toward lower provisions, not stronger revenue — Q3's QoQ profit lift leaned on a −14% provision charge (benign macro reassessment); if the slowdown (B2B turnover +1.2%) feeds through to asset quality, that tailwind reverses and COR normalizes up, pressuring the ~22% ROE.
3. Fee income has gone negative YoY (Q3 −1.9%, FY guide ~0%) — the non-interest leg of the model has stalled, leaving profit growth over-reliant on NII and margin, which are rate-cycle sensitive; a faster CBR easing path would compress NIM from today's >6.2%.
4. Growth is holding (loans +5.7% QoQ corporate, +2.9% retail; ROE still 23.5% in Q3) despite the macro cooldown — the near-term bull case, but increasingly a late-cycle one.
