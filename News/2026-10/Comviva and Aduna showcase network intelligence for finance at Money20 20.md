LAS VEGAS , Oct. 19, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Comviva , a global leader in digital transformation solutions, and Aduna,  the global aggregator of standardized network APIs, are advancing the use of network intelligence to address growing digital identity and fraud challenges in financial services. At Money20/20 USA, taking place October 18–21, 2026, in Las Vegas, the companies will showcase the progress of their collaboration and demonstrate how network APIs can help financial institutions strengthen identity verification, detect SIM-swap detection, enhance fraud prevention, and streamline customer onboarding. By enabling financial institutions to leverage network-derived insights, the collaboration aims to strengthen digital trust while supporting more secure and frictionless customer experiences.
The showcase comes nearly a year after Comviva and Aduna announced their strategic partnership in November 2025 to accelerate enterprise adoption and monetisation of Network APIs through Comviva's NGAGE platform . Through the platform, enterprises can access communication and network APIs through a single interface, while Aduna brings together standardised network capabilities from its global operator ecosystem. NGAGE platform already operates at significant commercial scale, supporting 7,000+ enterprises, 40+ deployments, 100+ industry use cases, 130+ Communication APIs, and 25+ Network APIs, while processing 250+ billion messages annually. NGAGE is deployed across industries.
For banks and fintechs, network-level intelligence can provide an additional layer of information for customer verification and risk assessment. Capabilities such as number verification and SIM-swap detection can help identify potential fraud , strengthen authentication and support faster, lower-friction onboarding and KYC journeys.
The focus on financial services builds on Comviva's established presence in the sector and its experience supporting digital financial services ecosystems globally. The collaboration brings this expertise together with network intelligence to address practical challenges across identity, fraud prevention and customer onboarding.
 Rajesh Chandiramani, Chief Executive Officer, Comviva , said, "Over the past year, NGAGE has evolved as a unified and scalable access point for enterprises looking to bring network intelligence into their digital journeys. Financial services is a natural priority because the impact can be measured directly through outcomes such as faster onboarding, stronger fraud controls and reduced dependence on OTPs and other verification steps. Our collaboration with Aduna, combined with Tech Mahindra's BFSI reach, enables us to take these capabilities to financial institutions across markets and help address these challenges at scale." 
 Peter Arbitter, Chief Commercial Officer, Aduna Global , said, "Standardisation is essential to scaling the use of network APIs across financial services. Aduna brings together CAMARA-standardised APIs from leading mobile operators, simplifying access to trusted network intelligence across operators and markets. Working with Comviva and Tech Mahindra makes it easier for banks and fintechs to embed these capabilities into customer verification, fraud prevention and onboarding processes, helping them strengthen security while reducing friction for their customers." 
The collaboration will continue to focus on expanding the use of Network APIs across financial services markets and exploring applications beyond identity and fraud , including lending risk, payments intelligence and other areas where real-time network information can support secure and seamless digital experiences.
Comviva will showcase its NGAGE, a new-age Communication Platform as a Service that draws its power and performance from robust AI engines at Money20/20 USA alongside its parent company Tech Mahindra  at MC-7 . Together, the companies will demonstrate how AI, advanced analytics, network APIs and digital platforms are helping financial institutions accelerate innovation, strengthen fraud prevention and identity verification, and deliver more seamless customer experiences.

# Context-enrichment: Comviva and Aduna showcase network intelligence for finance at Money20/20
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

**Freshness verdict: FRESH-but-thin.** No prior internal note covers Comviva×Aduna or Aduna at all; this is the first appearance in the corpus. BUT the underlying tie-up is ~1 year old (announced Nov 2025), and this Money20/20 item is a *conference showcase / re-framing* of that existing partnership toward financial services, not a new deal, new terms, or new funding. No bank customers, no contract, no numbers specific to finance. It is a marketing booth story (Tech Mahindra booth MC-7). Treat as low-weight visibility news, not a transaction.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- At Money20/20 USA (Oct 18–21, 2026, Las Vegas), **Comviva** (Tech Mahindra's digital-solutions arm) and **Aduna** (the Ericsson + 12-telco network-API aggregator JV) are *demonstrating* how network APIs can help banks with identity verification, SIM-swap detection, number verification and onboarding/KYC. This is a **showcase, not a launch** — the PR itself says "will demonstrate" and "aims to support". No named FI customer, no commercial figure, no live finance deployment is disclosed.
- The real substance is the **Nov 2025 strategic partnership**: Aduna exposes CAMARA-standardised network APIs from its global operator ecosystem; Comviva resells/monetises them through its **NGAGE (NGAGE.ai)** CPaaS platform via a single interface. The finance angle is a vertical go-to-market overlay on that pre-existing plumbing.
- NGAGE scale figures (7,000+ enterprises, 40+ deployments, 100+ use cases, 130+ comms APIs, 25+ network APIs, 250bn+ messages/yr) are **platform-wide, across all industries** — they say nothing about financial-services traction. Classic PR move: anchor a thin finance story to impressive cross-industry totals.
- **Why framed this way:** both parties need enterprise/vertical pull. Aduna has the supply (standardised APIs from AT&T, Airtel, DT, Jio, Orange, Verizon, Vodafone etc.) but needs channels that sell into verticals; Comviva/Tech Mahindra bring BFSI reach. Money20/20 is where you signal "we're serious about banks" — so a booth demo gets a press release.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Network-API-for-fraud is a **crowded, commoditising** space; Comviva/Aduna are a channel layer, not the only route:
- **Aduna's own ecosystem partners also compete at the channel level:** Vonage (Ericsson-owned), Sinch, Infobip, Google Cloud, Microsoft, Wipro — all resell CAMARA/Aduna APIs. **Vonage** went commercial with SIM-Swap Detection + Silent Authentication across major US carriers and 7 EU markets in **Nov 2025** — ahead of this finance showcase.
- **Telefónica Open Gateway**, **DT/O2/Vodafone Germany** (launched SIM-Swap APIs commercially 2024), and carrier federations (**Airtel, Jio, Vi** federated SIM-Swap/Number-Verify APIs in India, 2025) sell direct.
- **Pure-play identity/fraud:** Prove, Twilio (Verify/Lookup), BICS, Enstream, Syniverse — same signals, different packaging.
- **Substitute approaches in-corpus:** device-bound biometrics replacing OTP/SIM-swap exposure — [[Flipkart, Axis Bank, and PayU launch biometric card payments]] (India, Wibmo/PayU), [[Konfio moves to behavioral biometrics over one-time passwords]] (Mexico), [[UAE banks to replace OTPs with in-app verification]]. These attack the SAME problem (OTP/SIM-swap fraud) from the device side, not the network side.
- **Position:** catching-up/parity as a *channel*. Comviva/Aduna are not a technology leader here; they're assembling standardised supply + BFSI distribution. The differentiator is breadth of operator coverage via Aduna, not a novel capability.
- **Why the landscape is this way (analysis):** CAMARA standardisation deliberately commoditises the API itself → value migrates to (a) operator coverage/aggregation and (b) vertical distribution. Comviva plays (b); Aduna plays (a). Neither owns a defensible signal — the signal belongs to the carriers.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Comviva:** Tech Mahindra subsidiary, long history in mobile-money / digital financial services (its mobiquity Pay powers **Airtel Money's** cloud-native platform — see [[Airtel Money targets $7B valuation ahead of London IPO]]). So BFSI and telco-adjacent fintech are genuinely in its DNA; this is a logical extension, not a pivot.
- **Aduna:** Ericsson-led JV; equity close completed **Jul 2025** with 12 telco CSPs (AT&T, Bharti Airtel, DT, Ericsson, KDDI, Orange, Reliance Jio, Singtel, Telefónica, Telstra, T-Mobile, Verizon, Vodafone), 50:50 Ericsson/operators. CEO Anthony Bartolo (ex-Vonage COO) from Jan 2025. Mission: be the "super-aggregator" that standardises CAMARA APIs so developers/enterprises don't integrate carrier-by-carrier.
- **Fit (analysis):** Ericsson's bet is that network APIs become a new carrier revenue line; banks are the highest-willingness-to-pay vertical (fraud losses are large, quantifiable). Hence the finance push. Comviva is Ericsson/Aduna's distribution proxy into BFSI, especially EM markets where Tech Mahindra is strong.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** Nothing technically new is announced. SIM-swap/number-verification network APIs are already live elsewhere (Vonage Nov-2025 commercial, DT/O2/VF-DE 2024, Telefónica Open Gateway). This item adds a *vertical marketing wrapper*.
- **Traction for THIS tie-up in finance: zero disclosed.** No bank, no pilot numbers, no revenue. The impressive stats are cross-industry NGAGE totals.
- **Where real traction exists (external, for the category, not this deal):** Itaú Unibanco used the CAMARA SIM-Swap API 36.5M+ times through Apr 2025 (~5M calls/month); MovyPay reported ~80% chargeback-fraud reduction in 6 months post SIM-Swap API. GSMA Open Gateway: by Mar 2026, 86 operator groups / 300+ networks / ~80% of global mobile connections, 300+ commercial CAMARA instances across 65 markets. So the *category* is real and monetising — but the proof points are other players'.
- **Who captures the margin (analysis):** the scarce asset is the carrier's network signal; CAMARA commoditises the interface; aggregators (Aduna) take an integration cut; channel/CPaaS (Comviva/NGAGE) take a distribution markup. The thinner layer (channel) is most disintermediable — banks with scale (an Itaú) can integrate carriers/Aduna directly and skip the reseller. So Comviva's finance value-add is real only where it brings BFSI relationships EM banks can't self-serve.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Stated roadmap: expand beyond identity/fraud into **lending risk, payments intelligence**, using real-time network data. These are speculative ("exploring").
- **Backdrop is genuinely tailwind-y:** UK SIM-swap fraud surged 1,055% (289→~3,000 cases) 2023→2024, +38% further in 2025 — regulators and banks are hunting network-layer defences; OTP-over-SMS is being deprecated (UAE, India RBI guidance).
- **Structural limits / second-order (analysis):** (1) GSMA itself flagged in 2026 that API *availability* outpaces *enterprise revenue* conversion — the money is slower than the hype. (2) **Fragmented carrier coverage** is the core constraint: a check returns no signal if the subscriber's operator isn't in the aggregator's footprint → false "all clear" risk. (3) Commoditisation means the channel margin compresses; the counterintuitive effect is that Aduna's standardisation *helps carriers and big banks but squeezes resellers like Comviva* over time. So the central question for THIS item isn't "is network intelligence useful" (it is) — it's "does Comviva capture durable margin, or is it a thin, substitutable distribution skin on a carrier-owned asset?" — currently unproven.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits in telco **network-API / "Open Gateway"** infrastructure sold into financial services for identity, authentication and fraud. Market sizing (secondary, treat as directional): per MarketsandMarkets, the Network API market is ~$1.96bn in 2025 → ~$6.13bn by 2030 (CAGR ~25.7%); per Mordor Intelligence, the CAMARA API ecosystem is ~$1.51bn (2026) → ~$6.08bn by 2031 (CAGR ~32.1%), with Authentication & Fraud-Prevention APIs holding ~34% of that ecosystem in 2025 (per Mordor, via mordorintelligence.com). Structure: a thin-but-strategic layer in the value chain — the *data* (SIM-swap, number-verification signals) originates with mobile operators; value accrues to whoever **aggregates and standardises** across operators (Aduna) and whoever **distributes/monetises** the APIs to enterprises (Comviva's NGAGE). Entry barrier = network effects on the supply side: you must sign dozens of carriers. "Why now": (1) GSMA Open Gateway had ~86 operator groups / ~300 networks / ~80% of global connections aligned by early 2026 (per GSMA, via gsmaintelligence.com) — supply finally dense enough to sell cross-market; (2) regulatory/fraud pressure pushing banks off SMS-OTP toward network-derived verification. (analysis) The deal itself carries **no valuation, round or revenue** — it is a Money20/20 showcase of a Nov-2025 partnership, not a transaction.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: # operators/markets covered, # network APIs live, call volume, and (the hard one) **revenue per API call / monetisation rate** — GSMA itself flags monetisation as "the make-or-break challenge for network APIs in 2026" (per GSMA). Players & basis of competition:
- **Aggregators (supply):** Aduna (Ericsson + AT&T/T-Mobile/Verizon/Orange/DT/Vodafone/Jio et al., founded Jul-2025; sole comprehensive CAMARA coverage of the US per Morningstar/PRNewswire, expanding to ~40 operators). Competes on operator breadth + CAMARA standardisation.
- **Distribution / rival CPaaS fraud stacks:** **Twilio Lookup v2** already ships SIM-swap, line-type, identity-match and reassigned-number signals in one call (per twilio.com) — a direct substitute for the exact use case Comviva/Aduna pitch. **Vonage** (Ericsson-owned, also an Aduna partner) and **Prove** (passive/phone-centric auth, appears in corpus onboarding deals) overlap.
- **SIM-credential verification:** [[Glide Identity raises over $20 million Series A]] — Open-Gateway, SIM-based cryptographic auth, Singtel Innov8-backed.
Position: Comviva is a **distributor/platform** (NGAGE: 7,000+ enterprises, 40+ deployments, 130+ comms + 25+ network APIs, 250bn+ messages/yr — all self-reported, unaudited) riding Aduna's aggregated supply plus Tech Mahindra's BFSI channel. Moat = distribution/channel + parent reach, **not** proprietary data (the signals are the carriers'). (analysis) Relative to Twilio, it is catching-up on the direct-to-fintech fraud API, ahead on Aduna-sourced multi-operator breadth outside the US.

**Comps & multiples.** No deal economics here → no transaction multiple to compute. Public anchor: parent **Tech Mahindra** (NSE: TECHM) is listed, but no segment revenue for Comviva/NGAGE is broken out → EV/Revenue for this asset = **no data**. Aduna is a private JV, no disclosed post-money → **[UNSOURCED]**. Comviva's NGAGE "scale" metrics are vanity counts, not revenue, so no price-per-unit arithmetic is possible — **distribution not computed, qualitative only**. Internal comp: [[Glide Identity raises over $20 million Series A]] (Oct-2025, ~$20m+ Series A, Crosspoint-led, ~$25m total) — the closest corpus precedent on network/SIM-based identity economics; a VC round valuation, not a market cap, and a different (credential) sub-segment.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Disintermediation / value capture upstream.** The data belongs to the mobile operators (and Aduna aggregates it). Comviva is a reseller layer — if carriers/Aduna go direct to banks or margins get squeezed at the aggregator, NGAGE's take-rate compresses. Second-order: the "moat" is channel, which is the weakest of the four moats.
2. **Substitution by incumbents.** Twilio Lookup already sells SIM-swap + number-verification to the same fintech buyers today (live, not "showcased"). This is an "announced showcase" vs a live, scaled competitor — de-PR gap.
3. **Monetisation unproven at the layer.** GSMA itself names monetisation the 2026 make-or-break; no revenue, take-rate or paying-FI count disclosed for the FS vertical — "7,000 enterprises" is cross-industry and unaudited.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) The signal is sector-level, not company-level: telco network APIs are moving from MWC talking-point into the FS fraud/onboarding buying cycle, pressuring SMS-OTP and standalone KYC vendors. Falsifiable thesis: if network-API fraud/identity is real, expect **paid, named bank deployments with disclosed volumes** (not showcases) within ~2–3 quarters; watch Aduna's US carrier coverage + any Tech Mahindra FS reference accounts. Thesis breaks if, 12 months post-partnership, the only artifact remains conference demos — which, so far, is what this note is.
