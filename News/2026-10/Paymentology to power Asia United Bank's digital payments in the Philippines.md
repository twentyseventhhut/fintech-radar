Manila**, Philippines, 19 October 2026**– Paymentology, the leading global issuer-processor has secured a strategic deal with Asia United Bank (AUB), one of the Philippines' leading universal banks, to power the institution's modern digital payments platform. 
Announced at The Issuer Academy Thailand Masterclass, hosted by Paymentology in Bangkok, the partnership will integrate the company’s cloud-native issuing and real-time processing platform into AUB’s digital ecosystem. Paymentology’s platform will support AUB’s flagship e-wallet, HelloMoney, alongside its mobile banking services, QR Ph capabilities and digital transfer networks. In partnership with Alipay+, HelloMoney pioneered cross-border mobile payments for Philippine banks, enabling users to scan and pay via QR code across 60 major international destinations, including Japan, South Korea, Malaysia, Hong Kong SAR, and Singapore.  
  
The deal positions Paymentology at the center of the Philippines' rapid shift toward digital finance. Latest Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas data reveals that 62% of households performed online financial transactions in 2025, up from 53% the previous year.  
  
Through the partnership, AUB will draw on Paymentology’s modern issuing and payment processing capabilities as it continues to evolve its digital payments proposition. The collaboration will provide AUB with modern, scalable payments infrastructure designed to support faster innovation and the development of new payment products and customer experiences.  
  
**Wilfredo E. Rodriguez Jr., AUB Executive Vice President and Head of IT and Operations, said:**“Driving digital innovation is central to our mission of providing fast, secure and frictionless financial solutions. Our partnership with Paymentology marks an important milestone in AUB’s digital transformation. By combining Paymentology’s advanced payments technology with AUB’s strong presence and understanding of the Philippine market, we can accelerate the rollout of modern payment products and continue delivering enhanced digital experiences to our customers.”  
  
**Anna Porra, Chief Revenue Officer at Paymentology, added:** “The Philippines is an incredibly exciting payments market, with strong momentum behind digital adoption and financial inclusion. We’re proud to partner with AUB as it enters the next stage of its digital journey. Together, we have an opportunity to build payment experiences that are secure, scalable, and ready to evolve alongside AUB’s ambitions.”  
  
At The Issuer Academy in Bangkok, Mr. Rodriguez also joined Paymentology for a panel discussion titled **“Beyond Access: Building Digital Finance People Can Trust.”** The session explored how financial institutions can move beyond expanding access alone and instead create digital financial experiences built around trust, usability and long-term customer relationships. The discussion reflected a wider priority for AUB as it continues to strengthen its digital offering: combining trusted customer relationships with the technology needed to deliver simpler, faster and more relevant payment experiences at scale.  
  
For AUB, the partnership with Paymentology represents another step in that direction, giving the bank a modern payments foundation from which it can continue to develop its digital proposition and respond to the changing expectations of customers across the Philippines.

# Context-enrichment: Paymentology to power Asia United Bank's (AUB) digital payments in the Philippines
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Paymentology (London-based global issuer-processor) **signed a deal with Asia United Bank (AUB)**, a listed Philippine universal bank, to supply its cloud-native **issuing + real-time processing** platform underneath AUB's digital stack — the **HelloMoney e-wallet**, mobile banking, **QR Ph**, and digital-transfer rails. Announced 19 Oct 2026 at Paymentology's "Issuer Academy Thailand Masterclass" in Bangkok.

De-PR points:
- **This is a vendor-selection / platform win, NOT a product launch.** No new card, wallet or customer feature went live. AUB *chose* (or will migrate to) Paymentology's processing engine. The verbs in the release are future/aspirational ("will integrate," "will support," "designed to support faster innovation") → **announced, not live.** No go-live date, no migration timeline, no volumes committed, no commercials disclosed.
- **"Secures a strategic deal" = a signed processor contract.** The economically real content is that Paymentology displaces/augments AUB's existing issuing-processing arrangement. HelloMoney and QR Ph already exist and run today (HelloMoney launched 2020, QR rollout 2021) — so this is a **back-end processor swap/upgrade for an operating product**, not a greenfield build. That makes it a migration, with the attendant execution risk the PR omits.
- **The 62%-of-households BSP stat is market-tailwind framing**, not a deal metric. It justifies "why AUB is investing," not the size/impact of this specific contract.
- **Announced at Paymentology's own event**, with AUB's EVP/Head of IT (Wilfredo E. Rodriguez Jr.) on a Paymentology panel — i.e. this is as much a **Paymentology marketing/logo-win** moment as an AUB operational milestone.

→ Why structured this way: issuer-processors grow by **banking logos** — each named universal-bank client is proof of enterprise credibility and a reference account for the region. Paymentology is stacking PH wins (PayMongo SME card Jun-2026; now AUB) to establish APAC/Philippines density on the back of its May-2026 $175M raise. For AUB, outsourcing issuing-processing to a modern cloud vendor is cheaper/faster than modernizing legacy in-house rails.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Issuer-processor layer (who AUB could have picked instead):
- **Marqeta, Thredd (ex-GPS), Enfuce, Pismo (Visa-owned), Galileo (SoFi), i2c, FIS/Fiserv (legacy incumbents)** — the global modern-issuer-processor set Paymentology competes against for bank/fintech mandates.
- **Paymentology's own edge in EM/APAC**: 68-70 countries, strong Middle East/Africa/LatAm/APAC footprint, M-Pesa/TrueMoney/GoTyme/Snappi reference clients — it wins where legacy processors are weak and where cloud-native + local-market support matters.

Philippine payments/wallet layer (what AUB's HelloMoney competes against — the *demand* side, not Paymentology's rivals):
- **GCash** (~81M active users; Mynt/Globe-Ant-Ayala) and **Maya** (~45M; owns a digital-bank licence) dominate. HelloMoney is a **distant bank-owned wallet** — its differentiator is Alipay+-powered **cross-border QR across 60 destinations**, a niche GCash/Maya also chase.

→ Second-order: Paymentology winning AUB does **not** make HelloMoney competitive vs GCash/Maya — processing quality is table-stakes, not a consumer moat. The real beneficiary is **Paymentology's logo book**, not AUB's market share. And because modern issuer-processing is increasingly commoditized (many vendors, banks multi-source), the win signals Paymentology's regional sales motion is working more than it signals a structural shift in PH payments.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: low-to-moderate.** It is a **net-new, named universal-bank client** for Paymentology in a strategic market (genuinely new counterparty — not a re-report of PayMongo), which has some weight. But it is a **processor contract announcement, not a product/traction event**: nothing is live, no volumes, no commercials, no go-live date. It is Paymentology's 2nd PH logo in ~4 months and Nth global logo win of 2026 — the pattern is familiar.

Traction is **AUB's pre-existing** (HelloMoney, QR Ph, Alipay+ corridor already operating) — this deal reorganizes the back end; it does not create users. The value AUB gets is **speed/scalability/modernization**; the value Paymentology gets is a **reference logo + future per-transaction processing revenue** (which only materializes once AUB migrates live volume).

→ Who captures margin / what breaks it: Paymentology earns **per-transaction/SaaS processing fees** once live — so the upside is gated on **successful migration of a live, operating wallet**, the hardest part (core-system migrations slip, risk outages). If migration stalls or AUB multi-sources, the "win" stays a press release. The deal's real-world impact is unverifiable until go-live.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch for: (a) a **go-live / migration-complete announcement** (the real milestone); (b) **new AUB products** built on Lume (the PR hints at this); (c) **more Paymentology PH/APAC logo wins** post-$175M raise (T2P Thailand, PayMongo, now AUB — the regional land-grab). Group-level, Paymentology is pushing into **credit, stablecoin, tokenisation, AI** adjacencies per the May-2026 raise.

Sentiment/backdrop: PH digital-payments adoption is structurally rising (BSP: 62% of households transacted online in 2025 vs 53%); GCash/Maya dominate consumer wallets, leaving bank-owned wallets like HelloMoney to differentiate on cross-border/QR. Modern issuer-processing is a crowded, commoditizing vendor market (Marqeta/Thredd/Pismo/Galileo/i2c), so each named bank win is competitively meaningful for Paymentology but **not individually market-moving**.

→ Counterintuitive second-order: the thing that makes this a "win" (a legacy-bank wallet already in production) is also what makes it **riskiest to deliver** — migrating a live cross-border wallet is far harder than onboarding a greenfield fintech (PayMongo). The press-release optimism front-runs the execution risk; the deal is only as good as the eventual, undated go-live. `(analysis)`

**Sector & drivers.** Issuer-processing / card-and-wallet infrastructure for the Philippine digital-payments market. Size: combined PESONet + InstaPay transfer value grew 45% YoY to PHP 19.16tn (~$310bn) over Jan–Jul 2026 vs PHP 13.23tn ($214bn) a year earlier (per coingeek citing BSP, as of 2026-09); the Philippine digital-wallet layer specifically is put at ~$16.9bn in 2026, up from ~$13.7bn in 2025 (per IMARC, ~17.8% CAGR to 2034) — third-party market-sizing, treat as indicative. BSP data (cited in the PR) shows 62% of households transacted online in 2025 vs 53% in 2024; QR now ~60% of digital transactions (per GCash commentary, 2026). Structure — the rails are regulated and standardised (BSP since Feb 2026 mandates InstaPay/PESONet interoperability), while the front end is e-wallet-led and concentrated (GCash, Maya dominate P2P/merchant). Value chain: the bank owns the customer/licence; the *issuer-processor* supplies card issuance + real-time transaction processing underneath — a layer banks increasingly rent rather than build. Why now: legacy-core replacement + QR Ph / cross-border (Alipay+) expansion force banks onto cloud-native processing they cannot develop in-house at speed.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: transaction/processing volume (TPV), number of live card programs/clients, countries covered, take-per-transaction (Paymentology does not disclose a take rate → `[UNSOURCED]`). Paymentology scale: >400 clients, >500 staff, ~68–70 countries; new sales +117% and transaction volume +65% in its last fiscal year (per pymnts/Finextra, May 2026); clients include M-Pesa, TrueMoney, GoTyme, Wio Bank, Snappi, RedotPay, Albo. Basis of competition: breadth of certified rails + geographic coverage + cloud-native real-time processing, not price. Peers/recent moves: Marqeta (public, US) — Q2 2026 TPV $120bn, +32% YoY (IR); Thredd (private, UK) — the other global issuer-processor peer, joined Visa's Agentic-Ready programme (Jul 2026); Pine Labs active in SE Asia bank card platforms (e.g. Sri Lanka's Pan Asia Bank, Jan 2026); Pomelo in LatAm. Position: a leading EM-focused issuer-processor, ahead in emerging-market bank distribution, catching up to Marqeta on absolute disclosed volume `(analysis)`.
