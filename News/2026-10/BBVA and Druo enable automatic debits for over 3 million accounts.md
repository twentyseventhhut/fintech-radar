## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** BBVA Colombia and Colombian paytech Druo announced a partnership to enable real-time **automatic debits (recurring account-to-account charges)** on more than **3 million BBVA accounts** in Colombia. A user links and validates their BBVA account from a participating merchant's digital channel, pre-authorizes the arrangement, and from then on the merchant can collect recurring payments automatically under the agreed terms — with real-time confirmation, 24/7/365 (including Sundays and holidays). Druo states it intends to extend the capability to accounts at **other banks via Bre-B** (Colombia's instant-payment rail). Target use cases: insurance premiums, school/university tuition, utilities, telecoms, and subscriptions. (Source: [larepublica.co](https://www.larepublica.co/finanzas/bbva-y-druo-permitiran-debitos-automaticos-para-mas-de-tres-millones-de-cuentas-4511532), [portafolio.co](https://www.portafolio.co/tecnologia/bbva-y-druo-habilitan-debitos-automaticos-en-tiempo-real-para-mas-de-tres-millones-de-cuentas-en-colombia-505198), [latamfintech.co](https://www.latamfintech.co/articles/druo-y-bbva-se-unen-para-habilitar-debitos-automaticos-en-tiempo-real-para-mas-de-3-millones-de-cuentas-en-colombia).)

**[1] Who the players are.**
- **Druo** — Colombian paytech running a **direct-debit / account-to-account (A2A) payment network** that lets businesses debit or credit any bank account/wallet directly, bypassing card rails. Co-founded by Alejandro Pinzón (CEO) and Simón Pinilla. Operates in Colombia, Mexico, Peru and the US; says it connects ~10,000 financial institutions and serves nearly **2 million users** (per Pinilla's quote in the article; ~1M cited in the Oct-2025 seed release). Processed **$223M** in 2025 (+115% YoY). Raised an **undisclosed seed round in Oct 2025 led by Global PayTech Ventures** (whose founder Javier Pérez is ex-President of Mastercard Europe & LatAm), targeting European expansion in 2026. Internally: [[DRUO raises seed round for B2B payments]].
- **BBVA Colombia** — the Spanish bank's local unit, which has been an early mover on Bre-B rails (see [[BBVA Colombia launches first real-time payroll payments via Bre-B]]). Quote from Sergio Lizarazo, VP of Corporate & Government Banking, BBVA Colombia.
- **Bre-B** — Colombia's instant-payment system (central-bank-mandated, went live Oct 2025), the interoperability layer Druo wants to use to reach other banks' accounts. Internally: [[Colombia's Bre-B instant payment system launches]].

**[2] Why it matters.** This is the productization of **recurring A2A direct debit on top of Bre-B** — moving Colombian recurring billing off cards (and off clunky legacy PSE/direct-debit mandates) toward pre-authorized, real-time, card-free pulls from bank accounts. For billers (insurers, schools, telcos, utilities) it means instant confirmation of whether a charge succeeded, so they can reconcile and act without waiting for batch settlement cycles. For BBVA it extends its Bre-B product lead; for Druo it's a marquee distribution deal (3M accounts) and a template to resell to other banks.

**[3] Market context / competition.** This sits in LatAm's fast-growing A2A / open-finance push. Colombia's instant transfers grew **+152% in volume and +164% in value in 2025** (Banco de la República). Bre-B itself is also moving toward native recurring debits ("débitos recurrentes") without cards. Competitors/adjacent players: **Cobre** (B2B payments, real-time Bre-B interbank — [[Cobre to support real-time B2B payments in Colombia]]; ~$17B processed in 2025), **Yuno** (payments orchestration; Druo also has a Yuno alliance for direct bank collections), **dLocal** and **EBANX/MOVii** (Bre-B at checkout — [[dLocal enables instant transfers via Colombia's Bre-B]], [[EBANX brings Colombia's Bre-B instant payments to global firms]]). Globally the model mirrors GoCardless / TrueLayer / Volt (UK/EU open-banking direct debit). Card networks (Visa/Mastercard) are the incumbents being disintermediated on recurring billing.

**[4] What to watch next.** (a) Whether Druo actually lights up **other banks via Bre-B** — the whole "3M → multi-bank" thesis hinges on it, and today it's BBVA-only. (b) Economics vs cards: will billers migrate recurring charges to A2A for lower cost, and does Druo monetize per-transaction. (c) Fraud/authorization risk on pre-authorized pulls and consumer-protection/chargeback rules (A2A has weaker reversal rails than cards). (d) Whether Bre-B's own native recurring-debit standard competes with or subsumes Druo's layer. (e) Druo's broader regional/European expansion funded by the GPT seed.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. "Over 3 million accounts" — is this *enabled/eligible* BBVA Colombia accounts, or actual *users who have signed up* for automatic debit? The source frames it as accounts that "podrán programar" (will be *able to* schedule), i.e. addressable base, not adoption. Don't read it as live volume.
2. Is anything actually live today, or is this an announcement/launch of capability? The phrasing ("permitirán", future) and "podrán" suggest rollout, not steady-state usage.
3. "Extend to other banks via Bre-B" is stated as Druo's *vision/intention*, not a committed roadmap with dates. The multi-bank thesis is aspirational — flag it.
4. Is this genuinely new, or a rebrand of existing PSE/direct-debit/domiciliación that Colombian banks already offer? What is the concrete delta — real-time confirmation + card-free + merchant-channel linking?
5. User-base numbers conflict: article quotes Pinilla at "casi 2 millones de usuarios"; the Oct-2025 seed release said ~1M. Which is current? Possible growth or loose counting.
6. $223M processed in 2025 is small for a payments network — is Druo material, or still early-stage? Contextualize against Cobre (~$17B) and card volumes.
7. Who bears fraud/unauthorized-debit liability on pre-authorized A2A pulls? A2A lacks card-style chargebacks — is consumer protection addressed? Not covered in source.
8. Exclusivity: is BBVA an exclusive partner, or will Druo sign other banks in parallel (and will other banks build their own vs. buy Druo)? Unknown.
9. Does this depend on regulatory/open-finance rules (SFC open-finance decree) for account linking, or purely on Bre-B interoperability? Source doesn't specify the legal basis for cross-bank debits.
10. Revenue model unstated — does Druo charge per-debit, SaaS, or take spread? Can't assess economics or whether it undercuts cards.
11. Could Bre-B's own native recurring-debit standard (central-bank-driven, reportedly coming) commoditize Druo's layer, turning it from platform into a feature? Real disintermediation risk.
12. Is "24/7/365 real-time confirmation" a Druo capability or just a property of Bre-B rails that any participant gets? Differentiation may be thin.
13. Single primary source is a Colombian business outlet (press-release-driven); corroborated by Portafolio/Enter/LatamFintech but all appear to trace to the same BBVA/Druo announcement. No independent adoption data.
14. Geography check: this is **Colombia**, not Mexico or Spain — the prompt hedged "Mexico? Spain?". Confirmed Colombia (🇨🇴, BBVA Colombia, Bre-B, pesos, La República/Portafolio).
15. Does "link account from merchant channel" create a phishing/consent-hijack vector? Linking bank credentials via third-party merchant surfaces warrants scrutiny.

**Importance: 3/5** — A real, directionally significant step in LatAm's card-to-A2A shift and a marquee distribution win for a seed-stage paytech (Druo) on top of Bre-B, with a credible biller use case (insurance/tuition/utilities). But it's a single-bank capability announcement with no live adoption numbers, an aspirational multi-bank promise, a small current processing base, and real risk of being subsumed by Bre-B's own native recurring-debit standard. Notable for the Colombia/open-finance thesis; not a market-moving event on its own.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector: A2A / open-banking recurring debits, LatAm (Colombia).** BBVA Colombia and Bogotá-based fintech Druo have enabled account-to-account (A2A) automatic debits for 3M+ BBVA accounts: a user links/validates their BBVA account once from a merchant's digital channel, then recurring collections run 24/7 with real-time confirmation under the agreed terms. The target verticals are education (tuition), telecom, insurance and utilities — classic recurring-billing use cases. Druo's stated roadmap is to extend the capability to accounts at other banks via **Bre-B**, Colombia's interoperable instant-payments scheme operated by Banco de la República (live since Oct 2025; 500M+ transactions and 100M+ registered "keys/llaves" within ~five months, ~5M txns/day by May 2026). This positions A2A recurring debit as a card-rails alternative for billers: lower acceptance cost, instant settlement and no interchange, with authorization and real-time result that let merchants update systems without waiting for later processing cycles. The macro backdrop is strong: Banco de la República reported +152% growth in immediate transfers by volume and +164% by value during 2025.

**In-base comps.** The vault shows this is a global, multi-market structural shift toward A2A recurring payments, not a Colombia-only story: UK VRP / commercial VRP commentary via Yapily (News/2026-04), GoCardless launching "Recurring Pay by Bank" on UK Pay.UK rails (News/2026-06), EBANX expanding recurring alternative payments across six markets (News/2026-04), Pix Automático adding recurring payments in Brazil (News/2025-06), and Monnet Payments unifying LatAm payments (News/2026-02). Mastercard's open-banking-in-LatAm research (News/2025-09) frames Mexico/Brazil/Colombia as the leading real-time-payment + open-banking markets. Collectively these confirm recurring A2A/pay-by-bank is the direction of travel across UK, Brazil and now Colombia.

**Positioning & scale.** Druo (seed-stage; ~$2M seed led by Global PayTech Ventures, Oct 2025; BBVA Spark-backed) supplies the B2B pay-by-bank/collections infrastructure; BBVA provides the issuing base (3M+ accounts) and distribution. Druo says hundreds of regional firms already use its rails to automate collections for ~2M users directly from bank accounts, with expansion planned to Peru, Mexico and the US. Nearest functional comparables are GoCardless and Trustly (bank-debit/pay-by-bank collections), with card networks (Visa/Mastercard) and card-based recurring billing as the incumbent being disintermediated.

**Risk flags:**
- **Interoperability dependence / single-bank at launch.** The live benefit covers only BBVA's own 3M+ accounts; the multi-bank promise hinges on Bre-B cross-institution debit mandates being standardized and widely adopted — execution and regulatory timing risk.
- **Consumer-authorization & mandate friction.** Recurring A2A debit requires upfront user authorization and clear revocation/dispute handling; without card-style chargeback protections, consumer-protection and complaint-handling gaps could slow adoption or draw regulator (SFC) scrutiny.
- **Incumbent/card pushback & economics.** A2A recurring debit directly cannibalizes card interchange on recurring billing; card networks and issuers may defend economics, and biller adoption depends on failed-payment (insufficient-funds) retry performance vs. cards.
- **Execution risk at a seed-stage vendor.** Druo is early-stage and thinly funded (~$2M seed); concentration on a marquee bank partner and an ambitious multi-country roadmap raises delivery, reliability and balance-sheet risk.
- **Source/figure caution.** Single Spanish-language source (La República); Druo's "~2M users" reach is self-reported and some external profiles cite lower figures (~1M) — treat scale claims as unverified.
