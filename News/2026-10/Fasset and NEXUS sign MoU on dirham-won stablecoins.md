NEXUS signs strategic MoU with global stablecoin finance platform Fasset
Joint proof-of-concept for payment infrastructure integration and stablecoin settlement
  - Cooperation under review on AED- and KRW-linked stablecoin infrastructure 
 SEOUL — NEXUS CEO Henry Chang signed a strategic memorandum of understanding with Fasset FZE, a Dubai-based digital finance company, at Korea Blockchain Week (KBW 2026) on September 30, covering cooperation on stablecoin-based financial infrastructure.
Under the agreement, the two companies will review cooperation across payment infrastructure integration, stablecoin settlement, and co-branded digital financial products.
In payments, they will pursue a joint proof-of-concept (PoC) connecting NEXUS's payment infrastructure with Fasset's fiat–digital asset conversion infrastructure. In stablecoins, they will form a joint task force, meeting quarterly, to define the direction of cooperation on AED-linked and KRW-linked stablecoin infrastructure.
Fasset is an AI-powered, stablecoin-based digital finance platform headquartered in Dubai. It obtained a VASP license from Dubai's Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (VARA) in 2023 and provides digital asset services under it.
In August, Fasset raised a $68 million Series C led by SBI Group at a $1 billion valuation. It supports more than 3 million wallets and over 1,000 enterprise clients across 125 countries, operating payment and settlement infrastructure that connects multiple financial rails.
NEXUS is building infrastructure connecting distribution, payment, and settlement for games and digital content through the ONE ecosystem. In June, it brought ONE Store in as a subsidiary, securing a content distribution base, and is extending its fiat-centered payment infrastructure into stablecoins.
Mohammad Raafi Hossain, CEO of Fasset, said: "This partnership is about bringing any-to-any banking closer to reality: connecting payments, settlement and different financial rails so value can move more seamlessly across markets. Stablecoins are an important part of that shift because they give us a more flexible way to connect currencies, assets and networks around real economic activity.”
Henry, CEO of NEXUS, said: "The competitiveness of a stablecoin comes from actual use cases and distribution, not from issuance itself. This partnership connects Fasset's global payment and settlement infrastructure to the ONE ecosystem. Together we will broaden the payment foundation for global expansion."
 About Fasset 
 Fasset is an AI-powered stablecoin neobanking platform building a global financial system around ownership. Its financial account enables individuals, businesses, and institutions to receive, hold, move, spend, and invest across currencies, markets, and asset classes from one place. 
Founded in 2019 by Mohammad Raafi Hossain and Daniel Ahmed, Fasset has raised more than US$150 million in funding, following a US$68 million Series C raise that brought the company to Unicorn status. Fasset serves customers across more than 125 countries and operates through regulated entities and partnerships across the GCC, Asia, Europe, and other international markets. 
Fasset is powered by Own Network , its regulated financial infrastructure connecting banking, remittance, liquidity, custody, and settlement across markets. The company is building toward a financial system where access to stable money, global markets, and ownership is no longer determined by geography. 
 About NEXUS 
NEXUS Co., Ltd. is a KOSDAQ-listed blockchain infrastructure company connecting distribution, payment and settlement for games and digital content. Through the ONE ecosystem, NEXUS operates ONE Chain, fiat and stablecoin payment infrastructure, and in-game commerce services. In June 2026, NEXUS brought ONE Store, Korea's leading independent app market with 38M registered user number, in as a subsidiary, securing a content distribution base.

# Context-enrichment: Fasset and NEXUS sign MoU on dirham-won stablecoins
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5. Freshness: fresh._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- **Non-binding MoU**, signed **30 Sept 2026** at **Korea Blockchain Week (KBW 2026), Seoul**, between **Fasset FZE** (Dubai stablecoin neobank; CEO Mohammad Raafi Hossain) and **NEXUS Co., Ltd.** (KOSDAQ-listed Korean blockchain/gaming-infra firm; CEO Henry Chang). Source confirms "memorandum of understanding", i.e. intent, not a contract.
- **Scope = EXPLORE, not build.** Three areas "under review": (a) payment-infra integration, (b) stablecoin settlement, (c) co-branded digital financial products.
- **Only two concrete commitments:** (1) a **joint proof-of-concept** connecting NEXUS's payment rails to Fasset's fiat↔digital-asset conversion infra; (2) a **joint task force meeting quarterly** to "define the direction" of cooperation on **AED-linked and KRW-linked** stablecoin infrastructure.
- **Numbers/timelines/go-live: NONE.** No capital commitment, no volume target, no deployment date. The only firm cadence is "quarterly meetings." Everything else is "will review / will pursue."
- **Why structured this way (analysis):** An MoU with a quarterly task force is the minimum-commitment vehicle precisely because the two target assets **barely exist**. KRW stablecoin issuance is legally blocked in Korea (Digital Asset Basic Act Phase 2 delayed ~1 year amid a BOK-vs-FSC turf fight over banks-only vs fintech issuance); AED stablecoins exist but Fasset is a lister/converter, not an issuer. So the MoU anchors to a **future** both sides are betting on, not a product either can ship today → the real content is distribution-narrative, not plumbing.
- **Why the PR frames it as "dirham-won":** It dresses a KOSDAQ gaming-infra firm's stablecoin pivot (and Fasset's Korea market entry) in a cross-border-corridor story that sounds like FX rails, when the firm commitment is a games/digital-content PoC.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **AED stablecoin rails:** **AE Coin** (AED Stablecoin LLC / Al Maryah Community Bank) — first fully-regulated AED stablecoin, full CBUAE license ~Dec 2024. **Tether dirham stablecoin** with ADX-listed **Phoenix Group** + Green Acorn (CEO projected $12–24B liquidity in 2 yrs — promotional). Fasset claims to be first to **list DDSC**, a CBUAE-approved dirham stablecoin — so Fasset's edge is as AED on/off-ramp, not issuer.
- **KRW stablecoin:** no bank-issued won stablecoin live. Closest: **KRW1** by BDACS, launched Sept 2025 on Avalanche with Woori Bank escrow — PoC scale. Banks **Hana/Shinhan/Woori** and platforms **Kakao/Naver/Toss** are racing but gated by legislation.
- **Cross-border GCC stablecoin-settlement peers in corpus:** [[Bahrain's ARP partners with stc Pay on GCC digital-asset settlement]] (settlement/conversion partner model, same shape), [[Bahrain's ARP partners with stc Pay on GCC digital-asset settlement|ARP/stc Pay]]; broader local-stablecoin corridor theme in the Oct-2025 corpus.
- **Position (analysis):** Fasset is **ahead on AED on-ramp regulatory standing** (VARA VASP since 2023, lists a CBUAE-approved dirham token) but on the KRW side both parties are **behind the law itself** — they cannot build a won stablecoin until Korea permits it. Parity-to-laggard on the thing the headline sells.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Fasset** trajectory: founded 2019 (Mohammad Raafi Hossain & Daniel Ahmed); VARA VASP license 2023; Bahrain expansion with tokenised US stocks (2025, [[Fasset expands to Bahrain with tokenised US stocks and crypto]]); conditional **Labuan (Malaysia) Islamic digital-bank sandbox** approval Oct 2025 ([[Fasset secures provisional license for stablecoin Islamic bank in Malaysia]], [[Dubai's Fasset approved for Islamic stablecoin bank in Labuan]]) — **sandbox, not a full bank license**; appoints Rafiza Ghazali as Digital Banking CEO ([[Fasset appoints Rafiza Ghazali as Digital Banking CEO]]); **gold-backed Visa card with Tether/XAUt** ([[Tether and Fasset launch gold-backed Visa card with XAUt cashback]], [[Tether and Fasset launch first gold-backed Visa card globally]]).
- **Funding reconciliation (flag):** the note body conflates figures. External sources show **two distinct 2026 rounds**: **Series B $51M (May 2026)** led by SBI/Investcorp/Arz Portföy ([[Fasset raises $51 million Series B led by Investcorp]], [[Stablecoin neobank Fasset raises $51 million for emerging markets]]) **and** a **$68M Series C led by SBI (Aug 2026) at a $1B valuation** (unicorn). Lifetime >$150M. So "raised $68M Series C" and ">$150M total" in the note are both plausible — but the May $51M Series B is a separate earlier round, not the same money.
- **Why Fasset acts this way (analysis):** It is building an "any-to-any" stablecoin neobank across emerging-market corridors (50+ corridors, self-reported 3M wallets, 1,000+ enterprise clients, 125 countries). **SBI's lead** in both recent rounds is the key: SBI is a Japan/Asia distribution machine, and a Korean tie-up extends that Asia push. Korea entry via a listed local partner is the cheapest regulated beachhead.
- **Why NEXUS acts this way (analysis):** NEXUS (ex-CROSS blockchain gaming, rebranding mainnet CROSS→ONE Chain) bought **ONE Store** (Korea app market, ~38M users) June 2026 for ~$41M; its **shares fell ~29%** on that announcement. A stablecoin narrative + a Gulf partner is a credibility/distribution story layered onto a loss-making app-market acquisition — it needs payment monetisation of those 38M users.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty:** modest. "Explore AED-KRW stablecoins" is a **first-of-its-kind pairing** on paper, but it is an MoU, not a product. No AED-KRW corridor stablecoin exists; neither side can issue a KRW stablecoin yet.
- **Traction:** **zero** on this deal — no PoC result, no users, no volume. Fasset's own metrics (3M wallets, ~$40B annualised) are self-reported and predate this MoU; its Malaysia "500,000 users" figure (Oct 2025) vs "3M wallets" (2026) shows how fast the self-reported denominator moves → treat with skepticism.
- **Who captures the margin (analysis):** In a real AED-KRW flow, the issuer of each leg (AE Coin/Tether on AED; a future bank consortium on KRW) and the settlement network capture economics. Fasset here is the **conversion/on-ramp layer** and NEXUS the **distribution layer** — both are rail-adjacent, not the mint. That is a thinner, more commoditisable slice than "issuing the stablecoin," which is the recurring risk for neobank-on-stablecoin plays.
- **Value-add verdict:** real **optionality** (SBI-backed unicorn + a listed Korean distributor + a genuine CEPA trade corridor), but **unconfirmed value** as signed.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Next:** quarterly task-force; a payments PoC targeting Korea's games/digital-content economy (ONE Store's 38M users). Contingent on Korea's **Digital Asset Basic Act** Phase-2 (KRW stablecoin legality) — delayed ~1 year, BOK favouring banks-only issuance.
- **Backdrop that gives it rationale:** **UAE-Korea CEPA in force 1 May 2026** (first GCC pact for Korea; ~$6.9B non-oil trade 2025), and an existing **won-dirham currency swap line (~$5.4B)** to settle bilateral trade outside USD. So a **B2B trade-settlement** case is plausible even though **retail AED↔KRW remittance is negligible** (Korea is not a UAE remittance-receiving market).
- **Risks:** (1) Korean regulatory delay kills the KRW leg; (2) MoU never converts to a binding deal (common for KBW announcements); (3) NEXUS's weak standalone fundamentals (loss-making ONE Store, share drop) make it a fragile partner; (4) Fasset is thin-slice conversion layer, not issuer.
- **Second-order (analysis):** The deal's weight comes less from the MoU and more from **SBI's two-round backing of Fasset into unicorn status** — that is the durable signal; the NEXUS MoU is a cheap Korea-entry call option on an asset class Korea hasn't legalised.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Stablecoins as cross-border settlement rails. Size/growth: total stablecoin supply ~$308.0bn as of 2026-08-13, +14.3% YoY from ~$269.4bn (per Allium/reap.global); more telling for Fasset's use case, cross-border stablecoin transfers rose +77.5% YoY to ~$220.3bn — payment usage is outpacing pure supply growth. B2B cross-border stablecoin payments are cited at ~$13.4bn in 2026 with a $5tn-by-2035 projection (per axis-intelligence, secondary — treat the long-dated number as a vendor projection, not a fact). Structure: highly concentrated at the token layer — USDT ~59% + USDC ~23% ≈ 82% of supply (per Allium, 2026-09) — but fragmented and land-grabbing at the local-currency / corridor layer, which is exactly where AED- and KRW-pegged tokens and a connector like Fasset sit. Entry barriers are regulatory (VASP/VARA, EMI, Islamic-finance licenses) and distribution/liquidity, not technology. Why now: UAE (CBUAE approved RAKBANK AED stablecoin in-principle 2026-01-07; registered USDU as a Foreign Payment Token 2026-01-29; Tether announced an AED token with Phoenix Group/Green Acorn) and Korea (8-bank won consortium; Digital Asset Basic Act phase-2 for a won stablecoin delayed ~1yr amid BoK/FSC disagreement, BoK insisting on bank-led issuance) are both opening local-currency stablecoin regimes simultaneously — the MoU is an attempt to pre-position a dirham↔won corridor before either token exists at scale.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: annualized settlement/TPV, number of live corridors, take rate per transaction (undisclosed here → `[UNSOURCED]`), and for issuers, tokens outstanding / float. Fasset's own disclosed metrics (per the PR and Series C coverage): >$40bn annualized volume (note text says ">$32bn" in the May round and the PR's own "payment and settlement infrastructure" line — the newer Series C figure is ~$40bn, so volume is growing but the two numbers should not be summed), 3M+ wallets, 1,000+ enterprise clients, 125 countries, 50+ corridors. Basis of competition in this layer is distribution + regulatory coverage, not issuance — a point NEXUS's CEO makes explicitly ("competitiveness comes from actual use cases and distribution, not from issuance itself"). Key players in cross-border stablecoin infrastructure: Bridge (acquired by Stripe for ~$1.1bn, 2025; ~$10bn+ annualized), [[BVNK surpasses $20bn in processing volume|BVNK]] (Mastercard agreed to acquire for up to ~$1.8bn, 2026-03), Conduit (Africa/LatAm corridors). Regional/token peers: [[AE Coin brings Nephos Group onboard for stablecoin acceptance|AE Coin]] and Tether's planned AED token on the dirham side; [[Bank of Korea urges banks to lead won stablecoin issuance|the Korean bank consortium]], K Bank and BDACS KRW1 on the won side. Fasset's position: niche/catching-up as a connector, not an incumbent rail — ahead specifically on Islamic-finance + emerging-market corridor coverage and a VARA license (2023), which is a genuine moat (regulatory + distribution across GCC/Asia). NEXUS brings Korean distribution (KOSDAQ-listed, ONE Store ~38M users). `(analysis)`

**Comps & multiples.** Internal comps (prior Fasset financing): [[Stablecoin neobank Fasset raises $51 million for emerging markets|Series B $51M, May 2026]] / [[Fasset raises $51 million Series B led by Investcorp|same round, Investcorp/SBI/Arz Portföy]]; the current Series C is $68M at a $1.0bn post-money (SBI-led, announced 2026-08-24, per Bloomingbit/crypto.news/fasset.com), taking 2026 raises to ~$119M and unicorn status. Multiple arithmetic: EV/Revenue cannot be computed — Fasset does not disclose revenue → no data. A rough EV/volume sanity check: $1.0bn valuation / ~$40bn annualized settlement volume = ~0.025x of flow (valuation-to-flow, NOT a revenue multiple; useful only as a cross-peer scale check). Peer flow checks from the base: [[BVNK surpasses $20bn in processing volume|BVNK]] ~$1.8bn / >$20bn volume ≈ 0.09x; Bridge ~$1.1bn / ~$10bn ≈ 0.11x — both richer per unit of flow than Fasset, consistent with Fasset being a 1:1-valuation unicorn on much larger (but likely lower-take-rate, cross-currency) flow. Distribution not computed (only 2 external flow comps, different take-rate profiles) → qualitative: Fasset looks in-line-to-cheap on flow but the comparison is weak because take rates and revenue are undisclosed `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Risk flags.**
1. MoU-only, no economics. This is a non-binding memorandum: a quarterly task force and a joint PoC, with no AED or KRW token live, no volume commitment, no fee split disclosed. De-PR: "review cooperation" ≠ live corridor. Second-order: easily shelved if either token's regulation slips (Korea's won-stablecoin act is already delayed ~1yr).
2. Regulatory gating on both legs. A dirham↔won corridor needs an issued, approved AED stablecoin AND an approved KRW stablecoin. BoK is pushing to restrict won issuance to banks — Fasset/NEXUS are not banks, so they may end up as a distribution/connector layer dependent on bank-issued tokens rather than principals, compressing the economics they can capture.
3. Disintermediation / thin layer. Value in cross-border stablecoin flow is being captured by issuers (USDT/USDC ~82% of supply) and by acquired rails (Bridge→Stripe, BVNK→Mastercard). A connector sitting between fiat on/off-ramps and someone else's tokens risks margin squeeze from both the issuer above and bank-issued local tokens below.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Read this as a land-grab option on local-currency corridor infrastructure, not a re-rating event — Fasset is buying optionality on the dirham↔won lane ahead of either token existing. Falsifiable thesis: the MoU converts to a live, revenue-bearing corridor within 12 months. Trigger/catalyst: a shipped PoC with disclosed volume, plus at least one of (a) a live AED stablecoin Fasset actually settles in, (b) a won token (bank- or BDACS-issued) it plugs into. What breaks the thesis: Korea finalizes bank-only won issuance that cuts non-bank connectors out of the economics, or the task force produces nothing but quarterly minutes through 2027.
