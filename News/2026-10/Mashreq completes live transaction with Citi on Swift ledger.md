Ant International pilots HSBC tokenised deposits in the Middle East
Claudia Pincovski
06 Oct 2026 / 5 Min Read
Ant International has extended its work with HSBC to support real-time treasury management in the Middle East, using HSBC's global Tokenised Deposit Service (TDS). The collaboration makes Ant International HSBC's first client in the Middle East to use the service, according to the announcement.
Pilot transactions across two corridors
As part of the collaboration, Ant International has completed pilot transactions across two payment corridors connecting the UAE and Hong Kong. The first covered real-time, AED-denominated tokenised deposit transfers within the UAE. The second involved USD-denominated tokenised deposit transfers initiated from the UAE to international markets, including Hong Kong and Singapore.
The transactions were initiated through WhaleRTP, Ant International's proprietary blockchain-based treasury management platform, and executed in real time through HSBC's TDS. The two companies jointly designed the transaction workflow.
Tokenised treasury infrastructure
Ant International has used blockchain and tokenised deposit technologies since 2019 to support real-time treasury management across financial centres including Hong Kong, Singapore, Europe and the US. WhaleRTP is currently supported by more than 20 global banks, HSBC among them, and enables real-time transfers in more than 17 currencies, including USD, HKD and AED. The Middle East pilots add a new region to this network.
HSBC's TDS is live in six markets: Hong Kong, Singapore, Luxembourg, the UK, the US and the UAE. It supports multiple currencies, including CNH, HKD, SGD, EUR, GBP, USD and AED. The service gives clients instant, 24/7 access to liquidity across HSBC's network, while allowing them to continue relying on the bank's existing banking infrastructure and oversight.
According to an HSBC official, the interoperability between tokenised deposit networks demonstrates how the bank can connect different infrastructures for its clients. For corporates, the official said, this addresses practical challenges such as moving liquidity across markets and financial institutions, as well as improving cash visibility.
Regional treasury plans
Ant International stated that the expansion into the Middle East will support its regional growth and help its businesses, including Alipay+, Antom and WorldFirst, serve local enterprises and travellers. The pilots therefore link treasury infrastructure to the company's merchant payments, cross-border and travel-related services in the region.
A company official described the pilots as a milestone towards achieving real-time treasury management globally. With the support of regional bank partners such as HSBC, Ant International aims to establish a global treasury hub in the Middle East in the near future to support its global operations. The company also stated its intention to contribute to the development of the region's digital finance ecosystem.
For HSBC, the pilots extend its TDS client base into the Middle East and test the service's interoperability with a client's own blockchain-based treasury platform. For Ant International, the UAE corridors connect its regional activity with existing treasury operations in Hong Kong and Singapore, using USD and AED tokenised deposits settled in real time.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened.** On ~30 Sep 2026 (announced early Oct 2026) Mashreq and Citi completed a *live* cross-border payment using bank-issued tokenised deposits settled on **Swift's blockchain-based shared ledger**. The ledger acts as a neutral orchestration layer that links each bank's own tokenised deposits, letting value move 24/7 across institutions/time zones before final settlement runs through existing rails — explicitly preserving today's compliance, credit, risk and control standards. Sources: [fintechnews.ae](https://fintechnews.ae/33793/payments/mashreq-citi-swift-tokenised-deposits/), [thepaypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/mashreq-and-citi-complete-a-tokenised-deposit-payment-on-swift-ledger), [Mashreq/Zawya press release](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/mashreq-collaborates-with-citi-and-swift-to-advance-always-on-cross-border-payments-using-swifts-blockchain-infrastructure-1458253).

**What Swift's shared ledger (SSL) is.** Announced ready for use in **July 2026**; Swift went from concept to activation in ~9 months. It is a shared ledger that orchestrates *bank-issued tokenised deposits* (not a CBDC, not a stablecoin, not a Swift coin). **17 pilot banks** across six continents (ANZ, BNP Paribas, BNY, Citi, DBS, First Abu Dhabi Bank, FirstRand, HSBC, Itaú, Lloyds, **Mashreq**, OCBC, Standard Chartered, UBS, UOB, Wells Fargo, MUFG); 40+ institutions contributed to the design. Sources: [Swift press release](https://www.swift.com/news-events/press-releases/swifts-blockchain-ledger-ready-use-17-banks-set-pioneer-tokenised-cross-border-payments-trusted-global-infrastructure), [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2026-07-09-swift-launches-blockchain-ledger-for-tokenized-deposit-pilot-with-17-banks-407687).

**Why it matters.** This is the *first named live transaction* on the SSL to surface in our coverage — moving the Swift ledger from "ready/pilot" (Jul 2026) to a demonstrated bilateral settlement. Mashreq gets a MEA/UAE flag on frontier Swift infra; Citi extends a now-long blockchain-settlement track record. Strategically it is the **incumbent-rails answer to stablecoins and CBDCs**: tokenised deposits stay regulated bank liabilities inside existing frameworks, and Swift keeps itself central rather than being disintermediated by bank-owned networks.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is this an actual *live* value-moving transaction, or a "demonstration"/test run? Press language says "live" but no settlement finality/legal-tender transfer detail is given.
2. Exact date and corridor? Sources disagree: Zawya/INTLBM imply ~30 Sep 2026; fintechnews.ae says "October 2026." No currency pair or amount disclosed — thin on specifics.
3. Who initiated and in which direction (UAE→US? AED/USD?)? Not stated. The note's pasted body is about Ant/HSBC AED-USD corridors — do NOT attribute those to Mashreq–Citi.
4. Is the in-note primary source (thepaypers Ant/HSBC article) actually about this story at all? No — it is cross-wired contamination. The real Mashreq–Citi coverage is the fintechnews.ae link.
5. How is this different from the Jul 2026 "ledger ready" news — is one live transaction materially new, or incremental pilot noise?
6. Is Swift's SSL a durable neutral layer, or will bank-owned networks (JPM/BofA/Citi "bridge", Citi Token Services) bypass it? Citi is in BOTH — hedging, not committing.
7. Tokenised deposits vs stablecoins vs CBDC: does "stays a regulated bank liability" actually deliver 24/7 cross-border, or just re-wrap existing correspondent banking with blockchain veneer?
8. Settlement still "completes through existing systems" — so is the 24/7 benefit real, or deferred until legacy rails catch up (weekend/overnight fund availability vs final settlement gap)?
9. Regulatory status of tokenised deposits in the UAE/US for this transfer — any CBUAE/OCC approval, or sandbox-only?
10. Counterparty/credit risk during the 24/7 window before final settlement — who bears it?
11. Is Mashreq a lead or a minor pilot participant riding a Citi+Swift demo for PR? Likely the latter.
12. Scalability: 17 banks is a pilot; what volume/throughput has the SSL actually handled? Unknown.
13. Does this cannibalise or complement Citi Token Services and the US tokenised-deposit network — or signal fragmentation of standards?
14. Any independent confirmation beyond bank/Swift press releases? Coverage is largely press-release-derived.

**Importance: 3/5** — First named live transaction on Swift's shared ledger and a clean MEA data point, meaningfully advancing the tokenised-deposit-vs-stablecoin narrative; but it is a single undisclosed-value pilot transfer, one of 17 banks, with settlement still on legacy rails and heavy press-release sourcing — infrastructure signal, not a market-moving event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Cross-border correspondent-banking settlement modernization. Size: Swift carries ~$150tn/yr in cross-border value and >46mn messages/day (per Bank of England / Swift, via PYMNTS & Forbes); BoE projected cross-border flows rising from ~$150tn (2017) to >$250tn by 2027. Structure: highly concentrated at the infrastructure layer (Swift is the incumbent messaging monopoly linking ~11k institutions) but fragmented and slow at execution — correspondent chains, nostro/vostro pre-funding, cut-off windows, no 24/7 settlement. Barriers: network effects + regulatory trust + compliance tooling (sanctions/AML embedded in Swift messaging). "Why now": the stablecoin/tokenization threat is pulling value toward new rails (crypto B2B settlement est. ~$30bn/day per bex.co, 2026-03 `[UNSOURCED — vendor blog]`), and Swift's defensive answer is a shared blockchain ledger (built by Consensys on Hyperledger Besu, permissioned EVM, Chainlink CCIP interop) that went live 2026-07-09 with 17 pilot banks (per theblock.co, Swift press release, 2026-07). The Mashreq/Citi leg is a live tokenized-deposit transaction on that MVP, executed ~30 Sep 2026 (per thepaypers.com, fintechnews.ae, 2026-10).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: settlement speed / availability (24/7 vs business-hours), liquidity efficiency (pre-funded nostro balances freed), and messages/value throughput; none are disclosed for this pilot → `[UNSOURCED]`. This is a 3-layer race for cross-border settlement rails:
- **Swift shared ledger** (orchestration of bank-issued tokenized deposits; preserves ISO 20022, compliance, final settlement via existing systems) — 17 banks incl. Citi, HSBC, BNY, Wells Fargo, Standard Chartered, UBS, DBS, MUFG, First Abu Dhabi Bank, **Mashreq**, Itaú (per theblock.co/Swift, 2026-07). Related: [[Swift to develop blockchain with 30-plus banks for real-time payments]] (2025-10), [[SWIFT to integrate blockchain-based ledger for cross-border payments]].
- **Single-bank tokenized-deposit networks** — JPMorgan **Kinexys** (>$1.5tn cumulative since 2020; daily volume scaled from ~$2bn to >$5–7bn per JPM 2026 milestones) is far ahead on live volume: [[Why JPMorgan built Kinexys for institutional blockchain settlements]], [[Qatar National Bank taps JPMorgan Kinexys for dollar settlements]], [[National Bank of Bahrain adopts JPMorgan's Kinexys]]. HSBC **TDS** live in 6 markets incl. UAE (same note's Ant/HSBC leg): [[Ant International and HSBC expand tokenized treasury to MEA]]. US consortium tokenized-deposit network via The Clearing House targeted 1H2027: [[Top US banks plan tokenized deposit network for 2027]], [[US banks build collaborative tokenized deposit network]].
- **Neutral/utility settlement** — [[Fnality raises $136M for blockchain bank settlement]] (wholesale central-bank-backed), [[Chainlink and banks launch Project Pangea for T+0 FX settlement]], [[Emirates NBD launches blockchain cross-border payments on Partior]].
Basis of competition: network/distribution (who has the most connected banks), not price. Protagonist position: **Mashreq is a participant/early adopter, not the platform owner** — the moat here belongs to Swift (network effects) and to Citi (correspondent reach); Mashreq's edge is MENA distribution and being among the first UAE banks across multiple rails (Swift ledger + it also appears in Partior/ENBD-era MENA blockchain activity) `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/revenue attached to this pick — it is an infrastructure pilot, not a deal. Trading-comp multiples: **no data** (Swift is a bank-owned cooperative, not listed; Citi/Mashreq consolidated bank P/E not meaningful to this product line). Internal reference points for scale of rival rails: JPMorgan Kinexys >$1.5tn cumulative, >$5–7bn/day (per JPM, 2026) vs Fnality's $136M raise (2025-09) as the funding benchmark for a neutral settlement utility. Distribution not computed — qualitative comparison only. Takeaway: on live throughput, bilateral bank networks (Kinexys) dwarf the brand-new Swift MVP; Swift's bet is breadth of membership, not head-start volume `(analysis)`.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Pilot ≠ production / unsourced economics.** This is an MVP leg (one transaction, ~Sep 2026); no settled volume, fee, or liquidity-saving figure disclosed. "Live" is being marketed as traction — de-PR: a single live transfer is a demo, not adoption. Second-order: re-rating risk if the MVP stalls between pilot and GA.
2. **Disintermediation / Mashreq is a taker of someone else's rails.** Mashreq owns neither the ledger (Swift/Consensys) nor the dominant deposit-token network (JPM/HSBC). If settlement value migrates to whoever controls the token standard and interop (Chainlink CCIP, Canton, The Clearing House consortium), participant banks capture thin orchestration margin while the rail owner captures the economics.
3. **Standards-war / fragmentation.** Competing tokenized-deposit stacks (Swift shared ledger vs Kinexys vs US TCH consortium vs Partior vs Fnality vs wholesale CBDC) risk interop fragmentation; a bank betting on the "wrong" rail faces switching costs and stranded integration. Regulatory/compliance equivalence across jurisdictions (UAE/US/EU) for tokenized deposits is unresolved — execution + regulatory risk.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Signals Swift is actively defending the correspondent-banking franchise by co-opting tokenized deposits onto its own neutral ledger rather than ceding to stablecoins/Kinexys — potential consolidation of fragmented bank blockchain pilots onto one membership network. Falsifiable thesis: if Swift's ledger moves from MVP to GA with disclosed live corridor volumes and ≥25 banks by end-2027, it becomes the default interop layer and Mashreq's early-mover MENA positioning pays off; trigger to watch = Swift publishing production (not pilot) settlement metrics and a go-live date. Thesis breaks if pilots quietly lapse or banks default to their own bilateral rails (Kinexys-style), leaving the shared ledger a standards showcase.
