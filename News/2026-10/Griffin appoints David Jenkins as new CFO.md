Griffin, the UK-based banking-as-a-service provider, has appointed David Jenkins as Chief Financial Officer. For the fintech sector, this hire signals a transition from early-stage infrastructure building to a mature phase of scaling and capital management. Jenkins brings the regulatory and financial experience required for a bank to support high-growth platforms at scale.

Griffin has named David Jenkins as its new Chief Financial Officer to lead the bank's finance function, including financial strategy, treasury, capital, liquidity, and financial planning. Jenkins arrives with nearly 30 years of experience in financial services. Most recently, he served as CFO and executive director at Vemi Money, where he established financial operations and risk management frameworks covering hiring, technology, and governance.

His background includes a tenure as CFO of Recognise Bank and the AIM-listed City of London Group, where he successfully led capital raises exceeding £40 million. Jenkins also played a key role at Aldermore for six years, co-managing its IPO and FTSE 250 listing in 2015 while building the capital allocation and reporting frameworks that supported the bank's expansion. Earlier in his career, he held senior finance roles at Lloyds Banking Group, Prudential UK, ABN AMRO, and Credit Suisse, having started in audit at Deloitte and PwC.

At Griffin, Jenkins will work alongside CEO and co-founder David Jarvis and the executive team to strengthen the institution's financial foundations. The appointment is intended to provide the financial rigour necessary for a regulated bank that operates with the speed of a technology firm as it enters its next chapter of growth.

> "Griffin has built something rare: a bank that fintechs and platforms can build on, bringing the discipline and strength of a regulated institution with the speed of a technology company. I’ve spent my career helping banks at different stages of growth and I’m excited to bring that experience to a team with this much ambition."

David Jenkins, Chief Financial Officer, Griffin

Griffin is a UK-based bank specifically designed for fintechs and platforms. It operates as a "bank-in-a-box," providing the core banking infrastructure and regulatory umbrella that allows other companies to offer financial products without having to secure their own full banking licenses. By combining a full UK banking license with a modern API-first technology stack, Griffin positions itself as a critical layer of the UK's financial services ecosystem.

The firm focuses on providing integrated services such as accounts, payments, and safeguarding, aimed at reducing the friction usually associated with traditional banking partnerships. Unlike legacy institutions, Griffin’s platform is built to handle the high-velocity demands of modern software companies while maintaining the strict compliance and capital requirements of a regulated bank. With 28 previous mentions in FF News coverage, the company has established itself as a prominent player in the banking-as-a-service (BaaS) and embedded finance markets, competing by offering a more direct, vertically integrated alternative to middleware providers.

FF News has tracked Griffin’s expanding influence through several key partnerships and integrations. Recently, the bank played a central role in Countingup and Griffin Power MoneySuperMarket’s Strategic Entry into Business Banking, demonstrating its ability to support major consumer brands. This followed the news that Countingup Partners with Griffin to Launch Integrated Business Bank Accounts for UK SMEs, a move that brought streamlined banking to small businesses. Griffin’s infrastructure has also been utilized by specialized fintechs, as seen when Saible Raises £2.9m to Combat Late Payments in UK Construction Supply Chains. Additionally, Griffin’s presence in the SME sector was highlighted in coverage of how ANNA Money Unveils Product Expansion as SMEs Face Rising Tax and Admin Pressures.

The appointment of a CFO with IPO and large-scale capital-raising experience suggests that Griffin is moving beyond the "proof of concept" stage. In the current fintech climate, where regulators are scrutinizing banking-as-a-service (BaaS) models more closely, having a veteran from traditional and challenger banking like Jenkins is a strategic move. It provides the institutional gravity needed to reassure both regulators and enterprise-level partners. The industry is seeing a shift where technical agility is no longer enough for BaaS providers; survival now depends on sophisticated capital allocation and treasury management. This hire puts pressure on other infrastructure players to prove they have similar executive depth to manage the complexities of a full banking license.

Original Press Release

Griffin, the UK bank for fintechs and platforms, has appointed David Jenkins as Chief Financial Officer. David will lead Griffin’s finance function as the bank enters its next phase of growth, with responsibility for financial strategy, treasury, capital and liquidity, and financial planning.

David brings nearly three decades of experience across banking and financial services. He joins from Vemi Money, where as CFO and executive director he built their financial operations and risk management function, covering hiring, technology, treasury strategy, and governance. Before that, he served as CFO of Recognise Bank and AIM-listed City of London Group, where he built a function spanning treasury, regulatory reporting, financial control, and FP&A, and led capital raises of more than £40 million.

Earlier in his career, David spent six years at Aldermore, where he co-managed the bank’s IPO and FTSE 250 listing in 2015 and built the capital allocation, planning, and reporting framework that supported its rapid growth. He has also held senior finance roles at Lloyds Banking Group, Prudential UK, ABN AMRO, and Credit Suisse, and began his career in audit at Deloitte and PwC.

I’ve spent my career helping banks at different stages of growth and I’m excited to bring that experience to a team with this much ambition." - **David Jenkins, Chief Financial Officer, Griffin**

At Griffin, David will work closely with CEO and co-founder David Jarvis and the executive team to scale the bank and strengthen its financial foundations.

"David has helped banks grow, raise capital, and scale. He combines financial rigour with a commercial mindset. As Griffin enters its next chapter, we need a CFO who can be a growth partner to the whole business. We’re delighted to have him here." - **David Jarvis, CEO and co-founder, Griffin**

## [1] Why it matters

A CFO hire is a mid-importance signal on its own, but the profile is the message. Griffin deliberately recruited a CFO whose track record is **bank-scale capital raising and a public listing** (Aldermore IPO, £40m+ raises at City of London Group). For a regulated bank that must hold its own capital and liquidity (unlike middleware BaaS resellers), this reads as prep for the next phase: a larger fundraise and/or balance-sheet scaling, and institutional credibility with the PRA/FCA and enterprise partners. The press framing ("next chapter of growth", "growth partner to the whole business") is management guidance toward scaling, not a concrete funding event — treat the "fundraise signal" as a plausible read, not a confirmed fact.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** UK/EU banking-as-a-service (BaaS) / embedded finance. Market-sizing figures diverge wildly by definition — ~$26–34bn (2026) at ~13–19% CAGR per several secondary trackers (via researchandmarkets / fortunebusinessinsights / straitsresearch, as of 2026), vs an outlier ~$1tn "transaction value" figure; treat the dollar TAM as soft, not a hard number (`[UNSOURCED]` at a verifiable level). Structure: **bifurcated** — licence-holders that are themselves regulated banks (Griffin, ClearBank, Solaris, Swan) vs middleware/orchestrators riding third-party sponsor banks (Unit, Treasury Prime, Synctera, Stripe Treasury) (per upshift.finance, 2026). Entry barrier is capital + a full banking licence + PRA/FCA capital-and-liquidity regime — exactly the function Jenkins is hired to run. **Why now:** post-Synapse (June 2024, >$100m customer funds frozen) the "lightly-governed BaaS era is over" — US sponsor banks took ≥7 consent orders (Cross River, Evolve, Blue Ridge, Choice) 2022–25, and BaFin measures hit Solaris in Germany, lengthening onboarding and raising pricing (per natlawreview / it-finanzmagazin, 2026). Trust, reconciliation and unambiguous liability — not speed — now decide which providers win capital and enterprise clients.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: payments volume processed (TPV), number of platform customers, revenue growth, and capital/liquidity adequacy. Players + basis of competition: Griffin, ClearBank, Solaris, Swan compete on **owning the licence + API stack** (vertical integration), vs middleware (Unit/Treasury Prime/Synctera/Stripe Treasury) competing on breadth of connected banks (per upshift.finance, 2026). Recent peer moves: Solaris Series G $93.4m (6 Jan 2025) and a Mar-2026 "AI-native bank" pivot; Swan Series B-II €/$43.7m (30 Jan 2025), processing >€1.5bn/month for 150+ companies; Treasury Prime cut ~half its staff (Feb 2024) and retreated to a bank-led model — a direct cautionary tale for the middleware camp. Griffin's position: **catching up on scale but structurally advantaged** — full UK banking licence since Mar 2024, ~£3bn payments processed and 50+ platform customers (Uber, Marqeta, Access Group) with 5x revenue growth reported for 2025 ([[Griffin signs Uber and Marqeta as customers]]). Moat (analysis): regulatory licence + capital base = a real barrier middleware can't replicate; switching costs rise once a platform embeds its accounts/safeguarding on Griffin's rails.

**Comps & multiples.** Internal comps (in-base BaaS/core-banking deals): [[Griffin signs Uber and Marqeta as customers]], [[Thought Machine lands GBP 45M funding as losses widen]], [[Treasury Prime launches Prime Cash with Green Dot Network]], [[Synctera acquires compliance testing startup Cable]], [[Keel emerges from stealth as profitable BaaS platform]]. External peers (last private rounds — round valuations, NOT market caps): Griffin last Series A Mar-2024 raised $24m / £19m at ~$97m post-money (per pymnts / finsmes / tracxn); Solaris ~$1.6bn (Jul-2023, since diluted through a 2024 distressed round); ClearBank ~$636m raised cumulatively, revenue reported £100–500m band FY25 (per tracxn/pitchbook); Swan $108m raised cumulatively. **EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E: no data** — all four are private with undisclosed clean revenue/EBITDA, so no verifiable multiple can be computed (distribution not computed, qualitative comparison only). Qualitatively Griffin is the **smallest by capital raised** of the licence-holders (~$97m valuation vs Solaris ~$1.6bn): earlier-stage, not over-valued vs peers — the CFO hire is consistent with a sub-scale player professionalising ahead of a larger raise.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Sub-scale capital base for a regulated bank** — at ~$97m (Mar-2024) valuation and one modest Series A, Griffin is far smaller than Solaris/ClearBank; a full banking licence demands continuous capital/liquidity, and the Jenkins hire (£40m+ capital-raise, IPO track record) signals an imminent, non-trivial funding need. Second-order: dilution/terms risk if raised into a cooler 2026 BaaS market.
2. **Sector consolidation / survivorship** — German commentary explicitly frames 2026 as "BaaS-Sterben" (who survives consolidation); Treasury Prime's ~50% layoffs and Solaris's distressed trajectory show the downside. Second-order: client concentration risk — losing a tier-1 name (Uber/Marqeta) would dent the ~£3bn TPV narrative.
3. **Post-Synapse regulatory/liability overhang** — PRA/FCA and EU (PSD3/FiDA, DORA) tightening raises compliance cost and onboarding friction for all licence-holders; a reconciliation or safeguarding failure at any UK BaaS peer would trigger blanket scrutiny Griffin cannot opt out of. Second-order: higher fixed compliance cost pressures unit economics for a still-small book.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) A CFO with IPO + £40m-raise experience is a tell that Griffin is positioning for a larger institutional raise or longer-horizon public-markets optionality, not a near-term exit — treasury/capital rigour is the new table-stakes for licence-holding BaaS as the regulated camp pulls away from middleware. Falsifiable thesis: Griffin announces a materially larger funding round (≥ its prior $24m, likely at a step-up) within ~12 months; trigger/catalyst = that raise or continued tier-1 client wins. What would break it: a down-round, client churn, or an FCA/PRA restriction — any of which would reframe the hire as defensive rather than growth-stage.
