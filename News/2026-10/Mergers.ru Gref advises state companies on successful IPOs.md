# Mergers.ru: Gref advises state companies on successful IPOs

> [!info] 2026-10-09 · 1 упоминаний · 0 источника(ов) с текстом
> Каналы: Mergers.ru

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Mergers.ru] 🏦 Греф рассказал, как компаниям с госучастием выйти на успешное IPO. "Первое правило - не надо жадничать. <…> Если ты выходишь на IPO, ты должен понимать, что это большая ответственность, и ты должен часть собственных интересов похоронить. Ты должен начинать заботиться о своих публичных инвесторах. Если ты к этому готов, выходи, если нет, лучше не выходи - будет разочарование и для тебя, и для инвесторов, и подрыв репутации. Поэтому IPO, я бы сказал, это очень такой сложный процесс, и это сложный способ привлечения капитала для компании", - заявил председатель правления Сбера Герман Греф на инвестиционном форуме "Настоящее будущее". Он подчеркнул, что компаниям с госучастием важно найти компромисс между своими интересами и интересами инвесторов, отдать часть доходности рынку. В противном случае их ценные бумаги не будут пользоваться спросом.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)

German Gref (CEO / Chairman of the Management Board of Sber) gave advice to state-owned companies on how to run a successful IPO, speaking at the **"Настоящее – будущее" ("Real Future") investment forum** — a Sber Investments event in Moscow, **2026-10-08** (the Mergers.ru/preqveca item is a 2026-10-09 re-report of same-day Vedomosti coverage). His core message: **"don't be greedy"** at pricing; an issuer must accept that going public is "a big responsibility", bury part of its own interests, and start caring about public investors — giving part of the return to the market. Otherwise the securities "won't be in demand" and the IPO ends in disappointment and reputational damage. He framed IPO as "a very complex process and a complex way to raise capital."

- **De-PR'd read:** This is **commentary, not an event** — no deal, no filing, no numbers, no named company. It is generic, almost tautological advice ("price it so it trades up, treat minority investors fairly"). Its weight comes entirely from *who* says it (Sber CEO, the single most important equity-market franchise in Russia) and *when* (a stalled IPO market being nudged back open).
- **+ Why framed this way / what it reveals:** Gref's platform is itself a Sber Investments forum, and Sber (SberCIB / Sber Investments) is a leading IPO **underwriter** in Russia — it co-organised the DOM.RF IPO and has publicly said it is working on "more than 10" company listings. So "leave money on the table for the market" is **self-interested**: a well-priced IPO that trades up rebuilds retail trust and feeds Sber's underwriting + brokerage pipeline. Treat the remarks as advice *and* marketing for a franchise that profits from the IPO window reopening.
- **+ Second-order tell:** On the *same day* (2026-10-08), Bank of Russia First Deputy Chairman Vladimir Chistyukhin said the government's target to lift market capitalization to ~66% of GDP by 2030 is effectively **unreachable**. Gref's "price fairly so demand returns" and the CB's "the target is out of reach" are two sides of the same problem: retail demand is damaged and the state pipeline is thin. See [[Russia weighs selling up to 5% of NSPK to single investor]] for the state's appetite to monetize stakes.

## [1] Competitors / peers

Russian IPO landscape (the relevant "peer set" is recent domestic listings, not foreign comps):
- **2024:** ~14 IPOs, ~84 bn ₽ raised — the peak of the post-2022 boom.
- **2025:** collapse to **4 true IPOs, <33–37 bn ₽** ("near freeze"). CB's 2025 review: IPO+SPO combined 125.2 bn ₽ but issuer count fell 19→9, two large names dominated, retail buyer *count* fell ~4.5x YoY, and citizens largely sold within 30 days of listing.
- **Flagship peer — DOM.RF IPO (2025-11-20):** priced at top of range (1750 ₽), raised ~25 bn ₽, ~315 bn ₽ cap, 4–5x oversubscribed; first **privatisation IPO since 2020** and largest Russian IPO since 2021. Sber was an organiser. This is the live example of the "state company done right" that Gref's advice points at.
- Corpus comps for the domestic primary market: [[Mergers.ru Incab Holding raises 2 billion rubles in IPO]] (a rare live 2026 IPO), [[Mergers.ru SPB Exchange to run first 2026 SPO with NFK-sberezheniy]] (secondary-offering activity), and [[Russian pre-IPO platform Rounds shuts down]] (the pre-IPO segment contracting — a direct symptom of the frozen window).
- **+ Why the lay of the land is this way:** Underperformance of the 2023–2024 cohort scared retail. 12 of 16 IPO stocks from 2024–2025 traded below offer at six months: ВсеИнструменты.ру −52%, Диасофт ~−50%, МТС Банк −43%, IVA −36%, Элемент −36%, Займер −32%, Европлан −23%. This is the *empirical backing* for "don't be greedy" — aggressive pricing is exactly what produced the "unloved securities" Gref warns about. (analysis) The lesson is survivorship-biased: the few winners (Озон Фармацевтика, Аренадата, Промомед) were priced with room to run.

## [2] Company history / fit

- Sber is Russia's dominant bank and, via SberCIB / Sber Investments, a top-tier IPO organiser with a mass-retail brokerage base. Deputy Chairman Anatoly Popov (Sept 2025) said Sber was working on IPOs of "more than 10" companies; Sber now expects "at least 5" IPOs in 2026 with the main wave in 2027.
- **+ Why Gref acts this way:** Sber's underwriting + retail-brokerage economics depend on a *functioning* primary market and on retail investors who don't get burned. A string of below-offer IPOs poisons the retail funnel that is Sber Investments' core asset. So Gref talking down greedy pricing protects the long-run franchise even at the cost of near-term issuer-side fees. It fits a consistent Sber posture of courting the mass retail investor (cf. [[Sber launches state-backed long-term savings plan for children]], [[Финтехно Russia approves new private-savings mobilization law]] — mobilizing household savings into the market).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction

- **Novelty: low.** There is no new fact here — no deal, number, date, or policy. The advice ("price fairly, respect minorities") is conventional capital-markets orthodoxy. Traction = zero (it is talk, not a transaction).
- **+ Where the real (small) value is:** the signal, not the content. A sitting Sber CEO publicly telling *state* issuers to under-price relative to their own interests is a mild tell that (a) the IPO window is being coaxed open after a ~2-year freeze and (b) the sell-side itself judges that greed, not just rates, killed retail demand. (analysis) But it changes nothing on the ground; the binding constraints are the key rate and the near-exhausted state pipeline (Minfin: "3–4 companies left"; no state IPOs in 2026, pushed to 2027).

## [4] What's next / market sentiment

- **Rates:** Bank of Russia key rate peaked at **21%** (2024-10), eased to **16%** end-2025 and **14%** by 2026 (held at the 2026-09-11 meeting; next decision 2026-10-23). Further cuts are the single biggest swing factor for equity issuance — as risk-free yields fall, equity's risk premium improves and the IPO window can reopen.
- **MOEX:** IMOEX ~2312 on 2026-10-09 (−1.06%), historically depressed (vs 3200+ in 2021). "Overbought" short-term per analysts; weak structural backdrop.
- **Pipeline:** Sber expects a 2026 revival but "piecemeal", with the real deferred-IPO wave in **2027**. State privatisation IPOs are not happening in 2026 (Minfin). The 66%-of-GDP-by-2030 cap target is now judged unreachable by the CB itself (needs ~1–1.3 tn ₽ of placements/year).
- **+ Why / second-order:** (analysis) Gref's advice and the CB's pessimism point the same way — the bottleneck is **demand quality + supply scarcity**, not exhortation. Even a fully reopened window is gated on the key rate continuing down *and* on state sellers actually bringing deals (which Minfin signals they won't, near-term). Counterintuitively, the few live 2026 deals (Incab-type) matter more as sentiment proof than large commentary.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russian equity capital markets (ECM) remain in a near-frozen post-2022 state: closed to foreign capital, almost entirely domestic/retail-funded, and squeezed by a very high key rate. The Bank of Russia has only just begun easing, cutting from 16% to 15.5% (per nestcentre.org, 2026) — still restrictive, keeping fixed-income yields (deposits, OFZ) far more attractive than equity for retail, which structurally caps IPO demand. The pipeline is thin: VTB sees "up to three IPOs" in Russia by end-2026 with ~5 companies at high readiness (per VTB via TASS, 2026), versus the 15-20 "ready" names MOEX cited a year earlier ([[Moscow Exchange sees 15-20 companies ready for IPO]], 2026-07-13) — readiness ≠ execution. The state-privatisation agenda is the one genuine new driver: the Finance Ministry has proposed privatising ~7 major companies in 2026 (energy/transport/finance) for an estimated RUB 100-300bn in budget revenue, and expects two SPOs by state-affiliated firms; RZD (Russian Railways) is floated for 2026 (per Interfax/TASS, 2026). "Why now": a stretched federal budget needs non-debt revenue, and DOM.RF's November 2025 listing — Russia's first state IPO in ~18 years — reopened the template Gref is now commenting on. `(analysis)` Gref's "don't be greedy… bury part of your own interests, give some yield to the market" is precisely a response to the retail-only, yield-starved demand environment: with no foreign price-setters, pricing discipline is the whole game.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for an IPO window: offer-to-book coverage/subscription, first-day and 3-6M aftermarket vs offer price, retail-account participation, and the relative yield of deposits/OFZ (the opportunity cost). State issuers vs private issuers compete for the same shallow retail pool (MOEX retail accounts ~3.6m active, up from 2.2m in 2022, per secondary citation). The reference precedent — DOM.RF — priced at RUB 1,750, raised ~RUB 25bn placing ~10%, drew 50,000+ retail applications, and trades around RUB 2,189 vs the RUB 1,750 offer (per cbonds/investing.com, 2026), i.e. a positive aftermarket — the "success" case Gref implicitly holds up. Position of the protagonist: Gref/Sber is not the issuer here but the most credible domestic voice on ECM discipline — Sber is both the largest underwriter/custodian ecosystem and itself the benchmark blue-chip. Internal comps: [[DOM.RF plans 10% IPO]] type precedent, [[Bank of Russia proposes 3bn ruble minimum for IPO listing tiers]] (2026-07-06, raising the quality bar for listings), and the regulator's own stance that it won't cut rates "just to ease business" ([[Bank of Russia signals no rate cut just to ease business]], 2026-07-01).

**Comps & multiples.** No deal/valuation is attached to this note — it is commentary, not a transaction — so trading multiples are not computable here: multiples = **no data**. The only quantified precedent is DOM.RF's aftermarket: current/offer = RUB 2,189 / RUB 1,750 ≈ **1.25x** (≈ +25% vs offer, per cbonds/investing.com) — a supportive, in-line-to-rich reception for a well-anchored state deal, but it is a single data point, not a distribution (distribution not computed, qualitative comparison). Sber (state-controlled, the vantage behind the commentary) itself reported record FY2025 IFRS net profit RUB 1.7tn (~$22bn, +7.9% YoY) at 22.7% ROE (per The Moscow Times / TASS / Investing.com, 2026-02-26 — IR corroboration: Sber FY2025 Summary IFRS, sberbank.com, 2026-02-26). That ~22.7% ROE is the implicit benchmark a Sber chairman measures equity-return discipline against — and is far below current risk-free yields' appeal, underscoring his "share the yield" warning.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Rate/opportunity-cost overhang.** At a 15.5% key rate, deposits and OFZ out-yield most equity on a risk-adjusted basis; Russia's own central bank said on 2026-10-09 that Putin's stock-market-capitalisation target is "out of reach" (The Moscow Times) — a direct signal the ECM revival is behind plan. Second-order: state IPOs may be forced to price cheap (dilute budget proceeds) or stall.
2. **Thin, retail-only demand + no foreign price discovery.** With foreign capital sanctioned out, pricing rests on a ~3.6m-account retail base; one mis-priced large state float (Gref's exact warning) can sour sentiment for the whole 2026 pipeline — reputational contagion, not just a single deal's problem.
3. **Conflict of interest / minority-protection in state issuers.** Gref's "find a compromise, give yield to the market" flags the core governance risk: the state as controlling shareholder optimises for budget/strategic goals over minority investors, which caps free-float, liquidity, and re-rating potential.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is an establishment signal that the 2026 state-privatisation wave will be paced by pricing discipline, not ambition — expect modest free-floats (DOM.RF's ~10%) and conservative valuations rather than a boom. Falsifiable thesis: if a flagship 2026 state float (e.g. RZD or an energy name) prices and holds above offer like DOM.RF did, the pipeline unlocks; the trigger to watch is the first large-cap state IPO's book coverage and 3-month aftermarket. What breaks it: a key-rate reversal upward, or a state deal breaking issue — either would re-freeze the window.
