PSV Tech secures €56M Fund II to back early-stage Nordic startups
Copenhagen-based venture capital firm PSV Tech has closed its second fund at €56 million to back Nordic startups at the pre-seed and early stages, with a focus on AI, software, and digital infrastructure.
Launched in 2020 as part of PSV Venture House and based at the Technical University of Denmark (DTU), PSV Tech invests in startups before they reach product-market fit. The firm is managed by General Partners Helle Uth, Richard Breiter, Alexander Viterbo-Horten, and Christel Piron.
PSV Tech's first fund made 47 investments, with portfolio companies including Teton and Spektr, which have since raised $20 million Series A rounds.
In 2025, PSV Tech launched Fund II with a €70 million target to support founders across the Nordics. The fund has now reached its final close at €56 million and has made 19 investments across Denmark, Sweden, and Norway, including 12 over the past nine months.
With the new capital, PSV Tech aims to continue backing founders at the earliest stages, helping startups develop their products, reach product-market fit, and prepare for subsequent growth funding. The fund saw backing from new LPs Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker and Nordea-fonden, alongside existing investors including ATP, EIFO, IDA, Christian Lund, Henrik Printzlau, Sebastian Knutsson, Morten Ebbesen, and Niels Ebbesen.
The fund saw backing from Bank Gospodarstwa Krajowego, with ICEYE serving as its strategic partner.
Fuel Ventures has secured a £10 million commitment from the British Business Bank through its Regional Angels Programme.
September brought one of the CEE's largest funding rounds of 2026, as EnduroSat raised $205 million. Check out the top CEE funding rounds announced during the month.
The $5 million round was led by Odyssey Ventures, with participation from JSK Investments, 33East, and Alumni Ventures.
The round was backed by Next Frontier Capital, Grand Ventures, and ACTAI Ventures.
The round was co-led by Join Capital and PhotonVentures, with participation from Takeoff.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On **2026-10-07** Endeavor Catalyst — the co-investment arm of the nonprofit Endeavor — announced the **final, oversubscribed close of Fund V at $320M**, its largest fund ever and lifting AUM to **>$850M** across five funds (TechCrunch; BusinessWire/Morningstar). ~400 LPs, ~30% of them Endeavor Entrepreneurs; marquee names Reid Hoffman, Bill Ackman, Prosus, plus founders of Nubank, Revolut, Checkout.com, Globant, Careem.

De-PR'd: this is a **~+10% step-up over the $292M Fund IV (Jul 2022)** — the SMALLEST percentage jump in the fund family's history (→$32M beta 2012 → ~$80M 2017 → $134M 2020 → $292M 2022 → $320M 2026). The "founders elsewhere / more of the world builds what's next" framing dresses up what is, in rate-of-growth terms, a deceleration — consistent with a weak EM fundraising market, not an acceleration.
- **Why structured this way:** Catalyst is NOT a traditional picker. It is a **rules-based, passive minority co-investor** — it invests only when a vetted Endeavor Entrepreneur raises a priced round ≥$5M led by a qualified institutional lead, at the lead's exact price/terms, check $1–3M, capped at 10% of the round, no board seat. Its "edge" is deal access via the Endeavor network + capital efficiency, NOT selection alpha. → the central question is not "can they pick winners" but "does a passive <10% rider actually generate net returns" — and here the single biggest red flag: **no DPI/IRR/MOIC was disclosed** for a 14-year-old fund family (Managing Partner Allen Taylor explicitly declined).

## [1] Competitors / peers
In 2025–26, $200–500M emerging-markets funds are scarce. Comps: BECO Capital Booster Growth $250M (UAE), Shorooq $200M (QIA-backed), Oman Jasoor $180M, Lightspeed India V ~$250M (deliberately HALVED from its prior $500M fund), Blume Ventures V ~$250–275M target. LatAm/Africa single funds almost never reach $200M (Canary IV ~$150M Feb 2026; only ~6 Africa funds closed in all of 2025 for ~$107M combined). See also the much larger developed-market close [[Union Square Ventures raises $900M across two funds]] as a scale contrast.
- **Why the lay of the land:** a diversified pan-EM vehicle at $320M is genuinely large for the category — but diffused across 44 markets, diluting any single-geography thesis. The **give-back structure (~50% carried interest, ~half of fund profits to the Endeavor nonprofit)** is near-unique at this scale; closest analogue SOSV donates only ~10% of carry. Second-order: the 50%-carry model is a fundraising asset in a mission-aligned LP base but a fragility if returns soften (GPs keep half the already-lower carry).

## [2] Company history / fit
Monotonic ramp since the 2012 ~$10M beta fund, deploying into Endeavor-selected founders across LatAm (largest region — Rappi, Creditas, dLocal, Bitso, Clip, EBANX, Nubank-associated, [[Cashea expands across Latin America from Venezuela]]), MENA (Checkout.com, Careem, Tabby), Europe (fastest-growing — ElevenLabs, Bending Spoons, Preply), Africa ([[LemFi commits $135M to scale global expansion]]-type names: Flutterwave, Moniepoint, Moove), and SE Asia. Also co-invested alongside rounds in the LatAm fintech cohort it tracks ([[Argentina crypto fintech Lemon raises $20M Series B round]]). Track record (official, ~Sep 2026): 437 companies, 44 markets, 83 unicorns, 39 exits, 11 IPOs; notable exits FIGS/dLocal/VTEX (2021 IPOs) and Brex ($5.15B Capital One acquisition, closed 2026-04-07).
- **Why it acts this way:** the model exists to make Endeavor financially self-sustaining by decade-end — the fund IS the nonprofit's endowment engine, which is why carry is recycled rather than maximized for GPs.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Novelty is the **financing mechanism** (a nonprofit self-funded by recycled carry), NOT the investment edge. Traction claims are real at the portfolio level (83 unicorns / 39 exits verifiable by logo), BUT because Catalyst is a <10% passive co-investor, logo success does NOT establish fund-level net returns — which remain undisclosed.
- **Why the value-add is real-or-not, deeper:** who captures the margin? The LEAD investor sets terms and captures ownership; Catalyst rides at the same price without the diligence cost. That is capital-efficient but caps upside and means Catalyst never controls outcomes. The durable asset is the Endeavor network (proprietary EM deal access), not fund alpha — so the multiple rests on access + mission, both of which commoditize if other LPs build EM co-invest programs directly.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Plan: deploy ~$25–30M more in 2026 (already ~$55–56M across ~30 companies — Replit, Moove, Preply, ElevenLabs, Reflection AI), then 40–50 deals/yr into up to ~150 companies, tilted toward Europe and a stepped-up Asia push, ~90% ex-US. Backdrop: global VC fundraising at an ~8-year low (~$80B in 2025, −38% YoY); capital concentrating in large established managers; AI crowding out ~50% of venture dollars; LPs favoring pan-regional over single-geography EM vehicles.
- **Why the market goes this way + counterintuitive effect:** the hostile fundraising climate is precisely why a diversified, capital-efficient, mission-framed pan-EM rider resonated — but capital concentration that helped Endeavor raise also makes the broader EM category MORE fragile (fewer leads to ride alongside). Watch for: (a) first disclosed net-return figures; (b) whether the 50%-carry model survives a soft-return vintage.
