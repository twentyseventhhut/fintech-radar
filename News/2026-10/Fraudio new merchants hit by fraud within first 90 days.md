# Fraudio: new merchants hit by fraud within first 90 days

> [!info] 2026-10-14 · 2 упоминаний · 1 источника(ов) с текстом
> Каналы: This Week in Fintech

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[This Week in Fintech] Fraudio followed 3,923 merchants from their very first payment across more than 127.5 million transactions between January 2025 and April 2026, and found that 10.5% experienced fraud within 90 days. Among those hit, the median time to first fraud was 33 days, and one in five saw it in their first week. The per-transaction fraud rate ran 2.9 times higher during those first 90 days, and 82% of the fraudulent transactions looked unremarkable on amount, currency and payment channel alone.

[This Week in Fintech] The card networks are moving on the same window. Mastercard's Scam Merchant Monitoring Program, in effect since July, requires acquirers and payment facilitators to open an investigation within 72 hours when scam signals hit a threshold, with extra scrutiny for merchants with less than six months of processing history. A new merchant has no history of its own to compare against, so detection has to come from network and peer signals from day one, and a 72-hour clock leaves no room to wait for chargebacks to make the case.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
### [0] What happened (the claim)
Fraudio — an Amsterdam-based (founded 2019) AI payment-fraud and AML detection vendor — published an "Early Fraud" data study (released ~2026-10-14 via its hs-sites page) analysing a representative sample of **127.5M+ transactions (Jan 2025–Apr 2026)** and following **3,923 newly observed merchants** from their first-ever payment. Headline finding: **10.5% of new merchants hit at least one fraud-labelled transaction inside their first 90 days**; among those hit, **median time to first fraud was 33 days**, **~20% within the first week** and **~47% within the first 30 days**. Per-transaction fraud rate ran **2.9x higher** in the first 90 days than in established programs, and **82% of fraudulent transactions looked unremarkable** on amount/currency/channel alone. The note pairs this with Mastercard's **Scam Merchant Monitoring Program (SMMP)**.

### [1] Why it matters / stakes
The study quantifies a long-assumed but poorly-measured risk: new merchants are a structural blind spot because models that rely on a merchant's *own* history have no baseline on day one. It lands precisely as the card networks tighten the same window. **Mastercard's SMMP takes effect 24 July 2026** for all card-not-present merchants globally (only Jordan excluded): once a trigger fires, the **acquirer must open an investigation within 72 hours**, and a confirmed scam finding means **immediate termination of Mastercard/Maestro processing** (no fine, no grace period), with extra scrutiny for merchants with <6 months history. The combination — fraud arriving in days + a 72-hour regulatory clock that can't wait for chargebacks — is Fraudio's commercial wedge for real-time, network/peer-signal-based detection from the first transaction.

### [2] Key numbers / entities
- Dataset: 127.5M+ transactions, Jan 2025–Apr 2026; cohort 3,923 new merchants.
- 10.5% hit by fraud in 90 days; median 33 days to first fraud; ~20% in week 1; ~47% in 30 days.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
### Red-team / challenge

1. **Is the 10.5% figure independently verifiable?** Open — it comes solely from Fraudio's own dataset and marketing page; no third-party audit. Treat as vendor-supplied, directionally plausible.
2. **Is the sample representative?** Open — 3,923 merchants / 127.5M txns is a "representative sample drawn from its wider transaction data," skewed toward Fraudio's own acquirer/PSP client base (e.g. Viva Wallet-type portfolios), not all global merchants.
3. **Does "fraud-labelled transaction" overstate merchant fraud?** Partly — a fraud label can mean the merchant was a *victim* of card-not-present fraud, not a fraudulent merchant. The 10.5% conflates merchant-initiated fraud and third-party fraud hitting new merchants. The note doesn't disentangle these.
4. **Is this genuinely new, or a marketing artifact timed to Mastercard SMMP?** It is a vendor study released to ride the SMMP go-live (2026-07-24). Content is new data; framing is commercial.
5. **Is the Mastercard SMMP characterization accurate?** Confirmed — effective 2026-07-24, global CNP (ex-Jordan), 72h acquirer investigation window, confirmed-scam = immediate termination. Matches multiple independent sources.
6. **Does "2.9x higher fraud rate" survive scrutiny?** Open — plausible (new merchants lack a behavioural baseline) but unaudited and definition-dependent on the denominator ("established payment programs").
7. **Is Fraudio a material player or a small vendor?** Small — only ~$3.3M disclosed historical funding plus an undisclosed ~2026 Series A; not a market leader vs Sift/Signifyd/Feedzai/Riskified. Weight the *data point* over the *company*.
8. **Competitive moat claim ("centralised models, not per-client")?** Open — CEO quote is self-serving; centralised-model approach is shared by network-scale players (Feedzai, Visa/Mastercard network tools).
9. **Any double-counting with prior notes?** No — no prior archived note carries this Early Fraud report; related notes are adjacent fraud/risk stories, not the same study.
10. **Is the Mastercard tag justified?** Yes — SMMP is the regulatory hook making the Fraudio data actionable; Mastercard is a legitimate co-subject.
11. **Could the "82% unremarkable" stat be cherry-picked?** Open — supports Fraudio's need-for-behavioural-monitoring pitch; no methodology detail on feature set beyond amount/currency/channel.
12. **Durability / news half-life?** Moderate — the statistic is citable for months as SMMP enforcement ramps; not a breaking event, more a reference data point.
13. **Actionability for readers (acquirers/PSPs)?** High — directly informs onboarding risk policy and SMMP-compliance posture.

**Importance: 3/5** — A credible, well-quantified and timely data point on a real and intensifying problem (new-merchant fraud + Mastercard SMMP 72h clock), genuinely useful as a reference for payments/risk readers. Capped below 4 because it is vendor-sourced/unaudited, Fraudio is a small player, and it conflates merchant-victim vs merchant-initiated fraud. Fresh (no prior archived version).

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Fraudio sits in merchant-side payment fraud detection — the AI/ML risk-scoring layer sold to acquirers and payment facilitators, not to issuers or consumers. Market sizing is firm-dependent and should be read as a range, not a point estimate: the broad fraud detection & prevention market is put at ~$35.7bn (Markets&Markets) to ~$67–74bn (Fortune Business Insights / The Business Research Company) for 2026, with a ~17.5% CAGR to 2031; the narrower online payment fraud detection segment most relevant to Fraudio is ~$8.4bn in 2026 → ~$20.3bn by 2033 at ~13.4% CAGR (per Persistence Market Research, via secondary citation, as of 2026) [UNSOURCED on methodology — vendor market-research figures, treat as directional]. Structure: fragmented at the independent-vendor layer (Forter, Sift, Riskified, Signifyd, SEON, Resistant AI, Featurespace) but increasingly consolidated at the top, where Visa (Advanced Authorization) and Mastercard (Decision Intelligence) score the majority of U.S. card transactions in-network in <100ms — i.e. the networks own the default rail and independents must beat or complement it. Entry barriers: data network effects (fraud models need cross-merchant signal) and real-time infra. **Why now:** Mastercard's Scam Merchant Monitoring Program took effect 24 July 2026, forcing acquirers/payfacs globally to investigate flagged merchants within 72 hours, with explicit extra scrutiny for merchants with <6 months of processing history (per Solidgate / cside / Chargebacks911 explainers, 2026). That is a regulatory-style demand trigger landing squarely on Fraudio's "early fraud / new-merchant blind spot" pitch — a 72-hour clock plus no merchant history means buyers need network/peer signal from day one rather than waiting for chargebacks.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: fraud rate per transaction, false-positive rate, detection latency (time-to-first-alert), and chargeback/refund rate (Mastercard's SMMP watches a combined refund+chargeback rate >5% over any 30-day rolling window, min 500 tx). Basis of competition = model accuracy (false positives vs missed fraud) and breadth of cross-client signal, not price. Key players: networks (Mastercard Decision Intelligence / Threat Intelligence, Visa Advanced Authorization) at the top; independents Stripe Radar, Adyen RevenueProtect, Forter, Sift, Riskified, Signifyd, plus AML/fraud names SEON, Quantexa, Resistant AI, Featurespace. Recent moves: SEON raised $80m Series C (Sep 2025, led by Sixth Street); Quantexa raised $175m Series F at a $2.6bn valuation (Mar 2025); [[Resistant AI raises $25M Series B against financial crime]]; [[Thredd launches fraud solution with Featurespace]]. **Fraudio's position: niche / sub-scale challenger (analysis).** It raised only ~$3.3m seed and ~$5.2m total (per PitchBook/FintechNews, backers incl. Visa Innovation Program), ~27 staff, founded 2019. Differentiation it claims — centralised cross-client models (CEO João Moura: "we don't produce custom models per client") rather than per-client tuning — is a genuine network-effect play, but its moat depends on data scale it does not obviously have at this size (analysis). This data-led report reads as content marketing for its Payment Fraud Detection (PFD) product; treat the 10.5% / 33-day / 2.9x figures as vendor-published and self-selected (one sample, 3,923 merchants).

**Comps & multiples.** No market-cap or EV/Revenue multiple can be computed for Fraudio — it is private, seed-stage, with no disclosed post-money valuation and no public revenue (multiple = **no data**). Peer reference points (round valuations, NOT market caps): Quantexa $2.6bn post-money at >$100m ARR (Oct-2024 ARR) ⇒ EV/ARR ≈ $2.6bn / $0.1bn = **~26x** on round valuation (rich, but on a 40% license-growth name — growth-justified, not an automatic flag); SEON ~$34.8m est. **Risk flags.**
1. **Disintermediation by the networks.** Mastercard/Visa score most transactions in-network and are expanding safety/security into identity and threat intelligence; the very SMMP rule that creates Fraudio's demand also deepens network ownership of the monitoring layer, so independents risk being reduced to a thin complement (margin captured one layer up).
2. **Sub-scale vs a data-network-effect sector.** ~$5m total funding / ~27 staff against $80–175m-funded peers; a centralised-model moat needs volume Fraudio may not have — execution/scale risk, and vulnerability if a larger peer or acquirer bundles the same capability.
3. **Source is vendor marketing, not audited data.** The headline stats come from Fraudio's own self-selected sample promoting its PFD product; no independent validation, and "announced results" (false positives "decreased dramatically") are unquantified — adoption/efficacy claims should not be taken as live benchmarks.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** The SMMP 72-hour / new-merchant rule is a real regulatory-style catalyst that pulls forward demand for day-one, cross-portfolio fraud signal — favourable for the whole independent cohort, not uniquely Fraudio (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if the networks productise new-merchant scam scoring directly to acquirers as part of compliance tooling, sub-scale independents like Fraudio get squeezed into acquisition or niche-vertical survival; trigger to watch = whether Mastercard/Visa ship an SMMP-aligned new-merchant monitoring product, or whether a payfac/acquirer publicly standardises on an independent for SMMP compliance. What breaks the thesis: a marquee acquirer adopting Fraudio at scale for SMMP.

**IR (Mastercard, tangential — supporting evidence):** Mastercard Q2 2026 total revenue ~$9.3bn; value-added services & solutions net revenue ~$3.8bn, +20% YoY (+18% currency-neutral), with security solutions a named growth driver. Its Threat Intelligence platform identified >7m card-testing transactions across 192 countries and claims to have prevented ~$172m in fraud tied to malicious domains (Q2 2026 earnings/call coverage). This frames fraud/cyber as a scaling value-added-services revenue line for the network — the same budget line Fraudio competes against/alongside. Latest Mastercard filing on record: 10-Q FY2026 (period ending 2026-06-30, filed 000114139126000083).
