## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-10-20 UK banking-tech firm **Hyperlayer** announced a **£19.5M Series A2** round led by existing backer **Flintlock Capital**, valuing the company at **£195M** (digest/Sky rounded the raise to "£20M"). New/existing participants include **Mouro Capital** (Santander-backed) and **Susquehanna**. Flintlock co-founder/GP Joe Shamess joins the board. Proceeds fund product, engineering and implementation teams plus expansion across the US, Europe and APAC. This brings total raised to **~£50M since October 2025** — building directly on the [[Hyperlayer raises £30 million to help legacy lenders]] round (2025-10, reported £30M/$24.8M at ~£195M/$247.7M).

**[1] What Hyperlayer does.** A **programmable / "overlay" banking platform**: its software sits *above* a bank's existing core rather than replacing it, letting legacy lenders launch new products (accounts, rewards, lending, "Family Banking", "Integrated Wealth Banking") in weeks instead of years — without rip-and-replace transformation risk. It also operates the consumer-facing digital wallet **HyperJar**. Founded by ex-Morgan Stanley senior executives **Rob Rooney** (CEO, ~30 yrs at MS) and **Paul Rolles**.

**[2] Why it matters.** Core modernisation is one of banking's largest unsolved cost centres. Hyperlayer's bet is the *non-replacement* angle — a middle path between full core migration and status-quo paralysis. The rapid follow-on (£30M → £19.5M inside ~12 months at a flat-to-up £195M valuation) signals investor conviction in overlay/coreless architecture and in a founder team with tier-1 bank credibility.

**[3] Competitive landscape.** Head-on with cloud-native core vendors — **Thought Machine** (Vault, London), **10x Banking** (SuperCore, Chase UK/Westpac), **Mambu**, **Tuum**, **Pismo**, **Finxact/Fiserv**. Key differentiator: those target full core *replacement/migration*; Hyperlayer positions as an *overlay* that augments the incumbent core. On the consumer side HyperJar competes with digital-wallet/neobank apps. See adjacent UK/payments-infra context in [[EWG selects ClearBank to strengthen digital payments infrastructure]] and [[Brite Payments launches Pay by Bank in the UK]].

**[4] Numbers & timeline.** 2025-10: ~£30M raise (reported $24.8M at $247.7M val). 2026-10-20: £19.5M Series A2 at £195M val, Flintlock-led. Total ~£50M since Oct 2025. Note the **discrepancy**: digest/Sky headline "£20M / £195M valuation"; primary/trade sources (fintech.global, financialIT, Dealroom) report **£19.5M Series A2**. Valuation £195M is consistent across sources.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. **£20M vs £19.5M:** The digest says "£20M"; primary/trade press says **£19.5M Series A2**. Rounding, or a gross-vs-net figure? Headline risk if we repeat "£20M" uncritically.
2. **Round label:** Is this genuinely a *Series A2*, or an A extension dressed up? £30M "Series A" (2025) + £19.5M "A2" inside 12 months suggests the first raise may not have closed the round the company hoped.
3. **Flat valuation:** 2025 round reported ~£195M/$247.7M; 2026 again £195M. A flat valuation after 12 months + another raise can signal a down/soft market or slower-than-planned traction — not the up-round the PR implies.
4. **Insider-led:** Both rounds led by the *same* backer (Flintlock). How much genuinely new outside capital vs insiders defending their position? Thin new-investor roster can indicate limited external demand.
5. **Revenue / live banks:** How many banks are actually in production on the overlay (not pilots/LOIs)? Named marquee client logos? Without them the "modernise in weeks" claim is unproven.
6. **Overlay vs core — real moat?** Thought Machine/10x argue true modernisation needs the core replaced. Is "sit above the legacy core" a durable advantage or a stopgap banks outgrow once they commit to a real migration?
7. **HyperJar vs B2B pivot:** Company history is a consumer wallet (HyperJar); messaging is now B2B bank-infrastructure. Is this a genuine platform or a consumer app repackaged — and is the wallet still growing/funded?
8. **Burn vs raise:** £50M in ~12 months. What's the runway and burn? Rapid consecutive raises can mean high cash consumption rather than demand-driven scaling.
9. **Regulatory posture:** Overlaying core banking systems touches regulated data/ledger flows. Is Hyperlayer regulated (FCA/e-money), or purely a tech vendor relying on the bank's permissions?
10. **Geographic overreach:** Pre-scale firm promising US + Europe + APAC expansion simultaneously — classic spread-too-thin risk vs proven UK base.
11. **Founder credibility vs execution:** Ex-Morgan Stanley pedigree (Rooney/Rolles) opens doors, but tier-1 banking-tech sales cycles are 12-24 months. Does the team have *delivery* track record, not just relationships?
12. **Sky "digital wallet provider" framing:** Sky brands it a digital-wallet provider; trade press brands it bank-modernisation infra. Which is the real revenue driver today?
13. **Source quality:** Several hits are small UK local-radio syndications of one PA/Sky wire — not independent confirmation. Primary funding details rest on fintech.global/financialIT/Dealroom.
14. **$ vs £ confusion:** Dealroom lists the 2025 round as $24.8M/$247.7M while UK press said £30M/£195M — FX/double-counting across datasets muddies the "total £50M" figure.
15. **Exit/thesis:** With flat valuation and insider rounds, what's the realistic path — strategic acquisition by a core vendor (Thought Machine/10x/Temenos) rather than independent scale?

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Hyperlayer sits in core-banking *orchestration* infrastructure (B2B), not the consumer digital-wallet category the headline implies: founded 2023 as a B2B spin-out of the HyperJar consumer app, its HyperOS "programmable orchestration layer" plugs into existing bank cores rather than replacing them (per Hyperlayer corporate materials / Finovate, as of Oct 2026). HyperJar remains the ~0.5M-customer UK consumer app the tech was proven on (per Money To The Masses, 2025). Sector sizing: the UK prepaid-card & digital-wallet market is put at ~US$59.8bn in 2026, projected ~US$82.73bn by 2030 (per GlobeNewswire/Research intelligence report, Feb 2026); UK mobile-wallet spend is forecast to grow ~68% by 2030 to ~£453bn (per Global Payments, via The Paypers). But that is the *downstream* wallet-spend TAM; the relevant market for Hyperlayer is core-banking/orchestration software, which is structurally consolidating around a few platforms (Thought Machine, Mambu, 10x, Starling's Engine). Why now: banks are under pressure to ship AI-era product fast without ripping out cores — the "coexistence/overlay" thesis — against a cold UK funding backdrop (UK fintech funding at a decade low, per [[42sec UK fintech funding falls to lowest level in a decade]]), which makes a flat-to-up insider-led round notable.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: ARR / net revenue retention, number of Tier-1 logos, and migration/implementation cycle time (orchestration's pitch is faster go-live vs full core replacement). Key players & basis of competition (product depth + reference logos, not price): Thought Machine (>US$100M ARR mid-2026, last valued ~US$2.7bn in 2022, IPO pushed to ≥2028, per Tech.eu/getlatka); 10x Banking (raised £40M Series E led by AshGrove in Aug 2026 — see [[10x Banking partners Tungsten Automation to power Pay+ ledger]]); Mambu (~US$220M revenue, ~US$660M raised, per 6sense); and Starling's Engine (see [[Engine by Starling powers Tangerine Bank business offering]]). Recent peer move: 10x's Aug-2026 Series E and Thought Machine's July-2026 US$40M top-up both signal the category is still being funded through the downturn. Hyperlayer's position: niche challenger / catching up `(analysis)` — a sub-£200m-valued 2-year-old vs multi-hundred-million-ARR incumbents; its differentiation is the overlay/coexistence model plus a founder-credibility moat (Rob Rooney, 30-yr Morgan Stanley veteran) rather than scale or installed base. Disclosed revenue/ARR, logo count and implementation metrics: `[UNSOURCED]` — company is private and did not disclose.

**Comps & multiples.** This round: £19.5M Series A2 (the "£20M" is rounded) led by existing investor Flintlock Capital, with Mouro Capital and Susquehanna joining; £195M post-money (per fintech.global / Sky News / ITBrief, 20 Oct 2026). Note a currency/framing discrepancy: some trackers report the same round in USD at ~$24.8M / ~$247.7M post-money (per Dealroom) — treat the £195M figure as the round (post-money) valuation, **not** a market cap, and reconcile FX before quoting. Hyperlayer raised ~£30m roughly a year earlier (per fintech.global). EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA: **no data** — neither Hyperlayer's revenue nor those of 10x/Starling Engine are publicly disclosed, so no multiple can be computed honestly; distribution not computed, qualitative comparison only. Internal comps as reference points (valuation-reset context in the broader stack, NOT like-for-like orchestration peers): [[Checkout.com now valued at $12B, down from $40B]] shows the private-fintech re-rating Hyperlayer is raising into. Qualitative read: at £195M post-money for a pre-scale 2-year-old, the valuation looks **rich on absolutes but small vs incumbents** — defensible only if ARR is growing fast (growth-multiple logic), which is unverifiable here, so flagged as a watch-item rather than an over-valuation call.

**Risk flags.**
1. *Category mislabel / expectation risk* — marketed via its "digital wallet" HyperJar heritage but sells B2B core-banking orchestration; if buyers and investors price it as a consumer-wallet play, the £195M mark is harder to defend against infrastructure comps. Second-order: a down-round risk if the next raise is benchmarked to orchestration ARR multiples it can't yet support.
2. *Incumbent disintermediation / scale gap* — competes with far larger, better-funded platforms (Thought Machine >US$100M ARR, 10x fresh £40M, Mambu ~US$220M rev); a 2-year-old overlay risks being out-referenced on Tier-1 procurement where logo count is the moat it lacks.
3. *Insider-led, modest round in a cold market* — a £19.5M round led by an *existing* investor, into a UK fintech funding environment at a decade low, can signal limited new-investor conviction; execution risk on the promised US/EU/APAC expansion if follow-on capital tightens.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The signal is less "new wallet entrant" and more that the *core-banking-coexistence* thesis keeps attracting capital even through the UK downturn — incumbents being funded (10x, Thought Machine) and a new challenger marked at £195M suggest the orchestration layer is where banks are willing to spend without touching the core. Falsifiable thesis: Hyperlayer converts the raise into ≥1 named Tier-1 bank production deployment within 12 months. Trigger to watch: a disclosed Tier-1 logo or ARR figure (validates the mark); what would break the thesis: the next round comes flat/down or stays insider-only, implying the £195M was a mark, not demand.
