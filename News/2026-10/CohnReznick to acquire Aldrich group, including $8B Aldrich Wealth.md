CohnReznick expands West Coast footprint and wealth advisory through Aldrich acquisition
CohnReznick expands its West Coast footprint and wealth advisory services with the acquisition of Aldrich CPAs + Advisors and Aldrich Wealth.
 New York, NY – (October 13, 2026) –  CohnReznick, a leading professional services firm, today announced a strategic acquisition of the Aldrich group of companies, comprised of Aldrich CPAs + Advisors ($100M+ revenues) and Aldrich Wealth ($8B assets under advisement). The transaction is expected to close in mid-December 2026.
Aldrich is one of the 10 largest firms headquartered on the West Coast, creating a significant expansion for CohnReznick’s geographic footprint . In addition to 500+ professionals and 48 partners, Aldrich brings offices in Spokane, Washington; Portland (Lake Oswego) and Salem, Oregon; Carlsbad, Mission Valley, and Orange County, California; Salt Lake City, Utah; and expanded presence in San Diego and Denver. The team also strengthens CohnReznick’s industry capabilities across Private Client Services, Construction, Telecom, M&D (Agribusiness), and more to create significant growth opportunity for the combined organization.
“This is a significant milestone in CohnReznick’s growth and an important investment in our future,” said David Kessler (Opens a new window) , CEO, CohnReznick Advisory LLC. “Aldrich brings exceptional talent, strong market leadership, and highly complementary capabilities, including a wealth management platform that creates an important new avenue for growth and expands the solutions we can bring to clients. I’m very excited about this expansion on the West Coast, strengthening our ability to serve clients in some of the most dynamic markets and industries in the country, and creating even greater opportunities for our people.”
“For more than 50 years, the decisions we have made at Aldrich have been guided by what is best for our people, our clients, and our communities,” said John Lauseng, CEO + Partner, Aldrich. “Joining CohnReznick gives our people greater opportunities to grow, expands the capabilities and resources we can bring to our clients, and allows us to build on the relationships, culture, and commitment to our communities that have defined Aldrich. We are excited about what we can accomplish together, particularly as Aldrich becomes the cornerstone of CohnReznick’s new West Region.”
“Our clients have always been at the center of how we make decisions, and that was an important consideration throughout this process,” said Heather Wonderly, CEO, Aldrich Wealth. “Joining CohnReznick gives us the opportunity to build on the highly personal, fiduciary approach our clients value while expanding the resources and capabilities we can bring to individuals, families, business owners, and retirement plan sponsors. Aldrich Wealth becoming the platform for CohnReznick’s wealth management business creates an exciting opportunity to build on what our team has created and expand the impact we can make for our clients and our people.”
This is the latest in a number of important expansion efforts that CohnReznick has made in the past year, recently opening offices in Houston, TX (Opens a new window) ; Minneapolis, MN (Opens a new window) ; San Francisco, CA (Opens a new window) ; Tampa (Opens a new window) and Tallahassee, FL (Opens a new window) ; and Richmond, VA (Opens a new window) . Investing for organic growth and selective M&A opportunities have been successful strategies for the firm, now accelerated by its partnership with Apax Funds (Opens a new window) .
Dechert LLP is serving as legal counsel to CohnReznick. Hunton Andrews Kurth LLP is serving as legal counsel to Aldrich and Guggenheim Securities, LLC and Advisor Growth Strategies, LLC are serving as financial advisors.

# Context-enrichment: CohnReznick to acquire Aldrich group, including $8B Aldrich Wealth
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-13 CohnReznick (a Top-20 US accounting/advisory firm, ~$1.12B FY25 revenue, PE-backed by Apax Funds since early 2025) agreed to acquire the **Aldrich group**: Aldrich CPAs + Advisors (FY25 net revenue ~$103M, an IPA/AT Top-100 firm, one of the 10 largest headquartered on the West Coast) **plus Aldrich Wealth, an RIA with ~$8B assets under advisement**. ~500 professionals, 48 partners, offices across OR/WA/CA/UT (Lake Oswego, Spokane, Salem, Carlsbad, Orange County, Salt Lake City, plus San Diego/Denver expansion). Expected to close **mid-December 2026**. Dechert advises CohnReznick; Hunton Andrews Kurth advises Aldrich; Guggenheim Securities and Advisor Growth Strategies are financial advisors.
- **Financial terms were NOT disclosed** — no purchase price, no multiple, no cash/equity split. The headline "$8B" is Aldrich Wealth's AUA (assets under *advisement*), i.e. a book-of-business metric, **not** a deal value. That framing inflates perceived scale: $8B AUA at a typical RIA economics base is a fraction of the "$8B" the headline implies. (This is the classic PR anchor-to-a-flattering-base move.)
- **Why structured this way:** this is CohnReznick's **third M&A of 2026** and its first with a sizable wealth arm attached. The deal is really two assets bolted together — (a) a West Coast CPA platform (geographic fill-in; CohnReznick had no real West Coast base, now gets a "West Region cornerstone"), and (b) a bolt-on **RIA that becomes the platform for CohnReznick's entire wealth-management business**. The wealth leg is the strategically novel piece; the CPA leg is standard roll-up geography.
- Note the **alternative practice structure**: as with the 2025 Apax deal, PE/the holding entity owns the non-attest advisory + wealth business, while licensed CPA attest services stay partner-controlled. The press release leans on culture/"people" language that masks a straightforward PE-funded consolidation play.

## [1] Competitors / peers
This sits inside the most aggressive consolidation wave the US accounting profession has ever seen. PE-backed Top-30 platforms and their sponsors:
- **Baker Tilly + Moss Adams** — Hellman & Friedman, ~$7B deal value (Apr 2025), set to be ~6th-largest US firm, the largest PE-owned.
- **Grant Thornton** — New Mountain Capital; agreed to acquire **CBIZ** (Jul 2026) to become ~5th-largest. See [[Fintech Wrap Up Grant Thornton on tech implementation speed to value]].
- **Citrin Cooperman** — Blackstone. **EisnerAmper** — TowerBrook (continuation-vehicle recap Mar 2026, 27 acquisitions, $1.2B revenue). **Crowe** — KKR majority, ~$3B valuation (2026).
- **CohnReznick** — Apax (2025). By 2025, ~10–11 of the 30 largest US firms and ~a third of the Top-100 were PE-backed; deal volume went from ~12 (2021) to ~176 (2025).
- On the wealth side, the deal rides the record RIA M&A market (Echelon counted 262 deals in H1 2026, projecting ~500 for the year; ~a third of RIA-adjacent deals in H1 2026 involved tax/accounting sellers).
- **Position:** CohnReznick is a **catching-up/mid-pack** consolidator — smaller than Baker Tilly/Grant Thornton's mega-moves, but ahead of peers in explicitly pairing a CPA roll-up with an owned RIA platform. The CPA acquisition is parity (everyone's buying West Coast firms); the integrated-wealth angle is slightly ahead.
- **Why the land lies this way (2nd order):** PE wants recurring, scalable, high-multiple advisory/wealth revenue, not low-multiple commodity audit. Firms that pair tax/accounting client relationships with an owned RIA can cross-sell and re-rate the whole book to wealth-management multiples (RIA AUM revenue trades richer and stickier than hourly CPA work). The gap between a commoditized audit multiple and an RIA multiple is exactly what sponsors are arbitraging.

## [2] Company history / fit
CohnReznick: Top-20, ~5,000 staff, 350+ partners, 29 offices, $1.12B FY25 revenue. Secured its first institutional capital from Apax in early 2025 (Apax majority-owns the non-attest business). Since then it has been on an explicit buy-and-build run: new/added offices in Houston, Minneapolis, San Francisco, Tampa/Tallahassee, Richmond (PIASCIK), plus HL&B (Texas) and Smith Schafer (Minnesota). Aldrich is the biggest and westernmost move yet.
- **Why the company acts this way:** classic PE playbook — commodity CPA take-rate needs a software/wealth multiple, so expand service lines (wealth, advisory) and geography to build a **national platform** for an eventual re-rating/exit. The Apax capital exists precisely to fund this M&A cadence; Aldrich Wealth is the first time CohnReznick gets a real, scaled wealth platform rather than a tuck-in. The fit is logical: Aldrich's Private Client Services + construction/telecom/agribusiness industry depth slot into CohnReznick's verticals.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **What's genuinely new:** CohnReznick gaining an **$8B-AUA owned RIA as its go-forward wealth platform**, plus instant West Coast scale. For CohnReznick this is a step-change, not a tuck-in.
- **What's NOT new:** the overall move (PE-backed CPA buying another CPA) is industry-standard in 2025–26; dozens of comparable deals exist. The "accounting + wealth cross-sell" thesis is also widely pursued (Baker Tilly, Wealth Enhancement, Focus-type platforms, and the broader "tax firms selling into wealth" trend).
- **Traction / reality check:** this is an **announced agreement, not a closed/integrated business** (closes mid-Dec 2026). No disclosed price, no stated synergy/cross-sell targets, no retention terms for Aldrich's 48 partners. "$8B AUA" is a book metric; actual recurring wealth revenue and net-new-asset growth are undisclosed.
- **Why value-add is real OR not (deeper):** the durable value is the **cross-sell flywheel** — CPA clients (business owners, high-net-worth families) are a warm funnel into fiduciary wealth management, which is recurring and higher-multiple than attest work → **who captures the margin:** the integrated firm captures more wallet share per client and converts transactional tax revenue into AUM-based recurring revenue. **What breaks it:** (1) partner/advisor attrition post-close (RIAs are people-businesses; a culture clash destroys the AUA); (2) independence/conflict optics — a CPA firm pushing its own RIA to audit/tax clients draws regulatory and fiduciary scrutiny; (3) PE-ownership pressure on margins can erode the fiduciary, client-first brand Aldrich Wealth markets on.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Deal closes mid-December 2026; Aldrich becomes CohnReznick's "West Region" cornerstone and Aldrich Wealth becomes the firm-wide wealth platform. Expect CohnReznick to keep buying (third deal of 2026) and to plug more RIAs/CPA firms into these two platforms.
- **Sentiment:** the PE-into-accounting and RIA-M&A waves are both at record pace; consolidation is the consensus trade. CohnReznick/Apax are mid-tier players moving toward a national buy-and-build exit.
- **Risks / counterintuitive 2nd order:** (1) **valuation/leverage risk** — these platforms are being assembled on PE leverage at full multiples; a rate or deal-market reversal strands them. (2) **The "flip era"** — PE sponsors are increasingly selling PE-backed firms to *other* PE/continuation vehicles (EisnerAmper's recap), so the real question shifts from "is accounting+wealth a good business" to **"who holds the bag when the multiple-arbitrage music stops and these roll-ups must prove organic growth, not just acquired revenue."** (3) Regulatory/independence scrutiny of auditors owned by financial sponsors (PCAOB/SEC attention) is a structural overhang on the whole trend.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits at the intersection of two consolidating sectors: PE-backed accounting-firm roll-ups and RIA/wealth-management M&A. CohnReznick (FY25 net revenue $1.12bn) is itself a PE platform — Apax Funds closed as majority owner of its non-attest business, a deal "three years in the making" (per CPA Trendlines, Mar 2026). Accounting M&A hit a "post-consolidation" era in 2026 as PE-backed platforms rewrite the Top-100 rankings; PE is now embedded across the upper tier (Blackstone/Citrin Cooperman, New Mountain/Grant Thornton). RIA M&A is equally hot: 225 RIA deals and ~$100.9bn acquired AUM announced through Sep 2026, already past all of 2025's 193 deals (per FINTRX/AdvizorPro). "Why now": PE dry powder plus an aging partner base needing liquidity drives accounting roll-ups; the specific driver here is tax/wealth convergence — accounting-heavy RIAs (e.g. Mercer, Creative Planning targeting firms like Glass Jacobson) let buyers cross-sell "connected" tax + wealth to the same business-owner client. CohnReznick explicitly frames Aldrich Wealth as "the platform for CohnReznick's wealth management business" — a de-novo revenue leg, not a tuck-in.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for the CPA side — net revenue, revenue-per-partner, headcount (Aldrich: $103.3M FY25 net revenue, 500+ professionals, 48 partners); for the wealth side — AUM/AUA, % recurring (AUM-fee) revenue, organic net flows. Basis of competition is distribution + talent retention, not price: deals are won on cultural fit and partner lock-ups. Recent peer moves: CohnReznick has serially expanded in the past year (Houston/HL&B, Minneapolis/Smith Schafer, Richmond/PIASCIK, plus SF, Tampa, Tallahassee offices) — a classic post-PE buy-and-build cadence. Protagonist position: a scaling national platform building West-Coast density (Aldrich = one of the 10 largest West-Coast-HQ'd firms), catching up to larger PE-backed peers on the attest side while entering wealth management from a standing start (analysis). Moat is intangibles + switching costs: entrenched business-owner relationships at Aldrich (50+ year history) that are costly to migrate — the moat the deal buys.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal price undisclosed → transaction multiples = no data; no EV, EBITDA, or AUA-fee revenue published. Framing comps from live RIA data (2026): $1B+ platform RIAs ~11–16x adjusted EBITDA; $5B+ scaled platforms high-teens to 25x+, e.g. Cerity Partners ~24x (Feb 2026); median 2025 RIA at 11.6x EBITDA (Advisor Growth Strategies — note AGS is also advising CohnReznick here). On an AUM proxy: small RIAs trade ~1.5–2.7x recurring revenue; for $8bn AUA a crude 1% fee yield would imply ~$80M fee revenue, but the actual fee take and margin are undisclosed → multiple not computable, arithmetic withheld to avoid a fabricated figure [UNSOURCED]. Internal comps (accounting/wealth convergence, advisor tooling): [[Fiserv acquires Smith Consulting Group for advisory services]], [[Jump to acquire advisor transcription firm Mobile Assistant]], [[Grantd acquires StockOpter equity-planning platform]], [[Franklin Templeton completes Apera Asset Management deal]] — all undisclosed or non-comparable in size, so distribution not computed; qualitative comparison only. In-line vs sector on direction (consolidation), unquantifiable on price.

**Risk flags.**
1. Auditor-independence / conflict (regulatory). CohnReznick is PE-owned via an alternative practice structure (attest stays CPA-owned; non-attest + now wealth sit with the Apax-backed entity). The SEC has flagged PE ownership as a "significant threat" to auditor independence, and AICPA's ethics committee circulated a second APS-independence draft (Dec 2025). Bolting an $8bn RIA onto the non-attest side deepens shared-personnel / co-located / compensation-linkage frictions — a live regulatory overhang, not a settled structure.
2. Wealth/tax cross-sell conflict. Steering audit or tax clients into an affiliated RIA raises fiduciary and self-dealing optics; the "connected services" thesis that justifies the deal is the same thing that creates the conflict, inviting scrutiny of fee stacking.
3. Integration / retention execution. Value is in Aldrich's 48 partners and 500+ staff and the 50-year client book; post-PE roll-ups routinely lose partners after lock-ups, and a West-Coast firm absorbed into an East-Coast platform risks culture/attrition that would silently erode the AUA and fee base the price presumably rests on.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is consolidation with a twist — a PE-backed CPA platform using M&A to manufacture a wealth-management business from zero, turning the accounting firm into a cross-sell distribution channel for AUM (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: expect more CPA-platform-buys-RIA deals as the dominant 2027 structure. Trigger to watch: AICPA/SEC finalizing APS independence rules (could cap how tightly attest and wealth can be integrated) and whether Aldrich's AUA/partners hold 12–18 months post-close. Thesis breaks if regulators ring-fence attest firms from affiliated wealth arms, or if post-deal partner attrition shows the roll-up destroys the very relationships it paid for.
