# French fintech 3Pay acquires MoneyTrack assets alongside EUR1.5M raise

3Pay rachète MoneyTrack et veut changer d’échelle
 Et si nous n’avions plus besoin d'avancer nos frais de santé avant d'être remboursés ? C'est le pari de 3Pay. Depuis sa création en 2020, cette fintech française s'est donné pour mission de simplifier les paiements entre les assurés, les professionnels de santé et les assureurs. Et pour passer à la vitesse supérieure, elle vient de mettre la main sur sa première acquisition : les actifs et les équipes de MoneyTrack, une autre fintech française spécialisée dans le contrôle et la sécurisation des paiements. 
 Le montant de l'opération n'a pas été dévoilé. Mais pour 3Pay, dirigée par Jérémy Benhaïm, ce rapprochement doit lui permettre de muscler sa technologie, tout en ouvrant la porte à de nouveaux marchés au-delà de l'assurance santé. Dans le même temps, l'opération s'accompagne d’une levée de fonds d’1,5 million d'euros, la seconde levée depuis sa création.  
 Une carte de paiement pour ne plus avancer ses frais de santé 
 Encore relativement discrète dans l'écosystème français de la fintech, 3Pay a été fondée en juillet 2020 à Lyon par Jérémy Benhaïm et ses associés. C'est lors d'un Executive MBA à l'ESSEC, en 2018, qu'il rencontre ses futurs associés et commence à travailler sur le projet. Ensemble, ils interrogent plus de 500 personnes pour identifier les difficultés rencontrées dans le paiement et le remboursement des soins. 
 De ces échanges naît 3Pay. Le principe est de permettre aux patients de payer leurs consultations ou leurs soins sans avoir à avancer les sommes prises en charge par leur assurance. Pour cela, 3Pay a développé une carte de paiement dédiée aux dépenses de santé. Adossée au réseau Mastercard et disponible en version physique ou virtuelle, elle peut être utilisée chez les professionnels de santé disposant d'un terminal de paiement électronique. Fini l'avance de frais suivie de remboursements parfois interminables. 
 C'est là que MoneyTrack entre en jeu. Fondée en 2018 par Christophe Doré avec le soutien du fonds Truffle Capital , l'entreprise a développé une technologie capable de vérifier qu'un paiement respecte certaines conditions avant même qu'il soit effectué. Elle s'est notamment positionnée sur le secteur de l'assurance et avait racheté, en 2021, la société marseillaise Progexia, spécialisée dans le traitement des flux tiers payants  destinés aux complémentaires santé. 
 «MoneyTrack travaille aujourd'hui avec quatre mutuelles, principalement en outre-mer, et gère les données de plus de 300 000 bénéficiaires», ajoute Jérémy Benhaïm. Les neuf salariés de l'entreprise sont repris dans le cadre de l'opération. 
 En intégrant cette technologie, 3Pay veut aller encore plus loin. Désormais, l'objectif est de pouvoir vérifier en temps réel les garanties de l'assuré avant de déclencher un paiement. En clair, savoir immédiatement si une dépense est bien couverte par son contrat. Un moyen également de repérer en amont les anomalies ou les actes fictifs, plutôt que de les découvrir une fois les sommes versées. «Avec MoneyTrack, nous changeons d'échelle. Notre conviction est simple : dans l'assurance, on doit contrôler avant de payer, pas après. C'est ce qui permet à la fois de lutter efficacement contre la fraude et d'offrir aux assurés une expérience sans avance de frais» , précise le fondateur de 46 ans. 
 3Pay au-delà de la santé 
 La startup ne compte pas s'arrêter aux dépenses de santé. Avec cette acquisition, la fintech entend aussi faire ses premiers pas sur d'autres marchés de l'assurance. Elle annonce ainsi le lancement de Tyris, une nouvelle offre destinée à l'assurance automobile et habitation. 
 L'idée reste de vérifier qu'une dépense entre bien dans le cadre d'une prise en charge avant de débloquer les fonds. Par exemple, dans le cas d'un sinistre automobile ou d'un dégât des eaux, ce type de technologie pourrait permettre de mieux encadrer le règlement des réparations. 
 Pour mener à bien ses ambitions, 3Pay s'appuie sur plusieurs investisseurs, parmi lesquels Truffle Capital, Accurafy et Side Angels. La fintech compte désormais poursuivre son développement en France, mais aussi regarder au-delà des frontières. «Nous souhaitons notamment nous développer en Espagne, en Italie, en Belgique et au Luxembourg. Nous sommes également en discussions très avancées aux Émirats arabes unis» , confie l'entrepreneur.

_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Lyon-based healthtech-payments fintech **3Pay** (founded July 2020 by Jérémy Benhaïm) took **control of MoneyTrack** (controlled/"directed" payments, founded 2018 by Christophe Doré, backed by Truffle Capital). The maddyness PR frames it as "acquiring the assets and teams"; the sharper trade report (mind Fintech) says 3Pay **took control for ~€2.2M in shares (titres), not cash**. The combined entity is valued at **€6.2M** (CEO figure, not an external mark). A separate **€1.5M cash raise** (3Pay's 2nd ever) runs alongside. MoneyTrack's 9 staff are retained; it serves **4 mutuelles (mostly French overseas territories), ~300,000 beneficiaries**. 3Pay also announced **Tyris**, a new offer extending the "verify before you pay" logic to auto/home insurance.
**Why structured this way / what it reveals:** the acquisition is paid in **paper, not money** — the €1.5M cash is kept for operations, and MoneyTrack holders are rolled into 3Pay equity. At a combined €6.2M post-deal valuation with MoneyTrack's control stake at €2.2M, this is a **small, equity-financed absorption of a sub-scale asset**, not a cash exit (analysis). The €6.2M combined figure is a **founder-stated valuation**, so treat it as PR-anchored, not market-tested.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Two distinct competitive sets fused in one deal. (a) **Health-payment / tiers-payant modernization** in France: incumbent tiers-payant plumbing (Viamedis, Almerys, iSanté/CLC-style operators) plus assurtechs modernizing the flow (per argusdelassurance coverage). 3Pay's Mastercard-rail card bypasses classic tiers-payant integration — genuinely lighter to deploy, but it is one of several "no-advance-of-fees" plays. (b) **Controlled/earmarked payments** (MoneyTrack's turf): spending-conditioned disbursement rails — adjacent to purpose-bound money / voucher-and-benefits fintech globally.
**Why the lay of the land / second order:** in French health payments the margin sits with whoever **owns the real-time eligibility/garanties check**, not with the card. 3Pay buying MoneyTrack is a bet that **pre-payment control** (anti-fraud + eligibility) is the defensible layer; the card is commodity Mastercard rails (analysis). Against larger tiers-payant incumbents 3Pay is still **catching up on distribution** (4 mutuelles is tiny), so the thesis depends on winning mutuelle/insurer contracts, not on technology novelty.

## [2] Company history / fit
3Pay: founded 2020 in Lyon out of an ESSEC EMBA project; Mastercard-backed physical/virtual card for health spend; this is its **1st acquisition** and **2nd raise** — i.e. still early-stage and small. MoneyTrack: founded 2018 with Truffle Capital; raised **€2.3M in 2021** and itself **acquired Progexia** (Marseille, tiers-payant flow processing) that year; investors included Truffle, Accurafy, AG2R La Mondiale.
**Why the company acts this way:** 3Pay sells a card (commodity take-rate) and needed a **control/fraud-prevention software layer** to move from "no-advance-of-fees UX" to "verify coverage before paying" — a higher-value, stickier position with insurers. **Why it's cheap:** MoneyTrack raised €2.3M+ and did an acquisition in 2021, yet in 2026 it folds into a €6.2M-combined entity for a €2.2M paper stake — strongly suggesting MoneyTrack was **sub-scale / capital-constrained** and this is closer to a **rescue/consolidation in paper** than a premium strategic buy (hypothesis, consistent with the all-share structure).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
The genuinely useful delta is **real-time pre-payment eligibility + fraud detection** ("control before you pay, not after") fused with a consumer no-advance card — combining UX and control in one stack is relatively uncommon in French health payments. But **traction is thin and mostly inherited**: ~300k beneficiaries and 4 mutuelles come from MoneyTrack, concentrated in overseas territories; 3Pay's own card adoption numbers are not disclosed. **Tyris (auto/home)** is **announced, not live** — no clients, UX or numbers.
**Why value-add is real/not, deeper:** the margin-capturing layer is the **eligibility/anti-fraud engine**, where 3Pay now owns tech — but the economic value only materializes if **insurers pay for fraud reduction / tiers-payant savings**, and none of that is quantified (no fraud-loss-avoided, no per-claim economics). The card itself rides Mastercard, so **the rails margin is captured by Mastercard + acquirer**, not 3Pay (analysis). Net: real capability, unproven monetization, inherited rather than organic traction.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Plans: deepen France, launch **Tyris** for auto/home claims-payment control, expand to **Spain, Italy, Belgium, Luxembourg**, and reportedly **"advanced discussions" in the UAE**. Backers named: Truffle Capital, Accurafy, Side Angels.
**Why / second order:** geographic + cross-line expansion from a **€6.2M-valued, 2nd-raise company with ~€1.5M fresh cash** is ambitious relative to resources — the UAE/Southern-Europe talk reads as **direction, not funded roadmap** (analysis). Structurally, French tiers-payant modernization and insurance anti-fraud are real tailwinds, but this is a **small consolidation**, not a category-defining event; the counterintuitive risk is that an **all-paper merger of two sub-scale players** concentrates execution risk rather than de-risking either. Regulatory backdrop (health-data handling of 300k beneficiaries, insurance payment rules) adds compliance load for a thin team of ~9 + founders.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits at the intersection of French healthtech-payments (the *tiers payant santé* / third-party-payment rails) and insurance anti-fraud / "verify-before-you-pay" tooling. Size: the French insurance TPA (third-party administrator) market was ~$19.3bn (2023), projected to ~$32.2bn by 2030 at ~6.5% CAGR (per Next Move Strategy Consulting, as of 2024); the broader France insurtech market is small but fast — ~$0.43bn in 2025 toward ~$5.5bn by 2034 at ~31.6% CAGR (per IMARC, as of 2025) — attribute cautiously (secondary analyst reports, not primary). "Why now": French anti-fraud policy is actively tightening around *control-before-payment*. Assurance Maladie detected €628m of fraud in 2024, with true annual fraud estimated at €1.4–1.9bn (per Selectra/IGAS reporting, 2025–26); healthcare professionals drive ~69% of amounts (IGAS-IGF report, Aug 2026). The 2026 anti-fraud strategy conditions *tiers payant* on Vitale-card verification and legalises Sécu↔complementary-insurer data-sharing (validated by Conseil constitutionnel, 18 Jun 2026) — a direct regulatory tailwind for pre-payment verification tech like MoneyTrack's. Structure: fragmented — many sub-scale French fintechs orbiting mutuelles and complémentaires; value is captured by whoever owns the real-time eligibility/control layer between insurer, provider and patient. Barriers: regulatory (health-data/GDPR, payment licensing via Mastercard rails), plus relationships with mutuelles.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs run on: payment volume processed, number of covered beneficiaries, mutuelle/insurer partnerships, and fraud-detection hit-rate (none disclosed by 3Pay beyond MoneyTrack's "4 mutuelles, 300k+ beneficiaries"). Players: broad incumbents are the large French acquirers/processors (Worldline) and health-data/tiers-payant operators (e.g. Almerys, Viamedis, iSanté-type flux operators); disruptors are niche control-payment fintechs. Basis of competition: product (real-time garantie verification) + distribution (mutuelle contracts), not price. Recent moves: 3Pay's acquisition of MoneyTrack (announced 16 Oct 2026) is its first M&A and second raise (€1.5m) since 2020 launch; it simultaneously launched *Tyris* for auto/home insurance — an adjacency bet beyond health. Protagonist position: niche/sub-scale challenger (analysis) — €6.2m combined valuation per CEO Benhaïm is pre-scale. Moat (analysis): the thin edge is the controlled-payment/earmarked-funds technology (verify-before-pay) + the inherited mutuelle relationships and 300k beneficiary dataset — switching-cost/intangible, not yet network-effect; easily replicable by larger processors if the regulatory tailwind makes the niche attractive.

**Comps & multiples.** This is a sub-€10m venture-stage deal, not a public comp — public EV/Revenue/EBITDA arithmetic is not applicable (no disclosed revenue; valuation is a round/deal mark, not market cap). Deal marks: combined post-deal valuation **€6.2m** (per CEO, via Maddyness); the MoneyTrack leg was reported at **€2.2m in shares** by mind Fintech (and Dealroom), *contradicting* the Maddyness line that "the amount was not disclosed" — treat "undisclosed" as PR framing. Raise **€1.5m** (second round). No revenue/TPV published → revenue multiple = no data. Internal comps (grep fallback; semsearch 402/down) — French/EU payments-&-insurance M&A and venture precedent in-base: [[Shares completes Treezor acquisition from Societe Generale]] (French BaaS carve-out, far larger scale — directional only), [[French neobank Shine expands across Europe after SocGen sale]] (French fintech consolidation around incumbents), [[Seven European fintech deals raise EUR 95 million in a week]] (contains Instanda, a UK insurtech SaaS, €17.1m venture round — closest insurtech-funding reference point). Flag: at this stage comps are qualitative only — distribution not computed (<3 comparable revenue multiples).

**Risk flags.**
1. *Sub-scale / execution risk.* €6.2m combined value, €1.5m raise and nine inherited MoneyTrack staff is thin to simultaneously integrate an acquisition, extend into auto/home (Tyris) and internationalise (Spain/Italy/Belgium/Luxembourg/UAE "advanced talks"). Over-reach across too many fronts on a small balance sheet is the dominant risk — dilutes focus on the proven health niche.
2. *Customer concentration / fragile base.* MoneyTrack's revenue proxy is "4 mutuelles, mainly in overseas territories, 300k beneficiaries" — loss of one anchor mutuelle could be material; the acquired book is narrow.
3. *Disintermediation by the rails owner.* The verify-before-pay layer is attractive precisely because regulation now favours it — which invites larger processors (Worldline) or the Sécu/mutuelle data-sharing infrastructure itself to absorb the function, capturing the margin and leaving 3Pay as a thin front-end on someone else's rails (Mastercard + mutuelle data).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is micro-consolidation, not a re-rating: a small payments fintech buying a control-payment capability to ride France's 2026 anti-fraud regulatory wave — a template others in the fragmented tiers-payant niche may copy. Falsifiable thesis: if 3Pay converts the regulatory tailwind into new mutuelle/insurer contracts beyond the inherited four within ~12 months, it validates "verify-before-pay" as a defensible wedge; trigger to watch — Tyris (auto/home) landing a first named insurer, or a follow-on raise materially above €6.2m. What breaks it: the function gets absorbed into Sécu↔mutuelle data-sharing rails or a larger processor, stranding 3Pay sub-scale.
