## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On ~2026-10-18/19 the *Financial Times* reported that Accenture (NYSE: ACN) is exploring a sale of its **Greater China** business — mainland China, Hong Kong and Taiwan — at a valuation of **up to $1.5bn**. Accenture has retained **JPMorgan** to run the process after fielding unsolicited buyer interest over the past year. No deal is confirmed, and Accenture may **retain a significant minority stake** to preserve client continuity. ([ft.com](https://www.ft.com/content/2dfdd513-26a6-4a10-9cae-5242eb4ee3ed), [kfgo/Reuters](https://kfgo.com/2026/10/18/accenture-looks-to-sell-chinese-operations-for-up-to-1-5-billion-ft-reports/), [cityam.com](https://www.cityam.com/accenture-eyeing-sale-of-chinese-operations-for-up-to-1-5bn/))

**[1] Why it matters / scale.** Accenture reported **$74.2bn** FY2026 revenue (~814k staff as of 2026-08-31). Greater China sits inside the **Asia Pacific** segment, which booked **$10.68bn** in FY2026 — so the unit is a small slice of group revenue, and a $1.5bn headline valuation is a rounding error vs. Accenture's market cap. The signal, not the size, is what matters: a bellwether global integrator stepping back from the world's second-largest economy. ([Accenture FY26 earnings](https://investor.accenture.com/~/media/Files/A/accenture-v4/investors/earnings-reports/2026/accentures-fourth-quarter-fiscal-2026-earnings-release.pdf), [newsroom.accenture.com fact sheet](https://newsroom.accenture.com/fact-sheet))

**[2] Drivers.** (a) **Geopolitics/data rules** — since 2023 Beijing has tightened cross-border data-security and counter-espionage rules, barring Western consultants from advising many state-owned enterprises and shrinking the addressable market. (b) **Capital reallocation** — proceeds can fund AI-capability M&A, buybacks/dividends; Accenture is positioning as the "reinvention partner of choice" for AI. (c) **AI demand concern** — sale coincides with investor worry that GenAI compresses traditional consulting demand (ACN logged 104 YTD bookings ≥$100m, +13%, but ran an **$865m restructuring** and cut ~11,000 staff). ([thestreet.com](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/accenture-china-consulting-unit-sale), [tradersunion.com](https://tradersunion.com/news/companies/show/3862985-accenture-china-sale-ai-shift/), [cxtoday.com](https://www.cxtoday.com/contact-center/accenture-lays-off-11000-staff-as-part-of-ai-reskilling-strategy/))

**[3] Buyers / structure.** Process likely to draw **private-equity** firms and a limited set of **strategic buyers**; a management-buyout/local-partner "localization" structure (common exit route for Western firms in China) is plausible given data-sovereignty pressure. Minority-stake retention would echo how peers de-risk China exposure while keeping delivery capacity. ([equalocean.com](https://equalocean.com/news/2026101922308-accenture-explores-china-business-sale-up-us-1-5-billion-ft-reports), [gurufocus.com](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9129142/accenture-acn-considers-15b-sale-of-greater-china-unit-amid-ai-concerns))

**[4] Internal prior art (vault).** Accenture coverage in-vault is expansion/partnership-led, making this reversal notable: [[WSJ Google Cloud and Accenture build joint AI deployment team]] (2026-10-12, AI delivery push); [[Accenture signs $4.18B cybersecurity deal]] (2026-06, services scale-up); [[Accenture acquires Malaysia consultancy Aristal]] (2025-07, APAC bolt-on). No prior vault note covers the China divestiture — this is the first.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team (second-order):**

1. Is this a confirmed sale? **No** — FT reports *exploration*; JPMorgan is reviewing options, no binding deal or buyer named. Headline "plans to sell" overstates it.
2. Single-source risk? Largely **FT-originated**; Reuters/CityAM/EqualOcean/Bloomberg-adjacent outlets all trace back to the FT scoop. Treat $1.5bn as one sourced estimate, not a struck price. **Open** until confirmed.
3. Is $1.5bn gross proceeds or equity value net of a retained minority stake? **Open** — "up to $1.5bn" is a valuation ceiling; a retained stake means Accenture pockets less cash.
4. What's Greater China's actual revenue/EBIT? **Open** — not separately disclosed; buried in APAC's $10.68bn FY26. Can't verify the implied multiple.
5. Could this be a trial balloon to gauge interest / pressure a price, not a committed exit? **Plausible** — unsolicited interest + banker appointment is classic price discovery.
6. Does AI-demand fear actually drive this, or is it narrative overlay? **Partly overlay** — China data/geopolitics is the harder constraint; AI is the investor-facing framing. Both cited.
7. Regulatory approval risk? A sale to PE or local buyer still needs Chinese regulatory sign-off; cross-border data and foreign-investment rules could delay/block. **Open.**
8. Who actually buys a shrinking, data-constrained Western consulting unit? Buyer universe is thin — local strategics or a localization/MBO; PE appetite is uncertain. **Open.**
9. Client-continuity/talent flight — do MNC clients and senior consultants stay through an ownership change? Key value driver, **unverified**.
10. Precedent: how did peers (McKinsey, BCG, Big Four) handle China pressure? Mostly localized/ring-fenced rather than sold outright — an outright sale would be more aggressive. **Open.**
11. Timing vs. earnings — announced after FY26 results amid $865m restructuring and ~11k cuts; is this portfolio-cleanup optics for investors? **Plausible.**
12. Does exiting China cede ground to local integrators and cloud/AI rivals long-term? Strategic cost not quantified. **Open.**
13. FX/repatriation — can sale proceeds be moved out of China cleanly under capital controls? **Open**, material to the "fund buybacks" thesis.
14. Does group guidance change? No indication Greater China is material enough to move FY27 guidance — supports "signal > size" read. **Likely immaterial financially.**
15. Reversibility — does a minority stake let Accenture re-enter if conditions improve, or is this a one-way door? **Open.**

**Importance: 3/5** — Globally salient signal (bellwether integrator retreating from China amid geopolitics + AI-demand anxiety) and widely reported, but financially immaterial to Accenture, unconfirmed/exploratory, and single-source (FT). Watch-worthy, not decisive.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** IT services / management consulting. The global management-consulting market is large and fragmented (dozens of global players + regional firms), with value created in people-leverage (billable headcount × rate) rather than capital/IP — low entry barriers at the small end, scale/brand/relationships as barriers at the top. Accenture's FY2026 revenue was ~$74bn, of which Asia-Pacific contributed $10.7bn, or ~14% (per Accenture Fact Sheet FY2026, via stockanalysis/newsroom, as of fiscal year-end Aug 2026). Greater China (mainland + Hong Kong + Taiwan) sits inside that APAC bucket and is NOT broken out separately — public filings group it into Asia-Pacific (per SEC 10-K FY2026). "Why now": the move fits a documented secular retreat of Western tech/consulting from China under geopolitical pressure — IBM ceased its main China R&D entity from March 2025 (~1,800 staff), after its China revenue fell ~20% in 2023 and its China consulting client count dropped ~45% over two years (per Yahoo Finance/IndexBox, 2025). Not uniform: Deloitte got regulatory approval in May 2025 to expand a China consulting entity — so this is Accenture-specific/strategic, not a blanket sector exit (analysis).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: revenue/headcount, utilization, bill rate, bookings/book-to-bill, and geographic mix. Key players in China IT consulting: global firms (Accenture, IBM Consulting, Deloitte, Capgemini, PwC) vs. increasingly favored local vendors (localization/data-sovereignty tailwinds for domestic players). Basis of competition: relationships, delivery scale, data-residency compliance, and increasingly price vs. local incumbents. Recent moves: IBM exited China R&D (Mar 2025); Deloitte expanded (May 2025); Accenture retained JPMorgan for a strategic review, prompted by unsolicited approaches over the prior ~12 months (per FT, via Reuters/Benzinga, Oct 2026). Accenture's position: a global leader retreating from a sub-scale, politically exposed geography — reportedly weighing keeping a minority stake to avoid disrupting client service (per FT, Oct 2026), i.e. a managed carve-out rather than a clean exit (analysis). Moat is global scale/brand/intangibles — none of which is China-specific, which is precisely why the unit is severable.

**Comps & multiples.** Accenture (NYSE: ACN), as of ~20 Oct 2026: market cap ~$142bn (range $130–145bn across sources), P/E ~17x, EV/Revenue ~1.6x on ~$74bn revenue (per stockanalysis.com / multiples.vc / gurufocus, Oct 2026). Deal sizing: the China unit is reported at up to $1.5bn (per FT, Oct 2026). Against an APAC segment generating ~$10.7bn and a Greater-China sub-slice of unknown exact size, $1.5bn is a small fraction of ACN's ~$142bn cap — ~1% of market cap (analysis: $1.5bn / $142bn = ~1.1%); a non-material disposal financially, material strategically. Cannot compute an EV/Revenue on the unit itself — Greater China revenue is not disclosed → "no data" on the deal multiple. Internal comps (base): [[Accenture signs $4.18B cybersecurity deal]] (bolt-on expansion, Jun 2026), [[Accenture acquires Malaysia consultancy Aristal]] (APAC M&A, Jul 2025), [[WSJ: Google Cloud and Accenture build joint AI deployment team]] (AI pivot, Oct 2026) — the pattern: buying/partnering into AI and higher-value geographies while trimming China. ACN's ~1.6x EV/Rev and ~17x P/E are in-line/cheap for a mega-cap IT-services leader (analysis), consistent with slow-growth, labor-leveraged economics rather than a re-rating story.

**Risk flags.** (1) Deal is reported/exploratory — a strategic review with JPMorgan, NOT a signed agreement; may not result in a transaction (per FT). (2) Minority-retention structure (if used) leaves residual China exposure and governance entanglement — a partial de-risking, not a clean break (analysis). (3) Geopolitical/execution risk: buyer universe is likely local given Western-buyer scrutiny, which can cap price and complicate IP/data handoff and client continuity (analysis). (4) Disclosure opacity: China isn't segmented, so neither the unit's revenue nor its margin drag is verifiable — "no data" on whether the exit is accretive to group margins.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Reinforces a two-speed thesis for global IT services: capital/attention rotating toward AI-deployment and compliant growth geographies (Accenture–Google Cloud, the cyber deal) while politically exposed China is carved out or minority-held. Falsifiable: if the review closes with a clean majority sale to a local buyer at or near $1.5bn, the "managed, minority-retained exit" read is wrong. Watch next: a definitive 8-K/press release, deal structure (clean sale vs. minority retention), and any restated APAC segment guidance.
