# MNT-Halan pledges $10M in AI spending by 2027

MNT-Halan expects its investments in artificial intelligence to surpass US$10 million by 2027, reflecting a broader push to embed AI across its operations. The Egyptian fintech unicorn initially allocated around US$10 million to AI, but current projections suggest spending will exceed that figure as integration deepens. The plan highlights the rising computing requirements and efficiency ambitions now shaping financial technology.

* * *

## Expanding AI Investment Commitments

Ahmed Mohsen, MNT-Halan's Co-Founder and Chief Technology Officer, told Al Borsa that the company originally earmarked around US$10 million for artificial intelligence. Current estimates indicate that spending will surpass this amount as early as 2027, driven by wider integration of AI technologies across the organization. The shift reflects a strategic decision to prioritize intelligent automation and data-driven services.

## Leasing GPUs to Support Growth

MNT-Halan plans to direct most of its technology spending toward leasing graphics processing units, or GPUs, rather than purchasing them. This approach provides access to the high-performance computing power required to develop and run AI solutions without tying up capital in hardware ownership. Leasing also gives the company flexibility to adjust computing capacity as its AI portfolio expands.

## Efficiency Gains and Scalability

The company expects its AI investments to generate operational returns by reducing workloads and improving overall efficiency. These improvements could allow MNT-Halan to handle significantly higher business volumes without a proportional increase in costs or operational demands. The ability to scale transactions and customer interactions more efficiently is central to the company's growth strategy.

## Market Context and Recent Listing

The investment plan follows MNT-Halan's listing on the Egyptian Exchange under the ticker symbol HALN.CA. The initial public offering attracted strong demand from institutional and retail investors, with the public tranche oversubscribed 43.6 times and drawing more than 41,500 subscription orders. The private tranche recorded oversubscription of 11.2 times and 19.4 times, excluding cornerstone investors, while the offering covered 320 million ordinary shares representing 20% of issued share capital.

## Strategic Importance of AI in Fintech

The planned spending signals the growing importance of computing infrastructure in the financial technology sector. As fintech companies process more transactions and serve more customers, AI can help manage additional workloads with greater speed and accuracy. MNT-Halan's approach shows how specialized computing resources are becoming a critical input for modern financial services.

## What to Watch

A key milestone will be whether MNT-Halan's AI spending exceeds its initial US$10 million allocation in 2027. Market participants will also monitor how the company balances GPU leasing costs with the efficiency gains generated by AI applications. These factors could shape how other fintech firms plan their own infrastructure and technology investments.

* * *

MNT-Halan's AI roadmap reflects the growing role of advanced computing in the financial services industry. By prioritizing leased GPU capacity and targeting efficiency gains, the company aims to expand without proportionate cost growth. The success of this strategy will depend on effective integration, disciplined investment, and the ability to convert technology spending into measurable operational returns.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** MNT-Halan sits at the intersection of emerging-market microlending and the MENA/Africa fintech super-app. The $10m AI spend is small relative to the business it is layering onto: for FY2025 the listed Egyptian entity posted adjusted revenue of EGP 20.5bn (~$376.7m) and net income of EGP 1.98bn (~$36.4m), up 44% YoY (per Thndr/launchbaseafrica, Oct 2026). The company holds ~24.3% of Egypt's non-bank microfinance market by loan book and ~14% of non-bank consumer finance by disbursements at end-2025 (per launchbaseafrica, 2026-10). "Why now": the AI pledge lands days after the Oct-2026 EGX listing (biggest Egyptian IPO since 2021) — a de-PR read is that this is an investor-facing efficiency narrative ("scale volumes without proportional cost"), not a disclosed cost-savings commitment; CTO Ahmed Mohsen framed it as GPU leasing, i.e. opex not capex. The macro backdrop is harsh: CBE overnight deposit rate at 19% and urban inflation ~14.5% in Aug 2026 (per launchbaseafrica) — the real constraint on this lender is cost of funds, not AI.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for an EM microlender/super-app: active loan book, active customers, net interest margin / cost of funds spread, NPL ratio, and revenue mix (lending vs securitization vs payments). MNT-Halan: active loan book EGP 46.7bn (~$891–947m) and ~1.9m active customers at 2026-06-30; >$11bn disbursed and >8m customers cumulatively since launch (per launchbaseafrica/Thndr, 2026). Peers: Fawry (EGX-listed, mcap ~$1.2bn as of 2026-10-05, payments-led, 55.4m monthly users) competes on distribution/payments rails; Nigeria's OPay (filed NYSE S-1 Oct-2026, seeking ~$4bn; H1'26 revenue $467.1m +137%, net income ~$90.9m, lending now its largest segment) is the clearest super-app-plus-lending comp but at ~2x the revenue and far faster growth. MNT-Halan's position: regional leader in Egyptian microfinance (ahead domestically), but catching up on scale/growth versus OPay. **Risk flags.**
1. **Cost-of-funds squeeze (margin).** Subsidiaries owed banks EGP 31.69bn (~$605m) at end-Jun-2026, ~two-thirds of the loan book, against a 19% policy rate; net profit already fell from EGP 2.27bn (2023) to EGP 1.37bn (2024) as borrowing costs rose faster than loan yields before recovering in 2025 (per launchbaseafrica). AI efficiency does nothing for the dominant cost line — funding. 
2. **Securitisation dependence.** Securitisation was 45.5% of H1'26 revenue, up from 17.7% in 2024 (per launchbaseafrica) — a growing reliance on capital-markets access that tightens exactly when rates are high; a market freeze would hit both funding and the revenue line.
3. **Credit/legal concentration.** 124,839 active court cases (claims ~EGP 6.47bn / $123.5m), most loan-recovery in the Tasaheel microfinance arm; reported NPLs 1.5% look low versus that litigation load and provisions of only EGP 1.34bn (per launchbaseafrica) — asset-quality disclosure to watch. FX mismatch (EGP book, dollar-quoted scale) adds a second-order devaluation risk.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The AI/GPU-leasing pledge is a marginal efficiency story dressed as a strategic one; the real investment thesis post-IPO is whether a securitisation-funded microlender can defend net interest margin through Egypt's high-rate cycle. Falsifiable thesis: if the OPay NYSE listing prices well, it re-rates the EM-lending-super-app category and pulls MNT-Halan's ~2x P/S upward; trigger = OPay pricing (filed Oct-2026). Thesis breaks if NPLs drift above the reported 1.5% or securitisation spreads widen — then the $10m AI line is irrelevant noise against a funding-cost story.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Financial backdrop (not an earnings event).** This item is a technology-spend pledge — Co-Founder/CTO Ahmed Mohsen told Al Borsa MNT-Halan expects AI investment to surpass US$10M by 2027, mostly via leased GPUs rather than owned hardware. There are no quarterly results attached. The relevant "earnings" lens is the financials MNT-Halan disclosed through its October 2026 EGX IPO (ticker HALN.CA, listed Egyptian entity), which are the first detailed public figures for the group. MNT-Halan does not publish audited investor-relations earnings on a quarterly cadence (IR materials are stale, latest dated 2024-12), so the figures below are drawn from IPO-prospectus-based reporting, not a filed earnings release. Verdict: **no-data** on an earnings basis.

**Latest reported financials (FY2025, Egyptian entity, IPO disclosures):**
- Adjusted revenue: EGP 20.5bn (~US$377M) for FY2025.
- Net income: EGP 1.98bn (~US$37M), up ~44% YoY. Note a bumpy profit path — net income was EGP 2.27bn (2023) → EGP 1.37bn (2024, squeezed as funding costs rose faster than loan yields) → EGP 1.98bn (2025); so the 2025 result is a recovery, still below the 2023 peak. ([Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/10/15/the-hidden-costs-of-mnt-halans-mass-market-lending-model/))
- Gross loan book: EGP 46.7bn (~US$0.9bn) at 30 June 2026, roughly doubled from EGP 23.0bn at end-2023.
- Active customers: ~1.9M; holds a 24.3% share of Egypt's non-bank microfinance market by loan-book value and ~14% of non-bank consumer-finance disbursements (end-2025). ([Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/10/20/mnt-halan-ipo-2/))

**IPO terms (Oct 2026):**
- Offer price EGP 24.50/share; 320M shares (~20% of capital) for EGP 7.84bn (~US$149–150M) in proceeds — Egypt's largest IPO since 2021.
- Public tranche oversubscribed 43.6x (~EGP 126bn / US$2.3bn demand, 41,500+ orders); private tranche 11.2x (19.4x ex-cornerstone).
- Listed Egyptian entity valued ~EGP 39–42bn (~US$745–770M) — NOT ~US$4bn. The broader MNT-Halan parent (Egypt, Turkey, UAE, Pakistan) is valued ~US$1.4bn (June 2026 Al Ahly Capital-led round). Shares rose ~6.6%–18% on debut depending on the measure. ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/capital-markets/egypt-fintech-firm-mnt-halan-rises-6.6-on-debut-after-biggest-ipo-since-2021-1794118))
- Offer priced at ~20x 2025 net income / ~1.9x adjusted revenue; a ~EGP 4bn capital increase is planned that would dilute existing holders ~9% within about a month of listing. ([Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/10/15/the-hidden-costs-of-mnt-halans-mass-market-lending-model/))

**Thesis flags:**
- *Asset quality (Egyptian microlending).* Management reports NPLs below ~2.5% (1.5% of current portfolio overdue), but reporting flags 124,839 active court cases (~EGP 6.47bn / US$124M in claims across Tasaheel, Halan Consumer Finance, Mashroey). Credit-loss provisions of EGP 1.34bn (~47% above the EGP 908M regulatory minimum) cover only ~one-fifth of disputed claim value — a key sensitivity for a mass-market underbanked borrower base.
- *FX / EGP and rate risk.* Figures are EGP-denominated with a volatile pound; the central-bank overnight rate is ~19% and inflation ~14.5% (Aug 2026, down from 30%+ in 2023). Roughly two-thirds of the loan book is funded by bank debt (EGP 31.69bn / ~US$605M from CIB, HSBC Egypt, QNB, etc.); existing fixed-rate loans can't be repriced before maturity, so projections assume falling rates. Securitization generated 45.5% of H1-2026 revenue (vs 17.7% in 2024), raising reliance on loan sales.
- *Is $10M AI spend material?* No. ~US$10M of cumulative AI spend through 2027 is immaterial against ~US$377M FY2025 revenue (<3%) and ~US$37M net income; the GPU-leasing choice deliberately avoids capex. It is a strategic/efficiency signal, not a P&L swing factor.
