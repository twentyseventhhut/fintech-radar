## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
SBP's FY26 Payment Systems Review (year to June 2026) reports QR-enabled merchants grew ~245% to 3.8M, from ~1.1M a year earlier (3.5x; 3.8/1.1 ≈ 3.45x ⇒ +245% checks out). These merchants processed **297M QR transactions worth Rs ~1.6T** in FY26 — i.e. ~0.8M QR txns/day, avg daily value ~Rs 5.5B, implying an average ticket of only ~Rs 5,400 per QR transaction. **This is not organic virality — it is a subsidy print.** Growth was fuelled by the **PM's Cashless Pakistan Initiative**: a Rs 3.5B subsidy pool paying 0.5% of each eligible transaction value (capped Rs 100/txn), split between acquiring and issuing institutions.
- **Why anchor to "245% merchants"?** Merchant *count* is the easiest-to-pump headline because registering a Raast P2M QR alias is near-zero-cost — banks and the subsidy incentivise sign-ups, not usage. 3.8M merchants doing only 297M txns/year ⇒ **~78 QR txns per merchant per year (<7/month)**: most "merchants" are near-dormant. The real story is acquisition velocity, not transaction depth. (analysis)
- Context: QR sits inside the broader shift — digital carried **92% of formal retail payments** in FY26 (up from 88%), retail digital transactions **+58% YoY to 14.3B**. QR is a small-but-fast-growing sliver of that.

## [1] Competitors / peers
QR acceptance in Pakistan is almost entirely **Raast P2M** rails (SBP's instant-payment system), not card schemes. Raast carried **2,755M payments worth Rs 80.1T in FY26 (+116% volume, +171% value YoY)**; **2.6M+ merchants** on the Raast P2M alias by Mar-2026. Daily Raast P2M txns crossed 1.1M by mid-2026 vs ~60k a year prior. So QR-merchant growth is a downstream metric of the Raast P2M rollout — the SBP owns the rail, which it subsidises directly.
- Regional comp: this is Pakistan replaying **India's UPI QR** playbook (merchant QR proliferation on a central instant-payment rail) and, loosely, Brazil's Pix — but years behind in absolute scale (UPI does ~18–20B txns/*month*; Pakistan QR ~25M/month). Pakistan's distinctive lever is the **explicit per-transaction cash subsidy**, which UPI largely avoided (India used MDR-zero + ecosystem push, not a direct txn bounty). (analysis)
- Card/PoS acquirers (1Link, banks, EMIs like Barq) are the losers of margin here: SBP-mandated QR via Raast is cheaper than card PoS, compressing interchange.

## [2] Company history / fit
Consistent with SBP's multi-year digitisation arc: FY25 review reported 38% digital-payment growth (9.1B txns) [[State Bank of Pakistan reports 38% digital payments growth]]; prior to that 96M digital-banking users / Raast scaling [[Pakistan reports 96 million digital banking users]]. SBP is simultaneously pushing adjacent levers — financial-literacy targets, mandated eKYC/facial recognition, CBDC research. The QR number is the FY26 data point in the same quarterly/annual Payment Systems Review cadence.
- **Why SBP acts this way:** cash dominance (<10% formal retail was digital a few years ago) is a tax-base and monetary-control problem. The subsidy is a deliberate land-grab to hit critical mass before withdrawing support — classic two-sided-market seeding. The open question is durability once the Rs 3.5B pool is exhausted. (analysis)

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Not a new capability — Raast P2M QR launched earlier; this is a **new reporting period (FY26) with new figures**, so the item is *fresh* as data, not as a product launch. Traction is real on acquisition (merchant count genuinely 3.5x) but **thin on engagement** (~7 txns/merchant/month). The value-add that matters — moving cash to digital at the long tail — is only partly proven: the txn/merchant ratio and subsidy dependence are red flags that a chunk of growth is incentive-induced rather than habit.
- **Who captures the margin?** Nobody on QR itself — Raast P2M is low/zero-MDR by design and the state is *paying* participants. The payoff for SBP is systemic (formalisation, float, data), not fee revenue; for banks it's deposit/data capture, which only pays off if dormant merchants activate. (analysis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Raast is projected to process >$500B (~Rs 140T) in 2026, exceeding Pakistan's ~$452B GDP; govt plans to route all government payments through Raast. Expect continued merchant-count growth while the subsidy runs, then a **test of retention** when it tapers. Risks: (1) subsidy-cliff churn/dormancy; (2) informal-economy leakage — reports that hawala/misuse is finding its way onto the formal rails; (3) headline-metric inflation (counting aliases as "merchants"). Sentiment in local press is celebratory; the skeptical read is that merchant *count* is outrunning real usage.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Pakistan's digital-payments build-out is state-led, not merchant-pull. SBP's Payment Systems Quarterly Review (Q3 FY26) reports 3.7bn retail transactions worth Rs168.8tn in Jan–Mar 2026 (+9% QoQ), with ~92% of retail volume now digital (per SBP PS Review, via brecorder/dawn, as of Q3 FY26). The rails are Raast (instant P2P/P2M, ISO 20022) and PRISM+ (RTGS upgrade, [[Pakistan launches PRISM+ payment and settlement system upgrade]]); Raast P2M is the QR layer in this note. "Why now": SBP has mandated a unified interoperable Raast P2M QR standard and plans to route all government payments through Raast by end-FY26 (per SBP/Business Recorder, via nextgen.pk, 2026) — a top-down acceptance push, with QR as the cheapest merchant on-ramp for an unbanked/cash-heavy base. Structure: fragmented on the merchant side, but the clearing/standard layer is a regulated monopoly (SBP/Raast) — barriers are regulatory and network-effect, not capital.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for merchant QR: registered vs *active* merchants, transactions-per-merchant, and P2M TPV. Pakistan FY26: QR-enabled merchants +245% to 3.8M, processing 297M transactions worth Rs1.6tn (per SBP, via techjuice.pk, FY26). The protagonist here is the regulator itself, not a company — comparison is cross-market, not competitor-vs-competitor. Regional QR comps (mid-2026): Indonesia QRIS — 44.86M merchants, 65.77M users, ~97% MSMEs (per Bank Indonesia, via Antara); India UPI — ~65M merchants, 709M QR codes powering 59.3bn transactions in Q2 FY26 (per NPCI, via smefutures/statista). Pakistan's 3.8M merchants is early-stage versus both, and its 297M annual QR transactions sit far below India's ~60bn *quarterly* — a scale gap of two-plus orders of magnitude. Position: Pakistan is a fast-grower off a tiny base, catching up on merchant count but nowhere near on usage intensity.

**Comps & multiples.** No deal/valuation/round in this item — it is a regulator statistic, so trading multiples do not apply ("no data" by construction). Internal comps for trajectory/context: [[Pakistan reports 96 million digital banking users]] (FY26 target 120M), [[Pakistan launches PRISM+ payment and settlement system upgrade]], [[Kazakhstan launches interbank QR payment service]] (same interbank-QR playbook, neighboring market). Cross-market merchant-density check (analysis): Pakistan ~3.8M QR merchants against ~240M population ≈ 1 per 63 people; Indonesia 44.86M / ~280M ≈ 1 per 6; India ~65M / ~1.45bn ≈ 1 per 22 — Pakistan's acceptance density is a fraction of peers, so the +245% is growth off a thin base, not saturation.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Registration ≠ usage (headline inflation).** The 3.8M is QR-*enabled/registered* merchants; SBP's own data shows *actively transacting* merchants grew only from ~0.5M to ~2M (per SBP, via phoneworld/startup.pk, 2026). 297M transactions / 3.8M merchants ≈ 78 per merchant per year (~1.5/week) — and far fewer if the denominator is the ~2M active. The "245%" measures onboarding, not transacting behaviour; the second-order risk is dormant QR stickers that never convert, overstating real adoption.
2. **Low-base effect.** +245% comes off ~1.1M a year prior; one more doubling still leaves Pakistan an order of magnitude behind Indonesia/India on both merchants and usage intensity. High-percentage growth here is expected and not yet evidence of durable network effects.
3. **Rails dependence / state-driven demand.** Volume leans on SBP mandates (unified QR standard, routing govt payments through Raast). Growth engineered by regulation can plateau once the mandate-driven cohort is onboarded; private merchant-pull and repeat P2M usage are the unproven leg (why: without organic frequency, TPV flattens after the onboarding wave).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector this is an acceptance-infrastructure story, not a monetisation one — Raast P2M is free/low-cost by design, so the near-term value accrues to formalisation and data, not to a take-rate P&L. Falsifiable thesis (analysis): if the *active*-merchant count and transactions-per-merchant don't keep pace with the registered-merchant line over the next 2–3 quarterly PS Reviews, the 245% is a vanity metric and real QR adoption is overstated. Trigger to watch: the active-vs-registered gap and P2M TPV in SBP's Q4 FY26 / Q1 FY27 Payment Systems Quarterly Review.
