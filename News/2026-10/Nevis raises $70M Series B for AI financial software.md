# Nevis raises $70M Series B for AI financial software

> [!info] 2026-10-26 · 1 упоминаний · 1 источника(ов) с текстом

Americans want more financial advisors, but every day there are fewer to go around. One New York–based startup wants to help ease the squeeze by using AI to take paperwork and other routine tasks off advisors’ plates, giving them more time for clients. Nevis, a 70-person company launched in October 2024 by former executives at Revolut, the $115 billion fintech, announced Monday it had raised $70 million in a Series B round to continue building out its AI software for financial advisors. The latest round puts the company’s valuation at $500 million, according to a person familiar with the matter who requested anonymity to discuss private financial details.
“There was an opportunity to give financial advisors access to AI agents that can actually do that manual work for them, so that they can save time, grow their book of business, and expand the level of service,” said Nevis CEO Mark Swan, who founded the company along with Philipp Burda and Ivan Chalov.
Startups like Nevis are banking on AI to help advisors serve more clients just as many in the profession are nearing retirement. McKinsey estimated in 2025 that 110,000 advisors—more than a third of the workforce—would retire over the following decade. With demand for advice also growing, the firm projects a shortfall of roughly 100,000 advisors by 2034 unless the industry finds ways to serve more clients. Part of that demand stems from a massive shift in wealth between generations . Baby boomers and older generations are expected to pass nearly $100 trillion to heirs and charities through 2048, according to a 2024 projection from research firm Cerulli Associates.
Nevis has experienced considerable growth over the past year. Assets managed by advisory firms using its platform grew from $50 billion to $700 billion, with roughly $75 billion added each month, according to the startup. Its roughly 20 customers include Focus Financial Partners, IEQ Capital, and United Capital. The company has also opened a second office in London.
 EQT Ventures led the round, bringing Nevis’s total funding to $110 million. New investors QuantumLight and Charles Schwab Corp. joined existing backers Sequoia Capital, Iconiq, and Ribbit Capital. Carolina Brochado, a partner and head of EQT Ventures, will join Nevis’s board as part of the latest round.
For Brochado, Nevis’s appeal lies in how it fits into an advisor’s daily routine.
“An average advisor is dealing with about seven outdated systems to do different things within the workflow,” she said. “What Nevis has built … is a platform that does all these things.”
 Deskmates to cofounders 
Before Nevis was a company, it was an idea shared among three deskmates at Revolut’s London offices. Working side by side, Swan led global strategy and operations; Chalov ran the consumer retail business; and Burda headed AI.
The light bulb went off when Swan led a project using early OpenAI models to automate back-office work at Revolut. He saw an opening to build similar tools for traditional financial firms that might struggle to develop the technology themselves. Wealth management stood out: Advisors could spend up to 80% of their day on administrative work, juggling eight to 10 separate software systems, he said. Recognizing the idea’s potential, the trio left Revolut together to focus fully on building out Nevis. 
Swan named the company after Ben Nevis, Scotland’s (and the U.K.’s) highest mountain, as a nod to his Scottish heritage. The name also reflects the steady role advisors play in guiding families through major financial decisions, he noted.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened.** On 2026-10-26 Nevis, a New York– and London–based AI startup for wealth management, announced a **$70M Series B led by EQT Ventures** (reported locally as ~€62M). New backers **Charles Schwab Corp.** and **QuantumLight** joined existing investors **Sequoia Capital, ICONIQ and Ribbit Capital**. The round reportedly values Nevis at **$500M** (per a person familiar with the matter; the company itself did not confirm the valuation). Total funding to date is now **$110M**. EQT's Carolina Brochado joins the board.

**What the company does.** Nevis builds an AI "operating system" for financial advisors / RIAs that automates administrative and operational work end-to-end — CRM, meeting prep/intelligence, financial planning and custodial account workflows — so advisors spend less of their day (claimed ~80%) on paperwork and juggling 7–10 legacy systems. "AI financial software" here specifically means **advisor back-office automation**, not consumer finance or trading.

**Who's behind it.** Founded October 2024 by three ex-Revolut executives who shared a desk at Revolut's London office: **Mark Swan** (CEO, ex-global strategy & ops), **Philipp Burda** (CPO, ex-head of AI), **Ivan Chalov** (COO, ex-consumer retail). The product idea grew out of Swan's early-OpenAI back-office automation project at Revolut. ~70 employees; second office opened in London.

**This is a fast follow-on, not a duplicate.** See the prior Series A note [[Nevis raises $40M to build AI wealth platform]] (2025-12, $35M Series A + $5M seed = $40M total, emerging from stealth, ~$50B AUM on platform). This Series B comes **<1 year later** and reflects explosive reported growth: platform AUM jumped from **$50B → $700B** (~$75B added/month), revenue reportedly up **>25x** in 2026, ~20 customers including **Focus Financial Partners, IEQ Capital, United Capital** (United Capital already cited in the Series A). AI agents have reportedly completed 100,000+ operational tasks.

**Why it matters / market backdrop.** McKinsey (2025) projects ~110,000 US advisors (a third of the workforce) retiring over the next decade and a ~100,000-advisor shortfall by 2034, against the ~$100T "great wealth transfer" (Cerulli, through 2048). Advisor-capacity automation is the thesis multiple AI wealthtechs are chasing.

**Competitive landscape (internal + external).** Comparable internal notes: [[Feathery raises $30M to power AI-driven RIA operations]] (2026-07, AI-driven RIA ops) and [[Macro Technologies launches AI decision engine for asset managers]] (2026-07). External category leaders Nevis competes with: **Jump** (category leader, 27k+ advisors, #1 in 2025 T3 & Kitces surveys), **Zocks** (compliance logging / CRM), **Powder** (YC-backed, doc parsing / notetaking), plus Mili. Nevis positions as a broader end-to-end "OS" vs. point meeting-notetaker tools.

**Notable signal.** Charles Schwab — the largest US RIA custodian — investing is both capital and a distribution/endorsement signal (covered as such by RIA trade press, e.g. riabiz). EQT Ventures and QuantumLight (the VC arm linked to Revolut founder Nik Storonsky) add a strong Revolut-adjacent thread.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions:**

1. **$500M valuation is unconfirmed** — it rests on a single anonymous source cited by Fortune; Nevis did not state it, and some outlets report the round in euros (~€62M). Is the $500M real post-money, or a target/headline figure?
2. **$50B → $700B platform AUM in ~12 months** is a 14x jump — does "assets managed by firms using its platform" mean assets *touched/automated* by Nevis, or firm-wide AUM where Nevis is merely one of several tools? These are very different claims.
3. **">25x revenue growth in 2026"** off a near-zero post-stealth base (Series A was Dec 2025) is trivially large in percentage terms — what is the absolute ARR? Without a dollar figure this metric is near-meaningless.
4. **~20 customers** for a $500M valuation implies heavy per-logo concentration — how much revenue depends on the top 3 (Focus Financial, IEQ, United Capital)? Churn risk?
5. **Is Charles Schwab a strategic trap?** Schwab is a custodian AND increasingly an advisor-tech provider; its investment could cap Nevis's TAM (other custodians wary) or signal eventual acqui-pressure. Strategic investor ≠ neutral.
6. **QuantumLight is Revolut founder Storonsky's fund** — how much of the "momentum" narrative is Revolut-alumni network effects (founders, investor, talent) vs. independent product-market fit?
7. **Competitive moat vs. Jump (27k+ advisors) / Zocks / Powder** — Jump is the entrenched category leader by advisor count. Nevis has ~20 enterprise logos. Is "end-to-end OS" a defensible wedge or will incumbents + notetakers converge on the same scope?
8. **Custodian/compliance risk** — automating "custodial account opening" end-to-end touches regulated KYC/suitability/record-keeping. What's the liability model if an AI agent errs on a client account? Zocks explicitly leads on compliance logging; does Nevis?
9. **Dilution/round cadence** — seed (early 2025) → $35M Series A (Dec 2025) → $70M Series B (Oct 2026) in <2 years. Rapid serial raising can signal high burn. What's runway and burn multiple?
10. **"80% of advisor time on admin" and "7–10 systems"** are founder/VC talking points repeated verbatim across coverage — are these independently sourced or Nevis marketing?
11. **Model/vendor dependence** — platform is built on third-party LLMs (OpenAI lineage per founder story). Margin and reliability exposure to model-provider pricing/outages?
12. **Does this duplicate the Dec 2025 note?** No — distinct funding event (Series B, new lead EQT, new investors Schwab/QuantumLight, new valuation). Verdict below = fresh; link prior note as context.
13. **Geography/reg scope** — US RIA-focused but HQ'd NY + London. Is there a UK/EU product, or is London just an eng/ops hub? Regulatory differences could fragment the "OS."

**Importance: 3/5.** A notable, well-signaled growth round (reputable lead + Schwab strategic endorsement + Revolut-pedigree founders) in a hot AI-wealthtech category, at a $500M valuation. But it's a private Series B for a ~2-year-old, ~20-customer company with unverified metrics, not a market-moving event; above-average follow-on raise, not top-tier.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** AI-for-wealth/advisor-automation software sits inside the broader wealthtech stack, variously sized at ~$7–9bn in 2026 growing ~12–15%+ CAGR (per Mordor Intelligence, Research and Markets, Research Nester, via secondary citations, as of 2026); the narrower AI-powered wealth-management-solution slice is estimated at ~$2.0bn in 2026 (per Future Market Insights, via secondary citation). The structure is fragmented and tool-sprawl-heavy: EQT's Brochado states the average advisor juggles "about seven outdated systems," and Nevis CEO Swan cites advisors spending up to 80% of their day on admin across eight-to-ten systems — i.e. the value pool is back-office workflow consolidation, not asset allocation. "Why now" is a demographic squeeze: McKinsey (2025) projected ~110k advisors (a third of the workforce) retiring over a decade and a ~100k-advisor shortfall by 2034, while Cerulli (2024) sees ~$100tn passing to heirs through 2048 — demand up, supply down, so per-advisor capacity (automation) becomes the scarce lever. **Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for this subvertical: AUA served on-platform, customer count (advisory firms/RIAs), and — for the vendor — ARR / revenue growth and net retention (none of Nevis's revenue/ARR disclosed → `[UNSOURCED]`). Players compete on breadth of workflow coverage and distribution into RIAs/broker-dealers rather than price. Recent peer moves: Wealth.com closed a $65M Series B (2026-04-16) for AI estate/tax planning, citing 3x annual revenue growth over four years and advisory firms managing >$15tn in assets; FINNY raised a $17m Series A (announced 2025-12) for AI advisor prospecting. Nevis's own position: fast-scaling niche leader in end-to-end advisor admin automation — disclosed AUA on platform grew $50bn → $700bn in ~a year (~$75bn added/month), ~20 customers (Focus Financial Partners, IEQ Capital, United Capital), 70 staff, second office in London. Moat (analysis): switching costs from deep workflow integration + distribution signal from Charles Schwab joining as strategic investor; but the AUA figure is a vanity/reach metric, not revenue, and customer count is still small (~20).

Distribution not computed (no ≥3 disclosed valuation/revenue pairs); qualitative read only. $500M post-money on a ~12-month-old Series B company with no disclosed revenue is a rich, AUA-narrative-driven mark `(analysis)` — the $700bn AUA is assets on client firms' books, not Nevis revenue, so it does not underwrite the price by itself.

**Risk flags.**
1. Valuation set on a reach metric, not economics: $500M rests on AUA growth ($50bn→$700bn) with revenue `[UNSOURCED]`; if monetization lags the AUA ramp, the next round re-rates down — classic AI-premium compression risk.
2. Customer concentration: ~20 customers against $700bn AUA means a few large RIAs drive the platform metric; loss of one or two (e.g. Focus Financial / IEQ) would dent both AUA and the growth story. Second-order: pricing power sits with the big RIAs, not Nevis.
3. Disintermediation by the distribution partner: Charles Schwab is simultaneously a strategic investor and the custodian/broker-dealer platform advisors sit on — Schwab (and incumbents like Apex, cf. [[Apex Fintech Solutions debuts Apex AI Suite]]) can build competing agentic admin tooling into the custody rail, capturing Nevis's workflow layer.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is an up-round re-rating of the "agentic back-office for advisors" thesis, not a new category — Nevis, Wealth.com and FINNY are converging on the capacity-shortage narrative. Falsifiable thesis: Nevis sustains the mark only if it converts $700bn AUA into disclosable ARR with a customer base broadening past ~20 names. Trigger to watch: next funding/press disclosing revenue or customer count, and whether Schwab deepens as a channel or turns competitor. What breaks it: a flat/contracting AUA quarter or a Schwab-native equivalent launch.
