 NEW YORK , Oct. 15, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Fission, a blockchain-enabled financial technology company building liquidity infrastructure for tokenized investment funds, today announced it has closed a $10 million Series A funding round led by Pantera Capital and backed by a group of investors spanning traditional finance and digital assets.
Investment funds are rapidly moving on-chain. Tokenization has already transformed how fund interests can be issued, distributed and settled, but much of the underlying market structure remains unchanged. Tokenized funds still rely on periodic redemption windows, limited secondary liquidity and restrictive financing structures.
Fission is building the liquidity layer designed to change that.
The company enables investor liquidity for supported tokenized fund products, allowing eligible holders to access capital without waiting for traditional fund redemption cycles. Fission supports a growing universe of tokenized funds across asset classes including private credit, alternatives and other institutional investment products. The company has already processed millions of dollars in transactions and is expanding its capacity through both traditional and on-chain sources of capital.
"Putting funds on-chain is only the beginning," said Jonathan Shaffer, Founder and CEO of Fission. "The real promise of tokenization is creating fundamentally better markets around these assets: broader distribution, continuous liquidity and the ability to use fund interests as collateral in lending markets. Whether a fund is private or simply constrained by traditional liquidity structures, Fission is building the technology that makes those assets more liquid, transferable and useful across financial markets."
Fission's platform is purpose-built for institutional and permissioned markets, with infrastructure designed around the investor eligibility, transfer restrictions and compliance requirements governing regulated investment products. This allows Fission to bring on-chain liquidity to these assets while preserving the controls required by securities markets.
The new capital will support Fission's continued expansion across asset coverage, liquidity capacity and institutional integrations.
"Investment funds are moving onto digital rails and liquidity is one of the critical pieces required for that transition to reach its full potential," said Franklin Bi, General Partner at Pantera Capital. "Fission is building infrastructure that will help transform tokenized funds from representations of traditional products with legacy constraints into fundamentally more liquid and productive assets."
The round adds Pantera Capital, Circle Ventures, Borderless Capital and Ari Rubenstein to Fission's investor base, alongside existing backers including New Form, gumi Cryptos Capital, Anthony Scaramucci, Manhattan Venture Partners, Reflexive Capital and Lattice Capital.
For more on Fission's infrastructure and its vision for the future of on-chain investment funds, visit  Fission: The Liquidity Layer for Tokenized Funds .
 About Fission 
Fission is building the liquidity infrastructure for the next generation of digital investment funds. Its team brings experience from institutions including Point72 and Securitize, spanning institutional trading, private-market distribution, tokenization and regulated securities markets. Fission's mission is to make investment funds more liquid, accessible and financeable by powering the digital infrastructure required for these products to scale globally.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)

**Fission Labs** — a New York, blockchain-native fintech founded **2024** building a *liquidity layer for tokenized investment funds* — closed a **$10M Series A led by Pantera Capital** (announced via PRNewswire 2026-10-15; the round itself reportedly closed **September 2026**). New backers: **Circle Ventures, Borderless Capital, and Ari Rubenstein (CEO of HFT firm GTS)**, joining existing investors **New Form, gumi Cryptos Capital, Anthony Scaramucci, Manhattan Venture Partners, Reflexive Capital and Lattice Capital**. **Valuation not disclosed.** Leadership: **Jonathan Shaffer (Founder/CEO), Steve Bendit (CRO), Chris Humphrey (CTO)**; team pedigree cited from **Point72 and Securitize**.

**What it actually does (mechanics, de-marketed):** Fission is *not* a tokenization platform (it does not mint fund tokens). It is a **secondary-liquidity / early-redemption desk** for *already-tokenized* fund interests. An eligible holder connects a wallet and sells a tokenized fund position for **stablecoins via atomic settlement** (fund-share leg + payment leg in one transaction, no partial fills). Fission's headline claim: an exit in **~90 seconds** vs the **~200-day** traditional redemption/settlement cycle. It funds this by standing in as the liquidity provider (balance-sheet + on-chain capital), so the economics resemble a **discount-to-NAV market-maker / factoring book** rather than a true deep order-book exchange. Target users: hedge funds holding **tokenized private credit / alternatives / semi-liquid funds**, crypto treasuries, stablecoin issuers. It is purpose-built for **permissioned/institutional** markets (eligibility, transfer restrictions, compliance). Reported access to funds across the **Centrifuge** ecosystem and products from **Apollo, Janus Henderson, New York Life Investments**; "millions of dollars" in redemptions processed to date (small — this is early traction, not scale).

## [1] Why it matters

Tokenization solved *issuance* (mint a fund interest as an ERC-20) but left **market structure unchanged**: periodic redemption windows, thin secondary markets, multi-month exits. Fission attacks the **"getting out" half** of the thesis — the same gap [[Ondo launches Ondo Private Markets for tokenized exposure]] framed for pre-IPO equity exposure and that [[Grove launches Basin, a $1 billion tokenized-asset liquidity facility]] attacks for Treasuries/MMFs. If tokenized fund interests can be sold in seconds and pledged as collateral, they become genuinely *productive* assets, which is the whole institutional adoption argument. Pantera (GP **Franklin Bi**) frames it as turning tokenized funds "from representations of traditional products with legacy constraints into fundamentally more liquid and productive assets."

## [2] Internal context (prior notes)

- [[Grove launches Basin, a $1 billion tokenized-asset liquidity facility]] — **the closest and most important comparable**: Grove Basin (May 2026) offers up to **$1B/day** committed stablecoin liquidity for *instant redemptions* of tokenized RWAs, with **BlackRock BUIDL + Janus Henderson Anemoy** as launch funds and Securitize/Centrifuge as infra partners. Grove targets **Treasuries/MMFs** (deep, commoditized); Fission positions one tier riskier on **private credit / semi-liquid / private-market funds** — far harder to price, which is both its moat and its risk.
- [[Ondo launches Ondo Private Markets for tokenized exposure]] — same-month (Oct 2026) adjacent theme: Ondo brings **24/7 tradable tokenized exposure to private companies**; same "access + liquidity" private-markets pitch, different layer (Ondo issues exposure, Fission provides exit liquidity for fund interests).
- [[Bybit and DigiFT enable UBS tokenized fund as trading collateral]] — validates the "**tokenized fund interest as collateral**" leg of Shaffer's thesis (UBS uMINT used as trading collateral).
- [[Securitize plans to go public via $1.25B SPAC]] — Securitize (now NYSE-listed, ~$5B tokenized AUM) is the issuance incumbent several Fission founders came from; defines the issuance layer Fission sits downstream of.
- [[Libeara raises $14 million to expand tokenisation infrastructure]] / [[TurboFlow raises $6M seed led by Pantera for retail trading infrastructure]] — recent comparable early-stage raises; TurboFlow is another **Pantera** 2026 bet, showing Pantera's active on-chain-markets thesis.

## [3] External context / market (WebSearch 2026-10)

- **Market backdrop:** on-chain RWA has crossed **~$36B** in 2026 (up from ~$8B start of 2024); tokenized US Treasuries alone ~**$12.9B**; category projected **$50B+** by year-end. Leaders: **BlackRock BUIDL ~$3B**, **Ondo ~$3B TVL**, **Franklin FOBXX ~$0.84B** (8 chains), **Securitize ~$5B** (NYSE-listed July 2026).
- **The liquidity sub-sector is the new battleground.** Issuance is increasingly commoditized; the differentiated problem is **exit/secondary liquidity + collateralization**. Grove Basin is the incumbent at Treasury scale; Fission is the specialist bet on the *illiquid* end (private credit lock-ups of months/years where tokens can trade at steep discounts under stress).
- **Pantera** = first US blockchain fund (2013); consistent on-chain-markets infrastructure thesis; led this round via GP Franklin Bi.
- **Circle Ventures** participation signals stablecoin-settlement rails alignment (Fission settles exits in stablecoins).

## Challenge / red-team

1. **Is this a market or a balance sheet?** "90 seconds vs 200 days" only holds while Fission itself (or committed lines) buys the position. That's a **principal market-maker / factoring book**, not a neutral venue. At what discount-to-NAV does it buy, and what is its warehousing capacity? "Millions processed" suggests a very small book.
2. **Private-credit pricing risk.** Fission leans into the *illiquid* end (private credit, alternatives) where NAV is stale/quarterly. How does it price an exit between NAV marks, and who eats the loss if the real value gaps down (classic liquidity-provider adverse selection, worst exactly when everyone wants out)?
3. **Grove Basin comparison.** Grove already offers **$1B/day** liquidity with BlackRock/Janus. Why isn't Fission just a sub-scale clone? Answer hinges on whether the private-markets niche is genuinely defensible or merely the part Grove hasn't bothered with yet because it's un-underwritable at scale.
4. **$10M is tiny for a liquidity business.** A liquidity layer is capital-intensive; $10M equity funds the team, not the book. The PR says capacity expands via "traditional and on-chain sources of capital" — i.e., the real firepower is undisclosed debt/credit lines. Without those, "liquidity capacity" is marketing.
5. **Eligibility wall.** "Permissioned, eligible holders, transfer restrictions" — the addressable universe is accredited/qualified institutional only. How large is the actually-tradable float of tokenized private-market funds today (likely very small)?
6. **Counterparty/redemption path.** When Fission holds a tokenized fund interest, can it reliably redeem at the underlying fund's window to recycle capital, or is it stuck holding until the ~200-day cycle it claims to eliminate? If the latter, its own liquidity is the bottleneck.
7. **Regulatory.** Facilitating secondary transfer of securities-like tokens — is Fission acting as a broker-dealer / ATS? US securities-law posture is unaddressed in the PR.
8. **Discrepancy flag.** PR implies round "today announced" 2026-10-15; Crypto Briefing says round **closed September 2026**. Minor but note the announce-vs-close gap; valuation undisclosed throughout.
9. **Investor signal vs substance.** Pantera + Circle Ventures + Scaramucci is a strong crypto-native cap table but thin on the *traditional asset managers* whose funds Fission needs onboarded. Apollo/Janus/NYLI are cited as accessible *funds*, not as partners/investors — verify the relationship depth.
10. **Depth of "millions in redemptions."** Is that cumulative gross flow or net book? Against a $36B RWA market it's a rounding error; traction claim is aspirational.
11. **Does tokenization even need Fission?** If issuers (Securitize) and incumbents (Grove) add instant-redemption natively, the standalone liquidity-desk layer could be disintermediated. Is Fission a feature or a company?
12. **Stablecoin settlement leg.** Exits settle in stablecoins (Circle alignment) — introduces depeg/float risk and off-ramp friction for institutions wanting fiat.

Importance: 3/5 — A well-backed (Pantera-led), thesis-coherent bet on the most interesting unsolved part of tokenization (exit liquidity + collateralization of private-market fund interests), and a useful marker of where the RWA fight is moving. But it is a small ($10M, undisclosed valuation) early-stage company with minimal disclosed traction, a capital-intensive model it can't yet fund, and a credible larger incumbent (Grove Basin) already operating adjacent. Notable as a trend signal, not yet as a market-mover.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Fission sits in the tokenized-funds / RWA sub-vertical, specifically the *secondary-liquidity layer* for tokenized investment funds (private credit, alternatives, semi-liquid products). Total tokenized RWA on public chains grew ~300% YoY to ~$27.7bn by Apr 2026 and ~$31bn by Jul 2026 (per DeFiLlama/RWA.xyz aggregations, via onebullex/bex, as of mid-2026); tokenized cash/liquidity funds alone are put at >$15bn (per Sygnum, via itbrief.asia, 2026-10 — see [[Sygnum brings Fidelity tokenized liquidity fund to Singapore]]). Structure is still concentrated at the issuance layer — Securitize-managed funds (BlackRock BUIDL ~$2.5bn, Apollo ACRED, Hamilton Lane) plus Franklin BENJI and Ondo account for the majority of AUM — but the *liquidity/secondary* layer Fission targets is nascent and fragmented. Why now: issuance is solved (tokens exist), but redemption remains gated — Securitize-issued funds (ACRED, HLSCOPE) still cap on-demand redemptions at ~5% of NAV per quarter and otherwise run monthly/quarterly windows (per Yahoo Finance / fluidrwa, 2026). The gap between "transferable 24/7 between whitelisted wallets" and "actually redeemable for cash" is the whitespace Fission is funding against.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for a liquidity-infra play: liquidity capacity (balance-sheet + on-chain capital deployed), redemption volume processed, # of supported fund products, and spread/fee on early-liquidity provision (none disclosed by Fission → `[UNSOURCED]`; only "millions of dollars" in redemptions/transactions processed, which is small). Basis of competition is capital access + compliance plumbing (investor-eligibility, transfer restrictions) rather than price. Adjacent players: **Securitize** (issuer that also runs a capped in-fund liquidity pool — part competitor, part supplier; Fission's team includes Securitize alumni, note p.52), **Maple** (~$2bn TVL, overcollateralized on-chain credit — see [[Navra raises $19M Series A for onchain lending]], SoFi/Figure founder Cagney's onchain-lending/securities play, a structural peer), **Centrifuge/Goldfinch** (on-chain credit origination), and DeFi money-markets that could accept fund tokens as collateral. Position: niche new entrant (analysis) — Fission is a *liquidity/financing* layer rather than an issuer, so it's complementary to BUIDL/BENJI/Securitize rather than competing head-on; its moat, if any, is relationships with issuers + a regulated/permissioned liquidity book (switching cost once integrated), not yet network effects.

**Comps & multiples.** $10M Series A, valuation undisclosed → EV/Rev, P/S = **no data** (private, no revenue disclosed; multiples not computable). Internal round comps (10/2026 base):
- [[Navra raises $19M Series A for onchain lending]] — onchain lending/securities, Mike Cagney (SoFi/Figure); closest structural peer, ~2x Fission's round.
- [[Causa Prima raises $20M seed led by Base10 Partners]] — ~2x, seed vs Series A.
- [[Dinari integrates with Para for tokenized US equities access]] — tokenized-equity rails (product, not a round) — adjacent infra.
- [[OKX raises capital at flat $25B valuation]] / [[Polymarket weighs IPO and native token at $20B valuation]] — crypto scale references, not comparable.
$10M is a small, typical early Series A for pre-scale crypto infra; no valuation outlier flag possible without a number. Distribution not computed (rounds not size-comparable; qualitative only).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Fission carries the liquidity/credit risk it's selling.** Providing early liquidity against gated, illiquid tokenized funds means Fission (or its capital partners) warehouses redemption risk and a NAV/duration mismatch; a $10M Series A is thin balance-sheet to fund redemptions at scale → second-order: capacity is capped by how much "traditional and on-chain capital" it can raise, not by demand.
2. **Disintermediation by issuers.** Securitize already runs in-fund liquidity pools (5%/quarter) and could widen them, and Franklin/BlackRock can add native liquidity features — the liquidity layer may be captured by the issuer stack, squeezing an independent middle-layer provider.
3. **Regulatory / eligibility concentration.** Model depends on permissioned, whitelisted investors and securities-transfer rules; any tightening (or divergence across jurisdictions — CLARITY Act stalled in US, see [[Noah CEO weighs crypto regulation path after CLARITY Act fails]]) narrows the addressable pool and raises compliance cost.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Signals capital rotating from tokenized-fund *issuance* to the *post-issuance plumbing* (liquidity, collateral, financing) — a sign the RWA thesis is maturing from "put it on-chain" to "make it useful." Falsifiable thesis: if tokenized-fund interests become widely accepted as DeFi/lending collateral, independent liquidity layers like Fission capture a durable fee stream; trigger to watch — a named issuer (BUIDL/BENJI/ACRED) integrating Fission, or Fission disclosing redemption volume in $bn not "millions." What breaks it: issuers internalize liquidity, leaving third-party layers with only the riskiest residual flow.
