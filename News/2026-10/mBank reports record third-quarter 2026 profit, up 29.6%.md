## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)

mBank S.A. (Poland's 4th–5th largest bank by assets; legacy Commerzbank-controlled, but Commerzbank sold down and the stake is now widely held / largely free-float) reported Q3 2026 **net profit of PLN 1,085m, +29.6% YoY** — a quarterly record. 9M 2026 net profit was ~PLN 3.1bn, +24% YoY. Headline drivers (from the results slides / earnings call):

- **Net interest margin rebounded to 3.57%** (from 3.47% in Q2 2026) *despite* the NBP cutting the reference rate by ~200bps over the preceding ~10 months (to 3.75% by March 2026). NII hit a record **PLN 2,673m, +9.0% QoQ / +6.5% YoY**.
- **Record loan growth**: gross loans +17.7% YoY to PLN 164.4bn (the largest quarterly loan increase in the bank's history); deposits +19.0% YoY to PLN 254.7bn.
- Net fee & commission income record **PLN 594m** (+4.4% QoQ / +2.4% YoY).
- **CHF-mortgage legal-risk costs collapsed to PLN 78m, −82.9% YoY** (from PLN 457m in Q3 2025). 34,641 cumulative settlements by end-Sep 2026 (vs 30,735 a year earlier). 9M 2025 CHF provisions were PLN 1.66bn; management had already cut them ~50% YoY — the easing is the single biggest swing factor behind the profit jump.
- **ROTE 21.3% for 9M 2026** (up from 20.1% in 9M 2025).

**Why the headline reads this way / what it reveals.** The "+29.6% record profit" frame flatters because the YoY base (Q3 2025) was depressed by heavy CHF-mortgage provisions. Strip the provision normalization and the operating story is more mixed: the real engine is (a) volume — record loan/deposit growth — offsetting (b) a margin squeezed by rate cuts, with NIM only recovering because deposit repricing lagged. Two headwinds are buried in the PR: **cost of risk jumped to 67bps (from 37bps in Q2)** on a single large corporate default (management says ~55bps underlying, in line with full-year guidance), and **Poland's new 30% bank income-tax rate (effective 1 Jan 2026) cut 9M net profit by ~PLN 545m** vs prior year. So the record is partly a provision-release / low-base effect, not pure operating strength.

## [1] Competitors / peers

Polish large-cap banks are all posting records in 2026, so mBank's +29.6% is sector-wide tailwind, not idiosyncratic alpha:
- **PKO BP** (largest): record H1 2026, net profit PLN 5.29bn, +3.1% YoY — much slower growth than mBank because PKO's CHF provisions are declining more gradually (less of a base-effect kicker).
- **Bank Pekao**: ~PLN 6.9bn standalone, +~PLN 0.5bn YoY.
- **Santander Bank Polska**: continuing-operations profit +12.3% YoY (ex the one-off gain from Santander group selling the Polish unit — a structural ownership shift in the market).
- **mBank / Millennium**: the two with the sharpest CHF-provision reductions, hence the biggest % profit jumps.

**Why the lay of the land is this way (second order).** The whole sector is riding the same two one-off-ish tailwinds: (1) the CHF-mortgage saga winding down (settlements replacing litigation reserves) and (2) rate cuts only *starting* to bite margins. mBank's larger % jump is mostly a lower-quality driver (provision release off a bad base) than Pekao/PKO's slower-but-cleaner growth. The sector consensus is that 2026 is a "good but not another record" year — the easy provision-release gains are largely exhausted after 2026.

## [2] Company history / fit

mBank built one of Poland's largest CHF-denominated mortgage books in the 2000s; the subsequent Polish-court invalidation wave (borrowers winning franc-loan cases) forced years of multi-billion-zloty legal reserves that gutted reported profit 2021–2025. The 2026 result is the **tail of that cleanup**: by pivoting to mass settlements (34k+ contracts), mBank is converting an open-ended litigation liability into a known, shrinking cost — which is exactly why provisions fell 83% YoY. Simultaneously it is leaning into its core strength (digital retail + corporate lending) to grow the balance sheet fast while rates are still relatively high.

**Why the bank acts this way.** With the CHF overhang finally quantifiable, management is free to (a) chase volume growth and (b) resume dividends — mBank is now one of six Polish banks cleared to pay out up to 75% of prior-year profit. The structural logic: normalize the legacy liability → restore ROTE above 20% → re-rate toward a dividend story.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction

Not a new product or model — this is an **earnings print**. The genuinely new, citable facts: a quarterly profit record (PLN 1,085m), record NII (PLN 2,673m), record loan growth (+17.7% YoY), NIM recovery to 3.57%, CHF legal costs down to PLN 78m, ROTE 21.3%. Traction is real on volumes (balance-sheet growth is organic and record-setting), but the *profit* beat is substantially a provision-normalization and low-base effect, not margin expansion.

**Why the value-add is / isn't durable.** Durable: deposit franchise and loan growth, and the CHF liability moving from "unbounded" to "nearly resolved." Fragile: NIM depends on further NBP cuts (one more cut flagged for 2026 → downward margin pressure into 2027); the 30% bank tax is a permanent ~PLN 0.5bn+ drag; and cost of risk is creeping up (67bps, one corporate default). The low-quality part of the +29.6% (provision release) does not repeat once CHF reserves bottom out.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment

Guidance/outlook: continued double-digit loan growth and resumed/higher dividends; management targets underlying cost of risk ~55bps for the full year. Sector view: 2026 strong but record profits "unlikely to be repeated" — the provision-release and high-rate tailwinds fade, while the higher bank tax and further rate cuts are structural headwinds into 2027.

**Why the market goes this way (counterintuitive second order).** The very thing powering the 2026 records — CHF provisions falling and rates still elevated — is self-limiting: once the CHF book is settled and rates normalize lower, Polish banks lose both kickers simultaneously, leaving them more exposed to the 30% tax and to credit normalization. So a "record quarter" headline is a *late-cycle* signal for Polish banks, not an early-growth one. mBank's larger jump vs peers reads as higher-beta to the fading provision tailwind.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** mBank is #4–5 among Poland's listed banks by assets; its parent is Commerzbank (~65% stake). The Polish listed-bank sector is highly profitable but entering the downside of the rate cycle: the NBP cut the reference rate by ~200bps over the ten months to Q3 2026 (per Investing.com via the earnings call), compressing margins across the sector. The combined Q3 2026 net profit of nine listed Polish banks is expected around PLN 9.74bn, an 8.6% decline YoY (per Poland Insight) — i.e. mBank is outgrowing a sector that is, in aggregate, shrinking. Two drivers explain why mBank bucks the trend: (1) NIM rebound to 3.57% from 3.47% QoQ as deposit repricing caught up to asset yields, lifting net interest income to a record PLN 2,673m (+9.0% QoQ); (2) the fading of the Swiss-franc (CHF) mortgage legal-risk overhang — the dominant Polish banking headwind since 2019. "Why now": the CHF litigation wave is in run-off, letting record pre-provision profits finally drop to the bottom line.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: NIM, ROE/ROTE, cost of risk (bps), and CHF legal-risk provisions (the swing factor). Key players: PKO BP (#1, the largest listed bank), Pekao, Santander Bank Polska, ING BSK, mBank, Millennium, Alior — competition is scale/distribution-led, with CHF-settlement execution a differentiator. Recent moves: PKO reported record H1 2026 net profit of PLN 5.29bn at 18.4% ROE (per Investing.com); Pekao's Q1 2026 net profit fell to PLN 1.232bn from PLN 1.685bn YoY at 17.1% ROE; for Q3 2026 Pekao is forecast down ~22% to ~PLN 1,478m and PKO ~PLN 2.82bn (flat); Alior posted PLN 473m at ~15% ROE and a 5.17% NIM (per Investing.com transcripts). mBank's position: among sector leaders on profitability — ROTE 21.6% in Q3 2026 (9M: 21.3% vs 20.1% a year earlier), gross loans +17.7% YoY to PLN 164.4bn and deposits +19.0% YoY to PLN 254.7bn, its largest-ever quarterly loan increase (per Investing.com slides). Moat `(analysis)`: scale, a strong retail/SME franchise and Commerzbank backing — but no durable structural moat vs larger PKO/Pekao; the edge is cyclical (NIM timing + CHF run-off) and execution-driven.

**Comps & multiples.** ROE/ROTE comparison (profitability proxy; no clean public EV/P-E computed here as market-cap/EPS not in-source):
- mBank — Q3 2026 ROTE 21.6%; cost of risk 67bps (loan-loss + FV change PLN 261m).
- PKO BP — H1 2026 ROE 18.4%, FY2025 ROE 19.5%, cost of risk 30bps.
- Pekao — Q1 2026 ROE 17.1%, cost of risk 37bps (2027 target 65–75bps).
- Alior — ~15% ROE, NIM 5.17%.
mBank screens rich on profitability (highest ROTE of the group) but has the highest disclosed cost of risk (67bps vs PKO 30 / Pekao 37) — in-line-to-elevated on credit. Internal comp (CHF/Polish-unit context): [[Santander sells 3.5% stake in Polish unit for $473M]] (Santander exiting 49% of its Polish unit to Erste, Dec 2025) — signals a foreign parent de-risking Poland even as domestic ROEs stay high. Trading multiples (P/E, P/BV) = no data from these sources `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Rate-cycle margin risk.** The NIM rebound came despite ~200bps of NBP cuts; further easing could re-compress NIM once deposit-repricing tailwinds exhaust — the record is partly a timing effect, not a durable re-rate.
2. **Rising cost of risk.** Credit cost rose to 67bps (loan-loss + FV PLN 261m), well above PKO (30bps) and Pekao (37bps); the record 17.7% YoY loan growth could seed future losses if underwriting loosened into the cycle.
3. **CHF tail risk, not zero.** Legal-risk cost fell to PLN 78m (−82.9% YoY from PLN 457m) on 34,641 cumulative settlements, but residual non-settled contracts and any adverse court/regulatory shift could reverse the tailwind that drove much of the profit jump.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The quarter marks the inflection where Polish bank earnings stop being hostage to CHF provisions and revert to rate-cycle + credit-quality dynamics — a sector re-rate toward "normal" cyclical banks. Falsifiable thesis: if NIM holds ≥3.5% and cost of risk stays ≤70bps through Q4 2026 (mBank itself guides a strong Q4 and a better 2027), the record is sustainable; it breaks if a renewed NBP cut drags NIM back below ~3.4% or cost of risk climbs past Pekao's 2027 target band (65–75bps) on the fast-grown loan book. Watch: Q4 2026 NIM, the 2027 dividend resumption (30% payout, ramping to 75% by 2030), and whether loan-growth credit costs normalize.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (headline read).** IN-LINE (marginal miss on the headline) · net profit PLN 1,085m (+29.6% YoY; vs public consensus ~PLN 1.11bn / +32% YoY → ~2% light, ~-PLN 25m) · 9M net profit PLN 3,098m (+23.8%) · drivers: record NIM rebound (3.57%) + fastest-ever loan growth + an 82.9% YoY collapse in CHF-mortgage legal costs, partly offset by a higher Polish bank tax and a one-off corporate default lifting cost of risk · guidance: FY26 revenue only ~+2% vs 2025, 2027 profit guided stronger with a resumed 30% dividend payout. Record quarterly profit, but it undershot a Street that had penciled in +32%, so the "record" is a slight disappointment vs expectations, not a beat.

**Key figures (with growth).**
- Net profit: PLN 1,085m, +29.6% YoY (quarterly record). 9M: PLN 3,098m, +23.8% YoY.
- Net interest income: PLN 2,673m, +6.5% YoY / +9.0% QoQ (record) — the real engine.
- Net interest margin: 3.57%, +10bp QoQ (from 3.47% in Q2) — rebounded *despite* NBP rate cuts, which is the surprise.
- Fee & commission income: PLN 594m, +2.4% YoY / +4.4% QoQ — soft, lagging NII badly.
- Total income: PLN 3,250m.
- Profitability: 9M ROTE 21.3% (vs 20.1% in 2025); Q3 ROTE 21.6%.
- Efficiency: cost/income 31.4% in Q3; operating costs PLN 946m. FY guide: C/I below 33%.
- Capital (pro-forma 30 Sep 2026): Tier 1 13.8%, CET1 12.75%, MREL 22.3%.

**By segment / driver.** Growth is balance-sheet-led, not fee-led. Gross loans PLN 164.4bn (+17.7% YoY) — the largest quarterly loan increase in the bank's history; customer deposits PLN 254.7bn (+19.0% YoY); total assets PLN 307.4bn (+17.5% YoY); loan-to-deposit a conservative 63.2%. NII rode volume + the margin rebound. The weak link is fees (+2.4% YoY) — barely growing, so revenue mix is leaning hard on rate-sensitive NII just as Poland cuts rates. The second swing factor was legacy FX: CHF-mortgage legal provisions fell to PLN 78m from PLN 457m a year ago (-82.9% YoY), with 34,641 settlements cumulatively — this de-risking, not core operations, flatters the YoY profit jump.

**vs expectations / prior period.** Public consensus (five brokerages via PAP Biznes) was ~PLN 1.11bn (+32% YoY); actual PLN 1,085m (+29.6%) = ~2% / ~PLN 25m light — a marginal miss, characterized in coverage as "roughly in line." Why light: cost of risk spiked to 67bp from 37bp in Q2 (normalized ~55bp ex a one-off corporate default) — rising credit costs as the book grows fast is the drag. Asset quality headline still improved: NPL ratio 2.9% (from 3.4% YoY), coverage 75.7%. YoY momentum is genuine (record NII, record profit, ROTE up ~120bp) but the market had already priced a stronger print. No prior mBank earnings note exists in the corpus for a trajectory `[[wikilink]]`.

**Guidance / forward.** FY26 revenue guided ~+2% above 2025 — modest, consistent with NII pressure from rate cuts despite the Q3 margin bounce. 2027: net profit "should be stronger than in 2026," revenue growing faster than costs, and crucially a resumption of regular dividends at a 30% payout ratio — the first clear capital-return signal post-CHF cleanup, supported by CET1 12.75%. Tax is a swing: effective tax rate >37% now (9M tax burden ~PLN 545m higher YoY on the raised Polish bank tax), guided down toward ~26% in 2027 as Polish law normalizes — a mechanical ~11pp EPS tailwind into 2027 if it holds. Management tone: confident on the operational turn (margin, loans, dividend restart), quieter on the fee stagnation and on how durable the NIM rebound is if NBP keeps cutting.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. NIM rebounded +10bp QoQ *into* a rate-cutting cycle → suggests deposit-repricing/asset-mix discipline is working → if sustainable, decouples mBank's margin from NBP cuts; second-order: supports the +2% revenue guide and the 2027 profit uptick. But durability unproven — watch Q4.
2. CHF-mortgage overhang nearly resolved (provisions -82.9% YoY, 34,641 settlements) → the single biggest multi-year thesis risk is fading → enables the 2027 dividend restart (30% payout); second-order: re-rating potential as a "clean" Polish bank.
3. Credit costs turning up (CoR 67bp vs 37bp Q2) just as loans grow +17.7% → classic late-cycle flag; one-off default muddies it (~55bp normalized) but the direction is the thing to watch — fast growth + rising CoR can reverse the ROTE story.
4. Fee income near-flat (+2.4% YoY) + bank-tax-driven >37% effective tax → earnings quality leans heavily on rate-sensitive NII and a tax normalization that depends on Polish politics (2027 cut to ~26%), both outside management's control.
