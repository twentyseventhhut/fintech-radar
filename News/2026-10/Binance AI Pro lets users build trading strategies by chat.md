# Binance AI Pro lets users build trading strategies by chat

> [!info] 2026-10-19 · 1 упоминаний · 1 источника(ов) с текстом
> Каналы: Connecting the Dots in Fintech

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Connecting the Dots in Fintech] 🌐 Binance AI Pro lets users build trading strategies by chat. The conversational agent is now open to every Binance user with no coding. Strategies run on paper trading first, and live trades need separate user approval.

Binance AI Pro , a conversational trading agent that turns plain-language ideas into tested, automated strategies, is now open to every user of the largest crypto exchange by trading volume, with no coding and no separate sign-up. Announced on 19 October 2026 from Abu Dhabi, the agent lets a user describe the assets, conditions, parameters and risk limits they want, then builds, tests and renders the strategy as a visual workflow for review before any money moves. 
 For finance professionals, the detail that matters sits in the plumbing rather than the chat box. Every strategy runs first on paper trading, using virtual funds in a simulated environment. Going live requires a separate review-and-approve step and a dedicated AI Strategy Account, a virtual sub-account walled off from the user's main Binance balance. Only the user can fund that account, by manual transfer. The agent cannot move money on its own, cannot withdraw to external addresses, and any strategy can be paused, adjusted or revoked at any time. 
 What can Binance AI Pro actually do? 
 On desktop, the agent presents each strategy as a Strategy Canvas, a visual workflow shown alongside the chat. Users can click any step to inspect it, adjust parameters, add or remove components, or annotate a module with instructions. On mobile, building and refining happen entirely through chat, with the agent writing the underlying code. The workflows extend past trading into daily reports, market monitoring, subscription alerts and signal generation. 
 At launch the agent supports Spot and USDS-M Futures strategies, subject to regional restrictions, with more trading and exchange products promised over time. Each user can run up to 10 strategies across paper trading, live trading and signals. One constraint will stand out to serious strategy builders: backtesting against historical data is not available in this release. Binance offers automated code testing and paper trading instead, which validate that a strategy runs as written but do not show how it would have performed through past market conditions. 
 How much does it cost, and who pays? 
 All users start on a free Standard Plan with 250,000 monthly credits. A Premium Plan at 19.99 USDC per month raises the allowance to 10 million monthly credits, unlocks frontier models including ChatGPT  and Claude Opus  where available, and allows credit top-ups, paid through Binance Pay. Standard and Premium credits refresh monthly; Premium top-up credits do not refresh, expire after 12 months, and are drawn down only once the monthly allowance is spent. 
 The pricing points to the strategic logic. With more than 300 million registered users by its own count, Binance is turning a conversational agent into a metered subscription funnel, where heavier automation consumes more credits and nudges active traders towards the paid tier. Beta users keep their existing service and subscriptions, and can migrate their chat history and strategies through the app. 
 Why is Binance launching this now? 
 Binance AI Pro is the general-availability release of a tool that began as a beta on 25 March 2026. It now anchors Binance Intelligence, a three-part stack unveiled on 5 October 2026 by Co-Chief Executive Richard Teng and VP of Product Jeff Li, alongside a consumer assistant called Binance AI and a developer layer, Binance Agent OS. Agent OS, which connects outside AI applications to Binance's trading, wallet, payments and on-chain services, launched on 20 August 2026 and had surpassed 280,000 daily calls within weeks. 
 Binance arrives late to a market its rivals spent 2026 building out. Coinbase  opened account access to third-party agents through Coinbase for Agents on 11 June 2026 and added a registered in-app advisor. Robinhood  introduced agent-led trading in May, Kraken  rebuilt its mobile app around agentic trading from July, Revolut  wired external assistants into its Revolut X exchange, and OKX  enabled agentic trading before Binance did. A common thread runs through all of them: the agent proposes, the human approves. 
 Jeff Li has framed Binance AI Pro in the same terms, describing it as a way to give users professional-style strategy building without code while leaving every deployment decision in their hands. On that measure, user control looks like the compliance-safe default the whole sector has settled on, rather than a Binance selling point. Binance's edge is scale: it is bringing agentic trading to the largest user base in the market. 
 Does "bounded autonomy" hold up? 
 The design follows a principle Binance has been road-testing with policymakers. Its Global Policy team published an insights paper this month, "Know Your Agent: Building the Compliance Layer for Agentic Finance," authored by Steven McWhirter and Diego Montes Serralde, setting out a five-layer accountability framework: agent identity and provenance, a link to an accountable principal, least-privilege mandates that separate the power to recommend from the authority to execute, authenticated delegation with monitoring, and revocation with a full audit trail. The guiding idea, that agents may analyse and recommend but consequential actions stay subject to rules the agent cannot override plus human sign-off, is exactly what the AI Strategy Account enforces in product form. 
 Regulators are moving the same way. The International Monetary Fund  has described the shift as moving from "click-to-pay" to "decide-to-pay," the Monetary Authority of Singapore  published a runtime-safeguards framework in July 2026, and the UK's Financial Services AI Adoption Plan explicitly recommends a Know Your Agent protocol for agentic payments. The open question is durability. Credit-metered pricing rewards running more automation, and the value of a hands-off agent grows the less a user intervenes. Whether human-approval gates hold once traders start letting strategies run unattended is the test that will decide how far bounded autonomy actually binds. Binance, for its part, states plainly that the agent's outputs are not advice and that users bear all risk for their decisions. 
 Why This Matters to FinanceX Readers 
 Agentic trading has gone from novelty to table stakes among exchanges in a single year, and Binance bringing it to a 300 million-user base turns a product story into a distribution one. 
 For investors, the read-through is a new engagement and revenue surface layered onto Binance's existing scale, defended against Coinbase, Kraken and Robinhood. For compliance and risk professionals, the sharper signal is that "bounded autonomy," human approval before execution, isolated funding, instant revocation, is hardening into both a product norm and a regulatory expectation. The firms that treat it as architecture rather than marketing are the ones positioned for whatever Know Your Agent rules arrive next.
