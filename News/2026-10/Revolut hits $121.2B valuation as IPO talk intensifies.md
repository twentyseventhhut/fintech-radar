$2,125 a share, as of October 15, 2026. That's the fresh Forge Global mark putting Revolut's implied value near $121.2 billion. 
Forge Global, the marketplace that prices private company shares, marked Revolut stock at $2,125 apiece on October 15, 2026, putting the company's implied valuation at roughly $121.2 billion. That's up from the $115 billion secondary sale Revolut confirmed just three months earlier, in July. Keep going back and the climb gets steeper: $45 billion in August 2024, $75 billion by November 2025, now past $120 billion. Few fintechs have moved that fast. And almost none while actually turning a profit.
This isn't a funding round. Nobody wrote Revolut a check this week. A secondary sale just means existing shareholders, often employees, are cashing out stock to outside buyers at a price those buyers agreed to pay, and Forge's mark captures where that market currently sits. But the number matters anyway, because it's the clearest signal yet of what investors think Revolut is worth heading into a public listing that CEO Nik Storonsky says is now about two years out.
The profits underneath that valuation are real and growing fast. Revolut's 2025 results, released in March, showed revenue up 46% to $6.0 billion and profit before tax up 57% to $2.3 billion, the company's fifth straight year in the black. Its customer base grew 30% to 68.3 million retail users, and 11 separate product lines each generated more than $135 million in revenue on their own. That's not a single hit product carrying the balance sheet. It's a bank with its fingers in currency exchange, crypto trading, stock trading, business banking and subscriptions, and most of them pulling their own weight.
According to the Financial Times, Storonsky has briefed investors that Revolut's internal target for an eventual IPO sits between $150 billion and $200 billion, though people close to the company stress no formal number has been locked in. He's also said a US listing is his preference, a notable call for a London-born fintech that has spent years lobbying UK regulators for a full banking license there.
Australia's APRA has granted Revolut Payments Australia a full, unrestricted banking licence, ending years of restricted status and letting the fintech offer government-guaranteed savings accounts and loans to its roughly one million Australian customers. The move puts Revolut in direct competition with the big four banks and Judo Bank, and adds... - Revolut gets banking licence Australia APRA - how Revolut disrupts Australian banks 
There's a personal stake in that $200 billion figure beyond pride. Storonsky has said in past interviews that his incentive package would hand him roughly 40% of the company if Revolut reaches that valuation, a stake that would be worth around $80 billion on paper. Whether or not the company gets there by 2028, the number is doing double duty: it's a target for the business and a very specific number for the man running it.
Here's the thing worth sitting with: Coinbase, one of the most recognizable names in crypto, trades at a market cap nowhere near $120 billion most days. Stripe, the payments company every founder name-drops, has climbed even higher, hitting a $159 billion valuation in its own tender offer earlier this year. Revolut, a company plenty of people still associate with a free currency-exchange card they got for a European trip, is now worth more than Coinbase outright and is closing the gap on Stripe. That's not a hype number pulled from a press release. It's backed by $2.3 billion in actual annual profit.
None of this guarantees the IPO lands at $200 billion, or that it lands by 2028 at all. Private valuations compress fast when public markets turn, and fintech multiples have been volatile for years. But the trajectory is the story here, not any single mark. A company that was worth $33 billion in its 2021 Series E has nearly quadrupled that in five years, through a pandemic, a rate-hiking cycle, and a crypto bust, while most of its neobank peers were still trying to find a path to breakeven. Revolut found one years ago. It's been compounding on it since.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Global neobanking has re-rated hard through 2025–26 as the leaders turned durably profitable and the fintech IPO window reopened (eToro, Circle, Chime all listed in 2025). No single clean TAM here — "no data" on a sourced market-size figure — but the structural read is clear: the segment is consolidating around a handful of scaled, profitable players (Revolut, Nubank) while sub-scale peers (Monzo, N26) still trade at a fraction of the leaders. Barriers are now capital + regulation + network/distribution: a full banking licence unlocks deposit funding and higher-margin lending, which is exactly why Revolut has lobbied for years for its UK licence and just won unrestricted status in Australia via APRA (per note). "Why now": the leaders have crossed into GAAP profit at scale, so the market is pricing them on earnings-backed growth rather than user-growth promise — the thing that kept neobank multiples volatile since the 2021–22 rate reset.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: retail customers, revenue growth, pre-tax profit / profitability (fifth straight profitable year for Revolut), and revenue-per-product-line (11 Revolut lines each >$135m). Key players by latest marks: Nubank (public, NYSE), Chime (public, NASDAQ), Monzo and N26 (private). Basis of competition: product breadth + geographic expansion + licence footprint rather than price. Recent peer moves: Monzo turned down a ~£10bn Nubank takeover and is shopping a minority stake to CVC/Advent/TPG at up to €11.8bn (per Payment Expert / Startup Fortune, Oct 2026); Nubank reached ~135–139m customers (per publiccomps / RioTimes, 2026). Revolut's position: ahead of the private peers on both scale and valuation, trailing only Nubank on customer count (68.3m vs ~135m) but ahead of it on headline valuation. Moat `(analysis)`: scale economics + multi-product cross-sell + an expanding licence map (switching costs rise as it becomes a primary account).

**Comps & multiples.** Revolut implied P/S on 2025 revenue: `$121.2bn / $6.0bn = ~20.2x` (secondary-market mark, not a market cap — treat as a round/secondary valuation).
- **Nubank** (public): ~$70bn market cap / ~$21bn LTM revenue = `~3.3x` revenue (per publiccomps / multiples.vc, Oct 2026).
- **Chime** (public): ~$11.4bn market cap / ~$2.77bn FY2026E revenue = `~4.1x` (per stockanalysis.com + Chime FY26 guidance).
- **Monzo** (private): ~€11.8bn target stake valuation / ~$2.28bn (£1.7bn) FY26 revenue = `~5.2x` (per Payment Expert / Monzo FY26 report).
- Internal comps: [[Revolut raises $3B at $75 billion valuation]] ($75bn secondary, Jul 2025 → +62% to $121.2bn in ~3 months), [[Chime shares surge 59% in IPO at $18.4B market cap]] ($18.4B day-one, down from a $25B 2021 private mark — the reset Revolut has avoided), [[Chime targets $9.5-11.2bn valuation in IPO]], [[Monzo said to weigh fresh share sale with Morgan Stanley]], [[Nu Holdings ranked #4 on Fortune fastest-growing companies]].
- Flag: Revolut's ~20x revenue is a **rich outlier** — ~5–6x the public neobank cohort (3–5x). Per the growth-multiple lens this is only partly defensible: Revolut grew revenue +46% to $6.0bn with +57% pre-tax profit, materially faster than Chime (~26–27%) or Monzo (+39%) and comparable to Nubank (~50%) — yet Nubank, growing as fast and already public, trades at 3.3x. The gap is a private/secondary-market premium, not a fundamentals gap; it would compress sharply on a public listing. Median not computed as a hard threshold given the public/private mix — qualitative: Revolut is priced well above where the public tape values the same growth.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Valuation vs comps / public re-rating risk** — a 20x private mark against public peers at 3–5x means an IPO would likely price below the secondary (cf. Chime's $25bn→$18.4bn step-down); the $150–200bn internal IPO target looks aggressive versus the current public cohort.
2. **Licence/regulatory execution** — the UK full banking licence has repeatedly stalled on risk-control concerns (per in-base notes); the valuation assumes it keeps converting restricted/payment licences into full bank status across markets, which is not guaranteed on schedule.
3. **Secondary-mark fragility** — a Forge mark reflects thin employee-liquidity flow, not a priced primary round; private marks "compress fast when public markets turn" (per note), so the headline is a sentiment reading, not a locked valuation.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a re-rating of the profitable-neobank leader, widening the gap to still-private peers (Monzo, N26) and pressuring them to either sell stakes or list. Falsifiable thesis: if Revolut IPOs at or below Nubank's revenue multiple (~3–5x), the eventual listing prints well under $150bn — watch the first priced primary round or S-1 revenue multiple as the trigger. The thesis is wrong if Revolut sustains ~45%+ growth with widening margins and the market extends it a durable premium the way it has not for Nubank.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (headline read).** BEAT / strong fundamental backdrop (not a fresh quarterly print — this is a secondary-market valuation mark; the underlying numbers are FY2025, reported March 2026) · revenue $6.0B (+46% YoY) · PBT $2.3B (+57% YoY), PBT margin 38% (up from 35%) · fifth straight profitable year · 68.3M retail customers (+30%). The $121.2B Forge mark is justified by real, growing profit — but it sits ~1.9–2.5x above the implied ~$50–65B a pure comparable-fintech revenue/earnings multiple would support (analysis), i.e. the market is pricing in continued hypergrowth and the IPO narrative, not current earnings.

**Key figures (FY2025, from Revolut Group Holdings Annual Report 2025, year ended 31 Dec 2025).**
- Revenue: $6.0B (£4.5B), +46% YoY vs $4.0B (£3.1B) FY2024. Growth across all streams.
- Profit before tax: $2.3B (£1.7B), +57% YoY. PBT margin 38% vs 35% in FY2024 — operating leverage, not just top-line.
- Retail customers: 68.3M, +30% YoY. Business customers: 767,000, +33% YoY (Revolut Business = 16% of total income).
- Customer balances: $67.5B, +66% YoY — the deposit base that underwrites the lending/interest-income flywheel.
- Transaction volume: ~$1.7T.
- Revenue diversification: 11 separate product lines each generated >~$135M (£100M) — no single-product dependency.

**By segment / driver.** Growth is broad-based: FX/currency exchange, crypto trading, stock trading, business banking, subscriptions — 11 lines each >$135M. The standout structural driver is the deposit base (+66% to $67.5B), which converts rising rates and balances into net interest income and funds credit expansion; Revolut Business (+33% customers, 16% of income) is the fastest-emerging second engine. This diversification is the de-PR point: the valuation is NOT riding a single hit product.

**vs expectations / prior period.** No equity-analyst consensus exists (private company, no public float). vs prior period the trajectory is accelerating profitability: PBT +57% outpaced revenue +46%, and margin expanded 35%→38% — operating leverage is real, not a one-off. Valuation path itself: $33B (2021 Series E) → $45B (Aug 2024) → $75B (Nov 2025) → $115B secondary (Jul 2026) → $121.2B Forge mark (15 Oct 2026, $2,125/share). Near-quadrupling in ~5 years. Coinbase public market cap was ~$45.7B (16 Oct 2026) and Stripe's last tender valuation ~$159B (Feb 2026) — Revolut now exceeds Coinbase outright and is closing on Stripe.

**Guidance / forward.** No formal IPO price locked. Per FT, Storonsky has briefed investors on an internal IPO target of $150B–$200B, US listing preferred, timeline ~2 years out (CEO). Plausibility: at $6.0B revenue the $200B top of the range implies ~33x trailing revenue — aggressive even for a profitable hypergrowth neobank; the $150B floor (~25x) is still rich. Sandbag/stretch read: the $200B figure also triggers Storonsky's incentive package (~40% stake, ~$80B on paper), so treat the upper bound as an aspirational anchor, not a base case (analysis). Separately, APRA granted Revolut Payments Australia a full unrestricted banking licence (operational, ~1M AU customers) — a concrete adoption step, not just an announcement.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. Margin expansion is the real tell — PBT +57% > revenue +46%, margin 35%→38%. Fact: operating leverage. Why: scale + deposit-funded NII. Why it matters: profitability is compounding faster than growth, which is what separates Revolut from breakeven-chasing neobank peers. Second-order: supports a premium multiple heading into IPO, but raises the bar — any margin stall would de-rate hard.
2. Valuation runs ahead of earnings. The $121.2B mark implies ~20x revenue / ~53x PBT; the IPO target ($150–200B) implies ~25–33x revenue. Flag: multiple compression risk if public markets turn before the ~2-year listing window — private marks compress fast (secondary liquidity is thin and the $2,125 mark reflects a specific trade, not a cleared book).
3. Deposit flywheel = rate exposure. $67.5B balances (+66%) power NII; a rate-cut cycle before IPO would pressure the interest-income line that is doing heavy lifting on the margin. Management touts balances; the filing's sensitivity to NIM in a falling-rate environment is the thing to watch.
4. Incentive-linked target (~$80B founder payout at $200B) means the headline IPO number is partly a management-signalling device — discount it vs the independently defensible ~$150B floor.
