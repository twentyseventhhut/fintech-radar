Collaboration expands access to bank account and digital wallet verification across global markets, helping financial institutions improve payment accuracy and strengthen trust in cross-border transactions. 
 LONDON and DUBAI, UAE , Oct. 22, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- TerraPay , a global money movement company, today announced it has integrated TerraPay Verify , its payee verification solution, into Kinexys by J.P. Morgan's Liink Confirm application , enabling participants on the Kinexys Liink network and TerraPay Verify clients to validate bank account and digital wallet details across a broad range of global markets.
Through the collaboration, both financial institutions and non-bank financial institutions connected to the Kinexys Liink network will gain access to TerraPay Verify's extensive bank account and wallet verification capabilities, helping validate recipient details before payments are initiated.
As cross-border payments continue to grow, ensuring beneficiary accuracy has become critical. True Impact of Failed Payments 2026 study from LexisNexis Risk Solutions estimates that 10-15% of international payments fail due to incorrect beneficiary details, leading to settlement delays, increased operational costs, and greater exposure to fraud . This challenge is amplified as payments increasingly move across a mix of bank accounts and digital wallets, making recipient verification a key enabler of faster, more reliable money movement.
TerraPay's collaboration with Kinexys by J.P. Morgan – the firm's industry-leading blockchain business unit – reflects a shared commitment to bringing greater transparency, trust, and efficiency to what can often be an opaque cross-border payments landscape. By helping organizations verify recipients before funds are sent, the solution supports global efforts to improve payment transparency, accessibility, and reach, helping align with the broader objectives set by the G20 roadmap for enhancing cross-border payments while helping banks worldwide deliver a more seamless customer experience.
 Paul Atkinson, Head of Kinexys Liink Product, Kinexys by J.P. Morgan  said, "By integrating Kinexys Liink's Confirm application with TerraPay Verify, we're making it easier for financial institutions and non-bank financial institutions to improve control and help reduce payment fraud throughout the cross-border payment lifecycle. Together, we're supporting institutions as they adapt to a rapidly changing landscape and build greater trust with every transaction."
The collaboration also expands the reach of TerraPay Verify among global institutions though the Kinexys Liink network, while reinforcing TerraPay's commitment to building trusted payment infrastructure through interoperability.
"Trust is fundamental to every successful payment," said David Backshall, VP, Payment Partnerships at TerraPay . "As payment ecosystems continue to evolve, financial institutions need a reliable way to verify recipients across both bank accounts and digital wallets. Our collaboration with Kinexys by J.P. Morgan extends the reach of TerraPay Verify to more institutions globally, helping improve payment certainty, reduce avoidable exceptions and support a better customer experience."
The collaboration reflects TerraPay's continued focus on embedding trust into global money movement through compliance, verification and interoperable infrastructure, while creating opportunities to expand verification coverage through strategic ecosystem partnerships.

# Context-enrichment: TerraPay integrates Verify with J.P. Morgan's Kinexys Liink
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-22 TerraPay said it integrated **TerraPay Verify** (its payee/account-validation product, live in ~87 markets, pitched as reaching 7.5bn bank accounts across 156+ countries and 3.7bn wallets via its Xend network) into **Confirm**, the account-validation application that runs on **Kinexys by J.P. Morgan's Liink** network (Liink = the former Onyx/Liink information-exchange network; Kinexys is JPM's rebranded blockchain unit). Mechanism: participants on Liink/Confirm can now call TerraPay Verify to pre-validate a beneficiary's bank-account or wallet details *before* the payment is initiated, so that fewer cross-border payments bounce on wrong payee data.
- **De-PR'd core:** this is a **data-supply/distribution deal**, not a new product and not a blockchain story. Confirm already existed as the validation app on Liink; TerraPay becomes one more verification *source* reachable through it (alongside JPM's other Confirm feeds). TerraPay in turn gets distribution to Liink's bank + non-bank members. No clients, no live volumes, no economics, no exclusivity, and no go-live date are disclosed — the release reads "enabling"/"will gain access", i.e. **capability announced, adoption unproven**.
- **Why framed this way / what it reveals:** the only hard number is third-party — the LexisNexis Risk Solutions *True Impact of Failed Payments 2026* figure that 10–15% of international payments fail on wrong beneficiary details. That is the market-sizing hook because neither side will quote its own Confirm/Verify usage. The repeated "G20 roadmap for enhancing cross-border payments" language is positioning for regulators/correspondent banks, not a disclosed commitment. Two levels down: JPM is assembling Confirm as a *federated validation layer* (many verification providers plugged in) rather than building global coverage itself — TerraPay fills the emerging-market wallet/bank gaps JPM's own data can't. The real question is not "is verification useful" (obviously yes) but **whose coverage + liability model wins the global Confirmation-of-Payee layer**.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Pre-payment payee/account validation ("Know-Your-Payee", Confirmation-of-Payee, VoP) is a crowded, fast-consolidating layer:
- **iPiD** — the pure-play reference. Raised ~$16M backed by Citi + HSBC; **Citi Verify** extended into new markets via iPiD (Nov 2025). Direct corpus prior: [[Tazapay integrates iPiD payee verification for cross-border payments]] (May 2026, 70+ markets). The sharpest competitor to TerraPay Verify as an independent global validation feed.
- **Swift** — Swift GPI pre-validation / beneficiary account verification is the incumbent correspondent-banking answer; the mutualised bank-owned alternative to JPM's private Confirm network.
- **SurePay, Banfico, LSEG, Trulioo, tell.money** — regional/CoP and VoP specialists. Corpus: [[Moniepoint partners tell.money on UK Confirmation of Payee]], [[PayPoint partners SmarterPay on Confirmation of Payee fraud checks]] (both UK-CoP flavoured).
- **Nium + Kinexys** — Nium already plugged **account-validation services into Kinexys/Liink** for Malaysia, Thailand, Hong Kong. This is the key tell: **TerraPay is not the first validation provider on Confirm** — it is the latest, extending coverage into TerraPay's wallet/MTO strongholds (Africa, MENA, LatAm, South Asia). Position vs peers: **parity / catch-up on the network, differentiated on wallet + emerging-market reach.**
- **Why the landscape is this way:** validation is a coverage game with weak network effects at the provider level — no single feed covers every corridor, so aggregators (Confirm, Swift) multi-source. Second-order: that keeps verification a thin, commoditising data-toll; margin accrues to whoever owns the *aggregation point and the fraud-liability contract* (JPM's Confirm), not necessarily the data supplier (TerraPay).

## [2] Company history / fit
TerraPay is a London/Dubai money-movement network built on serial partnership deals — a dense corpus trail: [[TerraPay launches Xend to power borderless payments for billions of wallet users]], [[Mastercard collaborates with TerraPay on wallet acceptance]], [[National Bank of Bahrain partners TerraPay for international payouts]], plus 2025–26 corridor tie-ups (Cobre, Fipto, Reap, Raenest, MilX, Banco Ganadero, D360, OMT, botim). Verify is the logical **monetisation of the data exhaust** from running that payout rail: having to validate accounts/wallets to settle payouts, TerraPay repackages that lookup capability as a standalone verification product and now rents it to JPM's network.
- **Why it acts this way:** cross-border payout is a low-take-rate, capital-and-compliance-heavy commodity; attaching a verification data-service is a push for **higher-margin, recurring, asset-light revenue** and for embedding into tier-1 bank rails (JPM) that lend credibility. Fits the strategy cleanly; it is incremental, not transformative.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** The capability (pre-payment verification, failed-payment reduction) is well-established; TerraPay Verify itself is not new; Confirm is not new; another provider (Nium) is already on it. What is new is *this specific supply relationship* and the added emerging-market/wallet coverage it brings to Confirm. Genuinely a **fresh event** (new terms, new counterparties) but a **small one**.
- **Traction: none disclosed.** No named client, no corridors live at launch, no volume, no conversion-uplift stat, no revenue split. "Enabling / will gain access" = announcement, not adoption.
- **Who captures the margin (2 levels deep):** verification data is close to a commodity toll; the durable value sits with the party that (a) owns the aggregation/routing layer and (b) carries the fraud-liability contract with the paying bank — that is JPM's Confirm, not TerraPay. TerraPay monetises distribution volume but risks being a **replaceable data feed** the moment a cheaper/broader source (iPiD, local schemes) covers the same corridors. Real value-add is real but **thin and contestable**.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch for: (1) named Liink/Confirm participants actually consuming TerraPay Verify and any live-corridor list; (2) whether pricing is per-lookup or revenue-shared; (3) how this sits against regulatory CoP/VoP mandates (EU instant-payments VoP from Oct 2025, UK CoP) that structurally lift validation demand. Sentiment: neutral-positive, infrastructure plumbing. **Why the market goes this way:** mandated pre-payment verification (EU VoP, G20 roadmap) is turning validation from nice-to-have into table-stakes → demand grows, but precisely because it becomes mandatory and multi-sourced it **commoditises** → counterintuitive second-order: the regulatory tailwind that drives volume also compresses per-lookup margin and favours aggregators over suppliers.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Payee verification / payment pre-validation ("Know Your Payee") sits as a thin trust layer on top of cross-border payments. Sizing of the verification layer itself is **no data** (no credible free TAM for KYP specifically); the underlying flow it rides is large and growing — non-wholesale cross-border payments are projected at ~$64.5tn by 2032, ~6.2% CAGR (per secondary citation, via Swift/industry press, as of 2026) `[figure sourced to secondary press, treat as indicative]`. The sector structure is network-driven and fragmenting into competing verification rails: Swift's Payment Pre-validation (claims up to ~11,000 institutions / ~4bn accounts / 200 countries), the EPC's VoP scheme (2,782 participants as of Nov-2025 per EPC), national Confirmation-of-Payee schemes (UK Pay.UK, ~400 PSPs mandated from Oct-2024), and private KYP networks (iPiD: ~6,500 FIs / ~4bn accounts / ~30 countries, per iPiD). Entry barrier = network coverage (number of destination account/wallet data sources), not capital. **Why now:** regulation is forcing the layer into existence — EU Instant Payments Regulation (EU 2024/886) mandated VoP for euro credit transfers from 9-Oct-2025, dragging verification from "nice-to-have" to a legal precondition; the G20 roadmap (cited in the release) pushes the same for cross-border. Second-order effect: once mandated, verification becomes a commoditised utility where the only durable differentiation is destination-corridor and wallet coverage — exactly where TerraPay is strong.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: verification coverage (accounts/wallets reachable, countries), match/confirmation hit-rate, latency, and false-reject rate — TerraPay discloses none of these for Verify (`[UNSOURCED]`), only its payout-network reach (7.5bn+ accounts, 156+ countries, 3.7bn wallets via Xend; company figures, not independently verified). Players split into (a) interbank utilities — Swift Pre-validation, EPC VoP, Pay.UK CoP; (b) private KYP/API networks — iPiD (partnered with Visa), Yapily (open-banking account verification, signed Google Oct-2025), PayPoint/SmarterPay CoP in the UK; (c) payout networks bolting on verification — TerraPay Verify, Tazapay (integrated iPiD, May-2026). Basis of competition = coverage + distribution, not price. Recent moves: Tazapay+iPiD (May-2026); Swift VoP go-live and EU mandate (Oct-2025); Deutsche Bank VoP go-live (Apr-2026). **Protagonist's position:** niche-but-well-placed on wallet/EM corridors (its moat = destination coverage / network effects in wallet-heavy Africa/Asia/Gulf corridors where Swift/CoP are thin), but a distribution-taker, not owner, here — it is plugging Verify *into* J.P. Morgan's Kinexys Liink rather than running its own network (analysis).

Internal comps (base precedents for the pre-validation / payout-partnership pattern): [[Tazapay integrates iPiD payee verification for cross-border payments]], [[Yapily signs Google for bank account verification]], [[PayPoint partners SmarterPay on Confirmation of Payee fraud checks]], and on the Kinexys counterparty [[National Bank of Bahrain adopts JPMorgan's Kinexys]], [[Qatar National Bank taps JPMorgan Kinexys for dollar settlements]]. TerraPay's own prior distribution plays: [[TerraPay launches Xend to power borderless payments for billions of wallet users]], [[Mastercard collaborates with TerraPay on wallet acceptance]].

**Risk flags.**
1. **Commoditisation / margin capture by the rail owner.** Verification is becoming a regulatory utility; TerraPay rides on J.P. Morgan's Kinexys Liink as a plugged-in data provider, so the network (Kinexys) owns the customer relationship and the economics — TerraPay risks being a thin, interchangeable data feed. No pricing/revenue-share disclosed (who monetises the lookup is silent).
2. **Concentration on one distribution partner / disintermediation.** Reach here is contingent on Kinexys Liink adoption; if J.P. Morgan sources verification from multiple vendors (or builds in-house), TerraPay's slot is substitutable. Second-order: dependence on someone else's rails caps pricing power.
3. **Competing-standard fragmentation.** Swift Pre-validation, EPC VoP and national CoP schemes overlap the same job; a converged mandated standard could absorb the private KYP layer, squeezing independent providers into pure coverage-of-last-resort for exotic corridors.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Confirms the "verification-as-mandated-utility" thesis: value migrates to whoever owns destination-account/wallet *coverage* while the lookup itself commoditises (analysis). Watch next: whether TerraPay monetises Verify as a standalone revenue line (vs. a free trust-add to win payout volume) and whether more bank networks beyond Kinexys sign it — a second major network win would validate it as a neutral coverage layer; failure to disclose any economics or add networks would confirm it's a loss-leader bolt-on. Falsifiable: thesis breaks if Swift/EPC VoP coverage expands into EM wallet corridors, erasing TerraPay's coverage edge.
