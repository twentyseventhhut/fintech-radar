Stablecoins could be attractive for certain remittance corridors and less commonly traded currency pairs, Mastercard's Matthew Driver said.
Faster stablecoin payments depend on infrastructure supporting conversions between fiat currencies and stablecoins, Driver added.
Mastercard completed BVNK acquisition in August and launched new stablecoin payment and security solutions last week.
In this article
 MA 
Stablecoins could offer faster and more efficient payments, but realizing their full potential requires further development of the infrastructure supporting them, according to Mastercard.
Stablecoins, a type of cryptocurrency designed to keep a steady value via a peg to an asset like the U.S. dollar, could be attractive for certain remittance corridors and less commonly traded currency pairs, Matthew Driver, executive vice president of services for Asia Pacific at Mastercard, told CNBC in an interview.
"You don't want to have a 'stablecoin sandwich,'" Driver said, referring to a slow on-ramp into stablecoins, a fast transaction in the middle and a slow off-ramp back into fiat currency. That won't necessarily translate into faster payments, he said.
Connecting real-time payment systems to stablecoin exchanges on both ends of a transaction could help speed up payments, but "you still need to enable the infrastructure," he said, adding that market participants need to be able to use the technology effectively.
Opportunities for stablecoin adoption could vary across markets. In Singapore, where retail payments are already highly efficient, stablecoins may have fewer applications in consumer transactions, while corporate payments could offer greater opportunities, Driver said.
He also pointed to smart contracts as a potential use case for corporate payments, allowing transactions to be executed when certain conditions are met.
Interest in stablecoins has increased following the establishment of the U.S. GENIUS Act, Driver said.
His comments come as Mastercard expands its involvement in digital assets, including through its acquisition of stablecoin infrastructure provider BVNK, which was completed in August. Earlier this month, Mastercard and BVNK launched Global Money Platform, designed to help banks, fintechs and businesses manage payments involving traditional currencies and stablecoins through a single application programming interface.
Beyond payment speed, Mastercard is also looking to incorporate blockchain and identity data to establish the same level of trust expected across other payment systems, Driver said.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-10-27, Matthew Driver — Mastercard's EVP of Services for Asia Pacific — told CNBC that stablecoins can make payments faster and more efficient but will not deliver on that promise until the surrounding infrastructure matures. His headline phrase: beware the "stablecoin sandwich" — a slow fiat-to-stablecoin on-ramp, a fast on-chain transfer in the middle, and a slow stablecoin-to-fiat off-ramp. Net of the two slow ends, total settlement time need not improve. Driver argued the fix is connecting real-time payment systems to stablecoin exchanges on both ends, plus ensuring participants can actually use the rails. He framed the best near-term fit as cross-border/remittance corridors and thinly-traded currency pairs, and corporate payments (e.g. smart-contract conditional release), rather than retail in already-efficient markets like Singapore. He credited the U.S. GENIUS Act with lifting institutional interest. Sources: <https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/27/mastcard-stablecoin-bvnk-.html>, <https://www.tekedia.com/mastercard-says-stablecoin-payments-need-better-infrastructure-to-deliver-on-speed-promise/>.

**[1] Why it matters / what's new.** This is a notable piece of self-interested candor: the comments land ~2 weeks after Mastercard + BVNK launched the Global Money Platform (2026-10-15) and ~2 months after Mastercard closed its up-to-$1.8bn BVNK acquisition (Aug 2026). So the "warning" is also a pitch — the on/off-ramp friction Driver describes is precisely what BVNK's single-API fiat/stablecoin orchestration, Mastercard Move payouts (claimed ~17bn endpoints, card issuing in 130+ countries), and 24/7 multi-stablecoin settlement are positioned to sell against. In other words, Mastercard is monetizing the "last-mile conversion" gap it is publicly flagging. GMP early adopters cited: Nuvei, Marqeta, Ontop, Beem, Almond FinTech. Sources: <https://www.mastercard.com/global/en/news-and-trends/press/2026/october/mastercard-and-bvnk-launch-global-money-platform-to-power-a-mult.html>, <https://www.pymnts.com/partnerships/2026/mastercard-and-bvnk-unite-fiat-and-stablecoins-in-global-money-platform/>.

**[2] Prior context (internal).** This caution is a recurring Mastercard refrain, not a reversal. See [[Mastercard says stablecoins still face mainstream hurdles]] (2025-07, CPO Jorn Lambert making the same "not yet mainstream" point) and the acquisition [[Mastercard to acquire stablecoin firm BVNK for up to $1.8bn]] (2026-03). On the "promote" side: [[Mastercard expands settlement to include stablecoins and weekends]] (2026-06, opening settlement to multiple regulated stablecoins + weekend/holiday settlement), [[Mastercard joins Paxos Global Dollar Network]] and [[Mastercard expands stablecoin push with Paxos, Fiserv and PayPal]] (2025-06, PYUSD/USDG/FIUSD integrations), and [[Mastercard CEO says Ripple settlement moving to real-world execution]] (2026-02). The pattern: steadily build acceptance + settlement rails while repeatedly managing expectations on timing.

**[3] Competitive / structural.** Mastercard's multi-stablecoin settlement stance (opened its settlement layer to six regulated tokens — USDC, RLUSD, PYUSD, USDG, USDP, SoFiUSD — across eight blockchains, 24/7, from June 2026) contrasts with Visa, whose stablecoin settlement is reportedly larger today (~$7bn annualized run rate, nine chains by April 2026, USDC-centric). The strategic tension both networks share: stablecoins can disintermediate the very card-network settlement economics they sit on, which is why both are reportedly backing a jointly-affiliated stablecoin effort (Forbes, 2026-10). Driver's "infrastructure gaps" message is thus partly a moat argument — the networks' trust, FX, compliance and 17bn-endpoint distribution are the hard part, not the on-chain hop. Sources: <https://www.spotedcrypto.com/mastercard-stablecoin-settlement-expansion-2026/>, <https://www.forbes.com/sites/digital-assets/2026/10/09/why-visa-and-mastercard-backed-a-stablecoin-that-could-cut-into-their-own-business/>.

**[4] So what / watch items.** Watch: (a) whether GMP posts concrete corridor/volume metrics vs. BVNK's pre-deal run-rate; (b) whether Mastercard discloses stablecoin settlement volume the way Visa does (~$7bn), since Mastercard has not published a comparable figure; (c) GENIUS Act implementation effects on which stablecoins qualify for settlement; (d) the "stablecoin sandwich" claim is a thesis, not data — no quantified on/off-ramp latency was given. Treat this as strategy-signalling commentary (type/commentary), not a product or financial event.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is this news at all, or restated boilerplate? Mastercard's CPO Jorn Lambert said essentially the same "not yet mainstream" thing in 2025-07. What, if anything, is incrementally new beyond the BVNK/GMP timing peg?
2. Conflict of interest: Driver is selling the cure (BVNK/Global Money Platform) for the disease (on/off-ramp friction) he describes. How much of the "infrastructure gaps" framing is analysis vs. marketing for GMP?
3. Is the "stablecoin sandwich" quantified? No latency numbers were given. Without data on on-ramp/off-ramp times vs. incumbent rails, is the claim falsifiable?
4. Scope: these are APAC-services comments (Singapore example). Do they generalize to the remittance corridors (e.g. US–LatAm, US–Philippines) where stablecoins already show measurable wins, or is the example cherry-picked to markets where Mastercard's existing rails are hard to beat?
5. Counter-evidence: competitors/issuers (Circle, Visa's ~$7bn settlement run-rate, PYUSD) claim real throughput today. Does live volume contradict the "infrastructure not ready" thesis?
6. Does GMP actually solve the off-ramp problem, or just repackage BVNK's existing orchestration under Mastercard branding? What is genuinely new vs. pre-acquisition BVNK?
7. Endpoint/reach claims ("17bn endpoints", "130+ countries") are Mastercard marketing figures — are any independently verified or load-bearing for the story?
8. Is there a disclosed Mastercard stablecoin settlement volume to benchmark seriousness, or only Visa's? Absence is itself telling.
9. Strategic framing risk: is "networks own the hard part (trust/FX/compliance)" a real moat or a defensive narrative against disintermediation that the Forbes joint-stablecoin story undercuts?
10. Sourcing: both note sources are a single CNBC interview + Mastercard's generic homepage (not the GMP press release). Is the story over-reliant on one outlet's framing?
11. GENIUS Act causal claim: Driver attributes rising interest to GENIUS. Is that supported, or a convenient regulatory tailwind narrative?
12. Does the note conflate three distinct things — consumer payment speed, cross-border remittance, and bank-to-bank network settlement — which have very different "infrastructure" needs?
13. Timing: is 2026-10-27 commentary actually downstream of the 2026-10-15 GMP launch PR cycle (i.e. a planned follow-on), making it promotional rather than spontaneous?
14. Any sign Mastercard is retreating from stablecoins? No — all evidence (BVNK, GMP, multi-token settlement) is expansion. So "warns" in the headline may overstate caution.

**Importance: 3/5** — Credible, well-sourced strategy signal from a top-2 network tying its public "infrastructure gaps" caution directly to a just-launched product (GMP/BVNK), which is genuinely informative about how incumbents intend to monetize stablecoin rails. But it is commentary, not a product/financial event; it restates a long-standing Mastercard refrain; it carries an obvious sell-the-cure conflict; and it offers no quantified data. Useful context, not market-moving.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Stablecoin payments are the fastest-growing adjacency to card rails. Total stablecoin supply reached ~$305–308bn by Aug–Sep 2026, up ~14% YoY from ~$269bn (per Spark/Transak trackers, as of Aug 2026), with Citigroup/US Treasury projections cited at ~$420bn by year-end. The payments use-case specifically is scaling faster: cross-border stablecoin transfers grew ~77% YoY to ~$220bn in the 12 months to June 2026, and total stablecoin payments ran ~$401–527bn Jan–Aug 2026 (+42–63% YoY) (per blindpay/reap aggregations). Structure: the stablecoin stack is still fragmented and immature at the edges — the value is captured on-chain in transfer, but the "last mile" (fiat on/off-ramp, FX conversion, compliance) remains the bottleneck Driver flags. His "stablecoin sandwich" point is the core structural insight: a slow fiat on-ramp + fast chain leg + slow off-ramp nets out to no speed gain, so the incumbents' moat shifts from the ledger to the ramps + acceptance + fraud/identity layer. Why now: the US GENIUS Act (cited by Driver) legitimised issuance and pulled banks/corporates in, accelerating the 2025–26 adoption curve.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: GDV/TPV, cross-border volume, take rate on new-flows, and value-added-services (VAS) attach. Key players split three ways — (1) incumbent networks extending rails: Mastercard (BVNK, Global Money Platform launched Oct 2026) and Visa (stablecoin settlement live, ~$4.5bn annualised run-rate as of Feb 2026, four stablecoins/four chains); (2) stablecoin-native infra: Circle (USDC), Paxos, BVNK (now MA), Bridge (Stripe, 2024); (3) issuer/program layer: Nium (stablecoin card issuance on Visa/MC, Apr 2026), Fiserv/PayPal (PYUSD/FIUSD/USDG integrated into MC, Jun 2025). Basis of competition = distribution + acceptance network + compliance, not raw settlement speed. Recent moves: MA completed BVNK (Aug 2026) and launched the single-API Global Money Platform (Oct 2026); Stripe/Visa/MC were reported near a joint stablecoin platform (Jun 2026). Mastercard's position: ahead/defensive — it is co-opting rather than resisting, using its ~150m+ acceptance footprint + fraud/identity + VAS as the moat (network effects + switching costs). Driver explicitly notes MC wants to carry its trust/identity layer onto blockchain rails — i.e. defend the ramp and acceptance layers, concede the ledger.

**Comps & multiples.** MA (mkt cap ~$540bn, 20 Oct 2026) trades ~33–35x trailing P/E; Visa (~$728bn) ~31x TTM–36x (per macrotrends/financecharts, Oct 2026). On MA's own Q2'26 base — net revenue $9.3bn (+14%), GDV +8% LC to $2.9tn, VAS net revenue +20% — the stablecoin exposure is tiny vs the $2.9tn GDV it processes, so the 33x multiple reflects the card-flywheel + VAS, not crypto optionality. Internal comps (base): [[Visa stablecoin settlement reaches $4.5bn annualized run rate]] ($4.5bn annualised = <0.1% of Visa's volume → confirms early-stage), [[Mastercard and Visa extend support for stablecoin settlement]], [[Mastercard expands stablecoin push with Paxos, Fiserv and PayPal]], [[Stripe, Visa and Mastercard near joint stablecoin platform]]. Pure-play stablecoin disruptor multiples (Circle etc.) → no verifiable clean figure here → no data. Networks screen in-line with each other and rich vs the broad market (~20x) — justified by 14–20% revenue growth + >50% operating margins; MA cheaper than Visa on P/E despite faster EPS CAGR (~15.8% vs ~12.5% 2025–28, per analyst consensus via Motley Fool).

**Risk flags.** (1) Merchant-led disintermediation — the real tail risk is not consumers but large merchants (Amazon/Walmart/Shopify) routing checkout over cheaper on-chain rails to bypass interchange; this directly threatens MA's take rate on the ~$2.9tn GDV base, the opposite second-order of Driver's reassuring framing. (2) Ramp-layer commoditisation — if real-time-payment systems connect directly to stablecoin exchanges (the fix Driver himself describes), the on/off-ramp margin MA is buying via BVNK could compress, turning a moat into a toll others undercut. (3) Narrative/execution gap — "announced vs live": Global Money Platform is a single API launched Oct 2026, not proven adopted volume; Driver's own caveat (infra "still needs enabling") signals the product is early, so near-term revenue contribution is immaterial and the $1.8bn BVNK price is an option, not a cash-flow line.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a defensive co-option play, not a re-rating catalyst (analysis): MA is converting a disruption threat into an acceptance/orchestration business, keeping value at the ramp + identity + acceptance layer while conceding the ledger. Watch next: first disclosed stablecoin/BVNK flow or VAS-line contribution in an MA earnings release, and any direct large-merchant stablecoin-acceptance deal that bypasses the networks — the latter would falsify the "incumbents keep the toll" thesis. Trigger: GDV growth decoupling from stablecoin payment growth (if card GDV slows while stablecoin B2B flows accelerate, disintermediation is real).

**vs expectations / prior period.** Beat on both lines vs public consensus (EPS $5.04 vs ~$4.77; revenue $9.3bn vs ~$9.06bn; aggregator figures as of the 2026-07-31 report). Beat looks operationally driven (volume + VAS), not a one-off. Sequential context: Q1 2026 printed net income $3.9bn / diluted EPS $4.35 with GDV +7%, cross-border +13%, switched transactions +9% — so Q2 shows steady-to-accelerating earnings with cross-border decelerating slightly (13%→12%) and GDV ticking up (7%→8%).

**Guidance / forward.** The release did not headline a quantified full-year/next-quarter revenue or EPS target; management framed the quarter as "above expectations" with net revenue growth of 14% (12% cc). Real forward read comes with Q3 2026 on 2026-10-29 — watch whether cross-border holds ~12% and whether VAS stays ~20%, plus any first commentary quantifying digital-asset / stablecoin contribution. Magnitude of forward guidance beyond this: [UNSOURCED].

**Thesis-flags (tied to the stablecoin story).**
1. Stablecoins are positioned as *additive*, not cannibalistic, to the base that drives earnings. Fact: cross-border +12% and VAS +20% are what moved Q2 → why: Mastercard is monetizing the "stablecoin sandwich" problem (slow fiat on/off-ramps) by selling the rails and conversion/settlement infrastructure (BVNK, Global Money Platform) → why it matters: stablecoin volume that still needs fiat conversion routes back through Mastercard's network and services → second-order: a stablecoin "threat" is being repackaged as a VAS/money-movement revenue opportunity, consistent with VAS being the fastest-growing segment.
2. Mix shift toward VAS (+20% vs network +10%) is the durable margin/growth story — the stablecoin-security and digital-asset products land here, so adoption shows up as VAS acceleration before it shows up in network volume.
3. De-PR: the commentary is forward-looking ("still need to enable the infrastructure"), and the quarter carries NO disclosed stablecoin revenue line — treat digital-asset contribution as strategic optionality, not yet a reported driver. Management is vocal on opportunity, silent on near-term stablecoin economics.
4. Watch item for Q3 (2026-10-29): first post-BVNK-close quarter — any quantified digital-asset/Move commentary or cross-border deceleration would be the real signal.
