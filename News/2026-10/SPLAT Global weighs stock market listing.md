# SPLAT Global weighs stock market listing

> [!info] 2026-10-12 · 1 упоминаний · 0 источника(ов) с текстом
> Каналы: Mergers.ru

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Mergers.ru] 🪥 SPLAT Global рассматривает сценарий выхода на биржу. «У нас есть в стратегии такая опция. Мы уже давно аудированы, у нас все готово, технически мы даже сейчас к этому готовы, но понятно, что, скорее, не готов к этому рынок. Но сейчас следующий цикл бюджетирования у нас идет трехлетний, и для 2029 года это вполне рабочая опция», — рассказал генеральный директор и совладелец компании Евгений Демин. Как уточнили в SPLAT Global, в случае принятия решения по IPO потребуется перерегистрация ООО «Сплат Глобал» в форму акционерного общества. SPLAT Global основана в 2000 году предпринимателями Евгением и Еленой Демиными. Свою продукцию компания экспортирует более чем в 70 стран. В 2025 году выручка составила около 20 млрд рублей, в 2026-м ожидается 24-25 млрд рублей, прогнозирует Демин. По разным оценкам, доступным компании, говорит Демин, SPLAT Global занимает 28-29% рынка oral care (средств ухода за полости рта) и является «№1 в пастах и №1 в щетках».

# Context-enrichment: SPLAT Global weighs stock market listing
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
This is NOT an IPO announcement. It is CEO/co-owner Evgeny Demin saying, at an investment forum,
that a listing is "an option in our strategy" and a "working option for 2029" — i.e. ~3 years out,
contingent on market conditions he himself calls not-ready. The only concrete prerequisites named:
the company is already audited and would need to re-register from an LLC ("ООО Сплат Глобал") into a
joint-stock company (АО) if it ever proceeds. No mandate, no bankers, no filing, no price range, no
exchange formally named (implicitly Moscow Exchange / MOEX, the only realistic venue for a Russian
consumer issuer in 2026).
- **Why framed this way (→ what it reveals):** Demin was explicit that an IPO is "not a goal in
  itself" but a *tool* to finance a large deal, capex, or entry into new markets. Translation: there
  is no capital need today — this is optionality signalling, not a funding event. Flagging 2029
  conveniently pushes it beyond the current weak-IPO window and the next 3-year budget cycle, so the
  statement costs the company nothing and commits it to nothing.
- **Numbers (de-PR'd):** 2025 revenue ~20bn RUB; 2026 *forecast* 24–25bn RUB (growth projection, not
  a result). Claimed 28–29% share of the Russian oral-care market, "#1 in pastes and #1 in brushes."
  Founded 2000 by Evgeny and Elena Demin; exports to 70+ countries. Note a discrepancy worth flagging:
  external filings (TAdviser) show the *Russian legal entity* "Splat Global LLC" at only ~11.76bn RUB
  revenue for 2024 (+13% YoY) — so the ~20bn figure is almost certainly the consolidated group
  (including the Capella factory and export entities), not the single OOO that would be re-registered.
- **Valuation anchor (the real tell):** Demin floated a valuation of ~2x annual revenue, explicitly
  benchmarked to US beauty names **e.l.f. Beauty** and **Olaplex**, with a free float around 20%. On
  2026 revenue that implies a ~48–50bn RUB ask. This is the aspirational part and the weakest claim
  (see [1]).

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **The chosen comps undercut the pitch.** e.l.f. Beauty is a genuine US growth compounder (IPO 2016
  at $17; later acquired Rhode for up to $1bn) and trades on a software-like multiple because of
  double-digit organic growth and Gen-Z share gains. **Olaplex is the cautionary tale:** priced at a
  ~$13.6–15bn valuation in its Sept-2021 IPO, then collapsed ~90% as a single-category "hero-product"
  story faded; it was later taken out for ~$1.4bn. Citing Olaplex as a valuation anchor is
  double-edged — the P/S multiple SPLAT wants only existed at the *top* and did not survive. A 2x-revenue
  multiple is a mid-cycle consumer-staples multiple, not a premium beauty multiple, so Demin's own math
  is already conservative relative to his stated comps (analysis).
- **Domestic IPO peers / window:** Russian primary market is thin. 2025 saw only ~3 IPOs / 3 SPOs on
  MOEX (Glorax ~RUB2bn, DOM.RF ~RUB31bn), all non-resource. 2026's first IPO (B2B-RTS) priced at a
  heavy discount, and large portfolio managers declined, citing poor conditions and sub-scale deals
  (<RUB10bn is a liquidity problem). A ~20% float of a ~50bn-RUB company would be a ~10bn-RUB deal —
  right at the lower bound of what institutions will touch. [[Mergers.ru Gref advises state companies on successful IPOs]]
  (same-week Gref remarks: "don't be greedy," IPO is a hard way to raise capital) is the market mood music.
- **Oral-care rivals (context):** post-2022, Western incumbents (P&G/Oral-B, Colgate, GSK/Haleon
  Sensodyne) have scaled back direct Russian presence, which is exactly how SPLAT got to a claimed
  ~28–29% share — the share is partly a sanctions-dividend, not purely competitive win (analysis).

## [2] Company history / fit
SPLAT is a 25-year-old founder-run FMCG brand (toothpaste, brushes, floss, mouthwash, eco household
chemicals, kids' cosmetics; factories in Novgorod Oblast + Dzerzhinsk/Capella acquired 2022). It is a
rare Russian consumer brand with real export reach and a distinctive marketing identity (founder
letters in packs, no TV). The IPO musing fits a predictable founder arc: a mature, cash-generative,
fully-owned business with no external VC/PE investor clock. **Why float at all, then?** The honest
read from Demin's own words: there is no forced exit and no capital gap — so the listing only makes
sense as (a) a currency for a large acquisition, or (b) partial founder liquidity / succession
planning. Absent either trigger, "2029 option" is the default answer founders give to avoid saying "no."

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: essentially nil.** This is a verbal "we might, in ~3 years, if the market turns" — one of
  the lowest-information events in the IPO-pipeline genre. There is real *business* traction (growing
  revenue, category leadership, audited books, export footprint), but **no IPO-process traction**: no
  banks, no AO conversion done, no roadshow, no float decision.
- **Why the value-add is thin (→ deeper):** The durable asset is the brand and distribution, not any
  listing mechanics. For public-market value the question is margin quality and growth durability of a
  domestic-staples business whose share partly rests on competitor withdrawal — if Western oral-care
  brands ever re-enter, the 28–29% share and any premium multiple compress. A commodity-adjacent FMCG
  book gets a consumer-staples multiple; the e.l.f./Olaplex premium framing is aspirational marketing
  of the *valuation*, which is itself the PR layer to de-PR here.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Base case:** nothing happens near-term. The 2029 flag + "market isn't ready" is an explicit
  deferral. Watch for the actual signposts: AO re-registration, appointment of a bookrunner, an
  acquisition that would need equity funding. None are present.
- **Why the market is unreceptive now (→ second order):** Russian equity primary market is capital-
  constrained, high-rate, discount-priced (B2B-RTS), and institutions avoid sub-RUB10bn deals.
  A ~20% float of SPLAT is marginal on size and liquidity. Counterintuitive point: deferring to 2029
  is rational — rushing into the current window would likely force the same heavy discount that
  killed enthusiasm for 2026's first listing, so the "wait" protects the valuation Demin is anchoring to.
- **Risk:** the entire valuation thesis depends on a premium beauty multiple (e.l.f.) that Olaplex
  proved is fragile; and on a market share that is partly a sanctions artefact.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** SPLAT Global is a Russian oral-care / FMCG maker (toothpaste, brushes, eco-household, children's cosmetics; founded 2000, exports to 70+ countries) floating an *optional* Moscow listing pencilled for 2029, not a live deal. The protagonist sits at the intersection of two markets: (a) the Russian primary-equity market, and (b) consumer/FMCG. The Russian IPO window is contracting, not opening: the number of equity issuers fell from 19 to 9 in 2025 even as nominal volume rose ₽102.1bn→₽125.2bn — i.e. concentration into fewer, larger deals (per Bank of Russia, cbr.ru, 2025). 2026 opened muted, with VTB guiding to "up to three IPOs by end-2026" and ~5 companies technically ready (per TASS, Dec-2026 framing). De-PR note: management says the company is "technically ready" today but "the market is not" and defers to the 2029 budgeting cycle — the honest read is that this is a strategic optionality statement, not an announced transaction. In oral care, SPLAT claims 28-29% of the Russian market and "#1 in pastes and #1 in brushes" (per Demin, via sostav.ru/retail.ru, 2026-10-12) — a category that benefitted from the 2022+ exit of Western FMCG incumbents (Procter & Gamble / Colgate footprint reduction), the key "why now" driver for domestic consumer-brand value.

**Competitive landscape.** FMCG/consumer-brand KPIs that matter here: revenue growth, gross margin, category share, export mix, and (for a listing) free-float/liquidity. SPLAT: 2025 revenue ~₽20bn; 2026 guided ₽24-25bn → implied growth ~+20-25% YoY (`(24.5-20)/20 = +22.5%` at midpoint, `(analysis)` on an unaudited management forecast). Basis of competition in oral care is brand/distribution/shelf, not price. Recent peer/market moves: Russian consumer names that have tested public markets include Henderson (menswear retail, 2023 IPO) and Fix Price (hard-discount retail); no direct listed oral-care pure-play peer trades in Moscow, so comps are cross-category consumer, not like-for-like. SPLAT's position: category leader domestically with a genuine export moat (70+ countries) — the strongest of the four moats here is intangibles (brand) plus scale in a now-vacated competitive field `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** No comparable oral-care/FMCG IPO exists inside the news base — a targeted grep over `News/` surfaced only fintech/payments IPO notes (`[[Tencent-backed Uzum weighs London listing for 2027 IPO]]` is the nearest "weighs-a-listing" structural analogue, not a sector comp). So internal distribution is **not computed; qualitative only.** External anchor — Henderson Fashion (Russian consumer retail, Oct-2023 MOEX IPO): market cap ~₽27.3bn on revenue ~₽22.4bn → P/S ≈ `27.3 / 22.4 = 1.2x` (per Interfax / Marketscreener, 2023). Applying that order of magnitude to SPLAT's ₽20bn 2025 revenue implies a ballpark ~₽24bn equity value `(hypothesis — single cross-category comp, different margin profile, 3-year-old pricing)`; higher-margin branded FMCG could justify a premium to a clothing retailer, but with one comp this is illustrative, not a valuation. EV/EBITDA, EV/Revenue on an enterprise basis → `[UNSOURCED]` (SPLAT is a private LLC; no debt/EBITDA disclosure). Verdict: no reliable multiple; rich/cheap call = no data.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Timing / market-window risk.** Target is 2029 and explicitly conditional ("market is not ready"); the 2025 cohort fell avg −5.2% day-one / −8.7% in a month and retail participation dropped ~4.5x (per cbr.ru / United24) — a soft primary market can slip this option indefinitely. Second-order: optionality that never exercises offers no catalyst for holders.
2. **Liquidity / small-float risk.** Management floats ~20% free float on a mid-cap (~₽20-25bn revenue) consumer name; recent Russian floats (Henderson 13.9%, Diasoft 8%) show thin floats that trade poorly — low liquidity caps institutional demand and widens the IPO discount.
3. **Structure / governance risk.** A listing requires converting ООО «Сплат Глобал» to a joint-stock company (per the note); until that re-registration and audited AO accounts exist, the "already audited / technically ready" claim is unverifiable from outside. Second-order: concentration in founder-owners (Demin/Belous) means minority protections are untested.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a *signalling* data point about the depth of the domestic consumer-IPO pipeline post-import-substitution, not a tradable event — a category leader publicly keeping the option alive rather than committing. Falsifiable thesis: if the Russian IPO market re-rates and issuer count recovers above the 2025 trough before 2029, expect SPLAT and similar FMCG leaders to convert optionality into filings; trigger = re-registration to AO + a mandate to banks. What breaks it: continued <10 issuers/yr and negative first-day performance keeps SPLAT private indefinitely.
