SALT LAKE CITY , Oct. 22, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Verisave, a leading provider of payments optimization solutions, today announced the appointment of Chris Lefebvre as Chief Executive Officer, effective October 1, 2026. Lefebvre, who joins from Fiserv, succeeds Jeremy Layton, who will transition into the role of Founder and Chief Commercial Officer to ensure strategic continuity.
Lefebvre brings more than 30 years of executive leadership across payments, financial services and technology, with a track record of guiding companies through growth, transformation and successful exits. He led financial services for a major retailer and was selected to serve on the Merchant Advisory Board for the card networks. As COO of BankServ, he led the company's national expansion and its successful exit. Lefebvre has since held C-suite roles at eCommerce sales and marketing companies, after which he founded CRC, a management and strategy consulting firm serving regulated companies in healthcare, energy and financial services. Most recently, he served as Global Head of Relationship Management for Grocery, Petroleum, and Technology at Fiserv, and was instrumental in reshaping their client engagement strategy.
"I am extremely proud of the work Verisave has done over the years," said Jeremy Layton, Verisave Founder and Chief Commercial Officer. "I am confident in our ability to continue bringing solutions and transparency to an industry that is largely unregulated and highly complex. Our clients increasingly need better solutions to control costs and manage their payments proactively. Chris brings deep payments expertise and a track record of building products and solutions around this need."
"Verisave has built something rare: a trusted platform that delivers real and measurable value for its clients," said Chris Lefebvre. "What drew me to the role is how much opportunity lies ahead, both in deepening our core solutions and expanding our reach across the broader financial ecosystem. I'm energized by the prospect of working with such a talented team as we look to accelerate Verisave's growth and bring even greater value to our clients."
About Verisave Verisave is a payments optimization and consultancy firm, specializing in merchant account, payments stack, processing fee, and interchange fee solutions. Verisave identifies wasted spend and inefficiencies within the payments operations and merchant account settings, bringing significant direct and operational cost savings to clients. Headquartered in Salt Lake City, the company is backed by Ceto Capital. Learn more at verisave.com

### [0] What happened
Verisave, a Salt Lake City merchant-account / payments-cost-optimization consultancy, named payments veteran **Chris Lefebvre** as CEO effective Oct 1, 2026, announced Oct 22. ### [1] Who Verisave is (scale/context)
Verisave (founded ~2001–2002, HQ Salt Lake City) is a **small, privately-held fee-audit consultancy**, backed by Ceto Capital. It reduces clients' credit-card **processing / interchange fees by ~10–30% without switching processors**, identifying wasted spend in merchant-account settings on a contingency/no-upfront-fee basis (~10-week engagements).
- Scale is tiny: ~**25–26 employees** (PitchBook/Tracxn), revenue reportedly **<$5M** (ZoomInfo). This is a boutique advisory, not a platform/processor.
- Recent product move: Verisave partnered with Optimized Payments and Fee Navigator on an **AI-powered compliance tool for Visa's Commercial Enhanced Data Program (CEDP)** — the "product development" the CEO hire points at.
- Sources: [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/310226-32); [Yahoo Finance — CEDP tool](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/verisave-optimized-payments-fee-navigator-130500598.html).

### [2] Who Chris Lefebvre is
~30 years across payments/fintech: most recently **Global Head of Relationship Management (Grocery, Petroleum, Technology) at Fiserv**; earlier **COO of BankServ** (led national expansion + exit); founded **CRC**, a consultancy for regulated (healthcare/energy/financial) companies; served on a card-network **Merchant Advisory Board**. A relationship-management/commercial operator profile rather than a product-builder, despite the "product development" framing.

### [3] Why it (barely) matters / market backdrop
Merchant card-acceptance costs remain a hot, lightly-regulated fee pool that Verisave sells into: Australian merchant card fees hit **~$8B/yr** ([[Australian merchant card fees reach $8 billion annually]]); US swipe-fee rules are contested ([[OCC preempts Illinois interchange-fee ban on taxes and tips]]); networks keep layering value-added/data programs (Visa CEDP; [[Fintech Wrap Up Structural analysis of Visa's value-added services]]). Demand for cost-audit / fee-transparency advisory is structurally supported. But for Verisave itself a founder→professional-CEO handoff at a ~25-person shop is a routine growth/succession step, not a market-moving event.

- Any follow-on Ceto Capital funding, M&A, or a roll-up of fee-audit boutiques.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Verisave sits in the narrow "merchant payment-cost optimization / interchange audit" niche — a services layer that sits on top of the US card-acceptance cost pool and claims to recover 10–30% of processing fees without switching processors (per Verisave). That cost pool is large and growing: US merchants paid a record ~$198.25bn in credit+debit card processing fees in 2025, up from ~$187.2bn in 2024 (+5.9% YoY) and roughly tripled since the ~$62.1bn of 2009; the average Visa/Mastercard swipe fee hit ~2.35% (per Nilson Report, via Merchants Payments Coalition, 2025). The audit niche itself is fragmented and low-barrier — no capital/regulatory moat, competition on data tooling, processor relationships and performance-fee pricing. **Why now (the real demand driver):** Visa retired the legacy Level 2/Level 3 commercial interchange programs in April 2026, replacing them with the Commercial Enhanced Data Program (CEDP), under which only "Verified" merchants submitting complete, accurate enhanced data get the best "Product 3" rates; poor data can downgrade B2B card volume and raise interchange ~0.5–1.5% (per Finix/Versapay/Priority Commerce guides, 2025–26). This is a tailwind for audit/optimization firms — more complexity to arbitrage.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for an audit firm: client realized savings % (Verisave claims 10–30%), audit turnaround (Verisave claims 24–48h), and recurring vs one-off engagement mix; Verisave discloses none in hard numbers `[UNSOURCED]`. This is a crowded, undifferentiated set: **CMSPI** (forensic interchange/MCC optimization, enterprise-scale), **P3 Cost Analysts** (claims 15–20% savings), **Optimized Payments** (claims ~21% reductions), **weAudit**, **Business Solutions Group** (20–30% claims), plus processors' own tools (per cmspi.com, costanalysts.com, optimizedpayments.com, weaudit.com, 2026). Basis of competition = data/tooling + performance-fee pricing, not price or network. Notably, Verisave, Optimized Payments and Fee Navigator jointly launched an AI-powered Visa CEDP verification tool (per prnewswire.com) — so direct rivals are also partners on CEDP, signaling a shared land-grab around the new regime. Verisave's position: **niche, mid-market player** — Salt Lake City, PE-backed (Ceto Capital), bringing in a Fiserv/Merchant Advisory Board veteran (Lefebvre) explicitly to build product and expand "across the broader financial ecosystem." Moat `(analysis)`: thin — modest switching costs from embedded audit data and performance-fee contracts; no network effect or scale advantage vs CMSPI.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation, round, or revenue figure is disclosed for Verisave (PE-held by Ceto Capital) → market cap / EV multiples = **no data**. Verisave's revenue model is itself a share of client savings (per cetocapital.com) — i.e. contingency-based, so top-line scales with the swipe-fee pool above, not a fixed SaaS ARR. Peers CMSPI, P3, Optimized Payments, weAudit are also private with no public multiples → distribution not computed, qualitative comparison only. Internal corpus comps (context for the US card-fee economics this niche feeds on, not valuation peers): [[Visa and Mastercard reach revised interchange settlement]], [[OCC preempts Illinois interchange-fee ban on taxes and tips]], [[Australian merchant card fees reach $8 billion annually]], [[Fintech Wrap Up Structural analysis of Visa's value-added services]], [[PSR sets out changes for card payment fee transparency]]. Flag: comp set is **unvaluable** — treat Verisave as a sub-scale services boutique, not a fintech platform.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Demand depends on persistent fee opacity — and the headwind is regulatory simplification.** The Nov-2025 Visa/Mastercard settlement (court-approved Jun 2026) cuts interchange 0.1pp for 5 years, caps standard consumer rates at 1.25%, and expands surcharging/category choice for ~12m merchants (per CNBC/PaymentsDive/PYMNTS, 2025–26). Simpler, more transparent, lower posted rates erode the "wasted spend" Verisave monetizes — a second-order threat to the contingency-fee model even as the headline cost pool stays large.
2. **Disintermediation / margin captured upstream.** The optimization value Verisave arbitrages can be absorbed by the stack layers themselves — processors and Visa's own Value-Added Services (VAS ~$10.9bn FY25 rev, ~20% CAGR per Fintech Wrap Up) are productizing fee/data optimization; CEDP's automated ML data-verification could make third-party interchange tuning less necessary over time.
3. **Tiny, undifferentiated, PE-owned.** Fragmented niche with near-zero entry barriers and ≥5 look-alike rivals (several also CEDP partners); Ceto Capital ownership + the "successful exits" framing around Lefebvre points to a build-to-sell posture, raising execution/continuity risk around the founder-to-CEO handoff (Layton → CCO).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The CEO hire is a product/scale signal, not a market-moving event — read it as a PE-backed boutique professionalizing to ride the Visa CEDP transition and pitch an eventual exit. Falsifiable thesis: Verisave's addressable demand is *pro-cyclical with fee complexity*, so the April-2026 CEDP rollout is a near-term tailwind but the 2025 settlement's rate cuts + transparency are a structural headwind. Watch/trigger: whether Verisave moves beyond contingency audits into durable SaaS/monitoring (recurring revenue) — if it stays pure contingency, savings compress as the settlement's transparency takes hold. Thesis breaks if it lands a disclosed enterprise platform deal or raises a priced round at a software multiple.
