Summary

Bitcoin breaking news: On October 14, 2026, Strive (ASST) announced a $500 million fundraising plan through 5 million perpetual preferred shares (SATS) priced at $100 each. The funds will be used to purchase Bitcoin, income-generating assets, or repurchase shares. SATS will pay a fixed daily dividend of 15%, with lower claim priority than existing SATA shares. Strive also plans to establish a reserve fund to cover the first 18 months of SATS dividends. Last week, the company added 3,311 BTC to its holdings, bringing its total Bitcoin holdings to 32,773 BTC, valued at approximately $270 million.

ChainThink reports that on October 14, according to CoinDesk, Bitcoin treasury company Strive (ASST) plans to issue a new class of perpetual preferred shares, SATS, at $100 per share, offering 5 million shares to raise $500 million.

The raised funds are intended for increasing Bitcoin holdings, acquiring income-generating assets, or repurchasing company shares. SATS has a fixed dividend rate of 15%, paid daily, and ranks junior to Strive’s existing SATA preferred shares, which have a floating dividend rate currently at 13%.

Strive also plans to establish a reserve equal to the total dividends paid over the first 18 months of SATS upon completion of the issuance. Last week, Strive increased its Bitcoin holdings by 3,311 BTC, bringing its total holdings to 32,773 BTC, valued at approximately $2.7 billion.

# Context-enrichment: Strive plans $500M raise to buy Bitcoin and other assets
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 13–14 Oct 2026 Strive, Inc. (Nasdaq: ASST) announced the IPO of a **second** class of perpetual preferred stock, **SATS**: 5,000,000 shares at $100 each → **$500M target gross raise**. SATS pays a **fixed 15% annual dividend, accrued/paid daily**, and ranks **junior to the existing SATA** variable-rate preferred (SATA currently 13%, up from 12.75% in April 2026). Proceeds earmarked for (a) more Bitcoin, (b) income-producing assets, or (c) buying back stock. At closing Strive will set aside a **cash reserve covering the first 18 months of SATS dividends** (CoinDesk, 13 Oct; KuCoin flash).

De-PR'd read:
- This is **not equity** — it is a **high-coupon perpetual preferred** = quasi-debt yield instrument. Strive runs a **debt-free balance sheet**, so preferreds are how it adds leverage without a bond covenant or maturity wall.
- The "$2.7B / 32,773 BTC" headline is the **collateral story**, not the raise. Last week Strive bought 3,311 BTC (~$280M, avg ~$84.5k/BTC) — its largest buy since January — lifting holdings to **32,773 BTC as of 9 Oct 2026**, the **5th-largest public holder** (behind Strategy, Metaplanet, Twenty One, MARA). (The Block; Cryptowisser.)
- **Why structured this way:** the 18-month dividend reserve + daily accrual is a confidence-signalling device — it pre-answers "can you cover 15%?" for the first 1.5 years. But note the tell: 15% of $500M = **$75M/yr of hard cash obligations**, senior to common, on an asset (BTC) that yields **nothing**. The dividend is funded by the **same capital raise + any future SATS/SATA sales + the small legacy software business**, i.e. the Strategy "digital credit" circularity (new buyers' $100 partly pays existing holders' coupons), not by Bitcoin performance.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- [[Barclays initiates Strategy coverage at overweight, $130 target]] — Strategy (MSTR) is the archetype; it pioneered the perpetual-preferred stack **STRK (convertible), STRF (senior cumulative), STRD (junior non-cum), STRC ("Stretch", variable ~11.5%, $10.5B outstanding in <9 months)**. SATS/SATA are a near-literal clone of STRC/STRF. Strive even **bought $50M of MSTR's STRC** earlier in 2026 — it is both imitator and customer.
- [[Smarter Web raises £10M in Bitcoin treasury preferred share IPO]] (UK, Oct 2026) — same instrument (MORE perpetual preferred) but **undersubscribed** (£10M vs £15–25M target; 76% retail). Direct evidence that appetite for BTC-treasury preferreds is **patchy at the smaller end**.
- Other BTC-treasury capital raisers in corpus: [[France's Capital B raises €15.2 million to expand bitcoin treasury]], [[Amdax plans Bitcoin treasury listing on Euronext Amsterdam]], [[ProCap BTC raises $750M via SPAC to buy Bitcoin]], [[Meliuz raises $32.4M in share offering to buy Bitcoin]], [[Treasury B.V. secures $14.7 million for Bitcoin treasury company]], [[Orange Juice secures $40M for bitcoin-backed capital company]].
- **Position:** Strive is a **fast follower**, not an innovator. It is ahead of the micro-caps (Smarter Web) on scale/structure but years behind Strategy on size (Strategy holds ~600k BTC vs Strive's 33k).
- **Why the lay of the land / second order:** the entire sub-sector's valuation rests on **mNAV premium** (market cap ÷ BTC value). Strive common (ASST) trades at **~1.67x mNAV** (8 Oct) — still a premium, which is *why* it can raise. But the sector backdrop is grim: **MSTR enterprise mNAV fell below 1.0x in June 2026** and sat ~1.09x in Sept; **Metaplanet hit 0.99x on 14 Oct 2026** (trading below its BTC reserves); ~37 of the top-100 BTC treasuries were sub-NAV by early 2026. Preferred issuance is partly a **response to premium compression**: when common is no longer a cheap currency (dilutive below NAV), you switch to selling yield to income investors instead.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Strive began as **Vivek Ramaswamy's anti-ESG asset manager (Strive Asset Management, 2022)**. It became a listed BTC-treasury co via a **2025 reverse merger with Asset Entities (ticker ASST retained)**, a "Bitcoin war chest" vehicle that eyed a **$1B+ raise (up to ~$1.5B if PIPE warrants exercise)** (Bloomberg Law; Sherwood; Crypto Briefing).
- Trajectory of 2026: SATA preferred launched; SATA dividend raised to 13% (Apr); $50M into MSTR's STRC; 3,311 BTC buy (Oct) → 32,773 BTC; now SATS.
- **Why the company acts this way:** the legacy asset-management/Asset-Entities operating business is tiny — there is **no cash-generative engine** to service a 15% coupon organically. Structural logic = Strive is effectively a **leveraged, closed-end Bitcoin fund wearing an operating-company costume**. Its only durable edge is the **Ramaswamy brand/political-retail distribution** that lets it place paper at a premium. Hence the compulsion to keep issuing: growth in BTC/share requires *continuous* premium-priced issuance; the moment the premium closes, the flywheel stalls.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** SATS is a me-too of Strategy's STRC/STRF, launched after Strive already ran SATA. Nothing structurally new — a second, junior, higher-coupon (15% vs 13%) tranche.
- **Traction to watch:** whether the $500M actually clears. The 18-month reserve + daily dividend are **marketing features**, not demand proof. The **Smarter Web undersubscription** and **sector-wide sub-NAV** environment are cautionary. SATA trading data / the SATS book-build are the real signals; announcement ≠ funded.
- **Who captures the margin / what breaks it:** SATS holders get 15% and sit **senior to common, junior to SATA** — so in stress, common holders (and Ramaswamy insiders) absorb first losses while preferred stacks compound ahead of them. The **real value transfer** is: preferred buyers lend at 15% against volatile, zero-yield BTC; the company captures the **mNAV premium spread** (issue preferred near par, buy BTC, grow "BTC per common share" while the premium lasts). **What breaks it:** BTC drawdown + premium compression → asset coverage (quoted ~1.9x for SATA) erodes, new issuance to fund the $75M+/yr coupon turns dilutive/impossible, and the daily-dividend promise becomes a forced-seller trap. This is **leverage dressed as income**.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Watch: final SATS clearing size vs $500M; SATA/SATS secondary yields (a widening yield = market pricing higher default/cut risk); any further BTC buys funded by the raise; warrant-exercise cash (up to ~$1.5B PIPE).
- **Regulatory/macro backdrop:** BTC fell ~2.5% to low-$81k on the day amid hotter-than-expected US CPI and renewed Fed-hike concern — a **bad tape to launch a 15% perpetual** into. Higher-for-longer rates make a 15% coupon look less exceptional vs risk-free, squeezing the premium that makes the model work.
- **Why the market may go this way / counterintuitive second order:** BTC-treasury preferreds are marketed as "downside-protected high yield," but the yield is **structurally reflexive** — it depends on the issuer's continued ability to sell *more* preferred at par. In a BTC bear + sub-NAV sector, that reflexivity flips: the instruments that felt safest (senior daily-paid 15%) become fragile precisely because the **sponsor's capital-raising capacity, not Bitcoin, is the true collateral**. Capital concentration in a single Ramaswamy-branded vehicle makes it *more* fragile, not safer. The central question is not "is 15% attractive" but "**how long can Strive keep selling paper above NAV to fund paper it already sold**."

## FRESHNESS verdict: **fresh**
This is a **genuinely new event** — the IPO of a *second* preferred class (SATS) with new terms (15% fixed vs SATA's 13% variable, junior ranking, $500M target, 18-month reserve), announced 13–14 Oct 2026. It is **not** a reprint of the 3,311-BTC buy ([[The Block]] 13 Oct) nor of the earlier SATA launch. No prior corpus note covers the SATS offering. Peer notes ([[Smarter Web raises £10M in Bitcoin treasury preferred share IPO]], [[Barclays initiates Strategy coverage at overweight, $130 target]]) are context, not duplicates.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
### Top challenge / red-team questions (second-order)

1. **Is the 15% coupon funded by Bitcoin or by new buyers?** → Open-but-telling. BTC yields 0%; the small legacy software/asset-mgmt business can't cover $75M/yr. By the Strategy-STRC template this is substantially **reflexive** — new SATS/SATA sales + the 18-month reserve fund the coupon. The yield is a function of continued issuance, not asset return.

2. **Why a SECOND, junior preferred (SATS) instead of more SATA or common equity?** → ASST common trades ~1.67x mNAV (a premium), yet they chose preferred. Likely reasons: avoid common dilution, lock in fixed cost before rates rise, and tap income-investor demand. A **junior** 15% tranche also signals they'd exhausted easy senior capacity at 13%.

3. **Will the $500M actually clear?** → Open. Announcement ≠ funded. The directly comparable [[Smarter Web raises £10M in Bitcoin treasury preferred share IPO]] came in **undersubscribed** (£10M vs £15–25M) days earlier. Final book size is the key traction metric.

4. **What is SATA/SATS asset coverage and how fast does it erode in a BTC drawdown?** → SATA cited ~1.9x. Coverage is BTC-price-levered: a 30–40% BTC fall + the added senior-ish SATS claims could push coverage toward 1.0x, at which point the "downside protection" pitch breaks.

5. **What happens to the daily 15% dividend if Strive can't sell new paper?** → The 18-month reserve buys ~1.5 years; after that, without fresh issuance Strive must **sell BTC into weakness** or cut/defer. For a perpetual, "non-payment" mechanics (cumulative? deferrable?) materially change the risk — needs the FWP/prospectus to confirm. (Open.)

6. **Is ASST's ~1.67x mNAV premium durable?** → Doubtful. Sector peers have collapsed: MSTR enterprise mNAV <1.0x (Jun 2026), Metaplanet 0.99x (14 Oct 2026), ~37 of top-100 sub-NAV. If ASST's premium compresses toward 1.0x, the accretive-issuance flywheel stalls.

7. **How much of the "value-add" is just the Ramaswamy brand / political-retail distribution?** → Likely most of it. Structurally Strive is a leveraged closed-end BTC fund; the differentiator is a captive retail base willing to pay a premium. That is a **soft, sentiment-dependent moat**.

8. **Who absorbs first losses?** → Common holders (incl. insiders) sit below SATS; SATS sits below SATA. Preferred buyers are lending at 15% against zero-yield, high-vol collateral while the sponsor harvests the premium spread. Asymmetry favors the issuer in good times, the senior preferred in bad.

9. **Why launch a 15% perpetual into a hot-CPI, higher-for-longer tape?** → BTC fell ~2.5% to ~$81k that day on inflation data. Rising risk-free rates shrink the relative appeal of 15% and the mNAV premium simultaneously — arguably poor timing, or a rush to lock fixed cost before conditions worsen.

10. **Does buying $50M of rival MSTR's STRC signal confidence or a lack of differentiation?** → It shows Strive treats these preferreds as an asset class and validates Strategy's model — but it also underlines that SATS is **derivative**, not novel.

11. **What's the dilution path if warrants (PIPE up to ~$1.5B) are exercised?** → Open. Large warrant overhang could dilute common and complicate the mNAV premium that underpins the whole structure.

12. **Is 32,773 BTC / "$2.7B" materially mis-stated?** → The in-note KuCoin flash's Summary says "~$270 million" (an obvious typo) while its body says "~$2.7 billion"; at ~$81.5k BTC, 32,773 BTC ≈ **$2.67B** — body figure correct, Summary wrong. Flag as source error.

13. **How concentrated/fragile is the whole structure in one name?** → Highly. Capital concentration in a single Ramaswamy-branded vehicle makes it **more** fragile (single point of sentiment/political failure), not diversified/safer — a counterintuitive second-order risk.

14. **Average BTC cost basis vs current price?** → Strive paid ~$84.5k avg on the recent buy; BTC ~$81.5k on announcement day → the newest tranche is **underwater**, pressuring near-term coverage optics.

15. **What is the real exit for SATS holders in a perpetual?** → No maturity; exit = secondary sale at prevailing yield or an issuer call. In stress, liquidity thins and the "par-ish daily-dividend" comfort can gap down — the daily dividend is not a liquidity guarantee.

**Importance: 3/5** — A sizeable ($500M), named, well-structured raise by the 5th-largest public BTC holder, credibly fresh (new SATS class, new terms). But novelty is low (a me-too of Strategy's STRC/STRF, launched after Strive's own SATA), it is announced-not-funded, and it lands into a deteriorating sector (sector-wide sub-NAV, undersubscribed peer, hot CPI). Material enough to track as a bellwether of BTC-treasury-preferred demand; not a 4–5 structural first.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Strive sits in the bitcoin-treasury-company (BTC / "DAT") subvertical: listed vehicles whose equity is a leveraged wrapper on bitcoin, funded by issuing stock/preferreds above net asset value and plowing the proceeds into BTC. The financing wave has shifted from common/ATM equity to perpetual preferreds: this market has grown to ~$13bn in under two years, with yields of 10.8–15.2% and collateral coverage of 3.8–4.5x, per BitcoinTreasuries.net/Apyx via Bitcoin Magazine (as of Oct 2026); Strategy's preferreds (STRF/STRC/STRK/STRD) are ~$12.5bn of that, Strive's existing SATA ~$330M. "Why now": with BTC off its highs (~$81,600 on 2026-10-14, per KuCoin) and mNAV premiums compressed, common-equity ATMs are no longer accretive, so issuers reach for preferreds — equity-classified, no maturity, no forced repayment — to keep buying BTC without diluting common at a discount. (analysis)

**Competitive landscape.** The KPIs this subvertical runs on: BTC holdings, BTC-per-share (accretion), and mNAV (market cap ÷ BTC NAV) — the premium is the entire funding engine. Players: Strategy/MicroStrategy (the template, ~1.15× mNAV, down from a >3.3× Nov peak — per beincrypto/CoinDesk), Metaplanet, Semler Scientific (~29% discount to NAV), Nakamoto/NAKA (~0.7× NAV, -96% from peak), Twenty One. Recent moves: Strive announced the SATS raise 2026-10-13 (CoinDesk); K33 argued 2026-10-14 that Strategy's preferred-dividend load may force it to sell BTC (TheBlock). Strive's position: a smaller-scale, late-cycle entrant (7th-largest public holder) co-founded by Vivek Ramaswamy, now stacking a second preferred class on top of SATA. Moat is weak — no network effect or switching cost; the only edge is access to premium-priced capital, which is itself cyclical. (analysis)

**Comps & multiples.** Strive (ASST): 28,000 BTC as of 2026-09-30 (TheBlock) — note the KuCoin item's "32,773 BTC / ~$2.7bn" is higher and likely post-recent-buys; at ~$81.6k that implies ~$2.3–2.7bn BTC NAV. mNAV reads scatter: ~1.01× late-Sep (near parity), 1.20–1.34× mid-year, 1.67× on one tracker (mnav.com) — treat as ~1.0–1.3×, i.e. premium largely gone. Arithmetic for the raise: 5,000,000 SATS × $100 = $500M; SATS annual coupon = 15% × $100 × 5M = $75M/yr; stacked on SATA (~$330M at ~13% ≈ ~$43M/yr) → combined preferred dividend burden ≈ ~$118M/yr (analysis) with no operating cash flow to service it. Peer mNAVs: Strategy ~1.15×, Semler ~0.71× (29% discount), NAKA ~0.7×. In-base comps: [[Tether buys SoftBank's stake in Twenty One Capital]] (BTC-treasury M&A, SoftBank stake ~$679M), [[France's Capital B raises €15.2 million to expand bitcoin treasury]] (€15.2M / ~$17.8M private placement). Flag: at ~1.0× mNAV Strive is in-line-to-cheap vs the premium peers but richer than the deep-discount names — the raise is being done just as the premium that makes preferreds payable has thinned.

**Risk flags.**
- Dividend-vs-NAV death-spiral: ~$118M/yr combined preferred coupon (analysis) with zero operating income means dividends are paid by issuing more securities or selling BTC; the 18-month SATS dividend reserve only defers this — if mNAV stays ≤1× the funding engine stalls (K33 made exactly this argument about Strategy, TheBlock 2026-10-14).
- NAV-premium collapse / accretion reversal: at ~1.0× mNAV, issuing to buy BTC is no longer accretive to BTC-per-share; the whole thesis (buy above NAV) breaks once the premium is gone, as it already has for Semler (-29%) and NAKA (-0.7×).
- Subordination + BTC-price leverage: SATS ranks junior to SATA and senior to common, so in a BTC drawdown SATS holders absorb losses before SATA but the 15% daily coupon is fixed — a falling BTC price both raises effective leverage and makes the coupon harder to cover.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Strive is a late-cycle test of whether the preferred-financing playbook works once the mNAV premium is gone: issuing a second, higher-cost (15% vs SATA 13%) preferred junior to the first is a sign the cheap capital is used up. Falsifiable thesis: if BTC stays range-bound and ASST mNAV holds ≤1.1×, the SATS dividend reserve exhausts and Strive is forced to sell BTC or do dilutive common raises within ~18–24 months (analysis). Trigger to watch: SATS pricing/close and whether it clears at par; a below-par break or a widening discount on SATA would confirm the capital window is closing. (analysis)
