TAMPA, Fla., Oct. 15, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- AtlasClear Holdings, Inc. (NYSE American: ATCH) (“AtlasClear” or the “Company”), a company building regulated financial infrastructure for smaller institutions, fintechs and advisors, today announced that it has entered into a definitive business combination agreement to acquire Ark Financial Services, Inc. (“Ark”), the parent company of Dawson James Securities, Inc. (“Dawson James”), a full-service investment bank headquartered in Boca Raton, Florida. Concurrently with signing, AtlasClear completed the purchase of 24.9% of Ark’s outstanding equity. AtlasClear expects to acquire the remaining 75.1% upon receipt of FINRA approval and satisfaction of other closing conditions.
 A Larger, More Diversified Company 
On a combined basis, AtlasClear and Dawson James generated total revenues plus interest income* of approximately $47.0 million in their most recent fiscal years (AtlasClear’s fiscal year ended June 30, 2026, and Dawson James’s year ended December 31, 2025), more than double AtlasClear’s standalone total revenues plus interest income of $21.9 million* and up approximately 100% from a combined approximately $23.5 million for the companies’ respective prior fiscal years (AtlasClear’s fiscal year ended June 30, 2025, and Dawson James’s year ended December 31, 2024). AtlasClear presents its interest income, which is earned on balances held at its broker-dealer subsidiary, in other income; Dawson James includes interest income in revenue. Management believes that adding AtlasClear’s interest income to its total revenues presents the two companies’ results on a consistent basis. Combined total revenues were approximately $45.2 million for the most recent combined fiscal year, up approximately 110% from approximately $21.5 million for the prior combined fiscal year. The combination also broadens the Company’s revenue base. AtlasClear’s growth in fiscal 2026 was led by stock locate fees, and Dawson James’s revenue is driven primarily by investment banking and advisory fees. Together, approximately 73% of combined total revenues plus interest income for the most recently completed fiscal year of each company came from sources other than brokerage commissions. Dawson James was profitable in 2025, reporting audited net income of approximately $2.4 million.

 Strategic Rationale 
Dawson James is an active placement agent and underwriter for small-cap and micro-cap companies and ranked #14 by transaction volume in the first quarter of 2026, according to PlacementTracker’s Market League Tables. Dawson James has begun clearing through AtlasClearing, and the Company expects that business to grow over time. The combination allows AtlasClear to participate in the full life of a transaction — from capital raising and distribution through execution, clearing and settlement — and to bring the capital formation needs of Dawson James’s issuer clients together with AtlasClearing’s clearing, stock locate and custody services and, following completion of its planned acquisition of Commercial Bancorp of Wyoming, commercial banking.
 Management Commentary 
“Today we take a major step toward the company we set out to build,” said John Schaible, Executive Chairman of AtlasClear Holdings. “On a combined basis, this transaction more than doubles our revenue and interest income, to approximately $47 million on a historical basis, and adds a profitable, well-known investment bank to a clearing platform that grew revenue 85% last year. Dawson James brings issuers, deal flow and distribution; AtlasClearing brings clearing, stock locate and custody; and with Commercial Bancorp we intend to add banking. Very few firms serving small-cap and micro-cap companies can offer all of that under one roof. We also built this deal to protect our shareholders. Not one share was issued for our initial stake, and no shares will be issued unless the transaction closes. When shares are issued, they will be priced at the market, with no discount and no warrants.”
“Dawson James has already begun clearing through AtlasClearing,” said Craig Ridenhour, President of AtlasClear Holdings and Chairman of AtlasClearing, Inc. “Bringing an active underwriter and placement agent onto our platform gives our clearing, stock locate and custody operations direct access to new issuer and investor flow, and we expect that business to grow as we integrate the two firms.”
 Structured to Limit Dilution 
AtlasClear’s management structured the transaction to limit dilution to existing stockholders:
 No shares at signing. The initial 24.9% investment was funded entirely with $2.5 million in cash and a $2.5 million seller note. No AtlasClear shares were issued.
 Shares only at closing. AtlasClear shares will be issued only at the closing of the transaction, which requires, among other things, FINRA approval and the approval of AtlasClear’s stockholders.
 Priced at market and fixed in dollars. The stock consideration at closing is a fixed dollar amount — $13.8125 million — issued at the five-day volume-weighted average price of AtlasClear’s common stock ending on the closing date, with no discount to market and no minimum share count, price reset or ratchet.
 No warrants or convertible securities. The consideration includes no warrants, preferred stock or convertible securities.
 Stock earn-out paid only for performance. Up to $2.4375 million of additional shares is payable only to the extent Dawson James achieves its revenue targets for 2026 and 2027, and is likewise priced at the market at the time of payment.
 Lock-up. Ark stockholders receiving AtlasClear shares will be subject to a lock-up of six months and will receive customary registration rights.
 Transaction Terms 
Under the business combination agreement, AtlasClear purchased 24.9% of Ark’s outstanding equity from Ark’s majority stockholder for $2.5 million in cash and a $2.5 million promissory note payable within 90 days (or sooner under certain circumstances). At the closing of the transaction, AtlasClear will acquire the remaining equity of Ark for $2.4375 million in cash and $13.8125 million in AtlasClear common stock, valued at the five-day volume-weighted average price ending on the closing date, of which a total of $1.65 million in cash and stock will be held in escrow to support indemnification obligations. Ark’s former stockholders may also receive up to $3.25 million of deferred consideration, payable 25% in cash and 75% in AtlasClear common stock, based on revenue for the twelve-month periods ending December 31, 2026 and 2027. Total consideration is up to $24.5 million. In addition, as is customary for broker-dealer acquisitions, net capital of Dawson James in excess of $1.0 million at closing, valued at market, will be paid to Ark’s stockholders in cash or, at the majority stockholder’s election, in AtlasClear shares at the same closing price.
Completion of the transaction is subject to approval by FINRA of Dawson James’s continuing membership application under FINRA Rule 1017, approval of AtlasClear’s stockholders to the extent required by the NYSE American Company Guide, and other customary closing conditions. Because Bob Keyser, the majority owner of Ark, is a director of AtlasClear, the transaction was reviewed and approved by the independent members of AtlasClear’s Board of Directors. Greenberg Traurig, LLP is serving as legal counsel to AtlasClear, and ArentFox Schiff LLP is serving as legal counsel to Ark.

# Context-enrichment: AtlasClear to acquire Dawson James parent Ark Financial
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-15 AtlasClear Holdings (NYSE American: ATCH) signed a definitive business combination agreement to buy Ark Financial Services, parent of Boca Raton boutique investment bank/broker-dealer Dawson James Securities, for **up to $24.5M**. Concurrently it bought **24.9%** of Ark for $2.5M cash + a $2.5M 90-day seller note (no shares issued). The remaining 75.1% closes only on **FINRA Rule 1017** continuing-membership approval + ATCH stockholder approval.

De-PR'd terms:
- At closing: $2.4375M cash + **$13.8125M in ATCH stock** (fixed dollar amount, 5-day VWAP, no discount/warrant/ratchet), $1.65M escrowed for indemnity.
- Up to **$3.25M deferred earn-out** (25% cash / 75% stock) on 2026 & 2027 revenue targets.
- Plus excess-net-capital-over-$1M paid to Ark's stockholders (customary for BD deals).
- Combined "total revenues plus interest income" ~$47.0M FY (AtlasClear FYE Jun-30-2026 + Dawson James FYE Dec-31-2025) vs AtlasClear standalone ~$21.9M. Dawson James 2025 audited net income ~$2.4M.

**Why structured exactly this way — and what it reveals (the core red-team).** Two things dominate and neither is in the PR headline:
1. **The stock is a penny stock.** ATCH trades ~$0.17, down ~97-98% over 12 months, below NYSE American's $1.00 minimum since Mar-2025, and already did a **1-for-60 reverse split** (Dec-2024). The elaborate "no discount, no warrants, no ratchet, fixed in dollars, priced at VWAP" language is not shareholder-friendliness for its own sake — it is damage control for a near-worthless, dilution-prone equity that the seller would otherwise discount heavily. Paying a profitable bank partly in ATCH paper requires a 6-month lock-up + registration rights precisely because that paper is illiquid and falling. The "we issued zero shares for the initial stake" framing papers over that the *closing* consideration is majority stock whose value is highly uncertain.
2. **Related-party deal.** Bob Keyser, majority owner of Ark, is **a director of AtlasClear**. The deal was approved by the independent board members — disclosure confirms it, the PR buries it. A controlling-party selling his own firm to a company he sits on the board of, paid largely in that company's distressed stock, is the central governance fact.
3. **The "$47M combined" is non-GAAP and not pro forma.** It's an arithmetic sum of mismatched fiscal years (ATCH Jun-30 + Dawson James Dec-31), adds interest income to revenue, and excludes acquisition accounting, transaction costs, synergies, and Ark's other subsidiaries. AtlasClear standalone FY2026 had an operating loss of ~$9.8M (widened from ~$4.9M) and the 10-K flags **material weaknesses in internal controls**.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Vertically-integrated small-cap clearing/IB stacks: **Cowen (pre-TD acquisition), B. Riley Financial, Oppenheimer, StoneX, Interactive Brokers (clearing), Hilltop Securities, Clear Street** (private clearing fintech, ~$685M valuation 2024). On the micro-cap underwriting side Dawson James competes with **Maxim Group, EF Hutton (Kingswood), Aegis Capital, ThinkEquity, A.G.P./Alliance Global, Roth Capital**. Dawson James ranked **#14 by transaction volume Q1-2026** (PlacementTracker) — a genuine but small player.

**Why the lay of the land is this way + second-order.** The "own the whole transaction life — raise, distribute, execute, clear, settle, bank" thesis is the same pitch Clear Street and B. Riley run. The difference: Clear Street raised hundreds of millions of private capital and built modern tech; B. Riley has (had) scale and a balance sheet. AtlasClear is attempting the same vertical integration **from a sub-$1 penny stock with ~$14.4M net capital and recurring operating losses**. The strategic logic (clearing is a commodity → bolt on higher-margin IB fees + banking NIM) is sound in theory and is why the deal diversifies revenue (~73% of combined revenue is non-commission). But the execution base is the weakest among peers, so this is a catch-up/survival play, not a position of strength.

## [2] Company history / fit
AtlasClear went public Feb-2024 via SPAC merger with **Quantum FinTech Acquisition Corp**, simultaneously acquiring correspondent broker-dealer **Wilson-Davis & Co.** (now rebranded **AtlasClearing**; DTCC/NSCC member). Since then: 1-for-60 reverse split (Dec-2024), stock below $1 since Mar-2025, settlement/stockholder-approval dramas, a pending (Feb-2026) agreement to buy **Commercial Bancorp of Wyoming / Farmers State Bank** — whose Fed/Wyoming regulatory applications were **filed in Apr-2026 then withdrawn**, to be refiled "at an appropriate time". FY2026 revenue grew 85% to ~$20.1M, but **stock-locate fees jumped from ~$0.29M to ~$6.8M (~34% of AtlasClearing revenue)** — i.e. the growth is concentrated in a single, cyclical, short-selling-driven line.

**Why the company acts this way.** AtlasClear is a serial-acquirer-by-necessity: it bolted together a SPAC shell + a legacy BD and must keep adding operating businesses (a bank, now an IB) to justify the "vertically integrated platform" story and to generate real, diversified revenue before cash/listing runway runs out. Dawson James is attractive because it is **actually profitable and already clears through AtlasClearing** — i.e. a captive, low-integration-risk target that instantly doubles reported top line. The structural pressure: a loss-making micro-cap with commodity clearing + a volatile stock-locate spike needs durable fee revenue and an equity narrative, fast.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
What's genuinely real: (a) Dawson James is **profitable (~$2.4M net income)** and **already clearing on the platform**, so the revenue and the clearing synergy are not purely aspirational; (b) the deal adds a ranked (#14) micro-cap underwriter giving AtlasClearing captive issuer/investor flow. That is the one concrete traction point.

**Why the value-add is real-but-thin, deeper.** The flywheel ("Dawson James issuers → clear/custody/stock-locate at AtlasClearing → fund a future bank") is logical, but every link is small and some are not yet built: the bank (Commercial Bancorp) is **not closed and its applications were withdrawn**; the IB deal needs **FINRA 1017 approval** (not guaranteed, Dawson James has a history of FINRA fines — $500k texting matter, private-placement due-diligence lapses); and the largest recent revenue driver (stock-locate) is a **boom-bust short-interest product, not a durable moat**. Who captures the margin: clearing/stock-locate is commoditized and rate/volatility-dependent; the durable margin would come from IB advisory + banking NIM — exactly the pieces not yet secured. So the "double the revenue" headline overstates value creation: it is a sum of two sub-scale businesses, paid for largely in depressed stock, with integration/regulatory risk ahead.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Near-term gates: FINRA Rule 1017 continuing-membership approval of Dawson James; ATCH stockholder vote (NYSE American Company Guide); escrow/indemnity mechanics; possible net-capital true-up. Separately the Commercial Bancorp bank deal must have applications **refiled** and cleared by the Fed + Wyoming. Overhang: NYSE American **listing/delisting risk** (price below $1), **material-weakness** remediation, widening operating loss, and going-concern-adjacent financial strain.

**Why the market will / won't go this way + counterintuitive second-order.** Sentiment is deeply negative — ~-97/-98% equity return, penny-stock status — so even a value-accretive, profitable acquisition barely moves the needle; the market is pricing execution/dilution/listing risk, not revenue growth. Counterintuitive: the deal's "no-dilution" engineering can **backfire** — paying sellers in locked-up, falling stock means the headline $24.5M is likely worth materially less, and if ATCH keeps sliding the stock earn-out and closing consideration become a moving, potentially contentious target. The real question is not "does doubling revenue help" but **"can a sub-$1, loss-making, material-weakness micro-cap actually win FINRA + Fed approvals and integrate three acquisitions before its listing/cash runway expires"** — and nothing in this announcement answers that.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Securities clearing/settlement + vertically-integrated broker-dealer roll-ups serving small-cap/micro-cap issuers. No clean TAM from a free source — "no data"; structurally the US clearing/correspondent-clearing stack is consolidated at the top (a handful of DTCC/NSCC-member correspondent clearers; Apex Fintech, Pershing/BNY, and self-clearing scale players dominate retail flow) but highly fragmented at the micro-cap/restricted-securities fringe where AtlasClearing (ex-Wilson-Davis) operates. Barriers are regulatory (SEC/FINRA registration, net-capital rules, FINRA Rule 1017 continuing-membership approval) and capital (net-capital buffers), not technology — which is why the thesis is roll-up, not greenfield. "Why now": AtlasClear is executing a stated vertical-integration playbook (clearing → investment banking via Dawson James → commercial banking via the pending Commercial Bancorp of Wyoming deal), buying the capital-formation front-end that already feeds its own clearing pipe. Dawson James "has begun clearing through AtlasClearing" — the acquisition internalises a customer, the clearest economic logic in the deal (per GlobeNewswire, 2026-10-15).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for this niche: correspondent-clearing agreements signed (AtlasClear reports 6, up from 1 at the Feb-2024 Wilson-Davis close), net capital vs requirement, stock-locate fees, and investment-banking/advisory fee revenue. Basis of competition is distribution + regulatory capacity to handle restricted/micro-cap securities, not price. Players: large correspondent clearers (Apex Fintech — see [[Apex Fintech Solutions debuts Apex AI Suite]]; BNY/Pershing) sit far upmarket; the direct comp set is other small listed BD/clearing names — Siebert Financial (launched correspondent clearing 2022, an investment-banking division Feb-2025 — a near-identical "clearing + micro-cap IB" strategy) and, at vastly larger scale, Virtu Financial. Recent peer moves: Siebert Financial Investment Banking Division launch (Feb-2025); LSEG sold 20% of its post-trade unit to banks for GBP 170M ([[LSEG to sell 20% of post-trade unit to banks for GBP 170M]]) — signals institutional appetite for clearing infrastructure at the opposite end of the market. AtlasClear's position: a sub-scale niche player (analysis) — the only differentiator is the all-under-one-roof pitch for small/micro-cap issuers; moat is thin (switching costs from an integrated issuer→clearing pipe, plus regulatory licences), not network effects or scale.

**Comps & multiples.** ATCH itself is a micro-cap: market cap ~$25.3M at ~$0.17/share (mid-Oct 2026). Against FY2026 (ended 30-Jun-2026) GAAP total revenue of $20.1M → P/S = $25.3M / $20.1M = **~1.3x** (revenue multiple; in-line-to-cheap on the top line, but see risk flags — the P/S is flattered by a one-off 85% stock-locate-fee surge and GAAP net income that was essentially non-cash). Deal arithmetic: total consideration up to $24.5M for Ark/Dawson James; Dawson James audited FY2025 net income ~$2.4M → implied **P/E ≈ $24.5M / $2.4M = ~10.2x** on acquired net income (on the headline cap; the cash-at-signing component is far smaller — $8.25M cash incl. a $2.5M seller note + $16.25M stock priced at market at closing). That ~10x is a reasonable, not rich, price for a profitable small-cap IB. External public comps: Virtu Financial (VIRT) EV/Revenue ~3.5x, EV/EBITDA ~6.4x (per Yahoo Finance) — but VIRT is a ~$14bn-EV market-maker, not comparable in model or scale; include only as a loose ceiling reference. Siebert Financial is the closest strategic comp but its current multiple was not retrievable from a free source — "no data". Distribution not computed (fewer than 3 comparable figures); qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Micro-cap / going-concern lineage + 1-for-60 reverse split.** ATCH intends a 1-for-60 reverse stock split; going-concern substantial doubt was only *alleviated* as of 30-Jun-2026 after remediating a previously-reported material weakness in internal controls (per FY2026 10-K). A sub-$0.20, ~$25M-cap stock doing a 60:1 reverse split is a listing-compliance / distress signal — second-order: the "shares priced at market with no discount" dilution protection is worth less when the market price is itself fragile and about to be reverse-split.
2. **GAAP net income is non-cash and operating losses are widening.** FY2026 net income ~$1.96M was driven by an ~$11.1M fair-value/other-income gain that "brought no cash"; operating loss widened to $9.77M from $4.92M and expenses rose 89%. The business is loss-making at the operating line — the deal doubles revenue but there's no evidence it fixes the cash-burn. The "$47M combined revenue" figure is a non-GAAP arithmetic sum of mismatched fiscal years, explicitly *not* pro forma, excluding transaction costs, financing and acquisition accounting.
3. **Execution / conditionality + related-party overhang.** Only 24.9% is bought; the 75.1% balance needs FINRA Rule 1017 approval and an ATCH shareholder vote. Ark's majority owner Bob Keyser is an AtlasClear director (related-party deal, reviewed by independent directors only) — governance flag. Concurrent Commercial Bancorp of Wyoming acquisition stacks integration + financing risk on a company already burning cash.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a continuation of a roll-up, not a re-rating — AtlasClear is bolting a deal-flow front-end onto its clearing pipe to become a one-stop micro-cap shop (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if the vertical-integration logic is real, AtlasClearing's correspondent-clearing agreement count and stock-locate/clearing revenue should rise materially within 2-3 quarters post-close as Dawson James issuer flow routes through it. What would break the thesis: FINRA Rule 1017 approval stalls/fails, the reverse split doesn't stabilise the listing, or the combined entity stays operating-loss-making and funds the ~$8M cash leg via further dilutive raises — in which case the "doubles revenue" story is financial engineering over a shrinking equity base. Trigger to watch: the Form 8-K / pro forma financials (Article 11) and the FINRA 1017 decision.
