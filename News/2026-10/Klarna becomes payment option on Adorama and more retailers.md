# Klarna becomes payment option on Adorama and more retailers

**New York, October 26, 2026** — Klarna, the everyday finance network, and Adorama, a leading photography, video, audio, drone, and computer retailer, today announced that Klarna is now available as a payment option on Adorama.com, with availability on sister sites Scuba.com and SunnySports.com to follow. Adorama is a destination for photographers, filmmakers and content creators who are looking to buy cameras, lenses, lighting and professional video equipment.

Starting today, Adorama customers shopping online can use Klarna to pay in full for smaller purchases like memory cards and camera bags, split the cost of a new lens or speedlight into four interest-free payments, or finance bigger investments, from professional camera bodies and drones to the laptops and editing rigs that turn footage into finished work.

"Camera and video equipment is exactly the kind of considered purchase where flexibility matters," **said Lisa Robinson, Head of Partner Success, US East Coast and Canada at Klarna.**"We're proud to partner with Adorama to give creatives a more flexible way to invest in the tools and ideas that power their craft."

"Our customers are buying gear they'll rely on for years, so convenient payment solutions is an important part of the buying decision," **said Yoav Shargil, Chief Marketing Officer at Adorama.**"Partnering with Klarna enables us to expand our reach to a new audience of shoppers, and adding this level of choice and flexibility underscores our commitment to making our offerings as accessible as possible.”

**About Klarna**  
Klarna is the everyday finance network. With over 120 million global active Klarna users and 3.8 million transactions per day, Klarna's AI-powered payments and commerce network is empowering people to pay smarter with a mission to be available everywhere for everything. Consumers can pay with Klarna online, in-store and through Apple Pay & Google Pay. Over 1.2 million retailers trust Klarna's innovative solutions to drive growth and loyalty, including Uber, H&M, Saks, Sephora, Macy's, Ikea, Expedia Group, Nike and Airbnb. Klarna is listed on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE: KLAR). For more information, visitKlarna.com.

**About Adorama Inc.**  
Adorama is a leading photography, video, audio, drone, and computer retailer, serving customers for over 50 years. Adorama has grown from its flagship NYC store to include four successful online retailers: Adorama, SunnySports, Scuba.com, and Printique. More than a camera store, Adorama works with creators throughout their journey, providing new, used and rental gear, education, and professional services, while fostering meaningful connections and lasting impact through community engagement programs.

**[0] What happened.** On 26 October 2026 Klarna (NYSE: KLAR) announced its flexible payment options went live on Adorama.com — a ~50-year-old NYC-based photography, video, audio, drone and computer retailer — with sister sites Scuba.com and SunnySports.com to follow (a fourth, Printique, is not named as adding Klarna). 12. Any regulatory risk attached to pushing financing on big-ticket gear to US consumers? Not addressed; Klarna has active EU debt-collection scrutiny elsewhere.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Event.** Klarna goes live as a checkout payment option on Adorama.com (photo/video/audio/drone/computer retailer; 50+ yrs, flagship NYC store), with sister sites Scuba.com and SunnySports.com "to follow." Pay-in-full, interest-free pay-in-4, or longer financing on bigger buys. US-only, merchant integration. No disclosed economics, no committed GMV, no exclusivity.

**Classification: announcement, not a measured outcome.** This is a single-merchant go-live of a non-exclusive, multi-homed checkout feature — the same pattern as **[[Klarna deepens Wayfair partnership with 0% APR financing]]** (Oct 2026) and **[[Klarna partners The White Company on BNPL homeware]]** (Oct 2026, importance 2). Adorama is a specialty e-tailer, far smaller than Klarna's named anchors (Uber, H&M, Sephora, Nike, Airbnb; 1.2M+ merchants). Marginal GMV contribution; value is incremental US coverage, not a strategic inflection.

**Sector context.** US BNPL is large and maturing: BNPL providers originated ~$160B of US credit in 2025 (**[[BNPL providers originated nearly $160 billion of US credit in 2025]]**, Fed note, Jun 2026). Growth is now won merchant-by-merchant at checkout, where providers are largely interchangeable to a multi-homed retailer. The competitive reference point this month is Affirm's *exclusive multi-year* Sam's Club deal (**[[Affirm signs exclusive multi-year BNPL deal with Sam's Club]]**, Oct 2026, importance 3) — a large anchor with lock-in. Adorama is the opposite: small, non-exclusive, no lock-in. Klarna's own competing motions this month (Wayfair 0% APR, The White Company) underline that these are volume/narrative bolt-ons, not moats.

**In-base comps.** Peers pursuing identical merchant/platform-expansion plays: Scalapay×PPRO (Europe distribution, Feb 2026), Stitch adding BNPL across SA merchants (May 2026), Balance×Alibaba.com (B2B BNPL, Jun 2025), Splitit World Mastercard (Oct 2026), Zip adding US banking partners (Oct 2026). Common thread: BNPL is being embedded as a checkout feature; differentiation comes from exclusivity and anchor size, both absent here.

**Why Klarna acts this way (analysis).** As a de-rated, newly public stock (NYSE: KLAR), Klarna needs cite-able US GMV. KLAR traded ~$12.8–13.5 in Oct 2026 (mkt cap ~$4.9B) vs a $40 IPO (-~68%), 52-wk low $12.06. Bolt-on US merchant wins are cheap, low-risk GMV it can point to against its cut 2026 guidance. Creator/pro-gear vertical (considered, higher-AOV purchases) is a reasonable fit for longer-tenor financing.

**IR grounding (real figures).**
- Q2 2026 actuals (cited in the in-base Wayfair note, from Klarna 6-K / Q2'26 release): GMV $36.6B (+18% YoY), revenue $1.042B (+27%), first positive net income +$9M, credit losses 0.52% of GMV, merchants +54% to 1.2M, 120M+ active consumers — but Klarna **cut FY2026 guidance**, triggering a ~19% share drop. [Klarna Q2 2026 results, investors.klarna.com]
- FY2026 guidance (post-cut): GMV $149–151B, revenue $4.08–4.16B (reported take rate ~2.74–2.75% of GMV; implied ~2.84–2.85% ex-presentation change, up vs 2.80% prior), TMD $1.62–1.65B, adj. operating income $280–300M. Q3'26 guide: GMV $35–36B, revenue $940–980M, adj. op income only $5–15M. [investors.klarna.com]
- Prior disclosed take-rate trajectory: Q3'25 investor deck shows revenue +51% US / +34% group YoY "with increasing take rates" (take rate = revenue/GMV). [Klarna Investor Presentation, Q3'25; drive: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1sEqM5-bhdOD1m4R9qBxVLY852afN4gPH/view]
- Revenue model (20-F 2025, filed 2026-02-26): revenue driven by GMV × ARPAC; GMV is "a key driver of our revenue" but not itself revenue. [drive: https://drive.google.com/file/d/18vUQFIgWP8DBHEwonSJZooWgOUJ29Wie/view]

**Scale test.** Adorama + Scuba + SunnySports are specialty e-tailers; even generously, their combined GMV is a rounding error against a ~$150B FY run-rate. On Klarna's ~2.8% take rate, any plausible Adorama volume is immaterial to revenue. Treat as coverage/PR, not financial signal.

**Risk flags.**
1. **No disclosed economics / no committed volume.** No MDR, no GMV target, no AOV/conversion lift, no credit-loss split. Pure go-live announcement. (open)
2. **Non-exclusive, multi-homed.** Klarna is one of several checkout BNPL options; a feature, not a moat. Adorama can add/keep Affirm, PayPal, Afterpay at will. Zero switching cost. (structural)
3. **Immaterial scale.** Specialty retailer(s) vs ~$150B FY GMV — not a needle-mover; PR/narrative value only. (analysis)
4. **"To follow" is unexecuted.** Scuba.com and SunnySports.com are announced, not live — partial go-live risk. (open)
5. **Weak earnings/stock backdrop.** FY2026 guidance was cut; KLAR ~$12.8 (-68% from IPO), thin Q3'26 adj. op income guide ($5–15M). Market is skeptical of incremental US GMV claims. (IR-grounded)
6. **US credit-risk cyclicality.** Longer-tenor financing on higher-AOV pro gear carries more consumer-credit exposure; Klarna's 0.52%-of-GMV loss rate is a current, not guaranteed-forward, figure. (macro)

**Bottom line.** Immaterial, non-exclusive single-merchant US go-live — directionally consistent with Klarna's post-IPO US-GMV push but with no disclosed economics, no lock-in, and trivial scale. Announcement, not traction. Importance ~1–2/5.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (headline read).** BEAT on the print, MISS on the guide — the market read it as a miss (stock -19%). Latest reported quarter = Q2 2026 (reported 2026-08-18; this Adorama partnership is a 2026-10-26 US merchant-integration item, not an earnings event — figures folded as background on the merchant engine behind it). Revenue $1.042bn (+27% YoY; vs public consensus ~$992.8m → beat ~+$49m / ~+5%) · GAAP EPS $0.01 (vs est. ~$(0.05)) · TMD $446m (+42% YoY), the metric management optimizes against · but FY26 GMV guide cut to $149–151bn (from >$155bn) on currency + softer German volumes.

**Key figures (Q2 2026, with growth).**
- GMV: $36.6bn (+18% YoY); US GMV $7.9bn (+27% YoY), 22% of volume; ex-US $28.8bn (+15%).
- Total revenue: $1.042bn (+27% YoY) — outpacing GMV, a take-rate/monetization story not a volume one.
- Transaction margin dollars (TMD): $446m (+42% YoY) — growing faster than both revenue and GMV; management's lead KPI.
- Adjusted operating income: $91m (+214% YoY). GAAP operating income $27m (vs $(46)m loss in Q2'25). Net income $9m (vs $(53)m loss in Q2'25).
- EPS (GAAP): $0.01 (vs $(0.14) in Q2'25).
- Revenue per active consumer: +24% YoY — the core "greater share of everyday spend" thesis.
- Credit: provisions for credit losses 0.52% of GMV (vs 0.56% in Q2'25) — improving.
- Network: 120m active consumers (+8% YoY); 1.2m+ merchants (+54% YoY). Note consumer growth (+8%) is decelerating vs merchant growth (+54%) — growth is now per-user monetization, not user adds.

**By segment / driver.** Growth is quality-over-quantity: TMD (+42%) > revenue (+27%) > GMV (+18%), i.e. Klarna is earning more margin per dollar transacted (improved credit, better pricing/mix, US expansion). US is the standout (+27% GMV) while ex-US (+15%) is dragged by Germany. The Adorama/Scuba/SunnySports win fits the +54% YoY merchant-count surge — adding considered-purchase verticals (photo/video gear) that lean on the 4-interest-free / financing split, exactly the "greater share of everyday spend" lever driving revenue-per-consumer +24%.

**vs expectations / prior period.** Beat vs public consensus: revenue $1.042bn vs ~$992.8m expected (+~$49m / ~+5%); GAAP EPS $0.01 vs est. ~$(0.05) (beat ~$0.06). Sequential trajectory is clean acceleration: Q1'26 revenue $1,012m (+44% YoY), GMV $33.7b (+33% YoY), TMD $389m, adj op profit $68m → Q2'26 $1,042m rev, $36.6b GMV, $446m TMD, $91m adj op income. YoY revenue growth rate decelerated (Q1 +44% → Q2 +27%) partly on tougher comps/FX, but absolute profitability expanded sharply. (Public consensus as of earnings date 2026-08-18.)

**Guidance / forward.** CUT. FY26 GMV lowered to $149–151bn from >$155bn; FY26 revenue $4.08–4.16bn (down from the prior >$4.34bn implied), citing ~$600m currency translation + "a more measured view of primarily German volumes" — US assumptions left unchanged. Offsetting: FY26 TMD guided $1.62–1.65bn (1.09% of GMV) and adjusted operating income $280–300m — "more margin on less volume." Q3'26 guide: GMV $35–36bn, revenue $940–980m, TMD $340–360m, adj op income $5–15m — note Q3 revenue midpoint (~$960m) sits below Q2's $1,042m, implying a softer sequential top line. Management tone: confident on the TMD/margin narrative (CEO Siemiatkowski: "we measure our progress in transaction margin dollars"), deflecting the volume miss onto FX and one country. De-PR: the release leads with TMD +42% and profitability while the GMV guide-down and decelerating consumer adds (+8%) are the parts the market punished.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. Guide-down on German/volume softness → why it matters: BNPL's thesis rests on volume expansion; a demand crack in Klarna's largest market signals the European consumer, not just FX, is weakening → second-order: pressure to over-index on the US (hence partnerships like Adorama) to keep the growth story intact.
2. TMD +42% > revenue +27% > GMV +18% → the unit-economics flywheel is real and durable (better credit, monetization per user), supporting the pivot from "growth at all costs" to profitable scale → second-order: makes the stock a margin-expansion story, de-risking the volume shortfall if it holds.
3. Consumer adds decelerating to +8% YoY while merchants +54% → growth is now supply-side (more places to pay) + deeper per-user spend (+24% rev/consumer), not new-user led → second-order: US merchant wins (Adorama et al.) are the mechanical lever to convert the +54% merchant growth into GMV, so each such integration is thesis-relevant, not noise.
4. Analyst reaction post-print: BMO cut PT to $15 from $19 (Market Perform); TD Cowen cut to $18 from $19 (Hold); stock -19% on the day — Street read it as guide-down > beat (cited; not an internal PT).
