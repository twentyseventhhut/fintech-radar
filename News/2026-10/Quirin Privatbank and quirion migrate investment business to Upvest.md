 Quirin Privatbank and its digital platform quirion are partnering with Upvest to modernise their investment business by migrating it onto Upvest's API-first infrastructure
The partnership will start with offering the new Altersvorsorgedepot from early 2027, positioning Quirin Privatbank and quirion among the first movers on Germany's pension reform
With Upvest as its new investment infrastructure provider, Quirin Privatbank and quirion gain a modern technological partner, covering brokerage, custody, and settlement, and a digital-first foundation for AI-enabled, personalised investment experiences at scale
 Berlin, 15. October 2026 - Quirin Privatbank and its digital investment platform quirion today announce a partnership with Upvest, Europe's leading investment infrastructure provider. This collaboration lays the foundation to modernise Quirin Privatbank and quirion's investment infrastructure end-to-end, enabling faster innovation, including AI-enabled investments, automated portfolio management, and a better user experience powered by real-time data. The partnership starts with the launch of the Altersvorsorgedepot, Germany's new state-subsidised pension product, in early 2027.
Quirin Privatbank and quirion together manage more than €11 billion in AuM for over 118,000 end users 1 . Focussing on an end-to-end wealth approach offered to affluent investors, Quirin Privatbank is known for its fee based, independent advisory and tailored investment strategies based on end users’ financial needs and goals. As one of Germany's fastest-growing and most highly awarded robo-advisors, quirion alone grew its assets under management by 20% in 2025 2 . As such, this represents one of the largest infrastructure migrations in German private banking, underscoring the scale of Quirin's commitment to modernising its investment offering. 
By migrating to Upvest’s cloud-native infrastructure, the bank benefits especially from Upvest's portfolio engine. It will support discretionary portfolio management across both quirion's robo-advisory offering and Quirin's private banking business, enabling automated portfolio construction for custom mandates and rebalancing at scale. Quirin Privatbank and quirion will furthermore rely on cost-efficient technology built to scale, offering order execution and settlement, custody, and reporting, alongside modern use cases such as savings plans, fractions, and an end-to-end pension offering. Real-time availability of data alongside the whole value chain will enable the further extension of the AI capabilities of the financial planning and generative advisory tool quirion.AI .
As a first product, Quirin Privatbank and quirion will offer the Altersvorsorgedepot. Replacing the Riester-Rente as part of Germany's pension reform, the Altersvorsorgedepot gives savers a tax-efficient way to invest for retirement with more flexible investment choices than before. Leveraging Upvest’s Investment API, the bank aims to reach the approximately 83% of Germans 3 who currently do not invest with a transparent retirement savings offering.
Following this launch, new and existing end users of quirion will be migrated onto Upvest’s investment infrastructure, followed by Quirin Privatbank's own private banking investment business in a second phase. 
 "We are building an integrated, highly scalable platform for both Quirin Privatbank and quirion – from front-end to back-end. Upvest is a key building block on that journey: its regulated, API-first infrastructure enables us to launch the Altersvorsorgedepot at scale and offer hundreds of thousands of clients a transparent, low-cost path to retirement investing. This is the starting point for modernising our entire investment value chain." Sebastian Böttner CIO of Quirin Privatbank 
 "Quirin and quirion are modernising their investment infrastructure end-to-end, and we're proud to be the partner behind that transformation. We're starting with the Altersvorsorgedepot, giving hundreds of thousands of savers a tax-advantaged, transparent way to invest for retirement, many for the first time. But this is about more than one product: modern infrastructure is what makes AI-enabled, personalised wealth management at scale possible." Martin Kassing Co-founder and CEO at Upvest 
 About Quirin Privatbank and quirion 
 Quirin Privatbank and its digital subsidiary quirion combine personalised wealth advisory services at 15 locations across Germany with leading digital investment management in an innovative hybrid business model.
As the first private bank in Germany, Quirin Privatbank abolished industry-standard commissions entirely back in 2006 and has advised private investors on a fee-only basis ever since: transparent, independent and free of conflicts of interest. Its scientifically grounded investment concept "Markt – Meinung – Zinsen" ("Market – Sentiment – Interest Rates") draws a clear line between speculation and predictable wealth accumulation.
Personal advisory services are complemented by quirion AG, founded in 2013. As a pioneer in digital wealth management, quirion was named Stiftung Warentest's test winner for the third consecutive time in 2026. With around 118,000 clients and €11.1 billion in assets under management (as of June 30, 2026), the group ranks among the most established providers in the German market. With the state-subsidized Altersvorsorgedepot launching in 2027, the group is also placing a clear strategic focus on simple, low-cost and future-proof retirement provision for all.
 About Upvest 
Upvest is a technology company and investment firm that provides banks, brokers, and wealth managers with modern API-based investment infrastructure. In addition to software, it provides trading, settlement, custody, and back-office services on behalf of its clients. This enables financial institutions to modernise their investment operations comprehensively and cost-effectively, and to grow profitably. Upvest's clients include fast-growing fintechs such as Revolut, N26, bunq, Qonto, and Raisin, as well as digital banks like DKB and Openbank (Santander).
Founded in 2017, Upvest has 300 employees and processes over 100 million trades per year. The company continues to be independently managed by its founders Martin Kassing, Dr. Til Rochow, and Tobias Auferoth, and is securely financed for the long term by investors including Sapphire Ventures and Bessemer Venture Partners.

# Context-enrichment: Quirin Privatbank and quirion migrate investment business to Upvest
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- Quirin Privatbank and its digital robo-advisor subsidiary quirion signed Upvest as their new end-to-end investment infrastructure provider (brokerage, custody, settlement, reporting, portfolio engine), replacing legacy in-house/third-party rails. Announced Berlin, 15 Oct 2026.
- This is a **signed client win + migration commitment, not a go-live.** Nothing runs on Upvest yet. The first product (Germany's new Altersvorsorgedepot / AVD) launches "early 2027"; German trade press is more precise than the Upvest PR: AVD customers get an Upvest account from **Jan 2027**, new quirion clients migrate in **Q2 2027**, existing quirion clients in **Q3 2027**, and Quirin's own private-banking book only "by mid-2028" (dasinvestment / private-banking-magazin). So the full migration is a ~2-year program; today's headline is the earliest, smallest slice.
- Scale cited: >€11bn AuM (€11.1bn as of 30 Jun 2026) across ~118,000 clients; quirion grew AuM +20% in 2025. German press puts Quirin's **IT spend at ~€20m** on the Upvest switch — a figure absent from the Upvest PR.
- **Why framed this way:** Upvest's PR leads with "one of the largest infrastructure migrations in German private banking" and the €11bn/118k figures to signal enterprise credibility; it buries that phase one touches only the brand-new AVD product (zero legacy balances) and that the big private-banking book doesn't move until 2028. The AVD-first sequencing is the low-risk way to migrate — you launch a greenfield product on new rails before you dare move 118k live accounts.
- **Why it matters (2nd order):** The real anchor isn't the €11bn (which mostly moves in 2027-28) but the **AVD land-grab**. The AVD is a greenfield, state-subsidised, mass-market product with no incumbent holder — both Quirin and Upvest are positioning as first movers, and Upvest has already pre-built the AVD tax wrapper (see its $125m raise rationale). This is less "Quirin picked a vendor" and more "two players racing to own the Riester-successor pipe before 1 Jan 2027."

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Upvest's rivals (embedded investing / BaaS, Europe):** WealthKernel (UK-centric, API-first, 50+ clients, strong on UK tax wrappers — ISAs, SIPPs, JISAs) and Lemon Markets (Berlin, brokerage-as-a-service, ~3 API products). Per openbankingtracker, WealthKernel has the broadest API surface (7 products vs Upvest's 4), but Upvest leads on enterprise scale (>30 institutions, >100m orders/yr, MiFID II investment-firm licence). FNZ (incumbent platform, far larger/legacy) is the "old world" being displaced.
- **Who Quirin/quirion could have picked:** itself kept building in-house, FNZ-style platforms, or a rival API provider. Choosing Upvest — a German-licensed, Berlin-based firm already running DKB, Openbank (Santander), N26, Revolut — is the safe "proven at bank scale" pick for a regulated private bank.
- **quirion's own competitive front:** On the AVD it has a separate strategic deal with **Vanguard** (Sep 2026) for the legally-mandated low-cost "standard depot" — two Vanguard ETFs at 0.07%/0.08% TER. So quirion is assembling an AVD stack: Vanguard (cheap beta) + Upvest (rails). Rival robos (Scalable Capital, Trade Republic, Finanzguru, banks) are chasing the same AVD pool.
- **Why the lay of the land is this way (2nd order):** European banks face a build-vs-buy squeeze — legacy securities stacks are too slow/costly to localise for each new tax wrapper (AVD in DE, SIPP in UK). Upvest's pitch is "we absorb the regulatory wrapper complexity, you ship in months." That's exactly what sells to a mid-size private bank that cannot afford a multi-year in-house AVD build. The competitive question is less feature-breadth than **which vendor banks trust to carry regulated assets at scale** — and here Upvest's bank logos are the moat, not its API count.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Upvest:** founded 2017 (Kassing, Rochow, Auferoth), Berlin; ~280-300 staff; >100m orders/yr; raised $125m (Mar 2026: $90m equity led by Sapphire + Tencent, Bessemer, BlackRock + $35m debt) at a ~$735M / €640m valuation — explicitly to fund "localised pension products (AVD, SIPP) + AI wealth." Client roster: Revolut, N26, bunq, Qonto, Raisin, DKB, Openbank. The Quirin win is a textbook execution of that raise thesis — Upvest said it would win pension-wrapper migrations; here is one.
- **Quirin/quirion:** Quirin is a fee-only, no-commission private bank (abolished kickbacks in 2006), 15 locations; quirion (founded 2013) is one of Germany's largest/most-awarded robos (Stiftung Warentest winner 3x). The hybrid advisory+digital model is why it needs a single modern engine spanning both discretionary private-banking mandates and robo portfolios.
- **Why Quirin acts this way (2nd order):** A fee-only bank's margin is thin and its differentiation is UX + transparency + low cost, not product manufacturing. Owning/maintaining securities back-office is a cost centre with no customer-facing payoff. Outsourcing to Upvest converts a fixed legacy-IT burden into variable, scalable infra and frees quirion.AI (its gen-AI advisory tool) to consume real-time data. The €20m spend is the price of avoiding a far larger multi-year in-house rebuild before the AVD regulatory deadline.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low-to-moderate.** The *pattern* (bank/robo migrates its investment stack onto Upvest) is now routine — Openbank did literally this in Aug 2025, plus Salt Bank, IG, Satispay, Qonto, SumUp, LIQID, Webull in the internal corpus. The genuinely new element is the **AVD angle** and the depth (full end-to-end migration of an established €11bn German private bank, not just a neo-bank bolt-on).
- **Traction = announcement, not adoption.** Zero assets live on Upvest today; first real test is AVD in Jan 2027, and the load-bearing migration (existing clients + private banking) is 2027-2028. Execution risk on a 118k-account migration is material and unproven here.
- **Who captures the margin (2nd order):** Upvest earns per-order/custody/AuA fees — a volume game. The AVD is mass-market but ultra-low-margin (Vanguard ETFs at 0.07%), so Upvest's economics depend on *volume and multi-product attach* (savings plans, fractions, SIPP-style wrappers), not fat per-trade spreads. The risk: AVD could be a low-margin treadmill where the subsidised standard-depot commoditises everyone. Upvest's value-add holds only if it becomes the default pipe for *many* AVD providers (platform economics) rather than a per-client integration shop.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Market size:** industry estimates ~**4.5m AVD contracts and ~€7bn/yr new savings for 2027**, plus ~16m existing Riester contracts eligible to switch from 2027 (dasinvestment / die-privatbank). A quirion survey (May 2026) found 52% of eligible savers would consider opening an AVD. This is the structural prize driving the whole move.
- **Sequencing:** AVD launch Jan 2027 → new quirion clients Q2 → existing quirion Q3 → Quirin private banking mid-2028.
- **Risks:** (1) migration execution (118k accounts, regulated custody — the hard part is 2027-28, not the 2027 greenfield launch); (2) AVD take-up could disappoint vs the 4.5m estimate (German retail under-invests — "83% don't invest" is Upvest's own framing); (3) vendor concentration — Upvest becomes single point of failure for a bank's entire securities operation; (4) margin compression on a subsidised low-cost product.
- **Why the market goes this way (2nd order, counterintuitive):** The AVD reform is a government-manufactured demand spike with a hard Jan-2027 deadline — it forces every German wealth provider to re-platform on a fixed timeline, which is precisely the scenario that favours a "buy, don't build" infra vendor. The counterintuitive effect: the AVD's mandated cheapness commoditises the *product*, pushing all the competition into the *infrastructure and distribution* layers — where Upvest (rails) and Vanguard (beta) quietly capture the durable share while the customer-facing brands fight over thin spreads.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Investment-infrastructure-as-a-service (B2B2C brokerage/custody/settlement APIs) for banks, brokers and wealth managers. No clean independent TAM — "no data" on sector size; structurally it is a thin but concentrating layer: a handful of regulated API providers (Upvest, DriveWealth, Alpaca, Lemon Markets, former WealthKernel) sit between fintech front-ends and the trading/custody rails, with high entry barriers (brokerage/custody licences, scale economics on per-trade cost). Driver / "why now": Germany's pension reform — the Altersvorsorgereformgesetz passed the Bundestag 27 Mar 2026, signed 8 May 2026, launching the state-subsidised Altersvorsorgedepot on 1 Jan 2027, replacing Riester, allowing 100% ETF/stock investing with a hard 1% total-cost cap (per xpert.digital / germanpedia, as of 2026). A regulated low-cost pension product is a structural tailwind for cheap API rails over legacy in-house stacks — second-order effect: a cost cap pushes providers toward variable-cost infrastructure, exactly Upvest's pitch.

**Risk flags.**
1. Execution / timeline — this is "one of the largest infrastructure migrations in German private banking" (PR's own claim) staged in two phases (quirion first, then Quirin private banking) and gated on a brand-new regulatory product (Altersvorsorgedepot) that only goes live 1 Jan 2027; slippage in the pension launch or migration directly delays revenue and client experience.
2. Client concentration / counterparty dependence — Quirin/quirion hand their entire investment value chain (brokerage, custody, settlement, portfolio engine) to Upvest; second-order effect: an outage, mispricing or regulatory stumble at the infra layer now hits a licensed bank's core offering, and the bank is disintermediated from its own rails.
3. Margin captured by the infra layer + cost-cap squeeze — the 1% total-cost cap on the standard pension product compresses economics across the whole chain; whoever doesn't control the lowest-cost rail loses margin, and neither party discloses the take rate/pricing of the deal (`[UNSOURCED]` — "who's silent about what").

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The pension reform is turning German retail investing into an infrastructure land-grab: whoever powers the Altersvorsorgedepot at launch captures sticky, multi-decade savings flows. Falsifiable thesis: Upvest consolidates as the default EU pension-investing rail — trigger to watch: 2–3 more German/EU banks naming Upvest for AVD before 1 Jan 2027. What breaks it: the Quirin migration slips past the 2027 launch, or Alpaca Europe / DriveWealth win a comparable EU bank, signalling the market stays contested rather than consolidating.
