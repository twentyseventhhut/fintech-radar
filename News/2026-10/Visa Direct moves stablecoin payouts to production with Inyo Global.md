 Visa Direct Launches Stablecoin-Powered Money Movement as Pilots Move to Production
 
SAN FRANCISCO — Today, Visa (NYSE: V) announced the next phase of its stablecoin strategy as Visa Direct stablecoin capabilities advance from pilot stage to commercial deployment. The milestone marks an important step in bringing stablecoin-enabled money movement into real-world commercial operations. In the initial rollout, Inyo Global, a Visa Direct processor, is the first enabler of this new offering, powering it for eligible Visa Direct clients and partners.¹
Over the past year, Visa Direct has worked with clients and partners to explore how stablecoins can help improve the way businesses fund and move money across borders. As interest in faster, more flexible just-in-time funding solutions continues to grow, Visa is now seeing those efforts move beyond experimentation and into day-to-day business operations.
As part of this next phase, Visa is working with Inyo Global, a payments infrastructure provider serving money transmitters, fintechs, banks and payment businesses, to bring Visa Direct's stablecoin-enabled prefunding to market. Inyo has integrated the capability into its platform, giving the businesses it serves the option to prefund cross-border payouts through Visa Direct with USDC in near real time, rather than relying solely on traditional funding windows.²
Through a single connection, Inyo's platform lets businesses onboard users, manage fraud and compliance, accept payments and send domestic and international payouts across more than 180 countries, powered by Visa Direct.
"The next chapter for stablecoins is adoption," said Vira Platonova, Global Head of Visa Direct. "Over the last several years, we've worked with several partners and clients to explore how these technologies can improve money movement. We're now seeing those efforts translate into live implementations, helping businesses access more flexible and efficient ways to fund and move money globally through Visa Direct, starting with Inyo Global as our first enabler."
Visa Direct Head Vira Platonova will discuss how stablecoins are reshaping global money movement during several Money20/20 USA sessions next week.
From Pilot to Production
ln 2025, Visa introduced stablecoin prefunding and payout pilot programs through Visa Direct.
The pilots demonstrated how stablecoins could support cross-border funding, liquidity management and global money movement for eligible clients.
Today, those capabilities are moving from pilot programs and into real-world commercial operations.
Inyo Global is the first enabler to bring Visa Direct's stablecoin prefunding capability into commercial production.
"Innovation in money movement is ultimately about creating a better experience for customers, and that's exactly our focus at Inyo," said Robson Dantas, Chief Product and Technology Officer and co-founder at Inyo Global. "By integrating Visa Direct's stablecoin-enabled prefunding into the Inyo platform, we're giving the money transmitters, fintechs and payment companies we serve a new way to fund their payouts, one that is available around the clock and runs through the same Inyo APIs they already use."
The implementation builds on Visa's broader efforts to connect traditional payment infrastructure with blockchain-enabled technologies. By offering stablecoin funding and moving money, Visa is helping clients explore new ways to improve efficiency while maintaining the reliability, security and scale of Visa's global network.
Visa’s work with Inyo Global reflects growing demand for stablecoin-powered solutions that can help businesses manage liquidity, fund payment activity and move money internationally with greater speed and flexibility. As adoption continues to accelerate, Visa is working with clients and partners to bring stablecoin-enabled capabilities to additional money movement use cases around the world.

# Context-enrichment: Visa Direct moves stablecoin payouts to production with Inyo Global
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-15, Visa (NYSE: V) announced that Visa Direct's stablecoin-enabled **prefunding** capability has moved from pilot to "commercial production," with **Inyo Global** (a Visa Direct processor, Cambridge, Mass.) as the **first enabler**. Mechanism: businesses that process cross-border payouts via Visa Direct can now prefund those payouts with **USDC in near real time / 24-7**, through Inyo's existing single-API connection, instead of parking fiat in advance via traditional funding windows. Payouts reach across 180+ countries; recipients are still paid in local fiat (stablecoin is a funding/liquidity layer, not the end rail). Timed one week ahead of Money20/20 USA (Vira Platonova, Global Head of Visa Direct, speaking there).

**What this is NOT (de-PR):**
- Not a new product, only the first **named commercial enabler**. The pilot was announced at SIBOS Sept-2025 ([[Visa launches stablecoin prefunding pilot via Visa Direct]], [[Visa Direct taps stablecoins for faster business funding]]), targeted for "limited availability by April 2026." So "pilot → production" is roughly on the original schedule, ~6 months late on the "limited availability" marker.
- "Production" = one enabler live, eligibility "varies by geography," no clients, no volume, no GMV, no fee/economics disclosed. The footnotes ("subject to eligibility and availability") do a lot of work. This is "live but empty" — adoption unproven.
- Only USDC named (consistent with Visa's Circle alignment), despite Visa supporting 4+ stablecoins across 9 chains elsewhere ([[Visa adds four stablecoins across four blockchains]], [[Visa expands stablecoin settlement network to $7bn run rate]]).

**Why structured this way (the real story the PR hides):** Visa's ORIGINAL Visa Direct stablecoin payout/prefunding enabler was **BVNK** (Visa Ventures invested May-2025; partnership announced ~Jan-2026). **Mastercard agreed to buy BVNK for up to $1.8bn (17-Mar-2026) and closed 3-Aug-2026**, turning Visa's stablecoin ally into a wholly-owned subsidiary of its direct competitor. Visa was publicly "looking for a new stablecoin settlement partner" (Coindesk, Aug-2026). So "Inyo as first enabler" is substantially a **replacement/re-platforming** forced by losing BVNK to Mastercard — not a serene graduation from pilot. That reframes the central question from "is Visa's stablecoin story progressing?" to "how cleanly did Visa re-route around a competitor capturing its key infrastructure partner?"

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Mastercard / BVNK** — the sharpest rival. Mastercard now *owns* ~$30bn-annualized, 130-market, 25+-license stablecoin infra (closed Aug-2026), and co-launched a Mastercard+BVNK global money platform (mid-Oct-2026, essentially same week). Mastercard chose to **own** the rail; Visa relies on **partnering** with enablers (Inyo now, BVNK before). Second-order: owning gives Mastercard margin capture and roadmap control; Visa's partner-model keeps it asset-light but exposed to exactly the disintermediation that just happened with BVNK.
- **Stripe / Bridge** — building owned stablecoin payment infra (acquired Bridge). Another "own, don't rent" play.
- **Worldpay** — uses BVNK for stablecoin payouts (now a Mastercard-owned supplier).
- **Fireblocks-powered rivals** — Euronet chose Fireblocks for cross-border stablecoin payments ([[Euronet chooses Fireblocks for cross-border stablecoin payments]]).
- **Corpay + BVNK** — corporate payments peer using stablecoin wallets/treasury rails ([[Corpay partners with BVNK to add stablecoin wallets]]).
- Position: Visa is at **parity-to-slightly-behind** Mastercard on the *infrastructure ownership* axis, but still ahead on network scale and its separate $7bn-run-rate stablecoin **settlement** program (distinct from Visa Direct prefunding). Visa Direct's advantage is reach (180+ countries) and an established payout network; the Inyo news is Visa defending, not extending, its lead.

## [2] Company history / fit
Visa Direct stablecoin trajectory: Sept-2025 SIBOS pilot announced (prefund Visa Direct with stablecoins, "money in the bank"; target limited availability Apr-2026) → BVNK partnership (~Jan-2026) → BVNK lost to Mastercard (Aug-2026) → Inyo Global production (Oct-2026). Separately, Visa's **settlement** network hit a $7bn annualized run rate across 9 chains (Apr-2026). Visa also runs a USDC-settlement-tied card program in 50+ countries. The Inyo move fits Visa's consistent logic: be the **neutral network/common layer** and let third-party enablers do integration — an asset-light posture that preserves Visa's take-rate without betting the balance sheet on one stablecoin stack. **Why Visa acts this way:** a network business monetizes ubiquity and neutrality; owning one enabler would both cannibalize partner relationships and concentrate regulatory/operational risk. The downside, just demonstrated, is that an "enabler" can be bought out from under you.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Genuinely new vs the Sept-2025 pilot: a **named, live commercial enabler** and a concrete productized path (prefund USDC through Inyo's single API, 24/7). That is a real step past "pilot/explore." But value-add is **thin and unproven on traction**: zero disclosed clients, volume, pricing, corridors, or fraud/compliance liability split. "Production" with one enabler and geography caveats is closer to GA-readiness than to demonstrated adoption. **Who captures the margin:** Visa keeps the network take-rate on the payout; Circle earns on USDC float; Inyo earns integration/processing fees. The stablecoin layer here is **operational liquidity/treasury** (freeing prefunded fiat), not a consumer disruption — the CoinDesk framing ("stablecoins became idle cash / mostly operational use case") applies. The multiple-supporting claim (faster, cheaper cross-border funding) is plausible but rests on adoption Visa hasn't shown. Net: real but incremental; the news's weight comes more from the **competitive context (BVNK displacement)** than from the capability itself.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect Visa to add more enablers beyond Inyo and more use cases ("additional money movement use cases"), and to push the narrative hard at Money20/20 USA (next week). Regulatory backdrop is tailwind (US GENIUS/stablecoin regime maturing; Visa, Mastercard, Circle all leaning in). Visa stock hit fresh record highs around the announcement window (sentiment constructive, though not attributable to this item alone). **Why the market goes this way + counterintuitive second-order:** the strategic prize is being the **default liquidity/settlement layer** for stablecoin money movement. Counterintuitively, Mastercard *owning* BVNK may be the riskier posture — it ties Mastercard to one stack and invites channel conflict with other enablers — while Visa's "many enablers" model is more resilient *provided* no single enabler is again decisive. The real risk for Visa is not this launch failing but **stablecoin prefunding staying a niche treasury tool** (idle-cash problem) rather than scaling to material volume, in which case the whole Visa/Mastercard stablecoin arms race is strategically defensive, not growth-generative.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Cross-border B2B money movement + stablecoin-enabled prefunding. Stablecoin payment volume (ex-trading/automated transfers) reached ~$390bn in 2025, of which B2B was ~$226bn, up ~733% YoY (per KuCoin flash, as of 2025). Against a ~$35tn global B2B cross-border flow in 2025 (per secondary sources), stablecoin penetration is still a rounding error — the TAM is the slow displacement of correspondent-banking prefunding, not the whole flow. Structure: concentrated at the network layer (Visa/Mastercard duopoly) but fragmenting at the stablecoin-rails layer (Circle, Bridge/Stripe, BVNK, Thunes, bank issuers). Barriers = network reach + regulatory/licensing + compliance tooling, not tech. Why now: (1) prefunding is the pain point — treasury must pre-position fiat in destination corridors during banking windows; USDC lets clients fund "just-in-time" 24/7; (2) regulatory clarity (US stablecoin framework) + Money20/20 USA timing (Visa Direct head Platonova presenting next week) make this a commercial, not experimental, launch.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: Visa Direct transactions (12.6bn FY2025, +27% YoY per Q4 FY25 release); stablecoin settlement run-rate; endpoint reach; corridor count. Players & basis of competition — competition is on reach + funding flexibility, not price:
- **Visa Direct** — this launch moves USDC *prefunding* from pilot (started 2025, see internal comps) to production via Inyo Global; reach cited at 18bn+ endpoints / 195 countries (per Yahoo/Finance, as of Aug 2026); 180+ countries in the Inyo PR.
- **Mastercard Move** — competing stance via Thunes Direct Global Network (130+ countries, 80+ currencies); broader stablecoin support — USDC + Paxos PYUSD/USDG + Ripple RLUSD — vs Visa's USDC-first (per Transak/press, as of mid-2026).
- **Rails/disruptor layer** — Circle (USDC issuer, settlement partner), Stripe/Bridge, BVNK (>$20bn processed, see internal comp; Coinbase/Mastercard reportedly in talks to buy it), Thunes (own Pay-to-Stablecoin-Wallets product).
Position: Visa is **ahead on distribution/endpoints** but **narrower on stablecoin breadth** (USDC-only here) vs Mastercard. Moat = VisaNet reach + settlement relationships + compliance, i.e. network effects + scale (analysis); the stablecoin tech itself is not proprietary (Inyo is the integrator; Circle is the issuer) — Visa's edge is the rail, not the coin.

**Comps & multiples.** Public comps (as of 2026-10-08, per macrotrends/financecharts):
- Visa (NYSE: V): market cap ~$706bn; P/E ~32.2x.
- Mastercard (NYSE: MA): market cap ~$514bn; P/E ~32.3x — the two have converged (Visa was cheaper, ~29.8x, earlier in 2026).
P/E ~32x on both vs a rough network-payments sanity band (~25–50x for high-moat compounders) = **in-line**, not stretched; justified by double-digit net-revenue growth (Visa net revenue $40.0bn FY2025, +11% YoY; Q4 FY25 $10.7bn, +12%). Stablecoin is immaterial to the multiple today: Visa's own stablecoin *settlement* run-rate hit a ~$20bn annualized rate in fiscal Q2 2026, +15x YoY (per cryptodaily/onebullex) — but **no revenue is yet booked** against it (per Coinotag), so comps are driven entirely by the legacy card business, not this news.
Internal comps (`[[wikilink]]`): [[Visa launches stablecoin prefunding pilot via Visa Direct]] (2025-10, the pilot this productionizes), [[Visa to pilot pre-funded stablecoins via Visa Direct]], [[Visa Canada and Wealthsimple pilot USDC stablecoin settlement]] (2026-05), [[BVNK surpasses $20bn in processing volume]] and [[Thunes launches Pay-to-Stablecoin-Wallets solution]] (rails-layer peers). No private-round valuation disclosed for Inyo Global — "no data".

**Risk flags.**
1. **Economics undisclosed / unmonetized.** Visa books no revenue on stablecoin settlement yet (per Coinotag) and the PR is silent on who earns the spread on USDC prefunding — the headline metric (settlement run-rate) is volume, not margin; adoption could cannibalize card-funding economics rather than add to them (second-order: a reach story, not yet a revenue story).
2. **Single-enabler concentration + USDC-only.** "First enabler" = Inyo Global only at launch, and USDC-only; Mastercard Move already spans multiple regulated coins (USDC/PYUSD/USDG/RLUSD) via Thunes — if multi-coin treasury flexibility matters to clients, Visa's narrower initial surface is a gap until more enablers/coins go live.
3. **Disintermediation / rails commoditization.** The stablecoin layer (Circle, Bridge, BVNK) is where the new value could accrue; Visa's role here is distribution + compliance, so margin capture depends on Visa staying the mandatory rail rather than clients routing USDC payouts around VisaNet (regulatory clarity cuts both ways — it lowers entry barriers for non-card rails too).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a modest **"pilot → production" credibility marker**, not a re-rating event: it signals Visa can translate its stablecoin experiments into live commercial flows and defends Visa Direct's prefunding franchise against Mastercard Move (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if by the next 1–2 quarters Visa adds multiple enablers beyond Inyo and begins disclosing stablecoin-attributable revenue (not just settlement run-rate), the strategy is real monetization; if it stays one enabler + volume-only metrics, it's defensive optionality. Trigger to watch: Money20/20 USA messaging next week + fiscal Q1 FY2026 call commentary on stablecoin economics.
