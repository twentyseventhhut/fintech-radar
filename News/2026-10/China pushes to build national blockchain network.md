China is pushing to build a national blockchain network.
The Chinese Communist Party Central Committee and the State Council recently outlined the plan in an opinion on developing “new quality productive forces,” according to the People’s Daily, the party’s official newspaper, on October 10.
The plan is part of a national economic development strategy aimed at promoting integration between the real economy and the digital economy. To that end, China plans to build a nationwide integrated computing network and a national blockchain network, while optimizing the deployment and operation of data infrastructure.
Beijing also plans to refine systems for confirming data ownership, market trading, and the allocation and protection of rights and interests. Specific details, including the construction timeline and operating model for the blockchain network, were not disclosed.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened (one-liner).** On 2026-10-09, China's CPC Central Committee and State Council issued "Opinions on Developing New Quality Productive Forces" — a 19-measure policy document that, among other things, directs the construction of a *national blockchain network* alongside a *nationwide integrated computing-power network*; the plan was reported by People's Daily on 2026-10-10. No launch date, budget, operating model, or access rules for the blockchain network were disclosed.

**[1] Why it matters.** This elevates distributed-ledger technology to the status of *sovereign national infrastructure* rather than a financial product. The blockchain network is explicitly positioned as the verification/trust layer for a regulated **data-element market** (data ownership confirmation, trading, rights allocation) and for digitizing manufacturing and the industrial internet. It pairs blockchain with compute as twin pillars of China's real-economy / digital-economy integration — a top-down, permissioned, token-less model that continues to exclude cryptocurrencies (Beijing's 2021 crypto trading/mining ban is left untouched). For fintech, the near-term relevance is data infrastructure and the e-CNY ecosystem, not open crypto rails.

**[2] Timeline / prior art (dated).**
- 2020 — China launches the **Blockchain-based Service Network (BSN)**, a state-backed, low-cost deployment platform; domestic version runs 120+ public city nodes, international **BSN Spartan** expands to 20+ countries (MiddEast/Africa/SE Asia).
- 2021 — Beijing bans crypto trading and mining (not reversed by the 2026 opinion).
- Jan 2024 — authorities issue a plan to accelerate the **National Integrated Computing Power Network** (2.0 of "East Data, West Computing").
- End-2025 — pilot monitoring/scheduling platform for the national computing-power network released, covering the "8 hubs, 10 clusters."
- Mar 2026 — **15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030)** outline adopted; it already names the blockchain network and prioritizes AI, quantum, integrated circuits and the computing-power network.
- 2026-10-09 — CPC Central Committee + State Council publish the "Opinions on Developing New Quality Productive Forces" (19 measures) ordering the national blockchain network + integrated computing-power network.
- 2026-10-10 — People's Daily reports the plan (source for this note).

**[3] Internal context (prior vault coverage).** This is China's blockchain/compute *infrastructure strategy*; adjacent vault threads on China's state-led digital-money and payments rails: [[PBOC opens digital yuan international operations centre in Shanghai]] (e-CNY internationalization), [[China adds 12 lenders to digital yuan network]] (e-CNY domestic expansion), [[China weighs yuan-backed stablecoins to boost currency]] and [[China's central bank warns stablecoins pose global risks]] (the token-less, control-first posture mirrored in this blockchain plan).

**[4] Key unknowns / what to watch.** No timeline, budget, governance, or operating model disclosed; unclear whether it builds on BSN or a new chain, how access is granted to agencies vs. businesses, and whether a public-sector data market materializes. Watch for implementing regulations, a named operator/consortium, pilot cities, and any linkage to e-CNY settlement.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is this a genuinely *new* commitment or mostly a restatement? The 15th Five-Year Plan (Mar 2026) already named the blockchain network — how much of the Oct 9 opinion is new direction vs. repackaging existing goals?
2. "National blockchain network" is undefined — does it mean a single sovereign chain, a federation built on BSN, or a loose interoperability standard? The source doesn't say; the term may be aspirational.
3. With no timeline, budget, or operating model disclosed, is there any concrete deliverable, or is this a signaling/guidance document that may never produce a shipped network?
4. Does this touch fintech at all, or is it primarily industrial-data / compute policy? The crypto/payments angle is largely inferred, not stated.
5. The crypto ban is explicitly untouched — does a token-less, permissioned "blockchain" deliver anything a conventional government database cannot, or is "blockchain" here a political label?
6. Who operates and governs the network (ministry, state SOE, telecoms, a consortium)? Unknown — control structure determines real impact.
7. How would private businesses and foreign firms actually access it? No access model disclosed; could be effectively closed.
8. Is the "data-element market" (data ownership, trading) realistic given prior Chinese data-exchange pilots (Shanghai/Shenzhen data exchanges) that have struggled with liquidity? Base rate of such markets delivering is low.
9. Could the Conflux/other "China blockchain" token pumps reported around this news distort perception of scope? Markets may be overreading a token-less directive.
10. Is the BSN "$54.5bn roadmap" and "16.7tn RMB e-CNY" framing from secondary sources reliable, or inflated aggregation? These figures came from one external summary, not the primary note.
11. Does the integrated computing-power network ("East Data, West Computing") actually depend on blockchain, or are the two bundled rhetorically in the same opinion?
12. What is the international/geopolitical read — is this primarily domestic industrial policy, or a bid for digital-infrastructure influence abroad (BSN Spartan)? Over-reading either way is a risk.
13. For a fintech digest, is the actionable signal here anything more than "watch for implementing rules"? Risk of overweighting a non-operational policy statement.

**Importance: 2/5** — A high-profile, top-level (CPC Central Committee + State Council) policy signal that formalizes blockchain as sovereign infrastructure and underpins a data-element market and the e-CNY/control-first stance — genuinely strategic for China digital-economy watchers. But it is a guidance document with no timeline, budget, operator, access model, or technical detail; the direct fintech/payments relevance is indirect and inferred, and it largely restates goals already in the 15th Five-Year Plan. Material for context, low near-term actionability.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is state digital-infrastructure / permissioned-blockchain policy, not a crypto or payments deal — no valuation, round or metrics attach. The directive sits in the CCP Central Committee + State Council "Opinions on Developing New Quality Productive Forces" (19 measures), published ~Oct 9–10 2026 per People's Daily, pairing a *national blockchain network* with a *nationwide integrated computing-power network* under real-economy ↔ digital-economy integration (per crowdfundinsider / cryptobriefing, Oct 2026). Market *size* is "no data" — Beijing disclosed no budget, timeline, operator or technical spec. Structure: this is a **state-controlled, consortium/permissioned** stack, not an open market — the "value chain" is data-ownership confirmation, data trading and data-as-factor-of-production pilots, with the state as architect and standard-setter. Barriers are therefore political/regulatory, not capital or network effects. Why now: it extends a 7-year arc (Xi's 2019 "seize blockchain" remarks → BSN 2020) and formalises blockchain as *critical national infrastructure* inside the 15th Five-Year-Plan window — a tech-sovereignty / data-governance push, explicitly token-free (no Bitcoin/crypto reference in the document, per cointribune/cryptobriefing).

**Competitive landscape.** The sector runs on adoption KPIs — number of deployed chains/nodes, enterprises onboarded, cross-border corridors live — not revenue multiples. Key "players" are all state-anchored: the pre-existing **BSN (Blockchain-based Service Network)**, launched 2020, led by the State Information Center with China Mobile + China UnionPay; ~120+ city nodes domestically, ~1,500 companies, and an international export arm **BSN Spartan** in 20+ countries (per Wikipedia/cointelegraph/dlnews). Adjacent state rails: the **digital yuan (e-CNY)** and the China-led **mBridge** CBDC platform (>$55bn cross-border, bypassing SWIFT). Basis of competition is *standards-setting and export of architecture* to aligned economies, not price/product. Protagonist (the "national blockchain network") position: **(analysis)** likely a re-badge/upgrade layered on BSN rather than greenfield — unconfirmed by Beijing. Moat = state mandate + control of access/standards (intangible/regulatory), the strongest and least contestable moat type, but only inside China's sphere.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/revenue → **no multiples computable; "no data"** on EV/Revenue etc. (not a traded or funded entity). Qualitative internal comps from the corpus (China state-digital-infra cluster), as precedent not valuation:

- [[China-led mBridge CBDC platform tops $55 billion in payments]] — state cross-border settlement rail bypassing SWIFT (2026-01).
- [[PBOC opens digital yuan international operations centre in Shanghai]] — e-CNY internationalisation hub (2025-10).

Distribution not computed; qualitative comparison only. All are state-led, permissioned/CBDC infrastructure — in-line with this pick's model (sovereign, token-free, export-oriented).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Execution / vaporware risk** — zero timeline, operator, budget or spec disclosed; "pushing to build" ≠ live. Announced-vs-adopted gap is wide; the measure may remain a planning directive for years (second-order: little near-term vendor/enterprise impact).
2. **Geopolitical fragmentation / splinternet** — an exportable, state-controlled stack (BSN Spartan, mBridge) deepens a parallel non-Western digital order with Chinese standards; raises sanctions/interop and foreign-adoption friction, and invites reciprocal restriction in Western markets (second-order: bifurcated global blockchain standards, reduced cross-bloc composability).
3. **Adoption / disintermediation of open crypto** — permissioned-only mandate structurally excludes public chains (BTC/ETH) and tokens inside China; domestic enterprise adoption is coerced-by-policy, not demand-pull, so "traction" metrics may overstate real utility (second-order: margin/value accrues to state operators, not independent infra vendors).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** **(analysis)** Reinforces the "two-track blockchain world" thesis: permissionless public chains for the West vs. sovereign permissioned rails for China + aligned economies — and China treating the *technology* as critical infra while banning the *assets*. Watch for the trigger: a named operator/timeline or a BSN tie-in — that would convert directive → buildable program; absence of any spec within ~12 months would confirm this is policy signalling, not a near-term infra cycle. Falsifiable: if a concrete budget/operator ships and foreign (non-aligned) adoption of BSN Spartan materially accelerates, the "China-sphere-only" ceiling thesis is wrong.
