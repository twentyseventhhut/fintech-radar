New AI agent brings personalized financial guidance, product discovery, and assistance resources into one conversational experience. 
 LAS VEGAS , Oct. 19, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- TomoIQ, the AI-native personal finance platform developed by TomoCredit, unveiled today Money Finder at Money20/20 USA. The new AI-powered experience is designed to help consumers identify ways to lower everyday expenses, explore financial products, and find resources relevant to their financial circumstances.
Money Finder brings these capabilities into one conversational experience, helping consumers navigate decisions that often require searching multiple websites, comparing complicated offers, and figuring out where to begin.

 
 
"The next generation of consumer AI won't just answer questions. It will help people take action," said Kristy Kim, founder and CEO of TomoCredit. "For a single mother trying to lower her bills, a gig worker looking for a credit card, or an immigrant navigating the U.S. financial system for the first time, the value of AI is practical: What can it help me do? That's the question we're building Money Finder to answer."
 From Financial Questions to Financial Opportunities 
Money Finder is designed to help consumers:
 Discover Credit Cards and Loans: Explore personalized financial product recommendations based on individual financial profiles and lender eligibility criteria. 
 Manage Debt and Financial Obligations: Get actionable, personalized plans to manage debt, prioritize payments, and improve financial health. 
 Find Potential Savings: Identify opportunities to lower recurring expenses, compare service providers, and discover more affordable alternatives. 
 Navigate Everyday Money Decisions: Get AI-powered guidance on everything from credit card payments to long-term financial goals through a conversational experience.
The experience responds to the demand TomoCredit has observed among its own users. In a recent survey where TomoCredit analyzed over 100,000 consumer conversations with TomoIQ, its personal finance AI agent, 83% of consumers used AI-powered help to qualify for better credit cards, cars, or homes – as opposed to simply using AI for budgeting help.
Money Finder is designed to turn that interest into a more practical starting point: helping users understand their options and identify potential next steps based on their circumstances.
 AI Built Around the Consumer 
Money Finder builds on TomoIQ's broader vision of making personalized financial guidance more accessible, including for people whose needs and circumstances are not fully captured by traditional credit scores.
TomoIQ Money Finder is available for current TomoIQ customers now. To learn more please visit: https://tomocredit.com/boost/ai  
 About TomoIQ 
TomoIQ is an AI-native personal finance platform designed to help everyday Americans navigate credit, debt, bills, and financial decisions. Developed by San Francisco-based fintech company TomoCredit, TomoIQ combines conversational AI with financial insights and product recommendations to make personalized financial guidance more accessible. ## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
### [0] What happened
On **2026-10-19**, at **Money20/20 USA** (Las Vegas, Oct 18–21), **TomoIQ** — the AI-native personal-finance platform built by San Francisco fintech **TomoCredit** (founder/CEO **Kristy Kim**) — unveiled **Money Finder**, a conversational AI agent for mainstream consumers. Money Finder pitches "agentic finance": discovering credit cards/loans on eligibility criteria, building personalized debt-management/payment-prioritization plans, finding savings on recurring bills, and general money guidance in one chat experience. TomoCredit cites an internal survey of 100,000+ TomoIQ conversations where **83%** of users sought AI help to *qualify* for better cards/cars/homes rather than just budgeting. **Availability: current TomoIQ customers only, now** — a limited rollout, not a general public launch.

### [1] Why it matters
Money Finder is the clearest sign yet of TomoCredit reinventing itself around **agentic commerce/finance**, the dominant 2026 Money20/20 theme (Technology as the Great Equalizer: autonomous agents, AI-predicted fraud, digital identity). It moves the company from a *product provider* (its old credit card / credit-boost model) to an *AI advice + product-discovery marketplace* — a lead-gen layer steering consumers to lenders. Economically that implies an affiliate/referral revenue model (card & loan recommendations) rather than the subscription credit-boost fees that drew regulatory and legal fire.

### [2] Context & history (the critical backstory)
TomoCredit carries heavy baggage that this launch is arguably designed to outrun:
- Its earlier **$100–$1,000/yr "TomoBoost" credit-boosting product** was reported (Forbes, Jeff Kauflin, Feb 2026 — see [[Tomocredit scrutinized over credit-boosting product that fails users]]) to **rarely improve scores** while charging high fees.
- In **2024 all three bureaus — Experian, Equifax, TransUnion — cut off TomoCredit's reporting access** (Equifax "deactivated" it); its tradelines have shown virtually no records since Aug 2024, making the boost claim mechanically impossible.
- **Geagley v. TomoCredit** (filed ~Jan 15, 2026) attacks the boost claims and cancellation practices; multiple lawsuits exist.
- In **Mar–Apr 2026** the company **rebranded from "boosting credit" to "financial coaching"** (see [[TomoCredit pivots from credit-boosting to financial coaching]]) and leaned into the TomoIQ AI agent (claimed ~400k members; 2026 Inc. 5000 #2,052).
Money Finder is the next step of that same pivot — away from a legally/operationally fragile boost product toward AI-driven product discovery.

### [3] Players & positioning
- **TomoCredit / TomoIQ / Kristy Kim** — the actor; Mastercard-backed historically.
- **Money20/20 USA 2026** cohort of agentic-finance launches it competes with for mindshare: [[World unveils World ID for banking and finance at Money20 20]], [[Proof and Circle demo AI agents paying in USDC at Money20 20]], [[Crossmint launches Agent Commerce Toolkit for AI payments]], [[Fragment raises $12M Series A to turn agents into financial operators]], plus [[Musk proposes shopping via credit card photos on Grok]].
- **Counter-trend / guardrails:** [[JPMorgan blocks outside AI agents from accessing accounts]] — incumbents are actively restricting third-party agent access, a structural headwind for consumer-facing finance agents.
- Adjacent bill-savings competitors: ScribeUp (recurring-bill management, also at M20/20). Money Finder's "savings" pillar overlaps directly.

### [4] What to watch (freshness & adoption)
- **FRESHNESS: FRESH.** Dated announcement 2026-10-19 via PRNewswire; retrieved 2026-10-20 (<48h). No prior same-event duplicate in the vault. Not stale.
- **Adoption is ANNOUNCED-plus-limited, not broad-live:** available only to existing TomoIQ customers "now." No pricing, no independent user numbers, no partner lenders disclosed.
- Watch for: whether Money Finder monetizes via lender referrals (affiliate conflicts of interest in "personalized recommendations"); whether regulators/plaintiffs read the "agentic finance" repositioning as the same boost model in new clothes; whether any bureau relationship is restored (none reported); real DAU/retention vs the self-reported survey stats.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. **Is this a product or a press release?** Money Finder is available only to *existing TomoIQ customers* "now" — no public GA, no pricing, no independent user count. How much is live vs. Money20/20 booth-ware?
2. **Is "agentic" real?** Does Money Finder actually *take actions* (apply, cancel, switch providers) or just *recommend* in chat? The PR says the next gen "will help people take action" — future tense. What does it do today?
3. **Conflict of interest:** If it recommends credit cards/loans "based on lender eligibility," is it paid affiliate/referral fees? Are "personalized recommendations" actually ad placements?
4. **Does the regulatory/legal baggage follow it?** TomoCredit faces Geagley v. TomoCredit (Jan 2026) and Forbes reporting that TomoBoost rarely improved scores. Is Money Finder a genuine pivot or a rebrand of the same business under a less-litigated label?
5. **The bureau problem:** All three bureaus cut TomoCredit off in 2024; tradelines show ~no records since Aug 2024. If it still can't report data, any "improve financial health" claim is suspect — does Money Finder quietly reintroduce the same unsupported premise?
6. **Self-reported stats:** The "83% want to qualify, not budget" figure comes from TomoCredit's own survey of its own users — selection-biased and unaudited. Credible signal or marketing?
7. **Defensibility:** A chat UI over card/loan recommendations is trivially cloneable by banks, aggregators, and GPT wrappers. What is the moat beyond the TomoCredit customer base?
8. **Incumbent lockout:** [[JPMorgan blocks outside AI agents from accessing accounts]] — if major banks block third-party agents from account data, how does Money Finder get the financial-profile data it needs to personalize?
9. **Is the user base real?** Claimed ~400k members predate this — how many are paying vs. legacy boost subscribers who may churn as the product shifts?
10. **Unit economics:** Lead-gen/affiliate revenue on cards is lumpy and CAC-sensitive. Does this model fund an AI agent at consumer scale, or is it a bridge after the boost-fee revenue became legally toxic?
11. **Data privacy:** A conversational agent ingesting a user's full financial picture — what's the data-use/retention policy? Any consent beyond the ToS?
12. **Differentiation at M20/20:** It launched into a crowded agentic-finance field (World, Circle/Proof, Crossmint, Fragment). Why does a consumer notice TomoIQ over a bank's own AI assistant?
13. **Trademark/ops fragility:** Prior reporting flagged a Mastercard trademark dispute and vendor relationships ending in early 2026. Is the underlying company stable enough to support a new flagship product?
14. **Verifiability:** Every substantive claim traces to TomoCredit's own PR; no independent reviews or hands-on tests exist yet. Treat all capability claims as unverified.

**Importance: 2/5** — Real, dated, on-theme (agentic finance is *the* Money20/20 2026 story), and the TomoCredit backstory makes it a notable "troubled-fintech reinvents via AI" case study. But it is a limited, existing-customers-only launch from a small, legally-encumbered company with no disclosed metrics, pricing, or independent validation — low systemic impact, mostly a signal of the agentic-finance pivot narrative rather than a market mover.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Subvertical: AI-native consumer PFM / "agentic finance" (product discovery + debt/bill optimization), a lead-gen-adjacent layer on credit cards and loans. Size (sourced): the AI-personal-finance market is small but fast — ~$1.1bn (2025) → ~$1.34bn (2026), ~22.1% CAGR (per Research and Markets, as of 2026); the broader agentic-AI-in-financial-services market ~$7.78bn (2026) at ~41% CAGR to ~$43.5bn by 2031 (per Mordor Intelligence, as of 2026). Structure: fragmented and crowded — dozens of budgeting/advance apps, low switching costs, and the real moat sits downstream in monetization (affiliate/lead-gen on card & loan recommendations), not in the chatbot UI. Why now: (1) agentic UX shift — assistants that "take action" (recommend + initiate) rather than answer, and (2) incumbents already moving autonomous: Cleo shipped Autopilot (Feb 2026) that auto-adjusts savings and executes plans. Second-order effect: value migrates from DIY budgeting toward the point of product selection, where consumer-finance CACs are highest — i.e. whoever owns the "which card/loan" moment captures the economics.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: paying subscribers & MAU, ARPU/subscription ARR, and — for a recommendation layer — affiliate take / revenue-per-approved-application (TomoIQ discloses none of these [UNSOURCED]). Key players: Cleo (AI money coach + advances), Dave (NASDAQ: DAVE, advances + neobank), Rocket Money (bill negotiation/subscriptions), Albert, Brigit, plus Credit Karma / NerdWallet on the recommendation/lead-gen side and general-purpose LLM assistants encroaching from above. Basis of competition: distribution and data, not model quality. Recent moves: Cleo Autopilot (Feb 2026); Cleo hit ~$150m ARR US / ~$400m annualized revenue (May 2026, per Sifted/Sacra). Protagonist's position: niche challenger, catching up (analysis). TomoCredit's own disclosed signal is demand-side — a survey of 100k+ TomoIQ conversations where 83% sought help qualifying for better cards/cars/homes vs budgeting (per PR, self-reported, un-audited). Moat: thin — intangibles/brand only; no evident network effect or switching cost, and Money Finder is gated to existing TomoIQ customers at launch (announced, not broadly live).

**Comps & multiples.** Protagonist is private with no disclosed financials, so no TomoIQ multiple can be computed (no data).
- TomoCredit (protagonist) — total raised ~$39m over 4 rounds, est. valuation ~$222m, Series B, SF (per PitchBook/Tracxn secondary; treat as round valuation, not market cap). No revenue disclosed → EV/Rev = no data.
- Dave (NASDAQ: DAVE) — public comp. Market cap ~$4.61bn (Oct 12, 2026), TTM revenue ~$644m → P/S ≈ $4.61bn / $0.644bn ≈ 7.2x; EV/Revenue ~7.1x current (per companiesmarketcap/Yahoo/Macrotrends). In-line with its own growth (2026 guide $710–720m; Q1'26 +47% YoY), so the high-single-digit multiple is growth-justified, not stretched.
- Cleo (private) — ~$400m annualized revenue (May 2026), last priced valuation ~$500m at the $80m Series C (Jun 2022, Sofina; per Sacra/PitchBook). On stale 2022 price vs current revenue that's ~1.3x — clearly a dated, non-comparable mark, not a live multiple (round valuation, pre-growth).
Distribution not computed (fewer than 3 clean, same-basis figures). Qualitative read: public agentic-PFM comps clear ~7x EV/Rev on 40%+ growth; a sub-scale private challenger like TomoCredit would face a far lower multiple absent disclosed revenue/traction.
Internal comps: [[TomoCredit pivots from credit-boosting to financial coaching]] (2026-04) · [[Tomocredit scrutinized over credit-boosting product that fails users]] (2026-02) · adjacent recommendation/PFM peers [[Frich acquired in undisclosed deal]], [[ScribeUp raises $13M Series A for recurring-bill management]].

**Risk flags.**
1. Regulatory / trust overhang — TomoCredit faces lawsuits and prior scrutiny over credit-boosting claims that failed users (internal comps above); an AI that now *recommends* credit products and "prioritizes payments" raises UDAAP and suitability exposure, and the affiliate incentive to push products conflicts with the "neutral guidance" framing. Second-order: a regulator or AG action would hit the exact lead-gen revenue the product depends on.
2. Monetization / disintermediation — the economics live in affiliate card/loan placements, a layer Credit Karma/NerdWallet already own at scale and general-purpose LLMs can commoditize; if the "which product" decision moves to ChatGPT-class assistants, TomoIQ's rail gets disintermediated.
3. Execution / scale gap — gated to existing customers, no disclosed MAU/ARR, ~$39m lifetime raised vs Cleo's ~$400m revenue and Dave's ~$4.6bn cap; this is an announcement, not demonstrated adoption, and TomoCredit has already pivoted its core story once (2026-04).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Agentic PFM is re-rating the category from "budgeting UI" to "point-of-decision lead-gen" — but Money Finder reads as a catch-up re-positioning of a troubled credit-boost brand, not a new entry that reshapes the sector (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if TomoIQ discloses paying-user or approved-application growth within ~2 quarters it has a real wedge; absent that, it stays a niche feature. Trigger to watch: broad (non-gated) GA + any disclosed affiliate/approval metrics, or conversely a regulatory action on the recommendation claims — the latter would invalidate the thesis.
