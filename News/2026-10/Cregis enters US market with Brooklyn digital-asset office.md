LAS VEGAS , Oct. 20, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Cregis , a digital asset payment and custody infrastructure for enterprise, has entered the U.S. market with the establishment of an office in Brooklyn, New York. The move extends the company's international operations as it builds relationships with U.S. financial institutions, payment services providers and broader fintech businesses.

 Founded in 2017 and headquartered in Hong Kong, Cregis provides enterprise infrastructure for digital asset wallets, payments, fund flows and asset governance. The company serves more than 4,000 businesses across over 50 countries and regions, with cumulative transaction volume exceeding $300 billion.
"The U.S. is an important step for Cregis, but entering a market is only the beginning," said Shawn Yan, Founder and CEO of Cregis. "Our experience in Asia-Pacific and the Middle East has shown us that businesses need infrastructure that fits their existing operations. They shouldn't have to build an entirely separate function to manage digital assets. We want to bring that experience to the U.S. and work with businesses as they develop their digital asset capabilities."
The expansion comes as stablecoins gain a larger role in financial services, though business adoption remains uneven. The Federal Reserve reported that the stablecoin market reached approximately $317 billion in capitalization in April 2026, up more than 50% from early 2025. Yet a September 2026 Cleveland Fed report found that only eight of 148 surveyed businesses in its district were using or planning to use stablecoins . Respondents cited limited demand and satisfaction with existing payment methods among the reasons for holding back. The findings suggest that market growth alone does not guarantee adoption. Businesses must also consider how digital assets fit into existing payment processes and operational controls.
Cregis' U.S. expansion builds on its experience across Asia-Pacific and other international markets. In 2024, the company established Dubai as its Middle East hub. Over the following two years, it developed a base of approximately 200 long-term enterprise clients with active deployments in the region. More than half came from foreign exchange and other traditional financial businesses, reflecting demand beyond crypto -native companies.
Cregis serves financial institutions, payment service providers, fintech companies, digital banks, foreign exchange brokers and Web3 businesses. Its platform brings together three core capabilities: Wallet -as-a-Service (WaaS) , fund flow orchestration and custody infrastructure. Its MPC -based wallet technology supports transaction authorization and access controls, while its Payment Engine enables digital asset collections, payouts and settlement. Governance features, including approval workflows and audit trails, help businesses maintain oversight of asset operations.
The company holds a Hong Kong Trust or Company Service Provider (TCSP) licence and is registered with the U.S. Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN). Its security framework is supported by SOC 2 Type I, SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 certifications.
The New York office establishes a local base for Cregis to engage with U.S. businesses and partners. The company also made its U.S. exhibition debut at Money20/20 USA 2026 in Las Vegas, held October 18–21.
"Building financial infrastructure takes more than getting the technology right," Yan said. "It has to work within the systems, controls and responsibilities that businesses already have. As digital assets become part of more financial operations, our focus is on making them easier to manage and integrate. That is a long-term effort, and one we intend to pursue market by market."
 About Cregis 
 Cregis is a digital asset infrastructure platform, providing technology for digital asset collections, payouts and fund operations. Its offerings include wallet infrastructure, fund flow orchestration and regulated custody capabilities. These solutions help businesses manage digital assets with greater security, efficiency and control. Founded in 2017, Cregis serves financial institutions, payment service providers (PSPs), foreign exchange (Forex) brokers, fintechs and Web3 businesses. The company operates across Asia, the Middle East and Latin America. Today, Cregis supports more than 4,000 businesses across over 50 countries.

### [0] What happened
On 2026-10-20, Cregis — a Hong Kong-headquartered (founded 2017) enterprise digital-asset payment and custody infrastructure provider — announced its formal entry into the U.S. market by opening an office in Brooklyn, NY (68 Jay Street, Brooklyn 11201). The move was timed to its U.S. exhibition debut at Money20/20 USA 2026 in Las Vegas (Oct 18–21). Cregis positions itself as Wallet-as-a-Service (WaaS) + fund-flow orchestration + MPC-based custody for financial institutions, PSPs, fintechs, digital banks, FX brokers and Web3 firms. Headline scale claims: 4,000+ business clients across 50+ countries, cumulative transaction volume >$300B, no funding rounds disclosed (bootstrapped per Tracxn). Compliance posture: Hong Kong TCSP licence, FinCEN registration (MSB), and SOC 2 Type I/II + ISO 27001.

### [1] Why it matters
- Timing is the story: the U.S. push lands as the stablecoin/digital-asset regulatory regime crystallizes. The GENIUS Act (signed July 2025) is the first federal payment-stablecoin statute; OCC targets final rules ~Nov 2026 with a fallback activation of Jan 18, 2027. The market-structure CLARITY Act passed the House (Jul 2025), advanced through Senate Banking (May 2026) but is NOT yet law — custody/market-structure rules remain unsettled. Cregis is entering during the window where regulatory clarity is improving but incomplete.
- The enterprise custody/MPC infra market is crowded and Western-incumbent-dominated: Fireblocks (2,000+ institutions, NYDFS-chartered Fireblocks Trust Co., MPC-CMP), BitGo (NYSE IPO Jan 2026 ~$2B valuation, BitGo Bank & Trust N.A. federal charter), Anchorage Digital (federal trust bank). Cregis is characterized in third-party comparisons as an Asia-strong player (alongside Cobo, Safeheron) — i.e., a challenger, not a leader, in the U.S.
- Demand thesis: Cregis claims >half of its ~200 Middle East clients (Dubai hub, est. 2024) came from FX/traditional finance, not crypto-natives — a "TradFi onboarding" angle it wants to replicate in the U.S.

### [2] Key numbers / facts
- 4,000+ clients, 50+ countries, >$300B cumulative volume, founded 2017, HQ Hong Kong.
- Global footprint: Hong Kong, Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, Dubai, São Paulo, + new Brooklyn office.
- Regulatory/security: HK TCSP licence; U.S. FinCEN registration; SOC 2 Type I & II; ISO 27001; MPC (GG18 protocol, M-of-N / 2-of-2 signing), HSM+TEE framework.
- Macro cited in PR: Fed reported stablecoin market ~$317B cap in Apr 2026 (+50%+ vs early 2025); Cleveland Fed (Sep 2026) found only 8 of 148 surveyed district businesses using/planning to use stablecoins — adoption still thin.
- Founder/CEO: Shawn Yan.

### Red-team / skeptic's gate

1. **Is this a real market entry or a PR event timed to Money20/20?** — Mostly PR. An office lease (Brooklyn) + a conference booth is a beachhead, not committed operations. The CEO explicitly frames it as "only the beginning." No customers, revenue or capital commitment announced. OPEN as to substance.

2. **Does Cregis have the U.S. licences to custody assets?** — No evidence it does. It holds FinCEN MSB registration (AML/KYC) only — NOT a NY BitLicense, NYDFS trust charter, or OCC federal trust status. Incumbents (Fireblocks Trust Co., BitGo Bank & Trust N.A., Anchorage Digital Bank) do. ANSWERED: U.S. custody positioning is legally thin vs peers.

3. **Why Brooklyn, not Manhattan?** — Likely cost + proximity to a Web3/fintech cluster; not disclosed. Low signal either way. OPEN but immaterial.

4. **Are the headline metrics (4,000 clients, $300B volume) verifiable?** — No. Self-reported vendor figures, repeated verbatim across PR reprints; unaudited. Treat as marketing. ANSWERED: unverified.

5. **Is Cregis a credible challenger to Fireblocks/BitGo in the U.S.?** — Third-party 2026 comparisons rank Cregis as Asia-strong (with Cobo, Safeheron), not a U.S. leader. Entering a market where NYDFS/OCC-chartered incumbents already dominate institutional trust. ANSWERED: challenger/outsider.

6. **Does the macro backdrop support the thesis?** — Mixed. Stablecoin cap ~$317B (Apr 2026, +50%), but the very PR cites a Cleveland Fed survey where only 8/148 businesses use/plan stablecoins. Cregis is selling into demonstrably thin real-world enterprise adoption. ANSWERED: tailwind is narrative-heavy.

7. **Does the regulatory window actually favor entry now?** — Partly. GENIUS Act is law (stablecoin issuance), but final OCC rules slip to ~Nov 2026 / fallback Jan 2027; CLARITY (custody/market structure) is NOT law. So the rules governing a custody provider are still unsettled — entering ahead of clarity carries compliance risk. ANSWERED/OPEN.

8. **Is the "TradFi onboarding" angle (half of ME clients from FX/trad finance) transferable to the U.S.?** — Unproven. U.S. FX/banking clients face stricter supervisory expectations and already have incumbent vendors. ANSWERED: unproven claim.

9. **Any funding to back a U.S. buildout?** — Tracxn shows no disclosed rounds (bootstrapped). A self-funded firm expanding into the most expensive, most regulated market raises a capital-intensity question. OPEN.

10. **Is this a duplicate of the April Mexico note?** — No. Different region (U.S. vs Mexico), different date (Oct vs Apr), different event (office opening vs LatAm demand expansion). FRESH, not a duplicate.

11. **Could this be a de-facto regulatory arbitrage play (HK entity, FinCEN-only, no U.S. trust)?** — Plausible concern: offering custody-adjacent infra to U.S. institutions without a U.S. qualified-custody charter. Will depend on whether it operates as pure tech/WaaS vendor vs taking custody. OPEN.

12. **Security track record?** — Vendor claims "no security incidents" over 9 years; SOC 2 II + ISO 27001 are real signals but self-attested scope matters. ANSWERED: moderate positive, unaudited independently here.

13. **Who actually competes for the TradFi-to-crypto-rails buyer in the U.S.?** — Fireblocks, BitGo, Anchorage, plus payment-rail players (Euronet+Fireblocks, Circle/Paxos stack). Crowded. ANSWERED.

14. **Does the news move anything today?** — No measurable financial impact; private company, no raise, no named U.S. deal. News value is directional (Asian infra provider going onshore), not transactional.

Importance: 2/5 — A real but low-materiality beachhead: a bootstrapped, Asia-centric MPC/custody infra provider plants a U.S. flag at a conference with no disclosed customers, capital, or U.S. custody licences, into a market dominated by chartered incumbents (Fireblocks/BitGo/Anchorage). Useful as a datapoint on Asian crypto-infra going onshore amid the GENIUS/CLARITY regulatory thaw, but substance is thin and claims are self-reported.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Cregis sits in enterprise digital-asset infrastructure — MPC-based Wallet-as-a-Service, custody and payment/fund-flow orchestration for institutions. Market sizing is vendor-report territory, so treat figures as indicative: the crypto-custody-provider market is put at ~$3.5–3.7bn in 2026 (per The Business Research Co. / Research and Markets, via searchandmarkets/thebusinessresearchcompany, as of 2026) — small and fragmented, with MPC now the default architecture for new institutional launches ("70%+ of new institutional products MPC-first", per fystack blog, 2026 — vendor claim, treat as directional). The "why now" is on-shoring of US crypto infra post-GENIUS Act (signed July 2025; agency rules due by 18 Jul 2026), which restricts stablecoin issuance/custody to regulated entities and gives enterprises regulatory cover to transact. The note's own de-PR'd data points cut against the hype: Fed stablecoin cap ~$317bn in Apr 2026 (+50% vs early 2025), but the Cleveland Fed (Sep 2026) found only 8 of 148 surveyed businesses using/planning stablecoins — i.e. demand, not rails, is the bottleneck. Value accrues to whoever owns the compliance + key-management layer; barriers are regulatory licensing and institutional trust, not technology.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: assets-on-platform / AuC, cumulative transaction volume, number of enterprise clients, net (not gross) revenue take. Key players — Fireblocks and BitGo (institutional incumbents), Anchorage Digital (OCC-chartered), plus Coinbase Custody, Fidelity Digital Assets, BNY; Cobo is the closest Asia-origin peer by model (unified MPC/custodial/smart-contract wallet infra). Basis of competition: regulatory licences, security attestations (SOC 2 / ISO 27001), and distribution into banks/PSPs rather than price. Recent peer moves: BitGo IPO'd on NYSE 22 Jan 2026 (~$2bn offer-cap valuation); Fireblocks has been rolling up adjacent tooling (Dynamic ~$90M in 2025 [[Fireblocks acquires wallet platform Dynamic for $90 million]]; TRES Finance [[Fireblocks to acquire Tres Finance in $130M deal]]) and signed Western Union's USDPT stablecoin [[Western Union launches USDPT stablecoin with Fireblocks]]. Cregis's position: niche late entrant in the US (analysis) — real traction (4,000+ businesses, 50+ countries, >$300bn cumulative volume, per PR) but concentrated in APAC/Middle East; ~200 long-term enterprise clients in the Dubai/ME base built over two years, >half from FX/traditional finance. Moat is thin vs US incumbents: it carries a HK TCSP licence + FinCEN registration (a low bar — registration ≠ a trust/bank charter) against BitGo's OCC national-trust charter and Anchorage's bank charter. Internal comps: [[Cregis expands digital-asset infrastructure in Mexico]] (same LatAm/emerging-market, custody-demand playbook, Apr 2026).

**Comps & multiples.** Best public anchor is BitGo (only pure-play listed custodian): ~$2.08bn valuation at IPO offer (first-day close implied ~$2.59bn); 2025 net revenues ~$140M over trailing 9 months, +65% YoY; AuC ~$104bn at Sep-2025 (per coindesk/forge/odyssey, 2026). Using the offer valuation on annualised net revenue (~$187M) → ~11x revenue — rich on an absolute basis but defensible for 60%+ net-revenue growth (growth-multiple correlation; (analysis)). NB: BitGo's "$16bn 2025 revenue" in filings is gross platform/transaction throughput, NOT net revenue — do not use it as a denominator. Fireblocks: last priced at ~$8bn (Series E, Jan 2022; no fresh round since, so this is a stale private mark, not current — `[UNSOURCED]` on a current multiple). Cobo: no verifiable recent round — "no data". Cregis is private with no disclosed valuation or net revenue → EV/Revenue not computable; its $300bn figure is cumulative volume (a flow, not a stock or revenue), so it is not comparable to BitGo's AuC. Distribution not computed — qualitative comparison only: Cregis is materially sub-scale vs the listed/charter-holding peers.

**Risk flags.**
1. Demand, not supply, is the constraint — the note's own Cleveland Fed data (8/148 businesses) says US enterprise stablecoin uptake is thin, so a Brooklyn office may be front-running a market that hasn't arrived; execution/timing risk.
2. Regulatory thinness vs peers — FinCEN registration + a HK TCSP licence is a weak credential against OCC-chartered BitGo and bank-chartered Anchorage; without a US trust/bank charter, large institutions may not entrust custody, capping the addressable segment.
3. Disintermediation / stack risk — incumbents (Fireblocks, BitGo) are already embedded with US banks/PSPs and buying adjacent tooling; a late APAC entrant risks being relegated to long-tail/FX-broker clients while regulated-custody economics are captured above it.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a new-entrant land-grab, not a re-rating — one more Asia-origin infra player chasing US on-shoring ahead of the GENIUS rulebook. Falsifiable thesis (analysis): if Cregis can't name a US-regulated custody partner or a trust/bank charter within ~12 months, it stays a sales outpost rather than a US custody business; watch for a US licensing announcement or a marquee US bank/PSP client as the trigger that would prove the thesis wrong.
