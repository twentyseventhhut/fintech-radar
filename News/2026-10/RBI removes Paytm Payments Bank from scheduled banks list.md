RBI removes Paytm Payments Bank from list of scheduled banks

In April this year, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) had cancelled the banking licence issued to PPBL for non-compliance with norms
Updated - October 08, 2026 07:56 pm IST - Mumbai 

 The Reserve Bank of India said the affairs of the bank were conducted in a manner detrimental to the interest of its depositors. File
 | Photo Credit: Reuters
 
The Reserve Bank has officially removed Paytm Payments Bank Ltd (PPBL) from the list of recognised scheduled banks following the cancellation of its banking licence.
In April this year, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) had cancelled the banking licence issued to PPBL for non-compliance with norms, saying affairs of the bank were conducted in a manner detrimental to the interest of its depositors.
Later, the Delhi High Court ordered that PPBL be wound up.
"Paytm Payments Bank Limited has been excluded from the Second Schedule to the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934...," the central bank said in a statement on Wednesday (October 7, 2026).
A notification in this regard was published on July 31 and published in the Gazette of India in September.
The Second Schedule of the RBI Act is the official list of recognised banks that meet the central bank's financial standards and get special operational privileges. 'Scheduled bank' means a bank included in the Second Schedule.
PPBL, an associate firm of Vijay Shekhar Sharma-promoted fintech firm Paytm, came under the regulatory scanner on multiple occasions earlier, including in March 2022 when the central bank barred it from onboarding new customers.
While cancelling the licence, the RBI had said the affairs of the bank were conducted in a manner detrimental to its own interests as well as its depositors.
Earlier, PPBL was directed to stop onboarding new customers with effect from March 11, 2022 amid "material supervisory concerns" observed in the bank.
The bank was also directed to appoint an IT audit firm to conduct a comprehensive system audit of its IT system.
Thereafter, on January 31, 2024 and February 16, 2024 , certain business restrictions were also imposed on the bank, including disallowing any further deposits/credits/ top-ups in existing customer accounts, prepaid instruments, and wallets.

# Context-enrichment: RBI removes Paytm Payments Bank from scheduled banks list
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 7 October 2026 the RBI announced that Paytm Payments Bank Ltd (PPBL) has been excluded from the Second Schedule to the RBI Act, 1934 — i.e. it is no longer a "scheduled bank". De-PR'd: this is a **housekeeping/administrative consequence**, not a new regulatory decision. The underlying action — cancellation of PPBL's banking licence — already happened on **24 April 2026** (see [[RBI cancels Paytm Payments Bank license over compliance]]). The delisting notification was actually **dated 31 July 2026** and **gazetted in September 2026**; the 7 October "statement" is merely the public surfacing of a done-deal paperwork step.
**Why structured this way / what it reveals:** a bank with a cancelled licence cannot remain on the roster of recognised scheduled banks, so removal from the Second Schedule is automatic clean-up. The news framing ("RBI removes…") makes a mechanical registry edit sound like a fresh enforcement blow. The genuinely weighty events (2024 deposit curbs → April 2026 licence cancellation → Delhi High Court winding-up order + liquidator) are all prior. No new depositor action, no new restriction, no new numbers.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Scheduled-bank status is procedural, so competitor dynamics are the same as at licence cancellation. Paytm's payments business runs on the **TPAP (third-party app provider) model** via sponsor banks (Axis, HDFC, SBI, Yes Bank); UPI rivals **PhonePe** and **Google Pay** gained share during Paytm's 2024 curbs. Peers that avoided payments-bank entanglement (PhonePe, Google Pay as pure PSP/TPAP apps) never carried this regulatory tail.
**Why / second-order:** the delisting underscores the structural lesson — the payments-bank licence was Paytm's liability, not its moat; being removed from the Second Schedule changes nothing competitively because the bank already stopped operating in 2024–April 2026.

## [2] Company history / fit
Trajectory: Mar 2022 — PPBL barred from onboarding new customers; **31 Jan / 16 Feb 2024** — RBI bars fresh deposits/top-ups (≈$2.1bn market-cap wiped in two days, UPI volumes fell from ~1.56bn to ~1.1bn by Apr 2024); **24 Apr 2026** — licence cancelled; thereafter **Delhi High Court** ordered winding-up with an official liquidator; **7 Oct 2026** — Second Schedule removal published. Separately One97/Paytm pivoted away from the bank: FY26 first full-year profit ([[Paytm posts first full-year profit in FY26]]), Q3 FY26 profit ([[Paytm reports Q3 FY2026 profit on 20% revenue growth]]), full online payment-aggregator authorisation ([[Paytm Payment Services gets full online payment authorisation from RBI]]).
**Why the company acts this way:** Paytm has spent 2024–2026 decoupling the listed entity (One97) from the dead bank — migrating UPI/merchant settlement to partner banks — so the delisting is operationally irrelevant to the group; "no impact on operations" (Paytm's own line in April) holds.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Novelty ≈ zero. There is no new capability, no new money, no new restriction. The only "new" fact vs the April note is the registry mechanics (gazette dates). Traction/impact on One97: negligible — the bank's economics were already written off; the group returned to profit through the TPAP/PSP model.
**Why value-add is (not) real:** the economic value was destroyed in 2024–April 2026; a Second Schedule edit cannot destroy or create value that is already gone. This is the final administrative tombstone, not a live event.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Next: liquidator completes the winding-up; depositors are repaid (RBI said PPBL had enough liquidity to cover its deposit liability). No expected market reaction to the delisting itself. Risks are backward-looking (depositor repayment logistics), not forward business risk for One97.
**Why the market shrugs:** the signal was fully priced at cancellation; a known-dead bank leaving a registry carries no new information.

## Freshness / duplicate verdict
**STALE — duplicate of [[RBI cancels Paytm Payments Bank license over compliance]].** The Second Schedule removal is the automatic administrative consequence of the April 2026 licence cancellation already covered in that note (same event chain, same cause, no new terms/numbers/period). Per the red-team gate (near-identical prior note + same underlying event re-reported), this is a re-report, not a distinct regulatory step.

## Red-team / challenge questions

1. **Is this a new regulatory action or a consequence of an old one?** Consequence. It follows automatically from the 24 Apr 2026 licence cancellation. The licence-less bank cannot stay on the Second Schedule.

2. **When was the delisting actually decided?** Notification dated 31 July 2026, gazetted September 2026; the 7 Oct 2026 "statement" only surfaces it publicly. (The Hindu, Business Standard.)

3. **Does the corpus already cover this event chain?** Yes — [[RBI cancels Paytm Payments Bank license over compliance]] (2026-04). Same cause, same bank, same winding-up. → stale/duplicate.

4. **What does "scheduled bank" delisting mean operationally?** Loss of recognised status under the RBI Act's Second Schedule (access to RBI facilities/privileges). For a bank already licence-cancelled and under winding-up, this is purely formal — no live operations to lose.

5. **Any new impact on One97/Paytm (listed entity)?** None. Paytm's payments run on TPAP/sponsor-bank model since 2024; group returned to profit in FY26. Paytm's own April line: "no impact on operations". Open: exact depositor-repayment status.

6. **Was the real damage already done and priced?** Yes — ~$2.1bn market-cap wiped in two days after the Jan 2024 curbs; UPI volumes fell ~1.56bn→~1.1bn by Apr 2024. The delisting carries no new information.

7. **Does the April licence note need this as a follow-up fact?** Marginally — the gazette dates are a minor factual add, but not enough to constitute a fresh story.

8. **Who is silent about what?** No party is hiding anything; it is administrative. No new depositor numbers disclosed in the Oct item.

9. **Is there a depositor-protection angle that's new?** No — RBI already stated at cancellation that PPBL had enough liquidity to repay deposits. Liquidator handles repayment.

10. **Could "delisting" be confused with a UPI/wallet shutdown for users?** Yes, risk of misreading. In reality PPBL wallets/deposits were frozen/migrated in 2024; no new consumer action on 7 Oct 2026.

11. **Is Paytm (One97) itself being wound up?** No — only the payments bank associate entity. Important distinction; the parent is operating and profitable.

12. **Any precedent for a payments bank being delisted this way?** PPBL is the standout enforcement case among India payments banks; the mechanical delisting is standard for any de-licensed scheduled bank.

13. **Does this change the central question?** No — the central question (can Paytm thrive without its payments bank?) was already answered by FY26 profitability; the delisting adds nothing.

14. **Freshness verdict:** STALE. Duplicate of [[RBI cancels Paytm Payments Bank license over compliance]]; re-report of the same event chain, not a distinct regulatory step.

15. **If kept, what minimal value?** Only the archival tombstone/gazette-date detail. Not publish-worthy as a standalone story.

**Importance: 2/5 — rationale:** Administrative, lagging consequence of the already-covered April 2026 licence cancellation; no new capability, money, restriction, or market impact. Mildly notable as the formal end-of-registry marker and for the clean 2024→2026 timeline, but a duplicate of a stronger prior note — hence stale.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** India UPI is the sector backdrop. By volume share (April–May 2026), PhonePe ~46.5–47%, Google Pay ~33%, Paytm ~7.9–8.1%; the top two handle ~79–80% of all UPI transactions (per NPCI-cited app-share trackers, via demandsage/startupfeed, as of May–Jul 2026). Structure: highly consolidated at the app layer (duopoly), but UPI itself is zero-MDR public rails run by NPCI — value accrues from adjacent monetisation (lending distribution, merchant devices/soundboxes, subscriptions), not the transaction fee. Entry barrier is regulatory + network effects, not capital. "Why now": the trigger here is narrow — RBI formally excluded Paytm Payments Bank Ltd (PPBL) from the Second Schedule of the RBI Act 1934 (gazette notified 31 Jul 2026, published Sep, publicised 7 Oct 2026), the final administrative step after the 24 Apr 2026 licence cancellation and the Delhi High Court winding-up order (per The Hindu / Free Press Journal, 7–8 Oct 2026). De-PR note: this is the burial of an already-dead entity, not a new sanction — PPBL stopped onboarding in Mar 2022 and had deposits/wallet top-ups frozen from Jan–Feb 2024.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: UPI GTV, merchant GMV, take-rate on financial-services distribution, subscription-merchant count. Players: PhonePe (IPO-track, targeting ~$9–10.5bn vs last round ~$12bn in 2023, per Reuters/Outlook 2026), Google Pay, Paytm. Basis of competition: distribution + merchant hardware + lending cross-sell (UPI payments themselves are unmonetised). Recent moves: PhonePe crossed 50mn merchants (May 2026) and is on an IPO path; NPCI's 30% per-app volume cap deadline sits at 31 Dec 2026 (twice extended) — neither leader is near compliance (PhonePe ~46%). Paytm's position: niche/distant No.3 in UPI volume but monetises better than share implies — Q4 FY26 consumer UPI GTV grew 46% YoY to ₹5.5 lakh Cr (~2.2x the 21% industry rate), merchant GMV +27% YoY to ₹6.5 lakh Cr (per Q4 FY26 release, via Outlook/Upstox). Moat `(analysis)`: merchant soundbox/device install base + lending distribution rails, not consumer-UPI share.

**Risk flags.**
1. Regulatory posture / precedent — RBI's willingness to cancel a licence and wind up a bank over compliance signals a hard line; any residual depositor/creditor or reputational tail from PPBL's winding-up could still touch brand Paytm even though the listed entity no longer owns the bank. Second-order: elevated compliance cost across Paytm's remaining regulated arms (Paytm Payment Services).

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Context-only on the PPBL news; latest earnings print = BEAT.** The RBI's removal of Paytm Payments Bank (PPBL) from the Second Schedule is a regulatory formality, not an earnings event: it follows the April 2026 licence cancellation and the Delhi HC winding-up order, and One97 had **already fully impaired its PPBL investment (₹227.1 Cr write-off) as of March 31, 2024** — PPBL is an associate, deconsolidated, so there is **no direct financial impact on One97** (company statement; Trak.in; BusinessToday). This note therefore reviews the live numbers of the parent (One97/Paytm) against which the overhang is now immaterial.

**Verdict (headline read).** BEAT · latest reported quarter Q1 FY2027 (ended Jun 2026): revenue ₹2,448 Cr (+28% YoY; vs public consensus ~₹2,375 Cr, i.e. ~+3% / +₹73 Cr beat) · PAT ₹220 Cr (+79% YoY) · EBITDA ₹203 Cr (+182% YoY, ~16–20% above Street) · driver = financial-services distribution +45% and operating leverage · guidance: not formally given, but bonus-share plan dropped to prioritise growth. (Released ~Jul 2026 — within the anti-staleness window of today 2026-10-09; Q2 FY2027/Sep-quarter not yet reported, due early Nov 2026.)

**Key figures (with growth).**
- Q1 FY2027 revenue ₹2,448 Cr, +28% YoY (from ₹1,918 Cr).
- PAT ₹220 Cr, +79% YoY (from ₹123 Cr); comparable PAT ex-PIDF ₹212 Cr, +207% YoY.
- EBITDA ₹203 Cr, +182% YoY (from ₹72 Cr); EBITDA margin ~8% (from ~4%).
- GMV growth accelerated to +31% YoY (from +27% in Q4 FY2026).
- Fourth consecutive quarterly profit.
- Full-year FY2026 anchor (year to Mar 2026): operating revenue ₹8,437 Cr (+22% YoY), EBITDA ₹502 Cr (₹2,008 Cr YoY swing), PAT ₹552 Cr (first full year of profitability); financial-services revenue ₹2,593 Cr (+52% YoY).

**By segment / driver (Q1 FY2027).**
- Payment Services ₹1,384 Cr, +33% YoY — merchant payment volumes + online Payment Aggregator licence.
- Financial Services Distribution ₹814 Cr, +45% YoY — merchant loans + personal loans; the fastest-growing, highest-incremental-margin line and the real earnings engine.
- Marketing Services ₹239 Cr, flat.
The mix shift toward financial-services distribution (now ~one-third of revenue) is what drives PAT growing ~3x faster than revenue — classic operating leverage, not a one-off.

**vs expectations / prior period.** Beat vs public consensus: revenue ₹2,448 Cr vs ~₹2,375 Cr (+~₹73 Cr / +~3%); EBITDA ₹203 Cr vs ~₹178 Cr (Citi: +16% over its estimate; Goldman: ~+20% over its estimate). Sequentially PAT +20% QoQ. YoY momentum is accelerating on both GMV (+31% vs +27%) and revenue growth. Public consensus figures labelled as public aggregator/brokerage estimates (as reported around the Jul 2026 print); not from a paid feed.

**Guidance / forward.** No formal numeric guidance. Management signalled priorities: bonus-share proposal **dropped** in favour of reinvesting in growth; ₹100 Cr into Paytm Money; sought reallocation of ₹1,686 Cr IPO proceeds with timeline extended to Mar 31, 2029. Brokerage reaction was uniformly positive on forward trajectory — Morgan Stanley raised PT sharply to ₹1,450 (from ₹1,175); Goldman Sachs and Citi raised targets; Bernstein retained Outperform ("non-linear profit growth"); CLSA PT ₹1,050. Management tone: confident on "AI-led operating leverage."

**Thesis-flags.**
1. *PPBL overhang now fully closed (regulatory, not financial).* Fact: RBI de-scheduling → why: completes the Apr-2026 licence cancellation → why it matters: removes residual headline/regulatory tail risk that had capped sentiment since 2024 → second-order: with the ₹227 Cr already impaired and PPBL deconsolidated, the news is non-event for earnings; the thesis now rests purely on the de-coupled payments + lending engine.
2. *Operating leverage is real and repeating.* PAT +79% on revenue +28%, EBITDA +182% — fourth straight profitable quarter and FY2026 first full-year PAT ₹552 Cr. De-PR check: the leverage is visible in actual GAAP PAT and EBITDA, not just "comparable/adjusted" spin.
3. *Lending is the swing factor (and the main remaining risk).* Financial-services distribution +45% YoY is the earnings driver; it is also where credit-cycle, partner-concentration and take-rate risk sit. Watch asset quality / disbursement mix in the upcoming Q2 FY2027 print.
4. *Capital-allocation discipline signalled.* Dropping the bonus issue and reallocating IPO proceeds to FY2029 indicates reinvestment over shareholder optics — consistent with a growth-profitability inflection, but extends the "show me" window on capital returns.
