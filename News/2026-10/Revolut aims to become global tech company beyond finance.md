**Sector & drivers.** Global neobanking is ~28% CAGR consumer segment (per openpr/industry trackers, via web, as of 2026 — directional, treat as secondary); the live story is the pivot from "digital bank" to horizontal tech/super-app. Revolut's own FY2025 (IR, PRIMARY): revenue **$6.0bn (£4.5bn, +50% YoY)**, profit before tax **$2.3bn (~38% PBT margin, +57% YoY)**, customer balances **£50.2bn ($67.5bn)**, **~68.3M retail + 767K business customers** (up from 52.5M retail at end-2024), eleven product lines each >~£100m revenue (Revolut FY2025 results release, 2026-04-29). Structure: still fragmented globally but consolidating around a handful of scaled super-apps (Revolut, Nubank) vs single-market players; barriers are regulatory (banking licences) + data/network scale, now explicitly a **data moat** — the news hook is 30–40m transactions/day feeding proprietary AI models. "Why now": at $6bn revenue and GAAP-profitable, Revolut has the balance sheet and data volume to fund non-finance bets (eSIM/telco, AI tooling, wealth) ahead of a mooted IPO; the super-app framing is how it justifies a growth multiple outside slowing core payments.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: customers/ARPU, revenue growth, PBT margin, products-per-user (attach). Players: Revolut (global super-app, 68M), **[[Revolut surpasses 70 million customers globally]]** (70M early-2026, target 100M by mid-2027), Nubank (LatAm scale, ~120M+ customers, public), Monzo (UK-centric, 15M), Chime (US, public). Basis of competition: product breadth + distribution + price, increasingly AI/data. Recent moves: Revolut **[[Revolut raises $3B at $75 billion valuation]]** (2025), secondary to **[[Revolut valuation rises to $75 billion in secondary share sale]]** (Jan 2026), diversification evidence in-base — crypto (**[[Revolut hires Coinbase risk chief Michael Schroeder for crypto push]]**), wealth (**[[Revolut gets UK approval to expand investment and wealth services]]**), AI M&A (**[[Revolut acquires AI travel startup Swifty]]**), parallel "tech company" framing in **[[Revolut plans broader technology product suite beyond finance]]**, and US ambition in **[[Revolut CEO targets JPMorgan and Amex in US push]]**. Position: **ahead** of pure-play peers on diversification and margin; moat = data scale + licence stack + intangibles (brand/engineering), network effects within the app `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Revenue multiples (growth-adjusted; neobanks EBITDA-positive here run high):
- **Revolut:** EV/Rev ≈ $75bn / $6.0bn = **12.5x**; P/PBT ≈ $75bn / $2.3bn = **~33x** (pre-tax, not clean P/E — tax not folded; treat as indicative). At the mooted $100–120bn secondary (**[[Revolut plans secondary share sale at $100-120 billion valuation]]**) that re-rates to **~17–20x revenue**.
- **Nubank (public):** mkt cap ~$65–75bn / FY2025 revenue $16.3bn = **~4–4.6x** P/S (multiples.vc cites ~3.1x on TTM ~$21bn) — much cheaper per dollar of revenue despite similar growth (Q3'25 +39%).
- **Monzo (private):** ~£4.5bn ($5.9bn) share-sale valuation / FY26 revenue £1.7bn = **~3.5x** P/S.
- **Chime (public):** **[[Chime shares surge 59% in IPO at $18.4B market cap]]** — $18.4bn / FY2024 revenue $1.7bn = **~11x** at debut (since de-rated).
Read: Revolut is **rich vs public neobanks** (12.5x vs Nubank's ~3–4.6x) — the gap is ~3x on revenue. Not an automatic red flag given +50% growth and 38% PBT margin, but the premium prices in the super-app/AI optionality, not just the bank. Private-round marks (Revolut, Monzo) are not market caps and lack the liquidity discount public names carry.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Execution sprawl / focus risk** — simultaneously pushing telco (eSIM), proprietary AI, wealth, crypto, US + ~20 new markets while the UK full banking licence stayed restricted (**[[Revolut's UK banking licence bid hits risk-control setback]]**). Each non-core vertical has its own regulator, unit economics and incumbent (Amex/JPMorgan in US); "global tech company" framing can mask margin dilution if adjacencies underperform the ~38% PBT core.
2. **Valuation vs profitability gap** — at 12.5x revenue (33x PBT) the stock is priced for the tech-company thesis, not a bank; any growth or margin slip, or a cheaper Nubank print, compresses the multiple hard into an IPO window.
3. **Regulatory + data/trust** — AI built on 30–40m daily transactions raises data-use/privacy scrutiny; Revolut already leads UK fraud complaints (**[[Revolut tops UK bank fraud complaints for third year]]**) and had a data hack (**[[Revolut to cover costs for 680 customers hit by data hack]]**). Monetizing customer transaction data as a "tech" product invites GDPR/consumer-duty risk and reputational blowback.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a re-rating narrative, not an operating event: Revolut is reframing itself from neobank to data/AI platform to defend a growth multiple (~3x the public-neobank revenue multiple) ahead of a US-primary IPO (**[[Revolut favors US primary listing for potential IPO]]**). Falsifiable thesis: if the "tech beyond finance" bets are real, non-banking revenue lines should become disclosed, material (>10–15% of revenue) within ~2 annual reports; trigger to watch = first AI/telco product P&L disclosure or an IPO prospectus segment breakout. What breaks it: adjacencies stay <5% of revenue and the super-app stays a marketing frame — then the public float gets marked against Nubank's ~3–4x, not a tech multiple.

> [!note] Earnings layer — not from this news (a CEO-ambition commentary, no reporting), but from Revolut's own FY2025 Annual Report (year ended 31 Dec 2025, published 2026-04-29). This scales the "become a global tech company beyond finance" ambition against the balance sheet that actually funds it.

**Verdict (headline read).** BEAT / record year (vs own prior year — Revolut is private, unlisted, so no public consensus; growth read is YoY only). Group revenue £4,515.8m (+46% YoY; $6.0bn), net profit £1,304.6m (+65% YoY; ~$2.3bn PBT), PBT margin 38% (up from 35%). Fifth consecutive year of net profitability. Key driver: broad-based acceleration — card payments + a near-£1bn interest book + fast-growing subscriptions — not one segment. The ambition is underwritten by real, growing GAAP profit, but the profit engine today is bank/consumer-fintech economics, not "tech products." Guidance: Revolut does not issue formal guidance (private company).

**Key figures (FY2025, consolidated, from the Annual Report; decimal point).**
- Revenue £4,515.8m (+46% YoY, from £3,090.0m). Headline $6.0bn vs $4.0bn.
- Fee income £3,429.2m (+55% YoY, from £2,216.8m) — 76% of total turnover (+4.2pp YoY).
- Interest income £974.2m (+23% YoY, from £790.2m) — ~21.6% of revenue.
- Gross profit £3,517.6m (+41% YoY); cost of sales £998.1m (incl. credit losses on lending £61.2m, +107% YoY — book is growing fast off a small base).
- Administrative expenses £1,804.4m (+29% YoY) — grew far slower than revenue → operating leverage is the margin story.
- Profit before tax £1,713.3m (+57% YoY, from £1,088.7m); tax £408.6m; **net profit £1,304.6m (+65% YoY, from £790.4m)**.
- Retail customers 68.3m (+30% YoY, from 52.5m); business customers 767K (+33% YoY). Total customer balances £50.2bn (+66% YoY, from £30.2bn; $67.5bn). Customer lending portfolio +120% YoY (to ~£2.2bn / ~$2.9bn).

**By segment / driver.** Revenue mix (% of total): Card payments 22.2% (largest), interest income 21.6%, subscriptions 15.7%, wealth 14.7%, FX 13.4%, other income ~2.5%. Management counts 11 product lines each >£100m revenue — this is the quantitative backbone of the "diversification" narrative. Revolut Business is now 16% of total income and is out-growing consumer. What actually drives profit: (a) card interchange + FX — classic payments economics, the single biggest block; (b) the interest line — Revolut now earns ~£974m of interest income, i.e. it monetizes the £50.2bn deposit float like a bank, and 90% of assets sit in cash/Treasuries (prudent, low-risk carry). The "AI models / tech products" in the news contribute no disclosed revenue — they are an R&D/ambition item, not yet a reported segment.

**vs expectations / prior period.** No public consensus exists (Revolut is unlisted; no Street estimates) — beat/miss is qualitative vs prior year only. On that basis: comfortable beat of its own trajectory — revenue +46% YoY actually re-accelerated vs 2024's print, net profit +65% YoY, margin expanded. Momentum is broad: balances +66%, customers +30%, lending +120%. The quality flag is that profit growth leans partly on net interest (deposit float × rates) — a line sensitive to the rate cycle, not a durable "tech" moat. No prior Revolut earnings note exists in the corpus to wikilink.

**Guidance / forward.** None given (private company, no formal guidance). Independent read (analysis): with 68.3m retail customers +30% YoY and 11 product lines >£100m, FY2026 revenue plausibly reaches ~$8–9bn if growth decelerates toward the high-30s% — but net interest income (now ~20% of revenue) is the swing factor if ECB/BoE rates fall. The "global tech company beyond finance" framing (proprietary AI on 30–40m daily transactions) is a multi-year optionality bet; it is not yet in the numbers and will dilute margins before it adds revenue.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. Profit is real and growing (net profit £1,304.6m, +65% YoY) — the ambition is funded from cash flow, not a story. Second-order: Revolut can self-finance a US/tech build-out without raising dilutive capital.
2. The engine is bank/payments economics, not tech. Fee income (76%) + interest (21.6%) = essentially all revenue; the news's "AI/tech products" contribute £0 disclosed. De-PR flag: CEO reframes a highly profitable neobank as a "tech company" — aspiration ahead of the P&L.
3. Rate-cycle exposure. Interest income +23% YoY on a £50.2bn float is a tailwind that reverses if rates cut; watch the fee/interest mix (fee-based share rising +4.2pp is the mitigant management emphasizes). Second-order: pivoting to data/AI revenue is partly a hedge to reduce rate sensitivity.
4. Credit is small but accelerating. Lending +120% YoY and credit losses on lending +107% YoY (£61.2m) — immaterial today, but the fastest-growing risk line to watch as Revolut leans into lending for diversification.
