The capability expands TrustLinq’s partnership with Ripple and is live today for all verified clients, with no integration required. Here is what launched, why it matters for RLUSD specifically, and how a payment works step by step.
What Just Launched
TrustLinq has expanded its partnership with Ripple by enabling third-party fiat payments funded from RLUSD, the US dollar stablecoin issued by Ripple under a New York Department of Financial Services trust charter. The expansion builds on the existing collaboration under which Ripple Payments powers TrustLinq’s global fiat settlement , and it slots RLUSD in alongside USDT (ERC-20 and TRC-20), USDC and EURC as a funding asset for every payment type the platform supports.
In practice: if you hold RLUSD on ERC-20, every bank account on earth just became payable from your wallet.
What Is RLUSD, in One Paragraph
RLUSD is Ripple’s US dollar stablecoin, issued under a New York Department of Financial Services trust charter and built for enterprise-grade payments and institutional use: fully backed, redeemable, and regulated at issuance. What it shared with every other stablecoin until now was the last-mile problem: holding regulated digital dollars is easy, paying a supplier’s bank account with them was not. This launch is that last mile.
The Gap This Closes
Stablecoin rails move roughly $35 trillion a year, yet analysis by McKinsey and Artemis Analytics found only about $390 billion of it, around one percent, reaches the real economy as actual payments: invoices, payroll, rent, remittances. The rest circulates between exchanges, protocols and wallets.
The missing piece was never demand. It was the route: to pay anyone, holders had to sell on an exchange, withdraw to a bank account in their own name, and transfer onward, surrendering custody, waiting days, and feeding their bank a growing log of crypto-origin inflows. Crypto-funded fiat settlement replaces that detour with one step, and RLUSD holders now have it: crypto in, fiat out .

Why This Fits RLUSD Especially Well
Every payment through TrustLinq is executed by a Swiss-regulated financial intermediary, supervised by SO-FIT, a FINMA-recognised self-regulatory organisation, which gives recipient banks a regulated Swiss counterparty on every transfer. Pair that with a stablecoin issued under a New York trust charter and the result is a payment that is regulated at both ends: regulated money in, regulated settlement out. For businesses running supplier payments or payroll from a stablecoin treasury, and for individuals paying rent or fees from on-chain savings, that end-to-end regulatory chain is precisely what makes transfers land cleanly.

# Context-enrichment: TrustLinq lets RLUSD holders pay any bank account
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
TrustLinq CH AG — a Swiss-incorporated payment-execution firm regulated under SO-FIT (a FINMA-recognised SRO), license no. 1531 — **added RLUSD as a funding asset** for its existing crypto-to-fiat payout product. From Oct 13, 2026 (Chainwire PR), a holder of RLUSD (on Ethereum ERC-20) can push an ordinary bank transfer to any third party's account in 190+ countries / 80+ currencies, over SEPA, Faster Payments, ACH, local corridors or SWIFT, while the RLUSD stays in the sender's self-custodial wallet (linked to a per-user smart-contract vault). Same/next-day settlement; recipient gets fiat, needs no crypto.

**What this actually is, de-PR'd:** this is NOT a new product or a new rail. It is RLUSD being **slotted in alongside USDT (ERC-20/TRC-20), USDC and EURC** as one more accepted stablecoin on a payout flow that already existed. The underlying settlement engine — **Ripple Payments** — was integrated by TrustLinq back on **May 4, 2026** (then: 170 countries, 80+ currencies; see external PR). So the "expanded partnership" headline is the activation of a ~5-month-old relationship with one more supported token plus a coverage bump (170→190 countries).
- **Why framed this way → what it reveals:** the PR anchors on RLUSD because the news value is the *Ripple/RLUSD brand*, not the mechanism (which already shipped in May). Ripple benefits from every "RLUSD can now pay X" headline as it pushes RLUSD distribution; TrustLinq benefits from associating with Ripple. The "$35T stablecoin volume, only ~1% reaches real economy" stat (McKinsey/Artemis) is real but is marketing scaffolding — it frames TrustLinq as solving "the last mile" when in fact the last-mile off-ramp is a crowded category (Bridge, BVNK, Mercuryo, et al.).
- **Live vs announced:** genuinely **live** ("live today for all verified clients, no integration, no waitlist"), not a pilot. But TrustLinq publishes **no volume, no client count, no GMV** — so adoption is unverifiable.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Direct stablecoin→bank off-ramp peers, with where TrustLinq sits:
- **Bridge (Stripe-owned, $1.1B acq. closed Feb 2025):** USDC payouts incl. ACH/wire/SEPA same-day; API-first, embedded by Shopify/MetaMask/Remitly ([[This Week in Fintech Bridge powers Shopify, MetaMask, Remitly]]). Vastly larger distribution.
- **BVNK (Mastercard, $1.8B acq. closed Aug 3, 2026; talks surfaced in [[Coinbase and Mastercard in talks to buy stablecoin firm BVNK]]):** SWIFT/SEPA Instant/Faster Payments same-day, EMI + EU VASP. Now Mastercard-backed.
- **Circle Payments Network** (USDC/EURC, bank-grade off-ramps via ClearBank etc. — [[ClearBank to join Circle Payments Network for USDC and EURC]]); **Mercuryo, MoonPay, Transak, Ramp** on the retail side.
- **TrustLinq's differentiators:** (a) **self-custodial** — funds never touch TrustLinq, which most API off-rampers (Bridge/BVNK) do not emphasise because they're B2B2C embedded; (b) **"regulated at both ends"** pitch — NYDFS-chartered RLUSD in, Swiss SO-FIT intermediary out.
- **Position: catching up / niche.** TrustLinq is a small Swiss intermediary; the big rails are owned by Stripe and Mastercard. **Why the landscape is this way (analysis):** off-ramp is a thin-margin, compliance-heavy, liquidity-dependent business → it consolidates into firms with distribution and a balance sheet (Stripe, Mastercard, Circle). A standalone self-custodial niche player competes on *regulatory posture and non-custody*, not on reach — a defensible wedge for treasury-policy-sensitive clients, but not a category winner.

## [2] Company history / fit
TrustLinq's visible trajectory is short and tightly Ripple-linked: **May 4, 2026** — integrated Ripple Payments into live infra (self-custodial crypto→fiat to third-party banks, 170 countries); **Oct 13, 2026** — expanded that partnership to accept RLUSD + bumped to 190 countries. It positions itself as "the first platform to enable direct fiat settlement from self-custodial wallets to third-party bank accounts."
- **Why it acts this way (analysis):** a small Swiss payments firm cannot build global payout corridors itself → it rents Ripple Payments' settlement network, then adds Ripple's own stablecoin on top. Each incremental "now supports X stablecoin" release is a low-cost PR beat that keeps TrustLinq in Ripple's slipstream and in crypto headlines. Structurally it is a **distribution partner of Ripple**, not an independent rail.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Really new?** Marginal. The capability (self-custodial stablecoin → any bank account via Ripple Payments) launched in May. New here = +RLUSD as funding asset, +20 countries. The genuinely interesting primitive — **pay from a self-custodial wallet without surrendering custody to an exchange/off-ramp** — is TrustLinq's real value-add, but it predates this announcement.
- **Traction:** **none disclosed.** No volume, no clients, no GMV. "Live for all verified clients" ≠ adoption. Treat as announcement-grade.
- **Where the margin sits (analysis):** TrustLinq earns an FX/payout spread on each transfer; Ripple captures settlement-network economics + RLUSD float/distribution. In this stack **Ripple captures the strategic value** (RLUSD circulation, network usage); TrustLinq is a thin front-end taking last-mile spread. Its moat is the SO-FIT license + self-custody UX, both replicable. **Why the value-add is limited:** if Bridge/BVNK add a non-custodial mode, or Circle's CPN widens, TrustLinq's differentiation compresses to "Swiss-regulated and non-custodial" — a feature, not a franchise.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **RLUSD context:** RLUSD is Ripple's growth vehicle — ~$2.49B market cap as of late-Sep 2026, +86% YTD (from ~$1.34B), record supply ~$2.52B, closing on PYUSD (~$2.74B) but still only #8–9, far behind USDT/USDC. Ethereum ~56% / XRPL ~44% of supply. Expect more "RLUSD now pays/settles X" partnership beats (cf. [[DXC integrates Ripple RLUSD into Banking Core Hub]], [[LMAX Group and Ripple partner on RLUSD institutional adoption]], [[Ripple and Unloq test RLUSD payments on Singapore's BLOOM]]) — all distribution plays to grow RLUSD circulation.
- **Regulatory backdrop:** NYDFS trust charter (issuance) + Swiss SO-FIT (settlement) is a clean story post-GENIUS Act ([[Connecting the Dots Stablecoin competition heats up post-GENIUS]]); fits the "regulated stablecoin rails" thesis.
- **Risks / what's silent:** (1) zero disclosed traction; (2) no fee data in the item (pricing is "on the pricing page"); (3) nothing on who bears fraud/compliance liability on bad recipient banks; (4) corridor FX spreads unstated; (5) competitive squeeze from Stripe/Mastercard-owned rails.
- **Why the market goes this way (analysis):** stablecoin off-ramp is commoditising fast; value concentrates in (a) the stablecoin issuer (float + distribution = Ripple) and (b) the owners of distribution (Stripe/Mastercard). Second-order: a flurry of RLUSD-partnership PRs makes RLUSD *look* like it's winning the real-economy race, but supply growth ≠ payment volume — the open question is whether any of these integrations move material GMV, which none disclose.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits in the stablecoin off-ramp / payout-rail subvertical of cross-border payments: converting regulated digital dollars into ordinary fiat bank transfers. Sizing: total stablecoin market cap ~$302.8bn as of 2026-09-10 (per stablecoin.com); of the ~$390bn of 2025 "real-economy" payment volume (ex-trading), B2B accounted for ~$226bn, +733% YoY (per Allium, via allium.so 2026). But penetration is tiny — stablecoins were only ~0.31% of the $44.3tn retail cross-border market in 2025 and <0.5% of global B2B flows (per Flagship Advisory / Allium 2026). The note's own claim ("~1% of $35tn reaches the real economy") is directionally consistent with this. Structure: fragmented and horizontalizing — the value chain splits into issuance (Tether/Circle/Ripple), wallets/custody, and the last-mile off-ramp, where most residual friction and cost now sits (settlement itself is ~0.1–0.5%/txn; the friction is at fiat on/off-ramps — per eco.com/Allium 2026). Barriers are regulatory (local licenses, banking relationships per corridor) more than technical. Why now: GENIUS Act + MiCA clarity (Ripple itself secured preliminary MiCA CASP 2026-06-23 and EU EMI in Luxembourg 2026-02-02, per `pipeline/ir/ir_latest.json`) is pulling institutional treasuries into regulated dollar stablecoins; RLUSD grew +86% YTD to ~$2.49bn by 2026-09-28 (3rd-fastest-growing stablecoin of 2026, per KuCoin/RWA.xyz).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: payout corridors/countries + currencies covered, off-ramp fee/spread, settlement speed, and funding-asset breadth. TrustLinq claims 190+ countries, 80+ currencies, same/next-day, and USDT/USDC/EURC/RLUSD support. This is a crowded lane:
- **Thunes** — Pay-to-Stablecoin-Wallets, 130+ countries, single API, USDC/USDT (launched 2025-10-27) [[Thunes launches Pay-to-Stablecoin-Wallets solution]]
- **Paysend + Nodu** — stablecoin→43 currencies / 62 countries off-ramp (2026-03) [[Paysend and Nodu enable stablecoin payouts to banks and wallets]]
- **Dfns Payouts** — provider-agnostic off-ramp control plane (2026-03) [[Dfns launches payouts engine for global digital asset distribution]]
- **BVNK** (acquired by Mastercard, closed 2026-08-03, up to $1.8bn, ~$30bn annual stablecoin volume) and **Bridge** (Stripe, $1.1bn) — the infra incumbents now inside card networks.
Basis of competition: corridor breadth + regulatory cover + funding-asset neutrality, not price (fees converge). TrustLinq's differentiator is genuinely narrow-but-real: self-custodial funding (RLUSD stays in the user's wallet until execution) combined with a Swiss SO-FIT/FINMA-recognised intermediary on the payout leg — "regulated in, regulated out." Position: niche challenger `(analysis)` — it is private, discloses no volume/TPV/take-rate, and competes against card-network-backed rails with vastly larger distribution. Moat is thin: the self-custody angle is a product feature, not a durable switching cost, and the underlying settlement is Ripple Payments (not proprietary).

**Comps & multiples.** TrustLinq is private with zero disclosed financials → company-level multiples = **no data / [UNSOURCED]**. Reference points for the off-ramp lane (round/deal valuations, NOT market caps):
- BVNK: $1.8bn acquisition (Mastercard, 2026-08), vs $750m Series B (Dec 2024) → ~2.4x re-rating in ~18mo; ~$30bn annual volume implies the deal priced at ~6% of annual stablecoin TPV = `$1.8bn / $30bn = 0.06x` price/volume `(analysis)`.
- Bridge: $1.1bn (Stripe, 2025-02).

- RLUSD vs rivals (asset scale, 2026): RLUSD ~$2.49bn (0.8% share) vs USDC ~$75bn (24%) and USDT ~$187bn (59%); total ~$303bn (per KuCoin/Spark 2026). RLUSD is a small-but-fast-growing asset, so a payout rail betting on RLUSD-specific flow is betting on continued share gains, not current scale. Distribution not computed (comps are deal valuations, not trading multiples) → qualitative only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Rails dependence / disintermediation.** The entire payout leg runs on Ripple Payments; TrustLinq owns the self-custody UX and the Swiss regulated wrapper but not the settlement economics. If Ripple (or Mastercard-BVNK/Stripe-Bridge) offers direct non-custodial off-ramp, the thin differentiator collapses — second-order: TrustLinq's margin is captured by the rail owner.
2. **RLUSD liquidity/adoption concentration.** RLUSD is ~0.8% of stablecoin supply and ERC-20-only for this flow (other chains must bridge first). Conversion depth at scale in 80+ currencies is unproven; a payout promise is only as good as the thinnest-corridor FX liquidity — "live for all verified clients" is announced, actual volume is undisclosed.
3. **Regulatory/counterparty at both ends.** The "regulated in, regulated out" pitch is also the dependency: relies on NYDFS (RLUSD issuance) AND SO-FIT/FINMA (TrustLinq) staying favorable, plus recipient-bank willingness to accept crypto-origin inflows — de-PR'd, the note is silent on fraud liability, de-banking risk, and per-corridor compliance gates.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is incremental, not a re-rating: it extends an existing Ripple-powered rail to one more funding asset (RLUSD) with a self-custody twist — a feature race in a lane where Mastercard and Stripe already own the infra via BVNK/Bridge. Falsifiable thesis: if self-custodial off-ramp is a real wedge, expect disclosed TPV growth or a Ripple/strategic investment within ~12mo; what breaks it — Ripple or a card network shipping native non-custodial payout, making TrustLinq's wrapper redundant. Watch: RLUSD share vs USDC/USDT and whether any volume numbers ever get published.
