## Paytm’s Q2 profit jumps to $24m as revenue grows

Share

Add TIA as a preferred source

Indian payments and financial services firm Paytm reported net profit of 2.3 billion rupees (US$24 million) for the quarter ended September 2026, up from 210 million rupees (US$2.21 million) a year earlier, as growth in its payments and financial services businesses boosted results.

Revenue rose to 26.5 billion rupees (US$278 million) from 20.6 billion rupees (US$216 million).

Revenue from financial services distribution reached 9.1 billion rupees (US$96 million), while net payment revenue was 6.4 billion rupees (US$67.2 million).

Paytm said it had started offering its in-house AI platform to enterprise merchants.

The results come months after the Reserve Bank of India cancelled the banking license of Paytm Payments Bank.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** One97 Communications (Paytm) reported Q2 FY27 (quarter ended September 2026) results showing profit after tax (PAT) of roughly ₹225 crore (~US$24M), a more than 10-fold / ~971% jump from ₹21 crore a year earlier. Operating revenue rose 28% YoY to ₹2,648 crore (~US$278M). EBITDA climbed ~90% YoY to a record ₹270 crore, pushing the EBITDA margin above 10% for the first time (double digits). Profit before exceptional items was ₹229 crore (+9%). This is Paytm's sixth consecutive profitable quarter. (Sources: https://www.tradingview.com/news/moodys:50b2c6c234163:0-paytm-q2-fy27-profit-rises-over-10-fold-to-rs-225-crore-revenue-grows-28-to-rs-2-648-crore/ ; https://www.kotakneo.com/news/stocks/paytm-q2-fy27-results-net-profit-rises-as-revenue-and-ebitda-grow/ ; https://www.businessworld.in/article/paytm-q2-profit-surges-tenfold-as-ebitda-hits-record-rs-270-cr-630590)

**[1] Why the profit swing is less dramatic than "10x" implies.** The optically huge YoY jump is mostly a base-effect artifact. The year-ago Q2 FY26 PAT of ₹21 crore was itself depressed ~97% by a one-off gaming-business impairment (down from ₹930 crore the prior year — see [[Paytm posts Q2 net profit after gaming impairment]]). Against a cleaner reference, this ₹225 crore is flat-to-in-line with the ₹225 crore Paytm already posted in Q3 FY26 ([[Paytm reports Q3 FY2026 profit on 20% revenue growth]]) and up from ₹183 crore in Q4 FY26 ([[Paytm posts first full-year profit in FY26]]). So the real story is steady, consolidating profitability — not a step-change. The journey: first-ever quarterly profit in Q1 FY26 ([[Paytm posts first quarterly profit on 28% revenue growth in Q1 FY26]]) → first full-year profit FY26 → now a sixth straight profitable quarter.

**[2] What actually drove the swing to (durable) profit.** Three levers, in order of contribution:
- Financial-services distribution: revenue +49% YoY to ₹912 crore, the fastest-growing line — merchant loan distribution, personal loans, Paytm Postpaid, plus better monetisation in equity broking/wealth. This is the margin-accretive, capital-light business Paytm has been pivoting toward.
- Payments: payment-services revenue +25% YoY to ₹1,434 crore; net payment revenue +21% (comparable) to ₹644 crore. Merchant GMV ₹7.1 lakh crore (+26%). Subscription-paying merchant devices (Soundbox etc.) reached 1.64 crore, ~27 lakh net adds YoY.
- Operating leverage / cost discipline: management credits "AI-assisted operating leverage," though it did not quantify AI-specific savings. Headcount strategy has been net-neutral-to-growing with selective cuts ([[Paytm plans 10% staff increase in AI pivot with some cuts]]). The record 10%+ EBITDA margin is the clearest evidence costs grew slower than revenue.

**[3] Backdrop / overhang.** Results land against a still-unresolved regulatory saga: RBI cancelled the Paytm Payments Bank licence ([[RBI cancels Paytm Payments Bank license over compliance]]) and has now removed PPBL from the scheduled-banks list ([[RBI removes Paytm Payments Bank from scheduled banks list]]). Paytm has re-anchored on the One97 payments-aggregator stack (it holds RBI online + offline + cross-border aggregation approvals — [[Paytm Payments Services secures RBI cross-border aggregation approval]]) and capitalised the arm with ₹2,250 crore ([[Paytm infuses Rs 2,250 crore into payments arm]]). Ownership has shifted domestic (majority Indian-owned, 50.3% — [[Paytm becomes majority Indian-owned as domestic stake hits 50.3%]]) after Ant Group's full exit. Parallel bets: Europe (Luxembourg licence/entity) and Indonesia Soundbox rollout.

**[4] So what / why it matters.** For the first time Paytm can argue profitability is structural, not a single good quarter: six straight profitable quarters, double-digit EBITDA margin, and growth concentrated in the high-margin financial-services distribution line rather than low-margin payments throughput. The key read-through for investors is mix quality (FS distribution +49%) and margin trajectory — not the headline 10x, which is a weak-base illusion. Watch whether the ₹225 crore PAT keeps compounding (vs. plateauing at the same level it hit in Q3 FY26) and whether loan-distribution growth survives any credit-cycle or RBI unsecured-lending tightening. Note: the article's AI-platform angle overlaps a separate Paytm note this month ([[Paytm extends its in-house AI platform to enterprise merchants]]).

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is "tenfold increase" real growth or a base-effect illusion? The ₹21 crore year-ago base was gutted by a one-off gaming impairment; vs. Q3 FY26 (₹225 cr) PAT is essentially flat. Which framing is honest?
2. Is ₹225 crore PAT actually flat sequentially/YoY-adjusted? It matches Q3 FY26 exactly and is only modestly above Q4 FY26's ₹183 cr — is profit plateauing rather than compounding?
3. PAT ₹225 cr vs profit-before-exceptional-items ₹229 cr (+9%) — the operating profit grew only 9%, not 971%. Is the "10x" headline masking single-digit core-profit growth?
4. How much of the margin gain is genuine AI/operating leverage vs. management narrative? Company did not quantify AI savings — is "AI-assisted operating leverage" verifiable or a buzzword?
5. FS distribution revenue +49% to ₹912 cr is the growth engine — how much is lending (Postpaid/personal/merchant loans)? What is Paytm's credit exposure/FLDG structure and NPA sensitivity if the unsecured-credit cycle turns or RBI tightens?
6. Net payment revenue +21% "on a comparable basis" — what does "comparable" adjust for, and what is the unadjusted/reported number? Is the comparability caveat hiding a weaker trend?
7. Merchant GMV ₹7.1 lakh cr (+26%) but net payment take-rate — is payments margin compressing as MDR-free UPI dominates volume?
8. Does the EBITDA figure include or exclude ESOP cost? Indian fintechs often quote "EBITDA before ESOP" — is the record ₹270 cr / 10% margin an adjusted number?
9. Were there exceptional items/other income inflating PAT this quarter (e.g., treasury/interest income on the cash pile, tax credits)? ₹229 cr pre-exceptional vs ₹225 cr post suggests a small exceptional hit — what was it?
10. The PPBL licence cancellation and removal from scheduled-banks list — what residual customer/float/escrow migration risk or write-offs remain, and are any contingent liabilities unprovisioned?
11. Soundbox/subscription merchants 1.64 crore (+27 lakh YoY) — is net-add growth decelerating vs prior quarters, and is churn disclosed?
12. Europe (Luxembourg) and Indonesia expansion — are these cash-burning ventures that will drag the newfound margin, and are their losses consolidated here?
13. Source quality: TechInAsia body (the note's primary source) cites YourStory and gives rounded USD figures; does it match the company's actual investor release / BSE filing? Any discrepancy in the ₹2,648 cr revenue vs ₹2,650 cr seen elsewhere?
14. Guidance: did management give forward PAT/margin guidance, or only trailing results? The note has none — is sustainability asserted or evidenced?
15. Stock/valuation reaction — did the market treat this as a beat or already-priced-in? "Sixth consecutive profitable quarter" framing suggests investors may want acceleration, not repetition.

**Importance: 3/5 — rationale.** A quarterly earnings print from a major, well-covered listed Indian fintech. Materially positive (record EBITDA margin, double-digit for first time, 6th straight profit) and the FS-distribution mix shift is a genuine structural signal worth tracking. But the headline "10x" is a weak-base optical effect, PAT is essentially flat vs Q3 FY26, and it is an incremental data point in an already-established turnaround narrative rather than a surprise or inflection. Routine-but-solid earnings: above a one-off product note, below a licence/M&A/regulatory shock.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** India's UPI rails cleared ~24.5bn transactions worth ~₹30 lakh crore in Aug 2026 and account for ~85% of domestic digital payments (per NPCI data, via cnbc.com/imarcgroup, as of Sep 2026). The structural pivot of the quarter: after six years of zero MDR, NPCI's 15 Sep 2026 circular introduces a 0.4% merchant discount rate on person-to-merchant UPI transactions above ₹2,000, deferred to after Diwali 2026; NPCI's own data says only ~4% of merchant transactions will attract the fee (the other ~96% fall below ₹2,000 or sit in the zero-MDR P2PM bucket). Structure: a consolidated top layer — the top 3 apps process >90% of UPI volume — sitting on open, regulated public rails, so the barrier is distribution/merchant network, not technology. Drivers + "why now": (1) MDR monetisation finally gives acquirers like Paytm a price lever on a previously free rail, even if thin; (2) UPI volume growth (~26% GMV growth for Paytm itself) plus financial-services distribution is where the real margin sits, not raw payments.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: GMV/TPV, net payment margin (take rate), merchant-subscription (Soundbox) count, and financial-services distribution revenue. Key players and basis of competition: PhonePe (~46–48% UPI volume share) and Google Pay (~37%) dominate consumer volume on distribution/network effects; Paytm sits far behind at ~6.6–7.8% UPI share but competes on merchant monetisation (Soundbox devices, acquiring, lending distribution) rather than raw consumer volume. Recent peer moves: PhonePe crossed 50M registered merchants (Apr 2026) and is pursuing an H1-2026 IPO; Razorpay bought a majority stake in UPI app POP (Jun 2025); MobiKwik secured an RBI offline payment-aggregator nod (May 2026) explicitly to chase MDR/device-rental monetisation. Protagonist position: niche-but-monetising challenger — losing the consumer-volume race but ahead on merchant economics, with Q2 FY27 merchant subscriptions at 1.64 crore and financial-services distribution revenue +49% YoY to ₹912 crore (per IR/earnings coverage). Moat: installed Soundbox base + merchant data feeding lending distribution = switching costs on the merchant side `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Peers:
- **Paytm / One97 (NSE:PAYTM):** market cap ~₹1.08 lakh crore (~$11bn) as of mid-Oct 2026 (per stockanalysis/companiesmarketcap). Annualising Q2 FY27 revenue ₹2,648cr × 4 = ₹10,592cr (~$1.25bn). P/S ≈ ₹1,08,000cr / ₹10,592cr ≈ **10.2x** (run-rate; TTM would be lower given sub-₹2,648cr prior quarters). EBITDA ₹270cr/qtr → ~₹1,080cr annualised, EV/EBITDA not computed cleanly (net-cash balance sheet; EV ≈ market cap less cash → [UNSOURCED] exact net cash). P/E meaningful only on a thin, newly-positive base (₹225cr/qtr) and is therefore not a reliable comp this quarter — "distribution not computed".
- **PhonePe (private):** targeted IPO valuation ~$9–10.5bn, some reports $12–15bn, vs a $14.5bn Oct-2025 private mark (per samco/bwdisrupt). Round/IPO valuation, not a market cap; revenue not cleanly comparable → P/S "no data".
- Internal comps: [[PhonePe crosses 50 million merchants across India]]; [[Razorpay buys majority stake in UPI app POP]]; [[MobiKwik gets RBI nod for offline payment aggregator licence]]; [[Paytm infuses Rs 2,250 crore into payments arm]].

Flag: at ~10x run-rate sales on a business only just reaching double-digit EBITDA margin (10%) and a single profitable quarter base, Paytm screens **rich on revenue but justified only if the profit ramp is durable** — the multiple is a bet on the margin/financial-services ramp continuing, not on current earnings. PhonePe's down-round from $14.5bn signals private fintech marks are resetting, a read-through risk for Paytm's own multiple.

**Risk flags.**
1. **MDR upside is narrow, not transformational** — only ~4% of merchant UPI transactions clear the ₹2,000 / P2PM thresholds, so the 0.4% fee re-rates a thin slice; over-reading it as a step-change in payments economics is the trap. Second-order: if NPCI later tightens exemptions or caps fees, the already-small lever shrinks.
2. **Consumer-volume disintermediation** — at ~6.6–7.8% UPI share vs PhonePe/GPay's ~85% combined, Paytm's value increasingly depends on merchant monetisation and lending distribution, not its own consumer app; it rents consumer mindshare from rivals' rails. If the merchant-subscription/Soundbox base stalls, the moat thins.
3. **Earnings quality / durability** — the 10-fold PAT jump is off a tiny ₹21cr base and partly operating leverage on a single profitable quarter; one quarter of double-digit EBITDA margin is not yet a trend, and a stretched ~10x revenue multiple leaves little room for a miss.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The quarter reframes Paytm from a loss-making volume chaser into a lean, merchant-monetisation + financial-services-distribution play; the sector's zero-MDR era ending is a small tailwind that disproportionately rewards merchant-side acquirers (Paytm, MobiKwik) over consumer-volume leaders (PhonePe, GPay) who monetise little of their share. Falsifiable thesis: Paytm's re-rating holds only if financial-services distribution (+49% YoY) keeps outgrowing payments and EBITDA margin stays double-digit for 2–3 more quarters. Trigger/catalyst to watch: PhonePe's IPO pricing (sets the sector comp) and the post-Diwali MDR rollout — if either disappoints, Paytm's ~10x revenue multiple is the first to compress.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (headline read).** BEAT on revenue, IN-LINE on underlying profit, with a flattering reported-PAT optics. Revenue ₹2,648 Cr (+28% YoY; vs public consensus ~₹2,600 Cr → beat by ~₹48 Cr / ~+1.8%). Reported PAT ₹225 Cr vs ₹21 Cr a year ago (the "tenfold"/~971% headline), but that 10x is a base-effect artifact: the Q2 FY26 base carried a ~₹190 Cr exceptional charge (real-money gaming). On a like-for-like, pre-exceptional basis profit was ₹229 Cr vs ~₹211 Cr (+9% YoY) — and that undershot the ~₹267 Cr net-profit consensus (~27% YoY implied). The genuine positive is EBITDA ₹270 Cr (+90% YoY) with the EBITDA margin hitting 10% — double digits for the first time. Guidance: none formally issued (Paytm does not give quantitative guidance); management framed it as the sixth consecutive profitable quarter with continued operating leverage.

**Key figures (with growth).**
- Operating revenue ₹2,648 Cr, +28% YoY (vs ₹2,061 Cr in Q2 FY26).
- Reported PAT ₹225 Cr vs ₹21 Cr YoY (headline ~10x / ~971%); profit before exceptional items ₹229 Cr, +9% YoY (vs ~₹211 Cr) — the cleaner read.
- EBITDA ₹270 Cr, +90% YoY (vs ₹143 Cr); EBITDA margin ~10%, first time double-digit.
- GMV (gross merchant payment value) ₹7.1 lakh crore, +26% YoY (vs ₹5.7 lakh crore).
- Merchants on subscription plans 1.64 crore, +27 lakh YoY (higher revenue per merchant via loan distribution).

**By segment / driver.**
- Financial services distribution ₹912 Cr, +49% YoY — the fastest-growing line and the main profit driver; led by merchant-loan distribution, personal loans, Paytm Postpaid, plus improved monetisation in equity broking/wealth (Margin Trade Funding, Paytm Gold).
- Payment services revenue ₹1,434 Cr, +25% YoY; net payment revenue ₹644 Cr, +21% YoY on a comparable basis.
- Marketing services ₹287 Cr, +26% YoY.
Mix is shifting toward higher-margin financial-services distribution, which is what pulled EBITDA (+90%) far ahead of revenue (+28%) — operating leverage is real, not a one-off.

**vs expectations / prior period.** vs public consensus (five-brokerage average, pre-print): revenue ~₹2,600 Cr (range ₹2,575 Cr YES Securities – ₹2,633 Cr Motilal Oswal) → actual ₹2,648 Cr is a small beat (~+1.8%). Net-profit consensus ~₹267 Cr → reported PAT ₹225 Cr and comparable ₹229 Cr both fell short of that bar, though Jefferies noted profit-before-tax exceeded its estimate by ~17% (the net line was dragged below consensus by tax/exceptional-item mechanics, not operating weakness). QoQ: revenue ₹2,648 Cr vs ₹2,448 Cr in Q1 FY27 (+~8%); comparable profit ₹229 Cr vs ₹220 Cr (+~4%); EBITDA ₹270 Cr vs ₹203 Cr (+~33% QoQ, margin 10% vs 8%). YoY trajectory remains a clean recovery from the gaming-impairment base — see [[Paytm posts Q2 net profit after gaming impairment]] (Q2 FY26 base), [[Paytm reports Q3 FY2026 profit on 20% revenue growth]], and [[Paytm posts first full-year profit in FY26]].

**Guidance / forward.** No quantitative guidance issued (consistent with prior quarters). Management tone confident: emphasized sixth straight profitable quarter, double-digit EBITDA margin, AI adoption (in-house AI platform now offered to enterprise merchants), and continued merchant-base/loan-distribution expansion. What they leaned on: revenue growth, EBITDA margin, financial-services distribution. What to watch for de-PR purposes: the reported "tenfold profit" headline is base-driven, not a step-change in earnings power; comparable profit grew only +9% YoY and missed the Street net-profit number — management foregrounds EBITDA and the 10x optics over the comparable PAT line. Potential forward tailwind flagged by brokerages: proposed UPI merchant discount rate (MDR), not yet in the numbers.

**Thesis-flags.**
1.
