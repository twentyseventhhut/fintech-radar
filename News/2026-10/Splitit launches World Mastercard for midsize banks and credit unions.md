The new offering brings built-in installments to qualifying everyday spending 
 ATLANTA , Oct. 13, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Splitit , a leading global installment payments platform, today announced the upcoming launch of the Splitit World Mastercard, a new white-label offering built for mid-sized banks, credit unions, and other financial institutions. The card is designed as a credit product that combines simplicity and spending discipline with the flexibility of installment plans, all in a single offering under a financial institution's own brand.
Everyday purchases on the Splitit World Mastercard can be paid in full within 30 days. Cardholders will set their preferred spending threshold in the app, and purchases above that amount can be automatically split into predictable monthly payments. For example, a consumer can set their spending so that a morning coffee wouldn't qualify, but a new laptop could automatically be split into monthly installments. Cardholders stay in control throughout the process, with the flexibility to switch a purchase back to pay-in-full or adjust the installment plan in the app before their statement is due.
Splitit powers the installment technology and provides an AI-enabled mobile experience that banks can embed in their own app. Financial institutions bring their customer relationships, trusted brands, and distribution. This allows banks to expand their lending and payment offerings within their existing customer experience, providing greater choice and flexibility without outsourcing relationships. This is particularly important for smaller and mid-sized banks competing for a greater share of their customers' everyday spending.
"The Splitit World Mastercard is a new type of credit card built for banks and credit unions that resembles the simplicity of debit for consumers and pairs it with the flexibility of installments," said Nandan Sheth, CEO of Splitit. "Banks already have the relationship, the brand and the primary account. What many of them lack is a way to offer installment plans at the point of purchase without having to build a lending business from scratch. Our white-label card gives them that, under their own name."
The card combines Splitit's installment capabilities with Mastercard's trusted global payments infrastructure, acceptance network and deep relationships across the financial institution community. To participate, issuers, merchants, and distribution channels follow network guidelines, responsible lending practices, and data usage practices to ensure safe, secure, and consistent experiences. Consumers enjoy the same peace of mind that they receive from any other Mastercard product, including tokenization , dispute resolution, Zero Liability Protection, and more.
The Splitit World Mastercard is expected to be available to financial institutions in early 2027.
 About Splitit Splitit is a global installment payments platform that lets merchants, banks and credit unions offer pay-over-time under their own brand. About 4,500 active merchants in 30 countries use Splitit's card-linked installments inside their own checkout, so shoppers can pay using the credit card they already have, with no redirect and no new loan application. For banks and credit unions, Splitit provides installments that integrate into the institution's digital experience, along with the Splitit World Mastercard, a white-label card that combines everyday spending with built-in installments. In both cases, the brand and the customer relationship stay with the merchant or the bank. Splitit installments are also available inside Samsung Wallet . Founded in 2012 and based in Atlanta, Splitit serves shoppers in more than 100 countries. Learn more at Splitit.com .

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What was announced (2026-10-13, Atlanta).** Splitit unveiled the **Splitit World Mastercard**, a **white-label credit card** for mid-sized banks, credit unions and other financial institutions. Everyday purchases are paid in full within 30 days (charge-card style); the cardholder sets a **dollar spending threshold in the bank's app**, and anything above it automatically splits into predictable monthly installments (e.g. a coffee stays pay-in-full, a laptop auto-splits). The customer can flip a purchase back to pay-in-full or adjust the plan in-app before the statement is due. Splitit supplies the installment technology plus an AI-enabled mobile experience that banks embed in their own app; the bank keeps its brand, customer relationship and primary account. Availability to financial institutions is slated for **early 2027**. Protections are standard Mastercard (tokenization, dispute resolution, Zero Liability).

**Why it matters / strategic read.** This is a notable **pivot from distribution to platform**. Splitit's legacy model is card-linked installments that let shoppers split on the credit card they *already have* at merchant checkout (~4,500 active merchants, 30 countries). The World Mastercard inverts that: instead of riding merchant checkout, Splitit becomes the **issuer-processor/program manager** behind a bank-branded card, going after the bank's share of everyday spend. Per CEO Nandan Sheth, banks "already have the relationship, the brand and the primary account" but lack a way to offer point-of-sale installments without building a lending business — the white-label card fills that gap. Reporting (PYMNTS, American Banker) notes banks/CUs can choose **whether they or Splitit supply the capital and carry the credit risk** — a key commercial variable the press release itself omits.

**Corporate context (important).** Splitit is mid-restructuring: it is **delisting from the ASX to go private**, backed by a **~$60M capital commitment (Motive Partners ~$50M)**, with Motive's money contingent on the delisting. The World Mastercard is the visible product expression of that private-market, platform-for-banks strategy ("aspirations of providing service beyond BNPL"). Treat the launch as a strategic bet by a company under investor/regulatory pressure, not a steady-state product rollout.

**Competitive set.** Splitit is entering a crowded "BNPL-for-banks / card-linked installments" lane:
- **Issuer-side installment incumbents:** Amex Plan It, Chase Pay Over Time, Citi Flex Pay (eMarketer's Datos ratings put these ahead of Klarna/Affirm) — i.e. big issuers already offer the core feature in-house.
- **White-label BNPL-for-FI challengers:** **equipifi** (self-styled leading white-label BNPL for banks/CUs; raised $34M Series B in 2026; partnered with **Velera** to reach credit unions), **Jifiti**.
- **Network programs:** Mastercard Installments / Visa Installments give issuers network-level BNPL rails directly.
Splitit's differentiator is the single-card UX (debit-like simplicity + threshold-triggered installments) and its own installment IP + AI app, co-branded with Mastercard's acceptance network.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. **Announcement vs product.** This is a *pre-launch* announcement — availability is "early 2027." Is there any signed bank/CU design partner or pilot named, or is this vaporware/marketing ahead of the ASX delisting raise?
2. **Who carries credit risk?** The press release is silent; PYMNTS/American Banker say the bank *or* Splitit can supply capital. Which is the default? If Splitit carries risk, how does a company going private on a $60M commitment fund a card balance-sheet?
3. **Charge card vs credit card confusion.** The release calls it a "credit product" but the mechanic (pay-in-full in 30 days unless split) is charge-card-like. What is the actual revolving/APR structure, and who sets pricing — Splitit or the issuer?
4. **Economics for a mid-sized bank.** What does the bank actually earn vs pay (interchange split, Splitit SaaS/processing fee, Mastercard network fees)? Is the unit economics attractive enough for a small CU to displace its existing card program?
5. **Cannibalization of the legacy model.** Does an issuer-branded card compete with Splitit's own merchant card-linked installment business (and its 4,500 merchants)? Does becoming a card issuer-processor antagonize the banks that are its distribution?
6. **Regulatory exposure.** A bank-branded installment credit card pulls Splitit squarely into card/consumer-lending regulation (CFPB, Reg Z, fair-lending). Does "responsible lending practices" language cover underwriting obligations, and whose license issues the card?
7. **Competitive moat.** Amex/Chase/Citi already run installment-on-card in-house; equipifi (just raised $34M, Velera CU channel) and Mastercard/Visa Installments target the exact same FI buyers. What stops a mid-sized bank from using a cheaper network-native option?
8. **Threshold-trigger UX risk.** Auto-splitting purchases above a user-set threshold could surprise consumers into debt; how is this disclosed, and does it invite the same "debt-trap" criticism leveled at BNPL?
9. **Going-private overhang.** With Motive's $50M contingent on ASX delisting, is the World Mastercard a genuine roadmap item or primarily a narrative to support the restructuring/valuation?
10. **Distribution reality.** Mid-sized banks and CUs are notoriously slow buyers (core-system integrations, vendor risk reviews). Is "early 2027" realistic for a first live program, and what's the sales motion?
11. **Mastercard's role/exclusivity.** Is this exclusive to Mastercard, and is Mastercard a commercial partner (co-marketing/BIN sponsorship) or just the network? Could Mastercall Installments compete with Splitit's own offer?
12. **Capital/funding mechanics.** If banks fund the receivables, Splitit's revenue is fee-based (lighter but lower-margin); if Splitit funds, it needs warehouse lines. Which model is being sold, and is the $60M adequate?
13. **Evidence quality.** Nearly all coverage traces to the single PRNewswire release + a few analyst takes. Is there independent confirmation (a named launch partner, regulatory filing) beyond Splitit's own messaging?

**Importance: 3/5** — A credible, strategically coherent pivot by a known card-linked-installments player (Splitit) co-branded with Mastercard, targeting the active "BNPL-for-banks/CUs" theme where equipifi, Velera and the networks are all moving. But it is a forward-dated (early-2027) *announcement* with no named launch partner, undisclosed credit-risk/economics, and sits against the overhang of Splitit's ASX delisting and private-market restructuring — so near-term market impact is limited and execution risk is high. Solidly newsworthy for a fintech digest, not a headline-defining event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Subvertical: installment-on-card / BNPL embedded into bank & credit-union card programs (card-linked installments, not a separate loan rail). Global BNPL GMV was ~$560bn in 2025, +13.7% YoY (per thepaypers/Chargeflow data roundup, as of 2025); provider revenue ~$23.4bn in 2025 vs $19.2bn in 2024. Structure: two-tier — a few scaled BNPL balance-sheet lenders (Affirm, Klarna, Sezzle, Zip) sit at the top, while infrastructure/white-label players (Splitit) sell the installment engine to others. Entry barriers are capital (who funds the receivable) + network acceptance + bank distribution. **Why now:** mid-sized banks and credit unions are losing everyday-spend share to standalone BNPL apps and want a defensive, own-brand installment product without building a lending stack — exactly the gap Splitit's white-label card targets. Card runs on Mastercard rails: Mastercard's Value-added Services and Solutions net revenue was **$3,450m in Q1 2026 vs $2,818m in Q1 2025, +22.5%** (per Mastercard 10-Q, quarter ended 2026-03-31) — the network's fastest-growing layer, into which issuer-enablement products like this card feed; Mastercard does not break out installments separately `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: merchant sales volume / GMV, active shoppers, active merchants, take rate, and (for lenders) credit losses. Key players & basis of competition: Affirm/Klarna/Sezzle compete on consumer brand + underwriting + merchant distribution and carry the credit risk; Splitit competes on *not* being the brand — white-label, card-linked, no new loan application, bank keeps the customer. Recent peer scale (as of their FY/Q 2026 reporting): Affirm GMV ~$50.2bn FY-ended 2026-06-30 (+37%), revenue $4.26bn; Klarna Q1 2026 revenue $1.0bn (+44%) on GMV $33.7bn; Sezzle Q2 2026 GMV +38% YoY. Protagonist position: **niche infrastructure, not a scaled lender.** Splitit base (per its own PR, 2026-10-13): ~4,500 active merchants in 30 countries, shoppers in 100+ countries; ASX-era quarter (Sep, pre-2023 delisting) was only ~$70.9m merchant sales / 186k shoppers, i.e. two orders of magnitude below Affirm/Klarna GMV `(analysis)`. Moat = card-linked integration + now a Mastercard-branded issuing program (switching-cost/distribution moat via banks), not scale or balance sheet `(analysis)`. Internal comps / precedent: [[Splitit launches Splitit Go for card-linked installments in field sales]], [[Splitit partners with Antom on card-linked installments]], [[Samsung Wallet adds installment payments via Splitit]], [[Klarna launches debit card and digital wallet in UK]], [[Affirm expands Worldpay partnership with embedded payments]].

**Comps & multiples.** Splitit is **private** (delisted from ASX in 2023 when Motive Partners took a controlling stake via a $50m raise at US$0.20/share; total funding ~$350m; redomiciled to Cayman) — so no public market cap / trading multiple; last disclosed revenue ~$10.9m (2024) vs $6.3m (2023) per getLatka/third-party, treat as indicative not audited `[UNSOURCED]`. Listed peers for reference only (not comparable in scale/model — Splitit is an enabler, these are balance-sheet lenders): Affirm revenue $4.26bn FY2026; Klarna run-rate ~$4bn (annualized Q1 2026 $1.0bn). EV/Revenue arithmetic not computed: Splitit EV is private → no data; computing a multiple off peers would mis-size a sub-$15m-revenue enabler against multi-billion lenders — distribution not computed, qualitative only. In-line/rich/cheap: **no data** (no public EV). Not comparable to Mastercard ($3.45bn VAS revenue/quarter is the partner, not a comp).

**Risk flags.**
1. *Vaporware / timing risk:* card is "expected early 2027," no issuer signed, no economics disclosed — "announced" not "live." Second-order: a 12+ month gap lets incumbents (Visa Flexible Credential, Affirm card, Klarna card) lock in the same mid-bank/CU channel first.
2. *Who carries the credit risk / capital:* PR leaves open whether the bank or Splitit funds the receivable. If Splitit must warehouse it, a sub-$15m-revenue private firm takes on balance-sheet/funding-cost risk in a rising-rate print; if the bank funds it, Splitit is a thin tech-fee layer with weak unit economics `(analysis)`.
3. *Disintermediation / dependence on others' rails:* value sits on Mastercard's network + the bank's balance sheet + the bank's app — Splitit owns neither the customer nor the rails. Margin can be captured by Mastercard (interchange/VAS) or re-insourced by banks once the pattern is proven.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a distribution pivot: Splitit moving from merchant-checkout plumbing to *issuer* enablement, reselling a Mastercard-branded installment card to the long tail of mid-banks/CUs that can't build BNPL in-house — a new-entry wedge, not consolidation. Falsifiable thesis: if ≥1 named issuer goes live in 2027 with disclosed program economics, Splitit has found a durable B2B2C channel; **trigger/what breaks it:** no signed issuer or a "pilot only" update by mid-2027, or terms showing Splitit carries the receivable at thin spread → it stays a sub-scale enabler and the card is a marketing event.

The only recent public signal is Splitit's No. 1,614 ranking on the 2026 Inc. 5000 (fastest-growing US private companies, Aug 2026), which implies continued multi-year growth but gives no exact 2025/2026 MSV or revenue. The launch PR cites ~4,500 active merchants in 30 countries (operating scale, not a financial result). Treat all Splitit financials beyond FY2024 as no data.

**Mastercard (latest period, installments/new-flows context).** `ir_latest.json[mastercard]` has the latest 10-Q as Q1 2026 (period ended 2026-03-31, filed 2026-04-30) plus a 2026-02-11 FY2025 10-K; however Mastercard has since reported Q2 2026 (period ended 2026-06-30, 8-K/10-Q filed ~2026-07): net revenue $9.3bn (+14% reported / +12% currency-neutral YoY), with value-added services & solutions +20% (+18% cc) — i.e. new-flows/VAS continuing to outgrow the core network (+10% / +8% cc). Card-linked installments like Splitit's extend Mastercard's installment/new-flows footprint via a partner issuer, but are not separately quantified in Mastercard's disclosures and are immaterial to its ~$9bn/quarter revenue.
