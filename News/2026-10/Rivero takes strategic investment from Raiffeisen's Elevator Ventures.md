# Rivero takes strategic investment from Raiffeisen's Elevator Ventures

> [!info] 2026-10-22 · 1 упоминаний · 1 источника(ов) с текстом

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[This Week in Fintech] Zurich payment-operations firm Rivero, took a strategic investment of undisclosed size from Elevator Ventures, the venture arm of Raiffeisen Bank International, to expand in Central and Eastern Europe.

We're excited to welcome Elevator Ventures, the corporate venture capital fund of Raiffeisen Bank International (RBI), as a strategic investor in Rivero. For us, this is about more than capital. Elevator Ventures brings a network across European banking, and particularly across Central and Eastern Europe, that will help us bring our payment operations technology to many more banks and financial institutions.
Rivero was built to bring intelligence and automation to some of the most complex operational challenges in payments. As we grow across Europe, building great technology is only part of the job. We also need to earn our place in the wider European banking ecosystem, and that means working with partners who understand how banks buy, operate and stay compliant. Elevator Ventures does exactly that. Through its relationship with RBI Group and its network across Central and Eastern Europe, the team has built deep experience supporting technology companies that serve financial institutions and helping them navigate and expand within European banking markets. Elevator Ventures manages funds of more than €100 million, invests in Series A rounds across DACH and CEE, and has backed 23 companies to date.

Thomas Müller, CEO at Rivero, said: 
 "Europe's financial institutions are looking for better ways to manage increasingly complex payment operations, and we see significant opportunity to bring Rivero's technology to many more institutions across the continent. As we enter this next stage of growth, we want investors around the table who understand both the complexity of banking and the markets in which we operate. Elevator Ventures' financial services expertise and particularly strong network across Central and Eastern Europe make them a highly relevant strategic partner for Rivero."
 
The investment builds on our growth strategy following our Series A financing. We'll keep investing in our two products and deepening our relationships with financial institutions in existing and new European markets.
Kajo simplifies payment network compliance. It automatically distributes scheme updates, gives teams at-a-glance oversight and turns fragmented payment network documents and requirements into an AI-powered knowledge directory.
Amiko brings agentic AI to dispute management and fraud recovery. It triages cases, coordinates the next best actions and automates dispute workflows from intake to resolution, while guiding agents through the complex cases that need a human specialist. Institutions recover funds faster, cut operational effort and give cardholders a better experience.
Maximilian Schausberger, Managing Director at Elevator Ventures, said:
 "Rivero addresses a set of operational challenges that are becoming increasingly important for banks and payment institutions across Europe. The team combines deep payments expertise with technology designed specifically for the requirements of regulated financial institutions. We see a strong fit between Rivero's ambitions and our experience and network across European banking, and we look forward to supporting the company in its continued expansion." 
Our ambition is to become a leading European technology platform for payment operations, helping financial institutions manage complex regulatory and operational processes with greater efficiency and scale. Having Elevator Ventures alongside us, with its reach into Central and Eastern European banking, brings that a big step closer.
If you're a bank, issuer or payment institution in the region and want to see how Kajo or Amiko could work for your team, get in touch at hello@rivero.tech.
Rivero is a European payment operations technology company helping banks and financial institutions simplify complex payment processes. Its product suite includes Kajo, for payment network compliance, and Amiko, for dispute management and fraud recovery. Headquartered in Zurich, Rivero works with banks and financial institutions to replace complex, manual payment operations with modern, scalable technology.
For more information visit, https://rivero.tech/ 
Elevator Ventures is the venture capital fund backed by Raiffeisen Bank International, Raiffeisen-Holding Niederösterreich-Wien and Raiffeisen-Landesbank Steiermark. It manages funds of more than €100 million to support the growth of technology companies in fintech and beyond banking, investing in Series A rounds in DACH and CEE. To date it has deployed over €60 million across 23 companies, with seven exits, and into two additional funds.

## [0] What happened

Rivero — a Zurich/Basel-based (HQ Zurich) European payment-operations software company founded in 2019 — announced (Oct 2026) a **strategic investment from Elevator Ventures**, the corporate venture capital fund of **Raiffeisen Bank International (RBI)**. The amount was **undisclosed**. Rivero and multiple outlets frame it as **building on Rivero's earlier Series A** (some trade press loosely call it "Series A backing," but Rivero's own blog explicitly says the deal "builds on our growth strategy following our Series A financing," i.e. a strategic follow-on, not a fresh priced Series A). The explicit rationale: use Elevator Ventures' RBI-linked network across **Central and Eastern Europe (CEE)** and DACH to sell Rivero's two products — **Kajo** (payment-network/scheme-compliance automation) and **Amiko** (agentic-AI dispute management + fraud recovery) — to more banks and payment institutions.

Sources: [rivero.tech blog](https://rivero.tech/blog/elevator-ventures-strategic-investor-rivero-cee) · [startupticker](https://www.startupticker.ch/en/news/strategic-investment-fuels-riveros-expansion-in-central-and-eastern-europe) · [dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/162329-rivero-lands-series-a-backing-from-raiffeisens-elevator-ventures-to-grow/) · [startbase](https://www.startbase.com/news/raiffeisen-investor-elevator-ventures-steigt-bei-schweizer-fintech-rivero-ein) · [vestbee](https://www.vestbee.com/insights/articles/rivero-secures-investment)

## [1] Who / the players

- **Rivero** — founded 2019, Zurich. A **Visa Fintech Partner** selling SaaS to the *issuer/bank* side of payments (not merchants). Two products: **Kajo** (turns fragmented card-scheme bulletins/mandates into an AI knowledge directory; keeps licensees compliant with Visa/Mastercard scheme updates) and **Amiko** (digitises the full fraud-recovery + dispute/chargeback lifecycle for card issuers, now with agentic AI triage). CEO **Thomas Müller**.
- **Elevator Ventures** — RBI's CVC, backed by RBI + Raiffeisen-Holding Niederösterreich-Wien + Raiffeisen-Landesbank Steiermark. Manages **>€100M**; has deployed **>€60M across 23 companies**, with **7 exits** and stakes in two additional funds; invests Series A in DACH + CEE. MD **Maximilian Schausberger**. The strategic value is distribution: access to RBI's banking footprint across CEE.

## [2] Prior funding & traction

- **Jan 2024: $7M Series A**, led by **6 Degrees Capital** and **Inference Partners**, with **Kraken Ventures**, **Seed X Liechtenstein**, **PostFinance's venture arm**, and angel **Robert Kraal** (former Adyen COO) plus a roster of payments execs. ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2024/01/11/rivero-raises-7m/), [finsmes](https://www.finsmes.com/2024/01/rivero-raises-7m-in-series-a.html))
- **Marquee customer: SIX** (Swiss financial-markets infrastructure / card processor) adopted Rivero's **Amiko** fraud-recovery/dispute platform — a strong proof point for selling into large, regulated institutions. ([startupticker](https://www.startupticker.ch/en/news/rivero-inks-major-deal-with-six), [fintechnews.ch](https://fintechnews.ch/payments/rivero-to-provide-fraud-recovery-platform-to-six/74908/))

## [3] Market & competitors

Chargeback/dispute-management software is a growing niche: trade/market reports peg it ~**$2.6B (2025)** heading toward **~$9.3B by 2035 (~13.5% CAGR)**. Key difference vs. Rivero: most named rivals are **merchant-side** chargeback defenders; Rivero sits on the **issuer/bank side**.
- **Issuer-focused peers**: **Quavo** (issuer dispute automation — closest competitor), **Chargebacks911/Fi911** (recently launched "Disputes-as-a-Service" UDMS spanning issuers/acquirers/merchants — see [[Chargebacks911 launches Unified Dispute Management System]]), **Pagos** (payments data/ops analytics — adjacent, not pure dispute).
- **Merchant-side**: Justt, Chargeflow, Signifyd, Riskified, Verifi (Visa), Accertify, Kount.
- Scheme-compliance (Kajo) has fewer direct software rivals — mostly in-house ops teams + consultancies.
- Related dispute-infra moves in the DB: [[Pix adds dispute button against fraud in Brazil]], [[Proof partners with Visa to secure high-risk transactions]].

## [4] Why it matters / read

A small, undisclosed strategic cheque, but the signal is distribution, not dollars: an **incumbent bank's CVC (RBI) buying into an issuer-side payment-ops vendor** validates the "banks want to outsource scheme-compliance + dispute ops to SaaS" thesis and hands Rivero a warm-intro channel into CEE banking — exactly where it lacked reach. Watch whether this converts into RBI-group deployments (as the SIX deal did for Switzerland). Caveat: amount undisclosed + "strategic follow-on vs. new round" ambiguity means we should not overstate valuation/momentum.

1. **Is this actually a new Series A, or a strategic follow-on?** Trade press (dealroom) says "Series A backing"; Rivero's own blog says it "builds on our growth strategy *following* our Series A financing." These conflict — most likely a strategic/extension cheque, not a fresh priced round. Don't assert a clean "Series A" headline.
2. **Amount undisclosed** — any public figure (e.g. in German-language startbase/startupticker) is unconfirmed; treat as unknown.
3. **Is the $7M (Jan 2024) the whole prior-funding picture?** Any bridge/extension between then and now? Unverified — could understate total raised.
4. **HQ: Zurich or Basel?** Prompt says Basel; Rivero's blog + all sources say **Zurich**. Use Zurich; "Basel" appears to be an error in the ingest framing.
5. **"Strategic investor" vs. board seat / commercial agreement** — does RBI/Elevator get a board seat or a binding RBI-group procurement commitment, or just equity + "network access"? No procurement contract confirmed; "doors opened" ≠ signed deals.
6. **CEE expansion risk** — RBI's CEE footprint includes markets with sanctions/geopolitical exposure (RBI's Russia unit is under regulatory pressure). Does "CEE network" meaningfully help, or is RBI itself constrained?
7. **Customer concentration** — is SIX the only large reference customer? If so, revenue may be thin and lumpy.
8. **Kajo vs. Amiko split** — which product drives revenue? Dispute/fraud (Amiko) is the crowded, high-value segment; scheme-compliance (Kajo) is defensible but smaller TAM. Unverified mix.
9. **Competitive moat vs. Chargebacks911/Fi911 UDMS and Quavo** — both are expanding issuer-side agentic-AI dispute tooling. What stops a larger incumbent from winning European issuers first?
10. **"Agentic AI" claim** — Amiko's agentic-AI framing is marketing language; depth of autonomy vs. rules-based automation is unverified.
11. **Elevator Ventures track record relevance** — ">€60M / 23 companies / 7 exits" is self-reported; exit quality/returns unknown.
12. **Timing/double-count risk** — could this be the same event as an earlier 2026 funding mention in the DB? (No same-round note found; appears first coverage → fresh.)
13. **Revenue/headcount/ARR** — none disclosed; we can't gauge scale or burn.
14. **Regulatory tailwind dependency** — the thesis leans on card-scheme complexity (Visa/Mastercard mandate churn) persisting; a scheme simplification would erode Kajo's value.
15. **Is "Visa Fintech Partner" status material or just a badge?** Unverified whether it confers distribution advantage.

Importance: 3/5 — A credible, strategically interesting but small/undisclosed cheque from a bank CVC into a real issuer-side payment-ops vendor with a marquee reference (SIX) and a clear CEE-distribution angle. Matters as a signal (banks outsourcing scheme-compliance + dispute ops to SaaS; incumbent validation) more than as a capital event. Not market-moving; no amount, no new round confirmed.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Rivero sits in payment-operations SaaS for *issuing* banks — two wedges: dispute management & fraud recovery (Amiko) and payment-network scheme compliance (Kajo). The adjacent, better-measured market is chargeback/dispute management: the global chargeback management market was ~$2.6bn in 2025, projected to ~$11.35bn by 2034 at ~18.4% CAGR (per market.us / trendxinsights, via WebSearch, 2026) — but this figure is merchant/acquirer-weighted, so treat it as a loose proxy, not Rivero's SAM. Underlying volume: ~261m disputes and ~$33.8bn disputed value in 2025, rising to ~324m / ~$41.7bn by 2028 (per 2025 State of Chargebacks report, via WebSearch). Structure: fragmented, software-led (platforms ~72.8% of the chargeback market per market.us), with most vendors sitting on the *merchant* side; the issuer/bank side Rivero targets is less contested. Why now: card-dispute volumes are rising with "frictionless"-payment fraud and friendly fraud, scheme rulebooks (Visa/Mastercard VCR, etc.) keep changing, and banks are under cost pressure to automate manual back-office ops — the exact pain Amiko/Kajo address. The CVC angle: Elevator Ventures (RBI's arm, >€100m AUM, 23 companies, EV II launched 2026 with €70m) buys Rivero distribution into CEE banking relationships (analysis).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for dispute-mgmt SaaS: ARR/seat or per-dispute pricing, dispute win-rate / recovery rate, NRR, and (bank-side) number of institutions live. Players split by which side of the transaction they serve:
- Merchant/acquirer side: Chargeflow, Justt, Chargebacks911, Chargeback Gurus — AI chargeback automation for merchants.
- Issuer/bank side (Rivero's lane): far thinner; Rivero frames Kajo and Amiko as "the only" solutions for scheme compliance and end-to-end fraud-recovery respectively (vendor claim, de-PR: unverified; [UNSOURCED]).
Recent peer moves: Chargeflow raised a $35m Series A and launched an AI suite (Nov 2025, per PYMNTS/fintech.global); Justt has raised ~$100m total incl. a $30m Series C (Zeev Ventures/Oak HC/FT). Rivero's position: niche/early — $7m (~€6.3m) Series A in Jan 2024 (6 Degrees Capital + Inference Partners lead; Kraken Ventures, PostFinance arm, Adyen among backers), now an undisclosed strategic top-up from a bank CVC. Moat (analysis): regulatory/compliance depth + issuer switching costs + the RBI/CEE channel; weak on scale vs the merchant-side raisers.

**Comps & multiples.** Rivero's deal size and valuation are undisclosed → no multiple can be computed; distribution not computed, qualitative comparison only.
- Chargeflow — ~$49m total funding, est. valuation $100–250M (dealroom/startupintros, 2025); merchant-side, not directly comparable.
- Justt — ~$100m raised across 4 rounds; enterprise merchant-side.
- Chargebacks911 — bootstrapped-ish enterprise player, $2bn+ recovered; no public valuation ([UNSOURCED]).
Internal comps from the base (type/funding, dispute/chargeback): [[Chargeflow releases Prevent to block malicious payment activity]], [[Chargebacks911 launches Unified Dispute Management System]], [[Seven European fintech deals raise EUR 95 million in a week]], [[Coinflow raises $25M to challenge Stripe]]. Flag: undisclosed round size/valuation = no price signal; the only hard anchor is the $7m 2024 Series A, implying this is an intra-stage strategic add, not an up-round (analysis).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Undisclosed terms** — no size, no valuation, no new-money-vs-strategic split. "Strategic investment" from a CVC often signals a small cheque bought for distribution rather than a priced growth round; absence of a headline number is itself a soft negative (why: markets read silence on economics as weakness).
2. **CVC concentration / single-channel dependence** — the thesis leans heavily on RBI's CEE banking network. If that channel underperforms, Rivero has limited independent GTM muscle at its size; CVC alignment can also deter future lead investors wary of a captive cap table (why: second-order fundraising/exit friction).
3. **Narrow issuer niche vs better-capitalised merchant-side peers** — Chargeflow/Justt are raising $35–100m and expanding AI; if they move upstream into issuer/bank workflows, Rivero's "only solution" claim erodes against far deeper war-chests (why: disintermediation by scale).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Reads as distribution-led consolidation of the *issuer-side* dispute/compliance niche under a bank-CVC umbrella rather than a growth re-rating (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Rivero announces ≥3 new CEE bank clients or a priced Series B within ~12 months, the Elevator tie-up worked as a channel; if the next signal is another undisclosed strategic top-up or flat headcount, the raise was defensive bridge capital. Watch: whether merchant-side AI players (Chargeflow) cross into bank-side dispute tooling.
