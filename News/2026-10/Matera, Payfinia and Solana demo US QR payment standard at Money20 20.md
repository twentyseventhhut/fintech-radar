# Matera, Payfinia and Solana demo US QR payment standard at Money20/20

_Live demo of ANSI X9.150 shows how one QR code can let customers pay a bill over bank networks or in USDC on Solana, with payments landing in seconds_

LAS VEGAS--(BUSINESS WIRE)--Today, a consortium of banks, fintechs, and payment infrastructure providers including Matera, Payfinia and Solana Foundation held the first live public demonstration of X9.150, a new U.S. standard for QR code payments, at Money20/20 USA. The demo shows how a single QR code on an invoice lets a customer pay over FedNow® Service, the RTP® network, or with USDC on the Solana network, while the business’s invoice records update automatically.

> "X9.150 gives American businesses one QR code that works across payment rails. Solana’s job is to make the stablecoin option as easy as the bank option: a customer scans, pays in USDC, and the invoice is marked paid seconds later."
> 
> Share

For most U.S. businesses, getting paid is still slow and complicated. Customers enter account and routing numbers, log into payment portals, mail checks, or pay card fees. Each payment network works differently, so businesses often wait days for funds and spend hours matching payments to invoices.

X9.150 is designed to fix that. Approved in July 2026 through the American National Standards Institute’s Accredited Standards Committee X9, it gives banks, payment networks and software providers one common format for QR code payments. The platform enables financial institutions, payment providers and billers to deliver secure, interoperable account-to-account payment experiences across multiple payment networks, including FedNow®, RTP®, ACH credit transfers, the Zelle Network® and emerging digital payment methods such as stablecoins. A business can send one code, and the customer can pay with whatever payment method their bank or wallet supports.

At Money20/20, the companies are showing that process from start to finish. A small business sends an invoice with an X9.150 QR code on it. The customer or business financial platform scans the code with their credit union’s banking app, or with the Squads Altitude wallet to pay in USDC, and confirms the payment. The funds arrive within seconds, and the ERP marks the invoice as paid automatically.

The approach draws on Brazil’s experience with Pix, where one common QR code became the country’s most widely used everyday payment method within a few years. X9.150 applies the same principle in the U.S. while working across multiple payment networks, so businesses and banks don’t have to commit to just one.

“We open-sourced our X9.150 reference software because a standard only one company can build isn’t really a standard,” said Carlos Netto, Co-founder and CEO North America, Matera. “The faster others can adopt it correctly, the faster it reaches businesses and consumers everywhere.”

“X9.150 gives American businesses one QR code that works across payment rails,” said Jamal Raees, GM, Payments, Solana Foundation. “Solana’s job is to make the stablecoin option as easy as the bank option: a customer scans, pays in USDC, and the invoice is marked paid seconds later. Open standards like this are how stablecoins become part of everyday commerce.”

**Demonstration Participants**

*   **Matera**, a payments technology provider that helped build Brazil’s Pix ecosystem, released the first open-source X9.150 reference software. 
*   **Payfinia** enables embedded payment services and instant payment support for nearly 30 community financial institutions. Payfinia developed an X9.150 compliance QR code workflow that manages all data exchange and payment routing, and a plug-in experience that can work with any digital banking platform. Payfinia has successfully tested the workflow with Star One Credit Union’s mobile application. 
*   **Solana**operates the settlement rails, with Squads providing the option to pay in USDC on the Solana network. 

The demo is not a product launch. The participants are inviting more banks, credit unions, fintechs and merchants to test and adopt X9.150, since the standard becomes more useful as more businesses and banks accept it.

Organizations interested in piloting X9.150 can contact Eric Valenzuela, eric.valenzuela@matera.com.

**About the X9.150 QR Code Consortium**

The X9.150 QR Code Consortium is a cross-industry group of financial institutions, payment networks and technology providers working to bring X9.150, the U.S. standard for QR code payments, into everyday use. Participants include Matera, Payfinia, Early Warning Service LLC’s Zelle Network® and Solana Foundation.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-10-19 at Money20/20 USA (Las Vegas), a cross-industry consortium — [[Matera, Payfinia and Solana demo US QR payment standard at Money20 20|Matera]], Payfinia and the Solana Foundation — ran the first live public demo of **X9.150**, the new U.S. standard for merchant-presented QR-code payments. A single QR code on an invoice lets a payer settle over **FedNow**, the **RTP** network, or in **USDC on Solana**, while the biller's ERP marks the invoice paid automatically, with funds arriving "within seconds." In the demo a small business issued an X9.150 invoice; the payer scanned it with a credit union's banking app or the **Squads Altitude** wallet (USDC). Matera stressed this is a demo, not a product launch, and open-sourced its X9.150 reference software.

**[1] The standard itself (dated).** X9.150, "Merchant-Presented QR Codes for Secure Payment," was published **2026-07-14** by the Accredited Standards Committee X9 (ASC X9), the ANSI-accredited body for U.S. financial-industry standards. It specifies the structure, content, security requirements, data model and payment-notification flow for dynamically generated, merchant-presented QR codes that initiate push (account-to-account) payments. It is designed to be rail-agnostic — FedNow, RTP, ACH credit transfers, the Zelle Network and emerging methods such as stablecoins all plug into one common code format. The explicit model is Brazil's Pix "BR Code," where one interoperable QR became the dominant everyday payment method within a few years.

**[2] Who the players are.** **Matera** is a Brazilian core-banking / instant-payments vendor that helped build the Pix ecosystem; it processes 5B+ tx/yr (~40% of Brazil's electronic transactions, nearly half scan-to-pay), serves 250+ institutions, took **$100M from Warburg Pincus (2024, majority stake)** to fund its North America push, and released the first open-source X9.150 reference software. **Payfinia** is a California credit-union service org (CUSO) spun out of Star One Credit Union + Tyfone (Star One put in **$4.5M, Nov 2024**); its **IPX** platform is FedNow-certified and serves ~30 community FIs. It built the X9.150 compliance QR workflow (data exchange + payment routing) as a plug-in for any digital-banking platform and tested it in Star One's mobile app. **Solana Foundation** supplies the settlement rail; Squads' Altitude wallet provides the USDC pay option. Jamal Raees (GM Payments, Solana Foundation) and Carlos Netto (Co-founder & CEO North America, Matera) are the named spokespeople.

**[3] Why it matters / market context.** U.S. biller payments are still slow and fragmented (routing numbers, portals, checks, card fees; multi-day settlement; manual reconciliation). A rail-neutral QR standard is the piece Pix/UPI had and the U.S. lacked. Adoption is early but accelerating: ~58% of U.S. banks offering instant payments now use both RTP and FedNow; FedNow Q2-2026 volume rose 80%+ QoQ off a small base (8.4M tx in all of 2025), while RTP already does ~1.18M payments/day. For scale contrast, Pix clears 6B+ tx/month and UPI ~23B/month — the U.S. is years behind on merchant-QR deployment. X9.150 notably puts **stablecoins (USDC) on equal footing with bank rails** inside a formal ANSI standard, a meaningful legitimacy signal for on-chain settlement in mainstream commerce.

**[4] Internal connections.** This slots directly between Matera's other 2026-10 move, [[Matera launches Matera Workspace digital-assets path for banks]] (same Money20/20 window, digital-assets-alongside-fiat thesis), and its earlier stablecoin play [[Matera partners Circle to expand stablecoin payments in Latin America]] — consistent "Pix playbook + stablecoins into core banking" strategy. On the Payfinia side it extends the real-time-payments CUSO story in [[Achieva Credit Union invests in Payfinia CUSO for real-time payments]] (QR + P2P roadmap). ## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. This is explicitly "not a product launch" — a demo. How many of FedNow / RTP / USDC-on-Solana legs were actually live end-to-end vs. staged? (Business Wire says the full flow ran, but production readiness per rail is unverified.)
2. Who actually adopts X9.150? A standard is worthless without biller + payer-app ubiquity. What's the committed adopter count beyond the ~30 Payfinia FIs and Star One?
3. Does any major bank QR/wallet (Zelle/Early Warning, Paze, Apple/Google Pay, big-bank apps) commit to reading X9.150 codes? Zelle's parent Early Warning is listed as a consortium participant — is that active support or name-only?
4. Stablecoin leg: who bears compliance (KYC/AML, Travel Rule, sanctions) when a business is paid in USDC via Squads Altitude? Is the merchant knowingly accepting crypto, and is settlement final/irreversible like Pix?
5. FX / accounting: if paid in USDC, does the biller get dollars or tokens? Who converts, at what spread, and how does the ERP book it?
6. Is Solana's inclusion substantive or marketing? USDC settles on many chains (Ethereum, Base, etc.) — why Solana specifically, and does the standard privilege it or is it rail-neutral as claimed?
7. Dispute / fraud / chargeback model for merchant-presented push payments — QR-swap and malicious-code fraud plagued Pix early. What does X9.150 mandate for security and does the demo prove it?
8. Governance: ASC X9 published the spec, but who runs the "X9.150 QR Code Consortium," who funds it, and is Matera's open-source reference the de-facto implementation (vendor lock-in risk despite "open")?
9. Interoperability reality check — does one code truly route across FedNow AND RTP AND USDC at the payer's choice, or does each FI only wire up one rail, fragmenting the "single QR" promise?
10. Competitive threat: could the Fed, TCH, or a Zelle-led scheme ship their own QR directory and sideline this consortium? Does X9.150's rail-neutrality actually threaten card networks (Visa/MC) enough to draw a counter-move?
11. Timeline credibility: standard published Jul-2026, demoed Oct-2026 — what's the realistic path to any live merchant volume in 2027, given FedNow's still-tiny base?
12. Does the Business Wire release overstate "within seconds" / "automatically reconciled"? Reconciliation depends on ERP integration that the demo controlled end-to-end.
13. Is there any regulatory signal (Fed, OCC, Treasury) endorsing stablecoins inside a sanctioned U.S. payment standard, or is the USDC leg running ahead of regulatory comfort?
14. Matera is Brazilian with a fresh U.S. presence; does it have the U.S. bank relationships and compliance footprint to drive adoption, or is this early land-grab PR around its Warburg-funded expansion?
15. Sourcing: almost all coverage traces to one Business Wire release (2026-10-19) plus crypto-aggregator reprints — is there independent confirmation of the demo's scope?

**Importance: 3/5** — Genuinely strategic (first ANSI-standard U.S. merchant QR + stablecoins on equal footing with bank rails, Pix playbook imported by its original builder), but it is an early-stage demo with no committed volume, few adopters, and heavy single-source PR framing. Watch adoption and bank/Zelle buy-in before rating higher.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US instant-payments / account-to-account (A2A) rails, now bridging to stablecoins. Size/momentum: RTP cleared ~142M transactions / $576bn in Q2 2026; FedNow closed Q1 2026 at $271bn across 2.73M payments (+10.6% volume q/q), reaching 1,725 banks/CUs (~19.7% of US FIs); combined quarterly instant-payment value is now >$700bn (per Dwolla/Richmond Fed/emarketer secondary citations, as of mid-2026). RTP reaches ~90% of US demand-deposit accounts. Parallel stablecoin rail: payment volume rose 45% to ~$482bn by Sep 2026 on roughly flat supply (so usage-, not speculation-driven), with USDC ~80% of volume (per KuCoin/OneSafe secondary, as of Q3 2026). Structure: fragmented — each rail (FedNow, RTP, ACH, Zelle, card, on-chain) has its own format, so merchants face slow settlement and manual reconciliation. Barrier being dismantled: the X9.150 standard (approved July 2026 via ANSI's ASC X9, "Merchant-Presented QR Codes for Secure Payment") gives one QR format across rails. Why now: FedNow/RTP coverage has crossed ~80-90% of accounts, and the GENIUS Act (signed July 2025) gave stablecoins a federal framework — the two preconditions for a Pix-style single-QR layer in the US.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: transaction volume/value, FI count on-network, settlement time, reconciliation (straight-through-processing) rate; for the stablecoin leg, cost-per-transaction vs 2.9% card / $25-50 wire. Basis of competition: standards adoption and distribution (how many FIs/wallets support the format), not price. Players in this consortium: Matera (open-sourced the first X9.150 reference software; helped build Brazil's Pix; serves 250+ FIs, ~5bn Pix tx/yr, ~40% of Brazil electronic tx — per Finovate/Tracxn secondary), Payfinia (IPX platform certified on BOTH RTP and FedNow; ~30 community FI clients; backed by Star One CU's $4.5M, Nov 2024 CUSO launch — per Finovate/CUInsight), Solana Foundation + Squads (settlement rail / Altitude wallet for USDC), and Zelle/Early Warning in the consortium. Adjacent moves: Payfinia added RTP alongside FedNow (Jun 2026); Solana Foundation launched Pay.sh with Google Cloud (May 2026) and a DvP settlement program (Oct 2026). Protagonists' position: Matera is ahead as the standard's open-source reference author and work-group chair (moat = standard-setting / intangibles, not a product lock-in); Payfinia is a niche community-FI distribution play; Solana is competing to be the default stablecoin settlement layer (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** Private, no public multiples disclosed for the demo participants → EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E = no data. Reference valuation anchors (round valuations, not market caps): Matera raised $100M from Warburg Pincus (July 2024, majority stake, ~$120M raised total) on 2023 revenue of ~$77M; implied round P/S not computable (deal value ≠ post-money, and it was a majority secondary/primary mix) → [UNSOURCED]. Internal comps (same actors / theme): [[Matera partners Circle to expand stablecoin payments in Latin America]], [[Solana Foundation launches Pay.sh with Google Cloud]], [[Solana Foundation launches Solana DvP settlement program]], [[Achieva Credit Union invests in Payfinia CUSO for real-time payments]], [[Unit launches Real-Time Payments on its platform]].

**Risk flags.**
1. **Demo, not adoption — chicken-and-egg standard risk.** The participants explicitly say this is "not a product launch"; X9.150 only creates value once a critical mass of banks/wallets both issue and read the codes. A standard with few adopters is dead weight — second-order: Matera's open-sourcing is a bet to accelerate network effects it can't force.
2. **Stablecoin leg is thin and regulation-exposed.** Only one wallet (Squads Altitude) and one coin (USDC on Solana) in the demo; GENIUS-Act issuer rules and state-level nuances govern who can transact, and cross-border use is already being curbed elsewhere (Brazil Resolution 561, effective Oct 1 2026) — the USDC option could stay a niche vs the bank rails that carry the actual volume.
3. **Value may accrue to the standard/rails, not the enablers.** If X9.150 succeeds, banks and the networks (FedNow/RTP/Zelle) capture the payment economics; Matera/Payfinia risk being thin-margin software/plumbing layers, and Solana's take depends on USDC actually being chosen over free FedNow/RTP — dependence on someone else's rails.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is the first credible attempt to give the US a Pix-style single-QR A2A layer that also natively carries stablecoins — if adoption compounds, it re-rates account-to-account over cards for B2B invoicing and makes USDC a first-class, bank-equivalent payment option rather than a crypto niche (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: watch the count of banks/credit unions and wallets live on X9.150 over the next 2-4 quarters — a growing roster (beyond Star One + Squads) confirms network effects; if it stalls at the demo cohort through 2027, X9.150 remains a spec on paper and the stablecoin-at-POS narrative loses its strongest US on-ramp.
