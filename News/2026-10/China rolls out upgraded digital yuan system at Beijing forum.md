# China rolls out upgraded digital yuan system at Beijing forum

> [!info] 2026-10-23 · 1 упоминаний · 1 источника(ов) с текстом
> Каналы: Connecting the Dots in Fintech

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Connecting the Dots in Fintech] 🇨🇳 China rolls out an upgraded digital yuan system at a Beijing forum. The rollout adds DCEPS, a central bank payment system, and the e-CNY SMART Contract framework. Participating areas logged 4.3 billion transactions worth about 31 trillion yuan.

## Первоисточники

### econotimes.com
<https://econotimes.com/China-Rolls-Out-Upgraded-Digital-Yuan-System-at-Beijing-Forum-1759516>
*183 слов · direct*

At an Oct. 21 Beijing forum, China's Digital Yuan Operations and Management Center expanded the digital currency's operating infrastructure with DCEPS, a central bank payment system, and the e-CNY SMART Contract framework.
According to figures reported from the meeting by Xinhua, participating areas had recorded 4.3 billion digital yuan transactions worth about 31 trillion yuan as of the end of September 2026.
Beijing's municipal financial authority identified the new products as DCEPS and e-CNY SMART Contract. Their introduction fulfills a release plan announced before the Financial Street Forum .
An official at the Digital Currency Research Institute of the People's Bank of China (PBOC) described DCEPS as bringing together issuance management, retail and wholesale services, blockchain functions and smart contracts. The system could lower access and liquidity management costs, the official said, according to Xinhua.
The companion framework uses a shared platform and common standards to help financial institutions combine smart contract services for customers, Xinhua reported. The report described smart contracts, which automate the execution of digital agreements, as a way to address high trust costs, poor coordination and difficulties overseeing funds.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On Oct. 21, 2026, at a digital-yuan parallel session of the Financial Street Forum 2026 Annual Conference in Beijing, the People's Bank of China's (PBOC) E-CNY Operation Management Center unveiled two new pieces of e-CNY infrastructure: **DCEPS** (the central-bank-end Digital RMB system) and the **e-CNY SMART Contract** framework (Digital Renminbi Smart Contract Ecological Service Framework). DCEPS bundles issuance management, retail and wholesale services, blockchain functions and smart contracts into one official technical platform; the companion framework gives financial institutions a shared platform and common standards to build smart-contract services. PBOC officials (Digital Currency Research Institute) framed DCEPS as lowering access and liquidity-management costs. The products fulfil a release plan announced ahead of the Forum. Reported adoption: ~4.3 billion cumulative e-CNY transactions worth ~¥31 trillion (~$4.6T) as of end-Sep 2026 (Xinhua).
Sources: <https://english.news.cn/20261021/626a4737d1984784b20bc4812fede589/c.html> · <http://www.china.org.cn/2026-10/22/content_118744131.shtml> · <https://www.tokenpost.com/news/technology/36660>

**[1] Why it matters / stakes.** This is the domestic-side "wing" of a deliberate two-center e-CNY architecture: a Beijing/PBOC-run operations core (DCEPS) for issuance and system plumbing, paired with the Shanghai International Operations Center that handles cross-border rails (see [[PBOC opens digital yuan international operations centre in Shanghai]]). Adding programmable money (smart contracts) moves e-CNY beyond retail cash toward trade financing, wholesale settlement and conditional/escrow payments — the use cases where a CBDC can displace bank rails and, longer-term, dollar-denominated correspondent banking. China is explicitly positioning e-CNY as its state-controlled answer to private stablecoins, which the PBOC has publicly attacked as a vector for US-dollar dominance (see [[China's central bank warns stablecoins pose global risks]]).

**[2] Who/what is involved.** PBOC and its Digital Currency Research Institute (which oversees both the Beijing and Shanghai centers); Beijing's municipal financial authority (named the two products); Xinhua (figures). The Financial Street Forum is PBOC Governor Pan Gongsheng's flagship Beijing policy venue — the same forum where, a year earlier, he warned on stablecoins and vowed to broaden e-CNY. Cross-border counterpart infrastructure: the Shanghai center's CBETS cross-border transfer service (26 banks signed June 2026) and Project mBridge (mainland China, Hong Kong, Thailand, UAE, Saudi Arabia), on which the digital yuan reportedly drives ~95% of settlement volume.

**[3] Context / precedent / timeline.**
- Jun 2025 (Lujiazui Forum): Pan announces 8 measures, incl. the international operations center.
- Sep 25-26, 2025: Shanghai international operations center launches with cross-border payment, blockchain and digital-asset platforms — [[PBOC opens digital yuan international operations centre in Shanghai]].
- Oct 31, 2025: Pan warns stablecoins pose global risk, pledges wider e-CNY — [[China's central bank warns stablecoins pose global risks]].
- Jan 1, 2026: governance overhaul; e-CNY reportedly reclassified toward an interest-bearing/deposit-style instrument; 15th Five-Year Plan commits e-CNY expansion through 2030.
- Jun 16, 2026: Shanghai center signs 26 financial institutions to CBETS cross-border network.
- Oct 21, 2026: THIS — DCEPS + e-CNY SMART Contract unveiled in Beijing.
Comparable CBDC efforts: ECB digital euro ([[ECB advances to next phase of digital euro project]]), India's e-rupee/offline CBDC ([[India to launch RBI-backed digital currency, minister says]], [[RBI introduces offline CBDC payments]]).

**[4] Open questions / what to watch.** Is DCEPS genuinely "new," or a rebrand/consolidation of the existing two-tier issuance stack? Does the smart-contract framework reach production (trade finance, government disbursement) or stay pilot? Headline ¥31T cumulative is turnover, not stock/velocity — actual persistent balances and private-sector usage (vs government top-down pushes) remain unclear. Watch mBridge commercialization via a Hong Kong entity and whether programmability plus cross-border rails materially advance RMB internationalization / de-dollarization, or stall against capital controls and counterparties' reluctance to hold yuan.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
Red-team of the PR framing ("China rolls out upgraded digital yuan system"):

1. **Is DCEPS actually new tech, or a rebrand?** Partly open. Descriptions (issuance + retail/wholesale + blockchain + smart contracts in one platform) overlap heavily with e-CNY's existing two-tier design. Likely a consolidation/formalization of the central-bank-end stack rather than a greenfield system.
2. **Is "upgraded system" a product launch or a policy announcement?** Mostly the latter. Unveiled at a forum, fulfilling a pre-announced "release plan" — a staged policy rollout, not evidence of live at-scale deployment.
3. **Does the ¥31T figure prove success?** No. It is cumulative transaction turnover since inception (end-Sep 2026), not outstanding balance, active users, or velocity. Cumulative totals inflate steadily and say little about organic daily use.
4. **Is adoption organic or state-pushed?** Open/skeptical. Prior e-CNY growth has leaned on government salary/subsidy disbursements and promotional airdrops; the release gives no breakdown of voluntary private usage.
5. **Does the smart-contract framework work in production?** Unknown. No named live deployment beyond "shared platform + common standards"; risk it stays pilot, as earlier e-CNY programmability trials largely did.
6. **Cross-border relevance?** Indirect. DCEPS is domestic-side; cross-border runs through the separate Shanghai center / CBETS / mBridge. This note conflates easily with that — verify it's the Beijing domestic wing.
7. **De-dollarization claim credible?** Overstated near-term. mBridge is ~$55B cumulative and yuan-heavy because China dominates the roster; capital controls and limited willingness to hold RMB cap internationalization regardless of tech.
8. **Who is the sole source?** Xinhua/state media and Beijing municipal authority — official, promotional, no independent audit of the figures or system claims.
9. **Primary-source reliability?** The note's cited source is econotimes (secondary); corroborated by Xinhua/China.org/Beijing Review, but all trace to the same state announcement.
10. **Does it displace banks or stablecoins?** Aspirational. Smart contracts target trade-finance/settlement, but incumbent bank rails and (abroad) USD stablecoins remain entrenched; no migration data.
11. **Interest-bearing angle?** Context-dependent. Reports of a Jan-2026 reclassification toward yield-bearing e-CNY are not in this announcement — don't assert it as part of THIS rollout.
12. **Timeline accuracy?** Confirmed: Oct 21, 2026, Financial Street Forum 2026, Beijing. Consistent across Xinhua, China.org, Beijing Review.
13. **Duplicate of the 2025 Shanghai-center note?** No — that was the cross-border international center (Sep 2025); this is the domestic DCEPS/smart-contract upgrade (Oct 2026). Distinct; cross-linked, not a duplicate.
14. **Fintech-relevant beyond geopolitics?** Yes — programmable CBDC rails + wholesale settlement directly affect payments and blockchain infrastructure.

Importance: 3/5 — a credible, well-sourced step in the world's largest CBDC program, adding programmability and a formalized central-bank system; genuinely consequential for payments/blockchain and RMB strategy. Capped below 4 because it is an incremental, state-media-announced policy rollout with no verified at-scale production, soft headline metrics, and near-term cross-border/de-dollarization impact that remains speculative.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Retail+wholesale CBDC infrastructure, inside the broader "sovereign-money-vs-stablecoin" contest. Size/scale (sourced): cumulative e-CNY transactions reached 4.3bn worth ~CNY31tn (~$4.61tn) as of end-Sep 2026; Sep-2026 alone ran 99.67m transactions worth ~CNY1.87tn, +12% / +123% YoY respectively (per Xinhua/CGTN/Beijing Review, via note + CGTN, 2026-10-22). The cross-border leg, BIS-adjacent Project mBridge, has cleared >$55bn cumulative across ~4,000 transactions as of Jan 2026 (per Atlantic Council data cited by Reuters, via Ledger Insights / The Block). Structure: CBDC issuance is a sovereign monopoly by definition — zero private entry, barrier = statute not capital; the competitive surface is adoption vs incumbent rails (Alipay/WeChat Pay domestically) and vs USD stablecoins cross-border. Drivers / "why now": (1) de-dollarization — ~99% of fiat-backed stablecoin supply is USD-denominated (USDT ~59%, USDC ~23% of supply; per reap.global / CoinGecko 2026), which Beijing frames as a sovereignty threat and is answering with e-CNY rather than a yuan stablecoin (PBoC + 7 agencies banned yuan-pegged stablecoins Feb 2026); (2) adoption engineering — from Jan-1-2026 commercial banks may pay interest on e-CNY, turning it into a "digital deposit currency" to pull balances in (per CoinJournal/Bitget). DCEPS + e-CNY SMART Contract are the infrastructure layer meant to make (1) and (2) programmable.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: transaction count & value, cumulative vs active/velocity, cross-border settlement volume, number of participating jurisdictions. Key "players" are sovereigns, not firms, racing on timeline and go-live status:
- China e-CNY — live at national-pilot scale, now adding wholesale + smart contracts (the subject).
- Russia digital ruble — mandatory/commercial rollout from Sep-1-2026, free P2P for individuals (per cbr.ru / CryptoTimes). *Live.*
- Digital euro — still pre-issuance: ECB closed preparation Oct-2025, PSP selection in 2026, pilot H2-2027 (12mo), potential first issuance ~2029 and only if EU legislation passes (per ECB / Central Bank of Ireland). *Years behind.*
- India (RBI e-rupee) — live, with offline-capable retail CBDC (per in-base notes).
Basis of competition: go-live + real usage, not product gloss. China's position: *ahead* on retail scale and on the cross-border/wholesale push; moat = state mandate + mBridge network (analysis). But note a sharp counter-narrative: PIIE argues China is effectively de-emphasizing state-backed digital cash (via piie.com, 2026) — "announced upgrade" is not the same as organic demand; much of the 4.3bn count is pilot- and subsidy-driven (de-PR).

**Comps & multiples.** Not applicable — sovereign/CBDC story, no equity, no valuation, no round. Trading multiples = n/a. In-base comps (qualitative, by go-live status):
- [[ECB advances to next phase of digital euro project]] — digital euro, pre-pilot.
- [[ECB signs digital euro standards deals with ECPC, nexo and Berlin Group]] — euro CBDC standards build-out.
- [[PBOC opens digital yuan international operations centre in Shanghai]] — the Shanghai hub underpinning this cross-border push.
- [[India to launch RBI-backed digital currency, minister says]] / [[RBI introduces offline CBDC payments]] — India e-rupee, live retail.
- [[China's central bank warns stablecoins pose global risks]] — the stablecoin-containment flip side of this launch.
Relative read: China is the furthest-along large-economy retail CBDC by disclosed volume; the digital euro is the laggard, the digital ruble the other live-at-scale case.

**Risk flags.**
1. Adoption vs vanity-metrics risk — cumulative counts and interest-bearing incentives can mask weak organic velocity; if usage is subsidy/pilot-propped, the "upgrade" is infrastructure ahead of demand (PIIE's thesis). Second-order: a credible-looking rail that domestic users still bypass for Alipay/WeChat.
2. Cross-border fragmentation risk — mBridge's future is contested; a Forbes piece (2026-05) argues multilateral CBDC interoperability is "dead" after mBridge/Agora stalls. If multilateral rails fracture into bilateral deals, e-CNY's internationalization slows and USD stablecoins keep the cross-border default. [UNSOURCED on current mBridge membership stability beyond Macau/Mongolia joins.]
3. Geopolitical/surveillance friction — a state-programmable, smart-contract-enabled currency raises counterparty concerns abroad (fund traceability cuts both ways), capping voluntary foreign uptake outside aligned jurisdictions.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is China moving CBDC from retail pilot toward programmable + wholesale infrastructure — positioning e-CNY as the sovereign counter to USD stablecoins rather than competing head-on with Alipay. Falsifiable thesis: if the next PBoC disclosure shows cross-border/wholesale e-CNY and mBridge volumes compounding (not just cumulative retail counts), the de-dollarization rail is real; trigger to watch = new mBridge members + first non-pilot smart-contract use cases (trade finance, programmable subsidies). What breaks it: flat organic retail velocity and mBridge membership stalling — then DCEPS is plumbing without traffic.
