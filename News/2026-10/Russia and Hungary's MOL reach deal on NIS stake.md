 В Сербии заявили о договоренности России и MOL по доле в NIS 
Российские владельцы «Нефтяной индустрии Сербии» (NIS) и крупнейшая венгерская энергетическая компания MOL подписали договор о продаже российской доли в компании. Об этом исполняющий обязанности первого вице-премьера и министра финансов Сербии Синиша Мали заявил в эфире Bloomberg Adria, пишет РБК .
«Договоренность между российской стороной, которая является мажоритарным владельцем, и венгерской MOL достигнута. Договор подписан. Мы как государство также заключили акционерное соглашение с венгерской стороной», — сказал Мали (цитата по Tanjug).
Для завершения сделки необходимо получить одобрения антимонопольных органов Сербии и Боснии и Герцеговины, добавил он. Мали рассчитывает, что заявки будут поданы вскоре, после чего переход прав собственности удастся быстро завершить. Он также сообщил о письменной договоренности увеличить долю Сербии в NIS с примерно 30 до 35%.
Основные условия продажи российской доли в NIS согласовали еще в январе 2026 года. Речь шла о пакете в 56,15% акций: 44,85% принадлежали «Газпром нефти», еще 11,3% — компании «Интэлидженс» под управлением «Газпром капитала». Тогда сербские власти рассчитывали завершить сделку в феврале—марте.
В мае «Газпром нефть» подтвердила подготовку сделки с MOL. Компания сообщила о проведении необходимых корпоративных и регуляторных процедур и подчеркнула, что переговоров с другими покупателями не ведет. Перед этим Reuters со ссылкой на сербского бизнесмена Ранко Мимовича сообщил о предложении выкупить российский пакет за €2 млрд.
16 июня власти Сербии и MOL подписали акционерное соглашение о будущем управлении NIS. Оно определяло условия работы компании в случае перехода контрольного пакета к венгерской группе. Сама сделка по покупке российских акций на тот момент еще готовилась.
В конце июля занимавший тогда пост президента Сербии Александр Вучич вновь выразил надежду на завершение сделки в ближайшие дни. Он также допустил разговоры с Владимиром Путиным и премьер-министром Венгрии Петером Мадьяром по этому вопросу.
Продажу российского пакета потребовали США, которые ввели санкции против NIS в январе 2025 года. Вступление ограничений в силу неоднократно откладывали, однако осенью они начали действовать. Вашингтон настаивал на полном исключении российского участия. Впоследствии американский Минфин США выдавал временные разрешения на работу компании и переговоры о смене собственников.
«Нефтяная индустрия Сербии» (NIS) занимается добычей и переработкой нефти и газа, владеет единственным в Сербии НПЗ в Панчево мощностью около 4,8 млн т в год и сетью более чем из 400 АЗС. Компания обеспечивает страну моторным топливом. Ее значение для энергоснабжения проявилось в декабре 2025 года, когда завод приостановил работу из-за нехватки нефти на фоне санкций США. Помимо российского участия в NIS, Сербия связана с Россией поставками газа: он поступает через «Турецкий поток» и Болгарию. В сентябре договоренности о поставках продлили до конца 2026 года.
MOL — венгерская нефтегазовая группа, которая занимается добычей, переработкой и продажей топлива. Она управляет тремя НПЗ в Венгрии, Словакии и Хорватии, а также сетью почти из 2,4 тыс. заправок в десяти странах Центральной и Юго-Восточной Европы, следует из отчетности компании. Покупка контрольного пакета NIS позволит MOL участвовать в управлении единственным сербским НПЗ. В заявлении о сделке группа обозначила обеспечение стабильных поставок в Сербию и регион одной из своих задач.

# Context-enrichment: Russia and Hungary's MOL reach deal on NIS stake
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

Note: this is an energy / geopolitics M&A, not fintech. It lands in the DB via the capital-markets/M&A tag, but the "financial" angle is thin — it matters as a sanctions-driven forced divestment, not as a market deal.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- Serbia's acting first deputy PM and finance minister Siniša Mali said (on Bloomberg Adria, per Tanjug/RBC) that the Russian owners of Naftna Industrija Srbije (NIS) and Hungary's MOL have **signed a contract** to sell the Russian stake, and that Serbia as a state separately signed a **shareholders' agreement** with MOL. This is a milestone, not a close.
- The stake in play is **56.15%**: 44.85% held by Gazprom Neft and 11.3% by "Intelligence"/Intelligence Fund managed by Gazprom Capital. Serbia already owns ~29.87%–30%; the rest is minority float. Serbia has a written agreement to raise its stake from ~30% to **35%** (i.e. +5%) conditional on the MOL–Gazprom deal closing.
- **De-PR'd reality — "signed" ≠ "closed".** Closing still needs antitrust clearance from Serbia AND Bosnia & Herzegovina, and — critically, though Mali underplays it — a clean US OFAC posture. Mali expects filings "soon" and a fast ownership transfer. → Why framed as a done deal by Belgrade: a hard US deadline (OFAC waiver expiring ~Oct 30, 2026) and refinery supply security create strong incentive to project momentum publicly even before regulators sign off.
- **Price is undisclosed.** Mali did not state the purchase price or MOL's exact resulting stake. An earlier (Reuters, via businessman Ranko Mimović) figure floated a ~€2bn offer for the Russian package — unconfirmed and not the official deal value. Treat any number as speculative.
- This is the activation/culmination of a process, not a surprise: Heads of Agreement signed Jan 2026; Gazprom Neft confirmed deal prep in May 2026; Serbia + MOL signed a management shareholders' agreement on 16 June 2026; the Oct 2026 statement is the "talks → signed SPA" step.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- Direct comps are other **sanctions-forced Russian energy divestments** and regional refinery control plays, not fintech peers. MOL's rivals for Southeast-Europe downstream presence include OMV (Austria), NIS's own incumbent position, and Croatia's INA (already MOL-controlled).
- MOL is the natural / effectively sole bidder: it already runs refineries in Hungary, Slovakia and Croatia plus ~2,400 stations across 10 CEE/SEE countries, and controls INA. Buying NIS's Pančevo refinery (~4.8 Mt/yr, Serbia's only refinery) + 400+ stations consolidates MOL as the dominant downstream player in the Western Balkans.
- → Why MOL and not a Western major: a US-acceptable, EU/NATO-aligned buyer willing to operate a politically sensitive Serbian asset is a narrow field; Gazprom Neft publicly said it was NOT negotiating with other buyers. Second-order: this is less a competitive auction than a managed, sanctions-engineered handover, which weakens Gazprom Neft's price leverage.

## [2] Company history / fit
- NIS: Serbia's national oil & gas champion, majority-sold to Gazprom Neft in 2008/2009. Operates the Pančevo refinery (~4.8 Mt/yr) and 400+ stations; supplies the bulk of Serbia's motor fuel.
- US sanctioned NIS in **January 2025**; enforcement was repeatedly delayed but took force **9 October 2025** (JANAF/Croatia halted crude via the Adriatic pipeline). Pančevo suspended crude processing ~25 Nov 2025; OFAC granted a special license 31 Dec 2025; refinery restarted mid-Jan 2026, first fuel ~27 Jan 2026. → Why this deal is existential for Serbia: the Dec 2025 shutdown showed the country's fuel security hinges on removing Russian ownership so sanctions lift.
- MOL fit is clean: it is a strategic downstream consolidator already operating adjacent refining; NIS plugs a geographic gap. → Structural logic: MOL gains regional scale + secures its own supply corridor (it framed "stable supply to Serbia and the region" as a goal).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **What's genuinely new in THIS item:** the step from "agreement in principle / HoA" to a **signed sale contract** plus a state-level shareholders' agreement, and the concrete Serbia 30%→35% uplift. Earlier notes covered the HoA (Jan) and the management agreement (June); the signed SPA is the new fact.
- **Traction / what's real vs announced:** still "signed, not closed." No price, no antitrust clearances (Serbia + BiH), no confirmed OFAC green-light on the final structure, no completion date. Historically this process has slipped repeatedly (targeted Feb–Mar 2026 close originally). So the durable fact is modest.
- → Who captures value: MOL gains an operating, cash-generative downstream asset at a sanctions-depressed, undisclosed price with limited competition — likely a favorable entry. Gazprom Neft is a forced seller exiting under sanctions. Serbia converts geopolitical exposure into a larger domestic stake and restored EU/US-compatible supply.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Near-term gating: antitrust filings in Serbia and Bosnia & Herzegovina; OFAC posture (waiver had been extended to ~Oct 30, 2026); then ownership transfer. Mali expects speed; the track record says expect slippage.
- Energy backdrop: Serbia still depends on Russian gas via TurkStream/Bulgaria (supply extended to end-2026, per Sep 2026), so de-Russifying oil does not fully de-Russify Serbian energy. → Counterintuitive second-order: completing the oil divestment may increase, not decrease, Belgrade's political need to keep the gas relationship warm.
- Sentiment: for MOL this is strategically positive (regional dominance, supply security); for the Kremlin it is a managed retreat from a flagship Balkans asset under US pressure.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is CEE/Balkan downstream oil M&A forced by sanctions, not a fintech/capital-markets deal despite the `industry/capital-markets` tag. NIS runs Serbia's only refinery (Pančevo, ~4.8m t/yr) plus 400+ fuel stations — a strategic single-point-of-failure for national fuel supply (per note; mergers.ru). The driver is regulatory/geopolitical, not cyclical: the US sanctioned NIS on 2025-01-10 for its Gazprom Neft link; after multiple delays the measures bit on 2025-10-09, cutting crude via the Adria/JANAF pipeline and forcing a ~36-day Pančevo shutdown from 2025-11-25 until an OFAC license let it restart to 2026-01-23 (per Balkan Insight 2025-12-02, OSW 2025-10-21, Hydrocarbon Processing 2026-01). "Why now": the only way to lift sanctions is full exit of Russian ownership — Washington demanded total removal of Russian participation (per note). The deal is a forced divestment under duress, not a market-cleared sale.

**Competitive landscape.** Downstream KPIs here are refining throughput (Pančevo ~4.8m t/yr), retail network (NIS >400 stations; MOL ~2,400 in 10 CEE/SEE countries across 3 refineries HU/SK/HR — per note), and crude-access optionality. MOL is the natural strategic buyer: adjacent footprint, a route to secure its own SEE supply, and already the only serious bidder — in May 2026 Gazprom Neft confirmed no talks with other buyers (per note). Recent sequence: Jan 2026 heads-of-agreement on the 56.15% stake; May 2026 a rival €2bn bid from businessman Ranko Mimović surfaced (Reuters, per note); 16 Jun 2026 Serbia+MOL signed a shareholders' agreement on future NIS governance; late Oct 2026 Mali says the sale contract is signed, pending Serbian + Bosnian antitrust clearance, and Serbia will raise its own stake ~30%→35% (per note; BBJ, Hungary Today). MOL's position: sole buyer with a sanctions-waiver lever — strong leverage, which likely explains the price gap below.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal size is the key data point and it is contested, not confirmed. MOL's agreed range is up to ~€0.9–1.0bn for the 56.15% stake (per Investing.com, Hungary Today); the rival Mimović bid was ~€2bn for the same package — i.e. MOL is paying roughly half of an unsolicited third-party offer. No confirmed final euro figure was disclosed at signing; Mali gave no price (per BBJ, 2026-10). At ~€1bn for 56.15%, implied 100% equity ≈ €1.0bn / 0.5615 = ~€1.78bn (analysis; pre-sanctions NIS market cap was higher, so this is a distressed/sanctions discount, not a clean market value). EV/EBITDA / EV/Revenue = no data (NIS standalone financials not sourced here; refinery was idle part of 2025). Internal comps — Russian forced/strategic asset exits under sanctions: [[LUKOIL-Carlyle foreign asset sale agreement expired in July]] (foreign-asset divestment that collapsed — the base-rate warning for these deals), [[RusGazDobycha transfers 49% in oilfield-equipment supplier GMS]] and [[Putin places Tarkett's Russian assets under temporary management]] (the mirror: foreign owners losing Russian assets). Distribution not computed — single contested deal value, qualitative comparison only. Flag: price looks cheap vs the €2bn alternative, consistent with a distressed seller + sole-buyer dynamic.

**Risk flags.**
1. Deal-completion / closing risk. "Contract signed" ≠ closed — it still needs Serbian AND Bosnian antitrust approval plus de facto US/OFAC sign-off to lift sanctions (per note; BBJ). MOL itself gave no disclosure confirmation and flagged talks running through 30 Oct — the base rate for these Russia-exit deals is slippage/collapse (cf. LUKOIL-Carlyle expired in July).
2. Valuation/governance contention. MOL's ~€1bn vs a ~€2bn rival bid means Gazprom Neft is accepting ~half of a credible offer; a disgruntled seller or Serbian state (raising its own stake to 35%) could reopen terms, and minority/valuation disputes are a known MOL risk (cf. the historic INA-MOL dispute). Why it matters: an unhappy counterparty is closing-risk.
3. Sanctions-relief dependency. The entire rationale is removing Russian ownership to end OFAC pressure; if OFAC judges the Russian economic interest not fully severed (indirect links, payment mechanics), the waiver/relief may not follow and Pančevo faces renewed shutdown risk — the asset's value is contingent on a political decision MOL doesn't control.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** If it closes, this is the template for unwinding Russian energy ownership in sanctioned EU-adjacent assets: a willing CEE strategic (MOL) absorbs a distressed Russian stake at a sanctions discount, the host state tops up its holding, and OFAC relief follows (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: the deal closes within ~2 quarters with Serbian/Bosnian antitrust clearance and NIS exits the OFAC list. What would break it: regulators or OFAC stall, Gazprom Neft walks over price (the €2bn anchor), or the US rules Russian participation insufficiently removed — any of which repeats the LUKOIL-Carlyle "signed then expired" pattern. Trigger to watch: Serbian/Bosnian competition filings and an OFAC license/delisting action.
