# Paymentology appoints Sultan Alabdullatif Saudi Country Manager

**Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, 25 October 2026** – Paymentology, the leading global issuer processor, has appointed Sultan Alabdullatif as Country Manager for Saudi Arabia, reinforcing its long-term commitment to the Kingdom and its ambitions to support innovation across the country's rapidly evolving payments ecosystem.

The appointment builds on Paymentology's local entity establishment in 2025 and its continued investment in the Kingdom by localising its technology and capabilities to meet the needs of the Saudi market. With dedicated leadership now on the ground, Paymentology is strengthening its presence in the Kingdom, enabling closer collaboration with banks, fintechs, and ecosystem partners.

Paymentology's growing footprint in Saudi Arabia is reflected in its relationships with some of the Kingdom's leading financial services innovators, including Tabby and Enjaz. These partnerships demonstrate the increasing demand for modern, flexible issuer processing technology as financial institutions look to launch new products, scale their operations and deliver more seamless payment experiences.

Sultan brings more than 15 years of experience spanning payments, technology and financial services, having held senior positions across global organisations and emerging businesses. He joins Paymentology from Visa, where he served as Head of Acceptance Solutions in Saudi Arabia, helping to build and grow the company's Saudi business while developing strategic relationships with banks, fintechs and ecosystem partners.

Prior to Visa, Sultan was Country Manager at Amazon Payment Services, where he led the growth of its payment services business in Saudi Arabia. Earlier in his career, he held payments and product roles at J.P. Morgan. He holds a Master's degree in Computer Science from California State University, Fullerton.

In his new role, Sultan will lead Paymentology's continued growth in Saudi Arabia, deepening relationships with existing clients, developing new partnerships and working closely with the wider financial ecosystem to support the delivery of innovative, locally relevant payment solutions.

**Jeff Parker, CEO of Paymentology, commented:** "We've established fantastic momentum in Saudi Arabia, working with innovative businesses like Tabby and Enjaz, and we're focused on helping more banks and fintechs bring their ambitions to life. The Kingdom is a key market for Paymentology, and we're committed to investing in the local expertise, technology and relationships that will help our clients innovate, scale and succeed.

"Sultan's appointment is a significant step in that commitment. His deep understanding of the Saudi payments landscape, strong industry relationships and proven track record make him ideally placed to understand our clients' ambitions and help turn them into reality. We're delighted to welcome him to the team as we deepen our presence and support the next wave of payments innovation in the Kingdom."

**Sultan Alabdullatif, Country Manager for Saudi Arabia at Paymentology, added:** "Saudi Arabia is continuing a remarkable transformation in financial services, creating significant opportunities for banks, fintechs and businesses to rethink how payments are delivered and experienced.

"Paymentology combines world-class issuer processing technology with global expertise and flexibility to support the growth ambitions of banks and fintechs. We’ve already established strong foundations in the Kingdom, and my priority is to build on that momentum by working closely with our clients and partners.

"Our local presence reflects Paymentology’s long-term commitment to Saudi Arabia and the clients we serve. Being closer to our clients allows us to better understand their priorities, respond to their evolving needs and build lasting partnerships. My focus is on delivering on our commitments and providing the technology, expertise and support our clients need to achieve their business objectives.

The expansion comes as Saudi Arabia continues to make significant progress towards its Vision 2030 ambitions. According to the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA), electronic payments accounted for 85% of all retail payments in 2025, with 14.6 billion electronic transactions recorded throughout the year. Meanwhile, the number of operating fintech companies reached 371 by August 2026, reflecting the pace of innovation and growing opportunities across the Kingdom's financial services sector.

Paymentology's continued commitment to Saudi Arabia forms part of its wider growth strategy across the Middle East and globally, supported by a $175 million strategic investment announced in May 2026 to accelerate international expansion and technology innovation. By combining its global infrastructure with local market expertise, the company is helping banks and fintechs bring new payment propositions to market, scale their operations and respond to evolving customer expectations.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Issuer-processing / card-issuing platforms sit in the "issuing" half of the card value chain — they connect banks/fintechs to Visa/Mastercard rails and run the authorization, tokenization and ledger for programmes the issuer owns. The sub-vertical is globally fragmented between pure-plays (Marqeta, Thredd, Paymentology, Pismo, Galileo) and regional specialists (NymCard in MENA). Saudi Arabia is the "why now": per SAMA, electronic payments reached 85% of retail payments in 2025 (up from 79% in 2024) on 14.6bn transactions (vs 12.6bn in 2024), and the Vision 2030 Financial Sector Development Programme is actively pulling cash out of the system — POS cash fell from ~60% (2016) to under 15% by 2025 (per SAMA, via Arab News/SPA, Oct 2026). The note cites 371 operating fintechs by Aug 2026; secondary sources vary (over 280 fintechs; SAMA sandbox >70 admitted, >25 graduated to licensed) — treat 371 as Paymentology's figure, not independently verified. Barriers to entry are regulatory (SAMA licensing, local-entity/data-localisation requirements — Paymentology set up a local entity in 2025) and capital/certification (direct scheme connectivity), which favours incumbents already localised.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: processed/transaction volume, number of live programmes/cards, take rate per transaction, and client logo wins (this is a distribution/relationships game in a new geo, not a price game). Key players in-market: Paymentology (clients named — Tabby, Enjaz), NymCard (the main MENA-native rival, claims to be the only issuer processor in MENA that fully owns its processing+switching tech), Thredd and Marqeta (global pure-plays pushing into the region). Basis of competition: scheme connectivity + local certification + on-the-ground relationships with banks/fintechs. Recent peer moves: NymCard raised $33m Series B (Mar 2025, QED-led; ~$70m total) and went live with Visa stablecoin settlement in the GCC on 2 Feb 2026; Thredd has expanded in MEA (MuchBetter/Israel, Feb 2026). Paymentology's position: ahead of the global pure-plays on Saudi localisation (local entity + now a dedicated Country Manager) but defending against NymCard, which owns its stack regionally. This appointment is a distribution/go-to-market move, de-PR'd — a leadership hire, not a product launch, revenue win or licence. Moat is relationships/switching costs `(analysis)` — a Country Manager poached from Visa KSA (ex-Amazon Payment Services) signals a relationship-led land-grab, not technical differentiation.

**Comps & multiples.** Only one listed pure-play gives a clean public read:
- **Marqeta (NASDAQ: MQ)** — EV ~$1.2bn, TTM net revenue $687m → EV/Revenue = $1.2bn / $0.687bn ≈ **1.7x**; EV/EBITDA = $1.2bn / $0.137bn ≈ **8.8x** (market cap ~$1.9bn, as of Oct 2026, per stockanalysis.com). Q2'26: TPV $120bn (+32% YoY), net revenue $176m (+17%), adj. EBITDA $37m. A low revenue multiple reflecting slowing gross-profit growth and gateway-concentration risk — in-line-to-cheap for a profitable-but-decelerating processor.
- **NymCard (private)** — ~$70–72m total raised; post-money valuation not disclosed → multiple **no data** (round-stage, not comparable to MQ's public cap).
- **Paymentology (private)** — $175m strategic investment (May 2026, co-led Apis Partners + Aspirity Partners); post-money/round valuation **not disclosed** → no multiple. Self-reported growth: new sales +117% and transaction volumes +65% in last fiscal year (per PYMNTS/Finextra; company figures, unverified).
- Internal comps: [[Paymentology raises $175 million for issuer processing]] · [[Directory of UK and European card issuing and program platforms]] · [[Mawarid Finance and NymCard launch SME platform Lynq]] · [[Paymentology scales card programs in Saudi Arabia]].
With only one clean public figure, distribution not computed — qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regional disintermediation by NymCard** — a MENA-native that owns its full processing/switching stack and is further along on novel rails (Visa stablecoin GCC, live Feb 2026); Paymentology risks competing on relationships where a rival has both relationships and local stack ownership.
2. **No announced economics or licence, just a hire** — the note gives zero revenue/programme/TPV figures for KSA; "commitment" and logos (Tabby, Enjaz) are announced, not quantified, and no SAMA licence status is stated. Execution risk that the land-grab doesn't convert to volume.
3. **Scheme dependence / margin capture** — like all issuer processors, Paymentology sits on Visa/Mastercard rails; Visa itself (Sultan's former employer) is pushing deeper into issuing/acceptance in KSA, and schemes can compress processor economics.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Dedicated country leadership + 2025 local entity + the May-2026 $175m raise read as Paymentology building for a Saudi scale-up, not a toe-dip `(analysis)`. Thesis: Saudi issuer-processing consolidates around whoever converts localisation into bank/fintech programme wins first — watch for Paymentology's next KSA signal to be a bank/licensing announcement, not another hire. Falsifier: if NymCard or a global pure-play books the next marquee Saudi bank programme while Paymentology only announces personnel, the land-grab is stalling.
