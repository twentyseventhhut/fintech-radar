DUBAI, UAE , Oct. 12, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Bybit , the New Financial Platform trusted by more than 80 million users worldwide,  announced that stocks are now available for trading through Bybit, giving eligible users access to more than 1,000 U.S.-listed stocks and ETFs, including Apple (AAPL), NVIDIA (NVDA), Tesla (TSLA), Micron Technology (MU) and the Invesco QQQ ETF (QQQ).

 The development expands Bybit's multi-asset platform, bringing access to U.S. equities alongside its existing digital asset and traditional market offerings. Eligible users in permitted jurisdictions who have completed the required KYC verification can trade supported U.S. stocks and ETFs using USDC.
"Stocks are an important part of our evolution into The New Financial Platform. Financial markets have long been separated across different products and platforms, but users increasingly expect a more connected experience," said Ben Zhou, Co-founder and CEO of Bybit . "With stocks now available through Bybit, we are doing more than adding another asset class. We are giving people broader access to financial markets and greater freedom to pursue opportunities."
 Access to the U.S. Stocks and ETFs 
Stocks trading enables eligible users to buy and sell supported U.S. stocks and ETFs using USDC, bringing equity exposure into the same environment used for digital assets. Unlike price derivatives or synthetic instruments, Stocks trading gives eligible users an economic interest in the underlying securities they purchase.
Orders are routed through Bybit's regulated brokerage infrastructure and executed through Alpaca, a U.S. self-clearing broker-dealer and a global leader in agent-first infrastructure. Additionally, the underlying securities are held in custody by Alpaca.
"Bybit's expansion into U.S. stocks and ETFs is a major step forward in offering its global users a more complete, multi-asset trading experience," said Yoshi Yokokawa, Co-Founder and CEO of Alpaca . "We're thrilled to partner with Bybit and help power this next wave of global market access."
Key features at launch include:
1,000+ U.S. stocks and ETFs, including widely traded companies with fractional shares available for eligible securities
Trade with USDC, allowing eligible users to fund stock purchases directly from their Bybit account
Direct economic exposure to underlying stocks, rather than derivatives or synthetic price exposure
Allowing users to manage eligible crypto and equity positions within the Bybit ecosystem
Corporate-action support, including cash and stock dividends and stock splits
24/5 trading, providing access during U.S. pre-market, regular and after-hours sessions, subject to product availability
Zero commission, with no trading commissions charged on eligible stock purchases and sales
Eligible holders of supported U.S. stocks and ETFs can receive the economic benefits associated with their holdings, including cash and stock dividends, subject to applicable security and corporate-action terms. Cash dividends are converted from USD into USDC and credited to the user's Unified Trading Account (UTA), while stock dividends are reflected directly in the user's position.
Stock splits are also reflected in users' holdings in accordance with the applicable corporate action. Voting rights are planned for launch in a future phase.
 Building a Broader Multi-Asset Platform 
The addition of stock services adds to the exchange's growing range of products across traditional and digital markets. Alongside crypto spot and derivatives trading, Bybit offers access to traditional markets through its MT5 platform, tokenized equities and TradFi perpetuals.
"We believe the next chapter of finance is one where the walls between crypto and traditional markets no longer exist. Bybit is built to operate in both worlds and proves that tokenizing real-world assets at scale is not a future ambition. It is something we are delivering today," added Ben.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] TL;DR.** On 2026-10-12 Bybit launched a new "Stocks" service giving eligible users (outside the US and other restricted jurisdictions) access to 1,000+ **real** U.S.-listed stocks and ETFs — AAPL, NVDA, TSLA, MU, QQQ — funded and settled in USDC. Critically, these are *actual securities* (direct economic interest, dividends, splits, planned voting), not tokenized or synthetic/CFD exposure. Orders route through a Mauritius-regulated introducing broker (Infra Capital Ltd, FSC GB23202169) and are executed, cleared and custodied by **Alpaca Securities LLC** (US self-clearing broker-dealer, FINRA/SIPC). Zero commission, fractional shares, 24/5 trading.

**[1] Why it matters.** This is a structural step beyond the "tokenized stock" wave: Bybit is embedding a *regulated TradFi brokerage rail* inside a crypto exchange, letting 80M+ crypto users buy genuine equities with stablecoin and hold them next to crypto in one Unified Trading Account. It is the clearest expression yet of the "crypto exchange ↔ stock market" convergence, and it monetizes USDC balances as an on-ramp into equities. The "US stocks for global users — except the US" irony underlines the regulatory-arbitrage logic: offering US securities to non-US users via an offshore broker + US clearing partner.

**[2] Internal lineage (prior coverage in base).**
- [[Kraken and Bybit launch tokenized US stocks]] — Jul 2025: Bybit's *first* equity move was tokenized xStocks (Backed Finance, 1:1, on Solana). The Oct 2026 launch is a **different product** (real shares, not tokens).
- [[Coinbase and Bybit explore US tokenized stock collaboration]] / [[Coinbase and Bybit explore on-chain US stocks access]] — Mar–Apr 2026: Bybit had been exploring *tokenized/on-chain* US-stock access with Coinbase; this real-equity-via-Alpaca route is a separate, now-shipped path.
- [[trii launches US stock trading in Chile via Alpaca]] — Mar 2026: same Alpaca brokerage-infrastructure playbook (fractional US equities for non-US users), confirming Alpaca as the recurring execution/custody partner.
- [[Kraken acquires Backed Finance for tokenization]] — Dec 2025: Kraken's rival path (own the tokenization issuer) vs Bybit's path (plug into a TradFi broker for real shares).

- 2025-06/07: Backed Finance debuts tokenized stocks (xStocks) on Bybit & Kraken (Solana SPL, 1:1).

Features: USDC funding, fractional shares, zero commission, 24/5 (pre/regular/after-hours), cash dividends paid in USDC, stock dividends/splits reflected, voting rights planned later.
- Restricted jurisdictions for Bybit generally include the US, mainland China, Hong Kong, Singapore, Canada, etc. — so "US stocks" are *not* available to US residents.

**[4] Competitive landscape.**
- **Real equities via broker rails (closest comp):** Bybit's new model mirrors trii/Alpaca and Robinhood's traditional US brokerage — but delivered to crypto users in USDC.
- **Tokenized stocks (adjacent, different legal wrapper):** Kraken xStocks (100+ names, Backed Finance 1:1, Solana; $25B+ volume since Jun 2025, targeting 500+); Robinhood EU tokenized stocks (~2,000 tokens, but *derivative contracts*, not underlying shares); Gemini (Dinari dShares, 1:1). Telegram Wallet tokenized stocks via Kraken.
- **Differentiator:** vs tokenized/synthetic rivals, Bybit stresses *direct economic ownership* of the underlying security (via Alpaca custody) — a stronger investor-protection claim than token or CFD wrappers. IMF has flagged tokenized stocks as ~1.5x more volatile; Bybit's real-share route sidesteps that critique.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. "Real stocks, not tokenized" — but the PR *also* says Bybit "tokenizes real-world assets at scale … today" and lists tokenized equities as a separate product. Is the new Stocks service genuinely direct-custody equities via Alpaca, or is messaging blurring two distinct products?
2. If securities are custodied at Alpaca Securities LLC (a *US* FINRA/SIPC broker-dealer) but the service is explicitly *not* offered to US residents, what is the exact legal path by which non-US users hold US-cleared securities through an offshore (Mauritius) introducing broker? Does SIPC coverage actually extend to these foreign clients?
3. What is the full list of *eligible* jurisdictions? The PR only says "permitted jurisdictions"; the base/web give restricted lists but no positive eligibility list. How large is the real addressable user base after excluding US, China, HK, Singapore, Canada, EEA-global-platform, etc.?
4. USDC-only funding: who bears FX/settlement risk when buying USD-denominated shares with USDC, and how is the USD↔USDC conversion priced (hidden spread vs "zero commission")?
5. "Zero commission" — where is the revenue? Payment-for-order-flow via Alpaca? Spread on USDC conversion? Interest on USDC balances / float?
6. Dividends paid "in USDC converted from USD" — what withholding tax treatment applies to non-US holders of US equities (typically 30% US dividend withholding), and is that disclosed?
7. Voting rights are "planned for a future phase" — so at launch holders have economic but not governance rights. Is that materially different from beneficial ownership claims, and how are shares held (street name / omnibus)?
8. Counterparty/concentration risk: the entire equity offering depends on Alpaca (execution + clearing + custody). What happens to user positions if Alpaca has an outage, loses a license, or fails? (Alpaca is a venture-funded startup, not a legacy custodian.)
9. Mauritius FSC (Infra Capital Ltd) as the regulated entity — how robust is that oversight vs a tier-1 regulator for retail securities, and does it create investor-recourse gaps?
10. Does offering US equities to a global retail base via an offshore structure invite the same regulatory scrutiny that pushed Bybit out of the EEA under MiCA? Could the US SEC view this as evading US-person restrictions if geofencing leaks?
11. Is "24/5 trading incl. pre/after-hours" exposing retail users to thin-liquidity, wide-spread sessions, especially when funding in a volatile stablecoin environment?
12. How does this overlap/cannibalize Bybit's existing tokenized xStocks and TradFi perpetuals — does holding three equity-exposure products confuse users on legal rights and custody?
13. Corporate actions "may be restricted at this time" (per disclaimer) — which actions are unsupported, and does that degrade the "full ownership" pitch?
14. Numbers check: is "1,000+ US stocks and ETFs" at launch, or aspirational? Web sources consistently say 1,000+, matching the PR — but catalog vs tradable-now may differ.
15. Is there any independent (non-PR) confirmation of live trading and user funds flowing, or is coverage entirely press-release-derived as of 2026-10-13?

**Importance: 4/5** — A major, concrete product launch by a top-tier (80M-user) crypto exchange that moves beyond the tokenized-stock narrative into *real* US equities via a TradFi broker (Alpaca), settled in USDC. It materially advances crypto↔TradFi convergence, has clear competitive stakes (Kraken xStocks, Robinhood, Gemini), and names verifiable infrastructure (Alpaca, Infra Capital/FSC Mauritius). Not a 5 because it excludes the US and other large markets, rides proven rails (Alpaca already powers trii), and is so far documented almost entirely via Bybit's own PR without independent volume/adoption data.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Crypto-exchange → equities convergence is the defining 2026 "super-app" race: exchanges are bolting US-stock access onto crypto wallets to lift ARPU and lock in users. Note that two distinct models have emerged — (a) **real-share brokerage** (actual securities held in custody, routed via a US broker-dealer: Coinbase, Binance, now Bybit via Alpaca) and (b) **tokenized equities / stock tokens** (on-chain synthetic or debt-wrapped exposure: Robinhood EU, Crypto.com, Securitize, OKX's SEC filing). Bybit explicitly straddles both — this launch is the real-share path ("economic interest in the underlying securities … not derivatives or synthetic"), while it *also* already runs tokenized equities + TradFi perps via MT5. Market size: real figure is participation, not TAM — Bybit claims 80m+ users (per PR, Bybit, 2026-10-12); the exchanges competing for them traded ~$18.7tn in 2026 cumulatively across top venues (per MEXC/CoinGecko compilations, 2026). Why now: SEC Jan-2026 guidance drew a line between issuer-sponsored tokenized securities and third-party synthetic/custodial products (the latter facing stricter scrutiny), making the *real-share* route the lower-regulatory-risk way for exchanges to add equities — which is precisely the lane Bybit chose here (per CryptoSlate/SEC guidance, 2026-01).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: trading volume / market share, user count, take rate (here compressed — zero commission), AUC. Basis of competition: breadth of symbols, funding rail (USDC vs USD), geography of eligibility, and whether exposure is real vs synthetic. Peer moves (dated): **Binance** launched US-equities trading 2026-06-01 with 7,000+ symbols + previewed tokenized "bStocks" (per Yellow, 2026). **Coinbase** rolled out 24/5 US stock trading + Yahoo Finance discovery tie-up (News [[Coinbase launches 24 7 US stock trading with Yahoo Finance]], 2026-02). **Crypto.com** launched tokenized stocks (~1,500 underlyings, from $1) Aug-2026 (per Crypto.com/PRNewswire). **Kraken** added 24/7 perpetuals on tokenized US stocks (News [[Kraken rolls out 24 7 perpetuals for tokenized US stocks]], 2026-02). **OKX** filed with the SEC (OKXICE, ~63 companies) and **Securitize** put Apple/NVIDIA/Tesla on Solana with NYSE trading planned, both Oct-2026 (per Bloomberg/CoinDesk, 2026-10). Bybit's position: *catching up, not leading* — it is #2 CEX by volume (10.0% of top-10 CEX volume Q2'26; $1.5tn / 8.1% share FY2025, per CoinGecko/coinlaw) but a late mover on real-share equities (Binance ~4 months ahead, with 7× the symbol count: 7,000+ vs 1,000+). Moat `(analysis)`: distribution/scale (80m users) + unified USDC settlement across crypto+equity, not product differentiation — the equities product itself is outsourced.

**Comps & multiples.** Key dependency = **Alpaca**, the broker-dealer/custodian doing the actual securities work (News [[Alpaca raises 150M Series D at 1.15B valuation]] — $1.15bn post-money, 2026-01). Valuation anchors for the crypto-exchange cohort (private, so use round/implied, not market cap): **Kraken** $20bn (News [[Kraken raises 800m at 20bn valuation]], 2026-01). Bybit's own valuation is not publicly disclosed → `[UNSOURCED]`; EV/Revenue and P/E **not computable** (Bybit is private, revenue undisclosed — "no data"). Price-per-user is directional only: at Kraken's $20bn vs Bybit's 80m users a like-for-like isn't valid (different user bases) → distribution not computed, qualitative only. Flag: this is a competitive-landscape story, not a valuation re-rating — **no trading multiple can be verified for the protagonist**.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory / jurisdictional opacity.** The brokerage is **Infra Capital Limited** (introducing broker, regulated by the FSC **Mauritius**, GB23202169), with US clearing/custody via **Alpaca Securities LLC** (FINRA/SIPC). "Eligible global users in permitted jurisdictions" — i.e. *not* the US, and the perimeter is undefined. Mauritius-licensed introducing broker funneling retail into US equities invites host-regulator questions; peers' tokenized variants are already under EU probes (Bank of Lithuania on Robinhood, 2026). Second-order: eligibility/geography can be curtailed post-launch, capping the addressable base.
2. **Economics outsourced, margin thin.** Zero-commission + Alpaca does execution, self-clearing and custody → Bybit captures little direct equity-trading revenue; monetization is really USDC float / cross-sell / spreads, which the PR is silent on. Disintermediation risk: the broker (Alpaca) and the stablecoin layer capture economics; Bybit is the front-end. Second-order: if this is a loss-leader for engagement, equities add cost, not profit.
3. **Counterparty / custody concentration on one broker.** Entire US-securities stack (routing, clearing, custody) sits with Alpaca, a $1.15bn-valuation private firm — single point of failure for a product Bybit markets under its own brand. Compounded by Bybit's own track record (Feb-2025 hack drove share from 10%→6% before recovery, per coinlaw). Voting rights "planned for a future phase" = holders get economic-only exposure today.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The CEX field is now bifurcating: real-share brokerage (compliance-safer, outsourced to Alpaca-type rails) vs tokenized/on-chain (higher margin, higher regulatory beta). Bybit hedges by running *both* — a defensive catch-up to Binance/Coinbase rather than a new entry. Falsifiable thesis: equities become table-stakes for top-5 CEXs within ~12 months, so the edge accrues not to the exchanges (commoditized front-ends) but to the **brokerage-infra layer (Alpaca)** and the **settlement stablecoin (USDC/Circle)**. Trigger to watch: whether Bybit discloses equities volume/AUC in its next recap (it did not quantify at launch); what breaks the thesis: a regulator forcing geo-restrictions that make the product uneconomic, or Bybit securing its own broker-dealer license (vertical integration) and re-capturing margin.
