## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
[0] **What happened.** SBI Cards and Payment Services (BSE: 543066, SBICARD — India's only listed pure-play credit-card issuer, majority-owned by State Bank of India) reported Q2 FY27 (quarter ended 30 Sep 2026) consolidated net profit of ₹778cr (~$82M), up 75% YoY from ₹445cr a year earlier. Total revenue rose a muted ~3% YoY to ₹5,302cr. The profit surge is almost entirely a credit-cost story, not a revenue story: impairment/bad-debt expense fell 37% YoY to ₹819cr. Results were released 23 Oct 2026. ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/markets/stocks/earnings/sbi-cards-q2-profit-jumps-75-yoy-to-rs-778-crore-as-bad-loan-costs-fall-sharply/articleshow/135147815.cms), [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/sbi-cards-and-payment-services-reports-778-crore-net-profit-in-q2-fy27-total-revenue-up-3-year-on-year))

[1] **The number behind the number — credit cost normalisation.** Gross credit cost fell 342 bps YoY to 5.6%. Gross NPA improved to 1.90% (down 95 bps YoY, 14 bps QoQ) — the first time below the 2% threshold since the pandemic; net NPA at 0.77%. ROA jumped to 4.4% (+180 bps YoY) and ROE to 18.4% (+631 bps YoY). Management flagged credit costs easing to "post-COVID lows." The improvement caps a cleanup cycle: in Q1 FY26 (reported Aug 2025) profit was actually DOWN 6.4% as provisions rose 23% YoY to ₹1,352cr ([[SBI Cards posts $64 million net profit, down 6.4%]]), and in May 2026 the issuer offloaded a ₹1,800cr bad-loan portfolio to Integro Finserv ([[SBI Cards sells ₹1,800 crore bad-loan portfolio to Integro Finserv]]) — both direct precursors to today's lower-provision, cleaner-book print. ([GuruFocus earnings call](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9140311/sbi-cards-and-payment-services-ltd-bom543066-q2-2027-earnings-call-highlights-profit-jumps-75-as-credit-costs-ease-to-postcovid-lows), [scanx](https://scanx.trade/stock-market-news/companies/sbi-cards-q2-results-gross-stage-3-improves-1-90-2-04-qoq/54292964))

[2] **Volume side is tepid.** Cards-in-force grew 8% YoY to 2.32cr (23.2M). Card spends rose only ~3% YoY to ₹1,10,689cr. Receivables stood at ₹58,376cr. Portfolio yield 16.1%, NIM 10.8%, cost of funds 6.8% (up 18 bps QoQ). So the earnings beat is margin/provisioning-driven; top-line growth (revenue +3%, spends +3%) is soft and roughly tracking card-base growth minus churn — this is a profitability-quality story, not an acceleration story. Context: India card spends have been running stronger at the system level ([[India credit card spending rises 14% to Rs 1.92 lakh crore]]), implying SBI Cards is under-indexing the industry's spend growth, likely a deliberate consequence of tighter underwriting during the cleanup.

[3] **Why it matters.** SBI Cards is a bellwether for India's unsecured-retail-credit cycle. The swing from provision-hit FY24-FY26 quarters to sub-2% GNPA signals the RBI's 2023-24 risk-weight tightening and the sector-wide unsecured-lending stress may be rolling over. An 18.4% ROE with a cleaned book re-rates the stock's growth-vs-quality trade-off. But the quality of the beat is the open question: revenue +3% means EPS growth is being "manufactured" by provision reversal/decline, which is not repeatable once credit costs hit a floor (~5.6% already near post-COVID lows). ([Investing.com slides](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/sbi-card-q2-fy27-slides-profit-surges-75-asset-quality-improves-93CH-4968873))

[4] **Watch next.** (a) Whether the next quarter sustains sub-2% GNPA or whether it's a one-off helped by the FY26 write-off/sell-down lowering the denominator stress; (b) the additional management overlay — ₹30cr added in Q2, total overlay ₹100cr as of 30 Sep 2026 — and whether it's released (further boosting profit) or retained; (c) spends/receivables re-acceleration now that underwriting may loosen; (d) cost of funds trajectory (+18 bps QoQ) squeezing the 10.8% NIM; (e) SBI parent dynamics and any fresh RBI action on unsecured credit. ([Kalkine](https://kalkinemedia.com/in/news/announcements/sbi-cards-q2-fy27-results-strong-75-pat-growth-and-improved-asset-quality))

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
1. Is the 75% PAT jump "real" earnings growth or a provision-reversal artefact? Revenue +3% vs profit +75% screams the latter — how much of the delta is just the ₹819cr vs prior-year impairment line?
2. The YoY base (₹445cr in Q2 FY26) was itself depressed by elevated provisions. Is the comparison flattered by a weak base rather than genuine strength?
3. GNPA below 2% "first time since COVID" — how much is organic recovery vs the May 2026 ₹1,800cr bad-loan sale to Integro and FY26 write-offs mechanically shrinking the NPA numerator?
4. Credit cost is already near post-COVID lows (5.6%, down 342 bps). Where is further profit upside once provisioning normalises — is the easy gain largely banked?
5. Card spends +3% and revenue +3% are soft vs ~14% system-level India card-spend growth. Is SBI Cards ceding market share, and is that a deliberate underwriting choice or competitive weakness?
6. Cost of funds rose 18 bps QoQ to 6.8%. If rates stay sticky, does the 10.8% NIM compress and offset the credit-cost tailwind?
7. The ₹100cr management overlay (₹30cr added this quarter) — is it being built or released? A future release would inflate profit without operational improvement; quantify the sensitivity.
8. Receivables ₹58,376cr with only 8% card-base growth — is receivables growth stalling, and what does that imply for forward NII?
9. $ conversions in the note body (₹778cr≈$82M) — are FX rates consistent and dated, or mixing spot rates across quarters?
10. Does "consolidated net profit ₹778cr" match the standalone figure cited elsewhere, and are all third-party summaries (ET, FPJ, Kalkine, scanx) reconciling to the same official filing/investor presentation?
11. Is the RBI unsecured-credit risk-weight regime stable, or could a fresh regulatory tightening reverse the asset-quality trajectory mid-cycle?
12. Revenue reported variously as ₹5,302cr (total) vs ₹5,141cr (from operations, +4%) across sources — which basis is correct and is the note conflating them?
13. How sustainable is 18.4% ROE if it rests on cyclically low credit costs rather than structurally higher yields or operating leverage?
14. Any divergence between gross Stage-3 (1.90%) and underlying roll rates / restructured book that management isn't foregrounding?

Importance: 3/5 — Large, listed, systemically-watched Indian credit-card bellwether and a clean read on the turn in India's unsecured-credit cycle; but it's a single quarterly result where the headline (+75%) overstates a provision-driven, low-growth (revenue/spends +3%) print, limiting its standalone significance.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** India is a consolidated credit-card market: domestic lenders HDFC Bank, SBI Card, ICICI Bank and Axis Bank command >80% of spending (per market-share coverage, via whalesbook.com, Oct 2026). Industry spends run well above ₹2 lakh crore/month (credit-card spends touched ~₹2.0tn in a single month in 2025, per Business Standard). The defining driver this cycle is regulation: the RBI's Nov-2023 hike in risk weights on unsecured retail/credit-card exposures (to 125%) has compressed growth — new-card issuance decelerated from >20% YoY to ~4% YoY by Aug 2025 (per Business Standard/ABF coverage). Why now: the industry is at the back-end of a bad-debt cycle — issuers front-loaded provisions through 2024–25 as delinquencies rose, and the current profit surge is largely the *unwind* of that (lower credit costs), not revenue acceleration.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for a monoline issuer: cards-in-force (CIF) & market share, retail/total spends & spends share, gross NPA / Stage-3, credit cost (impairment as % of receivables), ROA/ROE. Cards-in-force by issuer (~June 2026, per market-share coverage): HDFC Bank ~26.8m (~22%), SBI Card ~22.6m, ICICI ~19.5m, Axis ~16.2m. SBI Card reports CIF of 23.2m (+8% YoY), CIF market share ~18.5%, spends share ~19.0% FY27-to-date (up from 18.1% FY26), total spends ₹118,475cr (+27% YoY), retail spends ₹94,033cr (+14%) (per SBI Card Q2 FY27 slides, via Investing.com). Position: #2 issuer and the only large listed *monoline* — unlike HDFC/ICICI/Axis, cards are its entire P&L, so it has no deposit franchise or diversified book to cushion the unsecured cycle (moat = SBI parent distribution / branch reach, `(analysis)`; weakness = pure-play concentration).

**Comps & multiples.** SBICARD (listed): share ₹545.55, market cap ₹53,692cr (~$6.4bn) as of 2026-10-23; P/E 24.78, P/B 3.41, ROA 3.79% (per Tickertape/GuruFocus, Oct 2026). On the reported Q2 PAT of ₹778cr (~₹3,100cr annualized run-rate), P/E ~24–25x is consistent. HDFC/ICICI/Axis are diversified banks, so their blended P/E (typically ~15–20x) is *not* a clean comp for a monoline card issuer — distribution not computed, qualitative only. Internal comps from the base:
- [[SBI Cards posts $64 million net profit, down 6.4%]] (2025-08) — the trough: profit *falling* on elevated bad-debt costs, the mirror image of this quarter.
- [[SBI Cards sells ₹1,800 crore bad-loan portfolio to Integro Finserv]] (2026-05) — the clean-up: ARC sale of stressed book that precedes the Q2 asset-quality improvement.
- [[India credit card spending rises 14% to Rs 1.92 lakh crore]] (2025-10) — sector spends growth ~14% YoY, roughly matching SBI's retail-spend growth, i.e. SBI is tracking, not outrunning, the market.
In-line / not cheap: ~25x P/E prices in the credit-cost normalization already; little margin for disappointment.

**Risk flags.**
1. *Earnings quality — profit is a provision unwind, not growth.* The 75% PAT jump came from impairment falling to ₹819cr from ₹1,293cr YoY while revenue rose only ~3% (per ET/APAC). Once credit costs fully normalize, YoY PAT growth mechanically collapses — the beat is not repeatable at this rate.
2. *Regulatory overhang on the whole model.* RBI's 125% risk weight raises capital intensity specifically for unsecured/card lending; any re-tightening or a fresh delinquency wave hits a monoline harder than a diversified bank.
3. *Concentration / thin revenue line.* ~3% revenue growth against 27% spends growth signals take-rate/NII compression (revolver mix down, reward costs up) — the P&L leans on the one lever (credit cost) that has now largely played out.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a late-cycle re-rating on asset-quality mean-reversion, not a growth story `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: if gross NPA stabilizes around ~1.9% and revolver/NII mix recovers, FY28 earnings can hold; it breaks if either (a) a renewed unsecured-delinquency uptick reverses credit costs, or (b) revenue stays stuck near low-single-digit growth, exposing the profit as a one-off. Watch: next-quarter credit cost vs this ₹819cr base, and whether revenue growth reconnects with spend growth.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Reported Q2 FY27 (quarter ended 30 Sep 2026), consolidated.** SBI Cards & Payment Services (NSE: SBICARD / BSE: 543066) posted its unaudited results on 23 Oct 2026.

**Headline: profit jump driven almost entirely by provision normalisation, not revenue.**

- **Net profit (PAT):** ₹778 cr (₹777.60 cr), +74.8% / ~+75% YoY vs ₹445 cr in Q2 FY26, and +17% QoQ vs ₹664 cr in Q1 FY27. The surge is a credit-cost story, not a topline story.
- **Total income / revenue:** ₹5,302 cr, up only ~3% YoY vs ₹5,136 cr. Interest income ₹2,511 cr (+4% QoQ); fees and other revenue ~₹2,630 cr. Revenue growth is muted — spends and receivables are growing slowly.
- **Profitability:** ROAA 4.4% (vs 2.6% in Q2 FY26); ROAE 18.4% (vs 12.1%). The margin expansion comes from the collapse in loan-loss provisions.

**The driver — credit cost / provisions:**
- Impairment losses & bad-debt expense fell ~37% YoY to ₹819 cr (vs ~₹1,300 cr year-ago).
- Gross credit cost dropped to 5.6% (annualised) from ~9.0% a year ago (down ~342 bps YoY) and from 6.5% in Q1 FY27 — i.e. credit costs are now easing toward post-COVID lows per management commentary.

**Asset quality (improving sharply):**
- GNPA 1.90% of gross advances (vs 2.85% a year ago; ~2.04% in Q1 FY27 → QoQ improvement).
- NNPA 0.77% (vs 1.29% a year ago).

**Business/volume metrics (soft):**
- Cards-in-force 2.32 crore (23.2 M), +8% YoY.
- Card spends ₹1,10,689 cr for the quarter, +3% YoY — spend growth has decelerated and is a watch item.
- New accounts ~11.06 lakh added, a second consecutive quarter above 1 M.
- Total balance-sheet size ₹71,102 cr (vs ₹66,328 cr at 31 Mar 2026).

**Beat / miss vs consensus:** No clean, dated sell-side consensus poll was retrievable for this quarter, so a formal beat/miss call cannot be grounded. On substance the print is better-than-feared on asset quality (GNPA 1.90% and credit cost 5.6% both improving sequentially and YoY), but the 75% PAT jump flatters a low base (Q2 FY26 ₹445 cr) and is almost wholly provision-led — treat it as a quality-driven beat rather than an earnings-power beat.

**Thesis risks:**
- *Credit-cycle dependence:* the entire profit delta is lower provisions; any re-acceleration in unsecured-retail stress (the segment regulators keep flagging) reverses this fast.
- *RBI risk weights:* elevated risk weights on credit-card / unsecured consumer exposure constrain growth and keep capital-cost pressure on.
- *Topline anaemia:* ~3% YoY revenue and spend growth shows the franchise is growing slowly; sustaining 75%-type PAT growth is impossible once the provision base normalises — YoY comps get much harder from here.
- *Competition:* UPI-linked RuPay credit on UPI and bank co-brand competition pressure spend share and fee economics.
