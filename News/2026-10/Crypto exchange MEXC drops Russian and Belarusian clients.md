# Crypto exchange MEXC drops Russian and Belarusian clients

> [!info] 2026-10-21 · 1 упоминаний · 0 источника(ов) с текстом
> Каналы: Финтехно

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Финтехно] Вторая за пять дней крупная международная криптобиржа отказалась от клиентов из России и Беларуси — MEXC. Единой даты ограничений для пользователей нет, биржа обещает прислать персональные уведомления всем, кого это затронет. Первой на прошлой неделе была Bitget. Потенциально спрос может приземлиться на российские банки и будущие регулируемые криптоплощадки, но заменить международные биржи по ассортименту криптовалют, торговым инструментам, ликвидности и тарифам будет проблематично как минимум в первое время. Получается, что российские криптоинвесторы стремительно теряют доступ к части глобальной торговой инфраструктуры ещё до того, как заработает полноценная отечественная альтернатива. Интересно, что глобально тренд шире России и СНГ. В сентябре после девяти лет работы объявила о полном закрытии CoinEx, сославшись на падение торговых объёмов, сокращение ликвидности и рост регуляторных расходов. MEXC параллельно сворачивает работу в Нидерландах, а Bitget ранее прекратила обслуживание

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd).** On **Tuesday 2026-10-20** MEXC — a top-tier global exchange (self-reported ~40M users, consistently top-5 by spot/derivatives volume) — announced it will **stop serving users in Russia and Belarus** "as part of our ongoing efforts to enhance global compliance / to comply with international regulatory requirements." No single hard cutoff date was published: affected accounts get **individual notices** setting per-account deadlines to close positions and withdraw. MEXC disclosed **no number of affected accounts, no Russia/Belarus volume, and named no specific regulator, licence action or sanctions order** as the trigger — the "global compliance" line is PR padding over a sanctions-driven de-risk. The move lands **days after Bitget** did effectively the same thing (Bitget Terms update 2026-10-16; trade-freeze 10-18; forced-liquidation/lockout 10-21) — see [[Bitget fully halts service to Russian residents]] — making MEXC the **second major international exchange to offboard Russia/Belarus inside ~5 days**. Why structured this way: individual-notice offboarding (vs one public deadline) lets MEXC triage by risk/KYC flag and avoid a single headline "exit date," softening the optics of a sanctions retreat while still clearing the exposure.

**[1] Competitors / peers (prior exchange exits, dated).** The Russia offshore-venue exodus is multi-year: **Binance** agreed to sell its entire Russian book to newly-created **CommEX** on 2023-09-27 (no revenue split, no buy-back, ~1-year offboard) amid a US DOJ sanctions probe — CommEX itself shut in **April 2024**. **OKX** pulled the ruble from P2P (2023); **OKX + Bybit** delisted sanctioned Russian bank cards (Sber/Tinkoff/Alfa) from P2P rails (2023–24) but **stopped short of full exit** — Bybit stayed the de-facto default for Russian traders (reported ~26–27% of traffic, 2024–25). **CoinEx** fully shut down globally on 2026-09-15 (spot ends 09-29, withdrawals to 12-22), citing falling volume, thinning liquidity and rising compliance cost. Position: MEXC + Bitget mark an **escalation from "trim the payment rails" to "prohibit the jurisdiction."** Second-order: the trigger is increasingly **secondary-sanctions + MiCA licensing exposure**, not Russian law — why MEXC/Bitget both bundle **Belarus** (and Bitget added Kazakhstan), jurisdictions outside Russia's own crypto regime.

**[2] Company history / fit.** MEXC has itself been in a **compliance-driven EU retreat**: on **2026-09-29** it announced a phased wind-down in the **Netherlands** (MiCA/CASP transition ended 2026-07-01; MEXC lacks ESMA CASP authorisation), offboarding Dutch users in four batches (last action ~Oct 23, auto-close of futures from Oct 24, assets to a regulated custodian by 2026-11-16) and — per a **2026-08-12 commercial partnership** — **referring its Dutch clients to Kraken** (MiCA+MiFID authorised); it separately steered Dutch users to **Bybit EU**. Structural logic: once MEXC committed to a licensed EU footprint, continuing to serve sanctioned-adjacent Russian/Belarusian flow is incompatible with that track — the Russia/Belarus cut is the same compliance-perimeter decision as the Netherlands exit, applied to the sanctions axis rather than the licensing axis.

**[3] Novelty / value-add / traction.** Genuinely a **new event** (MEXC-specific; distinct company and ~4-day-later announcement from the Bitget note). What's materially new: Russia's own onshore regime is now live (law №282-ФЗ core rules effective 2026-09-01; infrastructure admission from Oct-2026; CBR banned regulated-account→self-custody transfers 2026-10-12; direct P2P ban slated 2027-07-01), so each offshore exit no longer just "loses" Russian retail — it **channels it toward the bank-controlled domestic rail** (Sber/T/VTB and newly-named depositaries/exchangers). Traction caveat: these are **compliance postures, not technical walls** — Russian users can VPN/relabel and migrate to whoever stays (Bybit, KuCoin) or to P2P/grey venues. The real value destroyed is **legitimate, liquid offshore access**, not total access.

**[4] What's next / market sentiment.** Expect the squeeze to continue: MiCA's July-2026 CASP gate + the EU sanctions stack (19th package 2025-10-23 banned Russia-based crypto payment providers and the A7A5 ruble stablecoin; the HTX transaction ban took effect 2026-08-23) make serving Russia a one-way exit for any exchange seeking EU legitimacy. Falsifiable read (analysis): within ~6–12 months ≥1 more top-10 offshore venue formally restricts Russia (watch **Bybit/OKX**, who so far only trimmed rails); breaks if a major peer instead *expands* Russian access to absorb the exiting users, which would reframe MEXC/Bitget as firm-specific compliance rather than a sector-wide re-rating. Counterintuitive second-order: the faster the offshore walls go up, the stronger the **demand pull** onto Russia's nascent licensed exchanges and P2P — i.e. the sanctions pressure is effectively *subsidising* the Russian onshore build-out it was meant to isolate.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

**Red-team / second-order questions**

1. **Is this genuinely new or a duplicate of the Bitget note?** New. Different company (MEXC vs Bitget), announced ~2026-10-20 vs Bitget's 2026-10-16 terms update. The Bitget note ([[Bitget fully halts service to Russian residents]]) is a **sibling event in the same trend**, not the same event. **freshness=fresh.**

2. **Is "global compliance" the real reason, or PR?** PR padding. MEXC named no regulator/order. Real drivers (analysis): secondary-sanctions exposure + MiCA/CASP licensing track — the same logic behind its Netherlands exit. Bundling Belarus (outside Russian law) confirms it's a sanctions/compliance perimeter call, not a response to Russian regulation.

3. **Why no single cutoff date — only "individual notices"?** Lets MEXC triage offboarding by KYC/risk flag and avoid one headline "exit date," softening the optics while still clearing exposure. Also means affected-user count stays undisclosed.

4. **How many Russian/Belarusian users/how much volume is affected?** Open — undisclosed. MEXC self-reports ~40M global users; Russia share not broken out. Reputationally larger than financially (as with Bitget).

5. **Can users just VPN and keep trading?** Yes in practice — enforcement is KYC/geo-IP, not a hard wall. Expect migration to Bybit/KuCoin/P2P. The cut removes *legitimate liquid* access, not total access.

6. **Why does this matter beyond one exchange?** It's the **second top-tier exit in ~5 days** (after Bitget), signalling a trend-shift from "trim payment rails" (OKX/Bybit 2023–24) to "prohibit the jurisdiction." Russian retail is losing offshore infrastructure before a full domestic alternative is live.

7. **Does the raw note's "second in five days / first was Bitget" check out?** Yes — Bitget 10-16/10-20, MEXC 10-20. (Raw note says "first was Bitget"; some outlets/raw text earlier referenced "Bitget first.") Confirmed consistent.

8. **Does the raw note's CoinEx claim hold?** Yes — CoinEx announced global shutdown 2026-09-15 (spot ends 09-29, withdrawals to 12-22), citing falling volume/liquidity + rising compliance cost. Corroborated.

9. **Does the raw note's "MEXC winding down Netherlands" hold?** Yes — MEXC announced phased Dutch wind-down 2026-09-29 (MiCA/CASP), move assets by 10-31, offboard 11-16, referring clients to Kraken (partnership 2026-08-12) / Bybit EU. Corroborated.

10. **What's the EU sanctions backdrop — is "19th package" right?** Partly. EU 19th package (2025-10-23) banned Russia-based crypto payment providers + A7A5 ruble stablecoin; HTX transaction ban effective 2026-08-23 (later package). The sibling Bitget note cites a "20th package" May-2026 crypto-dealing ban. Net: a multi-package stack, not one trigger — treat specific package numbers with caution.

11. **Who benefits competitively?** Short term: whoever stays (Bybit dominant for RU, KuCoin) + P2P/grey venues. Medium term: Russia's bank-controlled onshore rail (Sber/T/VTB, newly-named depositaries/exchangers) — see [[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]], [[SPB Exchange plans crypto spot trading and settlement depositary]].

12. **Counterintuitive second-order effect?** Faster offshore walls → stronger demand pull onto Russia's nascent licensed exchanges + P2P. Sanctions pressure effectively subsidises the onshore build-out it meant to isolate. (analysis/hypothesis)

13. **Could MEXC partially re-enter via a licensed RU entity?** Conceivable under Russia's new intermediary regime but no signal of it; incompatible with its EU-licensing posture.

14. **Forced-liquidation / client-harm risk?** Present — like Bitget, unclosed positions face auto-close on notice deadlines, creating slippage/stranded-balance risk for leveraged users. "Funds safe" refers to withdrawal, not liquidation pricing.

15. **Verification confidence?** High on the core event (Moscow Times, crypto.news, CoinGape, FinanceFeeds all corroborate 10-20 RU+BY exit, individual-notice mechanism, no disclosed count). Medium on exact EU-package numbering.

**Importance: 3/5 — a real, well-corroborated escalation (2nd major exchange in ~5 days to prohibit Russia/Belarus, tightly tied to MiCA + the EU sanctions stack and Russia's hardening onshore regime), genuinely material for the Russia/crypto-regulation beat. Capped at 3 rather than 4 because it closely follows and largely echoes the already-published Bitget exit ([[Bitget fully halts service to Russian residents]], importance 4) — MEXC confirms the trend more than it breaks new ground, and group-level financial impact is undisclosed/immaterial.**

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Crypto-exchange *access* for Russian retail/HNW users — a shrinking cross-border segment, not a sized market here (no verifiable Russia-specific volume TAM — "no data"). MEXC's exit (effective 2026-10-20, per The Moscow Times) is the second major international venue to drop Russia/Belarus in five days, after Bitget added both (plus Kazakhstan) to its prohibited list on 2026-10-17 [[Bitget fully halts service to Russian residents]]. Structure: historically Russian crypto demand routed through a few large global order-book venues (liquidity, altcoin breadth, low fees) plus sanctioned domestic rails (Garantex, seized 2025-03; US+EU sanctions on successor Grinex, 2025-08, per Chainalysis/CyberScoop). Barriers to a compliant domestic replacement are regulatory, not technical: Russia's Experimental Legal Regime (ELR) restricts legal crypto trading to "super-qualified investors" — assets >RUB 100m (~$1.2m) or income >RUB 50m/yr (~$602k) (per CoinCentral, Mar 2026), i.e. a tiny addressable base vs the retail flow now being orphaned. Driver / why-now: secondary-sanctions and compliance-cost pressure on global venues (CoinEx cited falling volumes + regulatory costs when it fully shut in Sept 2025) — the economics of serving Russian users turned negative faster than a licensed local alternative can stand up.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for an exchange: spot/derivatives volume, liquidity depth, listed-asset breadth, take rate (fees), and — for this segment — fiat on/off-ramp coverage. Key players by role: (a) retreating global venues — MEXC, Bitget, CoinEx (closed); (b) still-serving global venues with P2P rails — Bybit, OKX, plus aggregator BestChange, which is where Garantex-era flow migrated (per TRM/updateprime); (c) sanctioned domestic shadow rails — Grinex/A7A5 (>$2bn bidirectional on-chain exposure, per Chainalysis Aug 2025); (d) nascent *regulated* domestic entrants — SPB Exchange's planned crypto spot + settlement depositary [[SPB Exchange plans crypto spot trading and settlement depositary]] and the first named Russian crypto-market depositaries/exchangers [[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]]. Basis of competition shifts from price/breadth/liquidity (global venues' edge) to *regulatory access* (who can legally serve Russians at all). MEXC's position: a departing incumbent for this geography — its exit removes supply, it doesn't win share. Recent moves: MEXC simultaneously winding down the Netherlands (per note), confirming a broader compliance-driven jurisdiction pruning, not a one-off.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/revenue disclosed for MEXC (privately held, opaque) → trading multiples = **no data** / `[UNSOURCED]`; this is an access/regulation event, not a deal. Qualitative comps (all internal, same Russia-crypto-access theme, Oct 2026): [[Bitget fully halts service to Russian residents]] (direct precedent, 3 days prior); [[SPB Exchange plans crypto spot trading and settlement depositary]] and [[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]] (the regulated domestic substitute being assembled). Distribution not computed — no comparable figures.

**Risk flags.**
- **Migration to sanctioned/shadow rails.** As compliant global venues exit, displaced Russian flow historically moves to P2P (Bybit/OKX/BestChange) and sanctioned successors (Grinex/A7A5) — second-order: concentrates volume on higher-risk, higher-cost rails (fees spiked ~0.1%→1.5%, ~15x, after the March 2025 Garantex action, per reporting), raising user cost and AML exposure rather than cutting activity.
- **Access gap before the substitute is live.** The ELR "super-qualified" threshold (~$1.2m assets) excludes nearly all retail; the regulated domestic alternative (SPB/depositaries) is announced, not operating — so there is a structural access vacuum, de-PR'd: "will serve Russians" is a roadmap, not a live venue.
- **Compliance-driven contagion.** MEXC exiting Russia *and* the Netherlands signals jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction de-risking; comparable venues (Bybit, OKX, Gate) could follow for Russia, compounding the supply withdrawal — concentration risk on whoever stays.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This looks like the start of a coordinated retreat of compliant international liquidity from Russia, front-running (not replacing) a licensed domestic market `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: within ~2 quarters either (a) another top-10 global venue blocks Russia/Belarus, confirming contagion, OR (b) a regulated domestic venue (SPB Exchange crypto / named depositaries) actually goes live and absorbs flow — watch which happens first. Thesis breaks if global venues quietly keep serving Russians via P2P with no further exits, i.e. the "exodus" stays cosmetic. Trigger to watch: next major-exchange jurisdiction change, and the SPB/depositary go-live date.
