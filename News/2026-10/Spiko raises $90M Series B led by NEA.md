_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Spiko (Paris/London, founded 2023 by Paul-Adrien Hyppolite & Antoine Michon) closed a **$90M Series B led by NEA at an $800M post-money valuation**, lifting total funding to **$120M** (confirmed Bloomberg/Techmeme, 2026-10-06). Participants: Index Ventures, Bpifrance, Speedinvest, Flourish Ventures, Shapers, White Star Capital, Blockwall, Frst, EQNX, Mirana Ventures, Wintermute Ventures + angels (ex-Bundesbank president Axel Weber, Qonto founders).

De-PR'd substance: Spiko is **not a stablecoin issuer** — it tokenizes shares of regulated UCITS money market funds (Smart Cash Euro / USD, now also GBP/CHF) whose underlying is short-dated Eurozone/US T-bills. The "programmable cash / AI-agent treasury" language is **roadmap, not live** ("retraits instantanés dès aujourd'hui" is live; "intérêts calculés en temps réel" and AI-agent rule execution are "bientôt/demain"). Use of proceeds: new products, geo-expansion (DE, IT, ES, NL, Nordics), hiring.

**Why framed this way:** The headline claim — "largest range of tokenized treasury funds in the world, ahead of BlackRock and Franklin Templeton (source: RWA.xyz)" — is a **narrow, sourced-but-slippery** boast: it means *by fund count / by tokenized-cash-fund category*, NOT by AUM. Spiko's $2.7B AUM sits BELOW BlackRock BUIDL (~$2.85–3B) and Circle/Hashnote USYC (~$3B). The PR anchors to "largest *tokenized cash fund issuer*" to dodge the fact that US single-fund incumbents are bigger. → The real claim is **"largest in Europe," repackaged as a global superlative.**

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **BlackRock BUIDL** (~$2.85–3B AUM, 8+ chains) — the US benchmark; single largest tokenized fund.
- **Circle / Hashnote USYC** (~$3B) — Circle acquired Hashnote Jan-2025; now vertically integrated with USDC.
- **Ondo Finance** (OUSG + USDY, ~$1.5B+) — DeFi-native, offshore/retail.
- **Franklin Templeton BENJI** (~$380M tokenized MMF) and **Goldman/BNY** (tokenized MMF rails, launched mid-2025, [[Goldman Sachs and BNY launch tokenized money market funds]]) — TradFi incumbents.
- **JPMorgan MONY** on Ethereum ([[JPMorgan launches tokenized money market fund on Ethereum]], Dec-2025) and **BNP Paribas** exploring public-chain MMF tokenization ([[BNP Paribas explores public blockchain for money market fund tokenization]], Feb-2026) — the European banks arriving.

**Position:** Spiko is the clear European-native leader but a mid-pack global player by AUM. **Why the lay of the land:** US products win on scale because US MMFs are a decades-old treasury habit and USD T-bills yield ~4% vs ~2% EUR — so the yield honey-pot is bigger in USD. Spiko's edge is distribution into an *underpenetrated* European retail/SMB base and multi-currency (EUR/GBP/CHF) UCITS wrappers that US issuers don't offer. The second-order risk: as BNP, JPM, Amundi tokenize their own MMFs, Spiko's "first-mover in Europe" moat narrows to UX/API, not product.

## [2] Company history / fit
Trajectory: launched first funds ~2023/2024 → **Series A €22M (Index Ventures + Revolut founder Nik Storonsky), July 2025**, AUM then ~$400M, ~1,000 clients ([[Index Ventures and Revolut founder back Spiko in $22M raise]]) → **March 2026: Amundi partnership (SAFO fund), reportedly $400M in 21 days** → now AUM **$2.7B (>5x in 12 months), 10,000+ clients, 25+ countries**, plus an S&P AAAf/S1+ rating on Smart Cash Euro.

**Why the company acts this way:** Take-rate on a risk-free MMF is thin (basis points), and the raw yield belongs to the saver — so Spiko's economics depend entirely on **scale of AUM × a small management fee**. That structural pressure explains the raise: it needs land-grab velocity across Europe before TradFi incumbents (Amundi — its own partner! — BNP, JPM) commoditize tokenized MMFs. The Amundi tie-up is double-edged: distribution now, a potential competitor later.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
What's genuinely real: **traction**, not technology. The tokenization of T-bill MMFs is not novel (BlackRock, Franklin, Ondo did it first). Spiko's value-add is (a) a clean API/app layer making MMFs usable as *operating cash* for European SMBs/startups, (b) multi-currency UCITS funds with an EU passport, (c) genuine AUM growth (5x YoY to $2.7B) and a top-tier S&P rating — these are hard numbers, not announcements.

**Why value-add is real but capped:** The underlying is a commodity (T-bills); the saver keeps the yield; Spiko keeps a thin fee → **the margin sits in distribution and switching costs, not the product.** Who captures margin in the stack: whoever owns the client relationship and the treasury-automation workflow. If "programmable cash / AI-agent treasury rules" ships and gets adopted, Spiko moves from "yield pipe" to "treasury OS" and earns a software multiple. If it stays a yield wrapper, it's a low-margin asset gatherer that incumbents can undercut. The $800M valuation (~0.3x AUM) prices in the OS optionality, which is **not yet demonstrated (roadmap).**

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Plans: new cash funds, local teams across Europe, real-time interest + AI-agent treasury rules. Backdrop: tokenized Treasury/MMF segment grew to ~$14–15B (from <$1B early-2024); ECB's Lagarde flagged tokenized MMFs doubling to ~€7B in 2025 ([[ECB's Lagarde argues stablecoins should not be issued privately]]) — Europe's regulators simultaneously *endorse* tokenized MMFs as collateral while *resisting* private stablecoins, which is tailwind for Spiko's model specifically. MiCA (fully applicable Dec-2024; CASP hard deadline Jul-2026) matters less directly because MMF shares are MiFID II financial instruments, not MiCA ARTs — a regulatory advantage over stablecoin rivals.

**Why the market goes this way / second-order:** Rate cuts are the key risk — Spiko's entire pitch is "cash earns ~2–4%." A sharp ECB/Fed easing cycle compresses both the saver's incentive to move and Spiko's fee base. Counterintuitively, the arrival of BNP/JPM/Amundi *validates* the category but *concentrates* eventual winners — a well-funded independent like Spiko is either an acquisition target (for a bank wanting the rails/clients) or gets squeezed. The $90M buys runway to be the one that's bought, not buried.

## Top challenge / red-team questions

1. **Is the "largest in the world, ahead of BlackRock/Franklin" claim honest?** Partly. It holds *by tokenized-cash-fund count / category*, sourced to RWA.xyz. By AUM, Spiko's $2.7B trails BUIDL (~$2.85–3B) and USYC (~$3B). → Marketing superlative; real claim is "largest in Europe."

2. **Is this a fresh event or a re-run of the Series A?** Fresh. Series A was €22M (July 2025, AUM ~$400M, [[Index Ventures and Revolut founder back Spiko in $22M raise]]). This is a new $90M Series B at a new $800M valuation with new lead (NEA) and 5x higher AUM. Different round, different terms.

3. **What is actually live vs roadmap?** Live: tokenized EUR/USD/GBP/CHF MMFs, app+API, instant withdrawals, cross-currency transfers, S&P AAAf rating. Roadmap: real-time interest accrual, AI-agent treasury-rule execution. The "programmable cash / AI" narrative is not yet live.

4. **Where does Spiko's margin come from if the saver keeps the yield?** A thin management fee on AUM. Durable only at scale; commoditizable. Open: Spiko does not disclose its fee/take-rate.

5. **Is $2.7B AUM sticky or hot money chasing rates?** Open. MMF balances are rate-sensitive operating cash; a Fed/ECB easing cycle could trigger outflows. No disclosed retention/churn figures.

6. **How much of the $2.7B is the Amundi SAFO co-branded fund vs Spiko's own funds?** SAFO reportedly hit $400M in 21 days (Mar-2026). If a large share of AUM is Amundi-dependent, the "independent leader" story weakens. Open.

7. **Is Amundi a partner or a future competitor?** Both. Amundi provides distribution now but is a €2T+ asset manager that can internalize tokenized MMFs. Structural tension.

8. **What is the $800M valuation actually pricing?** ~0.3x AUM — rich for a thin-fee asset gatherer, implying the market prices unproven "treasury-OS" optionality, not current economics.

9. **Does MiCA help or hurt Spiko?** Helps relatively. MMF shares are MiFID II instruments, not MiCA ARTs, so Spiko avoids the stablecoin reserve/authorization squeeze hitting private stablecoins (Lagarde's target, [[ECB's Lagarde argues stablecoins should not be issued privately]]).

10. **Who did tokenized MMFs first?** Not Spiko. BlackRock BUIDL, Franklin BENJI, Ondo, Goldman/BNY ([[Goldman Sachs and BNY launch tokenized money market funds]]) all predate or parallel it. Spiko's novelty is European distribution + UX, not the primitive.

11. **What happens to the thesis in a rate-cut cycle?** Core downside trigger. "Make cash earn" is only compelling at ~2–4% rates. Open — no stated hedge.

12. **Is the TradFi entry (BNP, JPM) a tailwind or a threat?** Validates the category but concentrates winners; raises the odds Spiko is acquired rather than scaled independently.

13. **Are client/geography numbers verifiable?** 10,000+ clients, 25+ countries are company-stated (blog + Bloomberg echo); no independent audit. Treat as directional.

14. **What's silent in the PR?** Fee/take-rate, revenue, burn, gross margin, AUM concentration by fund/client, and any custody/counterparty risk on the T-bill holdings.

Importance: 3/5 — A substantive, well-validated European round (NEA lead, Bloomberg-confirmed $800M valuation, genuine 5x AUM growth) in a hot, regulator-endorsed category. Marked down from 4 because the technology is not novel, the "world's largest" claim is a narrow superlative, the key value-add (programmable/AI treasury) is roadmap, and the economics (thin fee, rate-sensitive, commoditizing as TradFi enters) cap the strategic weight. Clearly the leading European tokenized-cash name, but a mid-pack global player whose moat is distribution, not product.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Spiko sits in tokenized money-market funds / tokenized cash — the largest RWA sub-segment. Tokenized treasuries/MMFs grew from under ~$1bn in early 2024 to ~$10bn by mid-2026 (per rwa.xyz, via coinpaprika/eco.com, as of May 2026); total tokenized RWA ex-stablecoins ~$22–33bn across 2026 (per rwa.xyz, via yellow.com/investax). Structure: still fragmented and early, but the top of the stack is dominated by TradFi balance sheets — BUIDL (BlackRock) ~$2.5bn, USYC ~$3.0bn, BENJI (Franklin Templeton) ~$0.83bn, OUSG (Ondo) ~$0.62bn (per rwa.xyz, as of May 2026). Why now: the $44tn of idle EU/US cash/deposits Spiko cites against 2.5% ECB / ~4% US policy rates (per Spiko blog) is only an opportunity while rates stay elevated — a rate-cut cycle compresses the entire value proposition (analysis). Entry barriers are regulatory (MMF/fund licensing, MiFID, GENIUS Act in the US) and distribution, not technology.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: AUM/encours, net yield passed to holders, client count, and AUM growth rate (Spiko: ~$2.7bn / €2.4bn, >5x in 12mo, 10,000+ clients, 25+ countries — per Spiko blog/Bloomberg). Basis of competition is distribution + yield + 24/7 programmability, not fund mechanics (the underlying is plain T-bills). Spiko's claim to be the largest *tokenized-cash-fund range* ahead of BlackRock/Franklin Templeton (per Spiko, citing rwa.xyz) is plausible on breadth of multi-currency funds (EUR/USD/GBP/CHF) but is NOT the same as largest single-fund AUM, where BUIDL/USYC lead — de-PR flag. Recent moves: JPMorgan launched a tokenized MMF on Ethereum (2025-12-16); Goldman Sachs + BNY launched tokenized MMFs (2025-07-24); Securitize listed on NYSE via SPAC (SECZ, 2026-07-02). Spiko's position: leading European-native independent (ahead in EU retail/treasury distribution) but niche vs TradFi incumbents globally (analysis). Moat = regulatory licensing + multi-currency API distribution + S&P AAAf rating on Smart Cash Euro (per Spiko); thin vs an incumbent that can bundle custody + balance sheet (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** Spiko round: $90M Series B at **$800M post/valuation** (per Bloomberg via Techmeme, 2026-10-06), $120M total raised. Price-to-AUM = `$800M / $2.7bn AUM = 0.30x` (analysis — valuation/AUM, not a revenue multiple). Note: platform revenue is a management fee on AUM (undisclosed); if fees are ~15–30bps, implied run-rate revenue ≈ `$2.7bn × 0.15–0.30% = ~$4–8M`, making the valuation ~100–200x revenue on that assumption — but the fee is `[UNSOURCED]`, so revenue multiples = no data. External comp: [[Securitize to go public via $1.25B SPAC]] — $1.25bn pre-money, >$4bn tokenized (per theblock/coindesk), i.e. ~0.31x on tokenized assets — in-line with Spiko's 0.30x. Internal round comps (smaller-stage stablecoin-yield/RWA peers): [[Agora raises $50M Series A for stablecoin banking]], [[OpenTrade secures $17 million for stablecoin yield infrastructure]], [[Osero raises $13.5 million for stablecoin yield infrastructure]], [[TownSquare raises $16.25 million for Monad DeFi money market]]. Distribution not computed (fewer than 3 comparable valuation/AUM pairs). Flag: $800M for a 2-year-old firm is rich in absolute terms but the AUM-multiple (0.30x) is in-line with Securitize — priced on growth, not current economics.

**Risk flags.**
1. Rate dependence — the entire pitch (yield on idle cash) decays as ECB/Fed cut; a sustained easing cycle shrinks both AUM inflows and the fee pool (second-order: growth-priced $800M valuation de-rates fast).
2. Disintermediation by TradFi incumbents — BlackRock/Franklin/Goldman/JPMorgan hold the balance sheet, custody and brand; an independent captures only the distribution/API layer, the thinnest-margin part of the stack.
3. Regulatory/yield-distribution risk — US GENIUS Act bars payment-stablecoin issuers from passing reserve yield; MMF-wrapper tokenized cash operates in a gray zone across jurisdictions, and multi-currency expansion multiplies the licensing surface (second-order: a single adverse ruling could wall off a target market).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Signals institutional validation of *independent* tokenized-cash distribution in Europe, not just TradFi pilots — a potential re-rating of the EU RWA-distribution layer. Falsifiable thesis: if Spiko holds >$2.7bn AUM and keeps >3x YoY growth *through* a rate-cut quarter, the model is rate-resilient; trigger/what breaks it — flat or declining AUM in the first full quarter after an ECB cut would confirm the pitch was a rate-cycle artifact.
