Strengthening Web Risk Decisions with Behavioral Biometrics 
 Behavioral biometrics gives fraud teams another source of evidence for understanding how someone is interacting with a web session. By evaluating signals like mouse movement, keyboard activity, clicks, and clipboard behavior alongside the broader risk picture, businesses can make more informed decisions about which sessions can proceed and which need stronger verification. When web evidence still isn’t enough, trusted mobile and physical-world context can help resolve the uncertainty. 
Subscribe to Incognia’s content

 Fraud teams can assess a lot about a web session. But compared with a trusted mobile interaction, due to the browsers sandboxes the web naturally provides less direct evidence about the customer, the device they’re using, and the physical context behind the session. 
 That can make an important question harder to answer: 
 What signal can make this web session trustworthy? 
 The answer rarely comes from one signal. 
 The more relevant evidence you can bring into the decision, the clearer that decision can become. 
 Behavioral biometrics adds an extra layer of protection to the picture. 
 Behavior adds another view of the web session 
 Behavioral biometrics adds another source of evidence for web risk decisions by helping fraud teams understand how someone is interacting with a web session. 
 Traditional web risk signals can provide context about the environment around a session. Behavioral signals add a different view: evidence about what is happening within the interaction itself. 
 Mouse movements, keyboard activity, clicks, and clipboard behavior can reveal patterns such as unusual cursor movement, synthetically generated events, or unexpected copy-and-paste activity. 
 That evidence can help identify patterns that may be consistent with automation or other higher-risk activity. 
 But behavioral signals are not definitive on their own. 
 A legitimate customer might copy and paste information. Their mouse movements may be unusual. One unexpected interaction does not automatically mean fraud. 
 The value comes from using behavioral evidence as one part of the broader risk decision, rather than treating any individual behavior as proof of fraud. 
 Bringing behavioral biometrics into Incognia’s web risk intelligence 
 Incognia is expanding its web risk intelligence with behavioral biometrics, giving fraud teams another source of evidence for evaluating web sessions. 
 Incognia can now assess supported behavioral signals including: 

 Mouse movement and clicks , including unusual cursor movement, spatial “teleports,” and whether mouse or click events appear to be trusted browser events or synthetically generated 


 Keyboard interaction , including keyboard activity and signals that can identify artificially generated key-down or key-up events 


 Copy, paste, and cut activity , including clipboard behavior and whether those events appear trusted or artificially generated 

 Those signals aren’t evaluated in isolation. 
 They can be assessed alongside Incognia’s existing web risk intelligence, including browser intelligence, device intelligence, integrity and tamper signals, bot and automation detection, and Verified IP Location . 
 Each answers a different question about the same interaction. 
 Behavior can tell you more about how the session is being used. 
 Browser and device intelligence provide context about the environment behind it. 
 Integrity and automation signals can indicate whether that environment or interaction may be manipulated. 
 Location evidence adds another piece of context around where the session may be originating. 
 Taken together, those signals give the business more relevant evidence for deciding what should happen next. 
 Turn more evidence into a clearer decision 
 Collecting more risk signals only helps if they improve the decision you need to make. 
 For most web interactions, that decision ultimately comes down to two paths: 
 There is enough evidence to let the session proceed. 
 Or: 
 The session requires additional verification before the action can continue. 
 That distinction matters because the alternative is often a bad tradeoff. 
 If every uncertain interaction receives additional authentication, legitimate customers inherit more friction. 
 If the business lets every ambiguous session through, it takes on more risk. 
 Correctly using this extra data intelligence layer will help reduce uncertainty and have a smaller gray area, allowing more directed friction, with less risk. 
 But there will still be sessions where web evidence alone isn’t enough. 
 When the web isn’t enough, bring the trusted device into the decision 
 Imagine a customer is attempting a higher-risk account change from the web. 
 Behavioral signals and the broader web risk assessment help the business evaluate the session. But for a sensitive action like this, the business may determine that stronger verification is required before allowing it to proceed. 
 That doesn’t mean the session is fraudulent. It means the action warrants an additional verification step. 
 Instead, it can ask the customer to take an explicit verification step on their trusted mobile device through Incognia’s Cross Device Authentication . 
 That opens up a new set of evidence. 
 Incognia can assess the customer’s trusted device relationship and device integrity, whether the mobile device and web session are physically near each other, and whether the mobile location is trusted for that customer. 
 The web session has gone from an interaction with limited physical context to a decision that can incorporate evidence tied to a trusted device and the customer’s physical world. 
 The business still makes the final risk and authorization decision. But now it has more to base that decision on. 
 What this can look like in practice 
 Behavioral signals become most useful when they add context to a specific decision. 
 A login that looks normal enough to proceed 
 A customer logs into their account from the web and moves through the session normally. 
 Mouse and keyboard activity appear to come from trusted browser events, and there are no meaningful behavioral anomalies that increase concern. 
 Behavioral evidence alone does not “prove” the customer is legitimate, but it can contribute to the broader assessment that there is enough evidence for the session to proceed. 
 A session showing signs of synthetic interaction 
 Another customer begins entering information into a web form, but some mouse or keyboard events appear to be artificially generated rather than trusted browser events. 
 The session may also show unusual cursor movement or other behavioral anomalies. 
 None of those signals automatically means fraud. 
 But together, they can give the fraud team more reason to treat the session as higher risk and require stronger verification before allowing a sensitive action to continue. 
 A higher-risk account change that needs more confidence 
 A customer attempts a sensitive account change from the web. The available behavioral evidence does not provide enough confidence to let the action proceed without another check. 
 The business can ask the customer to complete an explicit verification step on their trusted mobile device through Cross Device Authentication . 
 Incognia can then bring additional evidence into the decision, including the trusted device relationship, whether the phone and web session are physically near each other, and whether the mobile location is trusted. 
 Better web risk decisions come from connecting the evidence 
 No single signal will answer every web risk decision. 
 That’s the point. 
 Behavioral biometrics gives fraud teams another view into what is happening inside a web interaction. 
 Together with browser, device, integrity, automation, and location intelligence it provides more context around it. And when those signals still don’t provide enough confidence, a trusted mobile device can bring additional customer, device, and physical-world evidence into the decision. 
 The result isn’t simply more data. 
 It’s a clearer way to decide when a web session has enough evidence to proceed and when it needs stronger verification. 
 That’s the role behavioral biometrics now plays within Incognia’s broader web risk intelligence.

Frequently asked questions
 

 What is web behavioral biometrics? 
 Web behavioral biometrics uses interaction signals such as mouse movement, keyboard activity, clicks, and clipboard behavior to provide evidence about how someone is using a web session. These signals can help fraud teams better understand what is happening within an interaction and support broader web risk decisions. 

 What behavioral signals can be used to assess a web session? 
 Incognia can evaluate signals including mouse movement and clicks, keyboard interaction, and copy, paste, and cut activity. This can include patterns such as unusual cursor movement, synthetically generated mouse or keyboard events, and untrusted clipboard activity. 

 Can behavioral biometrics detect fraud on its own? 
 No single behavioral signal should be treated as proof of fraud. Legitimate users can behave in unexpected ways, so behavioral evidence is most useful when it contributes to a broader risk assessment. 

 How does behavioral biometrics improve web risk decisions? 
 Behavioral biometrics adds evidence about what is happening within the interaction itself. When combined with other available risk signals, it can help businesses decide which web sessions have enough evidence to proceed and which may require stronger verification. 

 What happens when web signals do not provide enough confidence? 
 When a session requires stronger verification, the customer can complete an explicit step-up on a trusted mobile device through Cross Device Authentication. Incognia can then bring additional evidence into the decision, including the trusted device relationship, physical proximity between the mobile device and web session, and whether the mobile location is trusted.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
De-PR'd: Incognia published a blog post describing a feature it already shipped/announced on Oct 6 — adding behavioral-biometrics signals (mouse movement, clicks, keyboard activity, clipboard/copy-paste) to its existing web risk stack (browser intelligence, device intelligence, integrity/tamper, bot/automation detection, Verified IP Location). When web signals are inconclusive, it can step the user up to Cross-Device Authentication on a "trusted" mobile device for added evidence (device relationship, physical proximity of phone↔web session, trusted mobile location).
- **No numbers, no clients, no benchmarks.** The post is entirely qualitative ("another source of evidence", "clearer decision"). It carefully disclaims that behavioral signals "are not definitive on their own" — a hedge that doubles as a sales argument to buy the *rest* of the Incognia stack.
- **Why structured this way (the real tell):** behavioral biometrics on the web is a commodity/catch-up feature for Incognia — its moat is mobile location + device fingerprinting, where the web gives "less direct evidence" (its words). So the post reframes a me-too web feature as a funnel into Incognia's genuinely differentiated asset: the **trusted mobile device + physical-world location** step-up. The value proposition is not "we do behavioral biometrics" (BioCatch et al. did that years ago) but "when the web isn't enough, we have the phone and the geography you don't." That is the actual message under the PR.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Behavioral biometrics for fraud is a mature, consolidating category — Incognia is a late/small entrant here:
- **BioCatch** — category pioneer, ~$185M ARR (2025); **Visa agreed to acquire it for $2.4B in Aug 2026** (expected close ~Visa FQ2-2027). The category leader is being absorbed into a network.
- **LexisNexis Risk Solutions / BehavioSec** — LexisNexis bought BehavioSec in 2022; behavioral biometrics bundled into its broader risk graph.
- **Feedzai, IBM** — named alongside BioCatch as leaders in behavioral biometrics + device intelligence (QKS Group).
- Position: Incognia is **catching up / at parity at best** on web behavioral signals; its differentiation is elsewhere (mobile location). **(analysis)** Why the lay of the land is this way: pure behavioral-biometrics is being commoditized and rolled into platforms (networks: Visa→BioCatch; risk-data: LexisNexis→BehavioSec), so a standalone "we added mouse/keyboard signals" launch has little standalone weight in late 2026. Second-order: with BioCatch inside Visa, network-grade distribution for behavioral biometrics raises the bar — Incognia must lean harder on the mobile/location angle it uniquely owns.

## [2] Company history / fit
Incognia (HQ San Jose/Palo Alto CA; Brazilian roots): location + device-fingerprinting fraud/identity; **$15.5M Series A (2022), $31M Series B (Jan 2024, led by Bessemer, w/ FJ Labs)**. 2026 has been a rapid-fire product-release cadence, all in the vault: AI Rule Builder (Mar), AI-Powered Browser ID (Mar, [[Incognia debuts AI-powered Browser ID for web identity]]), Verified IP Location (Apr, [[Incognia launches Verified IP Location for fraud teams]]), Location 2FA (Apr), AI Agent Detection + Web Behavioral Biometrics + MCP Server (Oct, [[Incognia launches AI Agent Detection for financial institutions]], [[Incognia launches MCP Server for AI-driven fraud investigations]]).

Expanding into web (Browser ID → Verified IP → behavioral biometrics) is the logical move to sell to the same fraud teams across channels — but it is **extending into categories others already dominate**, hence the heavy "+ our mobile/location step-up" framing in every web launch. ## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** Mouse/keyboard/clipboard-based behavioral biometrics is ~decade-old tech (BioCatch, BehavioSec). The one semi-distinctive idea — chaining an inconclusive web session to a *trusted mobile device + physical proximity/location* — is Incognia's real IP, but even that was already public via the Oct-6 release and prior products (Cross-Device Auth, Location 2FA).

For Incognia that's mobile-location + trusted-device graph, not mouse movements. So this feature is defensive table-stakes to keep fraud-team budget inside Incognia; it does not itself create pricing power. What breaks the story: if Visa/BioCatch bundle behavioral biometrics into network pricing, standalone vendors' web-behavioral features lose willingness-to-pay fast.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Behavioral biometrics is a fast-growing sub-vertical of fraud-risk/identity. Market sizing is vendor-report-only (treat as indicative, not audited): ~$2.7–3.1bn in 2025 rising to ~$3.5–3.8bn in 2026 at a ~22–27% CAGR across several syndicated reports (per Mordor/Straits/Coherent et al., as of 2025–26). Fraud detection & prevention is the dominant application (~42% of 2025 spend per Coherent Market Insights). Structure: fragmented and consolidating — standalone behavioral specialists (BioCatch), device/location players layering behavior on top (Incognia), and broader risk platforms embedding it (Feedzai Digital Trust). Value sits in the orchestration/decisioning layer that fuses many weak signals, not in any single signal — a point Incognia's own post makes ("no single signal"). Why now: (1) GenAI lowers the cost of synthetic/automated session attacks (bots, injected mouse/keyboard events), so static device signals alone decay — exactly the "trusted vs synthetically generated browser event" gap Incognia is addressing; (2) web sessions are signal-poor vs mobile (browser sandboxing), pushing vendors toward multi-signal fusion + cross-device step-up.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: NRR, ARR, devices/sessions covered, and efficacy deltas (ATO / fake-account / false-positive reduction). Key players & basis of competition: BioCatch (pure behavioral biometrics, FI-focused, 280+ institutions, 3 of top-4 US banks — competes on bank distribution + depth); Feedzai (behavioral bundled into a transaction-risk platform — competes on breadth/orchestration, named leader in QKS 2025 SPARK Matrix); Incognia competes on location + device identity with behavior as a newly-added layer (web risk intelligence + Cross Device Authentication step-up). Recent moves: BioCatch raised $35M Series E (Sep 2025) then was acquired by Visa for $2.4bn (announced Aug 2026); Incognia launched Incognia ID (2025, ~1bn devices), AI Rule Builder and AI Browser ID (Mar 2026), and now adds behavioral biometrics to web risk (Oct 2026). Protagonist position: niche/differentiated — strong in LatAm + mobile/location, now extending into web where BioCatch/Feedzai are entrenched; moat = location-intelligence + trusted-device graph (~1bn devices, ~500M places/mo per company figures) and cross-device step-up — intangible/data-network moat (analysis). Disclosed efficacy: 80% ATO reduction, 51% fewer fake accounts, 84% less new-user abandonment (company-stated, not independently audited).

Incognia: last round Series B $31M (Jan 2024, Bessemer; ~$47M raised total) — round valuation not disclosed, so no EV/Rev multiple ("no data"; revenue undisclosed → `[UNSOURCED]`). Read: the 13.0x BioCatch print sets a rich-but-justified bar for a scaled, bank-embedded behavioral asset; an earlier-stage Incognia would not command that — comparison is qualitative given no disclosed Incognia financials.

**Risk flags.**
1. Commoditization / disintermediation — behavioral biometrics is becoming a feature inside larger stacks (Feedzai bundles it; Visa now owns BioCatch and can push it through its network). A location-first player adding behavior late risks competing on an input others give away, with margin captured by the orchestration/network layer above.
2. Efficacy is self-reported and signal-fragile — the post itself stresses no behavioral signal "proves" fraud and legit users trigger anomalies; false-positive/friction tradeoffs and unaudited 80%/51%/84% figures mean real-world lift is unverified.
3. Big-tech/network bundling — Visa's $2.4bn BioCatch deal signals the networks intend to own fraud-decisioning distribution, squeezing independent vendors (incl. Incognia) on enterprise/bank channels.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** The Visa/BioCatch deal re-rates behavioral biometrics from a point tool to a strategic, network-owned fraud layer — a consolidation signal, not a new-entry one (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: independents like Incognia win by owning signals the networks can't easily replicate (precise location + trusted-device graph + cross-device step-up) and selling orchestration, not single signals. Watch/trigger: a larger priced Incognia round (or acquisition) that discloses revenue — if it lands near BioCatch's ~8–13x range it validates the moat; thesis breaks if network-owned stacks (Visa-BioCatch) commoditize web behavioral signals and Incognia's web traction stalls vs its mobile/LatAm core.
