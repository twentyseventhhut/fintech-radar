## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
### [0] What happened
On 2026-10-13 Waverly Advisors, LLC — a fee-only, SEC-registered investment adviser — announced it had **acquired MCS Family Wealth Advisors**, a wealth management firm with offices in Bend and Eugene, Oregon, holding **~$151M in AUM**. The deal **closed 2026-10-09**. MCS founders **Michael and Jeff Yamada** join Waverly as Partners and Wealth Advisors. MCS has operated for 30+ years building a reputation for disciplined, personalized wealth management.
Source: [Waverly press release](https://waverly-advisors.com/insights/waverly-advisors-acquires-mcs-family-wealth-advisors/) · [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/ria-news/waverly-apella-buy-412m-in-ria-assets-across-oregon-northeast/268581)

### [1] Who is Waverly / the buyer profile
Waverly is a serial RIA consolidator that has grown aggressively via acquisition. The MCS deal is Waverly's **37th transaction since December 2021**, when it took an equity investment from **Wealth Partners Capital Group (WPCG)** and **HGGC's Aspire Holdings** platform. Post-close Waverly manages **~$38.4B** as of 2026-10-09 (up from ~$34.2B in late April after the TruWealth deal), serving HNW individuals/families, corporate retirement plans, trusts, endowments and institutions. 2026 has been a heavy acquisition year — recent deals include [TruWealth Advisors](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260428706401/en/Waverly-Advisors-Acquires-TruWealth-Advisors), a $3.1B Louisiana platform, and a $447M Massachusetts RIA (Bridge Creek).

### [2] Why it matters
MCS at ~$151M AUM is a **small tuck-in** (~0.4% of Waverly's book) — material to the Oregon team and founders' succession, immaterial to Waverly's size. The signal is **pace and backing**, not deal size: a PE-sponsored platform executing its 37th deal in ~4 years, extending its geographic footprint into Oregon (Bend/Eugene). It is a textbook founder-succession tuck-in: a 30-year-old independent RIA selling to a well-capitalized aggregator rather than pursuing internal succession.

### [3] Market context — RIA M&A at record pace
2026 is on track to be the busiest RIA M&A year ever, with volume pointed toward ~500 transactions. H1 2026 logged **262 deals** vs 220 a year prior; **~72%** of Q1 2026 deals involved a PE-backed buyer (DeVoe & Co.). Drivers: the 1990s–2000s wirehouse-breakaway founder generation reaching retirement, and PE-backed aggregators offering faster, higher-multiple exits than internal succession. Waverly/WPCG/HGGC-Aspire is one of many such sponsored roll-ups competing for scale. Compare the same-week tuck-in [[Simplicity Group acquires Chicago's Zen Wealth Management]] — another small, succession-driven RIA roll-up acquisition.
Source: [InvestmentNews — RIA dealmaking record pace](https://www.investmentnews.com/ria-news/ria-dealmaking-races-to-a-record-pace-as-consolidators-bulk-up-on-scale/267615)

### [4] What to watch
- Whether Waverly keeps its ~monthly deal cadence toward a 40th+ transaction, and any new capital raise / re-sponsor event beyond the 2021 WPCG/HGGC-Aspire investment.
- Retention of the Yamada partners and MCS clients post-integration (the usual tuck-in risk).
- Macro: whether record RIA M&A volume holds through H2 2026 as consolidators digest scale (Q3 volume reportedly cooled).
- Related this week: [[Simplicity Group acquires Chicago's Zen Wealth Management]].

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US RIA/wealth-management consolidation is running at record pace: per Dakota, 1Q26 saw 102 deals worth ~$236bn AUM and 2Q26 103 deals worth ~$164.7bn; the first seven months of 2026 total ~238 announced deals (~$451bn client AUM), pacing toward ~475 deals vs the 2025 record of 466 (per Dakota, via dakota.com, as of 2026). Structure: highly fragmented at the base (tens of thousands of small, owner-dependent books) rolling up into PE-backed aggregator platforms — value accrues at the platform level via scale, shared compliance/tech and multiple-arbitrage (buy small books at single-digit EBITDA multiples, re-rate inside an $1bn+ platform at 11x–16x). "Why now": aging advisor-owner demographics driving succession-sale supply, plus abundant PE/institutional capital — roughly 70–75% of deal volume carries a disclosed sponsor (per Dakota, via dakota.com). This MCS deal (~$151m AUM as of 2026-06-30, two Bend/Eugene OR offices, founder Michael Stalker since 1991) is a textbook small succession tuck-in: founder + long-tenured advisor (Jeff Yamada, since 2004) join Waverly as Partners.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: AUM, organic (net-new) growth rate, revenue yield on AUM (~0.5–1.0% for fee-only RIAs), and adjusted-EBITDA margin. Basis of competition among acquirers: deal cadence, price/terms, equity/earn-out structure and post-close advisor retention. Waverly is a fee-only serial roll-up — MCS is its 37th transaction since the Dec-2021 equity investment from Wealth Partners Capital Group (WPCG) and HGGC's Aspire Holdings; firm-wide AUM reported at ~$35.5bn, and it was reportedly pursuing a recapitalization as of July 2026 (per InvestmentNews/Dakota, via investmentnews.com). Recent moves: began its 2026 calendar with Pure Portfolios (~$437m, Lake Oswego OR); bought Heartwood Wealth Advisors (~$1.7bn, Richmond VA) as its 36th deal. Oregon is a repeat target (Pure Portfolios + MCS), suggesting a deliberate regional density play. Position: Waverly is among the highest-cadence emerging aggregators (31+ deals), i.e. a scaled consolidator rather than a niche buyer (analysis); moat = scale + sponsor capital + an established integration playbook (switching costs on retained advisors).

**Comps & multiples.** Deal price/terms for MCS were not disclosed — multiple = **no data**. Sector benchmark for a $100m–$250m AUM book (per Ada Astra / ctacquisitions, via adastraequity.com): ~7.5x adjusted EBITDA (sub-$100m ~5.75x), ~1.5x–2.5x revenue; $1bn+ platforms re-rate to ~11x–16x EBITDA (2025 median ~11.6x). Illustrative arithmetic (hypothesis — no disclosed figures): at a 0.7% revenue yield, $151m AUM ≈ ~$1.06m revenue (`$151m × 0.7%`); at ~2x revenue that implies a ~$2.1m ballpark price `[UNSOURCED]` — not confirmed, directional only. The multiple-arbitrage thesis: book bought at ~7x EBITDA re-rated inside a $35.5bn platform at ~11x–16x. Internal comps are thin — the base has no comparable advisory-firm roll-up; closest asset/wealth-management M&A precedents: [[Franklin Templeton completes Apera Asset Management deal]] and [[JPMorgan acquires WealthOS to enhance pension offering]] (both different sub-verticals — asset mgmt / wealthtech, not RIA roll-up; weak comparability, qualitative only). Distribution not computed (fewer than 3 comparable deal multiples disclosed).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Key-person / retention risk** — $151m AUM is heavily tied to founder Stalker (since 1991) and Yamada; client relationships are personal, so attrition on founder transition directly erodes the acquired AUM (why: revenue is a yield on retained AUM, not a contracted stream).
2. **Roll-up integration + leverage risk** — 37 deals in <5 years under PE sponsors with a reported recapitalization in progress; serial integration strains systems/culture and a recap signals leverage, so rising rates or a down-cycle compress the platform's re-rating spread (why: the whole thesis depends on multiple-arbitrage holding).
3. **Undisclosed economics** — price, terms and earn-out structure not disclosed; without them the value-creation case is unverifiable and downside (overpayment, aggressive earn-outs) is hidden (why: "announced" ≠ accretive).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** A single $151m tuck-in is immaterial to a $35.5bn platform; it matters only as another data point that RIA consolidation remains supply-driven and at record cadence despite a 19% QoQ volume dip in 3Q26 (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: the aggregator model keeps compounding while rates/credit stay benign; trigger/what breaks it — a stalled or discounted Waverly recapitalization, or a jump in post-close advisor attrition, would signal the multiple-arbitrage spread is closing. Watch: deal cadence into 4Q26 and recap terms.
