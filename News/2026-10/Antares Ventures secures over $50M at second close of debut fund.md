## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
[0] **TL;DR** — Singapore-based **Antares Ventures** has secured **more than US$50M** at the **second close** of its debut institutional fund, **Antares Ventures I**, which targets a **US$100M** final size. The vehicle backs **deep-tech** (energy storage, industrial tech, hydrogen, water treatment, advanced mobility), not fintech per se — it is a cross-border Asia/global hard-tech fund, categorized here under infrastructure/global. (dealstreetasia.com, technode.global; firm update: antares.ventures/firm-update/antares-ventures-100million-fund)

[1] **Who / structure** — Founded by **Michael Gryseels** (founder & managing partner; ex-group chief digital officer at Thailand's Charoen Pokphand Group; has run this deep-tech model since 2019), with partners **Louis Murayama** and **Milena Nikolova**. Antares Ventures I is the firm's **first institutional fund**. LPs are **family offices plus corporate and strategic investors from Southeast Asia, Japan and South Korea** — i.e. a strategically-anchored, not purely financial, LP base.

[2] **Deployment / portfolio** — Already deployed **~US$27M across 9 companies**. Check sizes **US$1–5M** (most in the **US$2–3M** range). Named portfolio: **VFlowTech** (SG, long-duration energy storage), **IONATE** (UK, hybrid intelligent transformers), **enaDyne** (Germany, plasma tech), **The ePlane Company** (India, electric aircraft). Thesis is articulated in Antares' "Beyond Carbon & Silicon" report — a constraint-led framework for deep-tech investing (decarbonization + resource/energy constraints), explicitly global-in-reach but Asia-scaled.

[3] **Internal context (comparables)** — This lands amid a dense Oct-2026 run of venture-fund closes in the News DB. Nearest structural twin: [[Iliad Partners secures second close of $50 million fund]] (2025, UAE, also a $50M second close but fintech/proptech/logistics). Other recent first/early-institutional funds: [[Phoenix Venture Partners completes ~$23M debut fundraise]], [[PSV Tech closes €56M second fund for Nordic startups]], [[Pear VC closes $333M fifth fund for pre-seed and seed]], [[Union Square Ventures raises $900M across two funds]]. Deep-tech/climate-adjacent comps: [[Convective Capital closes $85 million disaster-resilience fund]]. Note the contrast: most DB fund-closes are fintech/AI/software; Antares is a rare **hard-tech/energy-transition** vehicle, so its fintech-radar relevance is tangential (capital-formation signal, not a fintech deal).

[4] **Why it matters (for a fintech digest)** — Modest in absolute dollars and off-core-theme (deep-tech, not fintech/payments/lending). Signal value = continued LP appetite in Asia for hard-tech via strategic/family-office capital, and a founder-GP pedigree (CP Group) story. Low direct fintech read-through.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Antares Ventures I is a debut Singapore-based deep-tech VC fund that reached >$50M at second close, targeting a $100M final close (per DealStreetAsia / TNGlobal, as of 2026-10-14). Deep tech has become a structural share of venture: ~36% of global VC funding, ~$9.6bn disclosed YTD 2026 (per Alumni Ventures / NewMarketPitch; secondary citations, treat as directional, not audited). Structure: the SE Asia deep-tech LP base is thin and historically local — the secular shift is international LPs (here family offices + corporates from SEA, Japan, South Korea) stepping in (per DealStreetAsia). "Why now": global capital is treating SE Asia as an emerging deep-tech node while the region's broad startup funding hit a record low in 2026 (per TechTimes, 2026-08-31) — so a debut deep-tech raise cuts against the regional tape.

**Competitive landscape.** KPIs for a sub-scale debut fund are deployment pace, DPI/exits (none yet — too early), and LP re-up ability. Antares has deployed ~$27M across 9 companies (energy storage, industrial tech, hydrogen transport, water treatment, mobility) — e.g. VFlowTech (SG long-duration storage), IONATE (UK transformers), enaDyne (DE plasma), The ePlane Company (India eVTOL) (per DealStreetAsia). Basis of competition for a first-time manager is thesis differentiation + LP relationships, not price. Recent peer moves in-base: [[Phoenix Venture Partners completes ~$23M debut fundraise]] (Abu Dhabi debut, 2026-10-12), [[Oraseya Capital doubles venture fund to AED1 billion]] (~$272M, multi-sector, 2026-10-17), [[Union Square Ventures raises $900M across two funds]] (established, AI tilt, 2026-10-10). Position: niche/sub-scale vs the established raises; moat (analysis) is cross-border deep-tech sourcing (SG hub + EU/India portfolio), not yet scale or track record.

**Comps & multiples.** Not applicable — this is a blind-pool fund raise, not an operating company or priced equity round, so no revenue/EBITDA/valuation multiples exist to compute. Comparison is by fund scale/stage. Debut/sub-$100M peers in-base: [[Phoenix Venture Partners completes ~$23M debut fundraise]] (~$23M, debut) — Antares is ~2x larger at second close and still open; vs established [[Union Square Ventures raises $900M across two funds]] Antares is ~18x smaller. Price-per-unit / DPI: no data (fund is undeployed/early). Fund economics (mgmt fee, carry, final LP mix) not disclosed → [UNSOURCED].

**Risk flags.**
1. Debut-manager / final-close risk: first-time VC funds globally raised just ~$3.6bn in 2025 (−85% vs 2021) (per SeedScope/NewMarketPitch); Antares is still ~$50M short of its $100M target and "continuing to fundraise" — a soft second close is not a commitment to reach $100M. Why: if the final close undershoots, portfolio-construction (15–20 names) and reserve/follow-on capacity break.
2. Distribution / re-up risk: SE Asia "never built the exit market" that returns cash to LPs, and without DPI, LPs don't commit to Fund II (per DealStreetAsia). Why: deep-tech exits are long-dated (energy storage, hydrogen, eVTOL), so the fund's survival depends on paper marks and continued LP conviction, not realized returns.
3. Concentration / capital-intensity: hard-tech (storage, hydrogen, plasma, eVTOL) is capital-hungry with long timelines; a <$100M fund has limited reserves to defend ownership through multiple later rounds. Why: dilution risk and dependence on larger downstream investors to fund the portfolio to exit.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) A signal that international family-office/corporate LPs will underwrite a SE Asia deep-tech debut even as regional funding hits record lows — a small new-entry data point, not a re-rating. Falsifiable thesis: Antares reaches its $100M final close within ~12 months. Trigger/what breaks it: a final-close announcement at/near $100M confirms it; a stalled raise, a scaled-down target, or no third-close news into 2027 would falsify it.
