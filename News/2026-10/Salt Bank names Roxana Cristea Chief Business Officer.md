Salt Bank has nominated Roxana Cristea for the position of Chief Business Officer, with the appointment pending approval from the National Bank of Romania. In her new role, she will oversee product development, commercial operations and the Care Center division, as the bank seeks to turn its rapid growth into sustainable commercial success.
Roxana Cristea has experience launching digital banking products, including digital loans, virtual credit cards and mortgage solutions. Previously, she coordinated teams and projects focused on simplifying and improving access to banking services.
At Salt Bank, she will work with the teams to develop new products, improve the customer experience and expand the use of the bank’s services. Cristea says that her experience in her first job, at a call center, gave her important insight into the need to listen to and understand customers.
Salt Bank CEO Robert Anghel says the bank has reached nearly 70% active customers, while its loan portfolio has doubled compared with the end of last year. Two and a half years after its launch, the institution is entering a new stage, with the goal of becoming the primary bank for an increasing number of customers and scaling its operations.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** Salt Bank — Romania's first fully digital bank, wholly owned by the Banca Transilvania group — nominated **Roxana Cristea** as **Chief Business Officer (CBO)**, with the appointment pending approval from the National Bank of Romania (BNR). In the role she will oversee product development, commercial operations and the Care Center (customer service) division. Announced 2026-10-26/27 ([informat.ro](https://informat.ro/en/economy/roxana-cristea-appointed-chief-business-officer-at-salt-bank-145255); [Business Review](https://business-review.eu/profiles1/whoss-news/salt-bank-appoints-roxana-cristea-as-chief-business-officer-302314)).

**[1] Who Roxana Cristea is.** She brings 20+ years in banking and, per Romanian press ([ZF](https://www.zf.ro/zf-24/cu-o-experienta-de-peste-doua-decenii-in-banking-roxana-cristea-de-la-ing-devine-chief-business-officer-la-salt-bank-fiind-in-curs-de-aprobare-la-bnr-23268133); [Wall-Street.ro](https://www.wall-street.ro/articol/banking/salt-bank-o-recruteaza-pe-sefa-creditarii-de-la-ing-bank.html)), joins from **ING Bank**, where she led lending/credit. Her track record centres on launching digital banking products — digital loans, virtual credit cards and digital mortgage solutions. She frames her approach around a customer-listening ethos tracing back to an early call-centre role.

**[2] Why a CBO now — the scaling thesis.** CEO **Robert Anghel** (who took over as CEO in early 2026 — see [[Robert Anghel becomes CEO of Salt Bank]]) positions the hire as the move from land-grab growth to "sustainable commercial success": the bank cites ~**70% active customers** and a **loan portfolio that doubled** vs. end-2025. 2.5 years after its 2024-04 launch, the stated goal is becoming the **primary bank** for more customers and scaling monetisation (cross-sell across more products per client). A dedicated CBO owning product + commercial + care is the structural answer to "activate and monetise the installed base," not just acquire.

**[3] Salt Bank trajectory & backing.** Built on the former Idea Bank (acquired by Banca Transilvania), Salt launched 2024-04-04, hit 100k sign-ups in days, 230k in the first months, 500k by its first anniversary, and surpassed **700,000 customers / ~2bn lei in assets** by Dec 2025 ([The Diplomat](https://www.thediplomat.ro/2025/12/10/salt-bank-surpasses-700000-customers-and-nears-2-billion-lei-in-assets/)). It runs on Starling's Engine SaaS core-banking platform. Positioned as a domestic challenger to Revolut in Romania. Prior build-out: [[Salt Bank appoints Vikram Tikoo as Chief Technology Officer]] (CTO, 2025-06), [[Salt Bank launches investment offering with Upvest]] (securities trading, 2025-10), [[Salt Bank launches Premium and Everyday plans in Romania]] (tiered plans, 2026-06).

**[4] Why it matters.** A C-suite commercial hire from an incumbent (ING) signals Salt is professionalising for the monetisation phase. It fits a broader pattern of Salt stacking senior operators (CEO, CTO, now CBO) and product breadth (investments, tiered plans) to convert a large, Banca Transilvania-funded user base into revenue-generating primary-bank relationships. The BNR-pending caveat means the appointment is not yet effective.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Romania is one of the most contested neobank markets in CEE. Revolut has surpassed 5M retail customers in Romania (per Finance Magnates / coinspectator, as of Oct 2026) and reportedly became the country's largest bank by cardholders in Feb 2026 (per Romania-Insider), adding 500k+ Romanian customers in under 12 months; it serves these clients through a branch of the Lithuanian-licensed Revolut Bank UAB rather than a local charter. Against this, Salt Bank — Banca Transilvania's 99.5%-owned digital bank, launched Apr 2024 as the first "made in Romania" neobank — crossed 700k customers and ~RON 2bn (~€0.4bn) in assets at end-2025 (per The Diplomat / Nine O'Clock, Dec 2025) and is adding 1,000+ customers/day (CEO, per ZF). Structurally the market is a duopoly-plus: a dominant cross-border challenger (Revolut) vs a domestically-licensed incumbent-backed neobank (Salt), with 17 mobile banks active as of Aug 2026 (per web search) but a long tail of sub-scale apps. "Why now": the CBO hire explicitly reframes Salt from land-grab (user growth) toward commercial monetisation and primary-bank status — BT shareholders have set end-2026 as the break-even target (per wall-street.ro), so 2026 is the profitability-proof year.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for consumer neobanks: active-customer ratio (engagement), products-per-customer (cross-sell / primacy), deposit base and loan book growth, cost-to-income / path to break-even. Key players: Revolut (scale + brand, "primary account" push in markets lacking a dominant local neobank, Romania named among its biggest pushes), Salt Bank (local licence, BT distribution + funding, lending ramp), incumbents' own apps (BT's George/BT Pay, which Salt partly cannibalises within-group), plus new entrants (Addiko Bank entered Romania's digital market Aug 2025 [[Addiko Bank enters Romania's digital banking market]]). Recent moves: Revolut opened a Romanian local branch and hit 5M customers (Oct 2026); Wiktor Stopa took CEE growth oversight (Mar 2026) [[Wiktor Stopa to lead Revolut growth for Central and Eastern Europe]] and Revolut targets 20M CEE retail customers [[Revolut targets 20 million retail customers with new CEE growth hire]]; Salt installed Robert Anghel as CEO (Mar 2026, the outgoing CBO) [[Robert Anghel becomes CEO of Salt Bank]]. Salt's position: clear #2 challenger, far behind Revolut on raw scale (0.7M vs 5M) but differentiated by local licence, BT's balance sheet and a doubling loan book — basis of competition is product depth + lending/primacy, not price. Moat (analysis): BT ownership = cheap funding, cross-sell and regulatory cover (switching costs are low in neobanking, so the moat is distribution/funding, not lock-in). **Risk flags.**
1. Scale gap / acquisition economics — Salt is ~7x smaller than Revolut Romania by customers and still pre-break-even (target end-2026); the CBO is tasked with converting growth into "sustainable commercial success," but closing a 5M-vs-0.7M gap against a profitable, better-funded rival risks rising CAC just as the group wants cost discipline. Second-order: break-even slips if monetisation lags growth.
2. Within-group cannibalisation & dependence on BT — as a 99.5% BT subsidiary, Salt competes with BT's own George/BT Pay base and depends on parent funding and strategic patience; a change in BT's appetite (or a weak Romanian macro — World Bank expects 2026 stagnation, per corpus) could throttle the lending ramp the thesis rests on.
3. Key-person / regulatory execution — the appointment is still "pending approval from the National Bank of Romania," and this is the second senior reshuffle in 2026 (CEO in Mar, CBO now); leadership churn plus doubling a loan book quickly raises credit-underwriting and execution risk if growth outruns risk controls.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Read as the start of Salt's "monetisation phase": a growth/product operator moved into the commercial seat to push products-per-customer and primacy ahead of the end-2026 break-even deadline — not a re-rating event, there's no price here. Falsifiable thesis: if Salt hits break-even by end-2026 with active-customer ratio holding near ~70% and the loan book still growing, it validates the BT-backed local-neobank model as a durable #2 to Revolut. Trigger to watch: BT's FY2026 results / Salt disclosures on break-even, active-customer ratio and loan-book size; thesis breaks if active ratio or loan quality deteriorates as acquisition slows.
