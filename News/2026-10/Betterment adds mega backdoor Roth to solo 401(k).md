New offering helps eligible self-employed investors save more for retirement 
 NEW YORK , Oct. 20, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Betterment , a trusted wealth and savings platform, today introduced mega backdoor Roth capabilities for its solo 401(k) offering, increasing retirement savings potential for eligible self-employed investors. The new capability is available to customers investing directly with Betterment and to financial advisors offering solo 401(k)s to their clients through Betterment Advisor Solutions. 
A   mega backdoor Roth  is an advanced retirement strategy that allows participants in eligible 401(k) plans to make additional after-tax contributions that they can then convert to Roth. This lets them save beyond the regular employee and employer limits while staying within the plan's overall annual limit—up to $72,000 in 2026, excluding eligible catch-up contributions.
"The next generation of workers is redefining what a career looks like, and retirement savings options need to evolve alongside them," said Sarah Levy, CEO of Betterment. "Entrepreneurs, freelancers and independent professionals deserve a seamless way to maximize their retirement savings. By bringing mega backdoor Roth capabilities to our solo 401(k), we're making it easier for self-employed investors to make the most of available contribution limits and build more tax-advantaged savings."
Eligible customers can make an after-tax contribution online and receive a notification when the money is ready to convert. They can then initiate the Roth conversion within their Betterment account. Betterment provides the associated tax forms, helping simplify the paperwork involved.
Financial advisors using Betterment Advisor Solutions can also manage the process on their clients' behalf, with automated tools to track contributions against annual limits and reminders when after-tax balances are ready to convert. Contributions, conversions and 1099-R reporting are supported within the platform.
Betterment plans to introduce mega backdoor Roth capabilities for its employer-sponsored 401(k) plans in the coming months. Learn more about Betterment's solo 401(k) for   retail customers  and   financial advisors .
 About Betterment Betterment is a trusted wealth platform that provides modern, technology-driven solutions for investing, saving, and retirement. We empower customers to build wealth with confidence and ease via automated and self-directed investing, personalized financial advice, and high-yield cash management accounts. Betterment tailors its products and services to three distinct audiences, offering an award-winning web and mobile experience for retail investors, a 401(k) solution for small to mid-sized businesses through Betterment at Work, and an all-in-one custodial platform for independent RIAs through Betterment Advisor Solutions. Betterment helps more than 1 million customers manage over $70 billion in assets. Learn more at betterment.com .

### [1] Mechanics, decoded for a non-US reader
US retirement plumbing in plain terms:
- A **401(k)** is an employer-style tax-advantaged retirement account. A **solo 401(k)** (a.k.a. one-participant 401(k)) is the version for a self-employed person with no employees — they are both "employer" and "employee."
- A **Roth** account is funded with *after-tax* money, but all future growth and withdrawals come out **tax-free** (the opposite of a traditional pre-tax account, where you deduct now and pay tax on withdrawal). Roth is prized because gains compound tax-free for decades.
- Normally a Roth is capped: direct Roth IRA contributions are small (~$7k) and income-limited; the Roth *401(k) employee deferral* is ~$24,500 in 2026.
- **The "mega backdoor" trick:** the IRS lets the *total* of all contributions into a 401(k) (employee deferral + employer contribution + **after-tax non-Roth** contributions) reach a much higher overall cap — the §415(c) limit of **$72,000 in 2026** (≈$80k at age 50+ with catch-up). The after-tax "headroom" is `$72,000 − your deferral (≤$24,500) − any employer contribution` — roughly up to **$47,500** of extra after-tax money.
- You contribute that extra chunk **after-tax**, then **immediately convert it to Roth** (in-plan Roth conversion or roll to a Roth IRA). Because it was already taxed, the conversion is (nearly) tax-free, and from then on it grows tax-free. Net effect: you shovel up to ~$47.5k/yr of *extra* money into Roth that the normal limits would never allow — hence "mega."
- Betterment's actual product contribution: it **automates the choreography** — make the after-tax contribution online, get notified when it's "ready to convert," click to convert, and Betterton issues the tax paperwork (1099-R). Advisors get tools to track contributions against limits and reminders when after-tax balances are convertible.
Sources: https://www.irafinancial.com/blog/mega-backdoor-roth-2026-limit-guide/ · https://www.solo401k.com/blog/mega-backdoor-roth-in-2026-72k-tax-free/ · https://carry.com/learn/mega-backdoor-roth-solo-401k

### [2] Why it matters / significance
- The mega backdoor Roth is well-known but operationally painful. The standard solo 401(k) documents from Vanguard, Fidelity and Schwab **do not even allow after-tax contributions**, so DIY savers historically had to buy a custom third-party plan document (My Solo 401k, Carry, etc.) and hand-manage the after-tax → Roth conversion and 1099-R reporting. Betterment packaging this into a mainstream, automated retail + advisor platform lowers the friction materially.
- Fits Betterment's positioning toward **self-employed / gig / freelancer** savers and its push into the **RIA custody** channel (Betterment Advisor Solutions) — a differentiator vs. incumbents whose off-the-shelf solo 401(k)s lack the feature.
- Modest-scope launch (one niche feature on one product), but strategically it deepens Betterment's retirement stack, which spans retail robo, Betterment at Work (SMB 401(k)) and advisor custody.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
### Red-team questions

1. **Is this genuinely new, or just repackaging an existing IRS strategy?** Repackaging. The mega backdoor Roth itself is an old, legal IRS technique; Betterton's contribution is *automation* of after-tax contribution → in-plan conversion → 1099-R reporting, not a new tax capability. Source: prnewswire release + sdocpa/solo401k guides.

2. **Is Betterment first-to-market?** No. Specialist providers (Carry, My Solo 401k Financial) already offer automated mega backdoor Roth solo 401(k)s; even "My Solo 401k" markets a Betterment-adjacent hack. Betterment's edge is reach (>1M customers + RIA custody), not novelty. Source: carry.com, mysolo401k.net.

3. **Does the $72,000 figure in the note mislead?** Slightly, if read as "you can add $72k of mega backdoor money." The $72k is the *total* §415(c) cap for all contribution types; the incremental after-tax room is only `$72,000 − deferral − employer contribution` ≈ up to $47,500. The note's own aggregated text says "up to the plan's $72,000 annual limit" which is technically the cap, not the extra Roth amount. Clarified in [1]/[3].

4. **Who can actually use this?** Only eligible **self-employed** investors with a Betterment solo 401(k) and enough net self-employment income to have after-tax headroom after deferrals/employer contributions. Not broadly applicable. Source: release.

5. **Does it apply to employer/workplace 401(k)s today?** No — only solo 401(k) at launch; employer-sponsored (Betterment at Work) support is promised "in the coming months," i.e. not yet shipped. Source: release.

6. **Revenue/AUM impact quantified?** No disclosed figures on expected inflows, fees, or how many solo 401(k) accounts exist. Betterment did not size the opportunity. Open.

7. **Pricing?** The release and coverage give no fee/cost detail for the feature. Open.

8. **Are there tax-risk caveats (pro-rata rule, timing of conversion, state tax)?** Mega backdoor Roth carries nuances (earnings on after-tax money before conversion are taxable; eligibility depends on plan provisions). Betterment's "notify when ready to convert" implies it manages timing, but the release doesn't detail edge cases. Partially open.

9. **Does Betterment's solo 401(k) plan document itself support after-tax contributions + in-plan conversion?** It must, or the feature couldn't exist — this is precisely the gap vs. off-the-shelf Vanguard/Fidelity/Schwab solo 401(k)s, which don't. Confirmed by design (sdocpa guide on required three provisions). Source: sdocpa.com.

10. **Is the scale framing (>$70B AUM, >1M customers) load-bearing for this story?** It's context, not causation — the launch is a single niche feature. Scale matters only insofar as it means more potential users than a boutique specialist. Numbers corroborated by Form ADV (~$69.5B / ~1.34M accounts, Feb 2026). Source: investingintheweb.com.

11. **Could this be a competitive reaction to Wealthfront / Carry?** Plausible but unstated. Wealthfront and specialists have been broadening self-directed and self-employed products; no source confirms a specific trigger. Open.

12. **Any regulatory risk to the mega backdoor Roth itself?** The strategy has periodically been targeted in US tax-reform proposals (e.g. past Build Back Better drafts aimed to curb it). No current rule change cited in sources, but it's a latent policy risk to the whole feature. Open / latent.

13. **Does "mega backdoor" conflate with the ordinary "backdoor Roth"?** For a non-US reader, yes risk of confusion: the ordinary backdoor Roth converts a small nondeductible IRA; the *mega* version uses after-tax 401(k) space for far larger sums. Disambiguated in [1].

14. **Is the PR the only source for the launch facts?** The launch itself is corroborated by NAPA-Net, InvestmentNews, 401kSpecialist, The Daily Upside and Yahoo Finance re-reporting the PR — independent outlets carried it, though all trace to the same company announcement. Source: napa-net.org, investmentnews.com.

15. **Duplicate of any prior note?** No — prior Betterment notes cover a data breach and the First Citizens SMB 401(k) partnership; none covers this solo-401(k) mega backdoor Roth feature. Fresh.

**Importance: 3/5 — rationale:** Credible, well-sourced, strategically sensible move that meaningfully reduces friction on a popular US tax strategy and reinforces Betterment's retirement/RIA stack. But it is a single niche feature (self-employed only, solo 401(k) only at launch), not category-first (specialists already offer it), with no disclosed economics and limited addressable base. Solid incremental product news, not a market-moving event.
