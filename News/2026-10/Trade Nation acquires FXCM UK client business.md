 FNG Exclusive … FNG has learned more details of the UK client business of Retail FX and CFDs broker FXCM, which was sold last week to rival online broker Trade Nation. FXCM UK was sold to Trade Nation ahead of the overall sale of (most of) the rest of FXCM to CFDs broker AvaTrade , announced just several days later.
FXCM UK stats
Via regulatory filings made by FXCM UK operator Stratos Markets Limited, FXCM UK brought in $10.8 million in Commission Revenue in 2025 , up by 73% from $6.3 million in 2024. We’d note that formally FXCM UK reported Revenues of $4.8 million in 2025 (and just $104K in 2024), but that was following an approximately $6 million intercompany “transfer pricing adjustment” made in each year. The $6 million adjustment is basically a significant allocation of parent company Stratos Global LLC expenses to FXCM UK.
 FXCM UK held client cash of $110.3 million as at December 31, 2025, up by 24% from $88.8 million the previous year.
Retail trading volumes at FXCM UK came in at $340 billion in 2025, or $28 billion monthly , up by 40% from 2024.
Intercompany charges
FXCM UK reported a Net Loss of $2.2 million in 2025. However it is somewhat difficult to ascertain the “real” profitability level of the company, due to intercompany charges included in its reported results. The company did report more than $12 million in Admin Expenses in 2025, consisting mainly of Staff Costs of $5.5 million for its employee base totaling 48 people, and Legal, Management and Professional fees of $3.6 million. The overall Admin Expenses are more than FXCM UK’s aforementioned $10.8 million in Commission Revenue.
FXCM UK employees
We understand from sources at the companies that no staff are moving from FXCM UK to Trade Nation with the purchase. FXCM ran much of its group function and other business lines from the FXCM UK entity.
What did Trade Nation acquire?
FNG has learned from sources at the companies that the figures reported by FXCM UK noted above include some other material business lines that are not included with the Trade Nation acquisition.
We’d also note that while the trading volume and client cash figures reported above are “real”, when it comes to Revenues the Company (i.e. Stratos Markets Limited) hedges its client trades with affiliates and is compensated in return with a commission, based upon a relative contribution to the total Stratos Group profits.
FXCM UK also earns other forms of revenue such as fees from white label arrangements with third parties to provide platform, back office and trade execution services, FX market prices and various ancillary FX and CFD related services. The Company also earns interest income on its cash balances.
We believe that most of the client cash and represented trading volume noted above will be moving to Trade Nation. But again, what that means in terms of Revenues is somewhat unclear.

# Context-enrichment: Trade Nation acquires FXCM UK client business
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

**Freshness: STALE — duplicate.** This is the SAME transaction already covered by [[Trade Nation to acquire UK client book of Stratos Markets]] (announced 2026-10-06). "FXCM UK" **is** Stratos Markets Limited (FRN 217689), the UK FCA-regulated entity operating the FXCM/Tradu brands; its UK client book is exactly what Trade Nation agreed to buy, transfer due **late November 2026**. This note (FXNewsGroup "exclusive," 2026-10-12) is **not a new deal** — it is a follow-up adding Stratos Markets Limited's 2025 regulatory-filing financials to the same acquisition. It is also a sibling of the parent-level [[AvaTrade to acquire majority of FXCM forex and CFD business]] (2026-10-09), the rest of the FXCM break-up. So: **stale, duplicate_of the Trade Nation/Stratos announcement; the only incremental value is the financial detail below.**

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
No new transaction. On **2026-10-06** Trade Nation (FCA FRN 525164) agreed to acquire the **UK client book** of **Stratos Markets Limited** — the entity that trades as **FXCM (and Tradu) in the UK** — with the client transfer due to complete **late November 2026**; price undisclosed. This 2026-10-12 FNG "exclusive" simply reports Stratos Markets Limited's 2025 statutory filings for that same UK entity:
- **Commission revenue $10.8M in 2025**, up 73% from $6.3M in 2024.
- **Formally reported revenue $4.8M** (2024: $104K) — the gap vs commission revenue is driven by an **~$6M/yr intercompany "transfer-pricing adjustment"** allocating parent (Stratos Global LLC) costs into the UK entity; i.e. reported profitability is a bookkeeping artefact, not economics. (analysis)
- **Client cash $110.3M** at 31-Dec-2025 (+24% from $88.8M).
- **Retail trading volume $340bn in 2025** (~$28bn/month, +40% YoY).
- **Net loss $2.2M in 2025** (2024: ~$2.0M); admin expenses >$12M (staff $5.5M across 48 people; legal/management/professional fees $3.6M).

**Critical scope caveats (the de-PR):** (1) FNG's sources say **no staff** move to Trade Nation — FXCM ran group functions from the UK entity, so these headcount/overhead figures leave with the entity, not the book. (2) FNG says the reported figures **include other material business lines NOT in the Trade Nation deal** (white-label platform/execution services, interest income). (3) Trade Nation is buying **clients + client cash + volume**, but because Stratos hedges client trades with affiliates for a commission, the **revenue that actually transfers is "somewhat unclear"** — the headline $10.8M is not a clean proxy for what Trade Nation gains. So the eye-catching numbers overstate the acquired asset. (analysis, per FXNewsGroup 2026-10-12)

## [1] Competitors / peers
Same consolidation node as the AvaTrade deal. Internal:
- [[Trade Nation to acquire UK client book of Stratos Markets]] — the **original announcement of this exact deal** (primary duplicate).
- [[AvaTrade to acquire majority of FXCM forex and CFD business]] — the parent/majority sale (2026-10-09) that followed days later; same Jefferies-driven FXCM break-up.
- [[Volt and Trade Nation launch real-time payments for Australian traders]] — prior Trade Nation internal mention (2026-06), payments/ops.
External peers: IG Group, Plus500, CMC Markets (UK-listed CFD/spread-bet incumbents); StoneX/Forex.com (absorbed FXCM's old US book via GAIN, 2017); eToro, XTB, Trading 212 (challengers).

**Why the landscape is this way:** UK/EU retail CFD & spread betting is a mature, margin-compressed, licence-gated niche. ESMA (2018) and the FCA's permanent PS19/22 (2019) cap retail leverage (30:1 majors → 2:1 crypto), mandate negative-balance protection and 50% margin close-out, and ban bonuses — structurally compressing per-client revenue and forcing scale-driven consolidation rather than product differentiation. A focused spread-bet/CFD specialist like Trade Nation grows cheapest by **buying a rival's regulated client book** (lower CAC) rather than organic acquisition. (analysis, per fca.org.uk PS19/22, liquidityfinder.com 2026)

## [2] Company history / fit
**Trade Nation:** founded 2014 as **Core Spreads** (legal entity Finsa Europe Ltd → renamed Trade Nation Financial UK Ltd, early 2022); rebranded Trade Nation 2019 on CFD expansion. Owned/controlled by UK entrepreneur **Jasper White**; CEO **Jon Noble** (took over from Stuart Lane after FY2024). FCA-regulated (FRN 525164) plus AU/Bahamas/South Africa/Seychelles/Portugal/Malaysia entities; fixed-spread model, named Best Forex/CFD Broker UK at TradingView Broker Awards 2025. FY2024 revenue reportedly +62% YoY (FXNewsGroup). Fit: a **pure spread-bet/CFD specialist** absorbing experienced UK traders — strategically on-model.
**FXCM / Stratos:** founded 1999, retail-FX pioneer; near-collapse in the **Jan-2015 CHF de-peg** ($225M loss), rescued by a Leucadia/Jefferies loan; **2017 CFTC $7M fine + permanent US ban** (trading against "No Dealing Desk" clients), US book sold to GAIN/StoneX; Jefferies foreclosed to 100% ownership (Sept 2023). Stratos Group CEO **Brendan Callan**. Jefferies is now exiting retail brokerage entirely — this UK carve-out + the AvaTrade majority deal are two halves of that wind-down.

**Why the fit works for Trade Nation:** it gains a "significant community of experienced traders" and ~$110M of client cash in its home market at (likely) low cost, with no overhead/staff attached — a clean book-grab. The risk: those clients are mobile and price-sensitive and must survive a platform migration. (analysis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch: (1) **completion of the client transfer, late Nov 2026**, and client-retention through migration; (2) any disclosed price or transferred-client/revenue figure (none so far); (3) the parallel **AvaTrade majority-FXCM close** (~120-day migration from 2026-10-09) that disposes of the rest of FXCM; (4) FCA sign-off on the client-book move. Sentiment: trade press (FXNewsGroup, FinanceMagnates, LeapRate) frames the Trade Nation + AvaTrade pair as the **symbolic end of FXCM as an independent broker** and a textbook emblem of the 2024–2026 UK/EU CFD consolidation wave.

**Second-order / counter-intuitive:** the leverage caps meant to protect retail traders have **concentrated** the market into fewer, larger brokers; "winning" FXCM's UK book is a prize of *declining* value once stripped of staff, group functions and affiliate-hedged revenue — Trade Nation is really buying client-cash balances and migration optionality, not a durable revenue stream. (analysis)

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** UK/global retail spread-betting & CFD brokerage — this is specifically the *UK client-book carve-out* half of the FXCM break-up (companion to the parent-level [[AvaTrade to acquire majority of FXCM forex and CFD business]] and the earlier PR note [[Trade Nation to acquire UK client book of Stratos Markets]]). No credible free TAM figure for the UK retail-CFD market surfaced → "no data", not an invented number. Structure: a mature, consolidating niche inside a hostile regulatory frame — the FCA caps retail CFD leverage at 30:1 on major FX down to 2:1 on crypto, mandates negative-balance protection and the standard "73.8% of retail accounts lose money" warning (per Trade Nation/Stratos FCA disclosures, 2026-10). "Why now": those 2018–2019 FCA/ESMA caps structurally compressed per-client revenue, and the ultimate owner of FXCM/Stratos — Jefferies Financial Group — is exiting retail brokerage worldwide, dismantling the group piecemeal (UK book → Trade Nation ~2026-10-06; majority → AvaTrade ~2026-10-09). The carve-out is the second-order effect of that exit. Entry barriers: FCA licence + segregated-client capital + client-acquisition cost; low product differentiation.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: funded/active accounts, revenue per active client, client trading volume, client money held, and operating/EBITDA margin. Basis of competition: pricing (fixed-spread model), platform/UX, service, and brand — not network effects. Key UK players: IG Group, CMC Markets, Plus500 (all LSE-listed), eToro, plus Trade Nation and Trading 212 as focused/challenger names. Recent moves: the three-day FXCM dismantling (UK→Trade Nation, majority→AvaTrade, per fxnewsgroup/financemagnates 2026-10); Trade Nation itself partnered with Volt on Australian real-time payments ([[Volt and Trade Nation launch real-time payments for Australian traders]], 2026-06) and was named Best Forex/CFD Broker UK at the TradingView Broker Awards 2025. Protagonist position: Trade Nation (private, founded 2014, London, FCA FRN 525164, offices in Sydney/Lisbon/Johannesburg/Bahamas etc.) is a focused spread-bet/CFD specialist buying a client book to scale its home market — catching-up-via-M&A, niche/focused rather than a listed-scale leader. Moat `(analysis)`: thin — brand + fixed-pricing proposition + the acquired client relationships; no switching-cost or network moat vs IG/Plus500/CMC.

**Comps & multiples.** This is an **asset/client-book purchase at an undisclosed price → deal multiple = no data** (FNG explicitly could not source the price, and notes no staff transfer). Any revenue multiple is further muddied because FXCM UK's $10.8m is *commission from hedging client trades with Stratos Group affiliates*, i.e. intercompany-dependent — it will not travel intact to Trade Nation, so the "real" revenue Trade Nation acquires is itself unclear (per fxnewsgroup, 2026-10-12). Listed peers give sector context only (fresh web figures below; arithmetic `(analysis)`):
- **Plus500**: mcap ~$2.40bn; Q1-2026 rev $242.1m (+18%) → run-rate ~$968m → P/S ≈ 2.40 / 0.968 ≈ **2.5x**; P/E (TTM) ~**11.5x** (per earlier Plus500 H1 read).
- **CMC Markets**: mcap £1.83bn; FY2026 rev £376.8m → P/S = 1.83 / 0.377 = **4.9x**; P/E ~**24.6x** (stockanalysis.com, 2026-10-09).
- **IG Group**: mcap ~£4.23–4.57bn; Q1-2026 rev £331.2m (+19%), implying ~£1.3bn run-rate → P/S ≈ 4.5 / 1.3 ≈ **3.5x**; P/E ~**8.8x** (stockanalysis.com / financemagnates, 2026-10).
- Internal comp: parent-level deal [[AvaTrade to acquire majority of FXCM forex and CFD business]] — also undisclosed price, Jefferies carrying only ~$5.5m goodwill, implying the whole FXCM is near-worthless.
In-line read: listed P/S clusters ~2.5–5x, P/E ~9–25x — within sanity ranges. But the **target here is loss-making** (FXCM UK net loss $2.24m in 2025 on $12.03m admin expenses > $10.8m commission revenue; interest income actually fell 39.6% to $2.98m), so earnings multiples don't apply and the price was almost certainly nominal. `[UNSOURCED]`: purchase price, number of clients transferring, Trade Nation's own revenue/accounts/valuation.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Revenue is intercompany-dependent, not organic** — FXCM UK's $10.8m "commission" comes from hedging client trades with Stratos Group affiliates plus white-label/interest income; with no staff moving and the affiliate chain gone, what Trade Nation actually monetises is unclear. Second-order: the headline $10.8m/$340bn-volume figures overstate the economic value of what transfers.
2. **Client-retention/migration risk** — the asset is a book of experienced, price-sensitive, mobile traders being moved platforms in a late-Nov-2026 transition; such clients can churn to IG/Plus500/Forex.com/CMC in a click. The whole deal's value lives or dies on retained accounts, none of which is disclosed.
3. **Regulatory headwind on the core product + legacy tail** — Trade Nation is buying scale in a leverage-capped, marketing-restricted, loss-making UK CFD niche (a shrinking pie), and inheriting clients from the tainted FXCM brand (2015 CHF event, 2017 US CFTC fraud ban at group level); gap-risk and further FCA tightening sit directly on the acquired economics.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Defensive consolidation, not a re-rating: a motivated seller (Jefferies exiting) splits FXCM between two scale-hungry mid-tier private buyers (Trade Nation takes UK clients; AvaTrade takes the rest). Falsifiable thesis: if regulated UK/EU retail-CFD economics keep compressing, expect more sub-scale brokers absorbed or exited within ~12–24 months. Triggers to watch: whether the late-November client transfer actually completes and how many accounts/how much of the $110.3m client cash survive it; any FCA objection; and whether listed peers (IG/Plus500/CMC) respond with their own M&A (confirms the wave) or instead chase organic/multi-asset growth (weakens it).
