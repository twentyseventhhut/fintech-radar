 Monfi Digital Bank Ltd. (Monfi Bank ), a fully licensed international digital bank headquartered in Malaysia, has launched the only regulated bank-issued USD stable coin. Its reserves are held under the same supervisory framework as the bank’s deposits. 
SINGAPORE, Oct. 19, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- Monfi Bank, the licensed and regulated digital bank that runs fiat accounts and digital assets on one unified platform, has launched Monfi USD (MUSD) , the only regulated bank-issued stablecoin. Monfi USD is a fully reserved, US dollar-denominated stablecoin issued by the bank itself. MUSD gives institutions and qualified private clients a digital dollar that is redeemable at par and whose reserves sit on a bank balance sheet. It is powered by Estable and Hadron by Tether.
Stablecoins have moved from a crypto settlement tool to core financial infrastructure. Market capitalisation is above USD 300 billion, and regulatory frameworks are now in force across the US, EU and Asia Pacific. For institutions, the question is no longer whether to hold digital dollars, but who stands behind them.
Almost every stablecoin in circulation today is issued by a non-bank entity, with reserves held at third-party custodians. Monfi USD (MUSD) is designed for institutions that want the alternative: a dollar token issued by a licensed, supervised bank, with reserves held in a segregated account on the bank’s own books, and redemption that is a bank obligation rather than a promise from a reserve manager.
 Built on a bank balance sheet, not a reserve pool 
MUSD is backed one-to-one by US dollar reserves held in a dedicated, segregated reserve account on Monfi Bank’s own balance sheet. The account is separate from the bank’s operational capital, its regulatory capital and other client deposits. The structure is the product.
 Reserves that stay reserves. Reserves are held exclusively in US dollar cash and cash equivalents. They are unencumbered and are never leveraged, lent or rehypothecated. They are reconciled daily against circulating supply, published quarterly and subject to annual independent third-party verification.
 A claim on a licensed bank. Holders have recourse to Monfi Bank, which is supervised by the same regulator that oversees the rest of its business. Redemption is at par, in US dollars.
 Institutional infrastructure throughout. MUSD is powered by Estable and Hadron by Tether. Fireblocks provides custody infrastructure and key management, and Chainalysis provides on-chain monitoring and sanctions screening.
 Built for settlement. MUSD supports cross-border payments between counterparties, trade settlement, treasury management and the settlement of tokenised assets.
MUSD is available now to institutions and qualified private clients who have completed Monfi Bank’s KYC and KYB onboarding; applications are made through Monfi Bank’s website. Secondary market access will follow as MUSD is listed on digital asset venues, subject to those venues’ eligibility frameworks.
The MUSD contract address on Ethereum is 0x157410Eeb85e9Dd2053338167ab33d119a45aF47, verifiable on Etherscan at https://etherscan.io/token/0x157410Eeb85e9Dd2053338167ab33d119a45aF47 .
MUSD was publicly launched at TOKEN2049 Singapore in October 2026, where Monfi Bank was a Gold Sponsor.
 About Monfi Bank 
Monfi Digital Bank Ltd. is a fully licensed international digital bank headquartered in Malaysia. It brings fiat banking and digital assets together in one account and one app, with multi-currency accounts, cross-border payments, a card for global spending and trading in major cryptocurrencies. In October 2026, the bank launched Monfi USD (MUSD), a fully reserved US dollar stablecoin powered by Estable and Hadron by Tether.
 Bank: Multi-currency accounts in US dollars, euros, British pounds, UAE dirhams and Singapore dollars. Clients can move funds between currency accounts and send cross-border payments directly from the app.
 Trade: Clients can buy, sell and swap leading digital assets, from Bitcoin to other major cryptocurrencies, using their fiat balances and convert back within the same account.
 Spend: A card that tops up from the account, issued virtually with a physical card on request, for contactless payments and ATM withdrawals.
 Settle: MUSD, a bank-issued USD stablecoin for clients who need a dollar balance that moves on-chain at any hour and is redeemable at par.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened.** Monfi Digital Bank Ltd. ("Monfi Bank") launched Monfi USD (MUSD), a fully reserved, US dollar-denominated stablecoin issued by the bank itself. The token was publicly unveiled at TOKEN2049 Singapore in October 2026 (Monfi was a Gold Sponsor). MUSD is marketed as "the only regulated bank-issued stablecoin": a digital dollar redeemable at par to institutions and qualified private clients who clear Monfi's KYC/KYB, with reserves held in a segregated account on the bank's own balance sheet. The Ethereum contract (0x157410...5aF47) is live and verifiable on Etherscan. The issuance stack is Estable + Hadron by Tether (tokenization/white-label), Fireblocks (custody/key management) and Chainalysis (on-chain monitoring and sanctions screening).

**Who Monfi actually is — the critical nuance.** Monfi is NOT a Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) onshore bank. It is licensed by the **Labuan Financial Services Authority (LFSA), licence no. 230148DB** — Malaysia's offshore International Business and Financial Centre (IBFC). The Labuan digital-bank regime is built for *non-resident international clients*, runs a relaxed transitional capital floor (MYR 50M, vs BNM's MYR 100-300M onshore) and is NOT a passport into the domestic Malaysian retail market. So "fully licensed international digital bank headquartered in Malaysia" is literally true but leans on an offshore licence that is lighter-touch than a mainstream central-bank banking charter. The press release's headline claim that reserves "sit on a bank balance sheet" and redemption is "a bank obligation" is therefore only as strong as the Labuan supervisory framework behind it. Monfi also operates a London representative office (UK Companies House FC041010 — a rep office, not a UK-regulated bank).

**Why bank-issued stablecoins matter (the real theme).** Almost all circulating stablecoins (USDT, USDC) are issued by non-bank entities with reserves at third-party custodians. The post-GENIUS-Act (US) and MiCA (EU) wave pushes toward issuers that are regulated credit institutions. Under MiCA, a euro/dollar EMT can ONLY be issued by a licensed credit institution or e-money institution — there is no non-bank path. The GENIUS Act similarly tightened reserve rules (no rehypothecation, narrow repo exceptions) vs legacy Tether practice. Monfi is positioning MUSD into exactly this "who stands behind the dollar" narrative — recourse to a supervised bank rather than a reserve manager.

**But not "the only" one — the claim is false marketing.** Prior art already exists:
- [[SoFi launches SoFiUSD, first stablecoin from a US national bank]] — May 2026, first stablecoin from a US national bank (FDIC-insured, far stronger charter than Labuan).
- [[Tetra launches CADD, Canada's first bank-issued CAD stablecoin]] — May 2026, first CAD stablecoin issued by a financial institution.
- [[Tether signs stablecoin MoU with National Bank of Kazakhstan]] — same-month central-bank-linked issuance.
- Bank consortia (Goldman/BofA/Citi/UBS joint venture announced Sept 2026). So "the only regulated bank-issued stablecoin" is at best "the only one from this particular Labuan bank," not a global first.

**Tether dependency.** "Powered by Estable and Hadron by Tether" means MUSD runs on Tether's white-label tokenization rails. That softens the "bank, not a reserve pool, unlike Tether" positioning: the bank controls the reserve structure, but the issuance/token infrastructure is Tether's. Reserve transparency is self-stated: daily reconciliation, quarterly publication, annual third-party verification — none independently confirmed at launch.

**Comparable/adjacent notes:** [[BNY explores tokenized deposits for payments network]], [[HSBC expands tokenised deposits to Singapore]], [[Bank of Korea urges banks to lead won stablecoin issuance]], [[SaaScada and Zumo give UK banks regulated crypto route]].

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. **Is Monfi a "bank" in the way readers assume?** It holds a Labuan FSA offshore digital-bank licence (230148DB), not a Bank Negara Malaysia onshore charter and not FDIC/deposit-insurance-backed. How much supervisory weight does "a claim on a licensed bank" actually carry under Labuan IBFC rules?
2. **"The only regulated bank-issued stablecoin" — demonstrably false?** SoFiUSD (US national bank, FDIC) and Tetra CADD both predate it by ~5 months. Is this phrasing a GlobeNewswire PR puff that should not be repeated uncritically?
3. **Reserve verification.** Reserves are "published quarterly" and "annually verified by a third party" — but at launch none of that exists yet. Who is the verifier? Is there any attestation available today, or only a promise?
4. **Rehypothecation claim.** "Never leveraged, lent or rehypothecated" — is this contractually enforceable and auditable, or a marketing assertion? Legacy Tether made similar claims that later proved partially untrue.
5. **Tether dependency vs differentiation.** MUSD is "powered by Hadron by Tether." If the whole pitch is "unlike Tether/Circle, a bank stands behind it," how independent is it really from Tether's infrastructure and operational risk?
6. **Segregated account = ring-fenced in insolvency?** Does Labuan law actually make a segregated reserve account bankruptcy-remote from Monfi's creditors, or is "segregated on the bank's own books" weaker than a bankruptcy-remote trust?
7. **Who is Monfi, really?** The bank only appears in 2026, has a London *representative* office (not a licensed UK bank) and a GlobeNewswire launch. Any audited financials, capital base, deposit book, or track record? Red flag for a one-release company claiming "core financial infrastructure."
8. **Market reach.** Labuan licences explicitly cannot serve Malaysian domestic retail and target non-residents. Who is the actual addressable client base, and does that constrain MUSD adoption?
9. **Regulatory passport.** MUSD is not MiCA-compliant (Monfi is not an EU credit/e-money institution) and not GENIUS-Act compliant (not a US issuer). So in which jurisdictions can institutions legally hold/transact MUSD? "Regulated" by whom, for whom?
10. **Liquidity & redemption.** Redemption "at par" depends on the bank's solvency and liquidity. With no disclosed balance sheet, what is the real counterparty risk vs USDC/USDT's T-bill-heavy reserves?
11. **Secondary market.** Listings are "to follow, subject to venue eligibility." Is there any trading venue committed today, or is MUSD effectively a closed-loop institutional token at launch?
12. **TOKEN2049 Gold Sponsor + Etherscan contract** are the only hard, verifiable facts. Does anything beyond the self-issued PR corroborate the regulatory and reserve claims?
13. **Single source.** All coverage traces to one GlobeNewswire release syndicated (Manila Times, Analytics Insight, TechFinancials). No independent reporting or regulator confirmation — treat all claims as issuer-stated.

**Importance: 2/5** — A thematically relevant data point in the 2026 bank-issued-stablecoin wave (GENIUS/MiCA pushing regulated issuers), but the specifics are weak: it rests on a Labuan offshore licence (not a top-tier banking charter), the headline "only regulated bank-issued stablecoin" claim is false (SoFiUSD, Tetra CADD precede it), all facts come from a single self-issued PR with no independent verification or disclosed financials, and it runs on Tether rails. Low issuer stature + unverified claims cap its significance; it is a trend footnote, not a market-moving event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Stablecoins have scaled to core payments infrastructure: total supply ~$302.3bn as of 2026-10-19, off a May-2026 peak of ~$322.8bn (per stablecoin trackers, via transak/coinspaid, as of Oct 2026). Structure is a tight duopoly — USDT ~$183.4bn (~59%) and USDC ~$72.7bn (~24%), together ~84.7% of supply (per market-cap trackers, via pluang/cryptobriefing, as of 2026-10-17) — i.e. near-total concentration in two NON-bank issuers with reserves at third-party custodians. The secular shift "why now": the US GENIUS Act (P.L. 119-27) created a federal issuance regime, letting insured-depository subsidiaries, OCC-chartered nonbanks, and state-qualified issuers mint payment stablecoins; within ~6 months SoFi Bank issued the first stablecoin from a nationally-chartered, FDIC-insured US bank, and by Sep-2026 a 21-institution consortium (Goldman, BofA, Citi, UBS) announced a joint venture (per Wolters Kluwer / Spark, as of 2026). The bank-issued "deposit-backed digital dollar" is the emerging second wave challenging the Tether/Circle reserve-pool model.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: circulating supply / market cap, reserve composition & attestation cadence, redemption-at-par guarantee, and settlement/on-chain volume. Basis of competition is shifting from distribution/liquidity (where USDT/USDC own the network effect) to *credibility of the claim* — who stands behind redemption. Recent bank-issued peer moves: SoFiUSD (first US national-bank stablecoin, 2026) [[SoFi launches SoFiUSD, first stablecoin from a US national bank]]; ODDO BHF's euro-backed EUROD [[ODDO BHF launches euro-backed stablecoin EUROD]]; and tokenised-deposit variants — Monument Bank's £250m initiative [[Monument Bank launches £250m deposit tokenisation initiative]], HSBC tokenised deposits [[HSBC expands tokenised deposits to Singapore]]. Monfi's position: niche/late new entrant (analysis). Its claim to be "the only regulated bank-issued stablecoin" is contradicted by SoFiUSD, which sits under a US national-bank + FDIC framework; Monfi's edge is at best "a bank-issued USD stablecoin out of an APAC offshore hub," not a first or a unique regulatory status. Moat is thin — it rides the same rails as the incumbents (powered by Estable and Hadron by Tether, custody via Fireblocks, monitoring via Chainalysis), so differentiation rests entirely on the bank-balance-sheet redemption claim, not proprietary tech (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/market-cap disclosed for Monfi or MUSD — all multiples "no data"; Monfi is a private offshore bank with no circulating-supply figure published at launch. Qualitative comps (internal): SoFiUSD [[SoFi launches SoFiUSD, first stablecoin from a US national bank]] = US national bank, FDIC perimeter — a stronger regulatory wrapper than Monfi's Labuan license; EUROD [[ODDO BHF launches euro-backed stablecoin EUROD]] = EU bank, EUR-denominated, likely MiCA-aligned; Monument [[Monument Bank launches £250m deposit tokenisation initiative]] / HSBC [[HSBC expands tokenised deposits to Singapore]] = tokenised *deposits* (bank liability on-ledger) rather than a reserve-backed stablecoin, a structurally different product. Distribution not computed (no numeric supply/valuation comps). Qualitative read: Monfi is the weakest-regulated of the bank-issued cohort by jurisdiction, strongest only on marketing claim.

**Risk flags.**
1. *Overstated regulatory status / Labuan offshore perimeter.* Monfi Digital Bank is licensed by Labuan FSA (license 230148DB) — an offshore international financial centre, "regulated but not onshore-prudential" (per Labuan FSA Act 1996 / Lexology, as of 2026). The "only regulated bank-issued stablecoin" claim is false relative to SoFiUSD (US national bank) and misleading on supervisory quality: a Labuan license is not equivalent to a GENIUS-Act/FDIC or MiCA regime, so the implied "bank obligation" is only as strong as an offshore resolution framework.
2. *Reserve transparency is promised, not proven.* Reserves are "reconciled daily, published quarterly, annual third-party verification" — but at launch there is no attestation, no named auditor, no disclosed reserve size; the segregated-account-on-balance-sheet structure is asserted via PR, unverified. Second-order: a bank-balance-sheet claim concentrates issuer credit risk onto a single small offshore bank vs USDC's diversified, audited reserve pool.
3. *No scale, no network, dependence on others' rails.* Available only to KYC/KYB'd institutions and qualified private clients; no secondary-market listing at launch; built on Estable + Hadron by Tether. Disintermediation risk — the economic value (reserve yield) and the on-chain plumbing both sit with partners, so Monfi captures a thin spread while a two-issuer duopoly owns liquidity.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Marginal (analysis): one more offshore bank-issued dollar does not dent the USDT/USDC duopoly; the real signal is the post-GENIUS structural pivot toward *bank-liability* digital dollars (SoFi, consortiums, tokenised deposits), where supervisory quality — not marketing superlatives — is the differentiator. Falsifiable thesis: MUSD stays sub-scale and institution-only unless it (a) publishes an independent reserve attestation and (b) secures a major-venue listing within ~2 quarters; absent either, it is a product announcement, not an adopted instrument. Trigger to watch: first MUSD reserve attestation / named auditor, and any tier-1 exchange listing.
