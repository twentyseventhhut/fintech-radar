Fintel plc has acquired Mortgage Brain Holdings, a move that significantly consolidates the UK’s retail financial services infrastructure. By integrating mortgage sourcing and submission technology with its existing wealth management and data assets, Fintel is positioning itself to solve the persistent problem of fragmented data and disconnected workflows for multi-disciplinary financial advisers.

Fintel’s acquisition of Mortgage Brain Holdings includes the core mortgage technology business as well as AE3 Media, which operates a portfolio of digital media and engagement platforms. The deal is designed to bolster Fintel’s Software & Data division, focusing on the digitisation of the mortgage journey—one of the most critical financial decisions for consumers.

Under the new structure, Zahid Bilgrami will continue to lead Mortgage Brain while taking on an expanded role as Chief Executive Officer of Fintel’s Software & Data division. This appointment marks a return for Bilgrami to Defaqto, a business he previously led for eleven years. Fintel has committed to material investment in Mortgage Brain’s technology, specifically targeting the development of AI solutions to streamline adviser workflows and enhance data connectivity.

The acquisition complements Fintel’s existing 49% stake in Plannr, a cloud-native financial planning CRM. A recent partnership between Mortgage Brain and Plannr already allows advisers to move from a client fact-find to a mortgage application within a single workflow. By integrating Mortgage Brain’s sourcing, criteria, affordability, and submission tools directly into its broader ecosystem, Fintel aims to eliminate the need for duplicate data entry across wealth, mortgage, and protection disciplines. This strategy targets the "re-keying" inefficiency that currently plagues advisers using legacy, disconnected systems.

> "Our investment in Plannr reflected our conviction that wealth advisers deserve modern, flexible technology that supports the evolving needs of advice businesses. In a market dominated by legacy tech providers, Plannr has proven that advisers want modern connected systems and they want to own their own data. The acquisition of Mortgage Brain extends that strategy into the mortgage market and strengthens our ability to support the growing number of advisers delivering multiple advice disciplines through a single client relationship."

Matt Timmins, CEO of Fintel

Fintel plc is a major provider of software, data, and support services to the UK retail financial services sector. The company operates across several divisions, providing compliance, regulatory, and technological infrastructure to thousands of intermediary firms. Its portfolio includes Defaqto, a prominent financial information business that provides ratings and insights to help consumers and advisers make informed decisions.

Mortgage Brain has been a fixture of the UK mortgage technology landscape for more than 40 years. It provides a suite of tools for mortgage intermediaries, including sourcing systems, criteria checking, and affordability calculators, as well as submission platforms that link advisers directly to lenders. Its acquisition brings deep industry relationships and a significant volume of transaction data into the Fintel ecosystem.

The deal also includes AE3 Media Ltd, a digital media company that provides engagement platforms and market insight for the financial services industry. By bringing these entities together, Fintel strengthens its position as a central hub for the data and software that powers the "interconnected" advice model, where a single firm manages a client's entire financial life across multiple product categories.

FF News has closely followed Fintel’s expansion strategy within the fintech and regtech sectors. In March 2024, the company announced the Fintel Announces Acquisition of Fintech Provider ifaDASH, a move aimed at enhancing its regulatory and compliance automation capabilities for advisers.

Furthermore, Fintel’s subsidiary, Defaqto, has been active in the M&A market to broaden its data reach. FF News previously reported on how Defaqto Acquires Leading Market Pricing Business to Strengthen Financial Services and Insurance Insight. These prior moves underscore Fintel’s ongoing effort to build a comprehensive data-driven platform that spans the entire retail financial services value chain, from pricing and compliance to front-end advice technology.

This acquisition is a direct challenge to the legacy technology providers that have long dominated the UK mortgage and wealth sectors. By vertically integrating mortgage sourcing with a modern CRM like Plannr, Fintel is betting that the future of the industry lies in "all-in-one" ecosystems rather than best-of-breed silos. This puts significant pressure on standalone software providers who lack the scale to offer cross-disciplinary data connectivity. The move also highlights a shift in the market toward the "multi-disciplinary" adviser. However, the success of this strategy hinges on whether Fintel can truly eliminate operational friction across disparate platforms without compromising the specialist depth that Mortgage Brain’s users expect.

Original Press Release

Fintel plc, a leading provider of software, data and support services to the retail financial services sector, today announces the acquisition of Mortgage Brain Holdings.

The acquisition strengthens Fintel’s Software & Data division and expands its presence in one of the most significant financial decisions consumers make: securing a mortgage. It also represents a huge leap forward in Fintel’s long-term mission to build the infrastructure powering the future of retail financial services. Fintel continues to invest materially in software and data capabilities, reflecting its belief that the future of financial advice will be driven by connected, specialist applications built on modern architecture, powered by trusted data and designed to simplify adviser workflows.

As part of the acquisition, Chief Executive Zahid Bilgrami will continue to lead Mortgage Brain as well as taking an enhanced role within Fintel as Chief Executive Officer of the Software & Data division. This enhanced role will see Mr Bilgrami reunited with Defaqto, a business he ran for eleven years previously.

Fintel will continue to invest materially in the development of technology by Mortgage Brain, supporting the fast-paced development of AI solutions.

As adviser businesses evolve and client needs become increasingly interconnected, technology must evolve too. Increasingly, advisers are operating across multiple disciplines, including wealth, mortgages and protection, yet many continue to rely on fragmented and outdated, legacy technology applications, leading to duplicate data entry and disconnected systems that create operational complexity and limit efficiency.

Fintel believes the future of financial advice will be built on connected specialist applications that work seamlessly together. By improving data connectivity and reducing the need to re-key information, advisers can spend less time on administration and more time supporting clients.

Mortgage Brain has been at the forefront of mortgage technology innovation for more than 40 years, providing sourcing, criteria, affordability and submission solutions that connect advisers and lenders throughout the mortgage journey. Combined with Fintel’s broader software, data, compliance and adviser support capabilities, the acquisition creates opportunities to accelerate innovation, improve adviser productivity and strengthen connectivity across the intermediary market.

Alongside Mortgage Brain, AE3 Media brings a portfolio of respected digital media and engagement platforms, providing additional opportunities to support industry collaboration and market insight.

The transaction works seamlessly alongside Fintel’s investment in specialist financial planning CRM platform Plannr, in which it holds a 49% stake, supporting the evolving needs of the wealth advice sector through a modern, cloud-native platform. Mortgage Brain extends that proposition into the mortgage market, enhancing Fintel’s ability to support the growing population of multi-disciplinary advisers who deliver wealth, mortgage and protection advice through a single client relationship.

As demonstrated by the recent partnership between Mortgage Brain and Plannr, connected adviser technology can significantly simplify workflows and reduce operational friction. By integrating Mortgage Brain’s market-leading sourcing, criteria, affordability and submission capabilities directly into the Plannr platform, advisers can now move seamlessly from client fact-find through to mortgage application within a single workflow.

Looking ahead, Fintel sees significant opportunities to further connect technology, data and workflows across the advice ecosystem. Central to this vision is a belief that advice firms should retain control of their own data while benefiting from specialist applications that integrate seamlessly together. By reducing friction and improving data connectivity, Fintel aims to help advisers work more efficiently and deliver better outcomes for advisers, lenders and consumers.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 23 October 2026, AIM-listed **Fintel plc** (LSE: FNTL) announced it had acquired **Mortgage Brain Holdings** — taking full control two years after buying an initial 5.8% stake for £1.5m in March 2024. Total consideration is **£38.4m**: £28.9m cash on completion (from existing resources) plus £9.5m deferred for 18 months. The acquired group includes Mortgage Brain Limited, criteria/affordability tools (Criteria Hub), submission platform Mortgage Engine, and **AE3 Media**, a financial-services digital-media/events business. The deal strengthens Fintel's Software & Data division. (Sources: [Introducer Today](https://www.introducertoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2026/10/fintel-acquires-mortgage-brain-holdings-in-38-4m-deal/), [Mortgage Strategy](https://www.mortgagestrategy.co.uk/news/fintel-buys-mortgage-brain-holdings-in-38-4m-deal/), [The Cap Table](https://www.thecaptable.co.uk/article/fintel-takes-full-control-of-mortgage-brain-for-38-4m-two-years-after-buying-a-5-8-stake-for-1-5m).)

**[1] Who the parties are.** Fintel is a leading UK provider of software, data and support services to retail financial services, best known for its ratings/data business **Defaqto**. It is a serial acquirer: FY2025 revenue +10% to £85.9m, adjusted EBITDA +17% to £25.9m; H1 2026 revenue +5.3% to £38.6m with recurring subscriptions ~57% of revenue. It has made nine Software & Data acquisitions (VouchedFor, Synaptic, etc.) folded into a unified "Defaqto Unity" platform. Mortgage Brain has been a UK mortgage-tech fixture for 40+ years, providing sourcing, criteria, affordability and lender-submission tools; its contracted customers write ~40% of UK first-charge residential and buy-to-let applications. (Sources: [Yahoo/Fintel FY2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/fintel-plc-lse-fntl-full-010224520.html), [The Market Reporter](https://www.themarketreporter.co.uk/a/13399833/fintel-buys-mortgage-brain-for-38-4m-to-boost-software-and-ai-capabiliti).)

**[2] Strategic rationale.** Fintel's thesis is vertical integration of adviser tech: combine Mortgage Brain's mortgage rails with its 49%-owned cloud-native wealth CRM **Plannr** (a recent Mortgage Brain–Plannr partnership already lets advisers go fact-find → mortgage application in one workflow), plus Defaqto data and compliance. The target is the "multi-disciplinary adviser" (wealth + mortgage + protection) who today re-keys data across disconnected legacy systems. CEO Matt Timmins frames it as building "the infrastructure powering the future of retail financial services." Zahid Bilgrami keeps leading Mortgage Brain and becomes CEO of Fintel's Software & Data division — a reunion with Defaqto, which he ran for 11 years.

**[3] Financials / what Fintel is buying.** Mortgage Brain is guided to ~£12m revenue in 2027 (£10m Software & Data, £2m events) with EBITDA ~£3.8m — implying roughly a 10x forward EBITDA multiple on the £38.4m price. Material further investment is promised, explicitly to accelerate **AI** adviser-workflow tooling. The ~40% application-flow footprint gives Fintel a large transaction-data asset to feed Defaqto's data/ratings franchise.

**[4] Internal context — related moves in the vault.** This extends a clear Fintel/Defaqto roll-up pattern: [[Defaqto acquires Pearson Ham Market Pricing for £11M]] (Feb 2026, insurance/market-pricing data). It sits within active UK mortgage-tech consolidation: [[Monzo completes acquisition of mortgage broker Habito]] (May 2026), [[MQube tokenises £1.3 billion mortgage debt]] (Oct 2025), [[HSBC first lender to automate remortgages with LMS DART]] (May 2026), and adviser-CRM/AI adjacencies like [[PlannerPal secures $6M for AI client intelligence in wealth]] and [[SmartSearch and Plannr partner on AML compliance]]. Primary source: [ffnews.com](https://ffnews.com/news/fintel-strengthens-software-data-strategy-with-acquisition-of-mortgage-brai-9158f3a5).

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
1. The note's headline ("build connected fintech ecosystem") is PR framing. What actually changed hands is a £38.4m full buyout of a company Fintel already held 5.8% of since March 2024 — is this a strategic leap or just mopping up a stake?
2. £38.4m for ~£3.8m 2027E EBITDA is ~10x forward. Is that cheap or full for a 40-year-old legacy mortgage-tech business facing its own re-platforming costs?
3. The £12m 2027 revenue guide is forward-looking — what is Mortgage Brain's *current* (2026) revenue and EBITDA, and is it growing or flat/declining?
4. "~40% of UK first-charge residential and BTL applications" — is that share of applications *touched by MB software* via contracted customers, not MB's market share of originations. How defensible is it versus lender direct channels and rivals (Twenty7Tec, Iress)?
5. AE3 Media (events/digital media) is a cyclical, lower-margin business bundled in. How much of the £38.4m and the £2m "events" revenue rides on it, and is it strategic or ballast?
6. Integration risk: Fintel claims nine prior S&D acquisitions into "Defaqto Unity." Is there independent evidence those integrations delivered synergies, or is this acquisitive revenue masking weak organic growth (H1 2026 organic +2.0% only)?
7. The Plannr tie-up is via a 49% minority stake, not control. Can Fintel actually force deep MB↔Plannr integration without owning Plannr outright — and does a future Plannr buyout loom?
8. Conflict/neutrality: Defaqto sells "independent" ratings while Fintel now owns distribution rails and application data. Does vertical integration compromise the perceived independence that is Defaqto's core asset?
9. Regulatory: does an FCA/CMA competition angle exist if Fintel controls both mortgage sourcing software AND a large share of application flow plus adviser data? Any Consumer Duty data-use concerns?
10. Zahid Bilgrami now runs MB, the S&D division, and reunites with Defaqto — key-person concentration. What retention/earn-out ties him in, and what happens if he leaves?
11. The £9.5m deferred-for-18-months slug implies earn-out/warranty protection. On what conditions is it payable, and does it signal uncertainty about MB's near-term performance?
12. "Material investment in AI" is unquantified. Is there a committed capex/opex figure, or is AI a narrative overlay on a sourcing/submission tool?
13. Source concentration: the note body is a near-verbatim press release (ffnews). External deal terms come from trade press. Is there an RNS/regulatory filing confirming the £38.4m and payment split independently of Fintel's own PR?
14. Customer-switching risk: if advisers resent a monoculture/"all-in-one" lock-in, could MB's intermediary customers defect to best-of-breed rivals during integration churn?

Importance: 3/5 — A clear, fully-funded £38.4m all-cash UK mortgage-tech consolidation with a credible strategic logic (adviser-workflow integration, ~40% application-flow data asset) and named leadership. Matters regionally and for UK adviser-tech consolidation, but it is a modest-sized bolt-on for an AIM small-cap with limited global read-through, hence mid-tier rather than high.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** UK mortgage-tech sits inside a fragmented, consolidating adviser-software stack. Three sourcing engines dominate the intermediary market — Iress (Lender Connect), Mortgage Brain, and Twenty7Tec — all plumbing broker→lender workflows; AMI has historically called these "our three main sourcing engines" and they jointly worked to standardise connectivity language (per Twenty7Tec/Mortgage Finance Gazette, 22-Mar-2022). Barriers are lender-integration depth, compliance/criteria data, and adviser switching costs rather than capital. "Why now": advisers increasingly run multi-discipline books (wealth + mortgage + protection) on disconnected legacy tools, and the economics of selling data+software recurring revenue beat one-off licences — Fintel's own Software & Data recurring revenue was £12.3m of £18.4m division revenue in H1 2025 (per investegate RNS, 15-Sep-2025). TAM figure: no data (no verifiable UK mortgage-software market size sourced).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: recurring/ARR mix, lender-panel coverage, and sourcing-market share among directly-authorised brokers. Players: Iress (ASX-listed, broad), Twenty7Tec (said to be most-used among directly-authorised brokers per industry surveys), Mortgage Brain (positioned as the most widely used point-of-sale/sourcing/compliance provider; 40+ year franchise). Basis of competition: lender connectivity, data breadth, and bundling into adviser CRMs. Recent moves: Fintel bought a 5.8% Mortgage Brain stake for £1.5m in Mar-2024, then took full control for £38.4m on 23-Oct-2026 (per thecaptable/mortgagestrategy). Fintel's position: catching-up-via-M&A into a vertically-integrated "all-in-one" ecosystem — pairing Mortgage Brain sourcing with its 49% Plannr CRM stake and Defaqto data. Moat `(analysis)`: switching costs + data/compliance network effects across the adviser desktop; thin vs best-of-breed depth risk.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal: whole-business implied value ≈ £40.8m (£38.4m for the remainder; £28.9m cash at completion, £9.5m deferred 18m). Mortgage Brain turnover ≈ £8m FY-Jun-2026 (up 21% from £6.6m); Fintel guides ~£12m revenue and ~£3.8m EBITDA for 2027 (per theindustryspread/mortgagesoup).
- EV/Revenue (trailing) = £40.8m / £8m = **~5.1x** — rich vs Fintel parent.
- EV/Revenue (2027E) = £40.8m / £12m = **~3.4x**.
- EV/EBITDA (2027E) = £40.8m / £3.8m = **~10.7x** — broadly in-line with Fintel parent.
- Fintel plc (FNTL, AIM): EV ≈ £235m, FY2025 revenue £85.9m, adj. EBITDA £25.9m → EV/Sales ≈ **2.9x**, EV/EBITDA ≈ **11.2x** (per stockanalysis/tradingview).
Flag: trailing EV/Rev ~5.1x is well above Fintel's own ~2.9x, but on forward EBITDA (~10.7x) it is ~in-line — the premium is justified only if the 21% growth + £3.8m EBITDA guide land. Internal comps (UK mortgage/adviser tech consolidation): [[nCino unveils mortgage-focused AI innovations]], [[Vernon Building Society partners with FintechOS on mortgages]], [[HSBC first lender to automate remortgages with LMS DART]]; and for scale-M&A precedent in mortgage-tech [[Rocket closes $14.2 billion acquisition of Mr. Cooper]].

**Risk flags.**
1. Execution/synergy risk — value hinges on Fintel delivering the ~£12m-rev / £3.8m-EBITDA 2027 guide while integrating Mortgage Brain + AE3 Media; the trailing ~5.1x EV/Rev leaves no margin for a growth stall. Second-order: if growth disappoints, the premium becomes a write-down drag on a group already on ~11x.
2. Specialist-depth vs all-in-one trade-off — the PR's own closing line concedes success "hinges on" eliminating friction "without compromising the specialist depth" Mortgage Brain users expect; forced migration onto the Plannr/Defaqto stack risks churn to Twenty7Tec/Iress.
3. Mortgage-cycle & concentration — revenue is geared to UK intermediary mortgage volumes; a rate-driven slowdown in transactions compresses sourcing usage. The deal economics (and AE3 ad-media revenue) are silent on cyclicality.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is consolidation, not re-rating: Fintel is vertically integrating the UK adviser desktop (sourcing + CRM + data) and pressuring standalone sourcing peers who lack cross-discipline connectivity. Falsifiable thesis: within 12–18 months watch whether Mortgage Brain + Plannr single-workflow adoption grows recurring Software & Data revenue faster than organic — a visible uplift confirms the ecosystem play; flat sourcing share or broker churn to Twenty7Tec/Iress would break it.
