SBI DigiTrust has started testing stablecoin-based remittances and QR payments between Japan and South Korea with NICE Information & Telecommunication and DSRV, with the three companies targeting completion by the end of December 2026.
SBI DigiTrust, NICE and DSRV are testing stablecoin payments for Japanese travelers visiting South Korea.
The partners expect their joint verification work to finish by the end of December 2026.
The test examines QR payments, fund flows, payment instructions, system connections and commercial viability together.
NICE brings a Korean merchant network spanning roughly 1.2 million merchants into the joint study.
The partners have not named a stablecoin, blockchain, exchange model or commercial launch date yet.
SBI Group said on Oct. 1 that the partners are testing a scenario in which Japanese travelers pay at NICE-affiliated merchants in South Korea by scanning QR codes. The work covers how money moves from the Japanese customer to the Korean merchant, what payment instructions are needed and how the companies’ systems would connect.
 SBI stablecoin test focuses on tourist QR payments 
The official announcement says the companies are studying a practical checkout flow instead of limiting the project to blockchain transfers between test wallets. NICE contributes its existing payment infrastructure and merchant operations, while DSRV examines the blockchain architecture needed to support the settlement process.
SBI DigiTrust handles the Japanese regulatory, financial and digital-asset side of the project. The SBI Group company said its role includes designing the payment structure while considering cooperation with other SBI businesses.
From the Korean side, NICE said the companies signed their three-way memorandum at its Seoul headquarters on Sept. 30. The test is expected to run through December before the companies decide how the model could be developed commercially.
The parties have not disclosed which stablecoin will be used. Their announcements do not identify a blockchain, wallet provider, exchange-rate system or settlement currency, and they have not announced a launch date for a commercial service.
 NICE gives the test access to a large merchant network 
NICE operates payment services across South Korea and supports roughly 1.2 million merchants nationwide, according to SBI’s announcement. The current verification uses NICE-affiliated merchants as the test setting, though neither company has said that all 1.2 million locations will participate.
NICE will examine merchant operations, connections with existing payment systems and the business case for stablecoin settlement. The company’s role gives the partners a way to study how a blockchain-based transfer could connect with payment infrastructure already used by Korean stores.
DSRV is responsible for the technical side. SBI said the Korean blockchain company will study system design, stablecoin requirements and connections between blockchain infrastructure and the existing systems used by the other partners.
The verification will examine transaction size and cost alongside technical feasibility. The partners said those findings will be used when considering whether a commercial service makes sense, meaning the December target is a testing deadline rather than a public launch date.
 Stablecoin rules differ between Japan and South Korea 
Regulation remains part of the test because Japan and South Korea are at different stages in their stablecoin frameworks.
Japan already regulates fiat-linked stablecoins as electronic payment instruments. The Financial Services Agency states that businesses providing services involving electronic payment instruments in Japan must operate under the relevant Payment Services Act registration requirements.
Japan expanded its digital-asset framework on June 1, introducing a registration system for businesses that intermediate certain stablecoin and crypto services on behalf of registered providers. The FSA confirmed that the new intermediary regime became effective on that date.
South Korea is still working through its rules for won-linked stablecoins. SBI’s official announcement specifically says the Korean regulatory framework remains under development and that the partners will continue reviewing policy changes while the test runs.
The Bank of Korea has been conducting its own work on digital payments, tokenized deposits and CBDC infrastructure while participating in discussions over won stablecoin legislation. Its 2025 payment systems report said the central bank had increased its research on virtual assets and was contributing to the policy debate around KRW stablecoins.
 SBI is testing several Japan-Korea payment routes 
The NICE and DSRV project is not SBI’s first stablecoin test connecting Japan and South Korea.
In August, SBI began work on a separate Japan-Korea stablecoin network using Canton with South Korean blockchain infrastructure company Nodeinfra. That project is examining payment tokens and eventual regulated stablecoin settlement between financial institutions.
Another experiment reached the testing stage in September. Kyobo Life and SBI completed a yen-won stablecoin payment pilot that tested direct exchange between representations of yen and won without routing settlement through the U.S. dollar.
The Kyobo test used the Canton Network and covered transfer, foreign exchange, settlement, tracking and reconciliation. No institutional funds moved in that pilot, and the companies did not announce a production launch date.
SBI Remit has pursued a separate international payment path. SBI Remit partnered with Fasset on cross-border stablecoin infrastructure covering remittances, payment services and settlement across multiple markets.
SBI Group has been building regulated stablecoin products inside Japan as well. Ripple and SBI VC Trade launched RLUSD in the country in June after receiving the required regulatory approval, giving Japanese customers access to the dollar-backed stablecoin through a licensed local distributor.
 December results will determine the next step 
For the current project, SBI DigiTrust said the partners will use the remaining months of 2026 to map the complete payment process from the Japanese user to the Korean merchant.
The work will cover fund movement, payment and settlement instructions, connections between company systems, transaction costs and the scale at which a future service could operate. The partners intend to identify operational and regulatory issues that would have to be addressed before commercialization.
SBI said the companies will use the findings as material for future business decisions once the verification concludes. The announcement sets the end of December 2026 as the completion target but does not commit NICE, DSRV or SBI DigiTrust to launching a commercial Japan-Korea stablecoin payment service afterward.

# Context-enrichment: SBI tests stablecoin QR payments for Japan-Korea travel
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

**FRESHNESS / DUPLICATE VERDICT: FRESH (but one of several SBI Japan-Korea experiments — see de-dup below).** New, distinct event: a three-way MOU (signed 2026-09-30 at NICE's Seoul HQ; announced ~Oct 1–2) between **SBI DigiTrust** (Japan), **NICE Information & Telecommunication** (Korea) and **DSRV** (Seoul blockchain infra) to test stablecoin QR payments by Japanese tourists at Korean merchants, joint verification due **end-Dec 2026**. It is NOT the SBI–Mesh–Money Forward domestic crypto-payments JV ([[SBI, Mesh and Money Forward plan Japan crypto payments JV]], 2026-10-08: different partners, Japan-domestic, non-binding JV shell), and NOT the domestic Korean won PoC by [[Toss and Gwangju Bank complete stablecoin QR payment PoC]] (2026-10-08: won-for-won, domestic, no cross-border/tourist leg). It is also distinct from, but part of the same SBI push as, two prior Japan-Korea tests on the Canton Network (see [1]).

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- **Non-binding three-way MOU**, not a product. SBI DigiTrust designs the payment structure + handles the Japan-side regulatory/financial/digital-asset work; **NICE** contributes its Korean merchant network (~1.2M merchants nationwide — but the test uses a subset of NICE-affiliated merchants, NOT all 1.2M); **DSRV** studies the blockchain architecture, stablecoin requirements and system interconnection.
- **Use case:** a Japanese traveler in Korea scans a QR at a NICE-affiliated merchant and pays with a stablecoin; the study maps fund flow (JP customer → KR merchant), payment/settlement instructions, system connections, transaction size/cost and commercial viability.
- **What is NOT disclosed (load-bearing):** no named stablecoin, no blockchain/ledger, no wallet provider, no FX/exchange-rate mechanism, no settlement currency, no transaction caps, no commercial launch date. The Dec-2026 date is a **testing deadline, not a launch**; the parties explicitly do not commit to commercializing.
- → Why structured this way: SBI supplies the Japan licence + design, Korea partners supply merchant rails (NICE) and chain research (DSRV). It is a feasibility study dressed as a corridor announcement; the headline "QR payments for Japan-Korea travel" overstates a lab exercise with zero live money.

## [1] SBI's broader Japan-Korea stablecoin program (the real frame)
This is the **third** SBI Japan-Korea stablecoin experiment in ~3 months, each with different partners/rails — a portfolio of bets, not one coherent product:
1. **Aug 2026** — SBI + Korean infra firm **Nodeinfra** started a Japan-Korea stablecoin network on the **Canton Network** (institutional FI settlement).
2. **Sep 2026** — **Kyobo Life + SBI** completed a **yen-won** stablecoin pilot on **Canton**, testing direct yen↔won exchange without routing through USD (transfer, FX, settlement, tracking, reconciliation; no institutional funds moved). Note Kyobo's Korea digital-asset activity also appears in [[Crypto.com and Kyobo Lifeplanet partner on digital assets in Korea]].
3. **Oct 2026 (this note)** — SBI DigiTrust + NICE + DSRV, **retail tourist QR** at Korean merchants.
Also adjacent: **SBI Remit × Fasset** cross-border stablecoin remittance infra.
- → So the novelty of THIS item is the **retail/tourist QR + merchant-acceptance** angle (vs. the institutional Canton tests). But it uses yet another partner set and another (unnamed) rail — fragmentation, not convergence.

## [2] Internal comps (Korea won-stablecoin QR race + SBI Japan stack)
- [[Toss and Gwangju Bank complete stablecoin QR payment PoC]] (Oct 2026) — domestic won-for-won QR, atomic pay+settle; the domestic mirror of this cross-border test. Same "QR stablecoin payment" theme, different geography.
- [[Danal and BNK Busan Bank validate Korean Won stablecoin]] (Jun 2026) — full-lifecycle won-stablecoin validation (payment processing cut to ~0.3s); a domestic acceptance-rail comp.
- [[SBI and Startale to launch Japan's first trust yen stablecoin]] and [[Ripple and SBI launch RLUSD stablecoin in Japan]] — SBI's own Japan-issued stablecoins (JPYSC yen; RLUSD dollar, ¥1m retail cap) that could plausibly be the JP-side instrument in a corridor product, though none is named here.
- [[South Korea stalls Digital Asset Basic Act over stablecoins]] (Jan 2026) — the Korean regulatory blocker: no won-stablecoin legal framework in force yet.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low-to-moderate.** Cross-border tourist stablecoin QR is a coherent, real-world use case (tourism is a natural stablecoin/FX-friction pain point), and the ~1.2M NICE merchant footprint is a genuine distribution asset. But "QR stablecoin payment" is a crowded Oct-2026 theme (Toss/Gwangju, Danal/BNK, Samsung-USDC).
- **Traction: zero.** No entity, no named token/chain, no merchants onboarded, no volume, no live money. Every concrete figure (1.2M merchants, Dec-2026) is about scope/scale targets, not adoption.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Regulatory asymmetry is the binding constraint.** Japan already regulates fiat-linked stablecoins as electronic payment instruments (PSA; intermediary registration regime effective 2026-06-01). **South Korea has NO won-stablecoin framework in force** — the Digital Asset Basic Act Phase 2 is stalled (BOK vs FSC turf fight), with passage targeted end-2026. So a cross-border corridor needs BOTH regimes aligned; the Korea leg has no legal issuance basis yet. The partners say they will track Korean policy changes during the test.
- Watch for: a named stablecoin + chain, FX mechanism, transaction caps, and whether the Dec-2026 findings lead to a definitive agreement vs. quietly lapsing. Absent those, this is a feasibility study timed to the regulatory debate.

9. How is this different from the SBI-Mesh-Money Forward JV? — **Different partners, geography and structure.** That is a Japan-domestic crypto-payments JV shell; this is a cross-border KR-tourist QR study.
10. How is this different from Toss/Gwangju? — **Toss/Gwangju is domestic won-for-won QR;** this is cross-border inbound-tourist QR. Adjacent theme, non-overlapping use case.

It is the third SBI Japan-Korea stablecoin experiment in three months with yet another partner set, signaling direction more than a shippable product. The real merchant-acceptance asset (NICE) and the tourist corridor are the interesting ingredients; otherwise it is one data point in a crowded, pre-regulatory Asian QR-stablecoin race. Not a 1 (named parties, concrete corridor, hard Dec deadline), but capped at 2 by zero traction and the Korea regulatory gate.

**Sector & drivers.** Subvertical: **cross-border stablecoin / QR-payment interoperability on the Japan↔Korea travel corridor** — stablecoin settlement wired into an existing merchant-acceptance rail, not a crypto-native wallet. Corridor size is real and growing: South-Korean visitors spent **¥318.2bn in Japan in Q1-2026 (+12.7% YoY)** and **¥248.9bn in Apr–Jun 2026 (+12.2%)**, and Korea was Japan's #1 inbound source in Apr-2026 (**878,600 visitors**) (per Japan Tourism Agency/JTB via travelvoice.jp, 2026); the reverse leg (Japanese spend in Korea) is substantial enough that Japanese travelers were among the biggest Alipay+ spenders in Korea in 2025 (per Alipay+/fintechnews, 2025). So the *travel-payment* demand the test targets exists and is double-digit-growing. But the **stablecoin** slice is near-zero today: no won stablecoin is legally issuable yet, and global stablecoin supply is ~99% USD-denominated — a won/yen cross-border coin is a sovereignty-driven greenfield, not a sized market ("no data" on stablecoin-corridor TAM). **Why now (two-sided regulatory clock):** Japan's revised Payment Services Act took full effect **2026-06-13**, and from **2026-06-01** the FSA recognizes foreign trust-type stablecoins (USDC-style) as "electronic payment instruments" with an intermediary registration regime (per FSA/globallawexperts, cryptoslate, 2026) — Japan has a live licensed path. Korea does NOT: the FSC's won-stablecoin bill (Phase 2 of the Virtual Asset User Protection Act / Digital Asset Basic Act) is only **targeted for submission ~Oct-2026, passage goal end-2026**, with a **bank-centred model (consortium banks holding ≥50%+1 share), 100% safe-asset reserves** the leading structure (per Cointelegraph/FSC, bitmarkets, 2026). So the test runs with one leg (JP) legally live and the other (KR) pre-legislation — exactly why SBI frames Dec-2026 as a *verification* deadline, not a launch. Structure: value chain = issuance (unnamed) + blockchain (DSRV studies) + acceptance/merchant rail (NICE's ~1.2M merchants) + FX/settlement; value accrues to whoever owns the licensed issuance + merchant acquiring, while the chain and the QR front-end commoditize.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Asymmetric regulation is the binding gate — and the Korean leg has no legal basis.** Japan's EPI/PSA regime is live (Jun-2026), but Korea cannot legally issue a won stablecoin until the Oct-2026 bill passes (goal end-2026); worse, Korea's leading **bank-anchored (≥50%+1) model** could leave a non-bank corridor operator with only thin acceptance/FX fees while licensed banks keep reserve float. 2. **Incumbent displacement risk — the corridor is already solved without stablecoins.** KakaoPay-PayPay and Alipay+ already give travelers seamless JP-KR QR acceptance across millions of merchants on existing rails. A stablecoin version must win on FX spread / settlement cost / finality, none of which SBI has quantified — if it only replicates existing UX, there is no adoption pull and the test becomes plumbing the incumbents disintermediate.
3. **Vapor-spec execution risk / disintermediation of SBI's own leg.** No named stablecoin, chain, wallet, FX model, volume or launch date; NICE owns the 1.2M-merchant distribution and DSRV the chain — SBI supplies the JP regulatory wrapper, i.e. the piece most easily substituted once economics appear. Classic pre-regulation flag-planting (this is one of ≥3 SBI JP-KR stablecoin tests running in parallel), with high MOU-lapse risk and no disclosed fraud-liability/refund/redemption mechanism.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Not a re-rating — it is SBI extending a *multi-track* JP-KR stablecoin hedge (Nodeinfra/Canton, Kyobo, Fasset, now NICE/DSRV) to buy optionality on a double-digit-growing travel corridor while Korea writes its rulebook. Falsifiable thesis: *SBI's durable edge is the breadth of its licensed JP crypto stack + first-mover corridor experiments, NOT this specific NICE tie-up, which is cheap feasibility-stage flag-planting against live incumbents.* Trigger that would make it material: the Dec-2026 verification ships with a **named issuer + chain + disclosed FX/cost advantage over KakaoPay-PayPay/Alipay+**, plus Korea's bill passing with fintech-friendly issuer eligibility. What breaks it: a bank-majority Korean model + incumbents (Alipay+, KakaoPay-PayPay) deepening their own corridor rails, commoditizing stablecoin settlement into invisible plumbing with no consumer pull.
