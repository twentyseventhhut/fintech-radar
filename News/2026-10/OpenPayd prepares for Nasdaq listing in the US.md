OpenPayd Sets Groundwork for 2027 US Stock Market Listing 
Payments infrastructure firm OpenPayd is reportedly setting the stage to go public in the U.S.

The U.K. company expects to list on the Nasdaq by the year’s end as it prepares to enter the America market and embark on new acquisitions, CEO Iana Dimitrova said in an interview with CoinDesk published Sunday (Oct. 4).
OpenPayd is in the final stages of the Securities and Exchange Commission’s (SEC) review of its proposed merger with Titan Acquisition Corp. , Dimitrova said, adding that the deal is expected to close this year, assuming no major external disruptions.
The CoinDesk report noted that the U.S. has become a more attractive market for crypto companies as it establishes rules for digital assets and stablecoins.
The GENIUS Act created a federal framework for payment stablecoins, while the SEC has proposed rules designed for some crypto assets.

For companies like OpenPayd, the report said that shift provides an opening to sell payments infrastructure to businesses linking traditional finance with blockchain networks, even as broader market-structure legislation is still unresolved.
The report added that crypto companies are taking a variety of roads to going public. For example, stablecoin payments company RedotPay is preparing for an initial public offering (IPO), while Kraken parent Payward has postponed its listing back to the middle of 2027 at the earliest, CoinDesk added.
OpenPayd has integrated with Circle Payments Network for cross-border transactions and is part of Fireblocks’ payments network, giving other participants access to its fiat infrastructure, the report said.
The company is a “global infrastructure platform for modern money movement,” Dimitrova told CoinDesk, and “there is no public market competitor that has the same combination of fiat and stablecoin capabilities that OpenPayd can deliver today.”
The listing would help finance U.S. expansion, with OpenPayd hoping to launch services for customers there by April of next year, she added.
PYMNTS spoke with Dimitrova in June about her company’s argument that modern payment infrastructure has become a business problem, not just a technical one, and that orchestration can provide the answer.
“It’s not the job of businesses to think about the infrastructure payment and to think about regulation,” she told PYMNTS CEO Webster in June. “They have to focus on what they do best, which is developing and selling their products and services.”

# Context-enrichment: OpenPayd prepares for Nasdaq listing in the US
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**FRESHNESS: fresh.** This is the first note on OpenPayd's US-listing plan. Prior OpenPayd notes cover product/people, not this capital-markets event: [[OpenPayd triples currency account access to 70+ currencies]] (2025-10, product) and [[OpenPayd appoints Yasemin Swanson as COO]] (2025-10, leadership). The event itself — the de-SPAC merger with Titan Acquisition Corp. — has no prior note in the corpus.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- The framing "prepares for a Nasdaq listing" is softer than reality: this is a **de-SPAC merger**, not a traditional IPO. OpenPayd is merging into **Titan Acquisition Corp.** (Nasdaq: TACHU), a blank-check SPAC. Definitive business-combination agreement announced **2026-06-01**; combined company to trade as **"OP"**. (SEC Form 425, 2026-06-01.)
- CEO Iana Dimitrova (CoinDesk, ~2026-10-03) says the deal is in the **"final stages of SEC review"** and expected to **close by year-end 2026**. → Why this matters: "final stages of SEC review" is CEO framing, not a legal milestone. It does NOT mean the S-4/registration statement is effective; SEC comment cycles routinely slip de-SPAC closings past target dates. (analysis)
- **Pro-forma equity value ~$1.145B** (Form 425). Trust cash **up to $276M gross, assuming zero redemptions** (Titan IPO 2025-04-10: 27.6M units × $10). No PIPE disclosed in the Form 425 reviewed (unconfirmed). → Why structured this way: the "$276M" headline is conditional — SPAC redemptions frequently gut trust proceeds (often 50-90%+), so real cash into the combined company could be a fraction of that, with sponsor promote + warrants adding dilution. (analysis)
- **Metric mismatch to watch:** PR leads with "ARR >$85M" and "annualized transaction volume >$240B" (Form 425, as of March 2026). But the CoinDesk-reported audited line (FY to 2026-04-30) is **revenue $73M** (vs $57M prior year), **EBITDA $13M**, **net loss $2.8M** (one-off $5.8M transaction costs). → ARR flatters the real base. On $73M revenue, a $1.145B equity value is ~15.7x revenue — demanding for a thin-margin payments business. (analysis)
- **US revenue is prospective, not present:** US customer launch targeted **April 2027**, after the listing, on 43 state money-transmitter licenses acquired via MSB USA (Sept 2026). The listing funds a forward thesis. (CoinDesk.)

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **BVNK** (stablecoin infra): Mastercard acquiring for up to $1.8B (2026-03); prior ~$750M Series B / bidding war with Coinbase ([[Coinbase and Mastercard in talks to buy stablecoin firm BVNK]]).
- **Banking Circle** (BaaS/cross-border): EQT reportedly exploring a sale at ~$3-4B (unconfirmed).
- **Solaris** (German BaaS): distressed — valuation collapsed to ~€90M from a €1.6B peak (2025-02).
- **Griffin** (UK BaaS, full bank licence): small, $24M raise.
- **Stripe/Bridge**, **Circle** (public, USDC issuer), **Fireblocks, Modulr, ClearBank** — infra peers.
- **Position:** OpenPayd's ~$1.145B mark sits below BVNK ($1.8B) and Banking Circle's rumored $3-4B, well above distressed Solaris. → Why it matters: the sector is consolidating via **trade sales to strategics** (BVNK→Mastercard, Bridge→Stripe), which paid premiums for scarce fiat+stablecoin infra. OpenPayd choosing a **SPAC** is the odd one out — typically the path taken when a trade sale or traditional IPO at the desired price isn't on the table. Second-order: a SPAC exit is a weaker demand signal than a strategic bidding war. (analysis)

## [2] Company history / fit
- Founded **2018, London**, by **Dr Ozan Özerk** (also founder of European Merchant Bank, MSB USA, Ozan SuperApp). CEO **Iana Dimitrova** (ex-Paysafe legal, joined 2017). Recently scaled: tripled currency access to 70+ currencies ([[OpenPayd triples currency account access to 70+ currencies]]), hired ex-ClearBank COO ([[OpenPayd appoints Yasemin Swanson as COO]]) "to secure additional regulatory milestones" — consistent with a public-listing run-up.
- → Why it acts this way: OpenPayd is a commodity-adjacent fiat-rails business chasing a software/stablecoin-infra multiple. Going public now lets it (a) finance US expansion, (b) use stock as acquisition currency ("pursue new acquisitions" per Dimitrova). The GENIUS Act's stablecoin framework is the stated tailwind for a "fiat+stablecoin, single API" pitch. (analysis)
- → Related-party flag: thin/opaque funding history (undisclosed Series B amount) plus founder-affiliated entities (European Merchant Bank, MSB USA) warrant proxy/S-4 scrutiny. (analysis)

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new:** the capital-markets event (public listing) is new for OpenPayd. The underlying product is not new but the stablecoin integrations are recent and concrete: **Circle partnership** (2025-06-17), live on **Fireblocks Network for Payments** (2026-07-23, first payments-infra provider in UK/Europe, ~2,400 participants reach OpenPayd fiat rails), **Circle Payments Network** integration (2026-08-25, near-instant cross-border fiat via USDC/EURC behind one API).
- **Traction (real, audited):** $73M revenue, $13M EBITDA, 1,100+ customers across 180 countries, named clients Kraken, OKX, B2C2. → Where value is captured: OpenPayd sits as the **fiat on/off-ramp + licensing layer** feeding stablecoin networks — a defensible position IF it stays the regulated connective tissue. But crypto-exchange client concentration (Kraken/OKX/B2C2) ties revenue to crypto-trading cyclicality and counterparty risk. (analysis)
- → What underpins vs breaks the multiple: a ~15.7x revenue mark rests on the stablecoin-infra growth story; it breaks if stablecoin flows commoditize the fiat ramp or if strategics (Mastercard via BVNK, Circle) disintermediate independent infra providers. (hypothesis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Near-term:** targeting listing by year-end 2026 (de-SPAC close), US launch April 2027. Dimitrova flags acquisitions post-listing.
- **Regulatory backdrop:** GENIUS Act (signed 2025-07-18) created the US payment-stablecoin framework — the stated reason the US is now attractive.
- **Peer pipeline is wobbly:** Kraken/Payward pushed its IPO to Q2 2027+; RedotPay's ~$1B US IPO reportedly on hold / now targeting larger raise (conflicting reports). → Why it matters: with stronger crypto names waiting for traditional IPOs, OpenPayd's "year-end 2026 via SPAC" looks aggressive — and the chosen vehicle signals a lower-bar path than peers. (analysis)
- **Risks:** redemption/dilution gutting trust cash; SEC timeline slip; US launch execution; crypto-client concentration; related-party cap table. Second-order: a heavily-redeemed de-SPAC can leave OpenPayd public but under-capitalized relative to its $1.1B mark — worse than staying private. (hypothesis)

## Top challenge / red-team questions

1. **IPO or SPAC?** De-SPAC merger with Titan Acquisition Corp. (Nasdaq: TACHU), not a traditional IPO. (SEC Form 425, 2026-06-01.) The "listing" framing understates the vehicle.
2. **Is "final stages of SEC review" a real milestone?** No — it's CEO framing. No confirmation the S-4/registration statement is effective; de-SPAC timelines routinely slip. (open on exact S-4 status.)
3. **What is the real valuation?** ~$1.145B pro-forma equity value (Form 425) — a negotiated mark, not market-tested. EV not disclosed in filings reviewed. (open.)
4. **How much cash actually lands?** "Up to $276M in trust" assumes ZERO redemptions; SPAC redemptions commonly run 50-90%+. Real proceeds could be a fraction. No PIPE disclosed (unconfirmed).
5. **ARR vs audited revenue?** PR leads with $85M ARR (March 2026); audited FY-to-April-2026 revenue is $73M. The smaller audited figure is the real base; ~15.7x revenue is rich for a thin-margin payments firm ($13M EBITDA).
6. **Is US revenue present or prospective?** Prospective — US launch is April 2027, after the listing. The growth thesis funding the valuation is forward-looking.
7. **Why a SPAC when peers were acquired by strategics?** BVNK→Mastercard ($1.8B), Bridge→Stripe (~$1.1B) were trade-sale premiums. A SPAC is typically chosen when a trade sale / traditional IPO at the desired price isn't available — a weaker demand signal. (analysis.)
8. **Client concentration risk?** Named clients are crypto exchanges (Kraken, OKX, B2C2) — exposure to crypto-trading cyclicality and counterparty/regulatory risk. How much of $73M revenue is from them? (open.)
9. **Who captures the margin?** OpenPayd is the regulated fiat on/off-ramp feeding stablecoin networks (Circle, Fireblocks). Defensible if it stays the connective tissue — but strategics (Mastercard/BVNK, Circle/CPN) could disintermediate independent infra. (hypothesis.)
10. **Related-party exposure?** Founder Ozan Özerk also founded European Merchant Bank and MSB USA; undisclosed Series B / opaque cap table. Warrants proxy/S-4 scrutiny. (open.)
11. **Is the stablecoin integration live or announced?** Live/dated: Fireblocks Network (2026-07-23), Circle Payments Network (2026-08-25), Circle partnership (2025-06-17). These are real, not aspirational.
12. **What underpins vs breaks the multiple?** Underpins: stablecoin-infra growth + regulated licensing (43 US MTLs). Breaks: commoditization of the fiat ramp, strategic disintermediation, or a heavily-redeemed de-SPAC leaving it under-capitalized vs its $1.1B mark.
13. **Is the regulatory tailwind real?** GENIUS Act (signed 2025-07-18) is a genuine US payment-stablecoin framework — the credible reason the US is attractive now. Broader market-structure law still unresolved.
14. **Timing vs peers?** Aggressive: Kraken/Payward pushed IPO to Q2 2027+, RedotPay's US IPO reportedly on hold/reshaped. OpenPayd's year-end-2026 target stands out — ambition or pressure? (open.)
15. **Freshness/duplicate?** FRESH — first corpus note on OpenPayd's listing; prior OpenPayd notes are product/leadership only. Not a reprint.

**Importance: 3/5** — A genuine, dated capital-markets event (de-SPAC, ~$1.1B mark) for a real, revenue-generating ($73M) embedded-finance + stablecoin-infra player with concrete live Circle/Fireblocks integrations and a GENIUS-Act tailwind. ## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** OpenPayd sits in payments infrastructure / embedded finance at the fiat↔stablecoin boundary — a "money-movement orchestration" layer. Embedded-finance market ~$148–155bn in 2025–26 at a ~20–31% CAGR depending on house (per Precedence Research / Mordor, via researchandmarkets & mordorintelligence, as of 2026); BaaS sub-segment ~$29bn in 2026 → ~$65bn by 2031 (per Mordor, as of 2026). Adyen/BCG framed a ~$185bn North America + Europe embedded-finance TAM with only ~$32bn captured (as of 2024) — low penetration = headroom, but these are vendor/analyst figures, treat as directional, not audited. Structure: fragmented at the orchestration layer, with value concentrating where regulatory licences + bank/stablecoin integrations are stacked (entry barrier = capital + multi-jurisdiction licensing, not code). "Why now": the GENIUS Act (US payment-stablecoin framework) and MiCA give enterprises regulatory cover to settle in stablecoins — the thesis OpenPayd is explicitly listing against. Second-order: regulatory clarity pulls IPO windows open (RedotPay IPO prep; Kraken/Payward listing pushed to ≥mid-2027 per CoinDesk) — i.e. the "why now" is a policy-driven re-opening, not proven unit economics.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/annualized transaction volume, ARR, take rate (TPV→revenue), and regulatory-licence breadth. OpenPayd discloses $85m+ ARR and $240bn+ annualized volume as of Mar-2026 (per Business Wire merger PR) → implied take rate ≈ $85m / $240bn ≈ **0.035%** (3.5bps) — consistent with a thin-margin infrastructure/rails model, NOT a merchant-acquiring take rate; the economics live on volume. Players: Circle (CRCL, stablecoin issuer + network), dLocal (DLO, emerging-markets cross-border), Payoneer (PAYO, SMB cross-border), plus private infra (Nium, Fireblocks for the crypto-custody leg). Basis of competition: regulatory footprint + rail coverage + the fiat-AND-stablecoin combination (OpenPayd's own pitch: "no public market competitor has the same combination of fiat and stablecoin capabilities"). Recent moves (from corpus): integrated with Circle Payments Network and Fireblocks' network; signed Kraken (virtual IBANs, 2025-08), Ripple (2025-07), RedotPay (2026-07); MiCA approval (2026-06). Position: niche but credible category-definer at the fiat/stablecoin seam — moat = intangibles (multi-jurisdiction licences across US/UK/EEA/Canada/South Africa) + switching costs of embedded API integration; NOT network-effect scale vs Circle. `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal frame: OpenPayd pro-forma equity value **$1.145bn** (Titan/TACH SPAC), up to $276m gross proceeds (per Business Wire / Investing.com). Against $85m ARR → **$1.145bn / $85m ≈ 13.5x ARR** (equity value, not clean EV — net cash/debt post-close not disclosed → treat as price/ARR, `[UNSOURCED]` for true EV/Rev).

| dLocal (DLO) | mkt cap ~$4.3bn, rev ~$1.3bn TTM | EV/Rev **2.60x** / P/S 3.30x |

OpenPayd at **~13.5x ARR** screens **rich** vs all three — but the comparison is apples-to-oranges: ARR is a subset of total revenue (so the "real" revenue multiple is lower and unknown), and dLocal (+56% rev YoY, +92% TPV) shows the market pays a premium for growth, not a flat multiple. OpenPayd's own growth rate is undisclosed `[UNSOURCED]`, so the premium cannot be validated against growth. Verdict: **rich on the disclosed metric, but not a confirmed over-valuation** absent a full revenue + growth figure. **Risk flags.**
1. **SPAC-completion & redemption risk.** The $276m proceeds assume *no* redemptions; the deal is still in SEC review and needs Titan shareholder approval (close targeted Q4-2026). High SPAC redemptions routinely gut trust proceeds — the US-expansion capex thesis is contingent on cash that is not yet certain.
2. **ARR-vs-valuation opacity.** $1.145bn is pegged to $85m ARR (13.5x) while total revenue, growth rate, and net-cash position are undisclosed → the valuation is being sold on a selectively-disclosed metric; de-PR flag — company is silent on full P&L and margins beyond "profitable."
3. **Disintermediation / rail dependence.** OpenPayd rides *others'* rails (Circle Payments Network, Fireblocks). If issuers/networks move downstream into orchestration, or clients (Kraken, eToro, RedotPay) integrate issuers directly, the thin 3.5bps take rate is squeezed between the bank leg and the stablecoin leg — value capture risk at the middle of the stack.
4. **Client/geography concentration (partial).** 1,100+ customers across 180 countries is disclosed, but blue-chip revenue concentration (eToro, Kraken) is not — crypto-exchange exposure ties volume to crypto-cycle beta.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` If it closes, OpenPayd would be the first public pure-play fiat+stablecoin orchestration name — a listed read-through for private infra (Nium, and the stablecoin-rails thesis generally), and a re-rating catalyst if it trades toward Circle's 7.9x rather than the cross-border 2.6–3.3x cluster. Falsifiable thesis: the stablecoin-settlement "why now" is real demand, not regulatory hype → watch Q4-2026 close + first public filing for (a) actual total revenue vs the $85m ARR headline and (b) redemption-adjusted proceeds. Thesis breaks if the full revenue multiple still prints well above the growth-adjusted peer line, or if redemptions force a down-round recut of the $1.145bn.
