HONG KONG, Oct. 08, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- Gate has announced a collaboration with Visa to launch a crypto-linked card, further expanding the use of digital assets in everyday spending and global commerce. The product is expected to launch across 40+ markets globally, allowing users to make payments with digital assets online and in-store wherever Visa is accepted and more conveniently connect their Gate accounts with real-world spending.
When users make payments with the crypto-linked Visa card launched by Gate, the relevant crypto assets will be automatically converted into fiat currency at the point of sale, allowing digital assets held in their Gate accounts to be used for everyday shopping, dining, travel, and other expenses. Users can also apply for and manage the card through the Gate app and access related payment services within a familiar platform environment. Details regarding supported markets, eligibility requirements, supported assets, and service availability can be found on the product page.
Visa has an extensive global digital payments acceptance network spanning more than 175 million merchant locations across more than 200 countries and territories. Through this partnership, Gate is connecting its digital asset capabilities with Visa's payments infrastructure, enabling users to use digital assets across a broader range of commercial scenarios and further expanding their application in everyday payments. More than 160 stablecoin-linked Visa card programmes are now live globally, with payment volume up nearly 200% year-over-year, demonstrating the rapid expansion of crypto card adoption.
This partnership builds on Gate's existing global user base and multi-asset service capabilities. Founded in 2013, Gate now serves more than 61 million users, supports trading across 5,300+ crypto assets and 12,800+ stock assets, and provides access to TradFi products spanning metals, stocks, indices, forex, and commodities. The platform was among the first to implement 100% Proof of Reserves and has built a diversified product and ecosystem portfolio. Its broad user base, multi-asset product offering, and platform capabilities provide the foundation for Gate to extend the use of digital assets into everyday payment scenarios.
Gate has officially launched Gate Money, a globally integrated financial services application. Gate Money brings digital assets, fiat currencies, stocks, ETFs, gold, bank accounts, and payments together in the Gate app. Eligible users can also apply online for a global bank account held in their own name and manage fund receipts, transfers, asset conversions, and payments in one place. Gate's crypto-linked Visa card further connects assets held in Gate accounts with global payment scenarios, extending Gate Money’s financial services into more everyday spending use cases and giving users greater flexibility in how they use their assets. Currently, users can update the Gate App to v8.39.0 to enable Gate Money.
Gate Founder and CEO, Dr. Han, said: "Digital assets are gradually becoming part of everyday financial life. This partnership with Visa connects the digital asset accounts Gate users already know with a broad range of commercial scenarios, making digital asset payments more intuitive and better aligned with everyday needs. As the service launches across more than 40 countries and territories, we aim to give users greater flexibility in how they use their assets and further expand the practical use of digital assets in global commerce."
Nischint Sanghavi, Head of Digital Currencies, APAC, said: "Consumers want payment methods that are flexible, convenient, and widely accepted. Visa is committed to working with partners to expand payment choices and connect new forms of value to established digital payments infrastructure. Through our partnership with Gate, we look forward to enabling more users to use digital assets wherever Visa is accepted, further enriching their everyday payment experience."
In recent years, digital assets have increasingly expanded into practical use cases such as payments, consumption, and cross-border commerce. By connecting digital asset accounts with Visa's payments network, crypto cards offer users a more intuitive way to use digital assets and serve as an important gateway to integrating them into everyday economic activity.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-08 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE, Hong Kong), Gate announced a "collaboration with Visa" for a crypto-linked Visa card that auto-converts crypto to fiat at the point of sale, usable "wherever Visa is accepted," managed inside the Gate app. Supported assets listed on Gate's card page: USDT, BTC, ETH, GT ("Instant Spend Mode").

De-PR'd reality:
- This is a conversion-at-POS debit/prepaid card, not a novel payment rail. The crypto is **sold to fiat at the register** — the exact mechanism Coinbase Card and the Crypto.com Visa card have run for years. No on-chain settlement, no stablecoin-native rails; Visa settles in fiat as usual.
- Status is **"expected to launch"** / "the service launches across more than 40 countries" — forward/aspirational, not confirmed live. No launch date, no cardholder numbers, no issuer/BIN sponsor named, no fee/spread disclosed, no per-market eligibility. "40+ countries and territories" is a planned footprint, not a live deployment.
- The "40+ markets" framing borrows Visa's 200-country / 175M-merchant network stats and the industry "160+ stablecoin-linked Visa programmes, +200% YoY volume" line (from Visa's own Oct-2026 disclosure, see [[Visa reports nearly 200% YoY stablecoin card payment growth]]) to inflate a single exchange card launch into a macro-trend headline.

**Why framed this way:** The release is a PR wrapper around the prior day's product, [[Gate launches Gate Money financial services gateway]] (TOKEN2049 keynote, Oct 7). The card is one feature of the Gate Money push; splitting it into a separate "Visa partnership" release doubles the news surface and attaches the Gate brand to Visa's credibility. The task's "higher-scale Visa-card framing" is accurate as framing, but the underlying product is the Gate Money gateway's card module.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Coinbase Card (Visa):** live for years, global Visa-debit acceptance, auto-converts any coin to USD at POS; USDC draws at par with no conversion fee, other coins via a spread. Added an Amex credit card with BTC rewards in fall 2025. This is the direct template.
- **Crypto.com Visa Card:** Singapore-based, multi-tier prepaid Visa with crypto/cash loading, staking rewards, lounge access — the mass-market incumbent in exchange crypto cards.
- **Gate's own prior rails:** [[Gate Pay launches "National QR Code" feature]] (2025-10), [[Gate and Flutterwave launch localized crypto payments in Africa]] (2026-05), [[Gate to Pay joins Mastercard Product Express in Jordan]] (2026-07). Gate already has a payments arm; this is an expansion, not a first move.
- **Position: catching up / parity.** Gate is a late entrant to a saturated category. The differentiator is distribution (61M claimed users) and asset breadth (GT token spendable), not mechanism.

**Why the landscape is this way:** Exchange cards are a retention/engagement play, not a margin business — the card monetizes via interchange share minus issuer costs and conversion spread, thin economics. Winners compete on rewards and acceptance, both commoditized by Visa. The margin in the stack is captured by Visa (network fee) and the BIN-sponsor issuer, not by Gate.

## [2] Company history / fit
Gate (founded 2013, Hong Kong), claims 61M users, ranks ~#2 in 24h spot volume (CoinMarketCap ~$95B 24h, top-3 by liquidity), 5,300+ crypto + 12,800+ "stock" assets, early 100% Proof-of-Reserves. The card fits the broader pivot from pure trading into a "financial services gateway" (Gate Money: bank accounts, FX, payments, ETFs, gold). (analysis) Structural logic: spot-trading take-rates are commoditized and cyclical; Gate needs recurring, sticky engagement → everyday-spend products convert idle balances into transaction flow and lock users in the app. The Visa card is the consumer on-ramp for that strategy.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: low.** Crypto-to-fiat Visa cards are a 5+ year-old product category; "40+ countries" and "convert at POS" is table stakes. Nothing here is a mechanism first — no stablecoin-native settlement, no new rail, no exclusive terms.
**Traction: none disclosed.** No live cardholders, no spend volume, no launch date — "announced," not "adopted." The only hard traction numbers belong to **Visa's whole program** (160+ programmes, +200% YoY), not to Gate's card.
**(analysis) Where value accrues:** Visa captures network economics; the issuer captures issuing economics; Gate gets engagement + a thin spread. For Gate the real value-add is defensive (keep users from migrating to Coinbase/Crypto.com cards and retain balances), not a new profit center. The "61M users" TAM is the only genuinely differentiating asset — if even a small fraction activate, distribution beats the incumbents' head start.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch for: an actual launch date, named issuer/BIN sponsor, per-market rollout, fee/spread and reward terms, and whether GT-token spend drives real volume. Regulatory overhang: the release itself carves out prohibited jurisdictions (card "not intended for" restricted regions), signaling a patchwork, licensing-gated rollout rather than a clean 40-country go-live. (hypothesis) Given it rode a keynote/PR cycle with no metrics, near-term it is a marketing milestone; materiality depends entirely on an actual live launch and activation that are not yet evidenced.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Crypto-linked cards sit at the intersection of the ~$315bn+ stablecoin market and card-network spend rails. Stablecoin card spending ran ~$1.2bn in a single month in late 2026, triple a year earlier (per PaymentScan/Paymentscan, via CoinDesk, Oct 2026); Visa's own stablecoin-linked card volume is up nearly 200% YoY with 160+ programmes live (per Gate PR citing Visa; corroborated by crypto.news, Oct 2026). Visa reports its stablecoin settlement run-rate near ~$20bn annualised (per WallStreetLedger, 2026) — settlement, not card spend, so a different pool. Structure: value chain = issuer/processor + network (Visa/MC) + crypto exchange/wallet front-end; the exchange owns the user and the conversion spread, the network owns acceptance (175m+ merchant locations, 200+ countries per Gate PR). Barriers are regulatory (per-market licensing, BIN sponsorship) and network access, not tech — hence the proliferation of near-identical "convert-at-POS" launches. "Why now": stablecoin regulatory clarity (US GENIUS-era framing) + exchanges racing to add payments/super-app surfaces on top of trading as spot volumes plateau.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: card spend volume / TPV, number of live programmes & markets, attach rate (cards per active user), FX+conversion take rate at POS. This is a crowded, undifferentiated wave — directly comparable 2026 launches: [[Stripe to expand stablecoin cards to 100+ countries]] (Oct 1, targeting 100+ countries by year-end, customers Kraken/Ramp/Morse), [[SoFi, Orbi and Mastercard launch crypto-linked card in Mexico]] (Oct 8–9, Mastercard rails), [[OKX launches OKX Money standalone stablecoin app]], [[RedotPay partners Nium to expand stablecoin off-ramp]], [[Yuno integrates Coinbase Payments for stablecoin checkout]]. Basis of competition: distribution (existing exchange user base) + breadth of supported markets/assets, NOT price — economics are undisclosed across the board. Gate's position: fast-follower / catching up, not a leader. Its edge is scale of base — 61m users, 5,300+ crypto assets (per Gate PR; registered base passed 60m on 2 Sep 2026 per CoinLaw) and the Gate Money super-app launched Oct 7. Moat (analysis): switching costs from in-app asset custody + the Gate Money account bundle; the network/acceptance layer is Visa's, not Gate's — so no card-side moat.

**Comps & multiples.** Gate is private; last identifiable round is an early Series A (Crunchbase) with no disclosed valuation — no market-cap comp computable, "no data". Nearest listed exchange comp [[Coinbase says it is building most complete trading platform]]: Coinbase Q2 2026 (qtr ended 30 Jun 2026) net revenue $1.2bn (down from $1.5bn YoY), net loss $359.5m (per Coinbase 10-Q, SEC). Private-round comp: [[OKX raises capital at flat $25B valuation]] — OKX held flat at $25bn (Oct 2026), a read-through signal that exchange valuations are not re-rating up on payments pivots. EV/Revenue and P/E not computable for Gate (no public financials) → `[UNSOURCED]`. **IR grounding (Visa, reported).** Visa Q2 FY2026 (qtr ended 31 Mar 2026): net revenue $11.2bn, +17% YoY (+16% constant-$); payments volume +9% const-$; cross-border volume ex-Europe +11% const-$ (drives international revenue); 66.1bn processed transactions, +9% (per Visa Q2 FY2026 earnings release / 10-Q, SEC EDGAR, filed 28–29 Apr 2026). Business/commercial programmes were ~17% of Visa's stablecoin-linked card volume in FY2026 (per Visa, via cryptorank, 2026). Read-through: a 40-country Gate card is immaterial to Visa's $11.2bn/qtr — Visa monetises the rails regardless of which exchange fronts the card.

**Risk flags.**
1. Commoditisation / no differentiation — near-identical convert-at-POS cards launched by Stripe, SoFi/Orbi, OKX, RedotPay within the same 2 weeks; Gate competes on distribution only, so pricing power and card economics compress fast (second-order: margin accrues to Visa's rails, not Gate).
2. Dependence on someone else's rails — Gate holds no acceptance network; Visa (or any sponsor) can reprice, restrict, or exit, as crypto-card programmes have historically been paused; Gate carries the regulatory/fraud liability while Visa keeps interchange.
3. Announcement vs live — "expected to launch across 40+ markets", eligibility/supported-assets/economics all deferred to a product page; no live volume, take rate, or fraud-liability split disclosed. De-PR: Gate is silent on who bears conversion FX spread and chargeback risk.
4. Regulatory/jurisdictional — card "not intended for jurisdictions where prohibited"; a Hong-Kong-based, lightly-regulated exchange pushing spend across 40+ markets invites per-market licensing and AML scrutiny.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Not a re-rating event — it's a me-too entry into a fast-commoditising category; the signal is that crypto cards are becoming table-stakes distribution for every large exchange, not a moat. Falsifiable thesis: if Gate discloses card TPV or attach-rate within ~2 quarters that materially lifts non-trading revenue, the super-app pivot is real; absent that (the base case), this is a user-retention/acquisition feature, not a P&L driver. Watch: whether any player discloses card economics (take rate), and whether Visa/MC start publishing per-partner volumes.
