## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-10-22/23, India's Pine Labs partnered with Sri Lankan payment aggregator WEBXPAY to let Sri Lankan merchants accept Indian payment methods — UPI, UPI AutoPay, cards from major Indian issuers, net banking across 100+ banks, EMI conversions and Credit on UPI — with T+1 settlement and integration into merchants' existing systems. It is billed as Sri Lanka's first Payment Aggregator-to-Payment Aggregator (PA-on-PA) tie-up under India's RBI-authorised PA-CB (Payment Aggregator — Cross Border) framework. Note the framing nuance: press coverage stresses online/e-commerce acceptance ("shop online with Indian payment methods"), so this is broader than physical in-store travel spend — it lets Indian consumers transact with Sri Lankan merchants using home rails. Service is live now; Pine Labs says it will replicate the model across Southeast Asia and other markets where Indian travellers/consumers are commercially significant. (Sources: [finextra](https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/111409/pine-labs-and-webxpay-bring-indias-upi-to-sri-lanka), [thepaypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/pine-labs-partners-with-webxpay-to-enable-indian-payments-in-sri-lanka), [ibsintelligence](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi-news/pine-labs-and-webxpay-partner-for-cross-border-payments-in-sri-lanka/), [fintechnews.sg](https://fintechnews.sg/139519/sri-lanka/sri-lanka-indian-payment-methods/)).

**[1] Why it matters.** India is Sri Lanka's top tourism source market — 530,000+ (5.3 lakh) Indian arrivals in 2025, momentum continuing into 2026 — yet Indian visitors historically couldn't pay with home methods (UPI, net banking, domestic cards). Closing that gap captures outbound-India spend for Sri Lankan merchants and gives Pine Labs its first live activation of the newly-licensed cross-border rail. For Pine Labs this is a concrete monetisation of the RBI cross-border PA licence it won post-IPO (one of three licences — PA, PG, PA-CB), turning a regulatory asset into a repeatable corridor product.

**[2] Pine Labs context (post-IPO, cross-border push).** Pine Labs listed on BSE/NSE on 2025-11-14 at ₹242 (+9.5% vs ₹221 issue price); the ~₹3,900 crore book-built IPO was subscribed ~2.48x. Post-listing it said it became the first company to hold all three RBI payment licences (PA, PG, cross-border). The Sri Lanka corridor fits a wider 2026 globalisation drive: UAE Wio Bank merchant-acquiring tie-up [[Wio Bank partners Pine Labs for merchant acquiring]], a card issuing/processing platform for Sri Lanka's Pan Asia Bank [[Pine Labs deploys card platform for Sri Lanka's Pan Asia Bank]], multi-year contracts with India's top-3 oil marketers [[Pine Labs wins contracts from top three Indian oil firms]], a stablecoin-prepaid-card launch across nine MEA/SEA markets [[Pine Labs to launch stablecoin payments in nine countries]], and strong domestic UPI-on-PoS growth (UPI txns +50% YoY on its terminals in Q2 FY26) [[Pine Labs launches Tap to Pay Online for smartphones]].

**[3] Sri Lanka cross-border corridor context.** Sri Lanka has been actively onboarding foreign consumer rails to capture inbound tourist spend: LankaPay enabled Alipay+ for cross-border LankaQR [[LankaPay integrates Alipay+ for cross-border QR in Sri Lanka]] and Weixin Pay (WeChat Pay) at ~400,000 merchants to tap Chinese tourism [[Sri Lanka's LankaPay enables Weixin Pay at 400,000 merchants]]. The Pine Labs/WEBXPAY deal is the India-facing equivalent — but structured through India's PA-CB regime and Pine Labs' acquiring rails rather than Sri Lanka's domestic QR network, i.e. a different plumbing/direction (Indian-issued instruments accepted at SL merchants).

**[4] What to watch.** (a) Online vs in-person: whether acceptance is predominantly e-commerce/net-banking or also physical PoS at SL merchants; (b) actual go-live breadth and merchant count (none disclosed); (c) FX/interchange economics and who bears conversion cost on UPI cross-border; (d) whether the "first PA-on-PA under PA-CB" claim holds vs other corridors; (e) replication timeline/first SEA markets; (f) competitive response from NPCI International (NIPL), which runs UPI's own cross-border acceptance deals directly with partner-country networks (potential channel conflict with a PA-CB intermediary model).

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits in India outbound-traveller / cross-border UPI acceptance — the "export UPI acceptance" layer that NPCI International (NIPL) has been building corridor by corridor. Cross-border UPI is now live in 8+ countries and still expanding (per NPCI, via Fortune India, 2026-06-02). Sri Lanka specifically: NIPL + LankaPay enabled UPI scans of LankaQR codes for Indian tourists (2026-03). India is the top tourist source to Sri Lanka — ~416k Indian visitors in 2024 rising to ~531k in 2025 (per ANI, 2026-03-17), so the addressable flow is sizeable but still a small, tourism-tied niche vs domestic UPI (₹29.9 lakh crore/month domestically, 2026-05). Structure is fragmented at the acceptance edge (each corridor = a bilateral switch/PA deal) but the rails are consolidated under NPCI/NIPL; entry barriers are regulatory (RBI PA-CB framework, local central-bank sign-off) and network (merchant distribution in-market). Why now: RBI's PA-CB framework (Oct-2023 circular) created a licensed path for cross-border aggregation, and this Pine Labs–Webxpay tie-up is pitched as the first PA-on-PA structure in Sri Lanka under that framework (per ThePaypers / Elets BFSI, 2026-10).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/GTV, take rate, and merchant-acceptance footprint. Key players operate on two fronts — (a) the rail owner NIPL (does government-to-government corridor deals, e.g. [[UPI comes to Cambodia as NPCI partners ACLEDA Bank]], [[Qatar's Commercial Bank signs NPCI deal for UPI QR acceptance]], [[India's UPI integrates with Malaysia's PayNet for cross-border payments]], [[UPI to debut in Japan]]); and (b) commercial PAs racing for the RBI PA-CB license — 19 entities on the RBI PA-CB list as of Jan-2026 incl. Cashfree, Razorpay, PayU, BillDesk, PayGlocal (per Winvesta/Skydo). Cashfree already took a cross-border suite live across 40 countries ([[Cashfree Payments takes cross-border suite live across 40 countries]], 2026-06). Basis of competition: regulatory licensing + local PA partnerships + merchant distribution, not price. Pine Labs' position: it is scaling an "online & omnichannel" / global payments push (shareholder letter frames the business as "Simplifying Payments for the Globe"), so outbound-acceptance corridors are an adjacency to its core India acquiring/issuing, not its main revenue engine — niche but strategically consistent (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** Protagonist IR (PRIMARY — Pine Labs' own filed results): FY26 (year to Mar-2026) revenue from operations ₹2,710.6 Cr, +19% YoY, with first full-year PAT of ₹112.5 Cr (vs −₹145.5 Cr loss FY25); Q4 FY26 revenue ₹700.5 Cr (+17% YoY) and PAT ₹59.4 Cr (vs −₹28.9 Cr) — Q4 FY26 Press Release 2026-05-26 (https://cdn.pinelabs.com/india/investors/pdf/legal-documents/Pine_Labs_20Ltd_Q4FY26_Press_Release_v1.pdf, drive_url https://drive.google.com/file/d/1_wmjzMmHuEAaA6zh3Eq1jVyMzlOpGJ8V/view). FY26 GTV ₹17.2 trillion on 7.4bn transactions; segments: DITP ₹1,836.8 Cr (~68%) and Issuing & Acquiring ₹873.8 Cr (~32%); blended take rate ~0.48–0.91% across quarters (per Q4 FY26 shareholder letter / MOFSL note). Earlier quarter for reference: Q2 FY26 revenue ₹650 Cr, +18% YoY, Adj. EBITDA +62% (Q2 FY26 PR 2025-12-03, drive_url https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RkjuwLTSbvISgP9vSo04DavXm3--Dtht/view).
- Valuation anchor: Pine Labs (NSE: PINELABS) IPO'd Nov-2025 at ₹221 (listed ₹242); offer-price market cap ₹23,296 Cr, ~₹25,377 Cr at listing (per Chittorgarh/Business Standard). On FY26 revenue ₹2,710.6 Cr, that implies P/S = ₹25,377 Cr / ₹2,710.6 Cr ≈ 9.4x trailing — rich on an absolute basis but on a ~19% revenue grower only just turning profitable; justified-vs-growth is debatable, not an automatic flag (analysis).
- Cross-border/PA peers (private, no clean market caps): Cashfree, Razorpay, PayU — EV/Revenue and EV/EBITDA = no data (not publicly disclosed / private). Qualitative comparison only; distribution not computed.

**Risk flags.**
1. Economics undisclosed — neither party states the take rate, FX spread, or fraud/settlement liability on this corridor; "announced/launched" is not the same as volume. Without ticket size or TPV, revenue contribution to Pine Labs is likely immaterial vs its ₹2,710 Cr base (de-PR).
2. Dependence on someone else's rails — acceptance economics are set upstream by NPCI/NIPL and RBI PA-CB rules; Pine Labs captures a thin PA margin while the rail owner controls routing, pricing and who else gets the corridor (disintermediation risk if NIPL signs more PAs or goes direct via LankaPay, as it already did in Mar-2026).
3. Concentration/seasonality — demand is tourism-tied (one country, one visitor flow), so volume is cyclical and policy-sensitive (travel advisories, FX, bilateral relations).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is a new-entry corridor, not a re-rating — incremental for the sector: it shows the RBI PA-CB framework is now productive enough to support PA-on-PA structures abroad, which could template faster corridor roll-outs by Pine Labs and peers. Falsifiable thesis: outbound-acceptance corridors become a disclosed, growing line for Pine Labs. Trigger to watch — Pine Labs quantifying cross-border/global TPV or "online & omnichannel international" revenue in an upcoming quarterly (Q1/Q2 FY27) filing; what breaks it — corridors stay PR-only with no disclosed volume, confirming they are strategic optics rather than a P&L driver.
