**[1] Key dated facts (survey figures).** From the Payments 2030 report (Finextra Research commissioned by ACI, surveyed summer 2025; 162 senior executives):
- A2A is the "leading disruptor": 63% forecast >10% annual A2A volume growth; 81% are upgrading or planning infrastructure upgrades.
- Cloud: only 7% fully cloud-based today; ~1 in 4 expect full cloud adoption within 5 years (hybrid is the interim path).
- Instant payments: ~4 in 10 (40%) currently facilitate instant payments; 41% plan adoption within two years; >40% of US transactions occur outside core banking hours (24/7 demand).
- Readiness gap: nearly half feel only "somewhat prepared" or less for digital-payment growth.
- Expert commentary from Deutsche Bank, The Clearing House, and the US Faster Payments Council.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** The item is an ACI/Finextra survey ("Payments 2030", 162 senior leaders across banks, fintechs and payment providers, fielded summer 2025) — it is a sector-sentiment data point, not a company deal, so sizing comes from the broader real-time-payments (RTP) software market. Market estimates diverge sharply by definition: ~$193bn by 2030 at 35.5% CAGR (per Grand View Research, as of 2024) and ~$226.5bn by 2030 at 36.2% CAGR (per The Business Research Company); narrower "instant payments" scopes land lower — ~$120bn by 2030 at 29.6% CAGR (per Virtue Market Research). Treat the absolute TAMs as soft (methodology-dependent, partly secondary citations), but the direction — high-20s-to-mid-30s% CAGR — is consistent across firms. Structure: fragmented at the rails layer (ACH/RTP/FedNow/SEPA Inst/Pix/UPI), but the modernization-software layer that banks buy is consolidated among a handful of incumbents (ACI, Fiserv, FIS, Jack Henry, Temenos) with high switching costs — core payment engines are mission-critical and multi-year to rip-and-replace. Why now: three secular drivers the survey itself quantifies — (1) A2A as the "leading disruptor," with 63% forecasting >10% annual growth and >80% upgrading/planning to upgrade capability; (2) cloud migration — only 7% fully cloud today but ~1 in 4 expect full adoption within 5 years (hybrid is the interim path); (3) 24/7 demand (per the report, >40% of US transactions occur outside core banking hours) plus compliance/ISO 20022 pressure folding into innovation budgets (per Finextra, "Payments 2030," via finextra.com, 2025).

**Competitive landscape.** Payments-software KPIs: recurring/software revenue growth, adjusted EBITDA margin, net revenue retention, and (for ACI) the mix between the modernizing "Payment Software" and the faster-growing "Issuing & Acquiring" lines. Key players and basis of competition: ACI Worldwide, Fiserv, FIS, Jack Henry, Temenos — competing on product breadth, cloud-native re-platforming and distribution into tier-1/2 banks rather than on price. Recent moves: ACI is pushing its cloud-native stack — ACI Connetic enabled across eight major US payment networks with two US customers signed, and Payment Software revenue +9% to $196m with Issuing & Acquiring +33% cc in Q2 2026 (per ACI Q2 2026 release, via stocktitan/yahoo). Peer operating cadence in 2026: FIS adjusted revenue +6.3% with EBITDA margin ~41.1%; Jack Henry revenue +8.7% (Processing +10%), 91% recurring; Fiserv a global merchant/issuer-processing leader (Clover) (per ccgcatalyst / company releases, 2026). ACI's position: a focused incumbent in bank payment modernization and real-time rails — niche-but-deep versus the broader Fiserv/FIS platforms; moat is switching costs on embedded core engines plus accumulating cloud-native reference wins (Connetic) (analysis). NRR and disclosed CAC/LTV not surfaced → `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Comps & multiples.** ACI Worldwide (ACIW): market cap ~$5.17bn; 2026 guidance revenue ~$1.895–1.925bn and adj. EBITDA ~$545–560m (per ACI, Q2 2026 release). Reported EV/EBITDA ~14.5x (per Yahoo Finance key-statistics). Implied EV ≈ `14.46 × ~$550m ≈ $7.95bn`, so EV/Revenue ≈ `$7.95bn / ~$1.91bn ≈ 4.2x` (analysis — mixes reported multiple with guidance midpoints). Peers: Fiserv EV ~$51.8bn / TTM EBITDA ~$9.0bn = `~5.75x` EV/EBITDA (per valueinvesting.io, as of 2026-09-25) — materially below ACI; the gap reflects Fiserv's lower-growth, higher-base processing mix versus ACI's smaller, faster software/I&A growth, i.e. the multiple is explained by growth, not obviously a mispricing (growth-multiple correlation). FIS / Jack Henry / Temenos clean EV/EBITDA not sourced here → "no data"; distribution not computed (only 2 verifiable multiples), qualitative comparison only. Internal comps (base): [[ACI Worldwide launches card payments on cloud-native ACI Connetic]], [[ACI Worldwide and Prosa upgrade Latin America payments infrastructure]], [[ACI Worldwide powers Rabobank's Wero instant payments]], [[Temenos launches AI money movement platform at Sibos]]. ACI screens in-line/modestly rich vs the one directly comparable listed peer (Fiserv), justified by faster growth.

**Risk flags.**
1. Survey ≠ spend. The report is sentiment from 162 leaders (intent to adopt cloud/A2A), not booked revenue — "80% planning to upgrade" is a pipeline signal that can slip; second-order, it inflates sell-side narrative ahead of actual bank capex cycles, and ACI co-authored it, so it is interested-party marketing, not independent demand proof.
2. Disintermediation / rails commoditization. If A2A and instant rails standardize (ISO 20022) and hyperscaler/cloud-native challengers (or bank-built stacks) capture the modernization layer, incumbents like ACI risk margin compression as value shifts down-stack — the very "cloud migration" the report cheers is also the vector that lowers switching costs over time.
3. Valuation vs peer. At ~14.5x EV/EBITDA versus Fiserv's ~5.75x, ACI leaves little room for a growth stumble; any deceleration in the ~9% Payment Software / 33% I&A lines would de-rate it toward the peer group fast (execution/concentration risk on a few large bank wins).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** The survey crystallizes the 2026–2030 payments-software thesis: the money is in helping incumbent banks go hybrid-cloud and A2A-ready, not in building new rails — a re-platforming/upgrade cycle, not a greenfield land-grab (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if A2A/instant adoption is real, ACI's "Payment Software" line and Connetic reference wins should keep compounding at high-single/double digits through 2027; trigger to watch — Connetic US customer count and Payment Software growth in the next 1–2 quarters. What breaks it: flat/decelerating software growth despite the survey's upgrade intentions, confirming sentiment-vs-spend risk.
