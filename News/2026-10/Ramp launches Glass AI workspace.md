Ramp spent $1.5 million on AI model tokens in a single week in May. Then it built a product to get more out of every dollar.

The corporate spend management company has launched Glass, an internal AI workspace that turns company-specific knowledge and workflows into reusable tools. CEO Eric Glyman announced the release on October 13, 2026, and early access signups are open through Ramp’s website.

## What Glass actually does

In the AI world, tokens are the small chunks of text that large language models read and write, and providers bill customers based on how many get processed.

Glass is meant to channel that usage into something more organized. A workflow that works once gets saved, packaged and reused by everyone else, rather than reinvented from a blank prompt every morning.

The platform sets itself up automatically through Single Sign-On (SSO), and plugs into the internal tools Ramp already uses. Its interface is a split-pane workspace, so users can see the AI and their work side by side.

The headline feature is a marketplace holding more than 350 reusable skills. Rounding things out are automation and persistent memory, which holds onto context between sessions so users do not have to re-explain the same background every time they open a new chat.

# Context-enrichment: Ramp launches Glass AI workspace
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**FRESHNESS: fresh.** Genuinely NEW product announced by CEO Eric Glyman on **2026-10-13** (early-access signups at ramp.com/glass). Distinct from the July 2026 [[Ramp launches AI Token Spend Controls dashboard]] (that product helps *customers* police their *external* AI bills across OpenAI/Anthropic/Gemini; Glass is an *internal productivity harness* Ramp built for itself and is now externalizing). Not a reprint of any prior note. Source dates are consistent (Ramp blog + cryptobriefing Oct 13; PYMNTS; kucoin).

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- Ramp launched **Glass**, a **model-agnostic internal AI workspace** ("harness"), on 2026-10-13. Not a finance product — an employee-productivity layer that packages one person's working AI prompt/workflow into a **reusable "skill"** the whole company can rerun. Headline: a **marketplace of 350+ reusable skills**, **30+ pre-connected tools** via one SSO (Salesforce, Snowflake, Gong, Slack, Notion, Google Workspace, Figma, Linear), **persistent memory** across sessions, split-pane UI.
- **Origin story (the real hook):** Ramp spent **$1.5M on AI model tokens in a single week in May 2026**; management concluded spend was being wasted because every employee re-built prompts from scratch. Glass is the efficiency response. Reportedly **cut Ramp's AI costs ~30% in "certain accounts"** (NOT across the board — explicitly hedged).
- **Availability:** **early access only** (signup at ramp.com/glass). NOT yet a priced, GA, buy-it-today product. Framed by Geoff Charles (CPO) as "the most effective way to deploy AI within your company" -> clear intent to sell it externally, but as of launch it is announced + early-access, not broadly live.
- **Why structured this way / what it reveals:** The PR frame ("we built a tool after a $1.5M token week") doubles as a flex - it signals Ramp's AI intensity to investors days after its **$60bn round** ([[Ramp raises $1.85bn at $60bn pre-money valuation]]). The "makes the whole company smarter" language (Simon Taylor, Glyman tweets) is positioning Ramp as an **AI company, not a fintech** - the narrative that underpins the valuation step-up. So this launch is as much **valuation storytelling** as product.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Internal-AI / enterprise-workspace layer (Glass's actual category):** **Glean** (enterprise search + agents), **Notion AI**, **ChatGPT Enterprise** / **Claude for Enterprise**, **Microsoft 365 Copilot / Copilot Studio**, **Dust**, **Moveworks**, **Google Gemini for Workspace**. This is a **crowded, well-funded field** where Ramp is a late, non-specialist entrant. The "350 skills marketplace + memory + connectors" feature set is **parity, not novelty** vs Glean/Dust/Copilot Studio.
- **Spend-management core rivals** (context for why this matters to Ramp's story): [[Brex launches ChatGPT integration for expense data]] (Mar 2026) + Brex now owned by [[Capital One to acquire Brex for $5.15 billion]]; [[Navan unveils AI agent to automate expense reporting]] / [[Navan unveils AI audit engine for travel and expense fraud]]; Navan is now public ([[Navan goes public raising $923M]]). Brex/Navan ship *vertical* AI inside T&E; Ramp's Glass is *horizontal* (any workflow).
- **Position:** On the AI-workspace battlefield Ramp is **catching up / at parity at best** - no evidence of a mechanism advantage over Glean/Copilot. On the *spend* battlefield Ramp retains its lead (99% internal AI adoption, Inspect) but Glass itself doesn't extend that moat.
- **Why / second-order:** The real differentiator Ramp claims is **dogfooding credibility** - it runs its own company on Glass (700 daily internal users within a month). But credibility != distribution; the incumbents own the enterprise seats.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Trajectory: [[Ramp raises $200M Series E at $16bn valuation]] (Jun 2025) -> [[Ramp raises $500M at $22.5B valuation]] (Jul 2025) -> [[Ramp hits $1B annualized revenue at $22.5B valuation]] (Sep 2025) -> [[Ramp raises $750M Series F at $44B valuation]] (Jun 2026) -> [[Ramp raises $1.85bn at $60bn pre-money valuation]] (Oct 2026). Valuation ~4x in ~15 months.
- AI product cadence: [[Ramp launches AI agents to automate finance operations]], [[Ramp unveils AI Accounting Agent for month-end close]], [[Ramp pushes AI agents across its expense management tools]], [[Ramp launches AI Token Spend Controls dashboard]], [[Ramp launches Applied AI Solutions for finance teams]], [[Analysis Coinbase and Ramp restructure as AI-native finance firms]]. Glass is the **internal-ops bookend** to a year of customer-facing AI launches.
- Internal AI metrics (self-reported): **99%+ of staff use AI daily**; **Inspect** software factory ships **66%+ of Ramp's code**; **323 employees -> 6,500+ edits** to Glass's knowledge.
- **Why Ramp acts this way:** Its revenue is still anchored to **card interchange** - a commodity, low-multiple take-rate business (the open question flagged at [[Ramp hits $1B annualized revenue at $22.5B valuation]]: can it escape interchange?). A $60bn valuation on ~$1B+ ARR demands a **software/AI multiple**, not a payments multiple. Every AI launch - Glass included - is structurally driven by the need to **re-rate the story from "corporate card" to "AI-native finance OS."**

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new?** The *concept* (shareable prompts/skills, memory, connectors) is **not new** - it mirrors Glean/Dust/Copilot Studio. What's modestly differentiated: Ramp is selling the **operational playbook of a company that already runs itself on AI**, with hard internal numbers.
- **Traction - real vs announced:** Internal is real and specific (700 daily users, 6,500 edits, claimed 30% cost cut *in certain accounts*). **External traction = ZERO**: early-access signup only, no customers, no pricing, no GA date, "broad real-world results are still ahead" (cryptobriefing's own hedge). So treat this as **announced, not adopted externally.**
- **Who captures the margin (the real question):** Glass is a thin orchestration layer over **third-party model APIs (OpenAI/Anthropic/Gemini/Grok)**. Ramp doesn't own the model; its value-add is *routing + reuse + cost control*. If models commoditize (token prices are falling per Ramp's own AI Index), the orchestration layer is where some margin can stick - but it's also exactly where **every incumbent (Microsoft, Google, Glean) sits with better distribution**. Glass risks being a feature, not a moat.
- **Analysis:** The "$1.5M week -> 30% savings" story is compelling internally but is **not a product-market-fit signal** for an external SaaS. Hypothesis: Glass is primarily a **recruiting + valuation + brand asset** today, with external commercialization an unproven bet.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Next:** convert early access -> GA + pricing; likely bundle Glass with Ramp's finance suite to differentiate from pure-play Glean/Dust. Watch for an actual priced tier and named external customers - that's the only real traction signal.
- **Sentiment:** Launch landed well on fintech X (Simon Taylor: "not the move of a fintech company"; positions Ramp as AI-native). Comes on the heels of the **$60bn round** - narrative tailwind is strong.
- **Macro backdrop:** Ramp's own **AI Spend Index** (Aug 2026: Anthropic **43.8%** of business AI spend vs OpenAI **39.8%**) gives Ramp a data moat on *where* enterprise AI money flows - arguably more strategically valuable than Glass itself, because it feeds both Glass (optimize routing) and Ramp's card-data business.
- **Risks / second-order:** (1) commoditization of the orchestration layer by hyperscalers; (2) the valuation is increasingly a **bet on the AI narrative** - if Glass and peers underdeliver externally, the gap between a $60bn tag and an interchange-anchored P&L becomes the fragility, not a safety; (3) model-provider dependence - Ramp's "AI company" story rests on APIs it doesn't control (same disintermediation risk as its agentic-payments reliance on Visa/Mastercard, see [[Ramp and Visa deepen partnership on autonomous finance]]).

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Spend-management / business-spend software. Market size per Research & Markets: spend-management software ~$19.8bn in 2026 → ~$31.9bn by 2030 at ~12.7% CAGR (broader BSM figures run higher; via researchandmarkets.com / thebusinessresearchcompany.com, as of 2026 — secondary vendor reports, treat as indicative, not audited). Structure: fragmented and converging — corporate cards, T&E, bill-pay, procurement and business banking are collapsing into one "all-in-one finance" stack, with incumbents (SAP Concur enterprise default, Amex) on one side and venture-backed disruptors (Ramp, Brex, Navan) on the other. Barriers: distribution, card-interchange economics that subsidise "free" SaaS, and now proprietary AI tooling. Why now: the 2026 race has converged on one claim — autonomous AI agents let finance teams "do more with fewer people" (via procurementaiagents.com, chatfin.ai, 2026). Glass is a second-order move: Ramp first *consumed* AI at scale ($1.5m in model tokens in one week, May 2026, per note), then productised its own usage into a reusable-skills workspace — monetising the cost side of the AI stack rather than only the spend side.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: annualized revenue/ARR, annualized transaction/card volume (TPV), customer count, NRR. Key players and basis of competition — product breadth + AI depth + distribution, not price (interchange funds the model):
- **Ramp** — fastest-growing US corporate-card/spend platform; crossed ~$55bn annualized card volume and 30,000+ customers by mid-2026 (via market-report citation; treat as vendor-sourced). Internal adoption flex: 99.5% workforce on AI, ~66%+ of code via its "Inspect" factory (per note / cryptobriefing).
- **Brex** — acquired by Capital One for $5.15bn (Jan 2026) → now a bank-owned challenger; mindshare rising (0.8%→2.4% YoY) but ranked only #9 in expense management (via peerspot/erpresearch, Oct 2026).
- **Navan** — travel-first T&E, public since Nov 2025; #4 in expense mindshare but mindshare *falling* (7.8%→3.3%).
- Others: Airbase, Spendesk, SAP Concur, Expensify.
Protagonist position: **ahead** on AI execution and growth velocity (analysis). Moat = intangibles/scale: proprietary agent tooling + interchange-funded free distribution + switching costs once a company runs cards+bill-pay+banking on one rail. Glass itself is an internal-efficiency moat first; external product-moat is unproven (early signups only).

**Comps & multiples.**
- **Ramp (IR-grounded):** Annualized revenue **$1 Billion** as of **2025-09-04** (PRIMARY, Ramp press release via prnewswire — url below); reportedly passed ~$1.5bn ARR by June 2026 (press, unaudited). Latest round **~$1.85bn at $60bn pre-money**, Oct 2026 (Dragoneer/Thrive, Founders Fund; Bloomberg). Implied EV/Rev on the $60bn round ≈ `$60.0bn / $1.5bn = ~40x` forward-ish, or `$60.0bn / $1.0bn (IR Sep-25) = ~60x` trailing. Re-rating pace: $22.5bn (Jul-25) → $32bn (Nov-25) → $44bn (early-26) → $60bn (Oct-26) — see internal comps.
- **Navan (public, best external anchor):** ~$6.2bn IPO cap on TTM revenue $613m (+32%) → P/S ≈ `$6.2bn / $0.613bn = ~10.1x` (via axios, Oct 2025).
- **Brex:** $5.15bn Capital One acquisition price (Jan 2026); revenue undisclosed → EV/Rev **no data**.
- Internal comps: [[Ramp raises $1.85bn at $60bn pre-money valuation]], [[Ramp raised about $800m at $32bn valuation]], [[Navan goes public raising $923M]], [[Brex was acquired by Capital One for $5.15bn in the largest bank-fintech]], [[This Week in Fintech Crowded corporate card and spend market]].
- **Flag: rich.** Ramp at ~40–60x revenue sits ~4–6x above Navan's ~10x public multiple. Partly justified by faster growth (ARR roughly 50% higher in <1yr) and a private-round premium — but the gap is large even against a growing public peer, and EBITDA/margins are undisclosed (`[UNSOURCED]`), so the multiple leans entirely on growth durability.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Valuation vs comps** — ~40–60x revenue vs Navan's ~10x public P/S; any growth deceleration re-rates hard, and no public margin/profitability figures exist to defend it (`[UNSOURCED]`).
2. **AI-cost dependence / disintermediation** — the Glass thesis *starts from* Ramp's own soaring token bills (OpenAI/Anthropic/Gemini); the "30% cost cut" is "certain accounts," not board-wide (per note), and the margin benefit depends on model-provider pricing Ramp doesn't control.
3. **Execution / de-PR** — Glass is early-access-only, launched Oct 13 2026; "350+ reusable skills" and internal adoption are vendor-reported, external traction unproven. Brex now has Capital One's balance sheet behind it — a better-capitalised competitor.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Spend-management is turning into an *agent platform* play, not just a card+SaaS play — Glass signals Ramp monetising the AI-ops layer (helping customers manage their own token spend across OpenAI/Anthropic/Gemini), extending its "control every business dollar" wedge to AI dollars.
