## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 29 Sep 2026 the Bank of Russia issued an order (published/reported 12 Oct 2026) approving a formal **methodology to score e-money (electronic-wallet) operators** — a 33-question checklist, with weights, a 0–82-point maximum and four compliance ratings (from "complies" to "does not comply"). The regulator may look back up to five prior years. The most heavily weighted questions carry weight 5 and account for ~40 of 82 possible points — roughly half the score sits on information disclosure to the client (pre-contract completeness, post-contract notifications) plus support quality and refunds.

**De-PR'd reality:** this is NOT a new rulebook. The substantive requirements — clear tariff/limit/refund disclosure, change notifications, 24/7 support via ≥2 channels (voice + written), fraud handling — already existed as **soft "methodical recommendations" No. 18-MR of 1 Oct 2021** ([garant.ru](https://www.garant.ru/products/ipo/prime/doc/402787969/)). What changed is the *measurement*: a single scoring methodology that converts five-year-old "recommended" conduct into a graded supervisory rating. → Why structured this way: the CBR cannot cheaply rewrite the National Payment System law, so it upgrades enforcement by productising its own 2021 guidance into a comparable score — the regulatory equivalent of moving from "please" to a report card. → Second order: a rating lets the CBR escalate proportionately (require remediation → fines → operational restrictions → up to **licence revocation**) without new primary legislation.

The timing is driven by the product, not the operators: through e-wallets in 2025 flowed **RUB 13.3 trillion (~1.6x YoY growth)**, growth the CBR itself attributes mainly to **marketplaces** (Ozon, Wildberries/WB, Yandex ecosystems embedding wallets). Supervision is catching up to a channel that quietly became systemic.

## [1] Competitors / peers (regulatory, RU context)
There is no "competitor" here — the subject is the regulator. The relevant peer frame is **CBR's broader 2026 posture of pulling non-bank payment rails toward bank-grade oversight**:
- Digital ruble consumer rollout — [[Russians open 220,000 digital ruble accounts in first month]], [[Финтехно Bank of Russia reframes digital ruble as consumer product]], [[Russia's Finance Ministry pays salaries in digital rubles]].
- Crypto perimeter formalisation — [[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]], [[Russia registers first crypto exchange operators and custodians]].
- Payment-messaging / anti-fraud control — [[Russia's central bank calls MAX transaction approval premature]].
→ The lay of the land: the CBR is simultaneously (a) building its own retail rail (digital ruble), (b) licensing crypto intermediaries, and (c) now grading private e-wallet operators. The e-money market (~40 operators, but only **6–7 widely recognised**) is concentrated enough that a public-ish rating has real reputational teeth for the handful that matter. → Second order: grading disclosure/support — not capital or AML per se — signals a *consumer-protection* lever, aligning e-wallet UX obligations closer to banks ("приблизить требования к банковским").

## [2] Company history / fit
Not a company — fit is with the CBR's method. The regulator has a consistent pattern in 2026: formalise, register, grade. The e-wallet methodology slots exactly between the 2021 soft recommendations (the dormant substance) and the 2026 enforcement appetite. → Why now: the RUB 13.3tn figure crossed a threshold where "recommendations" no longer match the channel's systemic weight; marketplaces made wallets a mass-retail payment instrument, so the CBR converts guidance into a scored supervisory tool it can act on.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Genuinely new:** the *scoring instrument* — 33 weighted questions, 82-point scale, 4 ratings, 5-year lookback. **Not new:** the underlying conduct standards (exist since Oct 2021). → Why the value-add is real but bounded: the mechanism gives the CBR a reproducible, comparable, escalatable metric rather than ad-hoc complaint handling — that is a real supervisory upgrade. BUT **traction is zero so far**: no operator has been scored, no rating published, no sanction applied. It is an approved methodology, not an outcome. → Who bears the cost: the ~6–7 large operators (marketplace and ecosystem wallets) face compliance/disclosure uplift; whether ratings are made public (sharpening the teeth) is **open** and not confirmed in sources.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect the CBR to begin scoring operators and issue remediation demands; repeat/unaddressed deficiencies escalate to fines, operational restrictions, up to licence revocation. Framing in RU press ("под микроскоп", "угроза отзыва лицензии", "приблизит к банковским") is tightening-of-control. → Why this direction: as wallets absorb marketplace volume, the CBR treats them as quasi-deposit consumer products and imports bank-style conduct supervision. → Counterintuitive second order: tighter conduct grading on only ~40 operators (dominated by a few ecosystems) may *entrench* the incumbents — compliance cost is a fixed burden that large marketplace wallets absorb easily but could deter new entrants, concentrating the market further even as the stated goal is consumer protection.

**Red-team**

- **Is it really new?** Partly. The *scoring methodology* (33 weighted questions, 82-point scale, 4 ratings, 5-year lookback), order dated 29 Sep 2026, is new. The *substance* (disclosure, notifications, 24/7 support, refunds) is a restatement of Bank of Russia Methodical Recommendations No. 18-MR of 1 Oct 2021 — five-year-old "recommended" conduct now made measurable. This is an enforcement/measurement upgrade, not new conduct rules.
- **Announced or launched?** Announced methodology only. **Zero traction**: no operator scored, no rating issued, no sanction applied. "Approved" ≠ "in effect against anyone".
- **Precise mechanism delta:** converts dormant 2021 soft guidance into a reproducible, comparable, escalatable supervisory score (remediation → fines → restrictions → licence revocation).
- **Who's silent about what:** whether ratings will be *published* (the thing that would give it market teeth) is unconfirmed; appeal mechanism unstated; whether the 5-year lookback is retroactively punitive is unclear.
- **Scale check (corroborated):** RUB 13.3tn wallet turnover in 2025 (~1.6x YoY), CBR attributes growth to marketplaces; ~40 operators but only 6–7 recognised — market is concentrated, so the rating's real reach is a handful of ecosystem wallets.
- **Second-order risk:** fixed compliance cost on a ~40-operator, few-giants market may entrench incumbents and deter entrants — concentrating a market in the name of consumer protection.
- **Freshness:** FRESH. No prior note covers this measure; adjacent CBR-2026 notes (digital ruble, crypto registration, MAX) are distinct events, not the same announcement. Not a reprint of an earlier item.

**Importance: 3/5** — A real supervisory tool over a now-systemic RUB 13.3tn channel, and a clean marker of the CBR pulling e-wallets toward bank-grade conduct oversight. Capped below 4 because: substance is recycled 2021 guidance, traction is nil (nothing measured or sanctioned yet), scope is conduct/disclosure only (not capital/AML), and the market is tiny/concentrated (~6–7 operators that matter). Directionally important as a regulatory signal; low on immediate real-world impact.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russian e-money / e-wallet is a fast-scaling sub-vertical of the domestic payments stack. Per the Bank of Russia (via iz.ru, Oct 2026), e-wallet transaction volume rose ~1.6x to RUB 13.3tn in 2025, on ~8.8bn operations (+1.4x); the regulator attributes the surge chiefly to the marketplace business model (buyers/sellers settling inside platform wallets). For scale, Russian e-commerce turnover was ~RUB 13.2tn in 2025, projected ~RUB 15.7tn in 2026 and ~RUB 33tn by 2030 (~20% CAGR, down from ~34% in 2016–25; per Interfax/TAdviser). Structure: value is captured at the platform/wallet layer rather than by standalone PSPs; barriers are mainly regulatory (CBR operator registry) and network/distribution (wallets embedded in dominant marketplaces), so the segment is concentrated around a few ecosystem players rather than fragmented. Driver / "why now": regulation is catching up to a product that outgrew its advisory-only rulebook — the underlying methodological recommendations date to Oct 2021, but only now (CBR order of ~29 Sep 2026, per iz.ru) does the CBR move from advice to a measured scoring regime, in parallel with e-wallet funds entering deposit insurance (DIA, up to RUB 1.4m, effective 21 Jul 2026, for verified holders; per cbr.ru/akm.ru). Second-order effect: both moves push e-money operators toward banking-grade transparency and consumer-protection standards.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: wallet TPV (RUB 13.3tn in 2025), transaction count (8.8bn), and now a regulatory quality score (max 82 points across 33 weighted criteria, with the nine weight-5 items worth 40/82 ≈ 49% — tariff disclosure pre-contract, change notifications, support, refunds). QR/biometric-initiated payments reached RUB 5.7tn (+1.4x) in 2025, a competing rail. Key players: marketplace-ecosystem wallets (Ozon, Wildberries ecosystems) as the growth engine, plus incumbent standalone operators such as YooMoney (Sber-owned, ex-Yandex.Money). Basis of competition: distribution via captive marketplace traffic and embedded checkout, not price. Recent moves (dated): DIA coverage extension 21 Jul 2026; CBR scoring methodology ~29 Sep 2026 reported ~12 Oct 2026. Protagonist here is the regulator, not a commercial operator — the CBR's "position" is a tightening supervisor moving e-money toward bank parity; its lever (moat) is statutory authority over the operator registry and a look-back window of up to five prior years. Operator-level KPIs (per-wallet balances, individual operator scores, fraud/complaint rates) are not disclosed → [UNSOURCED].

**Comps & multiples.** This is a regulator-driven regulation item with no deal/round/valuation, so trading multiples are not applicable — "no data" on EV/Revenue, P/E etc. Qualitative regulatory comps in-base: [[Финтехно Bank of Russia reframes digital ruble as consumer product]] (same regulator repositioning a payment product for consumers, Oct 2026); [[Saudi central bank mandates 25 free core banking services]] (foreign central-bank mandate raising baseline consumer-protection/transparency standards, Jan 2026); [[Bank of Russia warns brokers against manipulative gamification]] and [[Bank of Russia proposes 3bn ruble minimum for IPO listing tiers]] (2026 CBR conduct/standards tightening across other verticals). Distribution not computed — no numeric comparables; qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Compliance-cost asymmetry / consolidation pressure** — a 33-criterion, weighted scorecard with a five-year look-back raises fixed compliance cost; smaller independent operators may struggle to clear the weight-5 items (half the score), nudging the segment further toward the already-dominant marketplace ecosystems. Why: regulatory burden tends to entrench scale incumbents.
2. **Soft enforcement / "measured but not binding"** — the CBR frames output as a rating plus "recommendations to fix shortcomings"; it is unclear (not disclosed) whether a low score carries sanctions, public disclosure, or registry consequences. Why: a score with no teeth changes little for operator behavior — "announced" ≠ "enforced".
3. **Concentration in marketplace rails** — growth is explicitly pinned to a couple of marketplace models; a scoring regime that still lets the biggest ecosystems set the pace concentrates systemic consumer-protection risk in 2–3 platforms. Why: single-platform operational failure now affects a RUB-13tn+ flow.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This reads as the first step in converging e-money regulation with banking standards — rating methodology now, DIA coverage already in force, plausibly binding thresholds next (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: within ~12 months the CBR attaches concrete consequences (public scores or registry action) to the rating; trigger to watch = any CBR order making the score binding or published per-operator. What breaks the thesis: the rating stays advisory/internal through 2027, in which case it is disclosure theater rather than a real re-rating of operator conduct.
