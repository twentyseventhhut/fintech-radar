# Context-enrichment: Evident AI Index 2026 maps bank AI economics
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**FRESHNESS: FRESH.** This is the **Banking** edition of the Evident AI Index (50 banks, JPMorgan #1), published 2026-10-06 — a genuinely new annual release. It is NOT a duplicate of our prior [[Evident AI Index benchmarks payments networks' AI maturity]] note (2026-02-26), which covered a *different* index: the inaugural **Payments** edition (12 processors; Visa/Mastercard/PayPal lead). Same vendor, same four-pillar methodology, different sector and reporting cadence → new event.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Evident Insights published the 2026 **Banking** AI Index on **Mon 6 Oct 2026** — described as the fifth annual edition (launched 2023). It scores **50 of the world's largest banks** (North America, Europe, selected APAC) on AI *maturity* using ~75 public ("outside-in") indicators across four pillars:
- **Talent ~45%** (AI developers, model-risk talent, hiring/training) — by far the heaviest.
- **Innovation ~30%** (ventures, research, patents, open source).
- **Leadership ~15%** (exec comms, strategic positioning, visibility).
- **Transparency ~10%** (responsible-AI content/disclosure).

**Crucial de-PR point (changes the central question):** a high Evident score measures **organizational capability and positioning, NOT financial ROI** — Evident's own methodology says so. The aggregated note's framing ("банковский ИИ получил промышленную экономику… лидеры считают предотвращённые потери, выручку…") is **more optimistic than Evident's own data**. The hard numbers cut the other way: of the **~1,100 AI use cases** banks published over five years, **barely ~1% disclose a concrete financial result**, and only **~12% disclose impact vs operational KPIs**. So the real story is the *opposite* of "industrial economics has arrived": the sector still cannot (or will not) show the money. Evident itself is adding a **fifth "outcomes" pillar in 2027** — an implicit admission the current index does not measure results. The note's own "лишь ~1%" caveat is the load-bearing fact; the "промышленная экономика" headline is editorializing.

- **YoY:** 50-bank average score **+26% / +9.6 points**, ~3x the 2023–25 pace (confirmed, Evident). Fastest acceleration since launch.
- A small leading cohort IS starting to quantify value: **12 banks (up from 8)** report actual/targeted AI financial value; examples — Capital One ~$150M fraud prevention, TD ~CAN$195M value. But 12 of 50 ≠ "leaders bake AI into financial plans" as a norm.

## [1] Competitors / peers (here: the ranking + rival indices)
**2026 Top-10 (confirmed, exact):** 1) JPMorganChase 2) Capital One 3) RBC 4) CommBank 5) Wells Fargo 6) UBS 7) Bank of America (↑ from 10) 8) Citi (↑ from 12) 9) Morgan Stanley (↓ from 5) 10) TD (↑3). **HSBC fell to 11th — no UK bank in the top 10.** Top-3 (JPM/Capital One/RBC) has been locked for four straight years; JPMorgan #1 in every edition; every current top-10 member has placed in the top-10 before, six in every edition → leadership is **structurally sticky**.

**Why so sticky (2nd order):** the index is ~45% Talent, and AI-talent density compounds (hiring begets research begets more hiring). That means Evident partly measures *who can afford the biggest AI payroll* — i.e. the largest balance sheets. The ranking rewards scale, which is why the same mega-banks recur. Rival benchmark: Evident's own **Payments** index ([[Evident AI Index benchmarks payments networks' AI maturity]]) and an **AI Payments Index** flagging agentic AI — Evident is franchising the same methodology across sectors.

## [2] Company history / fit (JPMorgan as the anchor)
JPMorgan's #1 is consistent with its disclosed posture: ~$18B tech budget, **450+ AI use cases in production**, **~200k staff on its internal LLM Suite daily**, AI-attributed benefits growing 30–40% YoY. Fits our corpus: JPMorgan's broad AI/infra push (e.g. [[Bank of America rolls out AskGPS GenAI assistant for payments]] shows the peer-bank GenAI-assistant wave; [[Commonwealth Bank builds AI agent to detect fraud and build defences]] — CommBank, #4 — shows a top-ranked bank shipping agentic fraud AI). **Why banks act this way:** AI capability is now a competitive-positioning signal to investors and talent, so disclosure/visibility (Leadership + Transparency pillars) is itself rewarded — which incentivizes banks to *publicize* AI regardless of proven ROI.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**What's genuinely new in 2026 edition:** the explicit pivot toward *economics* framing and the "12 banks now report financial value" datapoint — a real but thin signal. **What is NOT new:** the four pillars, the JPM/Capital One/RBC podium (4th year), the top-10 cast. **Traction reality check:** the index measures inputs (talent, research, comms), not adoption outcomes. The one adoption-grade stat — ~1% of 1,100 use cases disclose a financial result — is a *negative* traction signal for the sector's ROI. Independent ROI skepticism is live in mainstream press (e.g. Yahoo/Zacks: JPM's ~$2B estimated annual AI benefit vs ~$18-20B tech spend, "near-term monetization uncertain"). Cambridge/industry survey: **76% of large FIs struggle to measure AI value.** So the value-add of the *Index* is as a capability scoreboard and PR instrument; the value-add of bank AI itself remains largely **unproven in disclosed dollars**.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **2027:** Evident's planned outcomes pillar will test whether maturity converts to ROI — the real question.
- **Sector economics:** McKinsey ~$700B potential banking cost savings but "much competed away"; pioneers +~4pts RoTE, laggards -1–2pts. KPMG Q1-2026: avg projected US AI spend ~$177M/12mo. Agentic AI is the 2026 frontier but outcome disclosure stays thin.
- **Counterintuitive 2nd-order effect:** because the index rewards Talent/disclosure, a bank can climb by *hiring and publishing* faster than it delivers P&L — so the "+26% average" may partly reflect an **AI-disclosure arms race**, not value creation. Headcount cuts (see [[Standard Chartered to cut 7,800 back-office jobs by 2030]], [[Orgvue 39% of firms cut staff over AI, most regret it]]) are where AI economics actually show up — and they're messy, not the clean "industrial economics" the headline implies.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

### Top challenge / red-team questions

1. **Is this a new event or a reprint?** FRESH. New annual edition (Banking, 2026-10-06). Our only prior Evident note is the Feb-2026 **Payments** index — different sector, different cadence. Not a duplicate.
2. **Does a high score mean a bank makes money on AI?** NO. Evident scores *capability/maturity* (~45% Talent), explicitly "not financial returns." The note's "industrial economics" framing overstates this.
3. **What's the strongest de-PR fact?** ~1% of ~1,100 published bank AI use cases disclose a concrete financial result; only ~12% disclose vs operational KPIs. The sector still can't show the money. (Evident/Finadium.)
4. **Is "+26% / +9.6 pts" real growth or disclosure inflation?** Confirmed figure, but Talent/Leadership/Transparency weighting means it partly reflects more hiring + more AI PR — an arms race, not proven value creation. (analysis)
5. **Is the ranking sticky because leaders are better, or because they're bigger?** Both — but ~45% Talent weight rewards the largest AI payrolls, i.e. the biggest balance sheets. Top-3 locked 4 years; JPM #1 every edition. Scale is baked in. (analysis)
6. **Top-10 exact order correct?** Confirmed: JPM, Capital One, RBC, CommBank, Wells Fargo, UBS, BofA, Citi, Morgan Stanley, TD. (Note's Russian list matches.) HSBC fell to 11th; no UK bank in top-10 — a detail the aggregated note truncates.
7. **Edition count — is "fifth" right?** Yes (launched 2023, 5th edition). Not "third."
8. **Does JPMorgan's spend justify its #1?** ~$18-20B tech budget, 450+ AI use cases live, ~200k on LLM Suite daily — but est. ~$2B annual AI benefit; mainstream press calls near-term monetization uncertain. Capability yes, ROI open.
9. **Who's silent about what?** Realized group-wide AI ROI — almost nobody discloses it. The index can't force it; hence the planned 2027 outcomes pillar.
10. **What breaks the "AI maturity = returns" read?** 76% of large FIs can't measure AI value (Cambridge survey); McKinsey says much of ~$700B savings gets "competed away." Maturity ≠ durable margin.
11. **Where do bank-AI economics actually appear?** In headcount, not revenue — see Standard Chartered's 7,800 cuts and Orgvue's finding that most AI-driven cuts are regretted. Messy, not "industrial."
12. **Is this independent or vendor marketing?** Independent outside-in benchmark, but Evident monetizes the franchise (Banking, Payments, Insurance indices) → incentive to publish flattering "leaders" lists. Treat rankings as directional.
13. **Any contradiction with our corpus?** None. Consistent with peer GenAI rollouts (BofA AskGPS) and top-ranked CommBank shipping fraud AI agents.
14. **What would make this a 4-5/5?** Hard ROI disclosure across the sector, or a methodology change proving value. Absent that, it's a capability scoreboard — informative, not market-moving.

**Importance: 3/5 — rationale:** A credible, widely-cited annual benchmark with concrete, confirmed figures (top-10, +26%, the 1%-of-1,100 ROI-disclosure stat) that genuinely frames the bank-AI debate. But it measures *maturity, not ROI*, the leadership is near-static (JPM #1 five years running, top-3 locked four years), and the headline "AI now has industrial economics" is contradicted by the index's own ~1% disclosure figure. High signal as context / debate-framing, low on new, market-moving developments → 3/5.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** The Evident AI Index is an independent, "outside-in" benchmark of AI maturity across the 50 largest banks in North America, Europe and APAC, built from 75 indicators across four weighted pillars: Talent (45%), Innovation (30%), Leadership (15%), Transparency (10%) (per Evident Insights / American Banker, 2026-10-06). The 2026 edition shows AI deployment accelerating: the average score rose +26% YoY (≈9.6 pts), nearly 3x the 2023–25 moving average — the fastest pace since the Index launched in 2023. The "why now": banks are moving from pilot sprawl toward measurable economics, but the gap is stark — barely 1% of disclosed use cases carry a concrete financial return, 38 of the 50 banks are not yet reporting or projecting any AI return, and in Q2 2026 only 27% of new use cases carried a disclosed outcome (per Evident, via Finance Connect / Banking Dive). This mirrors the broader enterprise picture: MIT's "GenAI Divide" found 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots delivered no measurable P&L impact despite ~$30–40bn invested, against Gartner's forecast of ~$2.5tn global AI spend in 2026 (per MIT NANDA study, via multiple secondary citations). Structure: banking AI leadership is highly consolidated — all 2026 top-10 have placed in the top 10 before, and six have been there in every edition; entry barriers are Talent (hiring/retaining AI builders, 45% of score) plus scale of data and capital, which structurally favors the largest incumbents.

**Competitive landscape.** The KPIs the Index runs on: Talent density (AI hires/retention), Innovation (research output, patents, ventures), Leadership (clarity of communicated AI returns), Transparency (responsible-AI practices). Rankings 2026: JPMorganChase #1 (5th straight year, first or second in all four pillars), Capital One #2 (leads Talent and Innovation), RBC #3 (top non-US lender); then CommBank, Wells Fargo, UBS, Bank of America, Citi, Morgan Stanley, TD. Basis of competition is talent + demonstrated economics, not price. Recent peer moves in-base: [[UBS appoints Daniele Magazzeni as chief AI officer]] (Talent/Leadership signal), [[Bank of America rolls out AskGPS GenAI assistant for payments]] (production GenAI deployment), [[HSBC plans deep UK wealth management job cuts in AI push]] (AI used to justify labor takeout). Protagonist position: JPMorgan is the clear leader and widening its lead over Capital One — it now estimates ~$2bn/yr of AI-driven benefits, up from an earlier $1–1.5bn (per American Banker / Analytics Insight). Moat `(analysis)`: scale of proprietary transaction data + ability to out-hire peers for scarce AI talent (intangibles + scale); the Index itself notes the top-10 account for ~25% of all use cases with impact claims, so the leaders' advantage compounds in the hardest-to-fake dimension (realized outcomes).

**Comps & multiples.** This is a research-report / benchmark, not a deal or round — there is no transaction valuation to multiple out, so trading-comp arithmetic is "no data" (the subject is a ranking of listed banks, not a fundable entity). The one disclosed economic figure is JPMorgan's ~$2bn/yr AI benefit; set against JPM's FY2025 scale (10-K dated 2026-02-13, data/jp-morgan/edgar/10-K) it is a small single-digit-percent-of-net-income contribution — material but not yet transformational, consistent with the Index's own "1% disclose financial returns" caveat (IR grounding: jp-morgan is in ir_coverage; latest filing is the 2025 10-K, 2026-02-13). Internal comps as qualitative peers rather than valuation comps: [[Bank of America rolls out AskGPS GenAI assistant for payments]], [[UBS appoints Daniele Magazzeni as chief AI officer]], [[HSBC plans deep UK wealth management job cuts in AI push]], [[Vedomosti AI adoption lifts demand for corporate data protection]]. Multiple distribution not computed — no comparable deal figures.

**Risk flags.**
- **ROI-disclosure gap (headline risk):** only ~1% of 1000+ published bank AI use cases disclose a financial result and 38/50 banks report no return at all — the sector's reported "maturity" is largely infrastructure/org readiness, not proven P&L, so the +26% score jump can overstate real value creation (second-order: a correction in AI-spend expectations would hit the laggards hardest).
- **Benchmark-construction / Goodhart risk:** a public, weighted ranking (Talent 45%) can be gamed — banks may optimize for disclosed hires, patents and communications (Leadership 15%) rather than outcomes; "outside-in" scoring rewards what is publicized, not necessarily what works.
- **Leadership entrenchment masking concentration risk:** six banks in every top-10 suggests the benefit accrues to a handful of incumbents; for the long tail, large AI spend without disclosed return (echoing MIT's 95%-no-ROI finding) is a margin risk, not an advantage.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The story of 2026 is the shift from "are banks adopting AI" (settled: yes, fast) to "can they prove it pays" (mostly unproven). Falsifiable thesis: if, in the 2027 Index, the count of banks reporting realized/projected AI returns does not rise materially above 2026's 12 (up from 8), the sector's AI narrative is maturity-theater and multiples priced on AI upside are vulnerable. Trigger to watch: whether JPMorgan's ~$2bn figure is matched by a second large bank disclosing a comparable hard number — that would validate the "economics" thesis; continued silence from the other 38 would falsify it.
