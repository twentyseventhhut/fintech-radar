# Finda set to become first Korean fintech to buy a savings bank

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Connecting the Dots in Fintech] 🇰🇷 Finda is set to become the first Korean FinTech to acquire a savings bank. The loan comparison platform agreed in June to buy Daewon Savings Bank for about 12 billion won ($9 million). Finda plans to turn the lender into an AI native bank.

Finda, a loan comparison platform, is set to acquire a savings bank, in what would be a first for a Korean fintech company as it looks to build an artificial intelligence (AI)-native bank, industry officials said Monday.
The Financial Services Commission's subcommittee approved Finda's application to acquire Daewon Savings Bank on Friday. The regulator is set to make a final decision at its regular meeting Wednesday.
Finda signed a deal in June to acquire all of Daewon Savings Bank's shares for about 12 billion won ($9 million). The acquisition would allow the company to move beyond matching borrowers with lenders and offer financial services directly, including loans and deposits.
Once the deal receives final approval, Finda plans to appoint a new CEO through board and shareholder meetings.
The fintech firm aims to turn the lender into an AI-native bank, relying on digital services rather than a traditional network of branches. It plans to draw on AI technology and loan-comparison data it has collected since 2019 to expand its online lending business nationwide, the firm said.
The company also expects to broaden its range of products for borrowers with low to midlevel credit ratings, drawing on its ties with JB Financial Group, the fintech firm's second-largest shareholder.
But the acquisition also brings challenges. Savings banks have come under growing pressure from losses tied to real estate project financing and rising funding costs, which could become a test for Finda's ability to manage bad loans and secure stable funding.
The company will also have to meet rules requiring savings banks to lend a certain share of their funds within designated business regions, even as it seeks to expand its online services nationwide.
Daewon Savings Bank, based in Gyeongju, North Gyeongsang Province, is a relatively small lender whose business region covers Daegu, North Gyeongsang Province and Gangwon Province. 
Since its existing shareholder, Daea Savings Bank, began seeking a buyer in 2015, the lender has de facto been out of business. As of the end of June, Daewon had total assets of 3.1 billion won and virtually no outstanding loans.

# Context-enrichment: Finda set to become first Korean fintech to buy a savings bank
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Finda — a Korean loan-comparison (brokerage) platform — is on the verge of final regulatory approval to buy **100% of Daewon Savings Bank** for **~KRW 12bn ($9m)**, a deal it actually **signed in June 2026**. The FSC subcommittee cleared it on Fri 2026-10-16; the full FSC board decides at its regular meeting Wed 2026-10-22/23 (the primary says "Wednesday"; news.jkn.co.kr says "the 23rd"). On final approval Finda installs a new CEO via board/shareholder meetings and converts Daewon into an "AI-native" digital bank.
- **De-PR the "first fintech to buy a savings bank" headline:** the substance is a cheap, near-shell **banking-license acquisition**, not a growth deal. Daewon is a Gyeongju-based micro-lender with **~KRW 3.1bn total assets and virtually no outstanding loans** (per primary; a March-2026 article cited ~KRW 3.5bn as of Sep-2025). Its prior owner Daea Savings Bank has been shopping it **since 2015**; the bank has "de facto been out of business." So Finda isn't buying a loan book or deposits — it is buying a **dormant charter** and a regional footprint (Daegu / North Gyeongsang / Gangwon). $9m is license-arbitrage pricing, not franchise value.
- **Why structured this way:** acquiring a comatose savings bank is the path-of-least-resistance route for a non-bank fintech to obtain a deposit-taking lending licence in Korea without a de-novo internet-bank application (the Kbank/KakaoBank/Toss route, which required consortia, capital and multi-year FSC processes — see [[Kbank advances third IPO attempt in South Korea]]). The flip side (what PR is silent on): Finda inherits a **regional business-region lending quota** — savings banks must lend a set share within their designated geography — which structurally **collides with Finda's "nationwide online" ambition**. That tension is the real story.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Finda is one of Korea's **top-3 loan-comparison platforms alongside Toss and Kakao Pay** (per Korea Times / KoreaTechDesk, 2023-24). Positioning:
- **Toss / Toss Bank** — already a licensed internet bank ([[Toss Bank posts 96.8 billion won 2025 net profit, doubling]]); has a joint-lending tie-up with Gwangju Bank. Toss is years ahead on charter + scale.
- **KakaoBank / Kakao Pay** — licensed internet bank + payments; KakaoBank/Jeonbuk Bank joint personal-credit loans ([[KakaoBank opens app to foreign residents in South Korea]], [[KakaoBank gains 93.3 billion won from Superbank IPO]]).
- **Kbank** — pursuing its 3rd IPO ([[Kbank advances third IPO attempt in South Korea]]).
Against these, Finda is **catching up, from behind, via the cheapest door** — buying a shell savings-bank licence rather than winning an internet-bank charter. **Why the landscape is this way (2nd order):** the FSC is actively easing regional/internet-bank boundaries (Toss+Gwangju, KakaoBank+Busan corporate-loan "innovative financial service" designations, 2026); regulators now *want* digital platforms to extend credit nationwide via regional charters. Finda is riding that regulatory opening, but as a savings-bank (second-tier) licensee it sits a rung below the internet banks on deposit trust and funding cost.

## [2] Company history / fit
Founded 2015; built a loan-comparison marketplace, collecting **loan-matching data since 2019** and claiming ~KRW 4tn of loans brokered in the year before its Series C. **Series C: ~$37m (KRW ~47bn), July 2023, led by JB Financial Group + 500 Global** (Korea Times / FintechFutures). Crucially, **JB Financial holds ~15% of Finda** (JB Holdings ~5% + Jeonbuk Bank ~10%) — the primary undersells this as merely "second-largest shareholder." Jeonbuk Bank has partnered with Finda on digital credit loans **since 2020**.
- **Why Finda acts this way:** a pure brokerage take-rate is a commodity margin — it only *matches* borrowers and lenders and never owns the balance sheet. Owning a licence lets it **capture the lending spread + deposits directly** (move up the value chain from referral fee to NIM). The data moat (6 yrs of loan-comparison data) is the stated underwriting edge for low/mid-credit borrowers. The JB tie is the connective tissue: JB's **Jeonbuk Bank just did Korea's first bitcoin-backed loans** ([[Jeonbuk Bank offers South Korea's first bitcoin-backed loans]]) — the same group is Finda's strategic sponsor, giving it balance-sheet/funding backing and a product pipeline for sub-prime/mid-credit lending.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new:** first time a Korean **fintech** (vs a chaebol-backed consortium) converts a savings-bank charter into a direct digital lender. The "fintech-owned savings bank" structure is the novelty.
- **Not new:** "AI-native digital bank" is a crowded Korean claim (KakaoBank, Toss Bank, Kbank all brand on digital/AI). Finda's differentiator is the proprietary loan-comparison dataset — plausible but **unproven on a balance sheet**; brokering loans ≠ underwriting and holding them.
- **Traction = zero so far on the bank side.** This is an *announced* license acquisition, not live banking. Daewon has no meaningful loan book, so Finda starts from near-scratch on deposits, capital adequacy and bad-loan management. The value-add is **entirely prospective**.
- **Who captures the margin (2nd order):** today Finda earns referral fees; owning a licence lets it keep the NIM — but savings-bank funding is expensive and trust-light vs internet banks, so the NIM it captures may be thinner and riskier than the internet-bank peers it wants to match.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Immediate:** FSC full-board vote ~Oct 22/23 2026 → new CEO → capital injection + rebuild of Daewon as a digital lender. Expect Finda to lean on JB/Jeonbuk for funding and product.
- **Sector backdrop (headwind):** the Korean savings-bank sector is **stressed** — overdue real-estate PF loans ~**KRW 2.69tn at end-Jun 2026 (+15.6% in 6 months)**; three lenders with property-loan delinquency >30% (Seoul Economic Daily, Sep 2026); sector-wide PF exposure ~KRW 230tn (~$170bn, ~10% of GDP); the 2025 delinquency improvement is widely called an "optical illusion" from normalization-fund sales (only 7.1% of purchased sites actually disposed by Sep 2026). **Why this matters (2nd order):** Finda bought a *clean* shell precisely to **avoid** inheriting that PF rot — a smart entry at a sector trough — BUT the savings-bank *brand* is in a trust/funding-cost downcycle (retail deposits draining from large savings banks, Sep 2026), so Finda must overcome negative sector sentiment to raise cheap deposits. **Regulatory wildcard:** the business-region lending quota vs nationwide-online ambition; watch whether the FSC grants Finda the same regional-partnership/innovation carve-outs it gave Toss+Gwangju and KakaoBank+Busan.
- **Sentiment:** mildly positive strategic logic, but execution-heavy and small-ticket; this is a *starting gun*, not a de-rating event.

## Top challenge/extra questions (second-order)
1. Is this really a "bank acquisition" or just a **licence purchase**? (Daewon ~KRW 3.1bn assets, no loans — answer: licence.)
2. How much **new capital** must Finda inject to make Daewon a viable lender, and from where (JB? new raise)? — open.
3. Does the **business-region lending quota** legally block Finda's nationwide-online plan, or will the FSC grant an innovation carve-out like Toss+Gwangju? — partly open; regulatory trend favorable.
4. Is JB Financial's ~15% stake a conflict (related-party lending/funding) or a crutch? Who needs whom more — Finda needs JB's balance sheet; JB gets a digital distribution arm. — analysis.
5. Does the loan-comparison **dataset actually confer underwriting edge**, or is it demand-side matching data with weak default-prediction signal? — open (unproven).
6. What is Finda's **funding cost** as a small savings bank vs internet-bank peers, and can thin savings-bank deposit trust fund nationwide online lending? — open; likely a disadvantage.
7. Why accept savings-bank (2nd-tier) status instead of pursuing an internet-bank charter like Kbank? — cost/speed; cheapest door. (analysis)
8. Is "AI-native bank" a real differentiator vs KakaoBank/Toss/Kbank who all claim it, or marketing? — likely marketing until live metrics.
9. What valuation does this imply for **Finda itself** (last known ~$37m Series C 2023; 2021 ~$90.9m)? Does a licence lift its multiple? — open.
10. Could sector contagion (PF losses, deposit drain) raise Finda's cost of entry even with a clean shell? — plausible via brand/funding channel.
11. Regulatory: will the FSC impose conditions (capital, lending caps, consumer protection) at the Oct 22/23 vote? — open.
12. Does owning the balance sheet **cannibalize** Finda's brokerage model (it now competes with the lenders it refers to)? — real channel-conflict risk. (analysis)
13. Timeline to "live" AI-native bank — months or years? Historically de-novo digital banks take years to reach profitability (Toss Bank only turned solid profit 2025). — open; expect multi-year.
14. Is $9m a signal that **no one else wanted Daewon** (on market since 2015) — i.e., is the asset cheap for a reason? — yes, dormant; cheapness = low franchise value, not a bargain. (analysis)
15. Second-order: if Finda succeeds, does it open a **wave of fintech-buys-savings-bank** M&A (a dozen+ small/distressed savings banks are exit candidates)? — plausible precedent-setter; this is the item's main systemic weight.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Korea's banking/lending market has two dynamics colliding here: (1) **internet/digital banks** (KakaoBank, Toss Bank, Kbank) scaling nationwide, and (2) a **distressed savings-bank sector** of 79 lenders. Sector structure: the savings-bank tier is **fragmented and stressed** — overdue real-estate PF loans ~**KRW 2.69tn at end-Jun 2026, +15.6% in six months**; three lenders carry property-loan delinquency **>30%** (per Seoul Economic Daily, Sep 2026); nationwide PF exposure ~KRW 230tn (~$170bn, ~10% of GDP). The 2025 headline delinquency drop (8.52%→6.04%) is widely called an "optical illusion" from normalization-fund sales (only ~7.1% of purchased sites actually disposed by Sep 2026). **Why now:** FSC is easing regional/internet-bank boundaries (2026 designations of Toss+Gwangju and KakaoBank+Busan joint corporate loans as "innovative financial services") — opening a window for digital platforms to reach borrowers nationwide via regional charters. Entry barriers: capital + a scarce **banking licence** — which is exactly why Finda bought a dormant charter rather than applying de-novo. Second-order: buying a *clean shell* at a sector trough lets Finda sidestep the PF rot while acquiring the scarce asset (the licence).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for digital lenders: **NIM / cost of funds, deposit base, loan-book growth, credit-cost (delinquency), capital adequacy (BIS ratio)** — plus, on Finda's legacy side, loans-brokered volume and take-rate. Key players & basis of competition: KakaoBank, Toss Bank, Kbank compete on **distribution/scale + funding cost + brand trust**; savings banks compete on **yield to sub-prime/mid-credit borrowers**. Recent moves: Toss Bank posted ~KRW 96.8bn 2025 net profit (doubling) [[Toss Bank posts 96.8 billion won 2025 net profit, doubling]]; Kbank advancing 3rd IPO [[Kbank advances third IPO attempt in South Korea]]; JB's Jeonbuk Bank launched Korea's first bitcoin-backed loans Oct 2026 [[Jeonbuk Bank offers South Korea's first bitcoin-backed loans]]. **Finda's position: catching up from behind, via the cheapest door** — it enters with a 2nd-tier savings-bank licence and ~zero deposits/loan book, a rung below the internet banks on trust and funding cost. Moat (analysis): proprietary 6-yr loan-comparison dataset + JB Financial sponsorship (~15% owner) — intangibles + strategic distribution, not scale.

**Comps & multiples.** No clean public EV/Revenue or EV/EBITDA computable — Finda is private and Daewon is a dormant micro-entity, so public trading multiples = **no data**.
- **Deal multiple (what IS computable):** ~KRW 12bn ($9m) for 100% of a bank with ~KRW 3.1bn total assets and ~0 loans → **P/Assets ≈ 12 / 3.1 ≈ 3.9x book-assets** (arithmetic shown), but this is meaningless as a franchise multiple because the asset base is a near-empty shell; the price is **licence value, not asset value**. [UNSOURCED] for any earnings-based multiple (no earnings).
- **Finda equity reference:** last Series C ~$37m (KRW ~47bn) July 2023 led by JB Financial + 500 Global; ~$90.9m valuation in Jan 2021 (round valuation, not market cap). No current post-money disclosed → **no data** on today's valuation.
- **Internal comps (licence/scale peers):** [[Kbank advances third IPO attempt in South Korea]] (internet-bank IPO valuation cut 20%), [[KakaoBank gains 93.3 billion won from Superbank IPO]], [[Toss Bank posts 96.8 billion won 2025 net profit, doubling]].
- Distribution not computed (fewer than 3 comparable public multiples); **qualitative comparison only**. Flag: the $9m ticket is *cheap-for-a-reason* (dormant since 2015, no buyer) — low franchise value, not a bargain.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regional lending quota vs nationwide ambition** — savings banks must lend a mandated share within their business region (Daegu/N.Gyeongsang/Gangwon); this structurally conflicts with Finda's nationwide-online plan. Second-order: without an FSC innovation carve-out (as granted to Toss+Gwangju), the growth thesis is capped.
2. **Funding cost / deposit trust** — savings-bank deposits are costlier and lower-trust than internet-bank deposits (retail deposits are draining from large savings banks as of Sep 2026). Second-order: a weak, expensive funding base undercuts the NIM Finda is trying to capture by moving up from brokerage.
3. **Execution + channel conflict** — zero live banking traction; owning a balance sheet puts Finda in competition with the lenders its brokerage refers to (cannibalization), and sector PF stress could raise entry/funding costs via brand contagion even with a clean shell.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is a **precedent-setter**: the first fintech-owned Korean savings bank establishes a **cheap licence-arbitrage M&A template** that other platforms could copy against a field of distressed/exit-seeking small savings banks — potential consolidation/new-entry wave. Falsifiable thesis: *if the FSC grants Finda nationwide-online carve-outs and Daewon reaches a live, growing digital loan book within ~18-24 months, expect copycat fintech-buys-savings-bank deals.* What would make it wrong: regional lending quota enforced strictly + savings-bank funding costs stay high → Finda's bank stays sub-scale and the template doesn't propagate. Trigger to watch: the FSC full-board decision (~Oct 22/23 2026) and any conditions attached.
