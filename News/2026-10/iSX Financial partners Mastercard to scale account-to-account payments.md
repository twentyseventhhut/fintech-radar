ISX Financial's PaidBy® and Mastercard team up to take account-to-account payments cross-border, with local-currency settlement for merchants in 60+ countries.

_Strategic partnership combines Mastercard’s global Open Finance connectivity and scale, with PaidBy®’s orchestration and settlement infrastructure to unlock international A2A payments_

## Beyond Borders: The Next Chapter for Open Banking

In June, at Money20/20 Europe in Amsterdam, Mastercard announced a partnership aimed at one of the most persistent limitations in digital payments: making account-to-account (A2A) payments work across borders at commercial scale. The partnership brings together Mastercard and PaidBy®, the global A2A payment platform developed by ISX Financial®, part of the Xryma Plc group.

Open banking now moves billions of euros across Europe every month, yet most people may have used it without ever hearing the term. Traditionally, there were two ways to pay a merchant online: card payments or manual bank transfers. The former is instant for customer, but slow and expensive for the merchant, requiring the customer to hand over card data. The latter is cheaper, but also slow, error-prone, and impossible to reconcile automatically.

Open banking has emerged as a third option. Introduced across the UK and the EU under payment services regulation in 2018, it allows a licensed and regulated payment provider to initiate a payment from a customer’s bank account on their instruction, authorised inside their own banking application. At checkout, customers choose to pay from their bank account rather than by card, approve the payment in their banking app as they would any other, and are then returned to the merchant’s website. No card number is entered, and no card details are stored. For merchants, funds arrive directly into their bank account rather than through a card network. Transactional costs are comparable to card acceptance, however the payment is confirmed and settled in real time as a credit transfer across Europe and the UK, providing a substantial cash flow advantage to merchants compared with card processing.

Nevertheless, the promise of seamless A2A payments still largely stops at national borders. Merchants seeking to expand internationally continue to navigate fragmented integrations, multiple providers and complex settlement arrangements.

## Mastercard and PaidBy® Introduce the Infrastructure for Global A2A Payments

The Mastercard and PaidBy® partnership introduces one of the first scalable cross-border A2A payment models. Mastercard provides the global connectivity, scheme-level trust, and scale required to make cross-border A2A payments viable for enterprise merchants. PaidBy® complements this with its proprietary orchestration layer, cross-border settlement infrastructure, dynamic currency capabilities, and merchant payout network. Mastercard’s Open Finance capabilities are being integrated directly into the PaidBy® platform.

The partnership will enable consumers to pay merchants directly from their bank accounts in their domestic currency, while allowing merchants in over 60 countries to receive settlement in their preferred local currency, with simplified reconciliation and next-business-day settlement across markets.

## PaidBy®’s Robust Infrastructure Behind the Partnership

Built on ISX Financial’s regulated banktech infrastructure, PaidBy® operates under ISX Financial’s authorisation from the Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC) as a European Economic Area (EEA) Electronic Money Institution (EMI), with UK activity conducted under its Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) authorisation.

Designed as a full-stack payment solution, PaidBy® extends beyond payment initiation to support the wider payment, settlement and reconciliation journey. With PaidBy®, funds are received in a dedicated merchant account, while API-based notifications alert the merchant when the funds have arrived, rather than merely when the payment instruction has been sent. A finance team can therefore release goods or close a ledger on confirmation of funds received, rather than on a promise of payment.

PaidBy® also offers value-added capabilities intended to streamline payment operations, including automated refund management, business-intelligence dashboards, fraud-prevention tools and integration with businesses’ Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems.

“Open banking is at an inflection point, but its potential has been capped by geography”, said Nikogiannis Karantzis, Group Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer of Xryma Plc Group. “The technology to initiate a payment has existed for years. What has been missing is everything that comes after it: conversion, settlement, reconciliation, and refunds working as one system across more than one country.”

## Built Inhouse, Designed for Global Commerce

ISX Financial® as part of the Xryma Plc Group, has been building the technology behind this model for more than a decade. Its infrastructure has been developed in-house and is protected by patents granted across Europe, the United States, Canada and Asia-Pacific.

The strategic partnership between Mastercard and PaidBy® comes amid accelerating global demand for faster settlement and reduced payment friction, as businesses increasingly seek alternatives to traditional payment rails for cross-border transactions.

It also perfectly demonstrates how regulated EMI and banktech infrastructure can be combined with global open banking to support digital commerce across borders and currencies serving businesses across multiple jurisdictions.

For businesses, the significance lies in replacing fragmented integrations and settlement relationships with a more unified cross-border payment model. For the wider payments industry, it represents a step towards an environment in which A2A payments can move beyond their domestic origins and operate as international infrastructure to serve digital commerce.

## About ISX Financial®

_ISX Financial® is the trading name of Xryma Plc, a regulated, vertically integrated banktech group headquartered in Cyprus with operation centres in Cyprus, Lithuania and the United Kingdom. Authorised as an electronic money institution by the Central Bank of Cyprus for the EEA and by the UK Financial Conduct Authority, ISX Financial® is one of the first non-bank direct participants in the Eurosystem's T2 settlement system, settling in central bank money. Its proprietary platform, enables businesses to accept, move and settle funds across cards, bank transfers, open banking and central bank rails, spanning account-to-account payments (PaidBy® | Mastercard), transactional banking (ISX Money®), mass payouts (MassPay), card acquiring and issuing (ISXPay®), identity verification (Paydentity™) and core banking software (Probanx®)._

**Who ISX Financial / Xryma is (the material backstory).** ISX Financial is the trading brand of **Xryma Plc**, a vertically integrated "banktech" group headquartered in Cyprus (ops in Cyprus, Lithuania, UK). Critically, this is the **ex-iSignthis** business: the entity was formerly *iSignthis eMoney Ltd* → *ISX Financial EU Ltd/Plc* → *Xryma Plc* (name change approved by shareholders March 2026; "Xryma" ≈ Greek χρήμα, "money"). Until Oct 2021 it was a subsidiary of ASX-listed **Southern Cross Payments** (the former iSignthis Ltd), which was suspended from the ASX in 2019 and later delisted amid a long ASIC/ASX dispute. See [[ASIC secures $10M penalty from iSignthis]] — in 2025 ASIC secured a A$10M penalty against iSignthis and its former MD/CEO **Nickolas (Nikogiannis) Karantzis** was disqualified from managing corporations for six years and fined A$1M for ASX-disclosure breaches. Karantzis is quoted in this very release as Group MD/CEO of Xryma. That regulatory history is the single most important piece of diligence context and is entirely absent from the promotional copy.

**Regulatory/infra credentials claimed.** EEA EMI authorised by the **Central Bank of Cyprus**; UK activity under **FCA** authorisation; claims to be one of the first non-bank direct participants in the Eurosystem **T2** settlement system (settling in central-bank money). Stack spans PaidBy® (A2A), ISX Money® (transactional banking), MassPay (payouts), ISXPay® (acquiring/issuing), Paydentity™ (KYC — the old iSignthis identity product) and Probanx® (core-banking software). Patents claimed across EU/US/Canada/APAC.

**How this fits Mastercard's A2A playbook (comps).** Mastercard is pursuing an infrastructure-first "arm the ecosystem" A2A strategy rather than leading with its own brand — the same Open Finance/Open Banking rails are being plugged into multiple partners: [[vobapay integrates Mastercard Open Finance for A2A payments]] (DE, Oct 2025), plus US deals with JPMorgan Chase and Worldpay. ISX/PaidBy is the cross-border-settlement flavour of the same template. This sits amid a broader A2A / pay-by-bank push: [[Lloyds and Lolly introduce Pay by Bank kiosks]], [[Peru's OKTO launches interoperable QR account-to-account payments]], and the EPI/Wero buildout ([[Wero to launch in Luxembourg with Payconiq and EPI]]).

**Strategic tension to flag.** A2A/open-banking payments bypass the card rails that generate Mastercard's interchange/scheme economics, so there is a structural cannibalisation question. Mastercard's answer is to monetise the *connectivity, trust and value-added services* layer (Open Finance APIs, fraud/AI payment intelligence, settlement) rather than the per-transaction card fee — turning a disintermediation threat into a toll on the alternative rail. The PaidBy deal is a clean example: Mastercard gets paid for connectivity/scale on a flow that never touches a card.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Account-to-account (A2A) / open-banking payments sit as a "third rail" alongside cards and manual bank transfers: a regulated PISP initiates a credit transfer from the payer's bank account, authorised in-app, with no card data handed over and real-time settlement to the merchant (per ffnews, 2026-10-15). Sizing: global A2A transaction value projected to rise from ~$1.7tn (2024) to ~$5.7tn by 2029, +230% (per Juniper Research, via telemediamagazine, 2026); cross-border A2A transactions forecast to surpass ~11bn in 2026 (per Juniper Research press, 2026). Separate market-sizing houses put the broader A2A market at ~$62.5bn (2025) growing ~14% CAGR (per Polaris/Intelevo secondary citations — vendor reports, treat as directional). Structure: fragmented — A2A works well domestically (open banking "moves billions of euros across Europe every month") but "largely stops at national borders," with merchants stitching together multiple PISPs, settlement and FX providers (ffnews). Entry barriers are regulatory (EMI/PISP licences) and network/scale. Why now: cross-border settlement demand + regulatory push (PSD2 since 2018, BIS Project Nexus linking national instant-payment schemes) make cross-border A2A the largest expansion lane.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/A2A volume, take rate vs card interchange, settlement speed (next-business-day here), conversion/auth rate, merchant/country coverage (60+ countries for PaidBy). Basis of competition: coverage + settlement/reconciliation depth, not just payment initiation. Key players: open-banking rails/aggregators (TrueLayer — acquiring Nordic rival Zimpler, 2025-10 [[TrueLayer to acquire Nordic open-banking rival Zimpler]]; Tink/Visa [[Coinbase and Tink partner to launch pay-by-bank crypto payments in Germany]]; Salt Edge [[Salt Edge and Sola partner on instant payments across Europe]]; Plaid), and Mastercard's own Open Finance distributed to multiple partners — notably vobapay, which integrated the SAME Mastercard Open Finance A2A stack ([[vobapay integrates Mastercard Open Finance for A2A payments]], 2025-10). Mastercard strategy context: this is squarely its multi-rail "beyond cards" push — value-added services and new payment flows (incl. Open Finance/A2A) are the growth engine. IR grounding: Mastercard's **value-added services and solutions net revenue grew +22% (+18% currency-neutral) in Q1 2026** against total Q1 net revenue of $8.4bn (+16%; +12% cn) — i.e. VAS/new-flows outgrow the card-network base (per Mastercard Q1 2026 earnings release, 8-K Ex-99.1, 2026-04-30). ISX/PaidBy position: niche but genuinely differentiated — a vertically integrated, EEA EMI + FCA + one of the first non-bank direct participants in the Eurosystem T2 settlement system, with patented in-house orchestration/settlement/FX (ffnews). Moat `(analysis)`: regulatory licences + direct central-bank-money settlement + patents = switching/scale moat, but distribution depends on Mastercard.

**Comps & multiples.** Internal comps: [[vobapay integrates Mastercard Open Finance for A2A payments]] (same Mastercard rail, EU A2A), [[TrueLayer to acquire Nordic open-banking rival Zimpler]] (open-banking consolidation), [[Coinbase and Tink partner to launch pay-by-bank crypto payments in Germany]], [[Salt Edge and Sola partner on instant payments across Europe]]. No valuation/round/revenue/TPV disclosed for ISX Financial (Xryma Plc), and the named open-banking peers are private — no publicly verifiable EV/Revenue or P/S for either side. Multiples = **no data**; distribution not computed, qualitative comparison only. Mastercard (public, MA) is the large partner, not a comp for the small subject — its VAS multiple is not meaningful against a private EMI. `[UNSOURCED]`: ISX TPV, take rate, merchant count beyond "60+ countries."

**Risk flags.**
1. **Dependence on someone else's rails** — PaidBy's cross-border scale is explicitly delivered via Mastercard's Open Finance connectivity being integrated into PaidBy; ISX does not own the distribution. Second-order: economics/renewal terms sit with Mastercard, and the same stack is offered to rivals (e.g. vobapay), so ISX's "differentiation" is partly shared.
2. **Announced vs live** — the deal was unveiled at Money20/20 Europe (June 2026) and re-amplified 2026-10-15; the release is silent on live volume, pricing/take rate, fraud liability and go-live timing. De-PR: "introduces one of the first scalable models" is a claim, not evidence of adoption.
3. **Regulatory/FX concentration** — A2A cross-border viability hinges on continued open-banking regulation (PSD2/PSD3, UK) and on multi-currency settlement across 60+ countries; any fragmentation in scheme access or FX/settlement risk falls on ISX's EMI balance sheet.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Signals that Mastercard is hedging card disintermediation by becoming the connectivity layer *for* A2A rather than defending cards — monetising A2A through Open Finance + VAS even as volume migrates off interchange (consistent with VAS +22% YoY). Falsifiable thesis: if cross-border A2A scales, watch whether ISX/PaidBy captures orchestration/settlement margin or is reduced to a reseller of Mastercard connectivity. Trigger/what would break it: disclosed live TPV and take-rate, or Mastercard signing additional PaidBy-equivalent partners (as it already did with vobapay), which would commoditise the layer.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Not an earnings event** — this is a cross-border A2A partnership (first announced at Money20/20 Europe, June 2026), surfaced here only because `mastercard` is IR-covered and iSX Financial (now Xryma Plc; note: no longer trading as ASX:ISX, renamed March 2026) is itself a small listed banktech group. Brief results context only, no full earnings deep-dive.

**iSX Financial / Xryma Plc (the small partner) — recent results.** FY2025 (year to 31 Dec 2025): profit after tax €17.3m, total revenue €53.4m (incl. €5.0m other income), seventh consecutive profitable year; own funds €49.0m, net assets €59.2m, total assets €188.9m. However the trajectory has sharply reversed in 2026: H1 2026 (six months to 30 Jun 2026) client revenue fell to €16.9m from €27.7m in H1 2025 (-39% YoY), and profit after tax collapsed to ~€0.03m from €12.3m in H1 2025 — i.e. the group is now barely breakeven at the half-year. So the Mastercard tie-up lands at a point where Xryma's core revenue and profit are contracting hard; the partnership is a growth lever it arguably needs, not a bolt-on to a company printing record numbers (analysis).

**Mastercard (the scale partner) — strategic context.** Mastercard's latest print is Q2 2026 (quarter to 30 Jun 2026): net revenue $9.3bn, +14% (+12% currency-neutral); value-added services & solutions net revenue $3.8bn, +20% (+18% currency-neutral), outpacing the payment network (+10%). This is fresher than the IR snapshot (`ir_latest.json[mastercard]` = Q1 2026 release 2026-04-30). The relevance to this item: A2A / Open Finance connectivity sits inside Mastercard's fast-growing new-payment-flows + VAS layer — the 20% VAS growth is the strategic engine this PaidBy® integration plugs into, so for Mastercard it is a small distribution-style deal feeding a segment already compounding at ~2x network growth; immaterial to Mastercard's numbers, potentially material to Xryma's.
