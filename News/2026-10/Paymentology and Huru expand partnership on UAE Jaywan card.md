Paymentology and Huru expand partnership to launch Jaywan card in the UAE
Paymentology will support Huru’s launch on the UAE’s domestic card scheme as the fintech expands its Universal Account ecosystem
 Dubai, United Arab Emirates – Paymentology, the leading global issuer processor, and Huru, the Emirati-owned financial app licensed by the Central Bank of the UAE, have expanded their longstanding partnership to support the launch of Huru’s card on Jaywan, the UAE’s domestic card scheme. 
The launch will see Paymentology continue to provide the processing technology underpinning Huru’s card programme, supporting the fintech as it broadens the services available to customers across the UAE. Huru has grown significantly since launching, with AED 3 billion in annualised salary flows now passing through Huru accounts and more than 24,000 employers using the platform to pay employees. The company is also working with nine exchange houses as it looks to accelerate adoption of the Universal Account, with a target of approximately one million accounts over the coming year. The Universal Account gives customers an individual IBAN-based account that is linked to them rather than to a specific employer. This means customers can continue using the same account even when their employment changes, reducing the need to repeatedly switch salary arrangements and providing a more consistent way to manage everyday finances. Through the Huru app, customers can receive their salary, make domestic and international payments, send money overseas, top up mobile phones, set money aside towards savings goals and use their Huru card, with the platform currently available in seven languages.
The addition of Jaywan will further develop Huru’s card proposition while supporting the UAE’s wider move towards a stronger domestic payments ecosystem.
 Nauman Hassan, Regional Director MENA at Paymentology, said:  “The UAE is building a payments ecosystem that is increasingly shaped by local innovation, digital-first services and greater customer choice. Jaywan is an important part of that evolution, and Huru is well placed to bring those benefits to a broad and growing customer base.
“Our role is to give Huru the processing flexibility and scale it needs to bring new capabilities to market quickly and confidently. Supporting its move onto Jaywan is a natural next step in our partnership, and one that reflects the strength of what we are building together in the UAE.”
 Abhimanyu Girotra, Chief Executive Officer of Huru, said: “Receiving a salary is only the starting point of someone’s financial journey. Our focus has always been on what happens next — how people can manage their money, make payments, send money home, save for the future and have greater control over their income.
“Paymentology has been an important partner in helping us build and scale that experience. Bringing Jaywan into the Huru ecosystem is another significant step forward, allowing us to continue evolving the Universal Account while giving our customers access to payment experiences that are increasingly aligned with the UAE’s own financial infrastructure.”
The collaboration reflects the continued development of the UAE payments market, where domestic infrastructure, digital-first financial services and modern processing technology are increasingly coming together to create more locally tailored payment experiences.

# Context-enrichment: Paymentology and Huru expand partnership on UAE Jaywan card
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

**FRESHNESS VERDICT: fresh.** This is a genuine NEW development, not a reprint. The Huru-Paymentology card-processing relationship itself was already reported a week earlier in [[Huru partners Paymentology and BitPanda Enterprise]] (2026-10-08), and the base relationship traces to Huru's 2025 launch ([[Huru launches in UAE for the unbanked]], where cards ran on **Visa**). What is NEW here is a concrete, dated capability: Huru's card will be issued on **Jaywan**, the UAE's domestic card scheme, with a Q4 2026 go-live. That is a real rail addition/switch (Visa → domestic scheme), so it is a follow-up event, not a re-report. Borderline — the 10-08 note is near-identical on the parties — but the Jaywan rail is a distinct new term, so fresh (not stale).

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- Paymentology (global issuer-processor) and Huru (CBUAE-licensed Emirati fintech) "expanded" their existing partnership so Huru's card program launches on **Jaywan**, the UAE's domestic card scheme (operated by Al Etihad Payments, a CBUAE subsidiary). Paymentology stays the processing layer underneath; Jaywan is the new scheme/rail on top. Target go-live: Q4 2026.
- De-PR'd: this is **not a new partnership** and **not a new processor win** — Paymentology already processes Huru's cards. The only incremental fact is "Huru's card gains the Jaywan scheme." At press time it is **announced, not live** (Q4 2026 target). No new commercials, volumes tied to the card, or issuance numbers are disclosed.
- The traction figures quoted (AED 3B annualised salary flows, >24,000 employers, 9 exchange houses, ~1M Universal Accounts targeted "over the coming year") are **Huru platform metrics**, not Jaywan-card metrics. The PR anchors to flattering platform scale to dress up what is operationally a scheme certification.
- Why structured this way: a single-employer WPS payroll account is sticky but employer-bound. Huru's "Universal Account" (IBAN tied to the person, not the employer) is the real product thesis; the card is just the spend instrument. Putting that card on Jaywan lets Huru (a) lower domestic acceptance/interchange cost, (b) align with CBUAE's sovereign-rail push, and (c) signal "we are part of UAE infrastructure" to regulators and employers — a trust/distribution play more than a product leap.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Processor layer (Paymentology's peers in MENA/Jaywan):** Tribe Payments (opened a Dubai office in 2025), Pismo, Marqeta, Enfuce, and incumbents like Network International/Magnati on the acquiring side. Jaywan certification is becoming table-stakes for processors serving UAE issuers; being Jaywan-ready is a competitive checkbox, not a moat.
- **Scheme layer:** Jaywan vs Visa/Mastercard/UnionPay — Jaywan is **complementary, not a replacement**: it runs domestic transactions on the local rail and co-badges to a global network for cross-border. So Huru "moving to Jaywan" almost certainly means co-badge, not dropping Visa entirely (the 2025 launch note had Huru on Visa ATM cards).
- **Neobank/payroll peers in UAE:** mainstream WPS payroll + card players (Edenred/C3Pay, the dominant payroll-card incumbent for blue-collar workers), plus bank neobanks (Mashreq NEO, Wio, Liv). Huru's position: catching-up challenger targeting the unbanked/underbanked salary-receiver segment — the same base Edenred/C3Pay has owned for years. Jaywan gives Huru cost/sovereignty parity, not differentiation.
- Why the landscape is this way: the UAE deliberately built Jaywan (national scheme, like India's RuPay with which it is technically linked) to repatriate interchange and transaction data from Visa/Mastercard. Every domestic issuer is being pulled onto it; adopting it is following the regulator's current, not a bold bet.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Huru:** CBUAE-licensed, founded by Badr Al Ghurair, launched 2025 ([[Huru launches in UAE for the unbanked]]) as a zero-balance IBAN + Visa ATM card for the unbanked; added Quick Cash microfinance with Mawarid ([[Huru launches Quick Cash microfinance with Mawarid Finance]]); 2026-10 struck parallel deals with Paymentology (cards) and Bitpanda Enterprise (crypto) ([[Huru partners Paymentology and BitPanda Enterprise]]). CEO Abhimanyu Girotra. Trajectory = steady feature-stacking on a WPS-payroll base; the Jaywan card is the next brick.
- **Paymentology:** global issuer-processor; raised **$175M** in 2026-05 co-led by Apis + Aspirity, rebranded its platform "Lume" ([[Paymentology raises $175 million for issuer processing]]). Heavy MENA push (Saudi scaling, Riyadh Oracle/AWS integrations). Jaywan support is a logical extension: a processor's growth = number of schemes/geographies it can certify into, so adding Jaywan readiness is pure footprint expansion.
- Fit: logical for both. Huru needs sovereign-rail alignment to deepen its regulator/employer relationships; Paymentology monetizes by riding each client onto each new scheme. Low-risk, incremental.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** The partnership pre-exists; Jaywan itself launched July 2026 and is being adopted across UAE issuers (Emirates/Etihad flight bookings, malls, e-commerce, UnionPay interop). Huru joining is one more issuer onboarding to a scheme, not a first.
- **Traction of THIS item: announced only** — Q4 2026 target, no live card, no card-level numbers. The impressive figures belong to Huru's payroll platform, not the Jaywan card.
- **Where the margin sits (analysis):** on the domestic rail, interchange compresses (that's the point of a sovereign scheme — lower cost floor), so the card is less a revenue engine and more a retention/engagement surface. Huru's real value capture is the **Universal Account's stickiness** (person-bound IBAN survives job changes) + cross-sell (remittance via exchange houses, savings, Bitpanda crypto, Mawarid credit). The card on Jaywan reinforces that flywheel cheaply; it does not itself create a new margin pool.
- Second order: for Paymentology the durable value is being the processor of record across every scheme a fast-growing issuer adopts — recurring processing fees scale with Huru's account growth regardless of which scheme wins.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Watch for the Q4 2026 actual go-live and any disclosed issuance/active-card numbers; the ~1M Universal Account target is the real KPI, the Jaywan card is an enabler.
- Regulatory tailwind: CBUAE is mandating/encouraging Jaywan adoption for financial sovereignty and cost reduction; Huru aligning early is low-risk reputationally.
- Risks: Jaywan domestic acceptance breadth is still ramping (acceptance network only recently broadening), so near-term cardholder utility may lag Visa; interchange compression could pressure any card-economics Huru hoped for; and the underbanked payroll segment is a thin-margin, high-competition space (Edenred/C3Pay entrenched). Sentiment: positive-but-minor — a sensible infrastructure alignment, not a needle-mover.

No credible UAE card-TAM figure is publicly verifiable → **no data** on market size; don't invent one. Structure: domestic schemes are a top-down, policy-driven, winner-takes-most layer (one sovereign scheme per country — RuPay/India, mada/Saudi, Elo/Brazil, UnionPay/China cited as the template), while the issuer-processing layer sits below it and is fragmented/competitive. Barriers: regulation (CBUAE licence — both Huru and the scheme are CBUAE-governed) and the capital/scale to run certified cloud processing. Why now: GCC payment-sovereignty push — governments displacing foreign rails to retain interchange; Jaywan already co-badging with Visa/Mastercard (2025 MoUs) and going cross-border via a UnionPay MoU (11 Aug 2026).

Processor side — basis of competition is product (cloud-native, speed-to-market), geographic reach, and scheme certifications. **Risk flags.**
1. **Scheme is not an exclusive moat / processor commoditization.** Jaywan is open to many processors (checkout.com, Network International, Telr all present); being "on Jaywan" is parity, not advantage. Second-order: as the sovereign scheme captures the rails, differentiation shifts to price and speed at the processing layer, compressing processor economics.
2. **Single-client / single-market concentration in this event.** The news is one client (Huru) in one market (UAE); the "~1m accounts" is a *target*, not booked — execution and adoption risk sit with Huru, and the PR is silent on per-transaction economics, fraud liability and who captures interchange.
3. **Disintermediation by the sovereign scheme itself.** Domestic schemes are explicitly built to retain value onshore and reduce dependence on foreign rails; the same logic that squeezes Visa/Mastercard could, over time, push AEP toward bringing more of the stack in-house, pressuring the processor's long-run position (hypothesis).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Confirms the GCC domestic-scheme theme is moving from issuance to *live app-based card programmes* — the processor layer is quietly re-rating as the picks-and-shovels of payment sovereignty (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Jaywan account/card adoption scales, incumbent processors with existing integrations (Paymentology at Huru) win disproportionate share. **Trigger to watch:** a "live, with volumes" update on the Huru Jaywan card (Q4 2026) and whether Paymentology discloses Jaywan transaction counts — absent hard volumes, treat as announced-not-adopted. **What breaks it:** AEP or banks standardizing on a rival processor, or Huru missing the ~1m-account target, would undercut the re-rating.
