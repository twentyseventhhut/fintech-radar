# This Week in Fin: card share of cashless payments falls to 62%

In 2025, chip and PIN, swipe, and mail and telephone order payments fell to 44% of global card spending, from 49% in 2024. Card spending as a whole still grew by 4%, to US$ 47.3 trillion, but that growth came from payments made online and via contactless. The latest Global Cards and Payments study from Datos Insights shows how the shift is playing out across regions and payment types.
 Almost Every Terminal in the Americas Accepts Contactless, Yet Only 38% of Purchases are made this way 
Contactless card spending grew strongly in 2025, and contactless took a larger share of card purchases worldwide than in 2024. Growing digital wallet adoption and expanding acceptance on public transport drove that growth.
Europe remains far ahead, with contactless accounting for the large majority of card purchases. The Americas tell a different story as while contactless payments are accepted at almost every terminal in the region, only 38% of card purchases there are contactless. Lower contactless card penetration in the USA accounts for much of this.
 Online Marketplaces Push E-Commerce to Almost a Third of Card Spending 
Online card spending grew strongly again in 2025, driven by the expansion of online marketplaces, and e-commerce took a larger share of card volume than in 2024. A larger share of credit card spending happens online than of debit or prepaid card spending. Online penetration is highest in the Americas and lowest in Asia-Pacific. In several markets, account-based payment methods continue to hold back online card spending.
 Fewer Trips Between the USA and Canada and Tariffs Slowed Cross-Border Growth in the Americas 
Cross-border card spending grew strongly in 2025, and Europe has by far the highest cross-border penetration of any region.
Growth slowed in the Americas, however, as travel between the USA and Canada fell and tariffs curbed business spending in the USA. Globally, cross-border volume is forecast to grow faster than card spending as a whole through to 2031.
 QR Codes, softPOS, and mPOS Are Holding Back Traditional Terminals 
Nine in ten EFTPOS terminals worldwide accept contactless payments, but the traditional terminal faces growing competition. QR payments, softPOS, and mPOS devices saw strong uptake in every region in 2025, particularly among small businesses, and this has held back the expansion of traditional terminals. Brazil had the highest terminal density of any market, as more small businesses entered the formal economy and began accepting cards. Even there, terminal growth continues to be held back by the rapid expansion of Pix, the country’s account-based mobile payment system.
 Cards Make Most Cashless Payments but Carry a Small Share of the Money 
Payment cards accounted for most of the cashless payments made worldwide in 2025, but only a small share of cashless volume, because they are used for everyday, low-value purchases. Credit transfers, mostly used for high-value business and salary payments, accounted for the vast majority of volume. That balance is moving away from cards. Their share of cashless transactions fell from 71% in 2021 to 62% in 2025, as instant payment systems competed with cards at the checkout in every region.
 Account-to-Account Payments Are the Main Brake on Future Card Growth 
Card spending is forecast to reach US$ 60.9 trillion by 2031, with e-commerce and contactless expected to take a growing share of card volume.
Account-to-account payment systems are expected to be the main brake on that growth, and they compete with cards in the same places that card growth comes from. At the checkout the competition is instant payments, and online it is systems such as UPI in India, iDEAL in the Netherlands, and BLIK in Poland. How well cards hold their position online and at the contactless terminal will decide how fast card usage grows from here.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What the number says.** Datos Insights' Global Cards and Payments study reports that payment cards' share of cashless *transactions* worldwide fell from 71% in 2021 to 62% in 2025. The qualifier matters: cards still make *most* cashless payments by count, because they dominate everyday low-value purchases, but they carry only a small share of cashless *value* — credit transfers (high-value business and salary payments) account for the vast majority of value. So this is a count metric, not a dollar metric. Card spending itself still grew ~4% in 2025 to US$47.3 trillion and is forecast to reach US$60.9 trillion by 2031; the share erosion is relative, driven by faster-growing instant/account-to-account (A2A) rails rather than any absolute card decline.

**Why the shift is structural, not cyclical.** Datos names A2A as the single biggest brake on future card growth because A2A competes with cards in exactly the places where card growth comes from: at the physical checkout (instant payments) and online (UPI in India, iDEAL in the Netherlands, BLIK in Poland, Pix in Brazil). These rails bypass the four-party card network entirely — consumer bank account to merchant bank account via QR/phone identifier — eliminating interchange and network fees. That is a different competitive dynamic from cash displacement; it is one cashless rail taking share from another.

**Regional evidence lines up with prior notes.** In India, [[News/2025-10/UPI accounts for 84% of India's digital payments in FY25|UPI reached 84% of retail digital payment volume in FY25]] (185.9B transactions, +41.7% YoY), and credit cards' share of India's digital payments has fallen sharply over the decade. In Brazil, Pix growth is actively holding back terminal and card expansion even in the world's densest terminal market; regulators keep widening Pix's reach, e.g. [[News/2026-10/Brazil removes fixed cap on contactless Pix payments|Brazil removed the ~$98 contactless Pix cap on 1 Oct 2026]], pushing Pix further into the in-person, card-sized ticket range. External data (EBANX/fxcintel) shows Pix at ~42% of Brazilian e-commerce and ~34% of POS value in 2025, with Pix projected to overtake cards by 2028. Instant-payment roll-outs are now near-ubiquitous across the markets we track (Bre-B in Colombia, SEPA Instant in the euro zone, FedNow in the US, PI-SPI in Senegal).

**The networks are not standing still.** Rather than cede A2A, Visa and Mastercard are selling the *missing layer* around instant rails — dispute/liability frameworks, fraud scoring, and cross-border connectivity — precisely where raw A2A is weakest. Mastercard's Open Finance underpins A2A checkout plays in our notes: [[News/2026-10/iSX Financial partners Mastercard to scale account-to-account payments|iSX/PaidBy–Mastercard on cross-border A2A]] and [[News/2025-10/vobapay integrates Mastercard Open Finance for A2A payments|vobapay integrating Mastercard Open Finance for A2A at checkout in Germany]]. So the networks monetize the shift as toll-collectors on A2A even as their core card volume share slips.

**Other Datos data points worth holding.** Traditional in-person payments (chip & PIN, swipe, MOTO) fell to 44% of card spending in 2025 from 49% in 2024; growth is online + contactless. Contactless is far ahead in Europe but only ~38% of card purchases in the Americas (US drag). E-commerce is ~a third of card spend. QR/softPOS/mPOS are eating into traditional terminals, especially for small merchants.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Read the metric carefully — it is share of transaction *count*, not value or spend.** The headline "card share falls to 62%" is easy to misread as cards losing money or volume. They are not: Datos says card spending grew ~4% to US$47.3T in 2025 and is forecast up to US$60.9T by 2031. Cards *gaining* dollars while *losing* count-share is the whole point — A2A/credit transfers are growing the cashless denominator faster. Don't frame this as card decline.

**Single-source stat.** The 71%→62% figure traces to one vendor study (Datos Insights' Global Cards and Payments). The blog is promotional ("contact us to dive deeper"), and the underlying methodology (country coverage, how "cashless transaction" is defined, whether wallet-on-card is counted as card) is not in the free text. Adjacent industry reports use different denominators and reach different splits — e.g. Worldpay/Global Payments' Global Payments Report 2026 pegs A2A at only ~7% of global e-commerce and ~4% of POS *value* in 2025, with digital wallets (often card-funded) at ~56% of online spend. The two studies are not directly comparable, and a casual reader could wrongly treat "62% card share" and "7% A2A share" as contradictory. They measure different things (count vs value, all-cashless vs e-com).

**Wallet attribution muddies "card vs A2A."** A large share of "digital wallet" spend is card-funded (Apple Pay/Google Pay tokenizing a card). Whether such payments count as "card" or not changes the 62% materially. Instant/A2A displacement is real, but some headline "non-card" growth is actually cards wearing a wallet.

**The Visa/Mastercard "existential threat" narrative is overstated for now.** Count-share erosion of ~1.8pp/yr is slow; the networks still clear tens of trillions, keep ~double-digit volume growth, and are inserting themselves into A2A via Open Finance, fraud, and cross-border overlays (iSX/PaidBy, vobapay). A2A's trillions are concentrated in B2B/payroll/treasury, not the consumer checkout where card economics live. The real pressure is market-specific (India, Brazil) where domestic rails + regulation deliberately disintermediate the networks — not a uniform global collapse.

**Forecasts are the vendor's.** US$60.9T by 2031 and "A2A is the main brake" are Datos projections; treat as directional, not fact.

Importance: 3/5 — a credible, widely-cited data point that crystallizes a genuine structural A2A-vs-card shift already visible across our Pix/UPI/instant-payments coverage, and directly relevant to Visa/Mastercard theses. Docked for being single-vendor, a count (not value) metric prone to misreading, and a slow-moving multi-year trend rather than breaking news.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a global cashless-payments mix story, not a single company. Per the Datos Insights study in the note, cards' share of cashless *transactions* fell from 71% (2021) to 62% (2025), even as absolute card spend grew +4% to $47.3tn and is forecast to reach $60.9tn by 2031. The shift is structural: cards dominate *count* (everyday low-value purchases) but carry a small share of *value*, while credit transfers / A2A move the bulk of money. Corroborating secondary data: instant payments are projected to reach ~22% of non-cash transaction volume by 2028, and A2A transaction value is forecast to rise from $1.7tn (2024) to $5.7tn (2029), +230% (per analyst estimates via Juniper/digitalbankexpert, 2025). **Why now:** the inflection is driven by government-backed real-time rails hitting consumer scale simultaneously across regions — a regulation-plus-tech shift rather than a cyclical one. Second-order effect: value is migrating from the card interchange stack toward bank-owned A2A rails, where no network sits in the middle to take a fee.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for rails — transaction count & value, share of e-commerce/POS, merchant cost per transaction; for networks — payments volume, cross-border volume, take/yield. Key players split into (a) card networks (Visa, Mastercard, incumbents) and (b) sovereign/bank A2A rails (Pix, UPI, FedNow, SEPA Instant, iDEAL, BLIK). Basis of competition is increasingly *cost + ubiquity at checkout*, not product. Recent moves with dates:
- **Brazil / Pix:** 63.4bn transactions / ~R$26.4tn in 2024, ~80% above combined card volume; became the #1 online checkout method in 2025 at 42% of e-commerce value vs cards 41%; forecast 50% of transactions by 2028 vs cards 36% (per EBANX, 2025-2026). Merchant cost ~0.33% vs ~2.34% for credit cards — the core displacement driver.
- **India / UPI:** P2M transactions +241% Jun-2022→Jun-2024 (2.56bn→8.72bn); debit-card use fell up to 34% YoY in H2-2023. Notably UPI has *sparked* credit-card growth (RuPay credit-on-UPI), so the effect is debit-displacement, not blanket card displacement (per EBANX, 2026).
- **US / FedNow:** Q1-2026 processed 2.73m transactions / $271.3bn, +108% YoY, but avg ticket ~$99k — still commercial/high-value, not yet consumer checkout.
- **EU / SEPA Instant:** ~500m monthly transactions, ~16% of credit transfers; now moving to mandatory under EU instant-payments regulation.
Protagonist positioning: Visa/Mastercard are *ahead on monetization but structurally on defense* (analysis). Both are pursuing the same hedge — "be a rail, not just a casualty": Visa launched Visa A2A (UK, with NatWest/Nationwide, 2025 target) and Visa Protect for A2A fraud. Moat = network effects + 5bn cards / 175m merchants + value-added services (Mastercard VAS +22% FY2025), which partly insulates revenue even as pure interchange is threatened.

**Comps & multiples.** Network scale (public filings, FY2025): Visa ~$36bn revenue / ~$19bn net income, 257.5bn transactions (+10% YoY), $16.7tn total volume; Mastercard $28.2bn revenue, cross-border +14%. Growth stayed strong into FY2026 (Visa F1Q26 revenue +17% to $11.2bn; Mastercard +16%) — i.e. *no visible dent yet* despite the mix shift, because card value keeps compounding. Trading multiples not computed: EV/EBITDA and P/E require a clean free market-cap/EV snapshot not verifiable here → **no data / [UNSOURCED]**. Internal comps (corpus):
- [[Pix adds dispute button against fraud in Brazil]] — Pix feature/scale evidence
- [[Banco do Brasil enables Pix transfers via WhatsApp photo]] — Pix distribution expansion
- [[Klavi, Trio and WePayOut approved as Brazil ITPs]] — open-finance initiated A2A payments building on Pix
- [[Colombia's TumiPay partners with Kamin to integrate Bre-B]] — next sovereign A2A rail (Colombia) following the Pix template
- [[ECB and Swiss National Bank explore linking instant payments]] — cross-border A2A interlinking
- [[Peru's OKTO launches interoperable QR account-to-account payments]] — regional A2A spread

**Risk flags.**
1. **Interchange disintermediation (5-10yr).** Sovereign A2A rails bypass the card network entirely; analysts estimate A2A could offset 15-25% of future card volume. The "be a rail" hedge (Visa A2A) earns far thinner connector fees than interchange — the open question is whether rail revenue can offset cannibalized interchange.
2. **Regional concentration / policy risk.** Displacement is sharpest where a government rail is mandated or free (Pix, UPI, now SEPA Instant mandatory). Regulatory tailwind for A2A doubles as a direct headwind for interchange — and is outside any network's control.
3. **Emerging agentic-commerce / stablecoin bypass.** AI checkout agents optimized for cost could route around 2-3% interchange via A2A or stablecoins (per Citrini Research, Feb-2026) — a tail risk that compounds the A2A threat at the online checkout, exactly where card growth is supposed to come from.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** The 71%→62% slide is a leading indicator, not the event itself: cards are losing *share of count* at the checkout faster than they're losing *spend*, so network revenue looks healthy right up until A2A reaches value parity online (Brazil already there). Falsifiable thesis (analysis): if A2A online e-commerce share crosses card share in a second large market (India P2M value, or a EU economy post-mandate) within ~2 years, expect network take-rate pressure and a re-rating from "toll-booth" to "contested rail." What breaks the thesis: credit-on-rails (RuPay-on-UPI style) keeps cards riding *on top* of A2A infrastructure, preserving network economics. Trigger to watch: next Datos/EBANX annual update + whether Visa A2A connector revenue is disclosed.
