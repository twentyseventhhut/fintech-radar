13 October 2026 – Tether , the largest company in the digital asset industry, has announced an investment in EFY Finance as part of EFY Finance’s seed round to accelerate institutional use cases for USD₮ and USA₮. The investment aims to strengthen the infrastructure connecting stablecoins to the traditional financial system across Latin America.
EFY Finance is a financial infrastructure company providing stablecoin-based cross-border payments, accounts, and digital asset custody across Latin America. It offers on- and off-ramp infrastructure for USD₮ and USA₮, OTC services, wallets, and cards across more than 12 currencies and corridors, with settlement to local accounts in 17 countries. The company is licensed as a Digital Asset Service Provider in El Salvador and operates regulated services for more than 25,000 active individual and business accounts. EFY Finance believes money should move at the speed of the internet: as simple, fast, and clear as sending a message, without borders.
Tether’s investment reflects its commitment to supporting the growth of regulated infrastructure that connects stablecoins with banks, businesses, and institutional users. EFY Finance’s strong distribution, established customer base, and licensed infrastructure position the company to drive the next stage of institutional stablecoin use cases, particularly in Bolivia, Colombia, Mexico, El Salvador, Panama, and Ecuador, where it already has a meaningful presence.
“Across Latin America, the US dollar is a lifeline against inflation and capital controls. USD₮ and USA₮ are how people access it, but doing so simply and reliably takes regulated infrastructure, banking connections, and real distribution. That is the gap EFY Finance is positioned to close, bringing the dollar within easier reach across the region,” said Paolo Ardoino, CEO of Tether .
“Stablecoins have already become part of the financial infrastructure of Latin America. The challenge now is making them work seamlessly with the financial systems businesses use every day. That means connecting digital dollars with bank accounts, local payment networks, cards, and cross-border settlement. That is the infrastructure we are building at EFY Finance. Having Tether as a strategic investor gives us an exceptional collaborator to accelerate that vision and expand institutional access to USD₮ and USA₮ across the region,” said Andrés Tobón, Co-Founder and CEO of EFY Finance .
Tether’s investment in EFY Finance reflects a broader shift already underway across Latin America. According to Chainalysis , the region recorded nearly $1.5 trillion in cryptocurrency transaction volume between July 2022 and June 2025, making it one of the most dynamic regions. Persistent inflation, currency volatility, and restrictive capital controls in several countries continue to drive demand for stablecoins as a stable store of value and a hedge against local macroeconomic risk. Stablecoin purchases now account for over half of all exchange purchases. As regulatory frameworks mature and licensed infrastructure strengthens, Latin America is proving what’s possible when stablecoins meet real financial infrastructure. Tether and EFY Finance are excited to be part of this evolution. About USA₮ 
USA₮ is a U.S.-dollar-backed stablecoin issued by Anchorage Digital Bank N.A., a federally chartered bank, that Tether, the global leader in stablecoin technology, has supported to launch. Purpose-built to serve the U.S. market and designed to comply with U.S. regulatory standards, USA₮ will be the foundational rail for the next generation of American commerce, trade, and finance.
USA₮ underscores Tether’s commitment to driving dominance and leadership in the evolving digital asset economy. As part of the broader Tether ecosystem, USA₮ will set a new benchmark in the U.S. for utility-driven stablecoins designed to deliver long-term value, strong governance, and real-world applications.
 Important Note: 
USA₮ is not legal tender (as described in section 5103 of title 31, United States Code) and will not be issued, backed, approved, or guaranteed by the U.S. government. USA₮ is not subject to the insurance protections of the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC), the Securities Investor Protection Corporation (SIPC), or any other government agency.
The issuer of USA₮ is Anchorage Digital Bank, N.A.

# Context-enrichment: Tether invests in EFY Finance for LATAM stablecoins
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Tether took an **undisclosed** minority stake in EFY Finance as part of EFY's **seed round** (announced 13 Oct 2026). No ticket size, no valuation, no round total, no board seat, no lead-vs-participant detail disclosed — the only hard numbers are EFY's own operating metrics (25,000+ active accounts, settlement to local accounts in 17 countries, 12+ currencies/corridors, a Digital Asset Service Provider licence from El Salvador's CNAD). The Block confirms the amount is undisclosed.
- **De-PR'd:** this is a *seed* cheque into a small infra startup, not a flagship deal. "Institutional stablecoin usage" and "connecting stablecoins to traditional finance" is the standard Tether press framing it uses for essentially every LatAm/EM investment. "25,000 active accounts" is a tiny base for a payments-infra claim; no GMV/volume disclosed — a notable silence for a "settlement" company.
- **Why structured this way:** Tether deploys its profits (self-reported >$10B/yr) into a *portfolio* of small regional on/off-ramp and distribution players rather than one big LatAm build. EFY's real asset is its **El Salvador DASP licence** + distribution, not technology — Tether is buying regulated last-mile reach for USD₮ and its newer USA₮ (Anchorage-issued) rather than a novel product. The PR leans on the Chainalysis macro number ($1.5T LatAm crypto volume Jul'22–Jun'25) precisely because the deal-specific numbers are thin.

## [1] Competitors / peers
LatAm stablecoin on/off-ramp + cross-border infra is crowded and further along than EFY:
- **Bitso** — processed ~$6.5B of US–Mexico crypto remittances in 2024 (~10% of the corridor); the regional incumbent.
- **Conduit** — hit ~$10B annualized cross-border volume (16x YoY), LatAm its primary market.
- **Stripe/Bridge, Mural Pay, VelaFi, Inswitch** — API-level infra; **dLocal, Félix Pago, Alfred, RedotPay** at last-mile payout.
- Tether's *own* LatAm portfolio companies overlap heavily: **[[Argentina's Belo raises $14 million Series A led by Tether]]** (3M+ users, same "hold local + digital dollars, cross-border" pitch), **[[Tether invests in Brazilian fintech Parfin]]**, **[[Tether makes strategic investment in Chilean exchange Orionx]]**, **[[Tether-backed Oobit launches in Colombia amid crypto adoption surge]]**.
- **Position:** EFY is a *catching-up* niche player (El Salvador-centric, ~25k accounts) vs Bitso/Conduit at billions in volume. Its edge is regulatory (CNAD DASP) + a few corridors, not scale.
- **Why the landscape is this way (analysis):** the moat in LatAm stablecoins has shifted from infra to **distribution + local banking rails + licences** (the emergingfintech thesis). EFY's value to Tether is exactly that licence/corridor coverage — which is also why it's commoditizable: many players hold similar on/off-ramp capabilities.

## [2] Company history / fit
EFY Finance (CEO/co-founder **Andrés Tobón**) is a Latin-America-focused digital-asset neobank registered in El Salvador, authorised by the **CNAD** as a DASP: wallet (USD₮/BTC/ETH), exchange, a Visa card, corporate international payments, on/off-ramps, USD sub-accounts, OTC and custody. Strongest presence cited in Bolivia, Colombia, Mexico, El Salvador, Panama, Ecuador.
- **Fit for Tether:** textbook. This is Tether's repeating EM playbook — buy regulated regional distribution for USD₮ and now USA₮. Direct analogues in the corpus: **[[Tether invests in Kenya's Kotani Pay]]** and **[[Tether invests in LemFi to expand USDT settlement in Africa and Asia]]** (Africa/Asia), Belo/Parfin/Orionx/Oobit (LatAm). Tether reportedly holds ~140 investments and channels >$10B annual profits into payments/AI/gold/energy ([[Tether plots global expansion as profits surge]]).
- **Why Tether acts this way:** as a stablecoin issuer its growth = USD₮ circulation. Buying small on/off-ramp distributors in dollarization-hungry, capital-controlled markets is the cheapest way to defend/extend circulation and seed USA₮ distribution ahead of US competition (Circle/USDC). The cheques are small and numerous — a spray-to-defend-the-network strategy, not concentrated bets.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** Nothing mechanically new — on/off-ramp + cards + cross-border settlement in USD₮ already exists at larger peers and inside Tether's own portfolio. The only genuinely fresh angle is EFY as a **USA₮** (Anchorage Digital Bank-issued) distribution point, tying Tether's new US-compliant stablecoin into LatAm corridors.
- **Traction: thin / unverified.** 25,000 active accounts and "17 countries settlement" are the only metrics; **no volume, GMV, or revenue** disclosed — the standard red flag for an infra PR. "Licensed/regulated" ≠ "at scale".
- **Who captures the margin (analysis):** in this stack the issuer (Tether) captures reserve yield on float; the ramp/distributor (EFY) captures thin transaction/FX spread in a commoditizing segment where dLocal/Bitso/Conduit compete on price. Tether's investment effectively subsidizes distribution that drives *its* float — the value accrues more to Tether than to EFY's standalone economics. That's why it's a seed-stage minority stake, not an acquisition.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Plans:** scale institutional USD₮/USA₮ use across Bolivia, Colombia, Mexico, El Salvador, Panama, Ecuador; deepen bank/card/local-rail integration.
- **Backdrop:** LatAm remains the hottest stablecoin region (Chainalysis ~$1.5T volume; stablecoins >50% of exchange purchases) driven by inflation/capital controls; the ~$112B LatAm remittance opportunity ([[This Week in Fintech the $112 billion LATAM remittance opportunity]]) is the prize.
- **Risks / second-order (analysis):** (1) Tether's EM portfolio companies increasingly **compete with each other** (Belo vs EFY vs Oobit on overlapping corridors) — portfolio cannibalization, not a coordinated moat. (2) Regulatory: Tether has been squeezed out of Europe under MiCA ([[Tether abandons Europe as MiCA ban wipes USDT from exchanges]]); LatAm/EM regulatory tightening (e.g. Brazil banning crypto cross-border settlement) is a tail risk to this distribution strategy. (3) USA₮ is new and unproven; EFY distribution is a bet, not adoption. **Sentiment: incrementally positive but low-signal** — one more small cheque in a long, repetitive spree.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** LatAm is the fastest-growing real-world stablecoin region: ~$324bn in stablecoin transaction volume in 2025, up ~89% YoY (per stablecoininsider.org / Fireblocks data, via web, as of 2026); the note's own PR cites Chainalysis at ~$1.5tn total crypto volume Jul-2022→Jun-2025 and "stablecoin purchases over half of all exchange purchases." USDT holds ~59% of the ~$300-316bn global stablecoin supply vs USDC ~24% (per stablecoininsider.org, via web, mid-2026). Structure is fragmenting fast on the infra/distribution layer — "15+ companies competing for the same enterprise clients" (per muralpay / emergingfintech.co, via web): Bitso, Circle, Stripe/Bridge, Conduit, Mural, Ripio, and now EFY. Drivers / "why now": (1) demand side — 100%+ inflation and capital controls make USD₮ a dollar-access rail, not a trading chip (in Argentina USD tokens are >70% of exchange purchases, per web); (2) supply side — the GENIUS Act and maturing local regimes (El Salvador's CNAD/DASP framework) legitimize licensed on/off-ramps, so the bottleneck moves from "is it legal" to "who owns bank connections + local settlement" — exactly EFY's claim (settlement in 17 countries, 12+ currencies, 25k accounts, El Salvador DASP license).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: settlement corridors/countries, on-off-ramp FX spread/take rate, local-rail coverage (Pix/SPEI), licensed-entity count, active accounts/TPV. Basis of competition has shifted from infrastructure to **distribution + banking connections + licensing** (per emergingfintech.co, via web). Key players & recent moves: Bitso (SPEI off-ramp at scale, MXNB stablecoin, El Salvador approval — see in-base comp); Conduit ($36M raise May-2025, Circle Payments Network member); Circle/USDC (owns transparency-sensitive institutional flows via monthly Grant Thornton attestation); Stripe-owned Bridge and Mastercard-owned BVNK on the global side. EFY's position: **niche challenger** — small (25k accounts; seed stage) but with a real moat claim: a licensed El Salvador DASP + already-live settlement in 17 countries and a USD₮/USA₮ preference baked in via its Tether tie. Moat type = regulatory/licensing + distribution, not scale (analysis). Tether as strategic backer is the distribution flywheel: Tether's reserve profits (~$10bn net 2025, ~$1.04bn Q1-2026, ~$8.23bn excess reserve buffer — see in-base [[Tether posts $1.04 billion Q1 profit and $8.23 billion reserve buffer]]) fund equity stakes in ramp/wallet players that then push USD₮ volume, feeding reserves again.

**Comps & multiples.** EFY's round is a **seed; amount undisclosed, no valuation disclosed → multiples = no data**. This is a strategic stake, not a priced growth round, so EV/Revenue etc. [UNSOURCED]. Internal comps (Tether's LatAm/EM ramp-&-wallet stake pattern):

Pattern: Tether writes small, undisclosed-to-single-digit-$M checks into licensed local ramps/wallets rather than one big consolidation bet — distribution land-grab, not M&A. Distribution not computed (no priced figures); qualitative: EFY is the smallest-disclosed-footprint of the set by accounts.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Rails-dependence / captive economics.** EFY is being wired to push USD₮/USA₮ specifically; its unit economics depend on Tether's token and reserve-yield flywheel, not a neutral multi-stablecoin stack. If USDC/local stablecoins win institutional corridors (where reserve transparency matters), EFY's USD₮-tilt is a liability, not an edge.
2. **USD₮ transparency / regulatory overhang.** Tether publishes attestations, not full audits; the note itself references expanding U.S. Treasury scrutiny (2,900 investigations, 1,600 U.S.-law-enforcement) and USA₮ carries explicit "not FDIC/SIPC-insured, not legal tender" caveats. Institutional adoption — EFY's whole pitch — is the segment most sensitive to this gap vs Circle's audited USDC.
3. **Concentration + fragmentation squeeze.** 25k accounts across 17 countries is thin; EFY sits in a crowded 15+-player ramp market where the moat is now distribution/banking relationships that larger, better-capitalized peers (Bitso, Bridge/Stripe, BVNK/Mastercard) also hold. Seed-stage + single strategic backer = execution/funding concentration risk.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Not a re-rating — it's Tether extending its *equity-backed distribution flywheel* from remittances (LemFi, Kotani) into LatAm *institutional* settlement, defending USD₮ share before GENIUS-era USDC pushes into enterprise corridors (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if EFY's institutional volume and country count grow while staying USD₮-first, Tether's "fund the ramps" strategy is working; it breaks if EFY has to go multi-stablecoin (adding USDC) to win banks — the signal that transparency, not distribution, is the real institutional gatekeeper. Watch: a priced follow-on round with a disclosed valuation, and whether EFY stays USD₮-exclusive.
