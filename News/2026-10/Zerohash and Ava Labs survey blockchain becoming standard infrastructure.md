About this Report 
The Onchain Readiness Report from zerohash and Ava Labs shows that institutional onchain adoption is further along than the market narrative suggests. Built on a survey of 162 operators and executives across banks, fintechs, payments companies, brokerages, asset managers, and crypto-native firms, the report replaces anecdote with data on what's working, what's holding progress back, and where investment is flowing. The question has shifted from whether blockchain infrastructure works to whether the organization around it is ready.
Adoption Is Real, but Uneven
More than a third of respondents (37.7%) already call blockchain infrastructure a standard part of their business, and 32.1% name onchain rails a core strategic priority. Payments companies (57.9%) and brokerages (45.8%) are setting the pace. Banks are the furthest from "already standard" today at 15.4%, yet 61.5% expect it within two years, the fastest expected catch-up of any segment.
Where the Investment Is Going
Stablecoin payments and settlement (37.7%) and RWA tokenization (25.3%) account for 63% of expected investment over the next 24 months. Priorities differ by vertical: Banks are betting on tokenization, while fintechs are betting on cross-border settlement. Looking ahead, 46.9% of respondents expect AI agents to routinely transact on behalf of customers within two years, rising to 63.6% among product leaders. The multi-chain picture is still taking shape, with 22.8% unsure how many networks they'll need to support.
Regulatory Clarity Is the Industry’s Biggest Lever 
Regulatory uncertainty is both the most-cited barrier to adoption (38.9%) and, in the form of regulatory clarity, the most-cited accelerant (68.5%). That makes it a rare issue that tops both sides of the ledger. Technical integration complexity weighs most on banks, payments companies, and large enterprises, while asset managers point to gaps in internal expertise. Even the most bullish respondents tied their outlook to regulatory conditions, not technical readiness.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
zerohash and Ava Labs published **The Onchain Readiness Report** (released 2026-10-08; this item is the 2026-10-14 digest pickup). It is a **vendor-sponsored survey of 162 operators/executives** across banks, fintechs, payments firms, brokerages, asset managers and crypto-native firms. External reporting (GlobeNewswire, 2026-10-08) says it was **fielded in Aug–Sep 2026 via a self-administered online questionnaire** — i.e. a convenience/opt-in sample, not a probability sample.

Headline claims: 37.7% already call blockchain "standard"; banks only 15.4% today but **61.5% expect it standard within two years**; investment concentrates in stablecoin payments (37.7%) and RWA tokenization (25.3%) = ~63% of expected 24-month spend; regulatory uncertainty is both the top barrier (38.9%) and, as "clarity," the top accelerant (68.5%).

**+ Why structured this way / what it reveals:** This is a classic **thesis-confirming vendor survey**. zerohash sells onchain settlement/stablecoin infrastructure; Ava Labs (Avalanche) sells the chain for institutional RWA/tokenization. The survey's two "winning" categories — stablecoin settlement and RWA tokenization — map *exactly* onto what the two sponsors sell. The "readiness paradox" framing (tech is ready, orgs/regulators aren't) is also self-serving: it relocates the blocker away from the product and onto regulators and the customer's own org, so the implied conclusion is "buy the rails now, you're just behind." Treat every number as directional sentiment from a self-selected, crypto-leaning respondent pool, not as market-share fact.

## [1] Competitors / peers (of the *survey format*)
Vendor-sponsored institutional-adoption surveys are a crowded genre in 2026, all telling the same "adoption is accelerating" story:
- **Coinbase × EY-Parthenon — 2026 Institutional Investor Digital Assets Survey** (fielded Jan 2026, ~351 investors): 73% plan to raise digital-asset allocations; 85% use/plan stablecoins. Larger N, named methodology partner (EY).
- **Fireblocks × SODA — Tokenization at Investment Banks 2026** (16 large sell-side banks): 9 in 10 expect repo/collateral tokenization to reshape liquidity, but **no bank calls its own infra "fully ready."**
- zerohash × Ava Labs (N=162, self-administered online): the **weakest methodology of the three** — mixed respondent types, opt-in online, small N once split by segment (banks are a sub-slice of 162, so segment figures like 15.4%/61.5% rest on small cells).

**+ Why the landscape is this way:** Surveys are cheap top-of-funnel marketing for infra vendors; the "data replaces anecdote" framing lends a neutral veneer. The consistent cross-vendor signal (institutions intend more digital-asset activity, led by stablecoins + tokenization) is probably *directionally real* precisely because independent-ish sources (EY-branded) echo it — but the specific percentages here are the least reliable of the set.

## [2] Company history / fit
**zerohash:** crypto/stablecoin infrastructure-as-a-service. Corpus trajectory: Mastercard pursued a ~$2bn acquisition (2025-10) → zerohash **walked away and stayed independent** ([[Zerohash in talks to raise $250M after dropping Mastercard deal]], [[Mastercard to acquire crypto firm Zerohash for ~$2 billion]]); subsequently pursued funding **above $1.5bn valuation** ([[Zerohash pursues funding above $1.5 billion valuation]]); got a **Dutch EMI licence** ([[Zerohash receives EMI license from Dutch central bank]]) and expanded in Amsterdam; applied for a **US national trust bank charter** ([[Zerohash applies for US National Trust Bank charter]]); named a **Tempo validator** days before this report ([[zerohash named a Tempo validator for stablecoin rails]]). Powers crypto for OnePay/Walmart, Gusto payroll pilots, Interactive Brokers USDC funding.
**Ava Labs / Avalanche:** positioning as *the institutional chain* — BlackRock's tokenized fund and rising RWA TVL on Avalanche; corpus has [[TIS launches multi-token platform on Avalanche]], [[Maalexi builds MAATEX on Avalanche to tokenize commodities]], [[BlackRock accelerates tokenization onto public blockchains]].

**+ Why they co-publish:** zerohash brings the institutional distribution/relationships + regulatory posture (EMI, trust-charter), Avalanche brings the chain-side RWA narrative. A co-branded survey is a low-cost way to *jointly* signal "serious, institutional, inevitable" during a fundraise/charter push — reputational tailwind, not a product event.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: low.** No product, deal, named customer count, or GMV — it is a marketing report. It fits the broader corpus thesis (see [[a16z: TradFi doesn't want DeFi, it wants blockchains]], [[Why JPMorgan built Kinexys for institutional blockchain settlements]], [[Anchorage launches tokenized deposit platform for banks]]) that institutions want *permissioned/controlled* blockchain rails. Traction cited is **intent, not adoption** — "expect standard within two years," "expect AI agents to transact" — exactly the "announced ≠ live" trap the skill warns against.

**+ Where value actually sits:** The only load-bearing, somewhat credible finding is the **regulatory-clarity lever** (barrier *and* accelerant), which is corroborated by the independent/EY-branded surveys and by the corpus (CLARITY Act discourse). That is useful signal. Everything framed as "already standard" is self-report from a crypto-tilted panel.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect continued drumbeat of such surveys as the stablecoin/RWA fundraising cycle runs hot. For zerohash specifically, the report is PR scaffolding around its **independent-growth / trust-charter / fundraising** arc. Watch whether the *intent* figures convert into named, live institutional deployments (the real metric). Regulatory clarity (US CLARITY/stablecoin rules, EU MiCA) remains the genuine swing factor across all these surveys.

**+ Second-order:** If regulatory clarity arrives, the *survey's own logic* predicts incumbents (JPMorgan Kinexys, Citi, Mastercard's own crypto program) move in force — which would **compress the independent infra vendors' window**, not widen it. So "blockchain becomes standard" is not unambiguously bullish for zerohash/Avalanche; standardization can mean the big banks internalize the rails.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Crypto-as-a-service / embedded stablecoin & settlement infra ("onchain rails behind the brand"). Market-size proxies, not a survey TAM: tokenized RWAs (ex-stablecoins) ~$19.3bn at end-Q1 2026, rising above $30bn by late May 2026 (per CoinGecko / investax, as of Q1–Q2 2026); stablecoin supply ~$319.9bn end-May 2026 (USDT ~$184.7bn, USDC ~$73.6bn, per stablecoinflows, as of May 2026). Structure: fragmented at the application edge, concentrating at the infra layer — a few licensed API providers (Zerohash, Fireblocks, Bridge/Stripe, BVNK, Circle) sit between banks/fintechs and chains; the moat is regulatory licensing + compliance, not tech. Why now: (1) regulatory clarity is the stated gating variable — the survey puts regulatory uncertainty as the top barrier (38.9%) AND regulatory clarity as the top accelerant (68.5%), the only factor topping both ledgers (per zerohash/Ava Labs report, 2026-10-08); (2) incumbent buy-vs-build has flipped to buy — Mastercard acquired BVNK for up to $1.8bn (closed 2026-08-03; ~$40m ARR, ~$30bn annualized volume at purchase). Second-order: value is migrating from chains to the licensed compliance/settlement layer that abstracts them.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: stablecoin payment volume (annualized), live institutional clients, licenses held (EMI / trust charter), API-integrated end-users. Players + basis of competition (licensing + distribution, not price): Fireblocks (scale leader — ~$300bn/mo stablecoin volume, per fireblocks/crossmint), Stripe/Bridge (distribution), Circle (issuer-infra), BVNK (now inside Mastercard), vs Zerohash (embedded CaaS, ~5m+ users across 190 countries via Stripe, DraftKings, Franklin Templeton, BlackRock BUIDL; ~$73m annualized revenue as of Sep-2025, single secondary source → treat as `[UNSOURCED]`). Recent moves: Zerohash EMI license from Dutch central bank (2026-05), US national trust bank charter application (2026-03), Selini Capital joined its liquidity ecosystem (2026-10-14). Position: niche-but-well-distributed embedded infra; stayed independent after walking away from Mastercard. Moat (analysis): switching costs (deep API integration) + regulatory licensing — durable but not unique; Fireblocks is ahead on raw volume.

**Comps & multiples.** Private; no public market cap. Internal comps (wikilinks):
- [[Mastercard to acquire crypto firm Zerohash for ~$2 billion]] — talks at ~$2bn (2025-10), deal dropped.
- [[Zerohash in talks to raise $250M after dropping Mastercard deal]] — $250m at $1.5bn (2026-01).
- [[Zerohash pursues funding above $1.5 billion valuation]] — raise above $1.5bn (2026-05).

Arithmetic (round valuation, NOT market cap): P/S ≈ $1.5bn / $73m revenue = ~20.5x — at the top of the EV/Rev 0.5–20x sanity range, and the revenue figure itself is a single secondary source. External peer anchor: Mastercard/BVNK = $1.8bn / ~$40m ARR = ~45x (strategic/control premium, not a trading comp). Zerohash flag: rich on P/S — justified ONLY if growth matches; growth rate not disclosed → cannot confirm, so richness is a watch-item, not a clean "overvalued." EV/EBITDA, P/E = no data (private, likely EBITDA-negative). Distribution not computed (fewer than 3 clean trading comps; all private rounds / strategic deals) — qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Vendor-sponsored-survey selection bias (primary).** The "162 operators" sample is recruited by zerohash + Ava Labs, both of whom sell exactly the stablecoin-payments + tokenization + Avalanche rails the survey finds "becoming standard." Respondents skew to firms already onchain (payments 57.9%, brokerages 45.8% "already standard"); the 61.5%-of-banks-within-2-years figure is *stated intent*, not live deployment. De-PR: "already standard" is self-reported, "expect within two years" is aspiration — treat all adoption percentages as directional sentiment, not audited penetration.
2. **Disintermediation / stack-layer capture.** Incumbents are buying the rails (Mastercard/BVNK). If card networks and banks in-house the compliance layer, embedded-CaaS providers risk being squeezed to a thin settlement utility — the same consolidation that removed BVNK as an independent comp.
3. **Regulatory dependence is two-sided.** The sector's own survey names regulatory clarity as both top blocker and top accelerant; a stall or adverse ruling directly caps the 24-month adoption curve the thesis rests on.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) The report reframes the sector from "whether blockchain works" to "whether the org is ready" — a re-rating narrative for licensed infra, with stablecoin payments (37.7%) + RWA tokenization (25.3%) as the ~63% investment magnet. Falsifiable thesis: if institutional stablecoin/RWA is truly moving pilot→production, Zerohash's next raise closes above $1.5bn with disclosed revenue growth validating the ~20x P/S; trigger to watch = terms of that round + whether its US trust bank charter is granted. What breaks it: raise slips, growth undisclosed, or regulators stall — then "standard within two years" stays a survey intention.
