## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd).** Per a single aggregator item (en.bloomingbit.io, relaying **Wu Blockchain, 2026-10-26**), MEXC — a top-tier global exchange (self-reported **~40M users across 170+ markets**; ranked **#2 by spot market share ~7.9%** per TokenInsight Q1-2026; futures >$20bn/day, top-3 derivatives) — confirmed it is **gradually ending services for users in the Netherlands**, with the **final shutdown set for 2026-11-16**, because it **does not hold a MiCA crypto-asset-service-provider (CASP) licence in the EU/EEA**. This is **not a new decision**: MEXC announced the phased Dutch wind-down on **2026-09-29** and has been steering clients out for weeks. The 10-26/10-27 item is a late **re-report of the deadline approaching**, not fresh news. Key de-PR point: "failed to secure MiCA authorization" overstates effort — MEXC **filed no EU CASP application** at all (per CASP trackers), so this is a chosen exit from a jurisdiction it was never licensed in, not a rejection.

**[1] The MiCA mechanism + AFM backdrop (dated).** MiCA's CASP regime reaches **full enforcement 2026-07-01** (end of the national-regime transition). The Dutch regulator **AFM issued a public warning against MEXC on 2025-09-16/-24**, stating it was providing crypto-asset services to Dutch consumers **without the required authorisation**. MEXC never obtained a CASP licence in any EU state, so post-transition it has **no passport** to serve EEA users — the Netherlands exit is the enforcement endpoint of that gap. Industry context: of >1,200 firms under prior national rules, only **~200–377 secured full CASP licences** by Oct-2026; leaders project **~80% of exchanges may exit** the EU market.

**[2] Prior art — other MiCA/EU-driven exits (dated).** This is **one data point in a wide, already-documented pattern**, not a standalone shock. **Binance** faced rejection of its **Greece MiCA application** (~2026-06) and began restricting EU services, pushing users to rivals — see [[Binance faces loss of EU access over Greece MiCA licence]] and [[Coinbase and OKX lure EU users as Binance restricts services]]. **KuCoin** got an Austrian FMA CASP licence (late-2025) but was barred from commencing operations. **HTX** is likewise restricted. Licensed stayers: **Coinbase, Kraken (CBI, Jun-2025), OKX, Bybit EU (Austrian FMA, ~May-2025), Crypto.com**. MEXC slots cleanly into the "unlicensed, exiting" bucket.

**[3] Novelty / overlap with existing vault coverage.** **Low novelty.** The Netherlands/MiCA exit is **already enriched and published** inside the sibling note [[Crypto exchange MEXC drops Russian and Belarusian clients]] (published 2026-10-21, importance 3), whose context [2] and red-team Q9 already lay out the 2026-09-29 phased wind-down, the **Kraken referral** (commercial partnership 2026-08-12; Kraken holds MiCA via CBI + MiFID via CySEC), the **Bybit EU** alternative, batch offboarding (move assets by ~10-31, auto-close futures, custodian transfer by **11-16**). This dedicated note adds only the single confirming data point that 11-16 is the final cutoff. Treat as a **duplicate** of already-covered ground.

**[4] What's next / read.** Mechanically: Dutch users must **self-migrate** (register separately with Kraken or Bybit EU — accounts do not transfer); residual positions after batch deadlines face processing/fees and remaining balances move to a **regulated custodian**. Falsifiable read (analysis): with the 2026-07 CASP gate now binding, expect **more unlicensed top-tier venues to formalise EEA exits** country-by-country through 2026-H2; MEXC's lack of any EU application signals it is **ceding the EEA entirely** rather than seeking a licensed entity. Breaks if MEXC unexpectedly files for / acquires an EU CASP vehicle. Financially immaterial at group level (Netherlands is a small slice of a ~40M-user book) — this is a **compliance-perimeter / optics** event, not a revenue event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** EU crypto-exchange *market access* under MiCA (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114) — not a sized revenue market here ("no data" on MEXC's Dutch/EU volume). The structural driver is a one-time regime consolidation: the EU-wide transitional period ended **1 July 2026**, after which serving EEA clients without a granted CASP authorisation is a breach of EU law (per Finance Magnates / Euronews). As of ~20 Oct 2026 there were **371–377 authorised CASPs across the 27 EEA home states** (per Outrun.at dashboard / stablecoinlaws), and reporting notes **>80% of EU crypto businesses had not obtained a licence** by the deadline (Euronews, Finance Magnates) — i.e. the market is mid-shakeout, not settled. MEXC's trigger is specific: the Dutch **AFM** issued a warning on **2026-09-24** (per crypto.news); MEXC confirmed it holds **no CASP licence** in the EU/EEA and will complete Dutch offboarding by **2026-11-16**, moving assets by ~Oct 31 and pointing users to **Bybit EU** (CASP authorised by Austria's FMA, May 2025, passported EEA-wide) (per Finance Magnates, crypto.news, Gate). Why now: MiCA converts a patchwork of national regimes into a **single passport** — one member-state licence unlocks ~450M consumers across 30 countries — so the binary becomes "get one licence" vs "exit every EEA market," and MEXC chose exit rather than licensing.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for an exchange: spot/derivatives volume, liquidity depth, listed-asset breadth, take rate (fees), and — decisively here — **regulatory coverage** (does the venue hold a passportable CASP licence). Basis of competition has shifted from price/breadth/liquidity toward *licensed access*: an unlicensed global venue is now legally locked out of the EEA regardless of its product edge. Players by posture: (a) **licensed / staying** — Coinbase (Luxembourg CSSF), Kraken (Ireland CBI), Crypto.com (Malta MFSA), Bybit (Austria FMA), plus OKX, Gate, KuCoin, Bitstamp, Gemini, Bitvavo (per spaziocrypto / casptracker CASP lists); (b) **exiting / excluded** — MEXC (Netherlands, Nov 2026) and, most prominently, **Binance, which lost EU access on 2026-07-01** (per Euronews/Finance Magnates). MEXC's position: a **departing incumbent** for the EEA — its exit *removes* supply and hands referral flow to a licensed peer (Bybit EU), so it loses share rather than winning it. Recent move de-PR'd: MEXC is pruning jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction — the Dutch exit runs in parallel with its Russia/Belarus offboarding (2026-10-20, see internal comps), confirming a company-wide compliance retreat, not a one-off.

**Comps & multiples.** MEXC is privately held and opaque — **no disclosed valuation / round / revenue / EU volume** → trading multiples = **no data** / `[UNSOURCED]`; this is a regulation/market-access event, not a deal, so EV/Revenue, P/S etc. are not computable. Qualitative internal comps (same MiCA / exchange-access theme): [[Crypto exchange MEXC drops Russian and Belarusian clients]] (sibling MEXC compliance exit, 2026-10-20) · [[Deutsche Bank prepares crypto custody launch in Germany]] (incumbent entering the licensed-EU lane) · [[TrueLayer extends Pay by Bank with SumUp across Europe]] (regulated EU fintech expansion, for passporting contrast). Distribution not computed — no comparable figures.

**Risk flags.**
- **Client-migration / self-custody friction.** The Bybit EU referral is a *recommendation*, not an account/asset transfer — users must independently register, re-KYC and move holdings before 2026-11-16 (per Finance Magnates). Second-order: stranded-balance and forced-liquidation risk for anyone who misses the window, plus leakage to non-compliant/P2P venues instead of the licensed destination.
- **Contagion of unlicensed-venue exits.** With >80% of EU crypto firms unlicensed at the July-2026 gate (Euronews), MEXC is one of a wave (Binance already out) — a thinning of global liquidity available to EEA retail, concentrating flow on the ~370 licensed CASPs and raising the regulatory premium those venues can charge.
- **Enforcement / reputational tail.** The AFM warning (2026-09-24) means the exit is regulator-pushed, not voluntary strategy; any residual EEA servicing post-deadline is a live legal breach, and MEXC's willingness to abandon a market rather than license signals it may deprioritise EU compliance elsewhere — a counterparty-risk read for EU users still holding balances.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** MiCA is doing what it was designed to do: forcing a binary **license-or-leave** and consolidating EEA liquidity into a licensed oligopoly (Coinbase/Kraken/Crypto.com/Bybit/OKX), with unlicensed global venues (MEXC, Binance) ceding the market `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: over the next ~2–3 quarters either (a) MEXC applies for / obtains a CASP licence to re-enter (reversing the retreat), OR (b) it stays out and more mid-tier unlicensed venues exit EEA markets, confirming consolidation — watch which. Thesis breaks if the EEA ends up under-served enough that regulators soften enforcement or MEXC keeps serving EU users via grey channels with no action. Trigger to watch: MEXC appearing (or not) on a national CASP register, and the next unlicensed-venue EEA exit.
