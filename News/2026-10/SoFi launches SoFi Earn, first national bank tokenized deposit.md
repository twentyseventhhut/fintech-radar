### SoFi Launches SoFi Earn (SOFIE), the First and Only National Bank-Issued Tokenized Deposit for Consumers

_Members can put their digital dollars to work earning 4.40% APY 1 with FDIC insurance 2, use them to buy crypto or move them 24/7 through SoFiUSD._

SoFi Technologies, Inc. (NASDAQ: SOFI), the everything app for digital financial services, today announced the launch of SoFi Earn (SOFIE), the first and only national bank-issued tokenized deposit for consumers. SoFi members can now earn interest on digital dollars while keeping them ready to use, representing the company’s fifth new crypto offering in less than a year.

SoFi Earn brings the best of banking into the digital money ecosystem, giving consumers a new way to earn while keeping their funds ready for what’s next. Each SOFIE represents $1 held on deposit at SoFi Bank, earning 4.40% APY with funds FDIC-insured up to applicable FDIC limits. When members are ready to put those funds to work, they can convert SOFIE 1:1 to SoFiUSD, then use the SoFiUSD to buy crypto directly in the SoFi app or move money over blockchain rails 24/7.

“Too much money in the digital asset ecosystem is sitting idle, leaving massive potential earnings on the table for millions of people,” said **Anthony Noto, CEO of SoFi.** “We believe your money should keep working for you until the moment you need it. SoFi Earn combines the earning power and protections of a bank deposit with the speed and utility of digital assets, giving people more value, flexibility and control over their money.”

**Earn on a deposit. Move with a stablecoin.**

Stablecoins have become an increasingly common way to hold digital dollars, but many do not pay interest and are not FDIC-insured. Fed research estimates that approximately $64 billion, or 21.2% of the stablecoin market, sits idle in rarely used, forgotten or inactive wallets.

SoFi Earn (SOFIE) is a separate, interest-bearing tokenized deposit at SoFi Bank, with FDIC insurance up to applicable limits. SoFi Earn offers consumers a bank-issued digital dollar designed to keep earning until they’re ready to convert it to SoFiUSD to buy crypto or move funds onchain.

For example, a member saving $5,000 for a future crypto purchase can hold those funds in a SOFIE deposit account, which they can access through their SoFi Crypto account, and earn the applicable APY while they wait. When ready, the member can convert SOFIE 1:1 to SoFiUSD and use the SoFiUSD to buy crypto or send it to a supported external wallet over blockchain rails 24/7.

**Building the digital money ecosystem**

SoFi Earn is the latest in a series of launches that, in less than a year, have rapidly built out SoFi’s digital money ecosystem, giving consumers and businesses more ways to earn, invest and move money while bringing the speed and utility of blockchain into a regulated banking environment.

In November 2025, SoFi became the first and only nationally chartered bank to launch crypto trading for consumers, giving members the ability to buy, sell and hold crypto alongside their banking, borrowing and investing in one app. Today, members can trade more than 40 cryptocurrencies through SoFi Crypto.

In December 2025, SoFi launched SoFiUSD (SOFID), the first stablecoin issued by a U.S. national bank, extending its digital money infrastructure to 24/7 dollar movement and settlement. SoFiUSD is already being put to work through settlement of SoFi’s entire card program across Mastercard’s global payments network, demonstrating how blockchain-based dollars can support real-world money movement at scale.

Earlier in 2026, SoFi launched Big Business Banking, giving companies a single regulated platform to manage both fiat and digital assets. Businesses can hold deposits and move money 24/7 across traditional payment rails, stablecoins and digital assets, helping clients operate seamlessly across traditional banking and the digital-asset economy.

SoFi Earn adds another critical layer to that ecosystem by giving consumers a way to earn on their digital dollars before putting them to work. Together, SoFi’s growing suite of digital money products is connecting banking and blockchain to make money more productive when it’s held and more useful when it moves.

SoFi Earn is rolling out to SoFi Crypto members now through November. Learn more here.

### [1] Why it matters / the mechanism
A **tokenized deposit** is a direct deposit *liability* of the bank put onchain — legally a bank deposit (hence FDIC-eligible), unlike a payment stablecoin (a claim on a segregated reserve). The product is explicitly structured to exploit the regulatory split: under the **GENIUS Act** (OCC final rule announced 2026-10-21, effective 2027-01-18) payment stablecoins are **barred from paying interest/yield**. Tokenized deposits sit *cleanly outside* that ban, so SoFi can legally pay yield on a digital dollar where SoFiUSD (a GENIUS stablecoin) cannot. SoFi is pitching SOFIE at the "idle stablecoin" problem — Fed research it cites estimates ~$64B (21.2% of the stablecoin market) sits in dormant wallets earning nothing.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
Red-team of the "first national bank-issued tokenized deposit **for consumers**" claim and the product economics.

1. **Is "first tokenized deposit" true unqualified?** No — JPMorgan's JPMD (live since Nov 2025 on Base, Oct 2026 on Ethereum) and Citi Token Services predate it. SoFi's claim survives only with the **"for consumers"** qualifier: rivals are institutional/wholesale. Claim is defensible *as worded*, misleading if stripped. (answered)

2. **Is SOFIE really FDIC-insured, or marketing gloss?** Structurally yes — it's a deposit liability of SoFi Bank, N.A. (Member FDIC), insured to $250k/depositor/category per standard FDIC rules. Distinct from SoFiUSD, which the disclosures explicitly flag as NOT FDIC-insured. (answered)

3. **Does the 4.40% APY beat alternatives?** It is competitive vs HYSA levels and vastly better than non-yielding stablecoins, but APY is **variable, no minimum balance, fees may reduce earnings** — not a locked rate. Sustainability depends on SoFi's rate strategy. (open)

4. **Why does SoFi get to pay yield when GENIUS bans stablecoin interest?** Because a tokenized *deposit* is legally a bank deposit, not a payment stablecoin — it sits outside the GENIUS/OCC interest ban (effective 2027-01-18). This is the core regulatory-arbitrage design. (answered)

5. **Is this regulatory arbitrage sustainable?** Plausible risk: regulators could scrutinize products that functionally replicate yield-bearing stablecoins via a deposit wrapper. No current rule bars it, but it's a live policy debate (community banks' $850B deposit-drain warning). (open)

6. **What chain does SOFIE run on / who custodies?** Not disclosed in the launch PR. SoFiUSD uses BitGo infra; unclear if SOFIE shares it. Material gap for assessing tech/counterparty risk. (open)

7. **Is "fifth crypto offering in under a year" real momentum or product sprawl?** Verifiable sequence (crypto trading, SoFiUSD, Big Business Banking, etc.) is genuine; whether it's coherent ecosystem vs. launch-cadence theater is a judgment call — stock softness on launch day suggests market skepticism. (open)

8. **Does 1:1 SOFIE↔SoFiUSD convertibility create run/liquidity risk?** Convert-on-demand to a stablecoin that can leave over blockchain rails 24/7 means deposits can exit instantly — a design tension for a deposit-funded bank. Not addressed in PR. (open)

9. **Is the $64B "idle stablecoin" TAM real?** Cited to Fed research (21.2% of stablecoin market in dormant wallets). Directionally plausible but SoFi-framed; idle ≠ addressable (much sits with non-SoFi users / institutions). (open)

10. **Could SOFIE cannibalize SoFi's own HYSA deposits?** Possible — members may shift from standard savings to SOFIE for the crypto optionality, with no clear net-new-deposit proof. Economics unproven. (open)

11. **Regulatory status of the OCC rule it leans on?** The GENIUS/OCC final rule was announced 2026-10-21 (one day before launch) and is **not yet effective** (2027-01-18). SoFi is building ahead of the live regime. (answered)

12. **Any precedent risk of consumer confusion between SOFIE / SoFiUSD / SoFi Crypto?** High — three similarly named digital-dollar products with different insurance/legal status; disclosure burden is heavy (the PR carries extensive FDIC/not-FDIC caveats). (open)

13. **Is rollout actually live?** Partially — "rolling out to SoFi Crypto members now through November," so phased, not GA. (answered)

Importance: 4/5 — First consumer-facing bank tokenized deposit in the US, cleanly exploiting the GENIUS Act's deposit-vs-stablecoin split to pay yield where stablecoins legally can't; strategically significant for the bank-token vs stablecoin competition and a template rivals (JPM/Citi retail) may follow. Not 5/5: phased rollout, single issuer, undisclosed chain/custody, and open questions on deposit cannibalization and run-risk keep it below a landmark/systemic event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Tokenized deposits vs stablecoins is the key 2026 structural fault line. A tokenized deposit (SOFIE) is a direct bank liability moved on-chain — it stays inside the deposit-insurance (FDIC) and bank-capital frameworks and can pay interest; a payment stablecoin under the GENIUS Act (signed 2025-07-18) is a 1:1-reserve-backed claim that may NOT pay interest and is NOT FDIC-insured. SoFi is explicitly arbitraging that gap: SoFi's own PR cites Fed research that ~$64bn (21.2% of the stablecoin market) sits idle in forgotten wallets — the 4.40% APY + FDIC wrapper is pitched at that idle float (per SoFi PR, 2026-10-22). Structurally the US bank-on-chain space is consolidating fast around two camps: (1) incumbent-led tokenized-deposit utilities — The Clearing House network led by JPMorgan, BofA, Citi, Wells Fargo announced 2026-06, targeting 2027, framed defensively around the ~$6.6tn of deposits BofA's Moynihan warned could migrate to stablecoins (via Genfinity/Yahoo, 2026-06); and (2) bank stablecoins (SoFiUSD, Fidelity FIDD, a 21-institution consortium incl. Goldman/BofA/Citi/UBS announced ~2026-09). Why now: GENIUS Act gave the federal license path, so the 2026 fight is incumbents racing to keep deposits on-chain before non-bank stablecoins (USDC/USDT) disintermediate them.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for this layer: on-chain float / circulating supply, APY passed through, redemption liquidity, and (for SoFi group) total deposits + deposit beta. Key players & basis of competition: JPMorgan (JPMD/JPM Coin — institutional-only tokenized deposit, now on Ethereum, 2026-10 [[JPMorgan brings JPM Coin deposit token to Ethereum]]); BNY (institutional tokenized deposits, [[BNY launches tokenized deposits]], 2026-01); the TCH bank consortium (wholesale/B2B). SoFi's differentiation: it is the first and only CONSUMER-facing national-bank tokenized deposit — the incumbents' tokenized deposits are wholesale/institutional, so SoFi occupies a genuinely distinct niche (retail on-chain yield + insurance) rather than catching up. Recent SoFi moves building the stack: crypto trading (2025-11, first national bank), SoFiUSD stablecoin ([[SoFi launches SoFiUSD, first stablecoin from a US national bank]], 2026-05, settles SoFi's entire Mastercard card program), Big Business Banking (2026), SOFIE (2026-10). Moat `(analysis)`: national bank charter + FDIC wrapper + 15.8M member distribution — a regulatory/distribution moat incumbents' institutional products don't contest at retail, but one the TCH consortium could extend into later.

**Comps & multiples.** This is a product launch, not a raise/deal — no SOFIE-level valuation exists ("no data"). Parent/sector anchors:
- **SoFi Technologies (SOFI):** mkt cap ~$18.79bn (2026-10-16, stockanalysis), TTM revenue ~$4.77bn → P/S = $18.79bn / $4.77bn = **~3.9x** (source quotes 6.71x on a different revenue basis — treat as [UNSOURCED] discrepancy; 3.9x is the clean arithmetic). Q3 2026 reports 2026-10-27 (consensus ~$1.25bn rev, EPS ~$0.17).
- **SoFiUSD (SOFID) circulating supply:** ~$337–349m market cap (RWA.xyz / CoinGecko, ~2026-10-22), from >$100m within weeks of the 2026-05 launch — the on-chain float SOFIE is designed to feed.
- Internal product comps (no multiples, qualitative): [[BNY launches tokenized deposits]] (institutional), [[Fidelity launches FIDD US dollar stablecoin]] (bank stablecoin), [[Bank of America CEO warns stablecoins could drain bank deposits]] (the deposit-flight thesis this product defends against).
Distribution not computed (no ≥3 comparable multiples at the product level); parent P/S ~3.9x is in-line for a profitable-but-slowing fintech bank, not stretched.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory novelty / untested wrapper.** SOFIE is a "tokenized deposit" that is NOT a payment stablecoin and sits outside the GENIUS Act's stablecoin regime — the OCC/FDIC treatment of a consumer tokenized deposit that freely converts 1:1 into an on-chain stablecoin is new; a supervisory reinterpretation (e.g. that convertibility makes it a de facto stablecoin, or questions on FDIC pass-through at the token layer) would hit the whole product. Why it matters: the entire pitch rests on the FDIC label.
2. **Deposit-cost / margin pressure.** Paying 4.40% APY on what is a demand-like, instantly-convertible deposit raises deposit beta and funding cost just as SoFi's growth decelerates (mkt cap -44% y/y); if SOFIE cannibalizes cheaper existing deposits rather than attracting new float, net interest margin compresses. Second-order: yield is variable "subject to change at any time" — SoFi can cut it, but that undercuts the differentiation.
3. **Incumbent disintermediation at scale.** The JPMorgan/Citi/BofA/Wells TCH tokenized-deposit network (2027) and the 21-bank stablecoin consortium can replicate the consumer tokenized-deposit model with far larger balance sheets and distribution; SoFi's "first-mover" niche is defensible only while it stays retail-only and incumbents stay wholesale.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` SOFIE reframes the bank-vs-stablecoin fight: rather than fighting stablecoin outflow, SoFi monetizes the idle float BY wrapping on-chain dollars in FDIC + yield — a template for retail banks defending deposits in the GENIUS era. Falsifiable thesis: if consumer tokenized deposits work, expect a mid-tier US bank to copy the FDIC-insured-token-+-convertible-stablecoin pairing within ~2–3 quarters. Trigger/catalyst: Q3 2026 print (2026-10-27) and any disclosed SOFIE uptake / SoFiUSD supply inflection; what breaks it — an OCC/FDIC clarification narrowing how convertible tokenized deposits may be marketed, or SOFIE simply cannibalizing existing deposits with no net new float.

2. **Raise-revenue-hold-EPS signals reinvestment, watch the margin.** FY26 revenue up, EBITDA/EPS flat → every incremental dollar is being spent (crypto, originations, Galileo). Financial Services contribution-profit growth (+13%) already trails its revenue (+29%). Second-order: if crypto build-out keeps compressing segment margins, the "operating leverage" thesis gets pushed out — Q3 EBITDA margin and FS contribution margin are the lines to watch on 2026-10-27.
3. **Tech Platform still shrinking (−23% YoY).** The Galileo/Tech Solutions "134M accounts" line SoFi touts in this note masks a declining revenue segment — announced reach vs monetized reach. If Tech Platform stays negative, SoFi is a two-engine (Lending + FinServ) story, not the three-engine platform narrative.
4. **FDIC-insured tokenized deposit is a regulatory first — execution/regulatory risk is the flag.** SOFIE as a national-bank-issued, FDIC-insured tokenized deposit (explicitly "not a payment stablecoin") is novel; upside is a defensible, regulated moat vs uninsured stablecoins, but it also concentrates regulatory scrutiny (OCC/FDIC) — a risk the earnings numbers can't yet price.
