San Francisco, Oct. 15, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- Stripe and Plasma today announced a  collaboration to launch multi-currency stablecoin cards, making it easier for people and businesses to spend across currencies and markets.
Built in close partnership with Plasma as a design partner, the aim is to make everyday spending simpler. Cardholders will be able to use the currencies they already hold, reduce unnecessary conversions and FX costs, and spend abroad as easily as they spend at home.
Plasma is building the financial account for people whose lives span countries, assets and businesses, starting with everyday spending. As a design partner, Plasma will work with Stripe to shape a multi-currency card experience around how these customers manage and use their money across borders. 
 “ We’re building Plasma for people whose lives and finances span countries. Partnering with Stripe gives us the opportunity to develop multi-currency cards around how those people actually live and spend, with a simple ambition: spend like a local, anywhere .”- Paul Faecks, CEO of Plasma 
 “Stablecoins make it possible to hold and move money globally. We're working with Plasma to bring that same flexibility to spending, giving businesses the infrastructure to offer cards across currencies and markets without having to build it themselves. ” - Henri Stern, Head of Stripe Crypto and Cofounder & CEO of Privy
Stripe is expanding its stablecoin card infrastructure to support more currencies and stablecoins, giving businesses greater flexibility to build card products for customers operating across markets. The expanded capabilities will support:
 Fiat currencies : USD, BRL, EUR, MXN, and COP
 Stablecoins: OUSD and eligible GENIUS-ready stablecoins
 Markets: Available in 30+ countries today, with plans to expand to 100+ countries in 2027
A cardholder holding euros, for example, will be able to spend directly in euros without converting through dollars first. Businesses operating across markets can issue cards denominated in currencies that match their spending needs, helping reduce unnecessary FX costs.
The launch marks the  first step toward a broader multi-currency experience. Stripe and Plasma are toward enabling cardholders to spend across multiple balances, with the appropriate currency selected automatically at the point of purchase.
The partnership brings Stripe’s fiat and crypto infrastructure and Plasma’s product together to develop a card experience around how people live and spend across countries.
With multi-currency support, people and businesses will be able to spend with stablecoin-backed cards across more currencies and markets.
 About Plasma 
Plasma is building the financial account for people whose lives span countries, assets and businesses. Its app, Plasma One, gives users a global dollar account and card for spending, sending and earning, starting with everyday spending. For more information, visit plasma.org
 About Stripe 
Stripe is building programmable financial services for millions of businesses globally. Millions of companies use Stripe to accept payments online and in person, embed financial services, power custom revenue models, and build a more profitable business.
Headquartered in San Francisco and Dublin, Stripe processes over $1.9 trillion of payments annually, equivalent to 1.6% of global GDP. Stripe users include all of the top AI companies, 90% of the Dow Jones Industrial Average, and 88% of the Forbes AI 50. 
Through its scale and investments in R&D, particularly artificial intelligence and stablecoins, Stripe accelerates the utility of frontier technology in the global economy.

# Context-enrichment: Stripe and Plasma team up on multicurrency stablecoin cards
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-15 Stripe and **Plasma** (Paul Faecks' stablecoin company — the Plasma L1 chain + "Plasma One" global-dollar neobank) announced a **collaboration to build multi-currency stablecoin cards**. De-PR'd:
- This is an **expansion of card-denomination currencies**, not a new product. Stripe's stablecoin card rails (via its Bridge/Privy crypto stack) already existed; what's new is support for **non-USD fiat denominations**: USD, EUR, BRL, MXN, COP, settled against **OUSD and "GENIUS-ready" stablecoins**. Live in **30+ countries today**, "plans to expand to 100+ in 2027."
- Plasma's role is explicitly **"design partner"** — it shapes UX/requirements and is an early card customer on Stripe's infra. Stripe owns the issuing/FX/crypto infrastructure; Plasma brings the consumer product (Plasma One) and the cross-border customer profile. This is a **customer-cum-co-development** deal, not a 50/50 JV.
- The flagship claim — "spend across multiple balances with the currency auto-selected at point of purchase" — is **explicitly roadmap**, not live ("the launch marks the first step toward"). What's live today is single-currency-denominated cards (hold EUR → spend EUR without routing through USD).
- **Why framed this way:** the PR anchors on "spend like a local, anywhere" (a consumer-FX story) to differentiate from the crowded USD-stablecoin-card pack. But the real substance is Stripe quietly turning its card stack into a **multi-fiat issuing platform** and lining up design partners/customers (Plasma) ahead of the 100-country push it already telegraphed two weeks earlier ([[Stripe to expand stablecoin cards to 100+ countries]]).

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Visa / StraitsX** announced the SAME 100+-markets stablecoin-card expansion on the SAME day, 2026-10-15 ([[StraitsX and Visa take stablecoin card programs to 100+ markets]]) — multi-market stablecoin card issuance is now a land-grab, not a differentiator. Visa reports strong stablecoin card growth with 160+ programs ([[Visa reports nearly 200% YoY stablecoin card payment growth]]).
- **Rain** ($1.95bn val, Visa+Mastercard principal member) is the closest pure-play card-issuing competitor, building enterprise/multicurrency stablecoin card stacks ([[Rain raises $250M Series C at $1.95B valuation]], [[Rain builds full stack for enterprise stablecoin cards]], [[Rain introduces wallets at center of stablecoin platform]]). **Mastercard + BVNK** launched a fiat↔stablecoin money platform ([[Mastercard and BVNK launch global fiat-stablecoin money platform]]); **Marqeta + Coinbase** ([[Marqeta and Coinbase collaborate on stablecoin-backed card payments]]).
- **Position: parity / slightly ahead on distribution.** Multi-fiat *denomination* (not just USDC-spend) is a genuine but narrow edge; Stripe's moat is its issuing/acquiring scale, not card novelty. Rain/BVNK already do multicurrency; Stripe's advantage is owning Bridge (issuer) + Privy (wallets) + Issuing + acquiring end-to-end.
- **Why the landscape is this way:** the GENIUS Act de-risked US issuance → every network rushed card programs in 2026 → margin is shifting from "can you issue a stablecoin card" (commoditized) to "do you own the full stack + distribution." Stripe is vertically integrating; most peers rent one layer.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Stripe's stablecoin arc: acquired **Bridge** (stablecoin infra) and **Privy** (wallets, 2025); launched **Open Issuance**/stablecoin products ([[Stripe launches Open Issuance and stablecoin products]], [[Stripe introduces stablecoin payments for subscriptions]]); backed **Tempo** stablecoin chain ([[Tempo raises $500M Series A at $5B valuation]], [[Stripe-backed Tempo launches stablecoin transaction framework]]); co-launched **Open USD (OUSD)** with Visa/Mastercard/Coinbase/BlackRock, issued by Bridge ([[Stripe challenges Circle with bank-backed Open USD stablecoin]], [[SBI Holdings and Startale join Open Standard OUSD stablecoin alliance]]). Henri Stern (Privy co-founder/CEO) now heads Stripe Crypto.
- Fit: **tight and logical.** OUSD gives Stripe its own settlement token; card issuing gives it the spend rail; multi-fiat denomination is the natural next SKU. Processes >$1.9tn/yr → stablecoin cards are an incremental, high-optionality bet on its existing merchant/issuing base, not a pivot.
- **Why Stripe acts this way:** core payments is a commodity take-rate business; stablecoins let Stripe (a) cut FX/settlement cost on cross-border, (b) capture new rails before Visa/Mastercard lock them down, and (c) sell "issue a card in any currency without building it" as embedded infra — a software-margin line on top of a payments-margin base.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new:** holding EUR/BRL/MXN/COP and spending *in that currency* off a stablecoin card (vs. the USD-only norm) — removes a double-conversion for non-US holders. **Not new:** stablecoin cards themselves, multicurrency card issuing (Rain/BVNK), or Stripe's 100-country ambition (already stated 2026-10-01).
- **Traction = thin.** Everything load-bearing is announced/roadmap: "30+ countries today" (no volumes, no cardholder counts for *this* multi-fiat variant), auto-currency-selection is future, Plasma is a "design partner" (no issued-card numbers). Market-level signal: stablecoin card spend ~$1.2bn/month, tripling YoY (CoinDesk/PaymentScan) — small but fast.
- **Who captures the margin:** Stripe owns issuer (Bridge) + wallet (Privy) + processing + OUSD → captures FX spread, issuing fees and settlement. Plasma gets distribution/UX but rides Stripe's rails — risk that Plasma stays a **front-end brand** while Stripe owns the economics. **Red flag for Plasma:** per web reporting, on-chain stablecoins on the Plasma network fell from ~$6.35bn (Oct 2025) to ~$1.31bn (Oct 2026) — the design-partner deal may be as much a **distribution lifeline for Plasma** as a product milestone.
- **Why value-add is real-but-narrow:** multi-fiat denomination is a real UX win for cross-border consumers, but it is a *feature*, not a moat — Visa/Rain can and do match it. The durable value is Stripe's stack integration + OUSD, not this specific card.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Roadmap: 100+ countries in 2027; auto-currency-selection across balances at point of sale; more stablecoins/fiats. Watch for **named issuing customers + actual volumes** — absence of those is the tell.
- Backdrop: GENIUS Act (US) enabling compliant issuance; EU MiCA; France advancing stablecoin tax rules ([[France advances stablecoin tax plan and crypto loss relief]]). Regulatory tailwind is the main driver of the 2026 card land-grab.
- **Risks / second-order:** (a) commoditization — if everyone offers multi-fiat stablecoin cards, the edge collapses to distribution/cost; (b) FX economics undisclosed — "reduce FX costs" vs. incumbent card FX is unquantified; (c) fraud/chargeback liability on stablecoin-settled cards is unaddressed; (d) Plasma's shrinking on-chain TVL raises counterparty/viability questions for the design partner. **Counterintuitive:** the simultaneous Visa/StraitsX 100-market announcement the same day suggests coordinated industry choreography around OUSD/GENIUS, not a Stripe-specific breakthrough.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Stablecoin-linked card spending is the fast-growing bridge between on-chain balances and everyday merchant acceptance. Stablecoin payment volume reached ~$482bn in the first nine months of 2026, +45% YoY (per KuCoin/CryptoBriefing, as of 2026-10). Visa's stablecoin settlement run rate climbed from $4.5bn annualized in Jan 2026 (see [[Visa stablecoin settlement reaches $4.5bn annualized run rate]]) to a ~$20bn annualized run rate by Sept 2026, across 130-160+ stablecoin-linked card programs (per TokenPost, as of 2026-10). Structure: the value chain splits into stablecoin issuance/reserves, card-issuing infrastructure (BINs, processing, Visa/MC principal membership), and the consumer/business neobank layer. Barriers are regulatory (licensing, GENIUS-ready stablecoins) and network membership (Visa/MC scheme access), which concentrate power at the rails. Why now: the US GENIUS Act framework and multicurrency demand push issuing beyond the USD — the Stripe/Plasma deal explicitly adds EUR, BRL, MXN, COP across 30+ countries (-> 100+ in 2027), so FX friction, not just dollar access, becomes the wedge.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: card TPV / annualized spend run rate, # of card programs & countries live, stablecoins/fiat currencies supported, take rate per transaction (interchange + FX spread, largely undisclosed here). Key players and basis of competition (distribution + scheme access + currency breadth, not price):
- **Stripe/Bridge** — acquired Bridge Feb 2025 for ~$1.1bn; Bridge cards fully integrated with Stripe Issuing (powers ~350M cards worldwide), expanding Visa stablecoin cards from 18 countries to 100+ in 2026 (per TokenPost/Bridge, as of 2026). Stripe-wide TPV $1.9tn in 2025, +34% YoY. Bridge stablecoin volume reportedly quadrupled over the prior year (Stripe 2025 annual letter, 2026-02-24).
- **Rain** — $1.95bn valuation (Jan 2026 Series C, see [[Rain raises $250M Series C at $1.95B valuation]]); Visa principal member, scaled to >$3bn annualized card spend; later merging with Naver's crypto arm (see [[Naver makes first US crypto investment in Rain ahead of merger]]).
- **Visa / Mastercard** — the rails; Visa 130-160+ programs, MC's Crypto Partner Program with ~85 firms (see [[Mastercard launches Crypto Partner Program]], [[Visa partners BVNK to power stablecoin on Visa Direct]]).
- Processor/issuer peers in-base: Lithic, Episode Six (see [[Monee and Episode Six launch ShopeePay card in Thailand]]), Thredd; neobank peers OKX Card, Bleap (see [[OKX Card launches in Europe]], [[Bleap raises $6m seed for EU stablecoin-linked card]]).
Protagonist position: Stripe is **ahead** on infrastructure/distribution (scale, Issuing integration, Visa partnership) (analysis); Plasma is the thin consumer front-end (Plasma One neobank). Moat for Stripe = scale + switching costs of embedded issuing; for Plasma, little — it is a design partner, not the rails.

**Comps & multiples.** Private names, so round valuations not market caps:
- Stripe: $159bn tender valuation (2026-02-24, Axios/CNBC), +~70% YoY. TPV $1.9tn -> implied price/TPV = $159bn / $1,900bn = **0.08x** (analysis; TPV is gross flow, not revenue — illustrative only, not a revenue multiple). Revenue not disclosed -> EV/Revenue = no data.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Disintermediation of the neobank layer.** Stripe supplies rails+Issuing+crypto infra; Plasma is only a "design partner." If Stripe productizes multicurrency issuing for all customers (its stated aim: "without having to build it themselves"), Plasma's differentiation thins — value accrues to the rails, not the front-end.
2. **Partner counterparty/liquidity risk.** Stablecoins on Plasma's chain fell from a ~$6.35bn peak (Oct 2025) to ~$1.31bn (Oct 2026) (per web sources, 2026-10) — a shrinking native-chain base raises questions about Plasma's standalone traction behind the co-branded launch.
3. **Economics undisclosed / "announced vs live".** PR silent on interchange split, FX spread, who bears fraud liability; multicurrency is "first step," auto-balance selection still "toward" — adoption is announced, not proven at scale.
4. **Regulatory dependence.** Tied to "GENIUS-ready stablecoins" and 100+ country expansion — each market adds licensing/scheme-approval execution risk.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Signals the stablecoin card race moving from dollar-access to **multicurrency/FX arbitrage** — attacking cross-border interchange + FX spreads that incumbents (and neobanks like Wise/Revolut) monetize. Falsifiable thesis: if Stripe reaches 100+ countries in 2027 with multi-balance auto-selection, it becomes the default multicurrency stablecoin issuer, squeezing standalone issuers like Rain. Trigger to watch: disclosed card TPV / # of live programs on the new multicurrency rails; what breaks it — Visa/MC capping scheme access or GENIUS-equivalent EU/LatAm rules stalling non-USD issuance.

**No full earnings report in the news** — this is a product partnership (Stripe + Plasma co-develop multi-currency stablecoin cards), not an earnings release. The figures below are the most recently *disclosed* annual/metric data (self-reported in Stripe's 2025 annual letter, Feb 2026) plus press-cited operating stats; treat as management-disclosed, not audited/independent.

**Most recently disclosed figures (FY2025, annual letter 24 Feb 2026).**
- Total payment volume: **$1.9tn in 2025, +34% YoY** (from ~$1.4tn in 2024); the partnership press release itself repeats "over $1.9 trillion of payments annually, equivalent to 1.6% of global GDP."
- Revenue (self-reported estimate): **~$6.8bn, +33% YoY** (from ~$5.1bn in 2024); EBITDA ~$1.2bn. Stripe says it remains **robustly profitable** — 2024 was its first profitable year (~$101.9M pre-tax profit vs a ~$1.2bn pre-tax loss in 2023). GAAP net income, margin, and cash flow are **not disclosed** (private). (analysis: profitability is self-asserted and unaudited in public domain.)
- Valuation: **$159bn** at the Feb-2026 employee tender offer, up from ~$91.5bn (Feb 2024) — a tender mark, not a public market cap.

**Stablecoin / crypto traction (the relevant strategic metric for this deal).**
- Stablecoin payment volume **~doubled to ~$400bn in 2025**; Bridge (acquired for ~$1.1bn, Feb 2025) saw volume **more than quadruple** in 2025 (was >$5bn annualized pre-deal).
- Stablecoin **card** spend specifically: **~$1.2bn spent through cards in the latest reported month (Oct 2026 press), ~3x a year earlier** (PaymentScan, press-cited — not a Stripe audited figure, mark `[press-cited]`). Current card users cited: Kraken, Ramp, Morse.
- Coverage expanding from **~18 countries today → 100+ by end-2026/into 2027**; the Plasma release says 30+ countries today, 100+ in 2027.

**How the Plasma deal fits Stripe's crypto strategy.** Stripe is stitching a full stablecoin stack from three pieces — **Bridge** (orchestration / on-off ramps), **Privy** (embedded wallets), plus native card issuing — now run as one crypto unit under **Henri Stern** (Privy co-founder, "Head of Stripe Crypto"). The Plasma tie-up extends stablecoin cards **beyond USD** into EUR, BRL, MXN, COP (and OUSD / GENIUS-ready stablecoins), i.e. moving from dollar-rails to multi-currency spend. Why it matters: card spend is the consumer-facing demand layer that monetizes the Bridge/Privy infrastructure, and multi-currency support attacks cross-border FX cost — the highest-value stablecoin use case (B2B stablecoin payments were cited at ~$226bn in 2025). Second-order: Plasma is a *design partner* (announced, not yet live at scale), so this is pipeline/optionality, not disclosed revenue — consistent with Stripe framing stablecoins as an R&D bet, not yet a broken-out P&L line.

**Explicitly NOT disclosed (private):** quarterly results, GAAP income statement, net margin, EPS, segment revenue (incl. stablecoin/Bridge/Privy revenue), take-rate, forward guidance, consensus — none exist in the public domain, so no variance table is possible.
