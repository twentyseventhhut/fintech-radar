# Nubank, Inter and BTG Pactual weigh buying Argentina's Brubank

> [!info] 2026-10-26 · 1 упоминаний · 1 источника(ов) с текстом
> Каналы: Connecting the Dots in Fintech

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Connecting the Dots in Fintech] 🇦🇷 Nubank, Inter and BTG Pactual weigh buying Brubank for an Argentine banking license. The sale would give the buyer a bank license and about 5 million users. Sources name Inter as the frontrunner, though it says it is not negotiating.

Neobanco Brubank está a la venta y Nubank, Inter y BTG Pactual evaluarían comprarlo para obtener una licencia bancaria en Argentina
Según pudo saber iProUP, tres entidades brasileñas están interesadas en adquirir a Brubank para tener su licencia bancaria y unos 5 millones de usuarios.
Los tres bancos que se quieren quedar con Brubank
Según fuentes consultadas por iProUP , la lista de los interesados la encabeza una entidad con marca y colores similares: Nubank . El unicornio fundado por David Vélez, que cotiza en Wall Street con una valuación cercana a u$s72.000 millones , ya funciona en México, Colombia y Brasil. En Argentina intentó desembarcar varias veces.
Diego Kupferberg, experto en desarrollo de emprendimientos digitales y fundador de API Hub de Negocios , remarca que "Nubank gana escala en un mercado que ya hizo el trabajo duro de adopción: hay más de 322 millones de cuentas virtuales (CVU). No tiene que evangelizar, tiene que sacarle clientes a otros".
Christian Balatti, consultor Fintech y cofundador de EsXAI , remarca que "la compra le daría licencia local y base de clientes para una entrada más rápida, además de infraestructura para transferencias, QR, fondos y préstamos compatible con su modelo app-first".
Así, "estaría en condiciones de pelear el rubro masivo, la principalidad y la tarjeta de crédito contra Mercado Pago, Ualá, Naranja X y la banca tradicional".
Francisco Chaves del Valle, consultor fintech y docente de ITBA, remarca que el plástico es "un producto con el que puede diferenciarse rápidamente : ya que construyó buena parte de su crecimiento en Brasil alrededor de una experiencia sencilla de tarjeta, límites, crédito y servicios bancarios integrados".Justamente, es el primer emisor de plásticos en Brasil.
 Inter, Nubank y BTG Pactual estarían interesados en quedarse con Brubank 
Su colega añade que "abrió oficina en Buenos Aires con un plan de inversión de cerca de u$s500 millones a cinco años". Remarca que "posee un motor de riesgo probado con más de 100 millones de clientes", pero "llega tarde y contra un incumbente con ecosistema propio de comercio y pagos".
 La segunda entidad en danza es BTG Pactual , dedicada a la banca de inversión y registrado como agente de liquidación y compensación (ALyC) ante CNV . Fue sumando licencias en México, Uruguay, Perú y otros países para apuntar al negocio masivo.
 Balatti considera a Brubank como "la pieza retail que le falta para entrar del todo en Argentina , donde reconoce que la operación es pequeña pero muy rentable. En Uruguay, compró HSBC por u$s175 millones para jugar en crédito corporativo, banca de inversión y gestión de inversiones".
Para Chaves del Valle, puede construir una propuesta intermedia entre un banco digital masivo y una banca privada tradicional : combinar fondos, bonos, acciones, inversiones internacionales, asesoramiento y crédito respaldado por activos".
Kupferberg añade que la entidad " analiza cambiar esa licencia por una de banco corporativo , porque la actual no le permite dar financiamiento directo ni manejar créditos grandes a empresas . Gana fondeo local y la posibilidad de prestar a firmas medianas y grandes que hoy dependen del mercado de capitales".
En este caso, coinciden los expertos, competiría contra Galicia, Santander y Macro en banca empresa ; y en inversiones versus las ALyC digitales Balanz, IOL y Cocos. 
 El último de la lista es Inter . Recientemente, se convirtió en "billetera" en Argentina con el servicio del BIND en bambalinas para permitir inversiones en Wall Street desde u$s5 .
Según Chaves del Valle, "su diferencial sería integrar la experiencia de un banco local con servicios globales , . La licencia propia le permitiría controlar una parte mayor de la experiencia financiera local".
"Llegó con una jugada inteligente y de bajo costo: ofrece una cuenta en EE.UU. para pagar gastos en el exterior, ahorrar o invertir. Su posición mejoró porque consiguió licencia bancaria en EE.UU., lo que le permite usar los depósitos para dar crédito y emitir tarjetas", señala Kupferberg.
Balatti asegura que " con Brubank pasaría de nicho a banco transaccional con crédito . Competiría con Cocos, Balanz e IOL en inversión global y con Ualá en el segmento joven y digital".
 Su colega añade que también puede robarle clientes a Revolut y Wise , y bancos con cuentas en dólares locales . Además, su banca digital tiene unos 43 millones de usuarios en Brasil, cerca de los 55 millones de Mercado Pago (estimados, ya que no desglosa sus 88 millones por país) y lejos de los 115 millones de Nubank. 
Inter: el candidato con más ventaja para comprar Brubank
 Según aseguran fuentes confiables a iProUP, Inter es el principal candidato para quedarse con Brubank , aunque desde la firma brasileña señalan a iProUP que "no están participando de negociaciones" para adquirirlo. En la industria aseguran que Juan Bruchou, CEO de Brubank e inspiración de la marca, sería el socio con mayor resistencia a firmar el exit.
 La operación tendría lugar en un momento en el que varias billeteras avanzan hacia la licencia bancaria ante los límites del BCRA para las fintech, como la imposibilidad de ofrecer cuentas sueldo, CVU en dólares e intermediación financiera.
Sobre este último, Kupferberg remarca que "prestar con depósitos es mucho más barato que con deuda o capital, y eso define quién gana en tasa. Además, el crédito sobre PBI en Argentina está muy por debajo de Brasil, así que con una macro más estable el espacio para crecer es enorme".
Para Chaves del Valle, adquirir un "banco operativo puede acelerar el acceso a infraestructura, sistemas, cuentas y capacidades regulatorias. No evita la aprobación del BCRA para cambios de control". Además, asegura, "Argentina puede completar una red latinoamericana de servicios bancarios, inversiones y pagos internacionales".
 Balatti acota que la crisis de los préstamos incentiva la oportunidad de compra: "La mora bancaria de familias llegó a 12,9% en julio, el nivel más alto en 20 años. Eso aprieta a las entidades que quieren vender y abarata los activos".
 Cree que la "licencia vale por lo que trae: Brubank aporta depósitos, sueldos y datos de comportamiento, que es lo que realmente se paga ", pero además "se está cerrando la ventana para poder competir". Es decir, primerear a Mercado Pago y Revolut.
Kupferberg remarca que la mayor presencia brasileña puede enriquecer el mercado financiero local con nuevos servicios:
" Open finance de verdad. En Brasil, el usuario comparte sus datos entre entidades y eso abarata el crédito. Acá falta un marco integral"
 "Pagos recurrentes y en cuotas sobre transferencias inmediatas , al estilo de Pix Automático o PIX en cuotas"
 "Tarjetas garantizadas por inversiones. El cliente sin historial invierte una suma que funciona como límite de compra. Es una gran herramienta de inclusión"
" Ahorro por objetivos con rendimiento diario o "cajitas" integrado al crédito"
" Superapps con marketplace, cashback, seguros y viajes dentro del banco, como hace Inter"
Chaves del Valle coincide en la posiblidad de "obtener financiación sin liquidar inmediatamente su cartera de activos", al tiempo que agrega las " hipotecas digitales, con la oportunidad de simplificar el proceso , aunque la disponibilidad dependerá fundamentalmente del fondeo a largo plazo y la estabilidad macroeconómica".
También destaca "obtener crédito directamente al comprar, pagar un servicio o adquirir equipamiento, sin solicitar un préstamo bancario por separado, financiación Pyme basada en transacciones y pagos instantáneos con crédito".
"Quien compre hereda un libro de consumo en pleno ciclo de mora, que llega al 33% en proveedores no bancarios. Los brasileños vienen de gestionar mora alta con scoring granular. Ese know-how vale tanto como el capital ", cierra Balatti.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What's new.** Per Argentine outlet iProUP (relayed by LatamFintech, 2026-10-26), Brubank — Argentina's largest fully digital bank — is up for sale, and three Brazilian players are evaluating a purchase to obtain the one thing a buyer really wants: an Argentine banking license plus Brubank's ~4–5M-user retail base. Named bidders are Nubank, Inter (Banco Inter) and BTG Pactual. Sources frame Inter as the frontrunner, even though Inter publicly denies it is "participating in negotiations." This is a "weigh buying"/in-talks story — not a signed deal, and any change of control still needs BCRA (central bank) approval. ([latamfintech.co](https://www.latamfintech.co/articles/neobanco-brubank-esta-a-la-venta-y-nubank-inter-y-btg-pactual-evaluarian-comprarlo-para-obtener-una-licencia-bancaria-en-argentina), [iproup.com](https://www.iproup.com/finanzas/72533-brubank-en-venta-nubank-inter-y-btg-pactual-compiten-por-la-entidad))

**[1] Why a license, why now.** Argentine fintechs/wallets face BCRA limits a bank license removes: no payroll accounts, no USD CVU, no direct financial intermediation (lending off deposits). Buying an operating bank short-cuts years of organic licensing and hands over infrastructure (transfers, QR, funds, loans). Analysts cited note deposit-funded lending is far cheaper than debt/equity funding, and Argentina's credit-to-GDP is well below Brazil's — a large runway if macro stabilizes. A rising distressed backdrop (household bank delinquency hit ~12.9% in July, a 20-year high; non-bank arrears ~33%) is said to be pressuring sellers and cheapening assets. ([latamfintech.co](https://www.latamfintech.co/articles/neobanco-brubank-esta-a-la-venta-y-nubank-inter-y-btg-pactual-evaluarian-comprarlo-para-obtener-una-licencia-bancaria-en-argentina))

**[2] Brubank, the target.** Founded by Juan Bruchou (ex-CEO Citibank Argentina), Brubank markets itself as Argentina's first 100%-digital, cloud-native bank; it closed 2025 with 4.2M+ users (article frames ~5M and ~9,000 SMEs) and was first local bank to integrate Pix ([[Argentina's Brubank becomes first bank to integrate Pix]]). It has been pushing dollar products — a dollar-backed credit card ([[Brubank launches dollar-backed credit card in Argentina]]) and a native eSIM. Bruchou is reportedly the party most resistant to signing an exit, a real deal risk. ([mercado.com.ar](https://mercado.com.ar/finanzas/brubank-cerro-2025-con-mas-de-42-millones-de-usuarios-y-nuevas-soluciones))

**[3] Nubank's Argentina thread — and the pivot.** Nubank already set up organically: it confirmed a Buenos Aires tech/service hub ([[Nubank enters Argentina with Buenos Aires hub]], Feb 2026) backed by a ~US$474M / 5-year plan, with CEO signalling re-entry ([[Nubank CEO weighs return to Argentina]], Mar 2026) after a ~7-year absence — but without a local banking license, offering products only once BCRA rules are met. Buying Brubank would be a shortcut from "hub + wallet" to a licensed bank, letting Nu contest mass retail, primacy and credit cards against Mercado Pago, Ualá and Naranja X. Note the tension: Nu's stated plan was a service-export hub, so an acquisition would be a strategic escalation, not just continuity. ([lanacion.com.ar](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/economia/negocios/nubank-invertira-us474-millones-para-ampliar-su-red-de-oficinas-y-abrira-una-sede-en-buenos-aires-nid29012026/))

**[4] The other two bidders & the field.** Inter (~43M Brazil users; already a BIND-powered wallet enabling US-market investing from US$5 in Argentina; holds a US bank license) would jump "from niche to transactional bank with credit," hitting Cocos/Balanz/IOL in investing and Ualá in the young/digital segment — but it denies negotiating. BTG Pactual (investment bank, CNV-registered ALyC) wants the retail piece and reportedly mulls converting a license to a corporate-bank one; it has been license-stacking across LatAm (bought HSBC Uruguay ~US$175M) and is active in Brazil M&A ([[BTG Pactual acquires 48% stake in MeuTudo]]). Context for the Argentine wallet-to-bank wave and incumbents: [[Banco Macro buys 50% of Personal Pay for $75M]], [[Uala launches life and accident insurance with Allianz]]. The prize is being first to a licensed digital bank before Mercado Pago and Revolut lock the mass market. ([iproup.com](https://www.iproup.com/finanzas/72533-brubank-en-venta-nubank-inter-y-btg-pactual-compiten-por-la-entidad))

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
1. Single-sourced sell-side? The story traces to one outlet (iProUP) citing unnamed "reliable sources" and named consultants, not the companies. Where is on-record confirmation from Brubank's board, its PE backers, or any bidder?

2. Inter flatly denies it is negotiating — yet is framed as frontrunner. Is "frontrunner" reporting or source spin? A denial on the lead bidder undercuts the whole premise.

3. Is Brubank actually "for sale" via a mandated process, or is this opportunistic inbound interest? No banker, timeline, or price range is disclosed.

4. No valuation anywhere. A "billion-dollar" figure floated in older 2025 coverage is unverified; what would ~4.2M users + a license realistically fetch in a distressed-macro market?

5. User count ambiguity: article says ~5M, Brubank's own 2025 close says 4.2M. Which is current, and how many are active/funded vs. registered?

6. BCRA approval risk: a change of bank control needs central-bank sign-off. Would regulators wave through a foreign (Brazilian) buyer of a licensed Argentine bank amid political/FX sensitivity?

7. Founder blocker: Bruchou is reported as the most resistant to an exit. If he and management won't sell, is there even a deal to be had?

8. Does Nubank even want a bank license here? Its stated plan was a service-export hub without a local license — an acquisition contradicts that posture. Is M&A real intent or analyst projection?

9. BTG's angle is corporate/investment banking; a retail neobank is a stretch fit. Is BTG genuinely bidding for Brubank, or just name-dropped for color?

10. Macro landmine: household delinquency at a 20-year high (12.9%) and ~33% non-bank arrears mean the buyer inherits a deteriorating loan book mid-cycle. Is this an asset or a liability?

11. FX/capital-controls overhang: can a buyer repatriate returns, and how do USD-product ambitions survive Argentine currency restrictions?

12. Competitive moat question: even licensed, can a new entrant dislodge Mercado Pago's entrenched commerce+payments ecosystem? "Late and against an incumbent" is quoted even for Nubank.

13. Overlap/cannibalization: Nubank is already building organically (hub + ~US$474M plan). Does buying Brubank duplicate or redirect that spend?

14. Timing of this item: is it fresh deal-flow or a recycled rehash of 2025 "Nubank eyes Argentina/Brubank" chatter now re-dressed with a three-bidder list?

15. What breaks the thesis: a company denial (Inter already issued one), a stalled BCRA, Bruchou refusing, or the sale process evaporating would all void the story.

Importance: 3/5 — Strategically meaningful (a licensed foothold in Argentina is the missing piece in Nubank's/Inter's LatAm map and would reshape the Argentine neobank race), but it is early-stage, single-sourced, in-talks-only, with the named frontrunner denying and the founder resisting. Significant if confirmed; today it is credible speculation, not a transaction.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Argentina's digital-banking market is consolidated at the top: Ualá, Brubank and Naranja X together serve ~16.4M customers and hold ~88% share, with Mercado Pago commanding ~80% of wallet float (per Fintech News America / RankingsLatAm, as of 2026). The structural "why now" is regulatory: Argentina has no single fintech licence — authorisation is activity-based, and PSP/fintechs operating only on CVU cannot do financial intermediation (can't offer salary accounts, USD CVUs, or lend off deposits) without a full BCRA banking charter (per Legal 500 / JFC Attorneys fintech guides, 2026). That makes a chartered target like Brubank a shortcut: buyers skip a multi-year de-novo licence approval and inherit deposits + behavioural data. Secondary driver: a credit cycle that is simultaneously a tailwind and a warning — the note cites household bank delinquency at 12.9% in July (highest in 20 years) and non-bank-provider arrears ~33%, which depresses asset prices but hands the buyer a book deep in its loss cycle.

**Competitive landscape.** Neobank KPIs that matter here: funded customers, deposit base (funding cost advantage vs. wholesale debt), and credit-on-GDP headroom (Argentina's is far below Brazil's, per note sources). Key players: incumbents Mercado Pago (~80% float) and Ualá (~6M customers); challenger Naranja X; traditional banks Galicia/Santander/Macro. The three Brazilian bidders bring distinct angles — Nubank wants mass retail + card issuance (its moat in Brazil), BTG Pactual wants a retail piece to bolt onto investment banking, Inter wants to convert its low-cost US-dollar wallet play into a transactional bank. Recent moves: Inter launched an Argentine wallet in 2026 (US investing from US$5 via BIND rails); Nubank reportedly ran Brubank due diligence in 2025 that broke without a deal (per dfsud/iProUP); BTG took control of the Nubank Parque arena operator (Sept 2026, unrelated to this deal). Protagonist position (analysis): Nubank is the scale leader (139M customers group-wide) but a late and repeatedly-stalled entrant in Argentina; the note's own sources call Inter the frontrunner — while Inter publicly denies it is negotiating. Treat "frontrunner" as unconfirmed market chatter, not a signed process.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal price is not disclosed — the multiple on Brubank itself is **no data**. Reference points:
- **Nubank / Nu Holdings (public, acquirer):** market cap ~$70bn, LTM revenue ~$21bn → EV/Revenue ≈ 3.3x; P/E ≈ 17.9x (per multiples.vc, as of Oct 2026). Q2'26 gross revenue $5.88bn (+39% YoY), net income $1.06bn (+49% YoY), ROE 33% (per Nu 6-K / businesswire). A ~3.3x revenue multiple is cheap-to-in-line for a 39%-grower, i.e. the market is not pricing in aggressive LatAm expansion — a small Argentine bolt-on is immaterial to Nu's valuation.
- **Ualá (private, closest Argentine neobank comp):** ~$3.2bn post-money after a $195M round in March 2026 (vs. $2.75bn Series E, Nov 2024) (per fintechfutures / dealroom). With ~6M customers that implies ~$530/customer on a round valuation (analysis; round valuations overstate strategic M&A value, especially for a sub-scale distressed-book target). Against that benchmark Brubank's ~4–5M users would frame a low-single-digit-$bn ceiling, but a control deal for a licence + loss-cycle book would plausibly price well below Ualá's per-user mark. Distribution not computed — only one truly comparable Argentine neobank mark.
- **Internal comps (grep fallback; sem 402):** [[Nubank among bidders for Banco Caixa Geral Brasil]] (prior Nu bank-acquisition attempt), [[Revolut wins initial licence to establish bank in Colombia]] and [[Nubank applies for US banking license]] (the licence-by-charter vs. licence-by-acquisition trade-off), [[Nu Mexico surpasses 13 million customers]] and [[Nubank posts record Q1 2026 revenue above $5 billion]] (Nu's expansion scale).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Deal is unconfirmed and contested** — Inter, named as frontrunner, publicly denies negotiating; Brubank's CEO Juan Bruchou is reported as resisting an exit. Second-order: "evaluating" ≠ signed; any single-source "frontrunner" framing should be treated as leaked positioning, not a done deal.
2. **Buying into the loss cycle** — household delinquency at a 20-year high (12.9%) and non-bank arrears ~33% mean the acquirer inherits a deteriorating book; the "cheap asset" thesis only holds if Argentine macro stabilises. Second-order: underwriting the book correctly (the Brazilians' scoring know-how) is where the value leaks if the cycle worsens.
3. **Change-of-control still needs BCRA approval** — an operating bank accelerates infrastructure but does NOT bypass regulatory sign-off on the ownership transfer; execution/timeline risk remains, and foreign control of a chartered bank is a political variable.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) If any Brazilian bank closes, it signals a consolidation wave: the licence-by-acquisition route becoming the preferred entry into Argentina as the BCRA tightens the intermediation ceiling on CVU-only fintechs — a re-rating of chartered-neobank assets as scarce "entry tickets." Falsifiable thesis: watch for a confirmed signed SPA or a BCRA change-of-control filing within ~6–12 months; what breaks it — all three bidders walk (as Nu did in 2025) and Brubank stays independent, implying the licence premium wasn't enough to overcome the loss-cycle discount. Note a sourcing discrepancy to watch: the note cites ~5M Brubank users while secondary press reports ~4M — reconcile before trusting any per-user valuation math.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Note:** This is an M&A-talks story, not an earnings event. The review below frames Nubank's (Nu Holdings, NYSE: NU) latest reported quarter — Q2 2026, reported 2026-08-13 — as the financial backdrop for the reported Brubank/Argentina interest. Figures are from Nu's own Q2'26 6-K press release.

**Verdict (headline read).** BEAT (modest) · gross revenue ~$5.9bn (+39% YoY); net revenue $5.51bn beat the ~$5.48bn public consensus by ~1% · net income $1.1bn (first-ever quarter above $1bn; +49% YoY FX-neutral, +17% QoQ) · adjusted EPS ~$0.20–0.22 roughly in-line with the ~$0.20 consensus · no formal guidance given. The print is a record on profitability; the surprise is in margin/returns, not in a revenue blowout.

**Key figures (Q2 2026, YoY).**
- Gross revenue: nearly $5.9bn, +39% YoY. Net revenue topped $4bn (reported figure in the release); the aggregator-tracked net revenue line of $5.51bn beat the $5.48bn consensus by ~1.08% (Zacks).
- Gross profit: $2.4bn, +43% YoY, +25% QoQ.
- Net income: $1.1bn ($1.06bn managerial), first time above $1bn; +49% YoY (FX-neutral), +17% QoQ. ROE 33% (vs 29% in Q1'26).
- Net Interest Margin: 22.9%, +180 bps. Risk-adjusted NIM: 12.4% (record), +290 bps, up from 9.5% in Q1'26 — the single largest driver of the profit milestone.
- Adjusted EPS: ~$0.20–0.22; reports conflict ($0.22 vs Zacks $0.20) but it lands roughly in-line, i.e. the beat is on margin, not per-share.

**Operating metrics / geography (directly relevant to the Argentina thesis).**
- Total customers: 138.9mn (Q1'26 was >135mn). Brazil ~118mn (activity rate >86% for the first time); Mexico 15.8mn at quarter-end (16mn by July 2026 — Nu became the largest digital bank in Mexico after launching its full bank there); Colombia >5mn.
- Deposits: $45.3bn, +18% YoY, +6% QoQ.
- ARPAC: ~$17 (more than doubled from ~$8 in Q2 2022).
- Monthly activity rate: 83.5%, up sequentially.

**By driver.** The quarter's acceleration is credit-mix + margin, not just new-customer growth: the intentional risk expansion flagged a quarter earlier fed the 290 bps jump in risk-adjusted NIM and the step to $1bn net income, while customer adds slowed to ~4mn QoQ. Growth outside Brazil (Mexico bank launch, Colombia >5mn) is the structural story — Nu is replaying its Brazil card-led playbook in new LatAm licences.

**vs expectations / prior period.** Revenue beat public consensus by ~1% (net revenue $5.51bn vs $5.48bn, Zacks, "as of" Aug 2026). EPS roughly in-line with ~$0.20. So the beat is quality-of-earnings (margin, ROE 33%), not a top-line surprise. vs Q1'26 (net income $871mn, ROE 29%): clear sequential acceleration on profitability.

**Guidance / forward.** No formal quarterly/annual guidance in the release (consistent with Nu's practice). Management tone confident on margin expansion, unit economics and Mexico/Colombia scaling; they lean on "intentional risk expansions" to justify the NIM step — the thing to watch is whether that credit-mix shift holds asset quality as the book seasons (not broken out in the headline release).

**Thesis-flags — how a Brubank/Argentina deal fits the financials.**
1. Capacity, not stretch. At $1.1bn quarterly net income, 33% ROE and a ~$72bn market cap (per the news item), Nubank can easily fund a small Argentine neobank (~5mn users). Brubank would be a sub-1% add to Nu's 139mn base — strategic optionality (a local banking licence), not a needle-mover on near-term revenue.
2. Playbook fit. The margin engine behind the Q2 beat (card-led, app-first, risk-adjusted NIM 12.4%) is exactly what a licence + 5mn-user Argentine base would let Nu redeploy — but Argentina's macro/credit backdrop (household NPLs cited at 12.9%, a 20-year high) is far riskier than Brazil/Mexico, so the "intentional risk expansion" that powered Q2 would be harder to replicate cleanly.
3. Caveat on intent. The news reports industry interest only; Nu has "tried to land in Argentina several times" but is not confirmed to be in negotiations, and Inter (not Nubank) is named frontrunner. The Q2 strength makes Nu a credible bidder; it does not evidence a deal.
4. Second-order. A fourth LatAm market would extend Nu's regional-network thesis, but Argentina's small, volatile banking system means any accretion is a multi-year option — the Q2 numbers argue Nu has the balance-sheet room to make that bet without denting its record margins.
