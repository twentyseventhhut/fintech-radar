## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened (2026-10-09).** Ramp reportedly raised ~$1.85bn at a **$60bn pre-money** valuation (so ~$61.85bn post-money), led by **Dragoneer Investment Group** and **Thrive Capital**, with **Founders Fund** contributing a large check (Bloomberg, via This Week in Fintech; corroborated by Techmeme/TheNextWeb). This is a genuinely NEW round, not a reprint: it is up from the **$44bn** June 2026 Series F ($750m) — a ~$16bn step-up in ~4 months. Verified fresh.

**Valuation timeline (dated, from prior notes + web).**
- Mar 2025 — ~$13bn (secondary / employee tender)
- Jun 2025 — $16bn, $200m Series E ([[Ramp raises $200M Series E at $16bn valuation]])
- Jul 2025 — $22.5bn, $500m Series E-2 ([[Ramp raises $500 million at $22.5 billion valuation]]); $1B annualized revenue by Sep 2025 ([[Ramp hits $1B annualized revenue at $22.5B valuation]])
- Nov 2025 — $32bn, $300m + employee tender ([[Ramp raises $300M at $32B valuation]]); recapped in [[Ramp raised about $800m at $32bn valuation]]
- May 2026 — WSJ reports talks for $750m at >$40bn pre-money ([[Ramp in talks to raise $750 million at over $40 billion valuation]])
- Jun 2026 — $44bn, $750m Series F; total equity >$3bn; $200bn annualized purchase volume; 70,000+ customers ([[Ramp raises $750M Series F at $44B valuation]])
- **Oct 2026 — $60bn pre-money, ~$1.85bn (this note).**

So the valuation has roughly **4.6x'd in ~16 months** ($13bn → $60bn). The $1.85bn is also Ramp's largest single raise to date, dwarfing the prior $750m.

**Scale / fundamentals (as of Jun 1 2026 PR; most recent hard disclosure).** >$1bn annualized revenue with positive free cash flow (Bloomberg put ARR >$1.5bn by June); $200bn annualized purchase volume; 70,000+ customers (Visa, Uber, Shopify, Figma, Notion, Cursor); 3,200+ customers at ≥$100k ARR; TPV up ~170% YoY in Mar 2026; founded 2019. The thesis investors are paying up for: the AI pivot — "token spend management," accounting agents, Ramp Stack for accounting firms ([[Ramp launches Stack, an AI platform for accounting firms]]), procurement agents, and the Visa autonomous-payments partnership ([[Ramp and Visa deepen partnership on autonomous finance]]).

**Competitive backdrop.** Ramp is now by far the highest-valued corporate-spend player. Its main historical rival, **Brex**, was acquired by **Capital One for $5.15bn** (announced Jan 2026, closed Apr 2026) — down ~58% from Brex's $12.3bn peak ([[News/2025-10/Brex launches stablecoin payment platform supporting Circle USDC]] predates the sale). **Mercury** raised $200m at a $5.2bn valuation (May 2026) and won conditional OCC bank-charter approval ([[Mercury wins conditional OCC approval for bank charter]]). **Navan** went public at only a $6.2bn valuation ([[Navan raises ~$920M in IPO at $6.2B valuation]]). The $60bn mark puts Ramp an order of magnitude above every named peer and reframes the category from "corporate cards" to "AI-native finance OS" ([[Analysis Coinbase and Ramp restructure as AI-native finance firms]], [[Ramp versus Limited global banking and spend comparison]]).

**Caveat / provenance.** The figure is reported ("the report said"), single-sourced to Bloomberg in our feed; Ramp had not issued a PR confirming the close at time of note. A September Bloomberg item described *early talks* for ~$1bn at $60bn — the Oct 9 report is the larger ($1.85bn), apparently-closed version; do not conflate the two.

14. Dilution/total raised: prior round put total equity >$3bn; adding ~$1.85bn → ~$5bn raised. Is capital intensity rising faster than revenue?

**Importance: 4/5** — A ~$16bn valuation jump in four months to $60bn makes Ramp the most valuable private fintech in corporate spend by a wide margin and the clearest AI-native-finance bellwether; materially reprices the whole category (Brex sold, Navan's weak IPO, Mercury an order of magnitude smaller). Held below 5/5 because the item is single-sourced/reported (no Ramp PR confirming the close), and it is one more step in a well-established markup series rather than a new strategic fact.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Spend-management software is a mid-sized, fast-growing niche sitting on top of the vast commercial-card/B2B-payments stack. Per Research and Markets (via researchandmarkets.com, as of 2026), the spend-management platform market is ~$29.19bn in 2026, +13.2% CAGR, heading to ~$45.93bn by 2030; the broader commercial/corporate card market is far larger (coherentmarketinsights.com cites ~$47.7bn 2026 at ~7.5% CAGR). Structure: still fragmented between incumbent issuers (Amex, bank commercial cards), software-first disruptors (Ramp, Brex, Navan), and neobanks drifting into spend (Mercury). Barriers are rising — interchange economics, underwriting/credit, fraud liability and now AI tooling. "Why now": the cohort is re-rating on an "AI-agent for finance ops" narrative (per TechCrunch 2026-06-04 "investors hunger for fintechs with an AI story"); Ramp itself has pushed AI across procurement, Bill Pay and fraud ([[Ramp pushes AI agents across its expense management tools]], [[Ramp launches AI agents across its procurement platform]]).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: ARR/run-rate revenue, net revenue retention, TPV (card + bill-pay volume), take rate, customer count. Players & basis of competition: Ramp vs Brex (direct card/spend rival, now inside Capital One), Navan (T&E-led, public), Mercury (business banking expanding into cards/cash-flow). Competition is shifting from price/rebates toward product breadth (card → AP/bill pay → procurement → travel → treasury) and AI automation. Recent moves: Ramp ~tripled valuation in a year ($22.5bn Jul-2025 → $32bn Nov-2025 → $44bn Jun-2026 → $60bn pre Oct-2026); it has been buying to widen scope ([[Ramp acquires Juno to expand business travel offering]], [[Ramp acquires B2B payments firm Billhop for Europe expansion]], [[Ramp acquires Jolt AI to strengthen AI platform]]). Position: category leader by scale/growth — ~70,000 businesses in June 2026, up from ~50,000 in January (per Bloomberg 2026-10-09) — with a moat built on data/network effects across spend flows and high switching costs once a finance team runs on the platform `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Ramp run-rate revenue reached **$1bn annualized as of 2025-09-04** (IR: "Ramp Reaches $1 Billion in Annualized Revenue", prnewswire.com) and crossed **>$1.5bn by May–June 2026** (per Bloomberg 2026-10-09), i.e. roughly +66% y/y and ~+$500M added in ~9 months.
- Ramp (this round): $60bn pre-money / ~$1.5bn ARR = **~40x** run-rate revenue; post-money ~$61.85bn / $1.5bn ≈ **~41x**.
- [[Mercury raises $200 million at $5.2 billion valuation]] (May-2026): $5.2bn / $650M ARR ([[Mercury hits $3.5 billion valuation and $650 million revenue]]) = **~8.0x**.
- [[Navan raises ~$920M in IPO at $6.2B valuation]] (Oct-2025): $6.2bn / $613M LTM revenue (+32%) = **~10.1x** — public-market mark.
- [[Capital One to acquire Brex for $5.15 billion]] (Jan-2026): $5.15bn take-out; Brex revenue not disclosed here, multiple = no data.
Flag: Ramp at ~40x run-rate is a 4–5x premium to private peer Mercury (~8x) and public Navan (~10x). Even for hyper-growth (+66%), the implied growth-adjusted multiple is rich versus peers growing +30–50%; the gap is carried by the AI-platform narrative, not by disclosed unit economics. NRR, take rate, gross margin and profitability are not disclosed → `[UNSOURCED]`. Distribution not computed as a median (only 2–3 clean comps, none a true like-for-like on both scale and public float); qualitative read: **richly valued vs comps**.

**Risk flags.**
1. Valuation gap — ~40x run-rate vs peers' ~8–10x bakes in near-flawless execution and sustained +60%+ growth; any deceleration re-rates hard, and the step-up from $44bn to $60bn in ~4 months is velocity that can reverse as fast in a cooling cycle (second-order: down-round/IPO-pricing risk).
2. Credit/interchange exposure — the card business depends on interchange economics and underwriting across SMB borrowers; a credit cycle or interchange regulation compresses the core take rate that the SaaS multiple is layered on (margin captured upstream by networks/banks).
3. Incumbent + consolidation pressure — Brex now sits inside Capital One (balance sheet + distribution), Amex/bank commercial cards defend share, and Mercury is pushing into cards; Ramp must out-execute better-capitalized rivals while its "AI moat" is early and partly dependent on third-party model rails `(analysis)`.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a re-rating of spend-management from "software reseller of interchange" toward "AI agent platform for finance ops" (analysis) — Ramp is the bellwether and a likely late-2026/2027 IPO candidate at a mark that sets the ceiling for the whole cohort. Falsifiable thesis: if the ~40x holds at exit, expect a wave of AI-narrative up-rounds across the peer set; trigger/what breaks it — the first disclosed quarter of sub-50% growth or a flat/down private mark, which would collapse the premium back toward the ~8–10x peer band.

**Key figures (disclosed ARR as results proxy, decimal point):**
- Annualized revenue: **$1.0bn** (as of Aug 31, 2025) → **$1.5bn+** (as of June 2026); ≈ +50% over ~10 months (analysis). Reached the first $1bn in ~5 years from launch of the first corporate-card product (founded 2019).
- Annualized purchase (card) volume: **$100bn** (Aug 2025 disclosure).
- Enterprise customers ($100k+ ARR): **1,700**, **+120% YoY** (Aug 2025).
- Treasury AUM: **$1.5bn+** since the Jan 2025 launch; Bill Pay volume "more than tripling YoY" (Aug 2025).
- Operating status: self-reported **operating-cash-flow positive** (Aug 2025).
- Customers: **~45,000** (Aug 2025) → **~50,000** (start-2026) → **70,000+** (June 2026).
- Profit / gross & operating margin / net income / EPS: **no data** (private, undisclosed).

The round is explicitly framed by investors/press as a bet on Ramp becoming an agentic B2B-spend platform, not just a card issuer; segment-level revenue splits are not disclosed.

**Guidance / forward.** None given — private company, no formal guidance. **Thesis-flags.**

4. Disclosure gap: no margin / net income / NRR / churn → de-PR flag — the company touts ARR, purchase volume and "cash-flow positive" but stays silent on unit economics; treat profitability as unverified.
