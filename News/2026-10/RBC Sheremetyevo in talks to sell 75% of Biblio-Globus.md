 РБК: Шереметьево начало переговоры о продаже 75% туроператора «Библио-Глобус» 
эропорт Шереметьево обсуждает возможность продажи 75% компании «Библио-Глобус Туроператор» — одного из крупнейших туроператоров России. Об этом пишет РБК со ссылкой на источники.
По словам одного из собеседников издания, приобретение «Библио-Глобуса» обсуждают в том числе структуры из периметра компаний, связанных с Wildberries & Russ (RWB). При этом обсуждение возможной сделки проходило еще летом, уточнил этот источник.
Другой источник на рынке авиаперевозок рассказал, что потенциальные покупатели предлагали среди прочего тестовый период длиной в несколько месяцев, в течение которого получили бы актив в управление. Третий источник РБК, близкий к одной из сторон сделки, сообщил, что правительство готовит директиву о продаже аэропортом доли в «Библио-Глобусе». «На данный момент планируется, что покупателем доли будет компания, близкая к группе Wildberries», — сказал он.
Еще один источник отметил, что круг претендентов не ограничивается «одним из возможных покупателей». Потенциальная сделка по продаже «Библио-Глобуса» связана с планами Шереметьево рассмотреть возможность участия в приобретении госпакета акций аэропорта по преимущественному праву, заявил один из собеседников РБК.
Как напоминает Forbes , Владимир Путин в августе подписал указ об исключении АО «Международный аэропорт «Шереметьево» (МАШ) из списка стратегических предприятий. Это позволяет приватизировать госпакет (30,43%) компании с сохранением «золотой акции», передавал «Интерфакс».
«Известия» в конце июля сообщали, что RWB после атак БПЛА на склады маркетплейса приостановила инвестиционную программу и поставила на паузу переговоры о приобретении новых активов. Газета ссылалась на инвестбанкира, работающего с компанией, и близкий к RWB источник. В ближайшее время компания намерена завершить «пару крупных тем», после чего сконцентрируется на основном бизнесе, говорил один из источников.
В самой RWB тогда заявили, что сосредоточены на перестройке бизнес-процессов, поддержании оборачиваемости товаров продавцов и помощи селлерам, чьи товары пострадали из-за ударов БПЛА. Как уточнял представитель компании, ресурсы направлены на поддержку тяжело пострадавших и семей погибших, а «абсолютным приоритетом» стали безопасность инфраструктуры и поддержка пострадавших продавцов. Инвестиционные планы в RWB комментировать не стали.
Wildberries уже несколько лет работает над развитием туристического направления. В сентябре 2025 года RWB объявила о заключении соглашения о стратегическом партнерстве с туроператором Fun&Sun. Как сообщила тогда пресс-служба RWB, партнерство с одним из крупнейших туроператоров позволяет «соединить развитую IT-инфраструктуру и рекомендательные алгоритмы цифровой платформы с многолетней экспертизой в сфере туриндустрии».

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened (2026-10-22).** RBC reports Sheremetyevo International Airport (SVO) has opened talks to sell its 75% stake in Biblio-Globus Tour Operator, one of Russia's largest tour operators. Sources name structures close to the Wildberries & Russ group (RWB) among potential buyers, though RBC notes the bidder pool is not limited to one candidate. The government is said to be preparing a directive authorizing the airport to sell the stake. Discussions reportedly began in summer 2026; one proposal included a multi-month "test period" granting the buyer management of the asset before a full purchase. RWB declined to comment.

**What Biblio-Globus is.** Founded in 1994 (originally by Yulia and Alexander Tugolukov), Biblio-Globus is a top-tier Russian tour operator (inbound/outbound/domestic), serving >2M clients/year via ~25,000 partner agencies. FY2025 revenue reportedly rose ~23% to RUB 207.2bn, net profit ~RUB 25bn — a profitable, scaled asset (per tadviser). Figures are media-sourced, not audited filings.

**Ownership chain / why the airport owns a tour operator.** Sheremetyevo acquired a majority (75%) of Biblio-Globus Rus in June 2022 — a vertical tie between the airport and a large flight/package buyer. SVO itself: ~66% held by Sheremetyevo Holding (ultimate owners undisclosed; pre-2022 Cyprus redomiciliation linked to Arkady Rotenberg and partners), with a ~30.43% state stake.

**Deal rationale — the privatization link.** On 2026-08-06 Putin signed a decree removing SVO from the list of strategic enterprises, enabling privatization of the 30.43% state stake while the state keeps a blocking "golden share" (foreign buyers barred; buyer must continue core business). RBC's sources say the Biblio-Globus sale is tied to Sheremetyevo's plan to use its pre-emptive right to participate in buying that state airport stake — i.e. the airport monetizes a non-core asset to help fund consolidation of its own equity.

**Wildberries/RWB travel angle.** RWB (controlled by Tatyana Kim) has been building a travel vertical: Sep 2025 strategic partnership with tour operator Fun&Sun, and WB Travel launched dynamic domestic tour packages ([[WB Travel launches dynamic Russia tour packages]]). Acquiring Biblio-Globus would be a major leap, pairing WB's platform/IT with an established operator. Caveat: Izvestia reported (late July 2026) that after UAV strikes on RWB warehouses, RWB paused its investment program and new-asset talks — a stated-intent contradiction worth weighing.

**Internal priors / RWB M&A pattern:** [[WB Travel launches dynamic Russia tour packages]] · [[Wildberries RVB to acquire Russian online pharmacy Eapteka]] · [[VTB to sell former Otkritie office to Wildberries-Russ]] · [[WB Bank launches savings accounts and consumer loans]]. Fintech relevance is indirect: the story matters as RWB/Kim-ecosystem M&A and as part of the Sheremetyevo privatization, not as a payments/banking event per se.

Sources: mergers.ru (RBC relay); tadviser (Biblio-Globus financials); rb.ru, expert.ru, business-gazeta.ru, aex.ru (RBC relays); NV/TASS/akm.ru (SVO privatization decree); russiabusinesstoday / rusprofile (2022 acquisition, ownership).

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is this a signed/announced deal or just talks? All reporting is "negotiations"/"may sell" from anonymous RBC sources — no binding agreement, price, or closing date. Headline "in talks" is accurate; treat as speculative.
2. Is the buyer actually Wildberries/RWB? RBC explicitly says the candidate pool is "not limited to one possible buyer." RWB declined to comment. Attribution to Wildberries is one scenario, not confirmed.
3. Does RWB even have appetite right now? Izvestia (late July 2026) reported RWB paused its investment program and new-acquisition talks after UAV strikes on warehouses. This directly undercuts the "Wildberries buyer" thesis — is the leak stale or has appetite revived?
4. What is the price / valuation? No deal value disclosed. For an operator with ~RUB 207bn revenue the stake could be large, but no figure exists — any valuation is a guess.
5. Are the FY2025 financials (RUB 207.2bn rev, RUB 25bn NP) reliable? Media-sourced (tadviser), not audited filings surfaced here. Revenue for a tour operator largely passes through to suppliers; "revenue" ≠ margin — treat the headline number cautiously.
6. Does the government directive exist? RBC says the government is "preparing" a directive — anticipatory, not published. No official confirmation cited.
7. Is the privatization-link rationale confirmed? That SVO sells Biblio-Globus to fund buying its own state stake is a single-source narrative ("one of RBC's interlocutors"). Plausible but unverified causality.
8. Golden-share / foreign-buyer constraints — do they bind this sub-asset or only the airport equity? The Aug 2026 decree restrictions apply to the SVO stake; their applicability to a Biblio-Globus sale is not established.
9. When did SVO acquire 75%? External sources say June 2022; the note's own source doesn't state the acquisition date. Verify the 75% figure refers to the same legal entity being sold ("Biblio-Globus Tour Operator" vs "Biblio-Globus Rus" vs "BG Agent" — multiple entities exist).
10. Ownership of SVO itself (Rotenberg-linked Sheremetyevo Holding, ~66%) — relevant to who benefits, but is it current? Pre-2022 attribution; post-redomiciliation ultimate owners are undisclosed.
11. Is there a fintech angle at all? Only indirect (RWB ecosystem M&A, WB Travel/WB Bank). Risk of overstating relevance to a fintech radar.
12. Fun&Sun partnership (Sep 2025) — does buying Biblio-Globus conflict/overlap with that existing WB travel tie-up? Suggests either consolidation or that WB is multi-sourcing; worth flagging.
13. Timeline consistency: talks "since summer," decree Aug 6, report Oct 22 — is anything actually imminent, or is this a slow-moving story being re-surfaced?
14. Sanctions/geopolitical overlay (airport privatization amid war financing) could stall or reshape any deal — execution risk is high and not price-able.

**Importance: 3/5 — rationale.** A genuinely notable Russian M&A/corporate-restructuring story: a top-5 tour operator potentially changing hands, tied to the politically significant Sheremetyevo privatization and the Wildberries/Kim ecosystem's travel push. But for a FINTECH radar it is peripheral (tourism/infrastructure, not payments/banking), it is early-stage talks with no confirmed buyer/price, and the leading buyer thesis is contradicted by RWB's own reported investment pause. Newsworthy as RWB-ecosystem signal, capped at 3 by stage uncertainty and weak fintech nexus.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russian outbound tourism is a growth market: trips abroad rose +16% YoY to 20.1m in 2025 (per TASS, as of 2026). Paid services of travel agencies/tour operators reached RUB 398bn in 11M 2025, +18% YoY vs RUB 337bn (per Rosstat-cited data, via Russian Travel Digest). Biblio-Globus itself grew revenue +23.2% to RUB 207.2bn with net profit +25.6% to RUB 25.0bn in 2025 (per TAdviser). Structure: the tour-operator segment is fragmented among a handful of large players — Biblio-Globus, Anex, Coral Travel, Pegas Touristik, Fun&Sun — competing on destination inventory (charter seats, hotel allotments) and price; low software/IP barriers, high working-capital and charter-logistics barriers. Why now: a corporate-finance trigger, not a sector cycle — Putin removed Sheremetyevo (MASH) from the strategic-enterprise list in Aug 2026, enabling privatization of the 30.43% state stake (with a "golden share"); the airport reportedly wants to free cash to bid for that state package by its pre-emptive right, so Biblio-Globus (a non-core 2021 purchase) becomes a divestiture candidate (per RBC/Forbes, via note).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: tourists served/year, charter-seat and hotel-allotment volume, package GMV, take rate, repeat-booking rate. Key players: Biblio-Globus (~3m customers/yr, described as Russia's largest operator), Anex, Coral Travel, Pegas Touristik, Fun&Sun — basis of competition is destination inventory + distribution + price. Recent moves: Wildberries/RWB has been building a tourism vertical for ~2 years — strategic partnership with Fun&Sun (Sep 2025); WB Travel branded hotels opened in Egypt (Feb 2026) and Turkey (May 2026, 4 objects); WB Travel dynamic domestic tour packages launched (Oct 2026, see [[WB Travel launches dynamic Russia tour packages]]); unit headed by Taras Demura, ex-Fun&Sun board chair. Protagonist's position (buyer-side read): RWB is catching up in travel via partnership + acqui-build; buying Biblio-Globus would be a step up to #1-operator scale and vertical integration (marketplace demand + recommendation algos → tour-operator supply). Its moat would be distribution/network effects from the Wildberries user base, not travel-ops expertise `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal price not disclosed for the 75% stake. Anchor: Sheremetyevo *bought* the same 75% in 2021 for RUB 410m (~$5.7m at Oct-2021 CBR rate) (per Russian Travel Digest). Against 2025 revenue of RUB 207.2bn / net profit RUB 25.0bn, the 2021 implied equity value (RUB ~547m for 100%) looks trivial — likely the business carried little attributable book equity / was pass-through in 2021, or revenue is gross package turnover not net margin; a 2026 arm's-length price would almost certainly be a large multiple of RUB 410m `(analysis)`. EV/Revenue / P/E = no data (no current price; and gross vs net revenue basis is unclear → do not compute). Internal comps (grep fallback; semsearch 402-down): [[WB Travel launches dynamic Russia tour packages]] (same buyer, same travel vertical, 2026); [[Tripadvisor sells TheFork to American Express for $700 million]] (2026 — a non-travel platform buyer absorbing a travel-vertical asset, analogous logic). Distribution not computed — qualitative only, 2 comps. Rich/cheap: cannot assess — price undisclosed `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Risk flags.**
1. **State/political gatekeeping.** Reporting says the government is preparing a *directive* for the airport to sell, and the buyer is "a company close to Wildberries" — i.e. the price and counterparty may be set administratively rather than by open auction, so the stake could change hands below fair value and/or be blocked if privatization politics shift `(hypothesis)`.
2. **Buyer execution / financing wobble.** RWB reportedly paused its M&A/investment program in summer 2026 after UAV strikes on its warehouses, redirecting resources to infrastructure security and affected sellers; a buyer with a stressed balance sheet and paused acquisitions is a soft, possibly non-binding counterparty — talks were "under discussion since summer" with no signed terms (per Izvestia/RBC, via note).
3. **Deal structure = low-commitment "test" control.** One source says buyers proposed a several-month management test period before buying — a conditional, reversible arrangement that signals low conviction and raises the odds the deal lapses `(analysis)`.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** If it closes, this is consolidation + platform vertical integration: Russia's largest marketplace absorbing its largest tour operator, turning WB from a travel distributor into a travel principal `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: a binding sale signs within ~6 months at a disclosed price. Trigger to watch: publication of the government directive and a named buyer entity; the thesis breaks if RWB's investment-pause persists or the state keeps the stake for the privatization bid itself.
