Etisalat money partners with Visa to expand cross-border money movement capabilities for consumers and businesses
Partnership will introduce new ways to send money abroad, including transfers to eligible cards and using recipients’ phone numbers
Partnership builds on etisalat money’s leading international transfer services across more than 150 countries
 Dubai, UAE: etisalat money, the fintech arm of etisalat, has announced a strategic partnership with Visa to expand international money transfer options for consumers and businesses in the UAE through Visa Direct, Visa’s global money movement platform,
The collaboration is designed to support a broader range of cross-border payment use cases, including person-to-person remittances and small business international payments, through Visa Direct's global money movement network. It will in also aim to introduce new cross-border payment capabilities within the etisalat money app, giving customers a secure and convenient way of sending money abroad.
The partnership was formalised during a signing ceremony at Visa’s Central and Eastern Europe, Middle East and Africa (CEMEA) headquarters in Dubai Internet City. It brings together etisalat money's digital financial services proposition and Visa Direct's global money movement capabilities, creating new opportunities for customers and businesses to send funds across borders through bank accounts, digital wallets and eligible cards.
Melike Kara Tanrikulu, Chief Executive Officer of etisalat money, said: “International money transfers are a critical part of how people and businesses stay connected across borders. As customer expectations evolve, the opportunity lies in offering greater choice and flexibility in how money moves. Our partnership with Visa Direct is a strategic step in broadening our international payment capabilities and strengthening our position in the digital financial services market. It also reinforces our focus on delivering practical financial solutions that meet the needs of our customers and support our long-term growth.”
Fadi Moukaddem, Senior Vice President and Group Country Manager for the UAE, Kuwait and Qatar at Visa: "In money movement, trust is one of the most important currencies of success, and at Visa, we have spent decades building trusted and secure ways to connect people, businesses and economies. By combining Visa Direct's global network and capabilities with etisalat money's digital financial services proposition, we have an opportunity to provide consumers and businesses with greater choice in how money is sent and received internationally."
Through the partnership, etisalat money plans to introduce four additional international transfer capabilities. Visa Direct to Card will enable customers to send funds directly to eligible recipients’ cards using their card numbers. Visa+, an innovative service that aims to help individuals move money quickly and securely between different person-to-person (P2P) digital payment apps, will simplify eligible transfers by allowing customers to use a recipient’s phone number instead of traditional banking credentials.
The synergy will also support personal-to-business transfers, allowing eligible customers to send money internationally from their personal etisalat money accounts to business accounts. In addition, SWIFT transfers will further expand the range of international payment options available through the app.These capabilities will complement etisalat money’s existing international money transfer services, which currently reach more than 150 countries. Customers can already send money through multiple payout methods, including bank accounts, digital wallets and cash pickup. The app also provides live exchange rates, estimated transfer times and transaction tracking, helping customers manage their transfers with greater visibility.
With both entities coming together, it will initially support transfers to key remittance corridors including India, Egypt, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Philippines, United Kingdom and China, while also enabling access to digital-wallet destinations across multiple markets. By connecting accounts, digital wallets and eligible cards through Visa Direct, the partnership is intended to support etisalat money's broader cross-border growth ambitions.
By bringing additional payment methods into a single digital platform, etisalat money aims to address the diverse needs of the UAE’s internationally connected population. The expanded offering will allow customers to select transfer methods based on their preferences and recipients’ requirements, while reducing the need to use multiple services for different payment needs.
The partnership reflects a shared commitment by etisalat money and Visa to support the continued evolution of digital money movement experiences in the UAE and beyond. Together, the companies aim to make international payments more connected, digital and accessible for consumers and businesses alike.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
etisalat money (the fintech arm of UAE telco etisalat / e& — the group's licensed digital wallet, ex-"eWallet", rebranded e& money in 2022) signed a partnership with Visa to route outbound international transfers over **Visa Direct**, Visa's global money-movement platform. The deal was formalised at Visa's CEMEA HQ in Dubai Internet City (announced ~22 Oct 2026).

De-PR'd: this is an **"aims to / plans to introduce"** announcement, not a live launch. etisalat money "plans to introduce four additional international transfer capabilities":
- **Visa Direct to Card** — push funds to an eligible recipient card via card number.
- **Visa+** — P2P-app interoperability using a recipient's "payname"/phone-number-style alias instead of bank credentials.
- **Personal-to-business** transfers (from a personal etisalat money account to a business account).
- **SWIFT** transfers added inside the app.
Initial corridors named: **India, Egypt, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Philippines, UK, China**.

Why structured this way / what it reveals: etisalat money already claims international transfers to **150+ countries** via bank accounts, digital wallets and cash pickup — so the press release is not "we entered remittances", it is "we are **plumbing in Visa's rails** (card-push + Visa+ + SWIFT) on top of an existing payout network". No fees, FX margin, go-live date, transfer caps, user counts or volume are disclosed. The vague "will aim to introduce" and the absence of a live date are the tell: treat every capability as **roadmap, not shipped**. Note the brand wobble — the PR says "etisalat money … fintech arm of etisalat", while the group markets the same entity as "e& money" (fintech arm of e& life); same wallet, inconsistent naming. (analysis)

## [1] Competitors / peers
UAE outbound remittance market ≈ **$39B in 2025** (one estimate; >$48B outflow in 2024), ~90% expat population. Exchange houses still handle **~80%+** of personal remittances: **Al Ansari** (largest branch network), **LuLu Exchange / LuLuFin (LuLu Money)**, Al Fardan, UAE Exchange/Unimoni. Digital challengers: **Botim** (CBUAE-licensed wallet, 200+ countries, incl. India UPI payout), **Careem Pay** (partnered LuLu Exchange for UAE→Pakistan), and now **Wise** (received UAE CBUAE SVF + retail-payments licences, Oct 2025 — see [[Wise gets UAE Central Bank approval for products]]).

Position: etisalat money is **catching up / parity-seeking**, not leading. It is doing what Botim already does (telco/app wallet bolting on remittance) and what Careem Pay did via an exchange-house partner — except etisalat money is partnering **Visa** rather than an exchange house, i.e. betting on card-push + Visa+ rather than on cash-pickup corridors. Why the landscape is this way: exchange houses own the **cash-pickup last mile** and trust in South Asia corridors; digital wallets win on UX and existing installed base but must rent rails (Visa Direct, SWIFT) and still lean on correspondent/payout networks for the final leg. Second-order: the real contest is **digital-channel share** (reportedly up from 14%→37% of transaction volume 2020–2025) — wallets are grabbing flow from exchange houses, so Visa's rails are the picks-and-shovels sold to every challenger at once, not an edge unique to etisalat. (analysis)

## [2] Company history / fit
Etisalat launched the UAE's first CBUAE-licensed mobile wallet in 2016; eWallet (with Noor Bank, 2020) → rebranded **e& money** (2022) → prepaid card (2023, 180k+ cards issued early on) → **first UAE wallet to link PayPal** (announced Oct 2025). CEO **Melike Kara Tanrikulu**. This Visa Direct tie-up fits the pattern: a telco "super-app/financial marketplace" strategy that keeps **stacking third-party rails** (PayPal, now Visa Direct + Visa+ + SWIFT) rather than building its own. Why: a telco wallet monetises an existing subscriber base and bill-pay habit; remittance is the highest-frequency, highest-stickiness expat use case, so adding corridors + card-push is the logical way to deepen wallet engagement without owning payment infrastructure. Compare regional peer [[Ooredoo Fintech to join PayPal World]] — same telco-wallet-plugs-into-global-rails playbook. (analysis)

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Novelty is **low**. Visa Direct card-push, SWIFT and multi-corridor payout are commodity capabilities; Visa+ is the only semi-novel piece (P2P-app interoperability via payname), but Visa+ itself is **largely US-centric and still thinly adopted** (PayPal/Venmo trial, partners DailyPay/i2c/TabaPay/Western Union) — so "Visa+ into UAE remittance corridors" is **more announcement than proven rail**, and no UAE go-live is confirmed. Traction: **zero disclosed** — no users-on-the-new-rails, no volume, no fees. Everything is "plans to". Who captures the margin: on card-push, **Visa takes interchange/network economics**; etisalat money captures FX spread + any send fee but **rents the rails**, so its durable edge is distribution (subscriber base + app install), not the payment layer. The value-add is real only if it meaningfully lowers cost/latency vs Botim/exchange houses — unconfirmed. (analysis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch for a **dated go-live** and actual fee/FX disclosure; until then this is positioning. Structural tailwind: UAE remittance market growing ~4.85% CAGR to ~$52B by 2031 and rapid **digital-share shift** favour wallets. Regulatory backdrop: CBUAE SVF/retail-payments licensing is maturing (Wise approval, Botim licensed) — a crowding field. Counterintuitive second-order: by leaning on Visa Direct + Visa+, etisalat money **deepens dependence on Visa's rails and economics** exactly as agentic/P2P money-movement consolidates around the card networks — it wins distribution but concedes the margin-capturing layer; its moat stays "the app people already have", which Botim and Careem Pay also claim. Risk: four simultaneous roadmap items ("aims to introduce") with no date = execution/slippage risk and possible PR front-running. (analysis)

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** The UAE is a top-3 global outbound remittance market — ~$43bn sent annually (per secondary data, via remit.ae/uaeinform, as of 2026), concentrated in Gulf→South Asia corridors; this deal explicitly targets India, Egypt, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Philippines, UK and China, which map to the UAE's largest expat-worker flows (note text). Structure: fragmented at the consumer front-end (exchange houses + banks + a crowd of wallets) but the *rail* layer is consolidating onto a few global networks — Visa Direct, Mastercard Move, SWIFT, Thunes/TerraPay. Value is migrating from cash-pickup/exchange-house counters toward in-app digital payout; mobile wallets now reportedly account for >20% of UAE remittance transactions (per secondary data, via search, as of 2026). Why now: Central Bank of the UAE licensing (SVF/RPS) is normalizing wallet-based cross-border, and Visa Direct push-to-card/wallet gives wallets an off-the-shelf global reach without building 150-country payout networks one-by-one.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: # of payout corridors/countries, payout-method breadth (card/account/wallet/cash), FX spread + fee take, and settlement speed. Key players in the UAE consumer wallet-remittance race: e& money, Careem Pay, Payit (FAB), Botim, Ziina — plus pure-play remitters (Taptap Send, Wise) and incumbent exchange houses (Al Ansari, LuLu). Basis of competition is distribution + corridor coverage + price, not technology (the rail is rented). Recent moves: Careem Pay expanded to 35+ countries covering the same India/Pakistan/Egypt/Philippines corridors (Jan 2026); [[Taptap Send launches money transfers in Bahrain]] added Bahrain as its 2nd GCC market after the UAE (Oct 2026); [[Wise gets UAE Central Bank approval for products]] put a low-cost incumbent directly in-market (Oct 2025). etisalat money's position: catching up on digital-wallet remittance via a well-trodden partnership model — Visa says it reaches 150+ countries already, and Visa Direct adds card/Visa+/P2B capability. Moat is thin on the rail (anyone can integrate Visa Direct — see [[Paysend expands Visa partnership for cross-border payments]], 100+ markets since 2023) and rests mainly on etisalat's telco distribution/brand to the UAE's migrant base (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/financials disclosed for etisalat money — it is the fintech arm of etisalat (e&), not a standalone fundraise — so EV/Rev, P/S, price-per-user all "no data". **Risk flags.**
1. Rail dependence / disintermediation — etisalat money is building on someone else's stack (Visa Direct, SWIFT). The economics (who keeps the FX spread, Visa's per-transaction cut, fraud liability) are undisclosed; margin can be captured by the rail layer, and Visa can offer the same to every rival wallet.
2. Crowded, price-led home market — Careem Pay, Payit, Wise, Botim and exchange houses all chase the identical India/Pakistan/Egypt/Philippines corridors; remittance is a thin-margin, fee-transparent commodity where cheaper, dedicated players (Wise, Taptap Send) compress spreads. Distribution, not product, decides share.
3. Announced vs live — the PR describes planned capabilities ("plans to introduce four additional... transfer capabilities": Visa Direct to Card, Visa+, P2B, SWIFT); no launch dates, volumes, pricing or go-live given, so adoption is unproven.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Incremental, not a re-rating: telco-wallet + global-rail is becoming the default UAE remittance playbook, and Visa Direct is quietly winning the GCC infrastructure layer regardless of which wallet wins the consumer (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if etisalat money's telco distribution converts into real corridor share, watch for disclosed transfer volumes or app-MAU remittance metrics within 2-3 quarters; absence of any usage number by mid-2027 would signal the partnership stayed a press release. ## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**No earnings print here — product partnership only.** This item is a strategic partnership between etisalat money (fintech arm of e& / etisalat, UAE) and Visa to add Visa Direct–powered cross-border transfer options to the etisalat money app. There are no etisalat-money-specific financials in the news (etisalat money is a private UAE business unit and does not report standalone results) — no revenue, volume, take-rate or user figures. So there is no beat/miss to assess; the only quantifiable layer is the size of the Visa Direct rail the deal rides on.

**Rail sizing — Visa Direct / cross-border (Visa's own latest reported, context only).** Visa's most recent reported quarter is fiscal Q3 2026 (quarter ended 2026-06-30, reported late July 2026); fiscal Q4/full-year 2026 is not yet released as of 2026-10-23, so figures here are Q3 FY2026 and are context, not a read on this deal:
- Visa Direct transactions: +21% YoY to 4.0 billion in fiscal Q3 2026 (growth in both domestic and cross-border flows). Prior quarter Q1 FY2026 was +23% YoY to 3.7 billion. Full-year 2025 ran ~12.5 billion Visa Direct transactions.
- Cross-border volume: +12% YoY in constant dollars in fiscal Q3 2026, while international transaction revenue grew only +6% YoY — i.e. reported cross-border revenue lagged volume (FX / incentive / mix drag).
- Commercial & money movement solutions revenue (the segment that houses Visa Direct): +17% YoY in constant dollars in fiscal Q3 2026 (was +20% in Q1 FY2026).
- Total payments volume crossed $4 trillion for the first time (+10% YoY constant dollars) in fiscal Q3 2026.

**What the rail numbers imply for the partnership (analysis).** Visa Direct is a high-growth but still-small "new flows" engine for Visa — the ~21% transaction growth and +12% cross-border volume show the network etisalat money is plugging into is scaling. The partnership gives etisalat money four new capabilities (Visa Direct to Card, Visa+, personal-to-business, SWIFT) layered on its existing 150+ country remittance footprint, initially targeting India, Egypt, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Philippines, UK and China corridors. For Visa this is incremental distribution in a high-remittance market (UAE); for etisalat money it is reach/optionality, not a disclosed revenue event. The one caution the Visa numbers flag: cross-border revenue growth (+6%) is running well below volume growth (+12%), so "more volume" does not translate 1:1 into economics on this rail.

**Bottom line:** context-only. Product partnership, no etisalat-money earnings data; no beat/miss. Visa Direct rail figures above are Visa's own fiscal Q3 2026 and are illustrative of rail scale, not of this deal's contribution.
