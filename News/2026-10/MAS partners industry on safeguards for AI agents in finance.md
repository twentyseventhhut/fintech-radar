# MAS Partners Industry to develop Safeguards for AI Agents in Finance

Singapore, 3 July 2026… The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS), together with leading financial institutions and FinTechs, today published an industry white paper on developing safeguards for AI agents in Finance. Titled “Safeguards for Agentic Finance at Runtime (SAFR)”, the paper proposes an industry-developed framework that enables AI agents in financial services to carry out financial tasks safely, securely and reliably. SAFR is developed under MAS’ BuildFin.ai BuildFin.ai is a collaborative initiative that brings together financial institutions, technology providers, and research institutes to co-develop AI solutions that address real market needs. Please see MAS’ Financial AI Builder Programme - Buildfin.ai for more information. initiative, which supports the responsible development and deployment of AI solutions in the financial sector.   
  
 2. As AI agents in financial services increasingly carry out tasks autonomously and at speed beyond practical human intervention, financial institutions need real-time safeguards to ensure that the behaviour of AI agents remain within predefined mandates, policies and risk boundaries set by financial institutions. The SAFR framework provides for a set of governance checkpoints that verifies and records an AI agent’s proposed actions before the execution of its tasks.   
  
 3. SAFR builds on MAS’ Project Mindforge’s AI Risk Management toolkit, with a focus on how safeguards can be operationalised at the point of action for AI agents. The SAFR white paper sets out the direction for how these safeguards, including policy bound execution, real time validation, auditability and interoperability, can be embedded into system operations so that financial institutions can deploy AI agents with trust and consistency.  
  
 4. Industry members have applied the SAFR framework across use cases, including:

*   Agent-assisted payments and treasury operations, where autonomous agents can execute routine transactions within predefined mandates, improving efficiency and reducing operational frictions;
*   Wealth management and advisory workflows, where AI agents review documents and generate structured assessments within narrowly scoped task boundaries, supporting faster and more consistent compliance review;
*   Client engagement, where AI agents generate client insights and draft materials within approved content boundaries, enabling staff to engage clients more effectively and productively.

5. Interested industry partners are invited to join the BuildFin.ai work group to contribute to and help shape subsequent iterations of SAFR. The recently announced Future of Finance Institute (FFI)MAS Establishes Future of Finance Institute to Scale Financial Innovation will support future adoption of the SAFR framework through the facilitation of industry pilots and sandbox experimentation. This will help financial institutions to test and deploy SAFR-aligned solutions. Expression of interest can be made to MAS here.

# Context-enrichment: MAS partners industry on safeguards for AI agents in finance (SAFR)
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

> **DATE / FRESHNESS FLAG:** The underlying announcement — the **SAFR white paper** — was published by MAS on **3 July 2026**, NOT 18 Oct 2026. The note was only *ingested* today (the digest re-surfaced it, likely off the 9 Oct 2026 INSEAD Digital Finance & Agentic AI Summit keynote in "Related News"). The event itself is ~3.5 months old. It is **not** a duplicate of any prior note, but it is stale as a "today" item. See [3]/freshness.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On **3 July 2026** MAS, with **eight** named industry members, published an **industry white paper** (not a rule, not a Notice, not Guidelines) titled **"Safeguards for Agentic Finance at Runtime (SAFR)," Version 1.0**. It is a **voluntary, industry-developed framework** for governing *AI agents* at the moment of action.
- **Named contributors (8):** Ant International, Circle, HSBC, J.P. Morgan Chase, Manulife, Mastercard, OCBC, Visa. (Full list in the paper's Annex A.) Note the mix: two global cards networks (Visa, Mastercard), a stablecoin issuer (Circle), two Singapore-anchored banks (OCBC + HSBC's Asia hub), a US money-center bank (JPM), an insurer (Manulife), and Alipay's cross-border arm (Ant International).
- **What SAFR actually is:** a set of **governance checkpoints** that *verify and record an AI agent's proposed action BEFORE it executes*. Four safeguard classes: **(1) policy-bound execution** (agent stays inside predefined mandates/risk boundaries), **(2) real-time validation** (checks at the point of action), **(3) auditability** (every decision/escalation logged), **(4) interoperability** (so safeguards travel across systems/vendors).
- **Lineage:** builds on **Project MindForge's** AI Risk Management toolkit, pushing it from *model-level* oversight to **runtime / point-of-action** control. Sits under MAS's **BuildFin.ai** program (the collaborative FI + tech-provider + research-institute vehicle).
- **Applied use cases cited (framed as "applied," read as pilots/illustrations, not production at scale):** agent-assisted payments & treasury within predefined mandates; wealth/advisory document review within scoped task boundaries; client-engagement content generation within approved boundaries.
- **Adoption vehicle:** the **Future of Finance Institute (FFI)**, announced **25 Jun 2026**, will run industry pilots + sandbox experimentation for SAFR and maintain the AI Risk Management Toolkit + a Programmable Compliance Toolkit. Real-world implementations reported as starting **late 2026, accelerating through 2027**.
- **Why structured this way (the red-team core):** This is the **soft/voluntary twin** of MAS's *binding* work. Two weeks after the hard-edged, timetabled **Guidelines on AI Risk Management** (finalized 7 Oct 2026 — see [[MAS mandates new AI risk rules for financial firms]]), SAFR is the **"here's HOW to comply" co-design layer**. MAS deliberately splits regulator-sets-expectation (Guidelines) from industry-builds-the-plumbing (SAFR/BuildFin.ai): it gets operational buy-in and a de-facto standard **without owning the technical liability** if the controls fail. The 8-member roster is a who's-pays-the-agent signal: Visa+Mastercard+Circle on the rail side means MAS is steering the standard before the agentic-payments rails ossify around US/private protocols.

## [1] Competitors / peers (regulators & standards)
- **EU AI Act** — the prescriptive, binding benchmark; high-risk obligations (credit scoring, access-to-finance AI) bite **2 Aug 2026**, with mandatory human oversight, logging, conformity assessments, fines to €15M/3% turnover. SAFR is *voluntary and complementary* — it tells you HOW to operationalize human-in-the-loop that the EU Act *requires*.
- **US (Fed/OCC/FDIC)** — replaced SR 11-7 with **SR 26-2** (17 Apr 2026); examiners now probe agent kill-switches, human-in-the-loop checkpoints, data lineage, subcontractor chains (venturemagazine, 2026). Principles/supervisory, no bespoke runtime spec.
- **UK (FCA/PRA)** — principles-based, no AI rulebook; criticized Jan 2026 for a wait-and-see stance ([[The Treasury Select Committee warned of a failure to tackle AI risks]]).
- **Industry prior art on agentic safeguards:** [[Worldpay partners Trulioo for AI agent commerce safeguards]] (Aug 2025 — trust/consent/accountability at the identity layer); [[Nuvei supports Visa Trusted Agent Protocol]] and Visa/Mastercard "Agent Pay"/"Trusted Agent Protocol" ([[PayPal integrates Mastercard Agent Pay into wallet]], [[Mastercard and PayPal partner on agentic commerce]]) — the **rail-level** agentic-payments standards SAFR's two card-network contributors also drive.
- **Position:** MAS is an **early mover specifically on *runtime* agentic governance** — a narrower, more operational slice than EU/US model-risk regimes. **(analysis)** Its edge is *interoperability framing* + getting Visa/MC/Circle/JPM to co-sign: a standard with those names has a real shot at becoming the Asian (and possibly cross-border) default. Second-order: whoever sets the runtime-checkpoint spec shapes *where the human-override and audit hooks sit* — and therefore who carries fraud/liability when an agent misfires.

## [2] MAS history / fit
MAS has a dense, consistent agentic-AI/governance trajectory, so SAFR is on-strategy, not opportunistic:
- **Project MindForge** (GenAI risk framework, 2023–) → SAFR's direct parent.
- **[[MAS launches PathFin.ai hub for financial AI adoption]]** (Oct 2025, 80+ FIs sharing AI deployment practice).
- **[[MAS mandates new AI risk rules for financial firms]]** (Guidelines finalized 7 Oct 2026) — the binding sibling.
- **Project Guardian** ([[UK and Singapore investment bodies join MAS Project Guardian]], [[Project Guardian advances tokenized settlement initiative]]) — the tokenization analog of the same playbook: MAS convenes global FIs → co-develops → exports a standard.
- Live agentic deployments in-market it is regulating: [[Bank of Singapore deploys agentic AI tool SOWA]] (Oct 2025).
- **Why MAS acts this way:** Singapore competes as a *trusted* financial hub, not the cheapest or biggest. Its structural play is **"regulated innovation" as an export** — convene the global names, co-author the standard, let FFI run the sandbox, and make Singapore the reference jurisdiction for agentic finance before the EU's extraterritorial Act becomes the only game in town.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new:** the **runtime / point-of-action** framing — governing the agent's *proposed action before execution*, not just validating the model upstream. That is a real conceptual step beyond MindForge and beyond most 2024–25 "AI governance" frameworks (which are model/lifecycle-centric). The four classes (policy-bound exec, real-time validation, auditability, interoperability) are a coherent spec, not a platitude.
- **But traction is thin:** it is a **Version 1.0 white paper** with "applied" use cases that read as **pilots/illustrations**, not production volume. No GMV, no live client counts, no deployed-agent numbers. Real implementations only start "late 2026" per coverage — i.e. *announced, not adopted*. FFI pilots are the adoption test, still ahead.
- **Where the value/margin sits (analysis):** the durable value isn't the PDF — it's **the standard + who controls the checkpoint layer**. If SAFR's interoperability wins, the agent-orchestration/audit vendors and the rails (Visa/MC/Circle) that embed the checkpoints capture value; FIs get reduced liability and faster deployment. The risk to the value-add: competing *private* runtime specs (Visa Trusted Agent Protocol, Mastercard Agent Pay, vendor MCP servers) could fragment the layer SAFR tries to standardize — the same contributors who co-sign SAFR also push their own rails.

## [4] What's next / sentiment
- **Next:** FFI-facilitated pilots + sandbox through late-2026→2027; BuildFin.ai work group open for more contributors; subsequent SAFR iterations (v1.1+). SAFR adoption dovetails with the binding Guidelines' 7 Oct 2027/2028 phase-ins — FIs will likely use SAFR as the *evidence template* for the Guidelines' human-oversight/auditability expectations.
- **Sentiment:** broadly positive among law/consulting commentators (Allen & Gledhill, Ashurst, A-Team) framing it as a global-first *runtime* standard; skeptics (e.g. ianloe/Medium) note it mostly re-states governance points made since 2024.
- **Risks / counterintuitive second-order:** (1) voluntary = **adoption risk** — a well-designed standard nobody operationalizes. (2) **Standard fragmentation** — the rail owners' own protocols could undercut SAFR interoperability. (3) By offloading the "how" to industry, MAS keeps the *standard-setting* leverage but **distances itself from liability** if a SAFR-"compliant" agent still causes loss — a gap that only surfaces after a real incident.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** This sits in AI-governance / RegTech for agentic AI in financial services — the "runtime safeguards" layer that sits between a bank's AI agents and live execution. Sizing is for the broader agentic-AI-in-banking adjacency (SAFR itself is a non-commercial framework): per BofA Global Research, total agentic-AI spend could reach ~$155bn by 2030 (via Yahoo Finance, Oct 2026); the agentic-AI-in-banking segment was ~$4.3bn in 2026 per Globe Market Research, and the narrower "AI agents in financial services" tooling market ~$985m in 2026 (Precedence Research) — both private vendor-report figures, so treat as order-of-magnitude, not verified TAM. Structure: emerging and fragmented — governance/assurance is being set partly by regulators (MAS, EU AI Act) and partly by vendors (observability, policy engines, agent-identity). Barriers are regulatory legitimacy and integration into FI risk stacks rather than capital. "Why now": (1) banks are moving from AI experimentation to governed deployment in 2026; (2) agents now act "at speed beyond practical human intervention" (MAS), so point-in-time model validation (SR 11-7-style) no longer covers the runtime decision — this is the "pre-execution assurance gap" SAFR is built around.

**Competitive landscape.** The thing to track here is not revenue KPIs (MAS is a regulator, SAFR is free) but adoption/legitimacy KPIs: number and tier of FI adopters, whether peer regulators converge on a compatible runtime standard, and whether SAFR becomes de-facto supervisory expectation. Competing/overlapping frameworks: the EU AI Act (credit scoring, fraud, automated decisions = "high-risk"; high-risk obligations bite from ~Aug 2026; penalties up to €35m / 7% of turnover) — binding and prescriptive, unlike SAFR; the US interagency model-risk guidance (SR 26-2 / OCC Bulletin 2026-13, Apr 2026) which explicitly excludes generative/agentic AI from scope, leaving a US gap; and voluntary standards ISO/IEC 42001 and the NIST AI RMF. SAFR's distinctive position: it is the first runtime (point-of-action) governance standard, versus the lifecycle/documentation focus of the others — it asks what an agent is authorised to do, how each proposed action is validated before execution, and what is recorded. Co-developed with eight heavyweight members (Ant International, Circle, HSBC, J.P. Morgan Chase, Manulife, Mastercard, OCBC, Visa), which is the real moat: credibility by consortium. MAS positioning vs other regulators: ahead on runtime-specific agentic governance (analysis), consistent with its earlier plays — see [[MAS launches PathFin.ai hub for financial AI adoption]] and Project Mindforge, on which SAFR builds. Private-sector adjacent moves in the base: [[Bank of Singapore deploys agentic AI tool SOWA]] (agent live in a MAS-regulated bank), [[Zania raises $18M Series A for agentic AI compliance platform]] and [[Harbor raises $10M seed to insure AI agent risks]] (commercial attempts to monetise exactly the AI-agent-risk layer SAFR frames), and [[JPMorgan blocks outside AI agents from accessing accounts]] — notable because JPMorgan co-authored SAFR yet publicly refuses external agent access, a clean "de-PR" tell on how real the runtime risk is.

**Risk flags.**
1. Voluntary, non-binding status: MAS states SAFR "does not constitute regulatory guidance or supervisory expectations." Why it matters — without teeth, adoption may skew to the eight co-authors; a framework that isn't mandated competes with cheaper "do nothing" and risks becoming a box-ticking reference rather than a control.
2. Correlated/systemic risk: SAFR itself flags that if many FIs run similar models or depend on a few common AI providers, agent behaviour can become correlated under stress — a concentration/systemic vulnerability that a per-institution runtime check does not resolve.
3. Author/practice gap (execution + credibility): J.P. Morgan co-authored SAFR yet blocks external AI agents from account access (Oct 2026, per CNBC). Why it matters — it signals even sophisticated members don't yet trust agentic execution in production, so "framework exists" ≠ "agents are safely live"; adoption claims should be read as announced, not operational.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** SAFR reframes AI governance in finance from pre-deployment model validation to point-of-action (runtime) control — if it diffuses, the growth layer shifts to policy-engine / agent-identity / audit-log vendors that can enforce it, benefiting the Zania/Harbor-type tooling cohort (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: SAFR becomes a de-facto regional baseline — trigger to watch is a second APAC/EU regulator (or MAS itself) referencing SAFR in supervisory expectations, or a named non-author FI shipping a SAFR-aligned agent into production. What breaks the thesis: SAFR stays confined to its eight co-authors with no third-party live deployment and no supervisory uptake through 2027, leaving it a well-cited paper rather than a standard.
