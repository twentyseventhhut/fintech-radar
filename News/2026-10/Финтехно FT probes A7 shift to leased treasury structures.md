# Context-enrichment: FT probes A7 shift to leased treasury structures
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 4/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
This is a **Financial Times follow-up investigation (reported ~2026-10-06)** into Russia's **A7 cross-border payments network** (controlled by sanctioned pro-Russian Moldovan businessman **Ilan Shor**, set up 2024 with roots in defence bank Promsvyazbank). The new claim: A7's evasion plumbing is **evolving away from a web of pure "shell companies"** (fictitious front entities with no real business) **toward "leased treasury structures"** — i.e. **real, genuinely-operating legal entities whose cash-flow turnover is borrowed/rented** so that sanctioned Russian payments can be commingled with, and disguised as, that company's ordinary commercial traffic. The stated advantage: much **harder to detect** than empty shells, because the host company has real invoices, real counterparties and real trade history.

**Important framing / what this is NOT:** This note is **analysis/commentary** from a Telegram channel (Финтехно) summarising an FT piece — the vault has **no loaded primary-source text** and **0 source bodies**. The FT article itself is paywalled (ft.com/content/372d018c...). So the specific term "leased treasury" and any numbers must be treated as the channel's rendering of FT, not independently verified here.

**Why structured this way / what it reveals (analysis):** The shift is a **direct, predicted response to the enforcement wall that slammed down days earlier**:
- 2026-09-22 — FT's major leak-based exposé: A7 moved **>$6.9bn through ~200+ shell companies** across Hong Kong, UAE, Kyrgyzstan, Indonesia, routed via correspondent banks (Citi, Standard Chartered named) using forged invoices/shipping docs.
- 2026-10-01 — **OFAC designates the "A7 Network" a Transnational Criminal Organization** (first time the TCO label is applied to a payments network) for laundering for IRGC/Hamas/DPRK actors; action is "Operation Economic Outcast."
- 2026-10-05 — **FinCEN special measure** (Federal Register) proposing to prohibit US FIs from transmitting funds involving **A7 "Sub-Agents"**; FinCEN cites **>$17bn Jan-2025→Jun-2026** (~13% of Russia's 2025 foreign-trade value).
→ Once the shell-company method is publicly mapped and the sub-agents are named/blockable, **empty fronts become a liability** (they match the typology regulators just published). Renting the turnover of **live companies** is the natural next move: it defeats pattern-matching on dormant/zero-substance entities and pushes detection cost onto banks' AML teams. **Second order:** this is the classic cat-and-mouse escalation — each public typology forces the network one layer deeper into "legitimate-looking" cover, raising compliance cost for the entire correspondent-banking chain, not just for A7.

## [1] Competitors / peers (of the evasion method, not a commercial market)
A7 is not a normal fintech "competitor set" — it is **sanctions-evasion infrastructure**, so peers = other Russia/Iran/DPRK illicit-finance rails and the techniques themselves:
- **A7A5 token** — ruble-backed stablecoin (issued by Old Vector LLC) tied to the same network; the "crypto leg" of A7 (see [[Crypto coin for Russian shadow payments moves $9 billion]], which flagged $9bn moved and the Keremet Bank / OFAC angle back in mid-2025).
- **Shell-company / trade-based money laundering (TBML)** — the prior-generation method A7 is now moving beyond; also used by Iranian oil-sales networks and DPRK procurement.
- **Ruble-backed crypto corridors in Africa** ([[Sanctioned Russian crypto network expands across Africa]]) — same strategic goal (bypass SWIFT/correspondent banking), different geography.
- **Legitimisation path:** A7 had publicly pitched itself as a *legal* global payments business ([[Russia's A7 cross-border payments firm plans global expansion]], Shor to Reuters, Jun 2026 — "undeterred by sanctions," plans for Russia's largest gold bank).

**Why the landscape is this way (analysis):** The "leased treasury" method sits one rung **above** TBML on the sophistication ladder: TBML fakes trade; leased treasuries **buy real trade** as cover. It mirrors how Iran's networks matured post-2012 — from crude front companies to embedded positions in real commodity traders. **Second order:** the harder-to-detect the method, the **more it implicates genuinely-operating third-country firms** (and their banks), which is exactly what the FinCEN sub-agent measure is trying to pre-empt by making the correspondent banks liable.

## [2] Company history / fit
A7 trajectory: **2024** founded (Promsvyazbank lineage) → becomes **leader in Russia's cross-border payments** as SWIFT/correspondent access collapses → **Jun 2026** Shor announces global expansion + gold-bank ambitions, publicly brushing off sanctions → **Sep 2026** FT leak exposes the $6.9bn shell-company machinery → **Oct 2026** OFAC TCO + FinCEN block. The "leased treasury" pivot fits this arc perfectly: an entity under maximal, immediate enforcement pressure (OFAC TCO is enforceable on announcement; FinCEN block pending) **must change its plumbing or die.**

**Why the company acts this way (analysis):** A7 exists to solve one structural problem — **Russia lost its correspondent-banking rails and needs ~$100s of bn/yr of trade settled anyway**. That demand is inelastic and state-backed, so A7 has no option to "wind down"; it can only **mutate the concealment layer.** The $17bn / ~13%-of-foreign-trade figure (FinCEN) shows it is **systemically important to Russian trade**, not a fringe operator — which is precisely why it is both heavily targeted and highly motivated to innovate on evasion.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**What's genuinely new here:** the *reported method shift itself* — from hollow shells to **renting the turnover of real operating companies ("leased treasuries")**. This is a **meaningfully different typology**, not a re-run of the Sept shell-company story: Sept = "how A7 did it (shells)"; this Oct-06 piece = "how A7 is **re-tooling** to beat the detection that Sept/OFAC just created." That makes it **fresh**, not a reprint.

**Traction / caveats (skeptical):** We have **no hard numbers** on how many leased-treasury arrangements exist, their volume, or whether this is **live vs merely observed/anticipated** by FT's sources. The channel summary is qualitative ("evolving toward," "potentially harder to detect"). So the *value-add is the intelligence signal* (the method is mutating), not a quantified new scheme. Treat volume/scale as **open**.

**Who captures the "margin" (analysis):** In evasion economics the "margin" is the **detection-avoidance spread** — the fee A7 can charge for guaranteeing a payment clears. Moving to leased treasuries **raises A7's cost** (it must pay real companies for cover + accept lower throughput per entity) but **raises its pricing power** (fewer rails can clear sanctioned Russian flows at all). **Second order:** the binding constraint shifts from "can we fake a company?" to "how many real companies will rent their books knowing the OFAC-TCO/FinCEN exposure?" — i.e. the enforcement actions **shrink the supply of willing hosts**, which is the actual chokepoint, not the technique.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Enforcement:** FinCEN's special-measure comment period runs before a final rule; once final, US FIs must block A7 sub-agent flows. Expect **secondary-sanctions pressure** on third-country banks (UAE, Kyrgyzstan, Hong Kong) and on any "leased" host companies FT/OFAC can name.
- **Adaptation:** leased-treasury cover **raises banks' AML burden** — they must now detect illicit flows hidden inside *legitimate* companies' real traffic, a far harder problem than screening dormant shells.
- **Risk to A7:** TCO label + FinCEN block raises counterparty fear; the mutation is survival, not strength.

**Why the market goes this way (analysis):** Western enforcement is pivoting from **entity-listing** (whack-a-mole on shells) to **systemic rail-blocking** (FinCEN special measure on an entire sub-agent network) — a recognition that listing individual fronts can't keep up. **Counterintuitive second order:** the "harder to detect" leased-treasury method may paradoxically make A7 **more fragile**, because embedding in real companies drags **real, reputable businesses and their banks into TCO exposure** — exactly the pressure point regulators exploit to collapse the willing-host supply. The central question is therefore not "can A7 hide better?" but **"how long can it find real companies willing to rent their balance sheets under a US TCO designation?"**

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Sub-vertical: sanctions-evasion / parallel cross-border settlement infrastructure for Russia (not a normal fintech vertical — closer to shadow correspondent banking). Scale of the host problem, not a TAM: A7 itself claimed >2,000 transactions/day and a cumulative ~7.5tn RUB (~$91.5bn) by Jan-2026, which it framed as ~13% of Russia's foreign-trade / cross-border transactions (per A7's own claims, via Moscow Times 2026-10-02 and FinCEN Alert FIN-2026-Alert007, 2026-10-01 — company-sourced, treat as unverified boast). FinCEN separately attributes >$17bn of sub-agent flow Jan-2025–Jun-2026. The FT leak alleges ~$6.9bn routed via international banks using forged invoices (FT, via Irish Times 2026-09-23). **Structure:** highly fragmented and deliberately opaque — a rotating mesh of front companies, now (per this FT piece) migrating to "leased treasuries," i.e. real operating legal entities whose genuine payment turnover is rented to hide Russian flows inside legitimate traffic. Entry "barrier" is not capital or network effects but access to compliant-looking counterparties and correspondent banks willing (knowingly or not) to clear. **Why now:** sanctions designations (US/EU mid-2025; FinCEN Oct-2026) burn shell-company networks faster than they can be spun up, so the operator is forced up the sophistication curve — from obviously-fake shells toward harder-to-detect embedding in live businesses. Second-order: detection shifts from entity screening to transaction-pattern / behavioural analytics, which banks are worse at.

**Competitive landscape.** KPIs this "sector" runs on: daily transaction count, cumulative settled volume, correspondent-bank reach (how many Swift-connected banks can be touched), and detection half-life (how long a channel survives before designation). "Peers" are other Russia/Iran parallel-rail schemes, not commercial competitors:
- **A7A5** (ruble stablecoin, Old Vector LLC / Kyrgyzstan, PSB-linked, same A7 orbit): >$100bn cumulative in year one, then avg daily volume collapsed to $24.3m in Jun-2026, −96% from the Jul-2025 peak after sanctions + Grinex going offline (Elliptic, 2026). A live example of the detection-half-life risk.
- **Grinex / Garantex** (crypto exchange successor chain): repeatedly sanctioned and disrupted; ~$15m of customer assets reportedly stolen Apr-2026 (TRM Labs; Cointelegraph).
Basis of "competition" is survivability vs enforcement, not price/product. A7's position: the largest and most institutionalised of these (state-bank parentage via Promsvyazbank, 51% Ilan Shor), i.e. ahead on scale — but that same prominence makes it the primary enforcement target. "Moat" (analysis): proximity to the Russian MoF / defence-bank ecosystem and gold-bank (Rosveksel) ambitions — a political/state moat, not a commercial one, and one that attracts rather than deters designation.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation multiples exist and none can be computed: A7 is a private, sanctioned entity with no disclosed revenue, take rate or equity value — EV/Revenue, P/E, price-per-user all = no data / `[UNSOURCED]`. Comparison is therefore qualitative and structural, not financial. Internal comps (base): [[Russia's A7 cross-border payments firm plans global expansion]] (same company, gold-bank/Rosveksel expansion), [[Russia to legalize crypto for cross-border trade payments]] (regulatory tailwind for the whole parallel-rails theme), [[Brazil's central bank bans crypto settlement in cross-border payments]] (counter-example — a regulator closing crypto back-ends). The only quantified peer datapoint — A7A5's −96% volume collapse post-sanctions — is the relevant "multiple": it sizes how fast enforcement can destroy a channel's throughput.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Correspondent-bank / clearing dependence** — the whole model rides on Swift-connected banks (Standard Chartered, JPMorgan, Citi named in the FT leak) unknowingly clearing flows; once they detect and de-risk, throughput can fall ~96% in months (A7A5 precedent). Dependence on someone else's rails is the core fragility.
2. **Enforcement escalation / designation half-life** — US (FinCEN Oct-2026), EU and UK are actively naming A7 and sub-agents; "leased treasuries" buy time but the trend is shorter channel lifespans, forcing constant, costly re-tooling.
3. **Counterparty / fraud risk inside the scheme itself** — forged invoices and the Grinex $15m theft show the ecosystem cannibalises its own users; the "leased" legitimate entities inherit legal and seizure exposure.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) The shift from shell companies to leased live treasuries is an escalation signal, not a durability signal: it concedes that pure-shell detection now works, so the operator is paying more for stealth. Falsifiable thesis: if "leased treasury" embedding is effective, A7's reported daily volume should hold or grow despite the Oct-2026 FinCEN action; trigger/what-breaks-it — a visible A7-wide throughput collapse mirroring A7A5's −96% would falsify the durability case and confirm enforcement is outrunning obfuscation. Watch: which correspondent banks de-risk next, and whether the Rosveksel gold-bank leg becomes the next channel once banking rails are squeezed.
