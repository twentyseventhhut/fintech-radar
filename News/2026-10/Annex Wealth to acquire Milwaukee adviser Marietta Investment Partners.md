# Context-enrichment: Annex Wealth Management to acquire Marietta Investment Partners
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Annex Wealth Management (Brookfield, WI; ~$8.05B AUM as of 31 Dec 2025, >9,800 clients / ~6,700 households per its ADV) signed to acquire **Marietta Investment Partners**, a downtown-Milwaukee RIA with **~$765M AUM** and a **seven-person team**. All seven join Annex; the deal takes Annex to **>$9B AUM**. Expected close **first week of November 2026**. **Terms were NOT disclosed** — no multiple, no earn-out, no consideration mix.

Why structured this way / what it reveals:
- Marietta is **employee-owned**: founder John Evans (PhD, founded 2000) sold himself out in an **internal, debt-financed succession** to six partners (Mary Allmon, Lori Brook, Robert Draper, Charles Evans, Amanda Grams, Jonathan Smucker) a few years ago. Selling now to Annex is the **second, external step** of a succession story — the internal buyout likely left the partners with leverage and no liquidity event, and a strategic buyer solves both. (analysis)
- The PR frame ("in-house portfolio management + institutional client business join Annex") is accurate but thin: this is a **small, in-market tuck-in** (same metro, ~9.5% of Annex's book), not a platform expansion. No disclosed terms = nothing to brag about and nothing material to the acquirer's economics.
- "Institutional client business" is the one genuinely additive line: Marietta serves institutions/trusts, which Annex (primarily households) can cross-pollinate. (hypothesis — size of the institutional book not disclosed.)

## [1] Competitors / peers
This lands in the middle of a **record 2026 RIA-consolidation wave** — several near-identical deals this very week, all captured in the corpus:
- [[Waverly Advisors acquires Oregon's MCS Family Wealth Advisors]] — PE-backed serial consolidator, out-of-state tuck-in.
- [[Simplicity Group acquires Denver advisory firm MAIA Wealth]] and [[Simplicity Group acquires Chicago's Zen Wealth Management]] — Simplicity doing two metro RIA grabs in parallel.
- [[CohnReznick to acquire Aldrich group, including $8B Aldrich Wealth]] — accounting-firm-into-wealth, an order of magnitude larger.
- [[Bill Harris launches digital RIA Evergreen Wealth]] (2025) — the "build" alternative to the "buy" roll-up.

Position: Annex is a **single-market strategic acquirer**, not a national PE-backed roll-up. It is a **price-taker, not a price-setter** in this market.

Why the landscape is this way / second-order:
- Macro backdrop: RIA M&A hit an all-time quarterly record in Q1 2026 (142 deals, average AUM/deal ~$1.8B); 2026 is on pace for ~475 deals, beating 2025's 466. **PE touches ~71.8% of transactions.** → Valuations for sellers are historically rich, which is exactly why an internally-bought-out, mid-size firm like Marietta sells NOW. (fact)
- The second-order point: because PE-backed consolidators dominate and bid up multiples, a **non-PE, self-funded strategic like Annex can only realistically win deals where culture/locality beats price** — i.e. same-metro firms where the selling partners care about client continuity more than squeezing the last turn of EBITDA. That is precisely the Marietta profile. The real question isn't "is this a good deal" but "can a non-PE buyer keep competing against leveraged roll-ups for the bigger books". (analysis)

## [2] Company history / fit
- Annex: Brookfield, WI founder-led RIA; **historically selective** acquirer — prior inorganic moves cited in 2011 and 2020, and most recently **WFA Asset Management** (~$440M Milwaukee-area RIA, seven-person, with in-house tax prep + estate planning). Marietta is the **next in that same pattern**: small, local, team-intact, capability-additive. [source: Citywire]
- Marietta: founded 2000, employee-owned, ~$765M, in-house portfolio management + institutional clients.

Why Annex acts this way — the structural logic:
- Annex grows mostly **organically** and uses M&A as **talent + capability + local-density acquisition**, not asset-aggregation-for-multiple. WFA added tax/estate; Marietta adds **in-house portfolio management and an institutional book**. The through-line: Annex buys **vertically integrated capabilities it can keep in-house** rather than outsourcing — consistent with a "holistic, high-touch" positioning. (analysis)
- Why now: at >$9B post-deal, Annex is reaching a scale where owning portfolio-management and institutional capability internally improves margin and defensibility vs. relying on third-party model providers. (hypothesis)

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** This is a conventional in-market RIA tuck-in — the dominant deal type of 2026, not a new mechanism. No new product, no tech, no new market.
- **Traction is real but boring:** the numbers are actual AUM ($765M) and an intact 7-person team (not an "announcement") — so the value-add (people + book) is tangible, unlike a vaporware pilot. But it's incremental: ~9.5% bump to Annex's AUM.
- Where the margin sits: in RIA roll-ups the acquirer captures the recurring ~1%-of-AUM advisory fee stream; the durable value is **client + advisor retention post-close**, not the transaction. The risk to the multiple is advisor/client attrition during integration and the fact that Marietta's team just went through ONE ownership change (internal buyout) and is now facing a SECOND (sale to Annex) within a few years — integration fatigue is a live risk. (analysis)
- Who captures margin: unlike PE-backed roll-ups (where the sponsor extracts the arbitrage between entry multiple and exit/recap), Annex as a self-funded strategic keeps the full recurring-revenue stream but **forgoes the financial-engineering upside** — it must earn its return from operations and retention, not leverage. (analysis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Close expected first week of November 2026. Watch: whether all seven Marietta staff and the institutional book actually stay 12 months post-close (the only metric that validates the deal).
- Sentiment: **neutral-to-mild-positive, local story.** Material to Milwaukee RIA landscape, immaterial to the broader fintech/wealth market — a data point IN the consolidation trend, not a driver of it.
- Structural driver: aging RIA founders + record valuations + PE firepower → the 2026 M&A record continues; expect more sub-$1B, in-market tuck-ins like this. **Counter-intuitive second-order effect:** as PE-backed consolidators concentrate the market, self-funded strategics like Annex get **squeezed out of the larger auctions** and pushed toward exactly these small, relationship-driven local deals — so Annex's acquisition cadence may *slow* precisely as the overall market heats up, unless it takes outside capital. (hypothesis)
- Regulatory backdrop: adjacent RIA-rule shifts in the corpus ([[SEC proposes crypto self-custody framework for advisers]], [[SEBI weighs allowing mutual fund distributors to hold RIA licenses]]) are not material to this deal.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US RIA (registered investment adviser) consolidation is at a record pace: per ECHELON Partners, Q1 2026 saw 142 RIA M&A transactions with ~$1.67tn AUM transacted — an all-time quarterly high vs the prior 125-deal record — and ECHELON projects ~475 deals for full-year 2026 (per ECHELON Partners / Yahoo Finance, as of 2026-05). Average deal size reached ~$1.8bn AUM, the highest since 2021 (per 401kSpecialist, as of 2026-05). Structure: highly fragmented at the bottom (thousands of sub-$1bn independent RIAs) consolidating into a handful of well-capitalized buyers; private equity touched 71.8% of Q1 2026 deals (per ECHELON). Entry barriers are low to form an RIA but high to reach scale (distribution, compliance, succession bench) — which is exactly what drives sellers into roll-ups. "Why now": aging founder-advisors facing succession gaps (Marietta founder John Evans, PhD, founded the firm in 2000), plus PE-fueled buyers willing to pay premiums for at-scale, recurring-fee books (per InvestmentNews, 2026).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: AUM, net organic growth rate, recurring fee-revenue %, EBITDA margin (30–45% for well-run firms). Basis of competition among acquirers = price/multiple paid, equity story for selling advisors, and tech/platform capabilities (AI, analytics, compliance). Annex Wealth Management is a sub-scale regional consolidator, not a national aggregator: fee-only RIA founded 2001 in Brookfield, WI, ~$8.1bn AUM / ~70 advisors pre-deal, going to >$9bn post-close (per Annex blog / FinTRX, 2026-10). This is Annex's third acquisition since 2021 (prior: Whitefish Bay-based WFA Asset Management; and a ~$440m Wisconsin RIA per Citywire). Recent peer moves in the adjacent advisor-tech tuck-in lane: [[Jump to acquire advisor transcription firm Mobile Assistant]] and [[Grantd acquires StockOpter equity-planning platform]] (2025-10). Position: niche/regional roll-up (Milwaukee/Wisconsin density + institutional-client expansion via Marietta's institutional book); moat is local relationships + succession solution, not scale — `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal terms were not disclosed, so no transaction multiple is computable — **no data** on EV/EBITDA or % of AUM paid for Marietta. For sector calibration (not this deal): 2026 RIA EBITDA multiples run ~5x for sub-$100m "lifestyle" practices up to ~13–15x for premium $1bn+ fee-only firms; median 2025 RIA deal ~11.6x EBITDA (per RIACatalyst / Iconic / adastra, 2026) — DATA extracted, not applied to this deal. Marietta: ~$765m AUM, 7-person team, employee-owned, in-house portfolio management + institutional book (per Annex blog, 2026-10-14). Its ~$765m AUM band ($500m–$1b) typically fetches ~10x EBITDA per the cited guides, but EBITDA for Marietta is **[UNSOURCED]** → no price-per-AUM or multiple arithmetic possible. Internal comps are adjacent-vertical, not pure RIA roll-ups: [[Jump to acquire advisor transcription firm Mobile Assistant]], [[Grantd acquires StockOpter equity-planning platform]] (advisor-tech), [[Franklin Templeton completes Apera Asset Management deal]] (asset-mgmt, different model). Distribution not computed — qualitative comparison only; no outlier flag possible on an undisclosed-terms deal.

**Risk flags.**
- Undisclosed terms = opacity: no way to judge whether Annex overpaid; a single ~$765m book is ~8–9% of combined AUM, so integration missteps or advisor/client attrition in the 7-person team materially dent the deal's economics — key-person and client-consent risk (RIA client assets don't transfer automatically).
- Serial small-ticket roll-up with thin M&A cadence (3 deals since 2021) and no disclosed PE backing: funding/leverage for continued consolidation is unclear, and Annex competes for targets against far larger, PE-funded national aggregators paying premium multiples — margin/price squeeze on future deals.
- Sector-wide over-valuation signal: record 2026 deal volume at the highest average AUM since 2021 suggests late-cycle pricing; a rate/market drawdown would compress AUM-linked fees and the multiples buyers can justify, pressuring recently-acquired books.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` A routine regional tuck-in, not a re-rating — confirms the thesis that the US RIA mid-market keeps consolidating via founder-succession deals rather than disruption. Watch whether Annex pivots from local-density deals to institutional-channel scaling (Marietta's institutional book is the strategic tell). Falsifiable: if Annex does a materially larger or out-of-state deal within ~12 months, it signals a shift from opportunistic roll-up to platform ambition (likely needing outside/PE capital); absence of that keeps it a niche regional player.
