**Partnership with Mayor of London's flagship support programme helps London's most underrepresented entrepreneurs unlock revenue**

Inclusive global payments platform and small business payments expert, Ecommpay, is partnering with Grow London Local to provide SMEs with valuable insights and support around payments. Investing in London's small business community through the Mayor of London's flagship support programme, London-headquartered Ecommpay will be a hands-on resource for small businesses, available through the Grow London Local website, events, consultations and the platform's provider directory.

There are an estimated 1 million+ SMEs in London, with 1 in 4 London businesses facing financial vulnerability . The UK Government's Small Business Plan (August 2025) prioritised access to finance and digital skills as top SME growth levers, and The Mayor's London Growth Plan (2025) identifies payment and finance access as a critical enabler of inclusive SME growth across London's boroughs.

“_With the overwhelming majority of businesses we support coming from underrepresented communities, Grow London Local has a strong mandate for inclusive entrepreneurship_,” commented Vanesa Perez Sanchez, Director of Small Business Service Delivery.

"_The launch of our Grow London Local Money Hub in April 2026 marked an important step towards our strategic priority of improving access to funding and strengthening financial literacy. Payments are central to this, and this partnership will help us advance that ambition_.

“_As a payment platform dedicated to increasing financial inclusion, Ecommpay is perfectly placed to support London’s small businesses with guidance and tips on all things payments. Embedded in the Grow London Local support ecosystem across digital, in-person, and events channels, Ecommpay will provide practical resources and guidance to micro and small businesses across London_.”

Through Ecommpay, SMEs and micro businesses can increase the range of payment solutions available to their customers, without the usual complexity or high cost of implementation.

Ecommpay’s self-serve payments platform is designed for UK-registered small businesses selling online. At the heart of the new platform is the capability to go live quickly after onboarding is complete, using the Ecommpay Hosted Payment Page, at a transparent transaction rate and without any volume threshold. Plus businesses can utilise payment links and e-invoicing so that they can accept card payments without a website or till and take phone and mail order payments via a virtual payment terminal -

"_Ecommpay is headquartered in London, and we are proud to be able to support other local London businesses as they grow and expand their customer bases_,” explained Max Ryzhov, Chief Product Officer at Ecommpay. “_Committed to reducing friction and increasing access to a wider range of payment solutions, we have built solutions that help drive business revenue for communities that would usually face barriers within payments access_.

“_Sharing Grow London Local’s vision and commitment to unlocking revenue for small businesses we are making enterprise-grade tools available to micro and small businesses, removing one of the biggest barriers to small business growth – complex and costly payment infrastructure. We are delighted to be able to share our expertise with London businesses through this partnership, enabling them to break down barriers and succeed locally, nationally and internationally_.”

# Context-enrichment: Ecommpay partners Grow London Local to support small businesses
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
De-PR'd: Ecommpay (London-HQ'd global PSP, acquiring + gateway) becomes a listed "support provider" inside **Grow London Local** — the Mayor of London's SME support programme delivered by **London & Partners**. Concretely, Ecommpay gets presence/visibility through the Grow London Local website, its **provider directory**, events and consultations, where it will publish "guidance and tips on payments" for micro/small London merchants.
- What this is NOT: no money changes hands publicly, no exclusivity, no committed merchant volume, no co-funding figure, no new product. The PR itself has to reach back to the **July 2026 self-serve "Ecommpay for Small Businesses" platform** (UK-registered online merchants up to ~EUR 2m revenue; 1.30% + 20p UK / 3.00% + EUR 0.20 intl; go-live in hours via Hosted Payment Page) to have a product to sell — i.e. this partnership is a **distribution/awareness channel** bolted onto an already-launched product, framed as CSR/financial-inclusion.
- **Why framed this way:** Ecommpay has leaned hard into an "inclusion / Ecommpay for Good / accessibility" brand for 2+ years ([[Ecommpay marks first year of inclusivity programme]], DAC approvals, Disability Confident). Wrapping a lead-gen channel in the Mayor's inclusive-growth programme is reputationally cheap and on-brand — but the quotes ("hands-on resource", "share our expertise") are marketing for a provider-directory listing, not a contracted engagement. The real, citable mechanism delta vs a plain press release is thin: it's placement in a government-backed signposting directory.

## [1] Competitors / peers
SME/micro online-payments acquiring in the UK is crowded and commoditised: **Stripe, Square (Block), SumUp, Zettle (PayPal), GoCardless, Worldpay, Checkout.com**, plus neo/BNPL rails. Grow London Local's provider directory is open — Ecommpay is one of many "support providers" (the programme actively recruits them), so this is a **non-exclusive listing**, not a Stripe/Shopify-style embedded default.
- Ecommpay's edge for this segment: a genuinely SME-tuned self-serve product (no-code HPP, no minimum volume, flat transparent rate) released July 2026 — comparable to Stripe/Square on simplicity but Ecommpay is late and sub-scale in pure SMB vs Stripe/Square.
- **Second-order:** distribution, not product, is the bottleneck in SMB acquiring — whoever owns merchant acquisition channels (POS ecosystems, website builders, govt/bank referrals) wins. A Mayoral directory is a low-intent, low-conversion channel vs Shopify/WooCommerce integrations, so the competitive needle barely moves.

## [2] Company history / fit
Ecommpay's 2025–26 trajectory is a deliberate pivot from enterprise/ecommerce acquiring toward the UK SMB/micro long tail: self-serve SMB launch (2026-07, [[Ecommpay launches self-serve payments platform for UK small businesses]]), "challenging lack of choice for micro enterprises", express checkout, Klarna BNPL ([[Ecommpay adds Klarna BNPL payment options]]), FStech/eCommerce awards, and a sustained inclusion-branding programme.
- **Why act this way:** enterprise acquiring is a commoditised take-rate business with heavy competition; the UK micro/SMB tail (1m+ London SMEs cited in the PR) is underserved and higher-margin per transaction but expensive to acquire one-by-one. A free, govt-branded channel is a cheap customer-acquisition experiment that doubles as PR — it fits the strategy logically, but it's a marketing move, not a structural one.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Novelty: **low**. The product (self-serve SMB platform) is 3 months old; the inclusion brand is years old; the "partnership" is a directory listing + content. No traction metrics are disclosed for Ecommpay (no merchant counts, no sign-ups via the programme). Grow London Local has scale (37,000+ entrepreneurs reached, 5,000+ helped to support in Q3 FY25/26) and launched a **Money Hub in April 2026** — that's the real asset; Ecommpay is renting audience from it.
- **Who captures the value:** Grow London Local gets a free payments-expertise provider; Ecommpay gets brand halo + a referral funnel with unquantified conversion. Margin still sits in the transaction take-rate IF merchants actually onboard — and nothing here proves they will. Value-add to merchants is "guidance/tips", i.e. content marketing, until a merchant signs onto the paid platform.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch for whether Ecommpay publishes any **onboarding/merchant numbers** attributable to the channel (the honest proof) — absence would confirm this is PR. Regulatory/market backdrop is supportive and the PR leans on it: UK Small Business Plan (Aug 2025) and the Mayor's London Growth Plan (2025) both flag SME access to finance/payments — real tailwinds, but generic to all PSPs, not an Ecommpay advantage.
- **Counterintuitive second-order:** govt-branded SME support directories flatten differentiation (every provider is "a trusted partner"), so being listed is table stakes, not a moat; the winners will still be decided by product friction and distribution economics, where Stripe/Square lead. Net: reputationally positive, commercially marginal, unverified traction.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** UK digital-acceptance for SMEs sits inside a UK payment-gateway market sized at ~$3.02bn in 2026, projected to ~$5.88bn by 2031 (~14.3% CAGR), per Mordor Intelligence (via mordorintelligence.com, as of 2026) — a figure to treat as a third-party estimate, not a verified TAM. Structure is barbelled: the **underlying card-acquiring layer is highly concentrated** — Worldpay, Barclaycard and Global Payments hold ~71% of UK merchant acquiring (per Business Expert, via businessexpert.co.uk, 2026) — while the **gateway/orchestration layer above it is fragmented** and is where Ecommpay competes. Entry barriers are regulatory (FCA authorisation; Ecommpay is an FCA-authorised payment institution) plus integration/distribution, not capital-light. "Why now": the PSR's card-acquiring market review concluded the market "was not working well" for merchants with card turnover up to £50m — opaque pricing, POS-terminal lock-in and switching friction — and imposed remedies (mandatory contract end-dates, summary price boxes, easier POS exit) (per PSR MR18/1.8, via psr.org.uk). This regulatory push to open up the SME long tail is the structural tailwind a self-serve, transparent-rate entrant like Ecommpay is explicitly leaning into (the note cites "transparent transaction rate and without any volume threshold").

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/processing volume, take rate (MSC), onboarding time-to-live, authorisation/success rate, churn. Basis of competition in the SME gateway tier is **price transparency + speed of onboarding + breadth of methods**, not raw scale. Key players for this exact segment are SME-first acquirers/gateways — Stripe, Square (Block), SumUp, Worldpay (incumbent), plus PayPal — against which a self-serve "go live in hours, flat transparent rate" pitch is table-stakes-matching, not differentiating. Recent own moves: Ecommpay launched "Ecommpay for Small Businesses," a self-serve platform for UK merchants up to €2m annual revenue, in July 2026 [[Ecommpay launches self-serve payments platform for UK small businesses]]; added Klarna BNPL in Oct 2026 [[Ecommpay adds Klarna BNPL payment options]]; processing volume reportedly +25% in 2024 (per ecommercenews.uk). Protagonist's position: **niche / catching-up** in the UK SME tier (its heritage is enterprise/orchestration). This Grow London Local tie-up is a **distribution/credibility play, not a commercial deal** — Ecommpay joins the Mayor's directory as a resource; no revenue-share, pricing or merchant-count commitment is disclosed. Moat here is weak-to-moderate (intangible/brand + FCA status); switching costs and network effects accrue to whoever onboards the merchant first. `(analysis)`

**Comps & multiples.** Ecommpay is **private and does not disclose audited current financials, TPV or take rate**, so no verifiable multiple can be computed — EV/Revenue, P/S = no data. **Risk flags.** (1) **No disclosed economics** — the partnership announces access/credibility but is silent on pricing, merchant targets and whether Ecommpay captures any acquiring spread; it reads as marketing/CSR-adjacent until live merchant numbers appear. (2) **Disintermediation / thin-slice margin** — Ecommpay sits on top of concentrated acquirers (Worldpay/Barclaycard/Global Payments); in the SME gateway tier it competes head-on with Stripe/Square/SumUp on price, so the very PSR-driven transparency that opens the door also compresses take rate. (3) **Segment-fit / execution risk** — the SME long tail is low-ARPU and high-churn; Ecommpay's enterprise/orchestration DNA may not translate to profitable micro-merchant servicing, and a public-sector directory listing is a weak customer-acquisition channel versus paid/self-serve funnels.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector, little structurally — this is one more entrant courting the PSR-unlocked SME tail via a public-body distribution channel, not a re-rating or consolidation signal `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: if Ecommpay's SME push is working, the next 2–3 quarters should surface **disclosed SME merchant counts or SMB-segment TPV**; watch for that. Trigger that breaks the thesis: continued announcements that are awards/partnerships/directories with zero adoption metrics would confirm this is brand-building, not a scaling SME book.
