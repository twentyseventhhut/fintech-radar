 BAKU, Azerbaijan, October 5. The Eurasian
Development Bank (EDB) has increased its financing for Uzbekistan’s
digital ecosystem Uzum from $70 million to $100 million, expanding
support for the company’s fintech operations and digital financial
services.
This was reflected in the statement by EDB.
The additional financing will support the further development of
Uzum’s fintech business and the expansion of digital financial
services for individuals and small and medium-sized businesses
across Uzbekistan, according to EDB.
The original financing agreement was signed in May 2026 and
marked EDB’s first investment in Uzbekistan after the country
joined the development bank.
Uzum Bank was the leader in the growth of bank card issuance in
Uzbekistan in 2026, while more than 50% of orders on the Uzum
Market marketplace are already paid using financial services
provided by the ecosystem, according to the company.
“Expanding our partnership with industry leaders reflects EDB’s
plans to scale its operations in Uzbekistan. Digital platforms are
a key element of economic infrastructure. By financing their
growth, we are investing not only in a specific company but also in
the region’s technological connectivity and access to financial
services for millions of people,” said Sergey Ignatov, EDB Senior
Managing Director.
Uzum founder and CEO Jasur Jumaev said the increased financing
reflects the results achieved by the company in recent months and
investor confidence in its strategy.
“We are building fintech infrastructure on a national scale that
is used by millions of people every day. It is particularly
important for us that as Uzum grows, the volume of international
capital directed toward Uzbekistan’s digital economy also
increases,” Jumaev said.
The financing comes as Uzbekistan’s digital economy continues to
expand, supported by rising adoption of cashless payments, digital
banking services and growing demand from younger consumers.
Uzum is an Uzbek digital ecosystem combining fintech, e-commerce
and banking services for individuals and businesses. Its services
are used by more than 20 million people in Uzbekistan each month,
according to the company.
EDB is a multilateral development bank focused on investment and
economic integration across Eurasia. As of the end of June 2026,
its accumulated portfolio comprised 348 projects totaling $22.1
billion in investments, with major areas including transport
infrastructure, digital systems, renewable energy, agriculture,
industry and engineering.
Under its 2022–2026 strategy, EDB is implementing three major
projects focused on Central Asia’s water and energy complex, the
Eurasian transport framework and the development of Eurasian trade
corridors.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 5–6 Oct 2026 the Eurasian Development Bank (EDB) said it raised its financing for Uzbekistan's digital ecosystem Uzum from **$70M to $100M** — a **$30M top-up** of the loan originally signed in **May 2026** (the activation of EDB's Tashkent office and its first-ever Uzbekistan investment after the country joined the bank). It is **debt (an investment loan), not equity**: no new valuation, no new cap-table entry. Use of proceeds is the same boilerplate as May — "expand fintech + digital financial services for individuals and SMEs."
- **Why structured this way:** a $30M increment to an existing facility is an operational drawdown/upsizing, not a fresh deal. EDB only has ~5 months of relationship; a modest top-up is low-commitment signalling, not conviction. EDB itself is a **~$22B-portfolio multilateral dominated by Russia (44.8%) and Kazakhstan (37.3%)** — Uzbekistan joined only Oct 2025 (10% allocated capital, mostly unpaid). So "international capital" here is regional development-bank money with a political/integration mandate, not blue-chip institutional validation.
- **Why the PR anchors to "50% of orders paid via ecosystem finance" and "card-issuance leader":** these are the genuinely strong Uzum metrics; the loan size itself is small versus Uzum's $11B 2025 payment volume, ~$691M revenue, $176M net income, so the release leans on traction not on the ticket.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Direct super-app comps in-market: **Click SuperApp** (Uzum's closest rival; note the 2023 "Uzum + Click" framing was separate — Click is a competitor ecosystem), **Alif** (Alif Nasiya is the #2 BNPL behind Uzum Nasiya; together ~two-thirds of BNPL), **Payme, Humans** (telco-to-fintch/marketplace), **TBC Bank Uzbekistan, Anorbank, AVO, Xazna**. Regional template is **Kaspi.kz** (Kazakhstan) — the proven super-app model Uzum is explicitly cloning; Kaspi is already public and acquisitive (Rabobank Türkiye, 2026 — see corpus). 
- **Why the landscape is this way:** Uzbekistan has no Kaspi-scale incumbent yet; Uzum has first-mover scale (20M MAU on ~36M population, ~half of 2025 debit-card issuance). On DFI financing specifically, Uzum's peers draw EBRD/IFC/ADB debt — EDB is the newer, more regionally-aligned lender filling that role. **Second-order:** EDB's Russia/Kazakhstan-dominated cap table could make EDB debt a double-edged flag for a company courting London/Nasdaq/Abu Dhabi listings — a Western-index IPO narrative sits awkwardly next to a Russia-chaired development-bank lender.

## [2] Company history / fit
Trajectory (corpus): Aug 2025 $70M equity at $1.5B (Tencent, VR Capital) → Mar 2026 $131.5M at $2.3B (Oman sovereign funds, Tencent, VR, FinSight) → May 2026 EDB $70M loan → Jun 2026 plans $250–300M pre-IPO round → eyeing 2027 IPO (London/HK/Nasdaq/Abu Dhabi). See [[Uzum raises $70 million at $1.5 billion valuation]], [[Uzbekistan's Uzum raises $131.5m at $2.3bn valuation]], [[Eurasian Development Bank extends $70 million loan to Uzum]], [[Uzum plans $250-300M pre-IPO round ahead of 2027 listing]], [[Tencent-backed Uzum weighs London listing for 2027 IPO]].
- **Why Uzum acts this way:** it is stacking non-dilutive debt alongside planned equity to fund fintech-vertical growth (lending book, card/POS infra) while preserving the cap table ahead of a pre-IPO round — debt from DFIs is cheaper than diluting at a pre-IPO step-up and signals "bankable" credit to future IPO investors.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Novelty is **low**: same lender, same borrower, same use-of-proceeds as May; only the number changed (+$30M). The real, citable traction lives in Uzum's own numbers (>50% of Uzum Market orders paid via in-house finance; ~half of Uzbekistan's 2025 debit cards; ~$400M unsecured loan book per 2026 web sources), **not in this loan**.
- **Why the value-add is thin:** $30M is immaterial to a company doing $11B payment volume — it funds the lending vertical at the margin. **Who captures the margin:** Uzum's edge is owning the full stack (marketplace demand → embedded credit → bank), so finance economics stay in-house; EDB captures only loan spread. What this loan genuinely signals is **DFI willingness to re-up fast**, i.e. no red flags in the May facility's early performance — a mild positive, not a step-change.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch the **$250–300M pre-IPO round (H2 2026/early 2027)** and the **2027 listing venue** decision — those are the weight-bearing events; this loan is a footnote to them. EDB flagged plans to **scale operations in Uzbekistan (up to ~$1.5B by 2031 per its Tashkent-office launch)**, so more Uzum/EDB tranches are plausible. Risks: single-country concentration, EM/soum FX, political reform reversibility, and the governance optics of a Russia-dominated lender versus a Western IPO ambition.
- **Why the market goes this way (analysis):** Uzbekistan's demographic + cashless-adoption tailwind makes it a DFI darling; capital will keep flowing. Counter-intuitive second-order: heavy reliance on regionally-politicised development finance can *narrow* Uzum's eventual IPO investor base rather than widen it.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Uzum sits in the Central Asian "digital ecosystem / super-app" vertical (fintech + e-commerce + banking), with this news a $70M→$100M debt expansion from the Eurasian Development Bank. Structure: highly concentrated around a single platform — Uzum reports ~20M monthly users vs Uzbekistan's ~36M population, and issued ~4.1M debit cards in 2025, ~half of all debit cards issued in the country that year (per The Paypers, as of 2026-06). Uzbekistan has >70M bank cards in circulation (per EDB/PRNewswire, 2026-05). Drivers / "why now": rapid cashless-payment adoption, a young digitally-active population, and — second-order — the arrival of development-finance (EDB) and sovereign (Oman) capital that normally funds infrastructure now treating digital platforms as "economic infrastructure," lowering Uzum's cost of capital ahead of a targeted 2027 IPO (venues floated: Hong Kong / London / Abu Dhabi / Nasdaq).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/payment volume, GMV, take rate, MAU, card issuance, consumer-finance book. Uzum 2025 (unaudited, via The Paypers 2026-06): ~$11bn payment TPV, ~$691M revenue, ~$176M net income, e-commerce GMV >$500M, fintech volumes ~$1.2bn. Basis of competition in-market is distribution + embedded finance: >50% of Uzum Market orders are already paid with the ecosystem's own financial services (per EDB, 2026-10) — i.e. the commerce funnel feeds the far more profitable fintech vertical. Position: Uzum is the clear domestic leader (ahead/entrenched); moat = network effects + switching costs from a bundled wallet/card/marketplace stack (analysis). Recent moves: Oman SWF-led $131.5M round at $2.3bn (2026-03); planned $250-300M pre-IPO (2026-06); EDB $70M loan (2026-05) now topped up to $100M (2026-10). The relevant external analogue is Kaspi.kz in neighbouring Kazakhstan — the proven Central Asian super-app end-state.
