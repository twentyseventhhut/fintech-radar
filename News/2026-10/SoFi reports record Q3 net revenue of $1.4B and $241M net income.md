SAN FRANCISCO--(BUSINESS WIRE)-- SoFi Technologies, Inc. (NASDAQ: SOFI), a member-centric, everything app for digital financial services that helps members borrow, save, spend, invest and protect their money, reported financial results today for its third quarter ended September 30, 2026.

“We had exceptional Q3 results against the backdrop of any environment, but they are even more remarkable given how 2026 has unfolded,” said Anthony Noto, CEO of SoFi. “Our focus is simple. We are adding more members, who are using more products, and generating more revenue per product to create the most value for our shareholders over the long run. In less than a decade, we have transformed SoFi from a single-product company into an everything financial app with eight different businesses each generating over $100 million in annualized revenue. Our conviction in the opportunity ahead has never been greater.”

Highlights

Raising Guidance on Continued Business Momentum. Given the strength and visibility across the business, SoFi raised its 2026 guidance above analyst estimates 1, reflecting both the third quarter beat and higher expectations for the fourth quarter of 2026. SoFi now expects $5.04 billion of adjusted net revenue which is a year-over-year increase of 40%, $1.66 billion of adjusted EBITDA, $840.0 million of net income and $0.61 of EPS. The company also introduced 2027 guidance of approximately $6.4 billion adjusted net revenue, $2.24 billion of adjusted EBITDA, $1.28 billion of net income and $0.90 of EPS, all of which are well ahead of analyst estimates 1. The 2027 outlook represents approximately 27% adjusted net revenue growth and an adjusted net income margin of approximately 20%.
Delivering Record Results with Durable Growth and Strong Returns. Adjusted net revenue increased 44% year-over-year to $1.4 billion, while adjusted EBITDA increased 70% to $469.8 million, representing a 34% margin. SoFi achieved the Rule of 40 for the 20th consecutive quarter, with a score of 79. SoFi also delivered its 12th consecutive profitable quarter with adjusted net income of $243.0 million, and a return on tangible common equity of approximately 10%. Fee-based revenue reached approximately $570.6 million, representing 42% of total revenue.
Growing Members, Products and Revenue Per Product. SoFi grew members 35% year-over-year to 17.1 million, adding a record 1.2 million members during the quarter, while products increased 44% to 26.7 million, including a record 2.2 million products added. Products per member increased to approximately 1.56 from 1.47 a year ago and 51% of new products were opened by existing members. This continued growth reflects the strength of SoFi’s Financial Services Productivity Loop (FSPL) strategy, as members use more products, relationships deepen and the opportunity to generate revenue from each relationship expands.
Achieving Record Loan Originations While Maintaining Strong Credit Performance. Total loan originations reached a record $16.9 billion, including approximately $3.8 billion of Loan Platform Business originations. Lending adjusted net revenue reached approximately $807.4 million, while Lending products increased 39% year-over-year to 3.4 million, and credit performance remained strong and in line with expectations. Personal Loan products reached approximately 2.6 million, Student Loan products reached approximately 760,000 and Home Loan products reached approximately 72,000. Lending adjusted revenue per product increased to approximately $942, compared with approximately $920 in the second quarter of 2026 and $782 in the third quarter of 2025.
Driving Financial Services Revenue Through Product Growth and Monetization. Financial Services revenue increased 25% year-over-year to approximately $525.1 million, while products grew 44% to 23.2 million. Money and Relay drove the greatest absolute growth, each surpassing 8 million products and increasing 34% and 45%, respectively. Invest grew 37% to more than 4 million products, while Credit Card grew 67% to approximately 656,000 products, with a high percentage of primary card users and strong credit performance. Annualized Financial Services revenue per product increased to approximately $90 in the third quarter from $88 in the second quarter of 2026, reflecting continued monetization through deposits, spending, brokerage, lending referrals, subscriptions, credit card balances and other fee-based services.
Setting Bold Ambitions for 2030. SoFi now has eight businesses that each generated over $100 million in annualized revenue. By 2030, the Company plans on reaching 100 million products and scaling five businesses to more than $1 billion in revenue, five businesses to more than $500 million in revenue and five businesses to more than $100 million in revenue. This reflects SoFi's strategy to evolve from a single-product student loan refinancing business in 2018 to an everything financial app serving 17.1 million members today.

Consolidated Results

SoFi reported a number of record financial achievements. For the third quarter of 2026, record GAAP net revenue of $1.4 billion increased 44% relative to the prior-year period's $961.6 million. Adjusted net revenue of $1.4 billion was also a record, increasing 44% from the prior-year period of $949.6 million.

For the third quarter of 2026, total fee-based revenue reached $570.6 million, representing 42% of total revenue in the quarter and increasing 21% from the prior quarter. This was driven by strong contributions from origination fees, SoFi Tech Solutions revenue, strong performance from our Loan Platform Business, interchange revenue, and brokerage fee revenue.

Net interest income of $929.5 million for the third quarter was up 59% year-over-year. This was driven by a 51% increase in average interest-earning assets and a 38 basis point decrease in cost of funds, partially offset by a 3 basis point decrease in average asset yields year-over-year. For the third quarter, net interest margin of 6.14% increased 16 basis points from the prior quarter.

During the quarter, average total deposits comprised 90% of average total liabilities. The average rate paid on deposits in the third quarter was 145 basis points lower than that paid on warehouse facilities, which translates to approximately $745.0 million of annualized interest expense savings due to the successful remixing of our funding base.

Third quarter record adjusted EBITDA of $469.8 million increased 70% from the prior year period's $276.9 million. This represents an adjusted EBITDA margin of 34%.

For the third quarter of 2026, GAAP net income reached $241.0 million and diluted earnings per share reached $0.18.

Equity grew by $218.9 million during the quarter to $11.3 billion and $8.71 of book value per share. Tangible book value grew by $234.7 million during the quarter, ending the period at $9.7 billion. Tangible book value per share was $7.48 at quarter-end, up from $5.97 per share in the prior year period, a 25% increase.

Member and Product Growth

Continued growth in both total members and products in the third quarter is the result of our continued investments in innovation and brand building and reflects the benefits of our broad product suite and unique FSPL strategy. SoFi has reached significant scale whereby member growth, product growth, and revenue per product are key drivers of business performance.

SoFi added a record 1.2 million members in the third quarter of 2026, bringing total members to 17.1 million, up 35% from 12.6 million at the end of the same prior year period.

SoFi also achieved record product additions of 2.2 million in the third quarter of 2026, bringing total products to nearly 26.7 million, up 44% from 18.6 million at the end of the same prior year period.

Financial Services products increased by 44% year-over-year to 23.2 million, primarily driven by continued demand for our SoFi Money, Relay and Invest products, and drove 88% of our total product growth. Financial Services products account for 87% of total products.

Lending products increased by 39% year-over-year to 3.4 million, driven by continued demand for personal, student, and home loan products.

Technology Platform-enabled accounts decreased 11% year-over-year to 140 million, including the impact from a large client which fully transitioned off the platform prior to December 31, 2025. Technology Platform-enabled accounts increased 6 million from the prior quarter.

Financial Services Segment Results

For the third quarter of 2026, Financial Services segment net revenue of $525.1 million increased 25% from the prior year period. Financial services revenue per product was $90 compared to $88 in the second quarter of 2026.

Financial Services segment performance was driven by a combination of noninterest income which increased 13% year-over-year to $243.8 million and net interest income which increased 38% year-over-year to $281.3 million, primarily driven by growth in consumer deposits.

In the third quarter, SoFi's Loan Platform Business added $153.4 million to our consolidated adjusted net revenue. Of this, $151.2 million was driven by $3.8 billion of loans originated on behalf of third parties as well as referrals to third parties.

In addition to our Loan Platform Business, SoFi continued to see healthy growth in interchange fee revenue and brokerage fee revenue. In the third quarter, Financial Services interchange fee revenue was up 77% year-over-year, as a result of $31 billion in total annualized spend in the quarter across SoFi Money and Credit Card. Brokerage fee revenue was up 47% year-over-year, reflecting strong member demand and increased monetization.

Contribution profit for the third quarter of 2026 reached $261.0 million, a $35.5 million improvement over the prior year period, while contribution margin declined 4 percentage points year-over-year to 50%.

Red-team questions (skeptical reader):

1. "Record net revenue $1.4B, $241M net income" — is this GAAP or adjusted? The headline conflates figures: $1,385.1M total net revenue is GAAP; $241.0M net income is GAAP; but the widely-quoted "$1.4B" and "$243M net income / $470M EBITDA" are the *adjusted* figures. The note's source separates them correctly — verify downstream summaries don't blur GAAP vs non-GAAP.

2. How much of the $241M GAAP net income is organic operating profit vs. one-off tax or fair-value marks? GAAP net income (+73%) grew faster than adjusted net income (+74% is similar), so the gap looks modest — but confirm no large deferred-tax benefit is flattering the YoY.

3. "+44% revenue" — how much is capital-light fee revenue vs. balance-sheet NII? NII is $929.5M of $1,385M total (~67%); fee-based is $570.6M (42% of total — note these two overlap in categorization). The growth is still heavily NII-driven, i.e. balance-sheet-dependent, not purely fee-light.

4. NIM 6.14% rose largely on a 38bps cost-of-funds drop from deposit remixing. Is this repeatable, or a one-time funding-mix benefit now largely harvested (deposits already ~90% of liabilities)?

5. Loan Platform Business added ~$3.8B third-party originations (~$153.4M revenue). How concentrated is this among a few partners, and is the revenue recognized upfront (gain-on-sale style) and thus lumpy?

6. Lending revenue per product rose to ~$942 — is that pricing power, or mix shift toward higher-yield (riskier) personal loans? Personal Loan products (~2.6M) dominate lending.

7. Credit quality: management says "in line with expectations," but gives no charge-off/delinquency number in this excerpt. What is the actual net charge-off rate and reserve coverage, and is it rising?

8. Technology Platform accounts fell 11% YoY (a large client exited). Is the "eight $100M+ businesses" narrative masking a structurally shrinking B2B segment (Galileo/Technisys)?

9. The 2027 outlook ($6.4B revenue, $0.90 EPS) implies ~27% growth decelerating from ~44%. Is the 2030 target (100M products, five $1B businesses) a credible plan or an aspirational framing device?

10. Products-per-member only rose from 1.47 to 1.56 despite the FSPL cross-sell narrative — is cross-sell actually accelerating, or is growth mostly new-member-driven?

11. Stock jumped ~11-13% — was the beat vs. a lowered bar? Street was ~$1.25B revenue; check whether estimates had been cut into the print.

12. Contribution margin in Financial Services fell 4pts YoY to 50%. Does scaling the newer products (Credit Card +67%, crypto) dilute unit economics?

13. EPS $0.18 flat with adjusted — but share count: did buybacks or dilution (convertibles, SBC) affect the per-share figure versus absolute income growth?

14. Interchange +77% and brokerage +47% are strong — but off small bases. How material are they to the $525M FS revenue, and are they durable or spend-cycle-sensitive?

15. Is any of this a duplicate of prior vault coverage? No — prior SoFi earnings note is Q4 2025 (released Jan 2026); this is a distinct new reporting period (Q3 2026, released 27 Oct 2026). Fresh.

Importance: 4/5 — A clean beat-and-raise from one of the few profitable at-scale US neobanks (17.1M members, 12th straight profitable quarter, 20th consecutive Rule-of-40), with FY2026 guidance ($5.04B) above consensus and a bold 2027/2030 roadmap; materially moves the SoFi investment narrative and sets a benchmark for neobank profitability. Short of 5/5 because it is an expected quarterly print rather than a structural/regulatory inflection, and much of the upside (NIM remix, deposit funding) is already largely harvested.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector context — US digital bank / neobank landscape.** SoFi's Q3 2026 print lands it firmly among the small cohort of US neobanks that have reached profitable scale. US digital-banking primary-account users crossed ~89M in 2025, with the market led by Chime and SoFi (Varo a smaller structural player). What distinguishes SoFi is that it is a chartered national bank running a genuinely diversified "everything app" model — eight distinct businesses each now over $100M in annualized revenue — rather than a single interchange-driven deposit product. That diversification shows in the Q3 mix: Lending adjusted net revenue ~$807M, Financial Services segment revenue $525M (+25% YoY), fee-based revenue $570.6M (42% of total), and net interest income $929.5M (+59% YoY) on a 6.14% net interest margin.

**In-base comps (news DB).**
- *SoFi itself, trajectory:* GAAP net income has climbed steadily — the Q4 2025 / full-year narrative (SoFi "hits billion-dollar milestone," Feb 2026 note) through to GAAP net income of $174M reported earlier in 2026; Q3 2026's $241.0M net income and $1.385B GAAP revenue (+44% YoY from $961.6M) extend a 12th consecutive profitable quarter and a 20th consecutive Rule-of-40 quarter (score of 79). This is a reacceleration, not just continuation: YoY revenue growth of 44% is above the ~43% nine-month pace.
- *Chime (US neobank, direct comp):* FY2025 revenue ~$2.19B (+31%), 87-88% gross margin, but still posting GAAP net losses (Q2 2025 net loss $923M, Q3 2025 continued losses) against ~22M customers / 9.5M active members. The contrast is stark — Chime has the larger raw user base but has not reached GAAP profitability, while SoFi monetizes a smaller 17.1M-member base (+35% YoY) across far more products (26.7M, +44%; 1.56 products/member) at bank-level economics.
- *Nubank (LatAm neobank benchmark for profitable scale):* Q4 2025 revenue ~$4.9B / net income $895M / 33% ROE; Q1 2026 >$5B revenue / $871M net profit / 135M customers. Nubank remains the global profitability benchmark; SoFi's ~10% ROTCE and $241M quarterly net income are an order of magnitude smaller but growing faster off a US bank cost-of-funds advantage (deposits 90% of average liabilities, ~145bps cheaper than warehouse funding, ~$745M annualized interest savings).
- *Robinhood (adjacent US consumer-finance comp):* Q3 2025 net income $556M (+271% YoY) — a reminder that SoFi's Invest segment (>4M products, +37%) competes in a brokerage market where Robinhood is both larger and highly profitable.

**Positioning read.** Against this set, SoFi occupies a differentiated niche: the only US player combining neobank-scale user growth, a national bank charter (funding/NIM advantage), and a lending engine (record $16.9B originations, incl. $3.8B capital-light Loan Platform Business). Its raised 2026 guidance ($5.04B adjusted net revenue, +40%; $1.66B adjusted EBITDA; $840M net income) and newly introduced 2027 outlook ($6.4B revenue, +27%; $2.24B EBITDA; ~20% adjusted net income margin; $0.90 EPS) both landed above FactSet analyst estimates (as of Oct 26, 2026).

**Market reaction.** The print was a clear beat-and-raise: adjusted EPS $0.18 vs ~$0.17 consensus and revenue $1.39B vs ~$1.25B estimate. SOFI shares jumped ~11% (to ~$16.08 premarket from a $14.46 close), with sell-side framing it as a relief rally. Consensus rating was Moderate Buy with an average price target around $20, implying meaningful upside if the 2027 outlook holds.

**Risk flags.**
1. *Valuation / expectations reset.* The 2027 outlook is set "well ahead of analyst estimates," which raises the bar for future quarters; an ~11% one-day move shows the stock now prices in sustained 27%+ growth and leaves little room for disappointment.
2. *Credit cycle exposure.* Lending is the single largest revenue driver (~$807M, 3.4M products incl. 2.6M personal loans). Q3 credit performance was "in line," but personal-loan-heavy consumer lending is cyclically sensitive; any deterioration in consumer credit or funding spreads would hit the segment that still anchors revenue.
3. *Tech Platform softness.* Technology Platform-enabled accounts fell 11% YoY to 140M on a large client's full transition off the platform — a concentration/churn risk in the B2B segment despite a 6M sequential rebound.
4. *Rate sensitivity of the NIM advantage.* The ~$745M annualized interest-expense savings and 6.14% NIM depend on a favorable deposit/warehouse funding spread; a shift in the rate environment or deposit-pricing competition (Chime, Cash App, high-yield challengers) could compress the funding edge.
5. *Contribution-margin pressure.* Financial Services contribution margin fell 4pts YoY to 50% even as contribution profit grew — monetization per product is rising only slowly (~$90 vs $88 QoQ), so growth is still more volume- than margin-led.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (headline read).** BEAT · GAAP net revenue $1.385bn (+44% YoY; vs public consensus ~$1.25bn, +$0.14bn / +11.2%) · GAAP EPS $0.18 / adj EPS $0.18 (vs Zacks $0.17, +$0.01 / +5.9%) · drivers: Lending re-acceleration (originations record $16.9bn) + Financial Services monetization (interchange +77%) · guidance RAISED (FY26 adj net revenue to $5.04bn, above Street) and 2027 outlook INTRODUCED. Stock reacted +11-13% after hours. Primary source: SoFi Q3 2026 release (quarter ended Sep 30, 2026).

**Key figures (with growth).**
- GAAP total net revenue $1,385.1mn, +44% YoY (vs $961.6mn in Q3 2025); 9M $3,704.1mn, +43%.
- Adjusted net revenue $1,371.1mn, +44% YoY (vs $949.6mn).
- GAAP net income $241.0mn, +73% YoY (vs $139.4mn); 9M $564.3mn, +83%. Adjusted net income $243.0mn, +74%.
- GAAP diluted EPS $0.18, +64% YoY (vs $0.11); adj diluted EPS $0.18. (FactSet-based consensus $0.17 per the release's footnote dated Oct 26, 2026.)
- Adjusted EBITDA $469.8mn, +70% YoY (vs $276.9mn); margin 34%.
- Net interest income $929.5mn, +59% YoY; NIM 6.14%, +16bps QoQ. Cost of funds -38bps YoY; deposits = 90% of average liabilities (≈$745mn annualized interest-savings vs warehouse).
- Fee-based revenue $570.6mn = 42% of total revenue, +21% QoQ.
- Members 17.1mn, +35% YoY (record +1.2mn adds). Products 26.7mn, +44% YoY (record +2.2mn adds). Products/member 1.56 vs 1.47 a year ago; 51% of new products opened by existing members.
- Rule of 40 score 79 (20th consecutive quarter ≥40). 12th consecutive profitable quarter. ROTCE ≈10%. TBV/share $7.48 vs $5.97 YoY (+25%); equity $11.3bn, $8.71 book value/share.

**By segment / driver.**
- **Lending** re-accelerated: record total originations $16.9bn (incl. ≈$3.8bn Loan Platform Business on behalf of third parties); Lending adj net revenue ≈$807.4mn; products +39% YoY to 3.4mn (Personal 2.6mn, Student 760k, Home 72k). Lending adj revenue/product rose to ≈$942 vs ≈$920 in Q2 2026 and $782 in Q3 2025 — monetization improving, credit "in line with expectations."
- **Financial Services**: net revenue $525.1mn, +25% YoY; products +44% to 23.2mn (88% of total product adds). Noninterest income +13% to $243.8mn; segment NII +38% to $281.3mn on deposit growth. Interchange +77% YoY (≈$31bn annualized spend across Money + Credit Card); brokerage fee rev +47%. Credit Card products +67% to ≈656k. FS revenue/product $90 vs $88 in Q2 2026. Contribution profit $261.0mn (+$35.5mn YoY) but contribution margin -4pts YoY to 50% — reinvestment / mix drag worth watching.
- **Tech Platform**: enabled accounts -11% YoY to 140mn (a large client fully transitioned off before Dec 31, 2025), though +6mn QoQ — the lone soft line; still de-emphasized vs the FS/Lending engine.

**vs expectations / prior period.** Beat on both lines: revenue $1.39bn vs ≈$1.25bn public consensus = +$0.14bn / +11.2% (surprise +9.08% per Zacks); adj EPS $0.18 vs $0.17 Zacks = +5.9% surprise (expected as of Oct 26, 2026). The revenue beat is larger than the EPS beat, consistent with the market reaction being driven by top-line/guidance rather than bottom-line. Sequentially: net revenue $1.385bn vs Q2 2026 $1.22bn (+≈13% QoQ); GAAP net income $241.0mn vs Q2 2026 $156.6mn (+54% QoQ); EPS $0.18 vs $0.12 QoQ. YoY momentum strong and broadly stable (Q2 2026 +43%, Q3 2026 +44% net revenue) — growth is holding at ~mid-40s% while profit growth outpaces revenue (operating leverage: NI +73% vs revenue +44%). No prior SoFi earnings note exists in the base to wikilink (prior corpus notes are product/ops items).

**Guidance / forward.** RAISED. FY26 now ≈$5.04bn adjusted net revenue (+40% YoY), $1.66bn adjusted EBITDA, $840.0mn net income, $0.61 EPS — up from the ~$4.75–4.85bn adj net revenue FY26 range guided at Q2 2026, and above FactSet analyst estimates per the release. Management framed the raise as both the Q3 beat and higher Q4 expectations. Separately INTRODUCED a 2027 outlook: ≈$6.4bn adjusted net revenue (+≈27%), $2.24bn adjusted EBITDA, $1.28bn net income, $0.90 EPS, with a ~20% adj net income margin — all described as ahead of estimates. A 2030 ambition (100mn products; five $1bn + five $500mn + five $100mn businesses) was added as a long-range frame. Management tone (CEO Noto) confident/aggressive ("conviction... never been greater"). Plausibility: the raise is credible given 12 straight profitable quarters and 20 straight Rule-of-40 prints, but a multi-year 2027 guide introduced on a beat is a high bar — sets up elevated expectations.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. **Lending re-acceleration is real, not just Loan Platform fees.** Record $16.9bn originations with revenue/product up to $942 (from $782 YoY) → fact: Lending is monetizing better; why: mix of capital-light LPB (~$3.8bn originated for third parties) plus balance-sheet lending; why it matters: validates the shift from pure balance-sheet risk toward fee-light origination; second-order: eases the perennial credit/capital bear case if credit stays "in line."
2. **Operating leverage: NI +73% vs revenue +44%.** Fact: profit growing far faster than revenue; why: NIM +16bps QoQ, cost of funds -38bps YoY (deposit remix saving ~$745mn annualized vs warehouse); matters: the deposit-funded bank model is now the margin engine; second-order: rate cuts could compress NIM — the 6.14% NIM is a watch item into 2027.
3. **FS contribution margin -4pts YoY to 50% despite +25% revenue.** Fact: segment revenue up but contribution margin down; why: reinvestment / faster growth in lower-margin products (Credit Card +67%, interchange-led); matters: tempers the "monetization flywheel" story; second-order: watch whether FS revenue/product ($90) keeps climbing or stalls as mix shifts.
4. **Tech Platform accounts -11% YoY (lost a large client).** Fact: the one declining line; why: a major client fully offboarded by end-2025; matters: Tech Platform was the original "AWS of fintech" bull case and it is now a drag, not a driver; second-order: SoFi is leaning on consumer FS/Lending, so platform softness is tolerable near-term but undercuts the diversification thesis.
