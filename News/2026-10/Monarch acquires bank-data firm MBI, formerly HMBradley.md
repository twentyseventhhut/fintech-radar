# PFM app Monarch buys banking infrastructure firm MBI

MBI previously operated as consumer fintech HMBradley before shifting its focus to B2B services in 2023.

Personal financial management app (PFM) Monarch has acquired California-based fintech MBI, formerly known as HMBradley, for an undisclosed sum.

Founded in 2018, MBI provides data infrastructure serving banks and credit unions. The company initially launched as a consumer fintech before shifting its focus to B2B services in 2023.

At the time of that strategic pivot, CEO and co-founder Zach Brunke acknowledged that, "Throughout 2023, it became evident that our consumer brand growth was falling short of our goals". The company had previously secured more than $70 million in funding.

The entire MBI team will transfer over to Monarch, including Brunke, who has started a new role heading Monarch Labs, a newly established internal innovation and product division.

The acquisition coincides with the PFM app hitting $100 million in annual recurring revenue. This financial milestone, along with the integration of MBI's team members, will support Monarch in its long-term goal to be a "one-stop destination for member financial needs", according to a company statement.

Serving over one million active users, Monarch's platform offers budgeting, planning, and financial tracking tools for both retail and business customers. Led by co-founder and CEO Val Agostino, the company is backed by the likes of FPV Ventures, Accel, and Forerunner Ventures.

# Context-enrichment: Monarch acquires bank-data firm MBI (formerly HMBradley)
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

**Freshness/duplicate verdict: FRESH.** This is the only note in the corpus on the Monarch/MBI deal; no prior coverage of Monarch-the-PFM or of HMBradley's 2023 B2B pivot exists in News/. (Internal grep: the only other "Monarch" hits are unrelated — a South African furniture retailer, a BoE banknote story, etc.) The deal itself was announced ~1-2 Oct 2026 (PR Newswire / monarch.com blog); the FinTech Futures write-up is dated Oct 5 and it entered our digest on Oct 15 — same single event, not a re-report. New development, not a re-run.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- PFM app **Monarch** (Monarch Money) acquired **MBI** — the B2B remnant of neobank **HMBradley** — for an **undisclosed sum**. Announced ~1 Oct 2026; Monarch's first-ever acquisition.
- This is substantively an **acqui-hire**: "the entire MBI team will transfer" and MBI reportedly had only **~18 employees** (search: multiple profiles). Founder/CEO **Zach Bruhnke** becomes head of a new internal "**Monarch Labs**" innovation/product division. The PR frames it as buying "data infrastructure serving banks and credit unions," but what actually moves is a small team + IP, not a book of bank/CU customers with disclosed revenue.
- **Why framed this way:** "acquires banking-infrastructure firm" + "$100M ARR milestone" reads as Monarch leveling-up into infrastructure. De-PR'd, it is a profitable-ish consumer subscription app ($100M ARR, >1M members) absorbing an 18-person engineering team whose own B2B pivot had stalled. No deal price, no MBI revenue, no named bank/CU clients, no synergy numbers disclosed → the silence is the story.
- **Second order:** the genuinely notable number is Monarch's own — **$100M ARR, >1M members** — not the acquisition. The MBI buy is a talent/IP bolt-on timed to (and overshadowed by) that milestone.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **PFM / budgeting:** Monarch's real battleground is consumer PFM after **Intuit shut down Mint** (Dec 2023/Jan 2024). Peers: **Copilot, YNAB, Rocket Money (Rocket/ex-Truebill), Empower (Personal Capital), Origin, Quicken Simplifi**. Monarch won the "Mint migration" (reportedly ~20x subscriber surge, 6x growth in 2024) and is now the category leader by ARR.
- **The "infrastructure" Monarch is NOT buying into head-on:** real bank-data aggregation incumbents — **Plaid, MX, Finicity (Mastercard), Akoya, Tink/Trustly, Yodlee** — plus up-and-comers like [[Quiltt raises $2.6M to simplify open banking integrations]] (unified aggregation API) and open-banking rail builders ([[TrueLayer to acquire Nordic open-banking rival Zimpler]], [[vobapay integrates Mastercard Open Finance for A2A payments]]). MBI is NOT a Plaid competitor; it is a "modular banking middleware" for FIs, a different and far smaller niche. So positioning MBI as a strategic entry into "bank-data infrastructure" is a stretch.
- **Why the landscape is this way:** consumer PFM is a thin subscription business dependent on aggregation it doesn't own (Plaid/MX sit upstream, extracting a per-connection toll and controlling data reliability). That dependency is the structural pressure driving Monarch's interest in owning data plumbing — see [2]/[3].

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Monarch:** founded 2018 (Val Agostino, ex-Mint PM); $4.8M seed (Jul 2021, Accel/SignalFire/Clocktower); $15M Series A (early 2022, Menlo); **$75M Series B May 2025 at ~$850M post** (FPV + Forerunner co-lead). Grew ~6x in 2024 post-Mint; now **$100M ARR, >1M members**, Monarch Plus premium tier. Context: Series B was done on only ~$12.6M ARR in 2025 — so the jump to $100M ARR in ~18 months is the real headline (if the ARR figure is clean and not gross-of-churn).
- **MBI/HMBradley:** founded 2018; raised **$70M+** (incl. an $18M round; backers reportedly Acrew, Walkabout, etc.). Hit a growth wall — partner bank (Hatch Bank) capped growth, 18-month new-user freeze, CAC more than doubled as rate competition intensified — and **wound down consumer banking in Nov 2023**, pivoting to sell its core/middleware tech to banks & CUs. That B2B pivot evidently didn't reach escape velocity either → sale for undisclosed (likely modest) terms.
- **Fit (analysis):** logical as a cheap acqui-hire of a founder (Bruhnke) with core-banking/ledger expertise to staff "Monarch Labs," plausibly to reduce reliance on Plaid/MX and/or to build toward deposit/account products ("one-stop destination for member financial needs"). The structural logic: a subscription PFM wants to climb the stack toward owning the data connection and eventually monetizable financial products (not just a $10/mo budgeting fee). **But** buying an 18-person team ≠ building Plaid-grade aggregation; this is direction-of-travel, not capability acquired.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Real novelty: low.** No new product shipped; "Monarch Labs" is announced, not launched. The value-add is talent + optionality, not a live infrastructure capability. MBI's own traction (named FI clients, ARR, deposits under management) is undisclosed — and the pivot stalling suggests it was thin.
- **The durable asset is Monarch's $100M ARR consumer subscription.** That is high-quality, recurring, software-multiple revenue. The MBI tuck-in is immaterial to the financials.
- **Who captures the margin / what breaks it (analysis):** today Monarch is a "dashboard on top of other people's rails" — Plaid/MX own the account connections and can raise prices or degrade reliability (classic PFM vulnerability; it's exactly why Mint-era aggregation was costly). If Monarch Labs can build native aggregation + account/deposit products, it moves from renting plumbing to owning it → better margins + a wedge into interchange/float. If it can't, MBI is just 18 hires and the central question stays "how durable is a premium budgeting subscription once Mint-migration tailwinds fade and Copilot/Rocket compete on price."

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Next:** Monarch Labs to "explore new growth areas and products" — watch for native data-aggregation, business/advisor tiers, or deposit/banking features. No timeline given.
- **Sentiment:** positive founder/VC framing (Accel, Forerunner, FPV); the $100M ARR number is the market signal, not the M&A.
- **Risks / second order:** (1) PFM is a graveyard — Mint died inside Intuit despite scale because monetization is hard; a premium subscription caps TAM (only paying users), as critics note. (2) Reliance on upstream aggregators is a standing margin risk. (3) Acqui-hire integration risk: a founder who couldn't scale either consumer or B2B now runs "Labs." **Counterintuitive:** the strongest read of this deal is defensive/opportunistic talent capture at a depressed price during a fintech down-cycle — not an infrastructure strategy. If judged as "Monarch becomes a bank-data infra player," importance is overstated.

## Top challenge/extra questions (10–15, second-order)
1. **Is the $100M ARR clean recurring subscription, or does it include gross/one-time items?** — Open (self-reported; Series B was on ~$12.6M ARR in 2025, so a ~8x jump in ~18mo warrants scrutiny). Decides whether the real story (ARR) holds.
2. **Was any MBI bank/CU client, revenue, or deposit figure disclosed?** — No. Silence implies thin B2B traction → acqui-hire read.
3. **Deal price / structure (cash, stock, earnout)?** — Undisclosed. Likely modest given MBI's stall; material to whether this is strategic or bargain talent.
4. **How many MBI employees actually transferred, and who are the key hires besides Bruhnke?** — ~18 total (search); key = Bruhnke → Monarch Labs. Small team confirms acqui-hire.
5. **Does Monarch intend to replace Plaid/MX with native aggregation, or complement it?** — Open. This is the hinge question for whether the deal changes Monarch's margin structure.
6. **What is Monarch's gross margin and CAC/LTV today?** — Open; determines how much "Labs" R&D the $100M ARR can fund.
7. **Did MBI retain any bank/CU contracts post-acquisition, or is the B2B line being shelved?** — Open. If shelved, "bank-data infrastructure firm" framing is marketing.
8. **How durable is Monarch's lead once Mint-migration one-off tailwind fully laps?** — Open; Copilot/Rocket/YNAB compete; churn unknown.
9. **Is a premium-subscription PFM structurally capped vs. ad/lead-gen or interchange models (why Mint existed)?** — Analysis: yes, TAM is smaller; monetization beyond subscription needs the very banking/data products Labs might build.
10. **What exactly is "modular banking infrastructure" — a core/ledger, a middleware layer, or just data plumbing?** — Middleware/core-adjacent layer over FI vendors (search); NOT consumer aggregation like Plaid.
11. **Any regulatory/data-privacy angle from Monarch moving toward holding deposit/account data directly?** — Open; relevant if it pursues account products.
12. **Why didn't MBI's B2B pivot work after $70M+ raised and "close to several bank deals"?** — Growth stalled; FI sales cycles long; likely ran short of runway → forced sale. (analysis)
13. **Does Bruhnke's track record (couldn't scale consumer OR B2B) make him the right person to run "Labs"?** — Open; integration/execution risk.
14. **Is this deal material to Monarch's trajectory at all, or a footnote beside the $100M ARR milestone?** — Analysis: footnote; the milestone carries the news.
15. **What would make this a 4/5 deal in hindsight?** — If Labs ships native aggregation that cuts Plaid/MX dependency OR a deposit product that adds interchange/float revenue. Absent that, it stays a 2/5 acqui-hire.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US personal-finance-management (PFM) apps are in a post-Mint land-grab: Intuit shut Mint at the start of 2024, vacating ~3.6m users and leaving Rocket Money, Empower, Monarch, YNAB and Copilot to split the subscription-PFM market. Weekly PFM usage has climbed to ~38% of US adults, the highest in Fed consumer surveys, with roughly 95m US monthly actives across the category in early 2026 (per TechBullion, as of 2026). Structure: two distinct layers that this deal fuses — (1) consumer PFM apps (subscription SaaS, fragmented, low switching costs, competing on insight/AI/breadth), and (2) bank-data aggregation/infra (concentrated around Plaid, MX, Finicity/Mastercard; high barriers via bank integrations, data-access agreements, regulation). Why now: the open-banking data layer is both the key input and the key chokepoint for PFM — read-only aggregation is being squeezed as banks move to monetize/gate screen-scraped access (see [[JPMorgan Chase is moving to restrict Checkbook's access to its customer data,]], 2026-01), so owning data-engineering talent de-risks dependence on third-party rails. (analysis)

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: ARR, paid-subscriber count / MAU, ARPU, net revenue retention. Monarch disclosed two hard numbers at close: **$100m ARR** (hit Sept 2026) and **>1m members** / ~1.8m MAU (per useLuminix, as of 2026) → implied ARPU ≈ `$100m / 1.0m = ~$100/yr` on members (analysis; Monarch Plus premium tier). Players & basis of competition: Rocket Money (~15m MAU, bundled bill-negotiation, Capital One/Truebill scale), Empower (~7m), Monarch (~1.8m), YNAB (~1.4m, methodology/loyalty), Copilot (~0.9m, Apple-ecosystem design) — competition is on product breadth, AI insight, and data-connection reliability, not price. Recent peer moves: OpenAI pushing into the consumer-finance surface ([[OpenAI acquires personal investing app Roi]] 2025-10; [[OpenAI acquires Hiro Finance to expand ChatGPT finance]] 2026-04) raises the risk of an AI assistant disintermediating standalone PFM. Monarch's position: #3-by-users but premium/paid-heavy and now vertically integrating — buying MBI (ex-HMBradley) brings a bank/credit-union data-infra team + CEO Zach Bruhnke into "Monarch Labs." Moat (analysis): switching costs (configured budgets/linked accounts) + now in-house data-connectivity engineering; not network effects.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal terms undisclosed (no price, no multiple) → EV/Rev, EV/EBITDA = **no data**. MBI context: founded 2018, raised **>$70m** as consumer HMBradley, pivoted to B2B data infra in 2023 after consumer growth stalled — i.e. an acqui-hire of a distressed pivot, not a premium infra buy (analysis). Reference points for the data-infra layer MBI plays in:
- Plaid — **$8bn** valuation (Feb-2026 tender) on **$546m** 2025 revenue, +40% YoY → implied `$8bn / $546m = ~14.6x` revenue (round/tender valuation, not market cap; per Crunchbase/Sacra, as of 2026-02) [[Plaid completes tender offer at $8 billion valuation]].
- Quiltt — open-banking integration layer, $2.6m raise ([[Quiltt raises $2.6M to simplify open banking integrations]] 2025-10), illustrates how cheap the long tail of aggregation tooling is.
Distribution not computed (only one usable revenue multiple). Qualitative read: the aggregation layer is richly valued at the top (Plaid ~14.6x, justified by 40% growth + profitability) but commoditizing below it; MBI sits in that commoditizing tail, consistent with an undisclosed (likely modest) price. Monarch's own $100m ARR, VC-backed (FPV, Accel, Forerunner), has no disclosed valuation → **[UNSOURCED]**.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Data-access chokepoint (not solved by this deal).** Owning MBI's engineers does not grant Monarch the bank data-sharing agreements Plaid/MX hold; as banks gate screen-scraping ([[JPMorgan Chase is moving to restrict Checkbook's access to its customer data,]]), Monarch may still depend on third-party aggregators — the vertical-integration thesis is partly aspirational.
2. **Acqui-hire of a failed pivot.** MBI twice changed its model (consumer→B2B→absorbed) and raised >$70m; integration + culture risk, and the >$70m prior funding implies investors likely took a markdown on an undisclosed-price exit.
3. **Disintermediation by AI assistants.** If ChatGPT/OpenAI-type agents ([[OpenAI acquires Hiro Finance to expand ChatGPT finance]]) absorb budgeting/insight natively, standalone PFM ARR (Monarch's $100m) is exposed at the exact moment it is scaling paid tiers.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Signals the PFM category's next phase is vertical integration down the stack — apps buying data/infra talent to control connectivity and feed "action, not just insight" (payments, products) on top of linked data. Falsifiable thesis: if Monarch Labs ships bank-sourced product features (e.g. direct account-opening / money-movement) within ~12 months, the MBI buy was strategic; if MBI's team is only used to shore up existing connections, it was a cheap talent grab. Trigger to watch: a Monarch data-partnership or licensing announcement with banks/credit unions — the real tell on whether it can bypass Plaid.
