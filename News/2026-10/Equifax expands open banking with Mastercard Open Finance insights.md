Equifax expands access to cash flow data solutions for confident decisioning
Expanded collaboration with Mastercard Open Finance extends access to consumer-permissioned financial insights alongside Equifax credit data
Equifax is providing more open banking choice for lenders and fintechs through consumer-permissioned insights from Mastercard Open Finance.
Lenders and fintechs can choose the cash flow data option that fits their needs and use it alongside Equifax credit data in their credit decisions.
Cash flow data insights allow individuals to build a more complete financial profile, helping lenders expand access to credit to the 76.1 million thin-file or credit-invisible consumers in the U.S. 1 

 ATLANTA , Oct. 16, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Equifax ® (NYSE: EFX) is expanding its open banking solutions with the addition of consumer-permissioned insights enabled via Mastercard Open Finance, giving lenders and fintechs more choice in the open finance provider they use alongside the Equifax consumer credit file. Mastercard Open Finance's Cashflow Analytics, Payment Risk Insights and Transaction Report are now available to Equifax customers.
 
Building on a longstanding commitment to expanding financial access, the full suite of Equifax Open Banking Solutions utilizes consumer-permissioned cash flow insights to integrate bank transaction data, attributes, or scores into lending and portfolio management strategies. This enhanced visibility helps consumers create a more robust personal financial profile and enables financial institutions to responsibly broaden credit access and streamline approvals for the 76.1 million thin-file or credit invisible U.S. consumers 1 .
These expanded solutions convert complex bank transaction streams into actionable, real-time credit intelligence designed for seamless integration into existing underwriting stacks:
Cashflow Analytics analyzes up to 24 months of credits, debits, and balances to produce cash flow attributes lenders can use in underwriting.
Payments Risk Insights delivers a score that helps lenders predict repayment risk and spot early signs of repayment stress.
Transaction Report provides six+ months of cleansed, categorized, and enhanced bank transaction data, giving lenders more flexibility to utilize cash flow in their underwriting and decisioning models.
"Financial institutions have diverse operational needs, meaning a single cash flow partner does not necessarily fit each organization's needs. Our goal is to give our customers the flexibility to choose the open finance provider that works for them and provide that data alongside Equifax credit and alternative data to make confident decisions that can open up new opportunities for consumers," said Jacob Osborne, Senior Vice President and General Manager, Fintech and Payments at Equifax.
Each organization's requirements for cash flow data can vary across product types from personal loans to credit cards to commercial credit lines, because each carries its own repayment structure, risk profile, and decisioning needs. Equifax gives lenders a choice of solution and open finance provider to best fit its specific consumers, systems, and strategy. This open approach is designed to eliminate integration friction, speed up deployment, and help lenders make confident decisions across every product line.
"As lenders and fintechs look to gain a more complete view of financial health, access to trusted, consumer-permissioned cash flow insights becomes increasingly important," said Bart Willaert, Executive Vice President, Open Finance, Americas, Mastercard. "Our collaboration with Equifax will help organizations make faster, more confident decisions while unlocking new opportunities to expand access to credit."
Whether a lender prefers to analyze detailed attributes or rely on ready-made scores that leverage bank transaction data, Equifax can help through our  Equifax Open Banking Solutions .
Mastercard Open Finance solutions are provided by Finicity Corporation, a Mastercard company.

# Context-enrichment: Equifax expands open banking with Mastercard Open Finance insights
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

**FRESHNESS: fresh.** New US event (announced Oct 16, 2026) — Equifax adds Mastercard Open Finance (Finicity) consumer-permissioned cash-flow products alongside the Equifax credit file. This is NOT a duplicate of [[Mastercard and Equifax launch Open Score in Australia]] (Jul 2025), which was a joint SMB "Open Score" product in Australia. Different geography (US), different mechanism (Equifax reselling Mastercard/Finicity cash-flow data/attributes into its own Open Banking Solutions suite, not a co-branded score). Related but distinct event → fresh.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Equifax (NYSE: EFX) is adding three Mastercard Open Finance (delivered by Finicity, a Mastercard company) consumer-permissioned data products — **Cashflow Analytics** (up to 24 months of credits/debits/balances → cash-flow attributes), **Payment Risk Insights** (a repayment-risk score / early-stress signal), and **Transaction Report** (6+ months of cleansed, categorized bank-transaction data) — as options that lenders/fintechs can consume alongside the Equifax credit file. Press release Oct 16, 2026 (investor.equifax.com / PR Newswire).
- **De-PR:** This is an "expanded collaboration," i.e. Equifax adding Mastercard as *another* open-finance supplier option into its existing Equifax Open Banking Solutions — a distribution/reseller-style expansion, not a new co-built product. The headline "expands open banking" overstates: the capability (cash-flow data in underwriting) already existed at Equifax; what changed is *provider choice*.
- **No economics disclosed:** no revenue terms, no exclusivity, no pricing, no committed-client count. "Now available to Equifax customers" = technically live, but zero named lenders or adoption metrics.
- **The 76.1M "thin-file/credit-invisible" figure** is Equifax's own TAM framing (footnoted "Equifax data and analytics"), a marketing anchor, not a conversion number.
- **Why structured this way:** Equifax explicitly positions multi-provider optionality ("a single cash flow partner does not necessarily fit each organization's needs") — i.e. Equifax wants to be the *aggregation/decisioning layer* and stay provider-agnostic, pulling margin at the credit-file + orchestration layer while commoditizing the underlying cash-flow feed (Mastercard/Finicity vs Plaid vs others).

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Experian** — the most direct competitor move: launched **Experian Credit + Cashflow Score** (Nov 10, 2025) and **Cashflow Attributes for commercial lenders** + a new "Cashflow Data Bureau" / cashflow CRA (Oct 6, 2026, days before this Equifax release). Experian is bundling its own cash-flow + 220M-consumer credit file.
- **FICO** — made **UltraFICO** (FICO score + Plaid bank data) generally available May 2026; upgraded its cash-flow-powered score with real-time data.
- **Plaid** — the aggregator feeding FICO/UltraFICO; pushes cash-flow underwriting directly.
- **Nova Credit, Prism, VantageScore** — also offer cash-flow scores/attributes.
- **Position:** Equifax is roughly at **parity / slightly catching up** to Experian, which announced a more integrated bundle (own score + own cashflow CRA) a week earlier. Equifax's differentiator is explicitly *provider choice* rather than a single proprietary cashflow score.
- **Why the landscape is this way (analysis):** all three bureaus face the same structural pressure — traditional credit-file data is a mature, slow-growth commodity; cash-flow/alternative data is the growth vector and the way to address the "credit-invisible" TAM. Second-order: if cash-flow attributes become table-stakes, the bureaus' moat shifts from *who has the data* to *who owns the lender integration + decisioning workflow*.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Equifax has an established **Equifax Open Banking Solutions** suite; this bolts Mastercard/Finicity into it. Fits the multi-year "differentiated data + cloud + analytics" strategy (Equifax Cloud).
- Prior Equifax+Mastercard tie: [[Mastercard and Equifax launch Open Score in Australia]] (Jul 2025) — so this is a *longstanding* relationship being activated in a new market/form, consistent with the PR's "longstanding commitment" language.
- Other 2026 Equifax moves in corpus: [[Equifax to acquire Círculo de Crédito in Mexico]] (Jul 2026, international expansion), [[NOTO partners with Equifax UK on real-time AML screening]] (Jul 2026). Shows Equifax actively expanding data breadth via partnerships + M&A.
- **Why (analysis):** Equifax's core USIS/credit-file revenue is a commodity take-rate business; it needs higher-multiple software/analytics attach. Adding provider-agnostic cash-flow optionality is a low-capex way to defend the decisioning layer against Experian/FICO without building/buying another aggregator.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** Cash-flow underwriting, Finicity data, and Equifax Open Banking Solutions all pre-exist. The genuinely new bit is narrow: Mastercard/Finicity products are *now one of the selectable providers* inside Equifax's suite. Experian/FICO already shipped comparable (arguably more integrated) offerings in 2025–2026.
- **Traction: unproven.** "Now available" ≠ adopted. No live lender names, no volume, no conversion lift stats. The 76.1M figure is addressable-market, not booked.
- **Who captures the margin (analysis):** the underlying bank-data feed (Finicity/Plaid) is commoditizing; the durable margin sits with whoever owns (a) the proprietary credit file and (b) the lender decisioning integration — i.e. Equifax's intent. But by making the cashflow provider swappable, Equifax also signals the cashflow layer itself is low-moat.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Regulatory backdrop is the key swing factor:** CFPB **Section 1033** (open-banking rule) is in limbo — finalized Oct 2024, preliminarily enjoined Oct 2025 (Forcht Bank / BPI / Kentucky Bankers v. CFPB, E.D. Ky.), and a CFPB rewrite was sent to OIRA Aug 6, 2026 that reportedly *reverses* the ban on banks charging for data access. If banks can charge for data access, aggregators' (Finicity/Plaid) input costs rise → squeezes cash-flow-data economics across all bureaus.
- **Market sizing:** global open-banking market ~$37bn (2025) → ~$46.5bn (2026) est.; US ~$9.92bn (2026) est. (secondary analyst estimates, unverified single-source — treat as directional). Mastercard open-banking ~1.8% revenue share (2025, per openbankingtracker, directional).
- **Expect:** more bureau "cash-flow + credit file" bundles; watch whether Equifax discloses any named lender wins or attach metrics on the next earnings call. Counterintuitive second-order (analysis): if 1033 lets banks meter data access, the "credit-invisible inclusion" narrative could stall on cost, not technology.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Subvertical: open-finance / cash-flow underwriting within the US credit-bureau market. Market size (directional, secondary single-source — treat as estimate, not verified): global open-banking ~$37bn (2025) → ~$46.5bn (2026E); US ~$9.92bn (2026E) (per gminsights/secondary aggregators, as of 2026 — [UNSOURCED] on a primary basis). Structure: the US bureau layer is a consolidated oligopoly (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion); the aggregator layer is more fragmented (Plaid, Mastercard/Finicity, Visa/Tink, FIS, MX). Value is created at two points — the proprietary credit file and the lender decisioning integration — while the raw bank-data feed is commoditizing. Barriers: data assets, regulatory/FCRA compliance, lender integration switching costs. Driver / "why now": bureaus are racing to attach alternative/cash-flow data to a mature, slow-growth credit-file business to (a) address the 76.1M thin-file TAM and (b) defend the decisioning layer; regulatory catalyst (CFPB 1033) is simultaneously the biggest headwind.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for bureaus — organic revenue growth, EBITDA margin, Workforce Solutions / USIS mix; for cash-flow products — lender adoption, attach rate, data coverage. Key players and recent moves: **Experian** — Credit + Cashflow Score (Nov 10 2025) and Cashflow Data Bureau / cashflow CRA + commercial Cashflow Attributes (Oct 6 2026, ~10 days before this release); **FICO** — UltraFICO (FICO + Plaid bank data) GA May 2026, real-time upgrade; **Plaid** — aggregator feeding FICO; **Nova Credit / Prism / VantageScore** — cashflow scores/attributes. Basis of competition: breadth/accuracy of data + depth of lender integration, not price. Protagonist position: **parity / slightly catching up** — Equifax's offering is provider-agnostic optionality (Mastercard/Finicity added as one option) rather than a single integrated proprietary cashflow score; Experian shipped a more vertically integrated bundle days earlier. Moat (analysis): Equifax credit file + Equifax Cloud decisioning integration (switching costs); the cashflow feed itself is low-moat by design.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Confirms cash-flow data is becoming table-stakes in US underwriting; the re-rating, if any, accrues to whoever converts it into attach/volume, not to the announcement. Falsifiable thesis (analysis): if Equifax discloses named large-lender adoption or a cashflow attach metric on a coming earnings call, this becomes a real growth lever; absent that, it's a defensive me-too. Trigger to watch: next EFX earnings call commentary + CFPB 1033 final rewrite.
