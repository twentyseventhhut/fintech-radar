 Adyen has marked ten years as a payments partner to digital travel platform Agoda .
The firm is currently Agoda’s payments acquirer across APAC, Europe and the US, and the two companies are exploring whether to extend the partnership into issuing and payouts.
The partnership began in 2015, when Agoda was accelerating its mobile-first strategy in Asia.
Agoda has since grown into a global, multi-product travel platform, and its payment needs have become more layered as it entered new markets.
According to the companies, Adyen has worked to widen Agoda’s currency selection and to find ways to improve payment performance while reducing costs.
That work covers support for local payment methods, so travellers can pay their preferred way in each market, and streamlined authentication for genuine bookings to reduce friction at checkout.
Adyen routes Agoda’s transactions to improve authorisation rates and overall reliability.
 “At Agoda’s scale, improvements in how payments are routed, authorised and settled can have a meaningful impact on performance and the traveller experience,” 
said Kai Tang, Head of Hong Kong, Adyen.
 “There are strong synergies around performance enhancement, whether that’s making payments easier, increasing reliability, or improving overall experience,” 
said Oliver Webster, Chief Financial Officer, Agoda.
The companies said they are exploring how to take the partnership further, including opportunities in issuing and payouts in supported markets, as Agoda continues to invest in customer experience and emerging technology.

### [0] What happened
Adyen and digital travel platform Agoda publicly marked **ten years** of their payments partnership (begun in 2015), and said they are **exploring extending the relationship into issuing and payouts** in supported markets. Adyen is Agoda's payments **acquirer across APAC, Europe and the US**, providing local payment methods, wider currency selection, streamlined authentication for genuine bookings, and intelligent routing to lift authorisation rates while reducing cost. Quotes come from Kai Tang (Head of Hong Kong, Adyen) and Oliver Webster (CFO, Agoda). Source: [fintechnews.sg](https://fintechnews.sg/139648/payments/adyen-agoda-payments-partnership-issuing/), [MARKETECH APAC](https://marketech-apac.com/agoda-adyen-mark-10-year-partnership-to-streamline-travel-payments-worldwide/), [cfotech](https://cfotech.asia/story/agoda-adyen-mark-decade-long-payments-partnership).

### [1] Why it matters
This is a **milestone / relationship-deepening** story, not a new contract. The headline substance is the *exploration* of issuing and payouts — i.e. Adyen moving from pure acquiring (money-in) toward money-out and card-issuing for a large OTA. If realised, it would expand Adyen's share-of-wallet per travel client and extend its **Embedded Financial Products / Issuing** stack into the OTA vertical. For Adyen, travel (airlines, OTAs, hospitality) is a named growth vertical; deepening with an anchor OTA reinforces land-and-expand economics. Nothing is contractually new yet — framing as "exploring."

### [2] Scale & background
- **Agoda** is part of **Booking Holdings** (alongside Booking.com, Priceline, Kayak, OpenTable). Booking Holdings reported **FY2025 gross travel bookings of ~$186.1B (+12.3%)** and revenue ~$26.9B; ~68% of gross bookings now run through its merchant-of-record model (Booking directly processes payments) — context for why OTA payments are strategically central. Source: [BKNG Q3-2025 release](https://s201.q4cdn.com/865305287/files/doc_financials/2025/q3/Q3-25-BKNG-Earnings-Release-Final.pdf), [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/booking-holdings-q4-full-year-2025-earnings).
- Agoda-specific processed-volume with Adyen is **not disclosed**; treat any "scale" claim as directional.
- **Adyen** processed **€649.0B in H1 2025 (+5% YoY, +23% ex one large customer)**; travel is split into airlines / OTAs / hospitality. Source: [Adyen H1 2025 results](https://www.adyen.com/press-and-media/adyen-publishes-h1-2025-financial-results).

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector context — travel/OTA payments & global acquiring.** Agoda sits inside Booking Holdings, the world's largest online travel platform, which reported gross bookings of ~$53.8bn in Q1 2026 (+15% YoY) and ~$51.0bn in Q2 2026 (+9% YoY), guiding full-year 2026 gross bookings to high-single-digit growth ([Booking Q2 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/booking-holdings-q2-2026-slides-record-capital-return-growth-slows-93CH-4836178)). Agoda is Booking's APAC-centric OTA, with a merchant-of-record / "merchant bookings" model where the platform collects payment itself — exactly the profile that drives dependence on a global acquirer. For cross-border OTA volume, the value proposition is concrete: holding direct acquiring licenses in many markets ("local acquiring") can lift authorization rates ~1–3pp vs routing through a third-party acquirer, and on high-volume cross-border portfolios that lift converts straight into booking revenue ([PaymentBrief teardown](https://paymentbrief.com/articles/stripe-vs-adyen-vs-checkout-2026-pricing-teardown/)). This is precisely the "route transactions to improve authorisation rates… widen currency selection… local payment methods" framing in the Agoda announcement.

**Competitive set.** Adyen's enterprise acquiring rivals for a client like Agoda are Stripe, Checkout.com, Worldpay and PayPal/Braintree. Worldpay — the largest card acquirer by transaction volume, present in ~146 countries — was absorbed by Global Payments in a $24.25bn deal closed January 2026, a reminder that scaled global acquiring is consolidating ([Worldpay review 2026](https://thefinrate.com/worldpay-review-2026-pricing-pros-cons/)). Both Adyen and Stripe now market AI-driven auth-optimization ("Adyen Uplift" claims +6% auth; "Stripe Authorization Boost" claims +3.8% acceptance / −3.3% cost), so the performance narrative in the Agoda release is a category-standard pitch, not unique to Adyen ([Stripe vs Adyen](https://bitrupt.co/blog/stripe-vs-adyen)).

**IR grounding — Adyen's own filed results (PRIMARY).** Adyen is a profitable, single-platform acquirer-processor, so Agoda-type OTA volume flows through its headline figures. Per Adyen's filed FY/H2 2025 disclosures:
- **Processed volume:** €745.3bn in H2 2025, +12% YoY (+19% ex. a single large-volume customer); FY2025 €1,394.3bn, +8% YoY (+21% ex. that customer) — the single-customer drag is a real reported distortion to watch ([Adyen H2 2025 shareholder letter](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1lG-dwLzTRsW-ALo6iThPLWhTMmpSDpcK/view)).
- **Net revenue:** FY2025 €2,364.2M, +18% YoY (+21% constant currency); H2 2025 net revenue +17% YoY (+21% CC) ([Adyen FY2025 annual report](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1wu2UZwvFAxcmSP5VlCy1fwdYzVPv_wdn/view), [H2 2025 release](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1acJAsxwwM-338tTKmKUhv3avurwUWJ6a/view)).
- **Profitability:** H2 2025 EBITDA €702.1M at a **55% EBITDA margin**; FY2026 target remains EBITDA margin above 50% ([shareholder letter](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1lG-dwLzTRsW-ALo6iThPLWhTMmpSDpcK/view)).
- **Guidance:** FY2026 net revenue growth of **20–22% YoY** on a constant-currency basis ([Adyen Q1 2026 Business Update, 2026-05-06](https://drive.google.com/file/d/17Jl5UE6vSNDW0CbyvZlijOyEaixNtSyj/view)).
Take rate is structurally low-double-digit bps and declining as volume mix shifts to large enterprises — i.e. big accounts like Agoda add volume at thin take rates, which is the economic logic behind the "reducing costs" language in the release.

**In-base comps (news DB).** This is one of a string of Adyen enterprise-acquiring expansions the pipeline has tracked in 2026: [[News/2026-02/Adyen and Uber expand partnership and launch Uber Kiosks]] and [[News/2026-02/Connecting the Dots Adyen's unified acquiring infrastructure for Uber]] (unified global acquiring for Uber), [[News/2026-03/Cathay Pacific expands global payments partnership with Adyen]] (another travel/airline win), [[News/2026-02/Accesso and Adyen expand platform payments partnership]], and [[News/2026-04/Adyen launches Intelligent Money Movement for enterprises]] — the "issuing and payouts" Agoda is exploring maps directly onto Adyen's Intelligent Money Movement push. Travel-payments demand context: [[News/2026-06/Connecting the Dots Ecommpay report on UK travel payments]].

**Risk flags (skeptical read):**
1. **No new economics disclosed — milestone, not expansion.** The "ten years" framing is a relationship anniversary; issuing and payouts are explicitly described as "exploring," with no signed scope, volume, or timeline. Treat as a renewal/PR beat, not a fresh revenue event.
2. **Thin, likely declining take rate on this account.** A decade-old enterprise OTA at Agoda's scale almost certainly sits at Adyen's lowest take-rate tier; incremental volume here barely moves net revenue and compresses blended take rate — consistent with Adyen's filed trend.
3. **Single-large-customer precedent = concentration/churn risk.** Adyen's own FY2025 numbers show how much a single large account distorts reported volume (8% vs 21% ex-customer). Large OTAs multi-source acquiring for resilience, so Agoda is unlikely to be sole-sourced and the relationship is renegotiable on price.
4. **Competitive pressure on the exact value prop.** Authorization-uplift and local-acquiring claims are now table stakes offered by Stripe, Checkout.com and Worldpay/Global Payments; Adyen's differentiation at renewal is narrowing, not widening.
5. **Issuing/payouts is unproven revenue and capital-/compliance-heavy.** Extending into issuing and cross-border payouts adds regulatory and balance-sheet exposure in multiple markets with no disclosed commercial commitment — optionality, not a booked win.
6. **Macro/travel-demand dependence.** The volume is only as durable as OTA bookings; Booking Holdings' own gross-bookings growth decelerated to +9% in Q2 2026 from +15% in Q1, so the underlying flow is slowing.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Earnings review — Adyen (AMS: ADYEN).** The Agoda item is a partnership/commercial note, not an earnings event, but Adyen is IR-covered, so here are its most recent reported actuals for context. Adyen reports on a half-year cadence; the latest half is **H1 2026, published 21 August 2026** (newer than the Q1 2026 business update that `ir_latest.json` still lists as latest — that index is stale).

**H1 2026 reported figures (six months to 30 Jun 2026):**
- **Processed volume: €803.8bn, up 24% YoY.**
- **Net revenue: €1,302.9m, up 19% YoY (+21% constant currency).**
- **EBITDA: €641.5m; EBITDA margin 49% (50% excluding one-time transaction costs).**
- **Free cash flow conversion: 86%; CapEx ~5% of net revenue.**
- Net income was reported at roughly **€544m (~+13% YoY)** in results coverage (figure sits in the shareholder letter rather than the headline press release).
- Source (Adyen IR, primary): <https://www.adyen.com/press-and-media/adyen-publishes-h1-2026-financial-results-3wjne>. (The H1 2026 filing is newer than anything mirrored in the local irdb, whose latest Adyen release is H2 2025.)

**Guidance (FY 2026):**
- Net revenue growth **21–23% YoY constant currency**, including the Talon.One and Orb acquisitions that closed 1 July 2026.
- Underlying EBITDA margin broadly in line with 2025; **including Talon.One/Orb, EBITDA margin expected ~1pp below 2025**.
- Medium-term: EBITDA margin target **above 55% by 2028**; CapEx up to 5% of net revenue.

**Vs. prior period / FY 2025 (published 12 Feb 2026, from irdb):**
- FY 2025 net revenue €2,364.2m, up 18% YoY (+21% cc); FY 2025 EBITDA €1,245.7m, margin 53%. H2 2025 alone: net revenue €1,270.7m (+17%), EBITDA €702.1m, margin 55%.
- Source: <https://www.adyen.com/press-and-media/adyen-publishes-h2-2025-financial-results-3pgu2> (drive: <https://drive.google.com/file/d/1acJAsxwwM-338tTKmKUhv3avurwUWJ6a/view>).
- Read-across: H1 2026 net revenue growth of +21% cc sits squarely inside the 21–23% cc FY guide; volume growth re-accelerated to +24%. The H1 margin of 49% is a step down from the 53%/55% 2025 levels, but this is the expected, guided dilution from the mid-year Talon.One/Orb deals plus one-time transaction costs (49% → 50% ex one-offs). Shares rose on the print.

**Verdict: in-line.** Revenue growth tracks the FY guidance and volume re-accelerated; the lower H1 EBITDA margin was pre-flagged as acquisition-driven dilution rather than an operating miss. No earnings surprise is attributable to the Agoda partnership itself, which is a relationship-milestone and potential issuing/payouts expansion story, not a disclosed revenue event.

**Thesis flags:** (1) margin trajectory — 2026 is a deliberate re-investment/acquisition-dilution year; the "above 55% by 2028" target is the metric to watch for operating leverage reasserting. (2) Growth quality — reported net revenue growth (+19%) lags constant-currency (+21%), so FX is a current headwind; the enterprise-volume engine (+24% processed volume) remains the driver. (3) The Agoda renewal (ten-year relationship, APAC/EU/US acquiring, exploring issuing + payouts) is an incremental expansion signal for the single-platform, land-and-expand thesis, not a near-term P&L mover.
