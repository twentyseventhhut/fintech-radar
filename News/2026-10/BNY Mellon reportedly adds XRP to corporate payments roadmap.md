BNY Mellon Names XRP in Corporate Payments Roadmap, Stablecoin Report Shows
Bank of New York Mellon Corp. has placed XRP squarely inside its corporate payments strategy, according to a stablecoin publication from the custody giant that is drawing fresh scrutiny from digital asset researchers.
The document, which outlines the bank's collaboration with Ripple Labs, states that the objective is to help corporate treasuries move money "24/7/365, in real time, through stablecoins and digital assets like XRP." The wording appears in a section titled "Ripple and BNY: Speeding Up Cross-Border Payments With Stablecoins."
Crypto researcher SMQKE surfaced the passage on X, writing that XRP is part of BNY Mellon's global payments plan and linking to the publication as documentation. The post quickly circulated among XRP supporters, who have long debated whether the token would gain traction at major financial institutions.
BNY Mellon, founded in 1784, is among the oldest banks in the United States and the world's largest custodian bank, overseeing $62.6 trillion in assets under custody and administration as of June 30, 2026. Its willingness to name XRP explicitly in an official publication marks a notable step for an asset that has spent years fighting regulatory uncertainty in the U.S. That uncertainty has since receded: the SEC dropped its appeal in the Ripple case in March 2025, and the first spot XRP exchange-traded fund began trading in the U.S. in November 2025.
The Ripple Connection
The publication confirms that BNY Mellon became the primary reserve custodian for Ripple USD, the stablecoin Ripple launched in December 2024 for corporate and institutional payments. BNY's appointment as custodian was announced in July 2025. RLUSD is designed to deliver faster settlement, lower costs, and greater efficiency for cross-border transactions.
In October 2025, Ripple acquired GTreasury, a treasury management systems provider, for $1 billion, to streamline corporate money movement across digital assets, including RLUSD and XRP. Jack McDonald, Ripple's senior vice president of stablecoins, said the intent is to strengthen, not replace, the connection between corporations and banks, with BNY Mellon playing a central role.
The report also stresses the importance of interoperability between tokenized deposits and stablecoins, describing it as essential for deploying these solutions at scale. That framing suggests the bank is thinking about how blockchain-based assets fit alongside, rather than displace, traditional banking infrastructure.
What the Document Does Not Say
While XRP is directly referenced in the payments discussion, the publication stops short of saying BNY Mellon currently uses the token for all payment transactions. That gap has left room for competing interpretations among market observers.
Some commentators noted that infrastructure may already exist but could remain dormant until demand materializes or regulatory conditions shift. Others, including industry analyst David Arnal, cautioned that an explicit mention does not guarantee the bank will conduct payments directly with XRP rather than simply listing it as an available option alongside stablecoins.
The distinction matters. Stablecoins such as RLUSD are designed for price stability, making them natural candidates for corporate treasury use. XRP, by contrast, is a volatile digital asset whose role in institutional payments has been the subject of ongoing debate since Ripple first pitched it to banks more than a decade ago.
Institutional Momentum
BNY Mellon's publication arrives as traditional financial institutions continue to expand their digital asset offerings. The bank has been building out custody and tokenization services, positioning itself as a bridge between regulated finance and blockchain-based settlement networks.
For Ripple, the mention reinforces its long-running strategy of embedding XRP and RLUSD within existing banking relationships rather than attempting to bypass them. McDonald's comments echo that approach, emphasizing partnership with financial intermediaries instead of disintermediation.
The pattern extends beyond U.S. borders. In Japan, SBI VC Trade — a subsidiary of SBI Holdings, a long-standing Ripple partner — began offering RLUSD trading on June 24, 2026, making it Japan's first stablecoin under the Type 4 electronic payment instrument designation. Ripple and SBI had signed a memorandum of understanding in August 2025 to bring RLUSD to the Japanese market.
The crypto market's reaction to the document has so far been measured, with the discussion centered on X rather than reflected in dramatic price movement. Still, the episode highlights how closely digital asset communities track institutional publications for signals about adoption.
Whether XRP ultimately becomes an active settlement tool at BNY Mellon or remains a named option within a broader digital asset framework, the document provides one of the clearest official acknowledgments to date that the bank views the token as part of its payments conversation.

Why now: SEC dropped its Ripple appeal (2025-03), the first US spot XRP ETF listed (2025-11), BNY became RLUSD's primary reserve custodian (announced 2025-07), and Ripple bought GTreasury for $1bn (2025-10) to own the treasury workflow — the rails and the regulatory cover now exist simultaneously.
