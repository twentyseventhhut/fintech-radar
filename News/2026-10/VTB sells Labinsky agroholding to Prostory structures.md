 ВТБ продала агрохолдинг «Лабинский» структурам компании «Просторы» 
Группа ВТБ договорилась о продаже агрохолдинга «Лабинский» структурам агрохолдинга «Просторы». Об этом « Известиям » рассказали топ-менеджер крупной агропромышленной компании, инвестиционный банкир и источник в крупном агрохолдинге, знакомые с ситуацией со слов участников сделки. По словам одного из собеседников, сделка состоялась в середине сентября, ее сумма составила около 50 млрд рублей.
«Лабинский» управляет бывшими активами концерна «Покровский» — одного из крупнейших агропромышленных предприятий юга России. Основными владельцами концерна были Андрей Коровайко и Аркадий Чебанов. В октябре 2023 года Лабинский городской суд Краснодарского края удовлетворил иск Генпрокуратуры об обращении имущества концерна в доход государства. Надзорное ведомство настаивало на коррупционном происхождении активов, однако Коровайко отвергал обвинения в правонарушениях. Позднее управление предприятиями «Покровского» перешло к ООО «Агрокомплекс Лабинский».
Ключевые активы расположены в Краснодарском крае. Предприятия холдинга выращивают зерновые, сахарную свеклу, подсолнечник и другие сельхозкультуры, а также занимаются молочным животноводством и мясопереработкой. На момент приобретения в состав холдинга входили 18 растениеводческих и животноводческих предприятий, пять элеваторов, три сахарных завода, три мясоперерабатывающих предприятия и два свинокомплекса.
По данным аудиторско-консалтинговой компании BEFL, в мае 2026 года земельный банк агрохолдинга составлял 218 тыс. га. По этому показателю «Лабинский» занимал 28-е место среди крупнейших владельцев сельхозземель в России. Консолидированная отчетность компании не раскрывается. По итогам 2025-го выручка ООО «Агрокомплекс Лабинский» по РСБУ достигла 2,6 млрд рублей — в 2,5 раза больше, чем годом ранее. Чистая прибыль выросла в 11,5 раза, до 224,4 млн рублей.
О приобретении «Лабинского» ВТБ сообщил в декабре 2024 года. Банк рассматривал актив как инвестиционный и рассчитывал впоследствии продать его с прибылью, говорил «Ведомостям» представитель группы. Стоимость покупки не называлась.
В апреле 2026-го глава ВТБ Андрей Костин заявил о намерении продать все сельскохозяйственные активы группы и сообщил, что на агрокомплекс есть несколько претендентов. В конце августа первый заместитель председателя правления банка Дмитрий Пьянов рассказал, что кредитная организация уже подписала предварительное соглашение об условиях продажи «Лабинского», однако имя покупателя не назвал. Группа рассчитывала завершить сделку до конца года.
«Просторы» объединяют предприятия в Ростовской, Нижегородской и Воронежской областях, Краснодарском и Ставропольском краях, говорится на сайте холдинга. В его состав входит АФГ «Националь» — один из ведущих российских производителей круп, который называет себя крупнейшим производителем риса в стране. Группа также занимается выращиванием зерновых и масличных культур, животноводством и мясопереработкой. Консолидированную отчетность «Просторы» не публикуют. По расчетам агентства AK&M, совокупная выручка предприятий холдинга в 2025 году составила 58,7 млрд рублей — на 10,5% меньше, чем годом ранее. Вместо чистой прибыли в размере 1,6 млрд рублей годом ранее группа получила убыток в 1,6 млрд. Официально бенефициары «Просторов» не раскрываются. «Коммерсант» со ссылкой на источник, близкий к компании, писал, что холдинг действует в интересах структур бывшего президента УГМК Искандара Махмудова.
В 2024 году «Просторы» расширили свои активы, приобретя предприятия группы «Дон Агро» в Ростовской области и получив контроль над производителем мраморной говядины «Заречное». В июле 2025 года входящее в холдинг АО «Малоорловское» приобрело группу «Зерно Дон», под контролем которой находится около 64 тыс. га сельхозземель в Ростовской области.
По оценкам экспертов, сделка может стать одной из крупнейших в российском агропромышленном комплексе (АПК) за последние несколько лет. Приобретая «Лабинский», «Просторы» продолжают расширять бизнес, считает управляющий партнер Agro and Food Communications Илья Березнюк. По его словам, создание сопоставимого по масштабу агрохолдинга с нуля потребовало бы многих лет, значительных инвестиций и сложной работы по консолидации земельного банка. Сделка же позволяет одновременно получить сельхозугодья, перерабатывающие мощности и действующую инфраструктуру, отметил директор практики «Потребительский сектор и АПК» Strategy Partners Вадим Аникин.
В рейтинге BEFL «Просторы» занимают 11-е место с земельным банком в 449 тыс. га. Если сделка с ВТБ охватывает все земли, находившиеся под управлением «Лабинского», совокупный земельный банк покупателя может вырасти до 667 тыс. га, подсчитал Илья Березнюк. В таком случае «Просторы» могут подняться на шестое место среди крупнейших владельцев сельхозземель в России, опередив «ЭкоНиву», «Био-Тон» и агрохолдинг «Степь». Однако итоговая позиция будет зависеть от фактического состава приобретенных активов и изменений земельных банков конкурентов, уточнил Вадим Аникин.
ВТБ сворачивает инвестиции в сельское хозяйство на фоне подготовки к введению с апреля 2027-го новых ограничений ЦБ на вложения банков в непрофильные активы. В 2023 году группа уже вышла из капитала «Деметра-Холдинга», продав свою долю в размере около 45%. Андрей Костин тогда заявил, что сделка принесла банку «приличную маржу», но не раскрыл название покупателя.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened.** VTB Group has agreed to sell the "Labinsky" agroholding to structures affiliated with the "Prostory" agroholding. Per *Izvestia* (sourced to an agro-industry top manager, an investment banker and a source at a large agroholding, all close to the deal), the transaction closed in mid-September 2026 at roughly RUB 50bn (~$0.6bn). VTB had signalled the sale for months: CEO Andrey Kostin said in April 2026 the bank intended to exit all agricultural assets ("there will be a competition, many interested parties"), and in late August first deputy chairman Dmitry Pyanov confirmed a preliminary term sheet had been signed without naming the buyer, targeting a close by year-end.

**What Labinsky is.** Labinsky manages the former assets of the "Pokrovsky" concern — one of southern Russia's largest agro-industrial groups, historically controlled by Andrey Korovaiko and Arkady Chebanov. In October 2023 the Labinsk city court (Krasnodar region) granted a Prosecutor General's claim converting Pokrovsky's property to state ownership on alleged corruption-origin grounds (which Korovaiko denied); management then passed to OOO "Agrokompleks Labinsky." Core assets sit in Krasnodar region: grain, sugar beet and sunflower cropping plus dairy, meat processing and hog production — at acquisition, 18 crop/livestock enterprises, five elevators, three sugar plants, three meat-processing plants and two hog complexes. Per BEFL, Labinsky's land bank was ~218k ha in May 2026 (28th-largest Russian landholder). Consolidated accounts are undisclosed; OOO Agrokompleks Labinsky's 2025 RAS revenue hit RUB 2.6bn (2.5x YoY) with net profit of RUB 224.4m (11.5x YoY).

**Why VTB held it.** VTB acquired Labinsky in December 2024, treating it explicitly as an *investment* asset bought cheap (price undisclosed) with the aim of reselling at a profit — a classic distressed/collateral-origin bank holding rather than a strategic agri play.

**The buyer.** "Prostory" spans enterprises across Rostov, Nizhny Novgorod and Voronezh oblasts and Krasnodar/Stavropol krais, including AFG "Natsional" (a leading grain/rice producer, self-described largest Russian rice producer), plus cropping, livestock and meat processing. 2025 combined revenue was ~RUB 58.7bn per AK&M (-10.5% YoY), swinging to a RUB 1.6bn net loss from a RUB 1.6bn profit. Beneficiaries are undisclosed; *Kommersant* has linked the holding to structures of former UGMK president Iskander Makhmudov. Prostory has been consolidating aggressively — buying Don Agro and gaining control of marbled-beef producer "Zarechnoye" in 2024, and "Zerno Don" (~64k ha) via AO Maloorlovskoye in July 2025.

**Market impact.** Prostory ranks 11th in BEFL with a 449k-ha land bank; if the deal covers all Labinsky land, its combined bank could reach ~667k ha (Ilya Bereznyuk, Agro and Food Communications), lifting it to ~6th-largest Russian landholder — ahead of EkoNiva, Bio-Ton and Agroholding Step. Final ranking depends on the actual asset perimeter acquired (Vadim Anikin, Strategy Partners). Analysts frame this as potentially one of Russia's largest APK deals in several years, and a fast way to buy scale (land + processing + infrastructure) that would take years to build organically.

**The strategic frame.** The sale is part of VTB's broader non-core divestment programme ahead of new CBR restrictions, effective 1 April 2027, that cap banks' exposure to non-core assets. VTB is methodically cleaning the balance sheet of anything not directly tied to banking: in 2023 it exited "Demeter-Holding" (grain-export logistics/trading), selling its ~45% stake with what Kostin called "a decent margin"; in 2026 it has also been shedding real estate (e.g. the former Otkritie office sold to Wildberries-affiliated structures — see [[VTB to sell former Otkritie office to Wildberries-Russ]]) and taking equity collateral (see [[VTB takes large Ozon share stake as collateral]], which illustrates how such non-banking stakes land on VTB's book in the first place). The agri exit is the largest and most visible leg of that programme.

Related internal notes: [[VTB takes large Ozon share stake as collateral]], [[VTB to sell former Otkritie office to Wildberries-Russ]].

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. **Is the deal actually done?** Reporting is anonymous (three unnamed sources via *Izvestia*); VTB and Prostory have not officially confirmed closing. "Closed in mid-September" vs VTB's August "preliminary term sheet" framing is consistent but unverified — treat as reported, not confirmed.
2. **Is RUB 50bn reliable?** The figure comes from "one of the interlocutors," not either party. Neither VTB's acquisition price (Dec 2024, undisclosed) nor this sale price is official, so any "profit" claim is unquantifiable.
3. **Did VTB make money?** VTB bought as an investment "to resell at a profit," but with no disclosed entry price, the margin — the whole rationale — is a black box. The Demeter "decent margin" precedent is rhetorical, not audited.
4. **Valuation sanity check.** RUB 50bn against OOO Agrokompleks Labinsky 2025 RAS revenue of RUB 2.6bn is ~19x revenue — implausible on an operating basis, which signals the price is a land/asset-value play (218k ha + 5 elevators + 3 sugar plants etc.), not an earnings multiple. RAS single-entity accounts understate a group with undisclosed consolidated figures.
5. **What exactly is being sold?** Bereznyuk's 667k-ha/#6-rank upside is explicitly conditional on the deal covering *all* land under Labinsky. Anikin flags the perimeter is uncertain. The headline ranking jump may not materialise.
6. **Legal/title risk.** Assets originate from Pokrovsky property seized via a Prosecutor General corruption claim in 2023. Buying ex-nationalised assets carries latent title/political risk; how clean is Prostory's title, and could prior owners (Korovaiko, who denied wrongdoing) litigate?
7. **Who really is the buyer?** Prostory's beneficiaries are officially undisclosed; the Makhmudov (ex-UGMK) link is *Kommersant* sourcing to an anonymous insider. Ultimate control and the sanctions/PEP profile of the acquirer are not established.
8. **Can Prostory afford/absorb it?** Prostory swung to a RUB 1.6bn net loss in 2025 on falling revenue (-10.5%). A ~RUB 50bn acquisition by a loss-making group raises financing questions — debt, related-party funding, or deferred terms are unaddressed.
9. **Regulatory-deadline framing.** The "CBR non-core limits from 1 Apr 2027" narrative is real but is it the true driver, or post-hoc justification? VTB wanted to resell for profit regardless; the regulation adds urgency/floor, not necessarily the motive.
10. **Fintech relevance.** This is an agri/M&A story about a bank's balance-sheet cleanup, not a fintech product/payments/tech story. Relevance to a fintech radar is indirect (bank capital/non-core policy signalling).
11. **Timing mismatch.** If the deal "closed in mid-September," why is it surfacing as news on 20 October? Possible the close is not final, or only now leaked — consistency of the timeline is worth flagging.
12. **Comparability of rankings.** BEFL/AK&M land-bank and revenue tables are estimates from consultancies, not audited; "6th largest landholder" is a modelled projection, not a verified post-deal fact.
13. **Macro signalling.** Does VTB's full agri exit say anything about Russian APK asset valuations or bank appetite for real-economy stakes? The data point is one forced-ish seller; don't over-generalise.
14. **Single-source dependency.** The entire note rests on one Mergers.ru rewrite of the *Izvestia* report. Corroboration exists across Russian outlets (Interfax, Moscow Times, Frankmedia) but all trace to the same *Izvestia* original — no independent confirmation chain.

**Importance: 3/5** — A large, headline-grabbing Russian APK M&A deal (~RUB 50bn, potential top-6 landholder) and a concrete milestone in VTB's regulator-driven non-core divestment programme, which is a genuine recurring bank-strategy theme (Demeter, Ozon collateral, Otkritie real estate). But it is agriculture/M&A-adjacent rather than core fintech, the price/profit/perimeter are all unconfirmed and single-sourced, and no official party has confirmed the close — material as a signal, not a verified or fintech-central event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a Russian-bank non-core / distressed-asset divestment, not a fintech deal: VTB is shedding an agri holding ("Labinsky") it acquired in Dec-2024 as an investment to flip at a profit, selling to agri consolidator "Prostory" for ~RUB 50bn (deal struck mid-Sep 2026, per Izvestia sources via Mergers.ru). The "why now" is regulatory and capital-driven: the Bank of Russia's risk-sensitive limit (RSL) on banks' non-core / immobilized assets — fixed assets, real-estate investments, and assets received in settlement — was deferred to 1-Apr-2027 (from 1-Oct-2026), starting at a 100% cap and ratcheting to a 30%-of-capital target over ~5 years; excess above the limit is deducted from regulatory capital (per Reuters/Interfax, 2026). That turns balance-sheet non-core holdings into a direct capital drag, so VTB — which runs thin capital buffers (CET1 N20.1 was 6.2% at 1-Mar-2025, 7.2% at 1-May-2025, barely above the regulatory floor; per TASS/AK&M) — has a structural incentive to monetize agri, real estate and equity stakes ahead of the 2027 start. Kostin in Apr-2026 said the group would "sell everything and only work in the city" (per Izvestia). The agri-land market itself is consolidating fast: the deal reportedly lifts Prostory's land bank to ~667k ha (from 449k ha), potentially to #6 nationally, per BEFL/analyst Berezniuk via Mergers.ru.

**Competitive landscape.** For a bank-divestment the relevant KPIs are the capital drag removed, realized gain/loss vs carry, and buffer headroom (CET1/N20.1) — none of which VTB has disclosed for this deal `[UNSOURCED]`. Basis of "competition" here is the pool of buyers able to absorb a RUB 50bn agri block: VTB said in Apr-2026 there were several bidders; Prostory (reportedly tied to ex-UGMK president Iskander Makhmudov's structures, per Kommersant) won — a strategic agri buyer, not a financial sponsor. VTB's position: a forced-ish seller clearing the runway to the 2027 RSL, not a strategic agri operator; it is methodically unwinding a non-core stack built partly via the Otkritie and Pokrovsky-concern asset flows. Recent VTB non-core moves: exited Demeter-Holding (~45% stake, 2023) at a "decent margin" per Kostin; agreed to sell the former Otkritie HQ (Vivaldi Plaza, ~24.4k sqm) to Wildberries-Russ (Jun-2026); and took a large Ozon equity block as collateral in the ~RUB 300bn+ AFK Sistema deleveraging deal (Jun-2026).

**Comps & multiples.** Agri target is private; consolidated accounts undisclosed, so EV/EBITDA and clean multiples are not computable — "no data" / `[UNSOURCED]`. The only public anchors: Labinsky's operating entity OOO Agrokompleks Labinsky RSBU FY2025 revenue RUB 2.6bn (×2.5 YoY), net profit RUB 224.4mn (×11.5) — but this is a single opco, not the consolidated holding, so a price/revenue of RUB 50bn / RUB 2.6bn = ~19x is NOT meaningful (RSBU opco revenue grossly understates a 218k-ha land-and-processing asset base); treat the RUB 50bn as a land-and-infrastructure replacement-value deal, not an earnings multiple `(analysis)`. Prostory group FY2025: combined revenue RUB 58.7bn (-10.5% YoY), swinging to a RUB 1.6bn net loss from RUB 1.6bn profit (per AK&M via Mergers.ru) — i.e., the buyer is loss-making and levering up into a large cash/financed acquisition. Internal comps (VTB non-core unwind, grep fallback — semsearch 402/down): [[VTB to sell former Otkritie office to Wildberries-Russ]] (real-estate non-core sale, Jun-2026), [[VTB takes large Ozon share stake as collateral]] (equity-stake-via-collateral, Jun-2026). Distribution not computed — qualitative comparison only; these confirm a consistent, multi-asset-class divestment program rather than a one-off.

**Risk flags.**
1. Capital opacity — VTB disclosed neither the realized gain/loss vs the (undisclosed) Dec-2024 purchase price nor the CET1/N20.1 uplift; with CET1 running ~6–7% near the floor, whether this materially relieves the 2027 RSL drag is unverifiable. Second-order: if sold near/below carry, the "investment to flip at a profit" thesis fails and signals forced selling.
2. Buyer credit quality — Prostory is loss-making (RUB 1.6bn loss FY2025) and funding a ~RUB 50bn purchase; if partly VTB-financed, the bank swaps a non-core equity asset for borrower-concentration / counterparty risk to a highly levered agri roll-up, i.e., capital relief may be partly cosmetic.
3. Execution / deal-perimeter risk — land-bank uplift to 667k ha assumes "all Labinsky land" transfers; actual perimeter (confiscated ex-Pokrovsky assets, originally obtained via Prosecutor-General expropriation) carries legal-title and antimonopoly-review overhang that could trim value post-close.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Read-through `(analysis)`: this is the clearest signal yet that the 1-Apr-2027 non-core RSL is already forcing Russian banks to liquidate balance-sheet assets — expect more VTB agri/real-estate/equity exits through 2026 and a broader sector wave as the 100%→30% ratchet begins. Falsifiable thesis: if the RSL stays on schedule, VTB discloses further non-core disposals (and/or a CET1 uplift) over the next 2–3 quarters; trigger/catalyst = FY2025/9M-2026 IFRS disclosure quantifying immobilized-asset reduction. What breaks it: a second CBR deferral past Apr-2027, which would remove the forced-seller urgency and slow the divestment pace.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
> Note: this is an asset-disposal (M&A) story, not an earnings print. VTB (public Russian bank, ticker VTBR) reports IFRS results; the figures below ground the ~₽50bn Labinsky sale in VTB's capital/divestment thesis. **Verdict (context read, not a quarter print).** IN-LINE / mild MISS trajectory for VTB in 2026 — profit declining under a capital squeeze. 8M 2026 net profit ₽314.8bn (−4% YoY); H1 2026 ₽225.2bn (−19.8% YoY vs ₽280.8bn); FY2025 ₽502.1bn (−8.9% YoY vs a record 2024). The Labinsky ~₽50bn sale is a capital-management move, not an earnings driver: VTB is shedding non-core agri assets ahead of the CBR's April-2027 cap on banks' non-core holdings, against thin N1.0 headroom.

**Key figures (with growth).**
- Net profit: FY2025 ₽502.1bn (−8.9% YoY); H1 2026 ₽225.2bn (−19.8% YoY); 8M 2026 ₽314.8bn (−4% YoY); Q1 2026 −6.2% YoY. Momentum negative but the YoY gap narrowed into 8M (July profit reportedly +73% YoY) as NII recovered.
- Net interest income / NIM: the key swing factor — H1 2026 NII ~₽405.6bn, up ~2.8x from ₽146.8bn a year earlier; NIM recovered to ~2.6% in H1 2026 from ~1.0% in H1 2025 (NIM had been crushed by the CBR's high-rate regime in 2024–25). This is the bull line in 2026: margin normalization as rates ease.
- ROE: 9M 2025 ROE 18.6% (vs 21.3% a year earlier); FY2025 run-rate ~15%. 2026 ROE pressured by the lower profit base.
- Capital adequacy (the disposal driver): N1.0 ~9.78% as of 2026-07-01 (down from ~9.98% a year earlier); N1.2 ~7.61% (from ~8.22%) — only a thin buffer over the ~9% / common-equity minimums. (analysis) This is precisely the constraint non-core disposals relieve.

**By segment / driver.** 2026 profit decline is driven by a high cost of funding and elevated credit/reserve costs compressing the bottom line even as NII rebounds; the margin recovery (NIM 1.0%→2.6%) is doing the heavy lifting on the top of the P&L while capital stays tight. The Labinsky agri-holding is a non-core "investment" asset (VTB bought the ex-Pokrovsky assets in Dec-2024 intending to flip at a profit); it contributes nothing to banking NII and consumes capital — classic candidate for disposal.

**Guidance / forward.** VTB reaffirmed its 2026 net-profit target at the 8M update. The capital story dominates forward guidance: CEO Andrei Kostin flagged VTB may need up to ₽700bn of additional capital over 2027–2028 to meet higher Basel-related buffers — which is why management (Kostin in Apr-2026, first deputy Dmitry Pyanov in Aug-2026) committed to exiting ALL agricultural assets, and why the CBR's April-2027 limit on non-core holdings is an explicit catalyst. VTB previously exited Demeter-Holding (~45% stake) in 2023 at a stated "decent margin." Management tone: confident on margin recovery, deliberately quiet on how it closes the ₽700bn capital gap beyond disposals and (possible) issuance.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. Capital, not earnings, is the binding constraint. Fact: N1.0 ~9.78% vs ~9% min; Kostin flags up to ₽700bn needed 2027–28 → why: thin buffer + Basel tightening + the April-2027 non-core cap → why it matters: forced, time-boxed selling of non-core assets (agri, earlier Demeter) → second-order: VTB is a price-taker/motivated seller, so ~₽50bn for Labinsky likely reflects a capital-relief urgency discount more than peak value.
2. NIM recovery is the real 2026 story (1.0%→2.6%), but it is rate-regime-dependent — a CBR reversal would re-compress margin and re-tighten capital, accelerating further disposals. (analysis)
3. Profit is still falling YoY (FY2025 −8.9%, H1 2026 −19.8%), so there is no earnings cushion to rebuild capital organically — reinforcing the divestment-over-retention logic behind selling Labinsky rather than holding for the originally planned profitable flip.
4. De-PR: VTB frames agri exits as "investment gains" (Demeter "decent margin"), but the Labinsky sale is being driven by regulatory/capital necessity, not opportunistic timing — management is silent on realized gain/loss vs the (undisclosed) Dec-2024 purchase price.
