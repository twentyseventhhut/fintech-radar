# Context-enrichment: SPB Exchange plans crypto spot trading and settlement depositary
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-09 SPB Exchange (СПБ Биржа, PJSC Saint Petersburg Exchange) confirmed to RBC/Vedomosti that it **intends to launch organised spot cryptocurrency trading before end-2026** and, to that end, is **building its own settlement digital depositary (расчётный цифровой депозитарий)** and preparing documents for inclusion in the Bank of Russia register of digital depositaries. This is an **intent/plan announcement, not a live product and not yet a registration** — the exchange is "creating the necessary infrastructure" and will "undergo procedures" for the register; no CBR act, no launch date beyond "by year-end", no asset list, no counterparties named (confirmed via Vedomosti/Pravo.ru — company declined to name specific coins, citing "legislation + CBR rules + liquidity/demand balance").

De-PR'd substance:
- The operative word is **"settlement" (расчётный)**. Under the 282-FZ regime the CBR tiers digital depositaries by function and capital: roughly **RUB 50m floor up to RUB 250m** for the top tier. A *settlement* depositary — the kind that can actually clear/settle crypto trades on organised venues — sits at the **top, ~RUB 250m minimum capital** rung (the figure in the aggregated source). So SPB is not applying for the cheapest custody licence; it is building the piece that lets it settle its own on-exchange trades in-house.
- **Vertical integration is the real story.** If the CBR registers it, SPB would own *every* link of the crypto stack inside one group — organised trading venue + settlement depositary — unlike peers who split the roles.
- Regulatory frame (context, not new): Law **282-FZ** (signed 2026-08-04, core provisions live **2026-09-01**) legalised regulated crypto *trading/custody/cross-border settlement* while **banning domestic crypto payments**. For **non-qualified** investors the CBR permits only **BTC, ETH, USDT**, after a suitability/knowledge **test**, with a **~RUB 300,000/year** purchase cap per intermediary; a wider line-up is reserved for **qualified** investors.

**+ Why structured this way / what it reveals.** SPB Exchange has been on the **OFAC SDN list since Nov 2023**, which froze its core US-equities franchise (the bulk of its pre-sanctions turnover) and cut it off from foreign counterparties. It therefore **needs a new business core**, and regulated crypto is the available greenfield. Building its *own* settlement depositary (rather than renting one, as MOEX does) is a bid to **own the whole value chain of a small but new market** — the only way a sanctioned, infrastructure-light exchange can manufacture a differentiated, fee-bearing franchise. The second-order signal: this is a **survival pivot framed as a strategy**, and it arrives *after* the first CBR registrations (6-7 Oct) in which SPB did **not** appear — i.e. SPB is a follower, not a first-mover, in this race.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Moscow Exchange (MOEX)** — the direct rival and clear leader: announced a **2026-12-01** launch of organised crypto trading, using its **existing** trading licence/infrastructure and a **partner** digital depositary for settlement (not building its own); initial assets reported as BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, TRON; already running tests with professional traders. MOEX treats crypto as "just another asset class" bolted onto a vast universal franchise.
- **Banks already registered (6-7 Oct 2026, via [[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]]):** exchange operators **VTB, T-Invest Lab (T-Technologies), Zefir, Sistema-Crypto**; digital custodians **Sber, VTB, Voltari, Atomyze, Cloud Infrastructure**. Sber plans BTC/ETH/USDT embedded in SberBank Online/SberInvestitsii, launch target ~1 Dec. These are the incumbents SPB is chasing ([[Финтехно banks race to control Russian crypto infrastructure]]).
- **SPB's position: catching up.** It missed the first registration wave and is still *building* infrastructure while MOEX and banks are *testing*. Its one structural edge vs MOEX is **full vertical integration** (own venue + own settlement depositary); its disadvantage vs banks is **no retail distribution** (no app, no deposit base) and a **sanctioned** brand that complicates any foreign liquidity.

**+ Why the lay of the land is this way.** The non-qual universe is capped to **three assets (BTC/ETH/USDT) + RUB 300k/year** — a genuinely narrow pool. Banks win on distribution (tens of millions of app users); MOEX wins on incumbent liquidity and clearing muscle; SPB has neither, so it competes on **owning the rails**. Second-order: in a market this small and this constrained, owning the full stack may not generate enough fee volume to matter — the venue could end up structurally subscale.

## [2] Company history / fit
SPB Exchange built its franchise on **foreign (mainly US) equities** for Russian retail; the **Nov 2023 OFAC SDN designation** destroyed that model and blocked client assets. Since then it has pivoted toward **domestic instruments** — e.g. running the first 2026 SPO (NFK-sberezheniy, Jun 2026, [[Mergers.ru SPB Exchange to run first 2026 SPO with NFK-sberezheniy]]) — and is now reaching for crypto. The Bank of Russia itself has publicly said SPB "will not be an exception" and is "already thinking about new services and products" (TASS). **+ Why the company acts this way:** a sanctioned exchange with a gutted core and thin volumes must **manufacture a new product line from scratch**; crypto is the one regulated greenfield where being infrastructure-light is less of a handicap because *everyone* is starting at zero. The fit is opportunistic-but-logical — it's the clearest path to a new fee base.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**What's genuinely new here (vs the corpus):** SPB Exchange as a named actor declaring a **crypto spot-trading venue + own settlement depositary** — SPB was **absent** from the first CBR registers of exchangers/custodians ([[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]]), so this is a **new, later development**, not a reprint of that registry event. The *settlement*-tier (RUB 250m) depositary detail and the vertical-integration angle are specific to this item.
**Traction / live vs announced:** **nothing is live.** This is intent + "building infrastructure" + "will apply to the register". No registration, no launch date beyond "end-2026", no assets, no clients. MOEX (1 Dec, testing) and Sber are ahead on concreteness. **+ Why the value-add is uncertain:** the thesis rests on owning the full stack, but (a) the addressable non-qual market is tiny (3 assets, RUB 300k cap), (b) SPB lacks distribution, and (c) a sanctioned venue struggles to attract liquidity/market-makers. **Who captures the margin:** likely the players with **distribution** (Sber/VTB apps) and **incumbent liquidity** (MOEX); a vertically-integrated-but-subscale SPB risks capturing the *plumbing* fees of a thin market rather than the flow.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Next milestones to watch: (1) does the CBR actually **register** SPB's settlement depositary; (2) a concrete **launch date and asset list**; (3) whether SPB can **attract market-makers/liquidity** despite SDN status. Market backdrop: Russia's regulated crypto regime is going live in waves (282-FZ live 1 Sep; first registers 6-7 Oct; MOEX 1 Dec), with the CBR deliberately keeping retail access narrow (BTC/ETH/USDT, tested, capped). Sentiment: the regulator is supportive in principle but **control-first** — it is channelling crypto into a tightly supervised, bank-dominated perimeter. **+ Why the market will go this way / second-order:** the constrained non-qual design means **volume concentrates with distribution-rich incumbents**, so SPB's vertical-integration bet is more likely to deliver a *defensible-but-small* niche than a growth engine. Counterintuitively, SPB's sanctioned status — the very thing forcing the pivot — also **caps the upside**, since cross-border crypto liquidity (the carve-out 282-FZ explicitly allows) is exactly where an SDN-listed venue cannot easily play.

### Red-team / challenge — SPB Exchange crypto spot trading + settlement depositary

1. **Is this live or just announced?** Answered: Just announced/intent. SPB is "building infrastructure" and "will apply" to the CBR register; no registration, no launch date beyond "end-2026", no assets. Not live.

2. **Is it a duplicate of the Oct 6-7 "first crypto registers" notes?** Answered: No. SPB Exchange was NOT among the first registered exchangers (VTB, T-Invest Lab, Zefir, Sistema-Crypto) or custodians (Sber, VTB, Voltari, Atomyze, Cloud Infrastructure). This is a distinct, later actor-specific plan (RBC/Vedomosti, 9 Oct). Fresh, not a reprint of [[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]].

3. **Why does SPB need this so badly?** Answered: OFAC SDN since Nov 2023 gutted its US-equities core; it needs a new business core. Crypto is the available regulated greenfield. This reframes the item from "exchange adds a product" to "sanctioned exchange searches for a reason to exist".

4. **What does "settlement" (расчётный) depositary actually mean vs ordinary digital depositary?** Answered: It's the top-tier role that can clear/settle crypto trades on organised venues; carries the highest CBR capital requirement (~RUB 250m vs a ~RUB 50m floor). SPB is building the expensive, settlement-grade piece — the lever for vertical integration.

5. **Is the RUB 250m figure reliable?** Answered: Appears in the aggregated source as the top-tier floor; corroborated by secondary reporting of a RUB 50m-250m tiered range for digital depositaries. Treat the specific 250m as the top rung, not a confirmed SPB-specific number.

6. **How big is the addressable market?** Answered/analysis: Narrow for retail — non-qual investors limited to BTC/ETH/USDT, after a test, ~RUB 300k/year cap per intermediary. Wider line-up only for qualified investors. The retail pool is structurally small.

7. **Who is ahead?** Answered: MOEX (1 Dec 2026 launch, existing licence + partner depositary, testing with pros) and Sber (BTC/ETH/USDT in its apps, ~1 Dec). SPB is still building. It is a follower.

8. **What is SPB's one genuine edge?** Analysis: Full vertical integration — own trading venue + own settlement depositary in one group, vs MOEX renting settlement. Edge is structural, not scale.

9. **What is SPB's fatal weakness vs banks?** Analysis: No distribution (no app, no deposit base, no retail franchise) and an SDN-sanctioned brand. In a distribution-driven retail market, this likely caps volumes.

10. **Does being sanctioned also cap the crypto upside?** Analysis (hypothesis): Yes. 282-FZ carves out cross-border crypto settlement — the most interesting use case — but an SDN-listed venue can't easily access foreign liquidity/counterparties. The sanction that forces the pivot also limits where it can win.

11. **Could the CBR decline to register SPB's depositary?** Open. Registration is prospective; capital/compliance bar is high (settlement tier). No CBR act yet. The whole plan is conditional on this approval.

12. **Any primary-source conflict?** Answered: No material conflict. Fintexno aggregation matches Vedomosti/Pravo.ru/RBC (end-2026 target, settlement depositary, register application, no named coins). The 250m capital point is from the aggregation; external sources confirm the tiered range.

13. **Is "organised spot trading" meaningfully different from what exists?** Answered: Yes — it is regulated, on-venue, CBR-supervised spot crypto, new under 282-FZ (live 1 Sep 2026). But novelty is the *regime's*, not SPB's; SPB is one entrant among several.

14. **Is the vertical-integration story durable or will it commoditise?** Analysis: Durable only if flow materialises; in a thin, capped retail market the plumbing may be subscale. Margin likely accrues to distribution (Sber/VTB) and incumbent liquidity (MOEX), not to a standalone integrated-but-small SPB.

15. **What would change the verdict upward?** Open: an actual CBR registration + a concrete launch with real volumes/market-makers, or a qualified-investor / cross-border angle SPB can uniquely serve. None present yet.

**FRESHNESS VERDICT: fresh** — a new, actor-specific development (SPB Exchange's own crypto venue + settlement depositary plan, 9 Oct) distinct from the 6-7 Oct registry notes, in which SPB did not appear. Not a reprint.

**Importance: 3/5** — A credible strategic pivot by a notable (if sanctioned) exchange into Russia's new regulated crypto market, with a specific differentiator (own settlement-tier depositary / full vertical integration). Not higher because it is purely an intent announcement (nothing live, no CBR registration, no date/assets), SPB is a follower behind MOEX and the banks, and the addressable non-qual market is structurally narrow (3 assets, RUB 300k cap) with SPB lacking distribution. Not lower because it marks a sanctioned incumbent carving a new business core, adds a distinct vertical-integration model to the race, and is genuinely new corpus-wise.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russia's newly-legalized regulated crypto market. Market size: ~20m crypto users holding ~3.7tn rub (~$44bn) per Russia's MinFin expert estimate, annual on-chain/market activity ~10tn rub (~$119bn); the Bank of Russia measured a narrower 827bn rub in exchange-wallet balances at end-Mar 2025 (per MinFin/Deputy FinMin Chebeskov, via Cryptopolitan/crypto.news, as of Sep 2026). Structure: this is a greenfield, regulator-gated oligopoly, not a free market — the base law (No. 282-FZ / "Putin's crypto law") took effect 1 Sep 2026, and the infrastructure-admission mechanism (registries of crypto exchangers + digital depositaries) went live 5 Oct 2026 (per [[Финтехно banks race to control Russian crypto infrastructure]]). Entry barrier is explicitly capital + regulation: a *settlement* digital depositary — the tier that can settle organized-trading crypto deals — faces the highest CD capital floor, ≥250m rub (highly-liquid, high-credit-quality assets; note text + CoinDesk/CoinMarketCap, Jul 2026). Drivers / "why now": (1) regulatory window just opened; (2) for SPB specifically this is existential re-tooling — after the Nov-2023 US OFAC sanctions, the Bank of Russia governor estimated ~80% of SPB's foreign-share volume was blocked, so legal crypto is "a new core" rather than an add-on. Second-order effect: whoever controls the *settlement* depositary captures a rails/toll position, not just trading flow.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: trading volume/turnover, number of admitted assets, infrastructure control (depositary + exchanger licenses), investor-tier access. Key players and basis of competition (control of the stack, not price):
- **Moscow Exchange (MOEX)** — the incumbent universal venue; targets **1 Dec 2026** crypto spot launch, starting BTC/ETH/USDT for retail (broader set for quals), reusing its existing trading-organizer license + a *partner* digital depositary (per Tangem/Cryptotimes/Coingape, Oct 2026). It already runs crypto perpetual futures (BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP/TRX, quals-only) since 22 Sep 2026.
- **Banks** racing for the rails: Sber (digital-depositary application, BTC/ETH/USDT, ~1 Dec target, embedded in SberInvest/online), T-Technologies (Atomyze as depositary + T-Invest Lab as exchanger), VTB, Alfa — per [[Финтехно banks race to control Russian crypto infrastructure]] and [[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]] (5 depositaries + 4 exchangers in the first wave).
- NAUFOR just became the first DFA-platform SRO (9 Oct 2026), adding a mandatory self-regulation layer — [[Bank of Russia registers NAUFOR as first DFA-platform SRO]].

SPB's position: a **catching-up niche challenger**. Its strategic bet is vertical integration — build its *own* settlement digital depositary so "all the main links of the digital-asset market sit inside the SPB ecosystem" (note text), vs MOEX's partner-depositary model. Moat (analysis): if the CD is registered, SPB owns a scarce, capital-gated, regulator-licensed asset (switching cost + regulatory barrier) — but it is a late mover vs Sber/MOEX who target the same 1 Dec window.

**Comps & multiples.** Comparability is weak: the obvious peer, MOEX, is a different scale/model, and SPB is a sanctioned, impaired franchise.
- **SPB Exchange (SPBE.ME):** market cap ~18.8bn rub; latest-quarter revenue ~3,412m rub (~3,233m prior quarter) (per investing.com/cbonds; treat as unaudited screen data → quarterly revenue, not annualized). - **MOEX (incumbent benchmark):** FY2025 net profit ~59.4bn rub (per moex.com). Scale gulf is the headline: MOEX equity-market cap base ~52.9tn rub vs SPB's ~18.8bn rub *own* market cap — ~3 orders of magnitude apart. P/E not computed (SPB profit not sourced).

- Distribution not computed (fewer than 3 comparable multiples; MOEX not comparable in scale). Flag: SPB screens cheap on an absolute basis, but that reflects a sanctions-impaired, shrunken franchise, not a bargain — qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Execution / registration risk.** The whole plan is contingent on the Bank of Russia *registering* SPB's settlement depositary and SPB clearing the ≥250m-rub capital floor; "announced to launch by year-end" ≠ live. If registration slips past MOEX's/Sber's 1 Dec window, SPB loses first-mover ground in an already narrow segment.
2. **Narrow addressable segment + retail gating.** For non-quals the CB allows only BTC/ETH/USDT (after testing, with caps — reported ~300k rub/yr per intermediary). The high-value book is quals-only, so the retail-driven volume that historically fed SPB is structurally capped — the TAM for the "new core" is thinner than the $44bn headline implies.
3. **Sanctions / concentration overhang.** SPB is OFAC-listed (Nov 2023); ~80% of its legacy foreign-share volume was blocked. A pivot into crypto rails does not remove sanctions risk and concentrates the firm's future on a single domestic regulator's goodwill — second-order: any CB policy reversal or further sanctions re-impairs the franchise.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is an infrastructure land-grab, not a trading-fee story: value in Russian regulated crypto will accrue to whoever owns the *settlement depositary* (the toll booth), and SPB is trying to climb the value chain rather than just list assets. Falsifiable thesis: SPB's vertical-integration bet only pays off if its CD is registered and live before the Sber/MOEX 1-Dec cluster; trigger to watch = appearance of SPB's settlement CD in the Bank of Russia registry. What would make it wrong: SPB fails registration or launches materially after peers, in which case it ends up a price-taker on someone else's rails — exactly the disintermediation it is trying to avoid.
