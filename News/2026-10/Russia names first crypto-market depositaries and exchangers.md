# Context-enrichment: Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 4/5. Freshness: FRESH (canonical primary for this event)._

**[0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd).** On 2026-10-06 the Bank of Russia published its first official registers of licensed crypto-market infrastructure under the new digital-currency law: **5 digital depositaries** and **4 digital-currency exchange operators** (9 slots, but fewer distinct firms because VTB sits on both lists). The Финтехно angle in this note is the sharpest de-PR: the "nine companies" framing overstates the count — it is a **concentrated, bank-dominated structure**, not nine independents.
- **Depositaries (5):** Sberbank, VTB, Atomyze, Voltari, «Облачная инфраструктура» (Cloud Infrastructure).
- **Exchangers (4):** VTB, T-Invest Lab (Т-Технологии/T-Bank group), «Зефир» (Zefir), «Система-Крипто» (Sistema-Crypto).
- Admissions granted under the **transitional provisions** of the law — firms can operate now but must fully conform to the rules by **2027-09-01**. Published on cbr.ru/registries/admissionfinmarket.
- **Why structured this way / what it reveals:** the register is a licence GATE, and the gate is calibrated to balance-sheet + compliance scale → it structurally favours state/incumbent banks (Sber, VTB) and the T-Bank group (which supplies BOTH an exchanger, T-Invest Lab, and a depositary, Atomyze). The one genuine anomaly the note surfaces — «Зефир», a RUB 10k-capital LLC registered 11.08.2025 with no public crypto product (GD Andrey Kokorev, owner Irina Egorova) — is the tell: either a front/SPV for an undisclosed backer, or a placeholder that undercuts the "regulated-market" framing. This is the note's real value-add over wire restatements.

**[1] Competitors / peers (who's in the land-grab).** This is an intra-Russia incumbent race, not a global-exchange story. T-Технологии moved earliest (Atomyze custody + T-Invest Lab exchanger); Sber (BTC/ETH/USDT custody targeted ~1 Dec); VTB (trading via "VTB Мои Инвестиции", as early as Nov); Alfa-Bank flagged its own depositary ambitions earlier. Position: Russia is EARLY among G20 in standing up a formal licence register for BOTH trading and custody — but regulatory-first, with zero de-PR'd live volume yet. **Second-order:** unlike Western qualified-custody regimes built on segregation of duties, Russia lets the SAME bank (VTB) hold both the exchange and the custody licence — the opposite of the West's separation principle, concentrating counterparty/conflict risk.

**[2] Company / regime history & fit.** Legal basis: the law "On Digital Currency and Digital Rights" (Russian channel cites № 282-ФЗ; English press: Federal Law No. 282-FZ, signed 2026-08-05), core provisions in force **2026-09-01**; the CBR admission mechanism itself opened **~2026-10-05**, which is why entries appear only now. This is the direct follow-through of the multi-year EPR debate (Finance Ministry vs CBR over cross-border crypto settlement). **Why they act this way:** sanctioned state banks need an onshore, supervised venue both to capture tax/oversight AND to build a cross-border-settlement rail insulated from Western rails — the law permits crypto for cross-border TRADE settlement while keeping DOMESTIC crypto payments BANNED.

**[3] Novelty / value-add / traction.** Genuinely new: first-ever legal crypto-infrastructure licences in Russia; moves the regime from statute to operational registry. BUT traction = zero so far — these are admissions, not trading volumes. Retail is throttled: non-qualified investors capped at **RUB 300,000/yr per intermediary** after a suitability test; qualified investors uncapped. **Who captures the margin:** the licensed incumbent banks that already own the retail/HNW distribution channel — adoption will track distribution, not regulation. What breaks the thesis: if «Зефир»-type shells turn out to be the real volume, or if sanctions keep the venue walled-garden and illiquid.

**[4] What's next / sentiment / risk.** Watch: Sber custody go-live (~1 Dec), VTB retail trading (Nov–Dec) — the first REAL de-PR'd volume. Risks: (a) **sanctions** — Sber (OFAC/EU since 2022) and VTB custodying BTC/ETH/USDT create de-facto sanctions-adjacent rails with no path to Western fiat liquidity; (b) **concentration/self-dealing** (VTB on both registers); (c) **source/figure reliability** — year-one market-size estimates (~$40–46bn, RUB 4tn) are secondary/local projections, not CBR-verified. **Counterintuitive second-order:** formalisation builds a PARALLEL onshore system deliberately unreachable by the global crypto stack — legitimacy at home, isolation abroad.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russia's regulated crypto infrastructure went live via Federal Law No. 282-FZ ("On Digital Currencies and Digital Rights"), effective 1 Sep 2026; the Bank of Russia's admission mechanism for infrastructure opened 5 Oct 2026, and the first registers were published 6 Oct 2026 — five digital depositaries and four crypto-exchange operators (per crypto.news / tokenpost, as of 2026-10-06). The named depositaries are Sberbank, VTB, Atomyze, Voltari and Cloud Infrastructure; the four exchangers are VTB, T-Invest Lab (T-Technologies), Zefir and Sistema-Crypto (per cryptodaily.co.uk / tokenpost, 2026-10-06). The note's protagonist, OOO "Zefir" (OGRN 1257700355131, registered 11 Aug 2025, charter capital RUB 10,000, micro-enterprise, no public product), appears on the *exchanger* register — a shell-like entrant sitting alongside the two largest state banks. Market size: Russia has no disclosed domestic crypto market cap; the economic rationale is cross-border, not retail. Secondary estimates put informal crypto-facilitated sanctions-era foreign-trade flows at ~$11bn/yr in 2024–25, with the A7A5-linked wallet network alone reportedly handling >$72bn of sanctions-related flows in 2025 (per techtimes/cryptonews, 2026; `[UNSOURCED]` for a clean official figure — no Bank of Russia TAM). **Why now:** sanctions-driven need to settle foreign trade outside SWIFT/correspondent banking — the law explicitly permits exporters/importers to settle cross-border contracts in crypto, while keeping crypto banned as a domestic means of payment and capping retail exposure at RUB 300,000/intermediary/year (~$3.7k) (per cryptotimes, 2026-10-05). Structure: deliberately **concentrated and bank-gated** — min capital RUB 15m (~$185k), Bank of Russia admission, "qualified investor"-style retail limits. Value accrues to the depositary/custody + exchange layer that the incumbents already control.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs this market will run on (none disclosed yet): custodied AUC (assets under custody), crypto exchange turnover / spread (take rate), number of qualified vs retail accounts, and cross-border settlement volume. Basis of competition = **distribution + licence + balance sheet**, not product novelty — the moat is regulatory admission plus an existing banking/brokerage funnel. Players and recent moves: Sber applied for depositary status, plans BTC/ETH/USDT launch embedded in SberBank Online / SberInvestitsii / SberBiznes, target 1 Dec 2026 (see [[Финтехно banks race to control Russian crypto infrastructure]]). T-Technologies filed for *both* links — Atomyze for depositary, T-Invest Lab for exchanger — launching after an internal employee pilot. VTB sits on both registers (depositary + exchanger). Protagonist position: "Zefir" is a **niche/opaque** entrant — a one-year-old micro-firm with no public product, low financial-sustainability markers (Rusprofile flags "low" solvency/efficiency, "risks present"), competing in the same exchanger register as VTB and T-Technologies. No disclosed moat; survival depends on who its ultimate beneficiaries/clients are, which is not public (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** No market-cap or valuation attaches to this regulatory listing — multiples are **not applicable** (no equity event, no round). Comparability is qualitative, by regime position. Internal comps from the base: [[Финтехно banks race to control Russian crypto infrastructure]] (same registers, Sber/T/VTB strategies, 2026-10-06), [[Russians open 220,000 digital ruble accounts in first month]] (parallel Bank-of-Russia digital-rail rollout — 220k accounts in month 1 vs ~60k expected, but low activity ~150k operations, i.e. supply-pushed adoption, 2026-10-07), and [[Russia tightens counter-sanction limits on retail non-resident clients]] (the capital-control backdrop, 2026-06). Valuation multiples: **no data** — private, non-listed infra operators with undisclosed economics (`[UNSOURCED]` for AUC, turnover, revenue).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Shell / undisclosed-beneficiary risk (Zefir).** A RUB 10,000-capital, one-year-old firm with no public product on a national crypto-exchange register invites questions about who stands behind it and for what flows; Rusprofile "low solvency / risks present" markers compound this. Second-order: if first-wave operators are vehicles for sanctions-related cross-border settlement rather than retail service, the "market" is a compliance channel, not a competitive consumer business.
2. **Sanctions / correspondent-banking contagion.** The entire rationale is sanctions-evasion-adjacent cross-border settlement; any OFAC/EU designation of a named operator or its rails (cf. A7A5) would strand custodied assets and freeze counterparties — concentration into Sber/VTB means the designation surface is also concentrated.
3. **State-bank capture + thin retail demand.** Registers skew to Sber/VTB/T-Technologies; the RUB 300k retail cap plus domestic-payment ban limits organic demand (echoed by the digital-ruble "high accounts, low activity" pattern). Execution risk: launches are announced (Sber 1 Dec, T-Technologies post-pilot), not live — "announced ≠ adopted."

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This formalizes a **bank-controlled, sanctions-oriented** crypto rail rather than an open exchange market — the incumbents that own payments and brokerage distribution are set to own crypto custody/exchange too, so the thesis is consolidation, not disruption (analysis). Falsifiable: if within ~2 quarters the small non-bank entrants (Zefir, Sistema-Crypto, Voltari) show no public product or get pruned from the registers — as a similarly-named MKK was excluded in Aug — the first wave was a placeholder/compliance list, not a competitive cohort. Trigger to watch: Sber's 1 Dec BTC/ETH/USDT launch and the first disclosed cross-border settlement volumes.
