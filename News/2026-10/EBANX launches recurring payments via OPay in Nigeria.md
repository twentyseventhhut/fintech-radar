 Integration gives global subscription businesses, from streaming to SaaS, a direct way to reach 40 million monthly active OPay users 
CURITIBA, Brazil and LAGOS, Nigeria, Oct. 22, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- EBANX today becomes the first global payments company to go live with OPay Direct Debit, a new solution for recurring transactions within the digital wallet of OPay , the leading digital bank in Nigeria. By offering this payment option at checkout through EBANX, global subscription businesses can now reach OPay’s 40 million monthly active users, representing one in three adults in the country.

 "With one of the continent’s highest mobile penetration rates and a young, fast-growing population, Nigeria is expected to remain Africa’s largest and most strategic digital market over the next decade,” said Eduardo de Abreu , Chief Product Officer (CPO) of EBANX. "Being the first global payments company to go live with recurring payments through OPay is a testament to the access EBANX has built in emerging markets.” 
Nigeria is Africa’s largest e-commerce market, valued at USD 30 billion, according to World Data Lab (WDL) data analyzed by EBANX, and is expected to grow further, with the country projected to add 32 million new consumers to the consumer class by 2036.
In Nigeria, credit card ownership stands at just 5% among adults, while mobile phone penetration reaches 84%, according to the World Bank . " This difference creates a significant opportunity for global merchants to expand recurring payment coverage beyond traditional card-on-file models ,” stated Abreu .
According to WDL, 95% of all online spending in Nigeria already comes from consumers without a credit card, a share expected to remain nearly unchanged for the next 10 years.
Digital wallets and bank transfers are Nigerians’ preferred way to pay, together accounting for 65% of the country’s e-commerce volume, according to Payment and Commerce Market Intelligence (PCMI) data analyzed by EBANX.

OPay lets Nigerian consumers make purchases through its digital wallet and bank transfers, as well as set up recurring payments. For these transactions, users make a one-time authorization directly in the app, enabling global merchants to collect future subscription charges without requiring customers to be present or re-enter payment details for every transaction.

 "Our partnership with EBANX isn’t just processing transactions; it’s shaping the future of digital payments across the region,” said Elizabeth Wang , Chief Commercial Officer, OPay. "This will give Nigerians an easy, fast and convenient way to pay for the global digital services they love, from streaming platforms to online tools. With OPay Direct Debit , customers can enjoy a simple payment experience while staying in control of their subscriptions,” added Wang . 
 Scaling subscriptions in Africa and beyond 
The announcement follows a similar EBANX initiative in South Africa, where Canva became the first international company to offer recurring payments through Capitec Pay’s account-to-account solution . With the integration, the SaaS merchant expanded subscription access for South African consumers via their bank accounts. EBANX’s integration of OPay Direct Debit extends this same alternative recurring strategy to Nigeria.

 "Nigeria is the latest proof point on how EBANX scales operationally across emerging markets, not by replicating a single playbook, but by building the right integration for each local market, ” said Marin Mignot , Chief Operating Officer (COO) of EBANX.
" The gap between how people actually pay and the payment methods merchants have historically supported is exactly where we see the most room to grow, and OPay Direct Debit is one more example of building that bridge for global merchants ,” noted Mignot .
The launches in Nigeria and South Africa are also part of a broader global push. Including these two countries, EBANX offers recurring alternative payment solutions in 13 emerging markets across Asia, Africa, and Latin America, enabling global merchants to potentially reach more than 1 billion consumers* who rely on local payment methods such as digital wallets and account-to-account transfers.
 *1.116 billion people use alternative payment methods (APMs) across the following 13 emerging markets, based on data from the operators of the most used APMs in each country and Statista: Nigeria (OPay) , South Africa (Capitec Pay) , Brazil (Pix) , India (UPI) , Colombia (Nequi) , Peru (Yape) , the Philippines (GCash) , Indonesia (DANA) , Malaysia (TrueMoney) , Mexico, Chile, Argentina, and Uruguay (Mercado Pago) .

# Context-enrichment: EBANX launches recurring payments via OPay in Nigeria
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
EBANX went live (2026-10-22) as the first global cross-border PSP to support **OPay Direct Debit**, a recurring-payment rail inside OPay's wallet, enabling global subscription merchants (streaming, SaaS) to bill Nigerian users who authorize once in-app. Headline reach: OPay's "40M MAU" (~1 in 3 Nigerian adults).

De-PR'd:
- This is an **integration/enablement announcement, not traction**. Zero merchants named live on the Nigeria rail, zero GMV, zero approval/churn numbers. Contrast the June Brazil note where a *named* merchant (Nord Security) and a real metric (Pix Automatico = 28% of volume in 6 months) existed — here there is nothing of the sort yet. "Can now reach" ≠ "is reaching."
- **"First global payments company" is a narrow, self-defined superlative.** Local PSPs Paystack and Flutterwave already run mature recurring/subscription rails and bank direct-debit in Nigeria; EBANX's claim is only about a *cross-border* PSP being first to wire OPay's specific Direct Debit product into international checkout. Narrow framing is doing a lot of work.
- The "40M MAU" number is **PR-stale/low**: OPay's own US IPO filing (Oct 2026) reported ~50.1M MAU / 26.8M DAU as of July. The release anchors to a rounder, older 40M — flatters nothing but signals recycled boilerplate.
- Mechanism: a **one-time in-app mandate** (consumer-authorized pull) — functionally an account/wallet-to-account direct debit, the same alternative-APM recurring pattern EBANX ships elsewhere (Pix Automatico in BR, Capitec Pay in ZA). Nothing technically novel; it's EBANX plugging into a local mandate primitive OPay already built.
- Why framed this way: EBANX sells "access to emerging markets" as its moat. Each country launch is packaged as a "first" proof point to support a software/platform narrative ahead of a potential IPO (EBANX last valued ~$1B in 2021; ~$360M ARR reported for 2025, 65% of gross profit now ex-Brazil).

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Local Nigerian rails (incumbent, ahead on product):** Paystack (Stripe-owned) has the most mature NG recurring/subscription + dunning stack; Flutterwave has recurring + "Recurring USSD"; Interswitch Webpay lacks recurring. For a *domestic* merchant these already solve recurring — so EBANX's wedge is strictly the *global/cross-border* merchant who wants local APM coverage without local entities.
- **Cross-border APM peers (direct comps):** dLocal (NASDAQ: DLO; added to Russell 2000 — see [[dLocal added to US Russell 2000 small-cap index]]) is EBANX's closest rival and also pushes African/emerging-market local methods. PayU, Rapyd, Thunes/Checkout.com compete for the same "one API to many local methods" role.
- **Position:** parity-to-catching-up, not ahead. EBANX's genuine edge is breadth of integrations + being early to wire *specific* new local primitives (Bre-B in Colombia from day one — [[EBANX brings Colombia's Bre-B instant payments to global firms]]; Pix Automatico — [[Nord Security partners EBANX to offer Pix Automatico in Brazil]]; Capitec Pay in ZA). The second-order dynamic: value accrues to whoever owns the **merchant relationship + integration breadth**, while the local wallet (OPay) owns the consumer and the mandate — so EBANX risks being a reseller of OPay's rail, not the margin capturer.

## [2] Company history / fit
EBANX (founded 2012, Curitiba, Brazil; cross-border PSP for LatAm → now Africa/Asia; Singapore tech/regulatory HQ added 2026) is executing a deliberate **"localize per market, don't copy-paste"** expansion. The Nigeria launch is the logical next node in a 13-market alternative-recurring footprint (Nigeria/OPay, SA/Capitec, Brazil/Pix, India/UPI, Colombia/Nequi, Peru/Yape, Philippines/GCash, Indonesia/DANA, Malaysia/TrueMoney, plus Mercado Pago markets) claiming ~1.116B reachable APM users.
Why the company acts this way: a cross-border PSP's take-rate is a commodity; the durable moat is **integration depth into local APMs that global card networks can't serve** (NG card ownership ~5%, mobile ~84%; 95% of NG online spend is from non-card consumers; wallets + transfers = 65% of NG e-commerce per PCMI). EBANX is racing to own the longest list of these rails before dLocal/PayU to justify a software multiple ahead of a possible IPO.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Real novelty = **low**. The *capability* (wallet direct-debit recurring) already exists in NG via local players; EBANX's contribution is making OPay's Direct Debit consumable by *international* merchants through one integration — incremental distribution, not a new mechanism. It is the third stamp of the same "alternative recurring" playbook in 2026 (Brazil/Pix, SA/Capitec, now NG/OPay).
Traction = **unproven for this launch**: no named merchants, no volume, no approval rates on the NG rail. The only hard traction datapoint in the corpus is the *Brazil* precedent (28% of Nord Security volume in 6 months) — evidence the model *can* work, but not evidence this NG launch has.
Who captures margin (analysis): OPay owns the 50M+ users and the mandate primitive and is IPO-bound at ~$4B ([[OPay files for US IPO as revenue more than doubles]], [[OPay secures $185M private placement with Standard Bank]]); EBANX is the cross-border conduit. If OPay (or Paystack/Flutterwave) builds its own cross-border merchant acquisition, EBANX's intermediary position commoditizes. The value-add is real only insofar as global merchants prefer one multi-market PSP over N local integrations.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect more "first with [local rail]" country-launch press from EBANX as IPO positioning, and likely named-merchant follow-ups (the tell to watch: does a Canva/Spotify/streaming name go live on OPay, as Canva did on Capitec). NG macro is genuinely attractive (Africa's largest e-commerce market ~$30B; +32M consumers by 2036 per WDL) and card-thin, so alternative recurring is structurally the right bet. 
Risks / second-order: (1) OPay's own stability — it has been losing PoS agents to Moniepoint ([[OPay loses Nigeria PoS agents to Moniepoint amid CBN deadline]]) and faces heavy local competition; concentration on one wallet is a single point of failure. (2) CBN regulatory tightening and FX/repatriation friction for cross-border subscription flows. (3) Disintermediation: Nigeria's new open National Payment Stack ([[Nigeria launches open-source National Payment Stack]]) could standardize mandates/direct-debit and erode the "first/exclusive" edge. The central question is not "is Africa a good market" (yes) but "can EBANX keep capturing margin as the conduit once wallets and local PSPs reach merchants directly."

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Emerging-markets cross-border payments: a global acquirer/PSP (EBANX) plugs a local alternative-payment-method (APM) rail — OPay Direct Debit, a wallet-based account-to-account mandate — into merchant checkout. The driver is the card-gap: credit-card ownership in Nigeria is ~5% of adults vs 84% mobile penetration; 95% of online spend already comes from non-card consumers; wallets + bank transfers = 65% of e-commerce volume (all per the PR, citing World Bank / WDL / PCMI data analyzed by EBANX — these are company-framed secondary figures, not independently audited). Nigeria is Africa's largest e-commerce market at ~USD 30bn (World Data Lab, via the PR), projected to add 32M consumers to the consumer class by 2036. "Why now": subscription merchants (streaming/SaaS) have exhausted card-on-file in markets where the card simply isn't present, so the growth edge shifts to mandate-based A2A/wallet recurring — the same pattern EBANX ran with Pix Automático in Brazil and Capitec Pay in South Africa. Structure: fragmented at the local-rail layer (each market has its own dominant APM), consolidating at the aggregation layer, where a handful of global PSPs (EBANX, dLocal, Checkout, Adyen) compete to be the single integration that unlocks all of them. Barriers: regulatory licensing per market + the operational cost of bespoke local integrations (EBANX's stated "not one playbook" model is itself the moat and the cost).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV, take rate (gross profit / TPV), authorization/approval rate, and — for recurring — mandate retention / churn. Key players in EM cross-border APM aggregation: **dLocal** (public, DLO), **EBANX** (private), **Checkout.com**, **Adyen** (card-first, thinner EM-APM depth); on the local side the counterparties are the APMs themselves (OPay, Flutterwave, Pix, UPI, GCash). Basis of competition: breadth of local-method coverage + approval rates + being first to a new rail, not price. Recent dated moves: EBANX launched OPay Direct Debit live 2026-10-22; prior A2A-recurring launches — Capitec Pay / Canva in South Africa and Pix Automático with Nord Security in Brazil, where recurring Pix reached 28% of Nord's Brazil volume within 6 months ([[Nord Security partners EBANX to offer Pix Automatico in Brazil]], per EBANX/PR 2026). EBANX now offers recurring APMs in 13 EM markets (~1.12bn reachable consumers, per PR). dLocal by contrast hit $17.7bn TPV in Q2 2026 (+92% YoY) and raised FY26 TPV-growth guidance to 60–70% (per Q2 2026 earnings) — the scale benchmark. Protagonist's position: EBANX is **ahead on being first-mover into new EM recurring rails** but **niche vs dLocal on absolute scale**; moat is intangible/relationship (local licensing + per-market integrations), not network effects. `(analysis)`

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This extends the A2A/wallet-recurring playbook from LatAm (Pix) to Africa's largest market — the signal to watch is whether "recurring beyond the card" becomes the default EM subscription rail, not a niche. What breaks it: mandate churn high (one-time authorization is easier to revoke than a card-on-file) or OPay deprioritizing payments for lending.
