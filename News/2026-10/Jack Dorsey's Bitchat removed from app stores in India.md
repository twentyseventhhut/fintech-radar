Jack Dorsey’s Bitchat disappears from app stores in India after government order
Bitchat, Jack Dorsey’s decentralized messaging app designed to work without an internet connection, has disappeared from Apple and Google app stores in India, months after New Delhi first sought to restrict access to the open source software.
Dorsey said Saturday that the Indian government had ordered Apple to remove Bitchat from its App Store in the country. In a notice from Apple that Dorsey posted on X, India’s Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology issued the demand under Section 69A of the Information Technology Act, the country’s primary legal provision for government-ordered online blocking.
Apple’s notice said Bitchat would remain available on its App Store outside India. However, access to the app through its TestFlight beta-testing service would also be blocked in the country.
Bitchat was also no longer available for download through Google Play in India when TechCrunch checked on Saturday. Bitchat’s website was also inaccessible across various internet service providers in the country. It was not immediately clear whether Google and internet service providers had also received directions from the federal government.
Apple, Google, and India’s IT ministry did not respond to requests for comment.
Launched in July last year, Bitchat uses Bluetooth mesh networking to allow nearby devices to exchange encrypted messages without relying on cellular networks, internet access, or centralized servers.
In July 2026, Dorsey revealed that Indian authorities had ordered GitHub to take down repositories associated with the open source messaging app, raising questions among digital rights advocates and legal experts about the legal basis for restricting software based on its functionality.
The Indian government said in its order to GitHub at the time that Bitchat’s architecture made it hard for law enforcement agencies to intercept communications or trace users, and pointed to its ability to continue operating during internet shutdowns. The July order relied on a provision of India’s information technology law that deals with intermediaries and their liability for third-party content. That differs from Section 69A, cited in Apple’s latest notice.
The Internet Freedom Foundation, a New Delhi-based digital rights advocacy group, called the latest order unconstitutional, arguing that Section 69A allows the government to block unlawful information but not a messaging app because of its ability to operate during internet shutdowns. It also said neither the blocking order nor the allegedly illegal content cited as the basis for the action had been made public.
The latest restrictions on Bitchat come as India is seeing a fresh wave of ⁠youth-led protests over changes to the country’s voter rolls. Police detained several people during demonstrations in New Delhi on Saturday, as protesters demanded the resignation of India’s chief election commissioner over alleged manipulation of electoral rolls. Authorities suspended internet services around New Delhi’s Jantar Mantar for 12 hours on Friday during protests there.
During July’s protests, demonstrators reportedly turned to Bitchat and rival app Briar after authorities suspended internet services.
Interest in Bitchat surged in India around the same time. The country accounted for about 85% of the app’s global downloads between July 17 and July 23, up from roughly 1% in the previous 30 days, data from market intelligence firm Sensor Tower showed.

# Context-enrichment: Jack Dorsey's Bitchat removed from app stores in India
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-03 Dorsey posted an Apple notice on X: India's Ministry of Electronics and IT (MeitY) ordered Apple to pull **Bitchat** from the India App Store under **Section 69A** of the IT Act (India's formal site/app-blocking provision). Apple confirmed the app stays live outside India; TestFlight beta access is also blocked in-country. On Google Play Bitchat was likewise unavailable, and the Bitchat website was unreachable across several Indian ISPs — though it was NOT confirmed that Google/ISPs received the same order (TechCrunch could not verify; Apple, Google and MeitY declined comment).
- **Why this framing matters:** this is the SECOND escalation. In **July 2026** India (I4C, Home Ministry) ordered **GitHub** to disable three Bitchat repos within 3 hours — but under **Section 79(3)(b)** + IT Rules 2021 Rule 3(1)(d), an *intermediary-liability* provision, NOT the blocking statute. The Oct order switches to **Section 69A**, the proper blocking mechanism. That shift is the real tell: the July action was legally shaky (IFF called it unconstitutional), so the state moved to the statute actually designed for blocking. The trigger in both cases was identical — youth/CJP protests at New Delhi's Jantar Mantar with internet shutdowns (12h shutdown on Fri before the Oct order), during which protesters used offline mesh apps.
- **De-PR'd reality:** there is no "Bitchat business" story here. No revenue, no users-as-customers, no payments flow. It is an open-source, no-registration, Bluetooth-mesh app. The "fintech" angle is thin: Bitchat is Dorsey-associated and nominally "bitcoin-linked" (CoinDesk framing), but the event is a **tech-policy/censorship** story, not a payments or crypto-rails development.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Offline Bluetooth-mesh messengers: **Bridgefy** (~12M users, BLE, the historic protest tool — Hong Kong 2019), **Briar** (P2P over Bluetooth/Wi-Fi/Tor, censorship-resistant), **Meshtastic** (LoRa radio, longer range), **FireChat** (defunct; inspired the category), newer **OSHI**. During the Jantar Mantar protests, protesters reportedly used Bitchat, Bridgefy AND Meshtastic together. Crucially, India's July takedown notices **targeted Briar and Bridgefy too** (per Medianama) — so this is a category-wide crackdown, not a Bitchat-specific one.
- **Mechanism delta (one line):** Bitchat ≈ Bridgefy on transport (BLE mesh) but adds **no phone number / no registration / no central logging** and is fully open source — which is precisely the property the state cites as un-interceptable. Briar is arguably more hardened (Tor). Bitchat's differentiation is brand (Dorsey) + zero-identity, not a technical moat.
- **Why the landscape is this way:** the category only matters during internet shutdowns, and India leads the world in shutdowns — so demand is episodic and politically triggered, not a growth market. Download spikes are shutdown-correlated (see [3]), which is why no player has monetized it.

## [2] Company history / fit
Bitchat launched **July 2025** by Dorsey (X/Twitter co-founder, Block/Square CEO). It is a side/open-source project, not a Block product — do not conflate with Block's payments/bitcoin line (see [[Jack Dorsey teases two new Square and Cash App hardware products]], [[Square rolls out bitcoin payments at POS]], [[Block rolls out USDC stablecoin payments to Cash App users]]). Dorsey's throughline is decentralization/censorship-resistance (bitcoin, Nostr, now mesh); Bitchat fits that ideology, not a commercial roadmap.
- **Why Dorsey acts this way:** the project exists to make a political/architectural point (speech should survive shutdowns), so Dorsey *amplifies* the ban (posts the notice on X) — the ban is free marketing and validates the thesis. The state blocking it is, for the project, a feature.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Not technically novel — BLE mesh messaging predates Bitchat by a decade (FireChat 2014, Bridgefy, Briar). The only "new" thing is the **regulatory escalation** (July GitHub/Section 79 → Oct App Store/Section 69A) and the app-store-level block of a foreign-hosted open-source app.
- **Traction, measured honestly:** the one hard number is a **spike** — India was ~85% of Bitchat's global downloads 17–23 July 2026 vs ~1% the prior 30 days (Sensor Tower). That is the signature of **shutdown-driven episodic demand**, not durable adoption. There is no retention, DAU, or revenue data.
- **Why the value-add is limited for US/fintech readers:** blocking an app store listing does little against an open-source, sideloadable, Bluetooth-mesh app ("you can't ban a whisper" — inkl/IFF framing); mesh works *because* it needs no network. So the ban's practical efficacy is low and the whole saga is more a free-speech/enforcement-limits story than a market event. No one in a payments "stack" captures anything here.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect: (a) continued cat-and-mouse — sideloading, mirrors, VPN/website access; (b) the Section 69A order likely challenged (Internet Freedom Foundation already calls it unconstitutional, notes neither the order nor the "illegal content" was made public); (c) more category-wide notices as India's shutdown-heavy posture persists. Regulatory backdrop is the active CJP/voter-roll protests and Jantar Mantar shutdowns.
- **Second-order:** each ban *raises* Bitchat's salience and downloads (Streisand effect), strengthening Dorsey's decentralization narrative while doing little to stop usage — a losing enforcement proposition for the state, and a reputational win (not a revenue win) for the project. For a fintech digest this is **adjacent color on Dorsey/India tech-policy, not a payments development.**

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Subvertical: decentralized / off-grid messaging (Bluetooth mesh), adjacent to crypto/privacy-tech rather than core fintech — the "payments" tag reflects Dorsey's Block/Square orbit, not a payments product. No addressable-market figure is publicly verifiable for mesh messaging specifically → TAM "no data". The real demand driver is quantifiable: India logged ~65 internet shutdowns in 2025, the most of any democracy, and ~24 by 22 Jul 2026 (per Access Now via Browsec, as of 2026-07); shutdowns cost India ~$179.8m in 2025 (per Top10VPN citations, 2025). Structure: highly fragmented, non-commercial — these are open-source / nonprofit projects (Briar), free apps (Bitchat), or thin-freemium (Bridgefy), with no revenue model and no capital/regulatory moat; the only barrier is engineering (reliable BLE mesh). Why now: mesh usage spikes are event-driven — India accounted for ~85% of Bitchat global downloads 17–23 Jul 2026 vs ~1% the prior 30 days (per Sensor Tower, via TechCrunch) during voter-roll protests and a Jantar Mantar internet suspension. The second-order effect: as India substitutes selective app bans for blanket shutdowns, mesh apps become the explicit target rather than collateral damage.

**Competitive landscape.** The category runs on non-financial KPIs: downloads/MAU, mesh range/reliability, and censorship-resistance (no central server to subpoena or block). Key players and basis of competition (product/resilience, not price — all effectively free): Briar (nonprofit, ~2.6m downloads, Tor + mesh), Bridgefy (claims ~12.5m users, commercial SDK), Bitchat (Dorsey, launched Jul 2025), plus Meshtastic and AirChat (all per cybernews/secumeet roundups, 2026). Recent moves: Jul 2026 India directed GitHub (via I4C) to disable Bitchat source repos; the same month telecom operators got — then were orally told to ignore — directions to block Briar and Bridgefy too (per Medianama, 2026-07); 3 Oct 2026 MeitY ordered Apple to pull Bitchat under Section 69A, with Google Play and the website also going dark. Bitchat's position: a niche, high-profile entrant — more notoriety (Dorsey) than installed base vs Bridgefy's claimed 12.5m. Moat: none durable; censorship-resistance is a feature competitors replicate, and the "moat" is really the adversary's inability to block a serverless app — which app-store removal now partially defeats (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation, round, revenue, or market cap for Bitchat or peers — these are open-source/nonprofit/free projects. EV/Revenue, P/S, price-per-user: all "no data"; no multiples computable, distribution not computed. Internal comps are regulatory/precedent, not financial: [[RBI removes Paytm Payments Bank from scheduled banks list]] (India platform-level regulatory removal of a fintech, 2026-10) and [[SF orders Apple and Google to purge 'nudify' apps]] (government-ordered app-store takedown executed via Apple/Google as chokepoints, 2026-07). Both show the same mechanism at work here: the state regulating a service by leveraging the centralized gatekeeper, not the (uncatchable) app itself.

**Risk flags.**
- Regulatory / distribution-chokepoint risk: a serverless app marketed as un-blockable is still blockable at the app store and ISP/DNS layer — Section 69A + Apple/Google compliance removes the main acquisition funnel, so "decentralized" ≠ "distribution-proof." Second-order: investors/builders in censorship-resistant tooling can't assume distribution immunity.
- Legal-basis / precedent escalation: India shifted from a July intermediary-liability order (GitHub) to a Section 69A blocking order (Apple) — IFF calls it unconstitutional and the order/underlying content are unpublished. The ambiguity lets the state target software by functionality, chilling any mesh/privacy project with India exposure.
- Jurisdiction concentration / fragility of the use case: demand is spike-driven and geographically concentrated (India ~85% of downloads in the Jul surge window), so a single large market's ban guts the app's relevance precisely when it matters most — no diversified, recurring usage to fall back on.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is a regulatory re-rating of "censorship-resistant" as a category claim: India is piloting surgical app-level bans as a substitute for costly blanket shutdowns, so the blocking surface moves from the network to the app-store/ISP gatekeepers. Falsifiable thesis: if the mechanism works, expect copy-paste orders against Briar/Bridgefy and in other shutdown-prone states within ~6–12 months; trigger to watch = a published Section 69A order or a second named mesh app pulled from Indian stores. What breaks the thesis: a court stay or the government quietly walking it back (as with the July telecom directive), signaling the legal basis won't hold.
