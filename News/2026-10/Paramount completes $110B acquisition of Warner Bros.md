# Context-enrichment: Paramount completes $110B acquisition of Warner Bros

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On **2026-10-06** Paramount Skydance Corp. closed its acquisition of **Warner Bros. Discovery (WBD)**, forming a combined company rebranded **Skydance** (ticker **SKYD**). De-PR'd specifics:
- **Price:** $31.00/share all-cash, ~$80.9B equity value at signing; headline "$110B" / "$111B" figures include assumption of WBD net debt (~$80–90B). The round "$110B" is the enterprise framing, not cash paid.
- **Financing/debt:** Combined entity opens with roughly **$80–90B of net debt**. Backed by the Ellison family (David Ellison = Chairman & co-CEO; Larry Ellison / Oracle money behind it); Ynon Kreiz (ex-Mattel) as co-CEO. The PR line "two companies with 100-year heritages… next-generation media company driven by creativity and technology" is boilerplate; the real story is a **highly leveraged roll-up** targeting **$6B of merger "synergies."**
- **Contested close:** This was NOT a clean deal. Paramount **outbid Netflix**. WBD's board had a prior all-cash agreement with Netflix (Jan 2026); in Q1 2026 Paramount paid a **$2.8B termination fee to Netflix on WBD's behalf**. A per-share **"ticking fee"** ($0.00277778/day after 2026-09-30) compensated holders for close slippage — a tell that timing risk was real.
- **Why structured this way:** All-cash + debt assumption (vs. stock) let the Ellison camp win a bidding war decisively and avoid diluting family control. Anchoring PR to "heritage/creativity+technology" deflects from the central fact — the combined company is debt-heavy in a declining linear-TV / streaming-loss environment. **(The Mergers.ru source text itself is thin — 217 words, a single PR quote; it adds nothing beyond the headline.)**

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US/global premium media & streaming (SVOD + studios + linear/news). No single credible TAM in-corpus; per Marketplace (via Netflix-WBD coverage, Feb 2026) US streaming share splits ~Netflix 19% / Amazon Prime 17% / Disney+ ~14%, with Paramount+ and HBO Max combined ~14% — i.e. the new Skydance roughly matches Disney+ but still trails Netflix and Amazon (per Marketplace, as of 2026-02-27). Structure: consolidating from fragmented — "too many players for all to make money," analysts expect more consolidation within ~2yrs (per Marketplace). Drivers / why-now: (1) streaming economics force scale (content spend is fixed-cost, so subscriber base is the denominator that must grow); (2) linear-TV/cable decline pushes studios to bundle direct-to-consumer; (3) the deal only closed after settlements with a state coalition and a Hollywood writers' union removed the main legal blocks (per NBC/CBS, 2026-10-06) — regulatory/labor friction is itself a driver of who can afford to buy.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: subscribers / ARPU, content spend, streaming segment EBITDA, net leverage. Key players: Netflix (scale leader, ~30% op margin, FY26 guide $51.0–51.4bn rev per Netflix 8-K), Disney (rev ~$101bn consensus FY26, ~15% op margin), Amazon Prime Video, and now Paramount Skydance/WBD. Basis of competition: content library + franchises (deal unites Harry Potter, DC, Mission: Impossible, CBS, CNN, HBO Max, Paramount+) and distribution scale, not price. Recent moves with dates: WBD terminated its Netflix merger 2026-02-27 (Netflix walked, paid a $2.8bn termination fee), accepting Paramount's "superior proposal"; a judge cleared closing, deal completed 2026-10-06 at $31.00/share cash (per CBS/NBC/CNBC). Protagonist position: catching up, not ahead — combined streaming share (~14%) is Disney-class but sub-Netflix; moat = franchise IP + news (CNN) + library scale (intangibles/scale), weak on the subscriber-network side `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal headline: $110bn (transaction/EV framing per Bloomberg via source; equity consideration $31.00/share cash). WBD FY2025: revenue $37.3bn, adj. EBITDA $8.7bn, net debt $29.0bn, 3.3x net leverage (per WBD FY25 release).
- Implied EV/Revenue on the $110bn headline: `$110bn / $37.3bn = 2.95x`.
- Implied EV/EBITDA: `$110bn / $8.7bn = 12.6x`. (Caveat: the $110bn figure is press "deal size"; whether it is strict EV or includes/excludes WBD's $29bn net debt is not disclosed in-source → treat as indicative, `[UNSOURCED]` on exact EV bridge.)
- Netflix (external peer, public): rev-multiple ~8.5x and market cap ~$279–399bn across 2026 prints; its reported EV/EBITDA swings 26.8x (Apr) → 8.74x (Q2 figure) in the same source set — figures internally inconsistent, so `[UNSOURCED]` for a clean Netflix EV/EBITDA; directionally Netflix trades richer than the WBD deal on revenue (8.5x vs ~3x).
- Disney (external peer): rev ~$101bn, no clean free EV/EBITDA sourced → "no data".
- Internal corpus comps: no comparable media/streaming mega-merger in base; nearest M&A notes are fintech, not comparable (different model/scale): [[Network International and Magnati to merge in Q3]], [[This Week in Fintech Bicecorp and Grupo Security merge to form BICE]] — flagged as NON-comparable, excluded from the multiple set.
- Read: ~3x revenue / ~12.6x EBITDA on a declining-revenue (-5% ex-FX) asset is in-line-to-full for legacy media, and cheap vs Netflix's growth multiple — but WBD is shrinking, so the gap is justified by Netflix's growth, not an obvious bargain `(analysis)`.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Leverage / integration.** WBD brings $29.0bn net debt (3.3x) onto a combined balance sheet during a secular linear decline; debt service + merger integration (two co-CEOs, Ellison + Kreiz from 2026-10-05) is the central execution risk — second-order: forced content-spend cuts could weaken the very IP moat the deal is premised on.
2. **Sub-scale vs leaders.** Combined ~14% US streaming share still trails Netflix (~19%) and Amazon (~17%); if scale was the thesis, the deal buys parity with Disney, not leadership — risk the merger doesn't fix the economics that triggered it.
3. **Regulatory/political overhang.** Closing required settling a state coalition and a writers' union; owning CNN under a controlling shareholder invites continued political/regulatory scrutiny — second-order: concessions or editorial constraints could cap value.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is consolidation, not a re-rating: it ratifies "scale or exit" in streaming and likely triggers the next round (Marketplace: more deals within ~2yrs) — watch for the next legacy player (e.g. a smaller streamer or studio) to be put in play, or for Skydance to divest/split linear/news. Falsifiable thesis `(analysis)`: if combined streaming subs/EBITDA don't inflect up within ~4 quarters post-close, the deal was defensive (debt + scale-for-survival) rather than offensive — trigger to watch: first combined-entity quarterly streaming segment print. What breaks the thesis: rapid subscriber synergy or a HBO Max + Paramount+ bundle that closes the gap to Netflix.
