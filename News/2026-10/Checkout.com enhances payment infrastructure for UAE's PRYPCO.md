Checkout.com enhances payment infrastructure for PRYPCO, a leading financial technology platform in the UAE

PRYPCO will leverage payout capabilities and network tokens from Checkout.com to improve the payment experience and increase security.
DUBAI, UAE – Checkout.com, a leading global digital payments provider, has announced a partnership with PRYPCO, the first all-digital real estate ownership platform in the MENA region. 
Through the partnership, Checkout.com has helped PRYPCO achieve a 20% increase in payment acceptance rates, creating a smoother, more reliable experience for its customers while helping protect revenue that could otherwise be lost to payment declines.  Building on this success, PRYPCO has also extended the partnership to include payment processing for its real estate tokenization platform, PRYPCO Mint.
The Dubai-based fintech ecosystem simplifies real estate investment, home financing, and ownership services through fractional property investments, blockchain tokenization, digital mortgages, gold investing, Golden Visa, and Will service assistance. PRYPCO offers a single point of entry to the entire real estate ecosystem across the UAE, breaking down traditional barriers to property ownership. To date, the company has facilitated real estate transactions worth over AED20 billion for more than 110,000 customers. 
As the UAE’s digital economy and real estate sector continues to expand, robust fintech infrastructure is becoming a critical driver for market scaling. High-value digital transactions require a careful balance between transaction speed and security. PRYPCO chose to work with Checkout.com for its direct acquiring capabilities and digital payment expertise, enabling fast, high-performing payments at scale.
PRYPCO has integrated Checkout.com’s acquiring and processing technology into PRYPCO Blocks, its leading fractional property investment platform, delivering faster, more seamless payments for customers. The collaboration supports higher transaction volumes, including during peak demand around new property launches, while providing more competitive processing rates. Checkout.com’s network tokenization technology further strengthens payment security by replacing sensitive card details with secure, unique digital identifiers, helping protect customer data and improve transaction approval rates by reducing false declines. 
Through Checkout.com’s global payouts capabilities, PRYPCO also enables customers to move their money quickly and confidently. Customers can fund and withdraw from their accounts with near-instant access, supported by secure, reliable payment infrastructure that makes the investment experience smoother from start to finish. 
Since going live with Checkout.com’s platform in July, PRYPCO has observed a significant increase in payment acceptance rates, while maintaining the speed and security needed to support its fast-growing customer base, making high-value transactions daily. 
Remo Giovanni Abbondandolo, General Manager for MENA at Checkout.com, said: “We’re proud to partner with PRYPCO as they reimagine how property investment and ownership work in the UAE. Their vision for a more accessible, digital-first real estate journey aligns closely with our commitment to powering high-performance digital payment solutions that help innovative businesses grow. At Checkout.com, we are committed to working with our partners across diverse sectors to strengthen digital payments in the region.”
Essa Ibrahim, Co-founder and President of PRYPCO, said: “Making property investment more accessible requires a platform people can rely on, especially when a new property launches and demand surges. Our partnership with Checkout.com helps us handle those peaks with fast, secure payments, giving more customers a smooth experience when they’re ready to invest. The improvement in payment acceptance demonstrates why this infrastructure matters. As we expand our collaboration, we’re strengthening our ability to bring real estate investment to more people while keeping the experience simple and reliable.”

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
1. **Who is the source?** This is a Checkout.com vendor press release syndicated via Zawya with an explicit "not independently verified" disclaimer. Every quantified claim is the vendor's own.
2. **Is the "20% acceptance uplift" audited?** No baseline, measurement window, or methodology is given. 20% uplift from what prior rate, and relative to which prior PSP? A low starting base inflates a percentage gain.
3. **Timeline inconsistency.** The release says live "since July" but the announcement is Oct 2026 — is it July 2025 or 2026? (Pre-Series A closed Sep 2025; General Catalyst backing suggests the 2025 go-live.) Why a 12–15 month lag before publicizing?
4. **Deal economics undisclosed.** No contract value, term length, volume commitment, pricing, or exclusivity. Is Checkout.com PRYPCO's sole acquirer or one of several?
5. **How material is PRYPCO to Checkout.com?** PRYPCO's transaction base (>AED20bn cumulative, 110k customers) is tiny next to Checkout.com's $300bn+ annual volume — this is a reference/marketing win more than a revenue needle-mover.
6. **Real-estate tokenization risk.** PRYPCO Mint's tokenized-property volumes are nascent (listings of a few hundred thousand dirhams). Does card-based acquiring even fit tokenized/blockchain settlement, or is Checkout.com only handling the fiat on-ramp?
7. **Regulatory exposure.** PRYPCO Mint operates under DLD/VARA/CBUAE; Checkout.com only got preliminary UAE SVF-licence approval in Jul 2026. Does it have full local acquiring authority for high-value flows, or rely on cross-border acquiring?
8. **Founder/governance clarity.** Web sources name Amira Sajwani as founder/CEO; the release quotes Essa Ibrahim as co-founder/president. Who actually controls PRYPCO, and is there any DAMAC/Sajwani-family linkage worth flagging (concentration/related-party risk)?
9. **Payout capability claims.** "Near-instant" withdrawals for a property-investment platform imply liquidity/custody arrangements beyond payments — is Checkout.com actually providing that, or is the release conflating payments with PRYPCO's own treasury?
10. **Competitive context.** UAE acquiring is crowded (Network International, Magnati, Checkout.com, Stripe, local banks). Why PRYPCO picked Checkout.com ("direct acquiring") — is this displacement of an incumbent or a greenfield integration?
11. **Fraud/chargeback exposure.** High-value real-estate transactions carry elevated chargeback and AML risk; does the network-token/false-decline narrative understate the fraud-loss and dispute side?
12. **Repeatability of the pitch.** "~20% acceptance uplift" echoes the "3% uplift" (Majid Al Futtaim) template — is the headline number a genuine outcome or standardized marketing framing?
13. **PRYPCO Mint extension — signed or aspirational?** The release says the partnership "has also extended" to Mint; is processing actually live for Mint, or an intent/roadmap item?

## What actually happened
Checkout.com announced it powers the payment infrastructure of **PRYPCO**, a Dubai-based all-digital real-estate ownership platform. Per the release, the integration (live since **July 2026**) lifted PRYPCO's payment **acceptance rate by 20%**, and the deal was **extended** to processing for **PRYPCO Mint**, PRYPCO's real-estate **tokenization** platform, plus **PRYPCO Blocks** (fractional investment). Stack = direct **acquiring + processing**, **network tokenization** (card-detail replacement to cut false declines), and **global payouts** for near-instant fund/withdraw. PRYPCO cites AED20bn+ transactions for 110,000+ customers. Source is a Zawya press release (vendor-originated, "as is"), so the 20% figure is Checkout.com's own, unaudited claim.

## Sector context — UAE/MENA PSP competition
- UAE payments market is sized ~$18.7bn (2026), projected ~$49bn by 2035 (~11% CAGR) per market-research trackers — a fast-growing, crowded acquiring space.
- The competitive set in-market: **Network International** (largest regional acquirer), **Stripe**, **Adyen**, **PayTabs**, **Tap Payments**, **Telr**, **Mamo**, plus BNPL/wallet players (Tabby, Tamara). Checkout.com's differentiator here is global-enterprise-grade direct acquiring + local MENA presence (Dubai office, MENA GM Remo Giovanni Abbondandolo, who fronts both this and the Nahdi/KSA deal).
- Real-estate **tokenization** is a genuine new rail, not just marketing: **PRYPCO Mint** launched May 2025 as the first platform to tokenize a title deed with a government entity (**Dubai Land Department**), under a regulatory sandbox with **VARA**, the **UAE Central Bank** and Dubai Future Foundation. Early traction was real (one offering fully funded in <2 min; AED9m/~$2.45m in month one; 10k+ waitlist). DLD projects tokenized real estate could reach ~7% (~$16bn) of Dubai property transactions by 2033 — the TAM Checkout.com is positioning against.

## Risk flags
1. **Vendor-sourced, unaudited metric.** The headline "+20% acceptance" is Checkout.com's own number in a Zawya press release ("as is", external content, no endorsement). No independent verification, no baseline disclosed, no absolute TPV for PRYPCO. Treat as marketing, not audited performance.
2. **Tokenization is early-stage / sandbox-dependent.** PRYPCO Mint operates in a VARA/CBUAE regulatory sandbox. Volumes (~$2.45m month one) are tiny versus Checkout.com's $300bn book — revenue contribution is immaterial today; the thesis is optionality on a nascent, regulator-gated market (DLD's $16bn-by-2033 is a projection, not a run-rate).
3. **Crowded, price-competitive UAE acquiring.** Network International, Stripe, Adyen, Tap, PayTabs, Telr all contest the same merchants; "more competitive processing rates" in the release implies margin pressure, and enterprise acquiring is a low-switching-cost, re-tenderable relationship.
4. **Concentration / counterparty risk on PRYPCO.** PRYPCO is a young, single-market (Dubai real estate) fintech whose volumes are tied to property-launch cycles and UAE real-estate sentiment — cyclical, peaky, and exposed to any cooling of the Dubai property boom.
5. **Checkout.com's own valuation reset.** Internal valuation cut to $12bn from a $40bn 2022 peak signals these regional logo wins are growth-optics in a still-recovering private-market story; one UAE deal does not move the company-level needle.
