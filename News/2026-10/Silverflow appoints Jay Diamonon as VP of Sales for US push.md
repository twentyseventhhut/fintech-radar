Silverflow expands US team as it targets further growth in North America
 Silverflow , the cloud-native payments processor, is stepping up its US expansion as it looks to grow its presence across North America and other international markets. As part of this push, the company has appointed Jay Diamonon as VP of Sales, strengthening its commercial leadership as it looks to build customer relationships and drive further growth in the region.
Jay brings two decades of experience across payments and financial services. He joins Silverflow as the company builds on the momentum of its $40 million Series B funding round, announced in March 2026, which is supporting its international expansion, product development and plans to grow its global workforce by more than 50%.
Before joining Silverflow, Jay held sales leadership roles at Adyen and BBVA. Earlier in his career, he led financial planning and analysis teams at Continental Airlines and Air Liquide. He holds a bachelor’s degree in economics from the University of Virginia.
Led by Picus Capital, the funding round also included Rabo Investments – Corporate Venturing and existing investors Inkef, GPT, Crane and Coatue. North America is a key focus for the investment, alongside further expansion in Asia-Pacific.
Silverflow aims to build on its existing presence in North America and expand its reach among acquiring banks, payment companies and commerce platforms. These plans form part of the company’s wider ambition to grow internationally as demand for modern payment processing infrastructure continues to develop. With $40 billion processed volume annually and 2 million transactions a day, Silverflow is well on the way to achieving their goals.
 Anne Willem de Vries, CEO and Co-founder of Silverflow, said: 
 The US payments market is highly competitive and businesses need to be able to adapt quickly as customer expectations and the way people pay continue to evolve. But that becomes difficult when you’re working with processing infrastructure that wasn’t built for the pace or complexity of modern payments. 
 There’s a real opportunity to give payment companies more flexibility, better access to their data and the ability to scale without adding unnecessary complexity. That’s where we see a strong fit for Silverflow. We’ve built momentum across our business, and now we want to bring that approach to more customers in North America. Bringing Jay on board is an important step in making that happen. 
 Jay Diamonon, VP of Sales at Silverflow, said: 
 What attracted me to Silverflow is the opportunity to help change how payment processing works for the businesses that rely on it every day. Payments companies need technology that can keep pace with their ambitions, rather than hold them back. I’m looking forward to working with customers across North America, understanding where they want to go and helping them get there. 
Silverflow’s expansion follows substantial growth in transaction volumes. In March 2026, the company reported processing almost 1.75 million transactions per day, up from around 180 per day two and a half years earlier. Its latest growth plans build on this progress, with investment in its team, technology and international operations.

# Context-enrichment: Silverflow appoints Jay Diamonon as VP of Sales for US push
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

**Honest framing up front:** this is a single mid-level commercial hire (one VP of Sales) at a ~120-person Series B startup. It is a real, verifiable appointment — not a duplicate — but it is a routine org-building step, not a strategic inflection. The news value is as a *marker* of Silverflow's North America intent, not as an event in itself.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- Silverflow (Amsterdam, cloud-native card **processor/acquirer-enabler**, founded 2019) hired **Jay Diamonon as VP of Sales** to drive North America commercial growth. Source: company blog "Silverflow expands US team" ([silverflow.com](https://www.silverflow.com/blog/silverflow-expands-us-team)), echoed by trade press ([finopotamus](https://www.finopotamus.com/post/silverflow-expands-us-team-as-it-targets-further-growth-in-north-america), [ibsintelligence](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi-news/silverflow-continues-growth-trajectory-with-us-expansion/)).
- De-PR'd: one person joins; no new product, customer, round, or revenue number is attached. The press-release anchors the hire to prior, already-reported facts — the **$40M Series B (closed 5 Mar 2026)**, the ">50% workforce growth" plan, and "$40B processed volume / 2M transactions a day."
- **Why framed this way (the red-team point):** the release bundles a routine hire with the company's strongest traction stats to make a staffing update read as momentum. The *only* genuinely new datum is the person and his pedigree (Adyen/BBVA). Note a small internal inconsistency to flag: this Oct release says "almost 1.75M transactions per day" was the *March 2026* figure and cites "$40B annually / 2M/day" now — consistent with the June note that Silverflow was "nearing one billion annualized transactions" ([[Silverflow nears one billion annual transactions amid US expansion]]). So the stats are recycled, not fresh.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- Silverflow sits in **cloud-native payment *processing* infrastructure** (single-API connection to Visa/Mastercard/Amex/Diners/Discover, adding UnionPay/JCB), selling to acquiring banks, PSPs and commerce platforms — i.e. it is a processor *behind* acquirers, not a merchant-facing acquirer.
- Direct/adjacent peers: **Adyen** (full-stack acquirer, where Diamonon ran US/Western-US sales), **Stripe**, **Checkout.com** (merchant-facing full stack); and the **legacy processor incumbents** Silverflow actually targets for replacement — TSYS/Global Payments, FIS/**Worldpay** (Worldpay absorbed into Global Payments Jan 2026, now ~$3.7T annual volume across 175+ countries, per [adyen/cbinsights/wise coverage](https://www.adyen.com/en_GB/knowledge-hub/stripe-alternatives)), Fiserv/First Data, Elavon.
- **Position:** niche challenger. On volume ($40B/yr) it is a rounding error next to the incumbents it wants to displace. Its wedge is "cloud-native vs legacy mainframe processing," not scale. **Second-order:** hiring an ex-Adyen US sales leader is a direct tell — Silverflow wants to sell the *processing layer* into the same US acquirer/PSP accounts Adyen's commercial org knows, i.e. buying a relationship network, not just a headcount.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Trajectory: founded 2019 → Deutsche Bank cloud-acquiring partnership/launch mid-2025 ([[Silverflow partners Deutsche Bank for European payments platform]], [[Silverflow launches cloud-native acquiring platform with Deutsche Bank]]) → Payabl. signed as customer Aug 2025 ([[payabl. adopts Silverflow's cloud-native processing platform]]) → **$40M Series B, Mar 2026**, led by Picus Capital ([[Silverflow raises $40 million Series B for cloud-native payments platform]], [[Silverflow plans global expansion after Series B funding]]) → "nearing one billion annual transactions amid US expansion," new US customer, +20% headcount, Jun 2026 ([[Silverflow nears one billion annual transactions amid US expansion]]) → this VP hire, Oct 2026.
- **Fit:** logical and on-script. The Series B explicitly earmarked North America (bolstering the New York office) and a >50% headcount jump (85→~120). A US sales-leadership hire is exactly the kind of "spend the round" step that plan implies. **Why the company acts this way:** processing is a relationship-and-trust enterprise sale (you ask banks/PSPs to route live card volume through you); you cannot grow US volume on inbound alone, so a named, locally-credible enterprise seller is the gating resource.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- Novelty of *this item*: low. No new capability; the only new asset is Diamonon's network. His CV is genuinely strong for the job — per [finopotamus](https://www.finopotamus.com/post/silverflow-expands-us-team-as-it-targets-further-growth-in-north-america) / [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jay-diamonon-42ab4b2/): ~20 yrs; SVP/Head of Commercial North America and SVP US Sales at **Adyen**; Director of digital-partnerships at **BBVA USA** (BaaS deals incl. Uber/Amazon); earlier Visa, First Data/Fiserv, GlobalCollect/Ingenico, plus FP&A at Continental Airlines / Air Liquide; econ degree, Univ. of Virginia.
- Traction to watch is Silverflow's *underlying* numbers, not the hire: $40B processed annually, 2M txns/day, growth from ~180/day (≈2.5 yrs earlier) to ~1.75M/day by Mar 2026 — a steep, real adoption curve for a 2019 startup. **Who captures the margin (one level deeper):** Silverflow is a thin processing layer; the acquirer/PSP customer owns the merchant relationship and the economics. Silverflow's value-add is "lower cost + faster time-to-market + richer data vs legacy mainframe processors." That is real *if* it holds, but it is a **commodity-adjacent, volume-based take-rate** business — so the US-sales push is about winning enough enterprise logos to reach scale before incumbents modernize or Adyen/Stripe push down into the processing layer.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Plan: grow NA presence among acquiring banks, payment companies and commerce platforms; this hire plus the Series B-funded NY office and APAC build-out. Expect follow-on signals to matter more than the hire: **named US customer wins and volume routed**, not more staffing PRs.
- Sentiment: neutral-positive trade coverage; no analyst price targets (private). **Structural driver:** demand for modern processing infra is real as banks tire of legacy "legacy drag." **Counterintuitive second-order risk:** a European challenger's biggest US threat is not legacy incumbents but the full-stack acquirers (Adyen/Stripe) who can bundle processing for free — so poaching Adyen's own US sales DNA is as much defensive mimicry as offense, and a single VP cannot move the needle unless matched by local support, integration/compliance muscle, and reference accounts.

**Sector & drivers.** Silverflow sits in cloud-native payment processing / acquiring infrastructure — the layer between merchants/acquiring banks and card networks (Visa, Mastercard, Amex). Market sizing: the broader payment processor market is ~$71.15bn in 2026, growing ~11.4% CAGR to ~$122bn by 2031 (per Mordor Intelligence, via thebusinessresearchcompany, as of 2026); the narrower payment-gateway segment is ~$33.3bn in 2026 (per Precedence Research, 2026). Treat these as adjacent proxies, not a clean Silverflow TAM — "no data" on the specific cloud-acquiring-rail TAM. Structure: in the US the acquiring/processing base is consolidated and legacy-dominated — Fiserv, J.P. Morgan and newly-merged Global Payments/Worldpay each process >$2tn US volume (per American Banker / acquiry, 2026); Global Payments closed its $24.25bn Worldpay purchase on 2026-01-09. "Why now": (1) the GP/Worldpay/FIS three-way restructuring is a textbook case of legacy integration being "expensive and slow" (FIS spent six years failing to integrate) — exactly the modernization pain cloud-native entrants target; (2) Silverflow's own $40m Series B (announced 2026-03-05, led by Picus Capital) earmarked North America as a key focus. Why-ladder: incumbents carry old monolithic stacks → multi-year integrations stall → merchants/acquirers want single-API cloud rails → window for a modern processor, but only where switching cost is tolerable.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: processed/total volume (TPV), take rate (bps), transactions/day. Silverflow discloses ~$40bn annual processed volume, ~2m transactions/day, nearing ~1bn transactions annually (up from ~180/day ~2.5 years earlier) — real scale but orders of magnitude below incumbents. Take rate: not disclosed → `[UNSOURCED]`. Players & basis of competition: incumbents (Fiserv, Global Payments/Worldpay, FIS — scale + bank relationships); tech-led scaled players Adyen and Stripe (each >$1tn throughput, product + single-platform distribution); card-issuing/processing peers Marqeta; cloud-acquiring challengers incl.
