Engine by Starling powers Tangerine Bank’s new business client offering
London, UK | 05 October 2026
Tangerine Bank, Canada’s original digital bank, launched an innovative proposition for business clients today, less than a year after signing an agreement to adopt Engine by Starling’s cloud-native banking platform.
The launch marks a significant expansion milestone for Engine, representing its third client deployment in another new market within 12 months. This follows the successful launches for Romania’s Salt Bank and AMP Bank GO in Australia.
Delivering Tangerine’s mobile-first proposition in under a year reinforces Engine’s track-record of consistently implementing in under 12 months, setting a new industry benchmark for speed-to-market.
Launching as ‘Tangerine Business’, the new offering is designed for modern entrepreneurs, bringing together banking solutions, connections, insights, and support they need to move their businesses forward at every stage of growth. Through a multi-phase rollout, Tangerine Business will offer a suite of products and capabilities, including the Tangerine Business Spending Account, Tangerine Business Savings Account, and Tangerine Business FlexiDraft™, which provides flexible funds to support business continuity and give entrepreneurs greater clarity as they navigate the demands of running a business. They will also benefit from innovative features like Spaces, which gives entrepreneurs the self-serve tools to effortlessly manage their money for saving, spending and proactive budgeting, using dedicated saving buckets.
Terri-Lee Weeks, President and CEO of Tangerine, said: “When entrepreneurs thrive, Canada thrives. Supporting them means creating intuitive banking experiences that help them move forward with confidence. With Engine by Starling’s cloud-native banking platform, we’ve built a modern technology foundation that gives us the flexibility to innovate faster, adapt to evolving client needs and continuously improve the experiences we deliver. The launch of Tangerine Business marks an important milestone in Tangerine’s transformation journey and our commitment to reimagining banking to help Canadians build momentum in their businesses and their lives.”
Sam Everington, CEO of Engine by Starling, added: “Engine’s technology and operating model is a tried and tested blueprint for building market-leading digitally-native banks. It is fantastic to see our software enabling ambitious, innovative and customer-centric banks all over the world. Launching Tangerine Business in less than 12 months marks our third client deployment in a third international market in under a year. It is testament to the collaboration between our teams, and Engine’s capabilities as a platform for innovation, in enabling banks to transform both customer propositions and operating efficiency.”
Tangerine is Engine by Starling’s first North American client after the British firm announced its North American global expansion, with offices opening in New York and Toronto last year, and most recently in Charlotte. Born out of the UK’s Starling Bank in 2022, the company currently supports Salt Bank in Romania, AMP Bank GO in Australia, and more recently, it announced a partnership with SBS Bank in New Zealand.
In November 2025, Tangerine, an award-winning subsidiary of Scotiabank (one of the ‘Big 5’ banks in Canada with assets of approximately C$1.5 trillion), entered into a 10-year agreement with Engine by Starling to upgrade its core digital banking system and deliver a next-generation banking platform for more than 2 million clients in Canada.
Members of the Tangerine Business Founders Circle – a group of business owners from a range of industries and business stages – will be among the first to experience this new offering, with a waitlist open for entrepreneurs interested in joining future rollout phases.

# Context-enrichment: Engine by Starling powers Tangerine Bank's business offering
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**Freshness verdict: FRESH.** This is the LIVE product LAUNCH of "Tangerine Business" (05 Oct 2026), the follow-through of the 10-year core-banking deal first reported in Nov 2025 ([[Tangerine signs with Engine by Starling]]). Classic "signed → live" progression, not a reprint: new event (first business-banking product on the new Engine core), new named products, new timeline fact (under-12-months implementation). The Nov 2025 note is the signing of the contract; this is the first customer-facing output of it — a genuinely new development.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- Tangerine Bank (Scotiabank's wholly-owned digital subsidiary, ~2M+ clients, Canada) launched **Tangerine Business**, its first-ever business-banking proposition, on **05 Oct 2026**, running on Engine by Starling's cloud-native SaaS core. ([[Tangerine signs with Engine by Starling]]; enginebystarling.com newsroom.)
- **Live vs announced — partial.** The launch is real but gated: it is a **multi-phase rollout** starting with a "Founders Circle" of selected business owners plus a **waitlist**. So this is a controlled/soft launch, not open general availability. The PR phrasing "launched an innovative proposition" overstates reach; read it as "limited first cohort live."
- Product suite (phase 1): Tangerine Business Spending Account, Business Savings Account, Business FlexiDraft (overdraft/flexible-funds line), and "Spaces" (sub-account saving buckets). No monthly account fee, interest on operating balances, optional overdraft (per web reporting). These are table-stakes SME features, not novel capabilities.
- **Core de-PR claim: "under 12 months" implementation.** The signing was announced 04 Nov 2025; the launch is 05 Oct 2026 — so ~11 months from signed deal to a limited first product. True but to be read carefully: (a) it is a first product for a *subset* of users, not full re-platforming of Tangerine's 2M+ retail base onto Engine; (b) migrating the existing retail book is the hard part and is NOT what shipped here. Engine's "track-record of implementing in under 12 months" is a genuine marketing differentiator but is measured against greenfield/limited launches, not full core migrations.
- **Why framed this way:** Engine's central sales narrative is *speed-to-market*; anchoring on "third new market in 12 months" (Salt Bank Romania, AMP Bank GO Australia, Tangerine Canada) and "under 12 months" is designed to sell the next prospect (notably the still-unsigned US client — [[Starling targets US expansion by selling Engine software]]). The news value for Engine is the *reference case*, not Tangerine's SME product per se.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Core-banking SaaS field:** Thought Machine (Vault Core; crossed USD 100M revenue in FY2025/Q2 2026, ~68 banks signed across 30+ countries incl. JPMorgan, Lloyds, Standard Chartered — [[Thought Machine tops $100m revenue for FY2025, up 57%]], [[Thought Machine secures GBP 30m funding from new investor]]), 10x Banking (Antony Jenkins; Westpac, Co-operative Bank — [[Philip Barnett named executive vice chair at 10x Banking]]), Mambu (260+ clients, composable; [[Krom Bank renews Mambu partnership in Indonesia]], [[Mambu selected as core banking provider by Nyla]], [[Wio Bank leverages Mambu for composable banking]]), plus incumbents Jack Henry, Finastra, Fiserv, FIS (named as Engine's US targets in [[Starling targets US expansion by selling Engine software]]), and Tuum / ION Group.
- **Engine's position: small but fast-executing challenger.** On client count Engine (~4 named: Salt Bank, AMP Bank GO, Tangerine, SBS Bank NZ — [[Starling signs 10-year Engine deal with New Zealand's SBS Bank]]) is dwarfed by Mambu (260+) and Thought Machine (68). Its differentiation is NOT scale or tier-1 logos but *proven-at-scale-on-itself* (it runs Starling's own ~5M-customer UK bank) plus speed. Thought Machine sells "configurability/smart contracts" to tier-1s; Engine sells "the whole operating blueprint, fast" to digital-first mid-tier banks.
- **Why the landscape is this way (analysis):** Core-banking replacement is multi-year, high-risk; buyers de-risk by picking either (a) the most-proven-at-scale (Thought Machine's tier-1 roster) or (b) a turnkey blueprint (Engine, which comes with Starling's own operating model). Engine deliberately targets the gap incumbents (FIS/Fiserv/Jack Henry, "90%+ of US market" per Everington) serve with ageing tech. The Tangerine reference matters because it's Engine's first North American AND first large-incumbent-subsidiary win — a credibility bridge toward the still-elusive US deal.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Engine spun out of Starling Bank in 2022 as a SaaS business after Starling abandoned direct EU consumer expansion (withdrew EU licence bid, 2022 — [[Starling targets US expansion by selling Engine software]]). Trajectory: Salt Bank (Romania) → AMP Bank GO (Australia) → Tangerine (Canada, signed Nov 2025, "largest deal to date") → SBS Bank (New Zealand, signed Feb 2026). North American offices: New York, Toronto, Charlotte; Delaware subsidiary; Jody Bhagat (ex-McKinsey) as US president; USD 50M NY office expansion.
- **Financial reality check:** Engine reported only ~USD 4.6M ARR and a USD 16.5M pre-tax LOSS in the 12 months to March 2025, against a stated target of ~USD 136M ARR ([[Starling targets US expansion by selling Engine software]]). So despite the marquee logos, Engine is sub-scale and loss-making today; each live reference case (like Tangerine Business) is strategically about de-risking the sales pipeline, especially the US.
- **Why Starling acts this way (analysis):** Starling's own UK consumer growth is maturing (profit slid as rates fell — [[Starling's profit slides as falling interest rates bite]]) and it is prepping for a possible IPO ([[Starling Bank restructures ahead of public listing]]). An "asset-light," globally scalable software multiple is more valuable to equity-story than a rate-sensitive UK deposit bank. Engine is the growth narrative; Tangerine going live is a proof point for that narrative.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **What's genuinely new:** (i) First *live product output* of the Tangerine-Engine deal (prior note was only the signing); (ii) Tangerine's *first entry into SME/business banking* at all; (iii) Engine's first North American deployment going live. The "third continent in 12 months" claim is a real, verifiable traction signal for Engine's delivery capability.
- **What is NOT new / overstated:** The SME product features (spending/savings accounts, overdraft, saving buckets) are commodity — Starling's own UK business banking, Tide, Revolut Business, and Canadian incumbents all have equivalents. "Spaces" = Starling's long-standing "Spaces" feature, re-badged. No disclosed customer numbers, pricing economics, or migration of the core 2M retail base. It's a limited cohort + waitlist, so *traction is still an announcement, not adoption*.
- **Where the margin sits (analysis):** Engine's value-add is real only if (a) implementations stay under-12-months at low cost AND (b) it converts references into tier-1 / US wins at USD-136M-ARR scale. The risk: Engine remains a boutique with prestige logos but sub-scale ARR, out-executed on breadth by Mambu and out-logo'd by Thought Machine. For Tangerine/Scotiabank the value is optionality — a modern core to compete in SME against neobanks — but the hard migration (retail book) and real adoption numbers are still ahead.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Next:** Multi-phase Tangerine Business rollout beyond the Founders Circle; broader GA; eventually migrating the 2M+ retail base onto Engine. For Engine: convert momentum into the long-sought US client (target: USD 5-50B-asset bank/credit union) — still unsigned as of the latest reporting.
- **Sentiment / structural drivers:** Favourable tailwind — incumbent cores (FIS/Fiserv/Jack Henry) are ageing and mid-tier banks face "technology obsolescence"; cloud-native challengers are winning greenfield/digital launches. But the segment is crowded and consolidating, and switching costs/risk keep tier-1 wins slow.
- **Counterintuitive second-order (analysis):** Each fast, flashy *limited-launch* reference may paradoxically raise the bar Engine must clear — prospects will increasingly ask about *full-core migration* and *adoption/economics*, not greenfield speed. If Engine can't show Tangerine's 2M+ book actually migrating and running on Engine (not just a new SME app bolted on), the "speed" narrative risks being read as "we only did the easy part fast."

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** SaaS/cloud-native core banking — the "replace the ledger" layer, distinct from the BaaS-account resale this note is tagged under. Market sized at ~$16–17bn in 2026 growing ~20–23% CAGR (per researchandmarkets / SNS Insider / The Business Research Company, via WebSearch, as of Oct 2026 — vendor-report ranges, treat as indicative, not audited). Structure: a barbell — legacy incumbents (Temenos, FIS, Fiserv, Jack Henry) holding installed base vs cloud-native challengers (Thought Machine, Mambu, 10x, Vodeno, and Engine). Barriers are high: a core migration is a multi-year, deeply-integrated replacement programme (the SBS/Tangerine deals are 10-year terms), so switching costs and reference-customer credibility are the real moat, not price. Why now: incumbents' mainframe cores are ageing; mid-tier banks and Big-5 subsidiaries want cloud elasticity and faster product velocity — Tangerine cites "innovate faster / adapt to evolving client needs" as the rationale (note text).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: committed ARR, net revenue retention, client count, and time-to-live (Engine leans hard on the last — "under 12 months" implementation as its stated differentiator). Key players and basis of competition: Thought Machine (Vault Core; ARR crossed $100m Q2 2026, 200+ banks, JPMorgan Chase marquee win — per tech.eu/fintechfutures, via WebSearch); Mambu (rev ~$128.6m, +17% YoY — WebSearch); Temenos (rev ~$1.1bn — the scale incumbent); 10x Banking (UK cloud-native, still private). Basis of competition is product + proven-at-scale credibility and distribution, not price. Recent moves: Engine's own cadence — Salt Bank (Romania), AMP Bank GO (Australia), Tangerine (Canada, signed Nov 2025 — its largest deal to date), SBS Bank (New Zealand, Feb 2026) [[Starling signs 10-year Engine deal with New Zealand's SBS Bank]]; Thought Machine £30m raise May 2026 + JPMorgan deal [[Thought Machine lands GBP 45M funding as losses widen]]. Protagonist's position: niche but accelerating challenger — ahead on speed-to-market messaging, well behind on scale. Moat (analysis): proven at Starling's own scale + a second subsidiary acting as live referenceability; real switching costs once a core is migrated.

**Comps & multiples.** Engine itself is not separately valued; the only hard figures are revenue/ARR from Starling's FY2026 results (year to Mar 2026): revenue £10.9m (+25% YoY), 4 clients, £70m committed ARR, and £19.7m of group investment poured in — i.e. deeply loss-making, funded by the parent (per tech.eu/Starling FY2026, via WebSearch & IR below). External comps:
- Temenos (public, SWX:TEMN): EV ~$6.1bn / rev ~$1.1bn = **~5.5x EV/Revenue**; P/E ~23x (gurufocus/stockanalysis/companiesmarketcap, via WebSearch, Sep–Oct 2026). The scale/profitable benchmark.
- Thought Machine (private): ARR >$100m (Q2 2026); last *confirmed* post-money $2.7bn (2022) — if still ~$2.1–2.7bn that implies a round-valuation/ARR of ~21–27x, a stale 2022 mark, NOT a market cap — treat as `[UNSOURCED]` for a current multiple.
- Mambu (private): rev ~$128.6m (+17%); no fresh post-money found → "no data" on multiple.
Engine on £70m committed ARR vs Thought Machine's $100m+ live ARR shows the gap: Engine is roughly one notch behind the leading challenger on booked recurring revenue, and an order of magnitude behind Temenos on realised revenue. Distribution not computed (<3 comparable *current* valuation marks) — qualitative comparison only. Flag: the growth (+25% rev, client base doubled) is real but off a tiny base; no public standalone valuation exists to call rich/cheap.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Revenue vs investment gap / parent dependence.** £10.9m revenue against £19.7m of group cash — Engine is a subsidised bet entirely reliant on Starling's balance sheet and IPO proceeds; if parent profits keep sliding ([[Starling's profit slides as falling interest rates bite]]) the funding runway for global expansion tightens.
2. **Committed-ARR ≠ live revenue (de-PR).** £70m is "committed" and deals are 10-year with multi-year migrations; recognised revenue lags for years and implementations can slip — the PR is silent on per-client economics, churn risk, and what "committed" contractually guarantees.
3. **Concentration + scale credibility.** Only 4 clients; one delayed go-live or a reference bank leaving would materially dent the speed-to-market narrative that is Engine's core sell. North American expansion (NY/Toronto/Charlotte offices) has one client (Tangerine) and no independent US bank win yet.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Tangerine *Business* going live <1 year after signing converts Engine from "promising subsidiary" to "repeatable deployment machine" — strengthening the thesis that Starling's SaaS arm is the real IPO re-rating lever, not UK retail banking. Falsifiable thesis: Engine reaches its stated >£100m ARR pathway and books a US client within 18 months. What breaks it: a stalled/delayed major migration or no net-new logos beyond the current four — which would expose the £19.7m/£10.9m spend-to-revenue gap as structural, not investment-phase.
