ANCHORAGE, Alaska, and MEDFORD, Ore., Oct. 22, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- Northrim BanCorp, Inc. (NASDAQ: NRIM) (“Northrim” or the “Company”), the holding company of Northrim Bank, and PBCO Financial Corporation (“PBCO”), the holding company of People’s Bank of Commerce (“People’s Bank”), an Oregon state-chartered bank, jointly announced today that they have received all required shareholder approvals related to Northrim’s proposed acquisition of PBCO. The final vote results on the proposal presented at the special meeting of Northrim’s shareholders on October 20, 2026, will be set forth in Northrim’s Current Report on Form 8-K to be filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission. PBCO’s shareholders approved the merger agreement with Northrim at a special meeting of PBCO’s shareholders held on October 20, 2026.
“We appreciate the strong support our shareholders have shown for this merger. This approval is an important step toward bringing together two organizations that share a commitment to community banking, exceptional customer service, and strong local relationships,” said Michael Huston, President and CEO of Northrim Bank.
“As we move toward completing the transaction, we are excited about the opportunities ahead and what we can accomplish together. As a combined organization, we expect to be able to provide a more robust product offering coupled with local decision-making, and plan to continue delivering excellent customer service,” added Julia Beattie, President and CEO of People’s Bank of Commerce.
Completion of the transaction remains subject to the receipt of regulatory approvals and the satisfaction or waiver of the remaining customary closing conditions set forth in the merger agreement. Northrim and PBCO anticipate the transaction to close on or about January 1, 2027, subject to satisfaction or waiver of such remaining customary closing conditions.
 About Northrim BanCorp, Inc. and Northrim Bank 
Northrim is the holding company of Northrim Bank, an Alaska-based community bank with 21 branches throughout the state of Alaska. Northrim Bank differentiates itself with its detailed knowledge of Alaska’s economy and its “Customer First Service” philosophy. Northrim Funding Services, a division of Northrim Bank, operates a factoring and asset-based lending division in the State of Washington. Sallyport Commercial Finance, LLC, a specialty finance company, and Residential Mortgage, LLC, a regional home mortgage company, are wholly-owned subsidiaries of the Bank.
 About PBCO Financial Corporation and People’s Bank of Commerce 
Founded in 1998, People’s Bank of Commerce is a full-service, commercial bank headquartered in Medford, Oregon with branches in Albany, Ashland, Central Point, Eugene, Grants Pass, Jacksonville, Klamath Falls, Lebanon, Medford, and Salem. People’s Bank acquired Steelhead Finance in 2017. An accounts receivable factoring company headquartered in Medford, Oregon, Steelhead supports the transportation industry throughout the United States by providing cash flow and backroom management services to carriers and freight brokers. PBCO Financial Corporation’s stock trades on the over-the-counter market under the symbol PBCO.

[0] **What happened.** On 2026-10-22 Northrim BanCorp, Inc. (NASDAQ: NRIM), the Anchorage, Alaska holding company of Northrim Bank, and PBCO Financial Corporation (OTC: PBCO), the Medford, Oregon holding company of People's Bank of Commerce, jointly announced that they had received **all required shareholder approvals** for Northrim's proposed all-stock acquisition of PBCO. Both companies held special meetings of shareholders on **2026-10-20**; PBCO shareholders approved the merger agreement and Northrim shareholders approved the share-issuance proposal. Final vote tallies are to be filed in a Northrim Form 8-K. ([source: globenewswire.com, 2026-10-22](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/22/3386821/0/en/northrim-bancorp-inc-and-pbco-financial-corporation-announce-shareholder-approvals-for-proposed-acquisition.html))

[1] **Deal terms & origin.** The merger was first announced via a definitive agreement signed **2026-07-22**, valued at approximately **$167.3 million** all-stock. PBCO shareholders receive **1.160 Northrim shares** per PBCO share, pricing the deal at roughly **1.73x tangible book value**. As of 2026-06-30 PBCO had ~$776.6M in consolidated assets, ~$570.1M in gross loans, ~$610.1M in deposits and ~$100.2M in stockholders' equity. ([Northrim definitive-agreement release, 2026-07-22](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/22/3331817/0/en/northrim-bancorp-inc-signs-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-pbco-financial-corporation.html))

[2] **Timeline / milestone position.** Announcement (2026-07-22) → shareholder votes (2026-10-20) → joint approval announcement (2026-10-22) → anticipated close **on or about 2027-01-01**, subject to regulatory approvals and remaining customary closing conditions. This note is the **shareholder-approval milestone** — a genuine step forward from the July announcement, not a restatement. No prior vault note covers the original announcement; this is the first Northrim/PBCO note on file.

[3] **Who the parties are.** Northrim Bank is an Alaska-based community bank with 21 branches statewide, plus subsidiaries Northrim Funding Services (WA factoring/ABL), Sallyport Commercial Finance and Residential Mortgage. In Q3 2026 Northrim earned $14.4M, or $0.63/diluted share. People's Bank of Commerce (founded 1998) is a full-service Oregon state-chartered commercial bank headquartered in Medford with ~11 branches across Southern Oregon and the Willamette Valley (Albany, Ashland, Central Point, Eugene, Grants Pass, Jacksonville, Klamath Falls, Lebanon, Medford, Salem); it owns transportation-sector factoring firm Steelhead Finance (acquired 2017). Leadership quoted: Michael Huston (Northrim Bank CEO) and Julia Beattie (People's Bank CEO).

[4] **Why it matters / strategic read.** This is Northrim's **first expansion outside Alaska**, adding a ~$0.8B-asset Pacific-Northwest franchise and ~11 Oregon branches to a bank historically confined to one state with a concentrated, resource-driven economy. The all-stock structure (1.73x TBV) dilutes Northrim shareholders but diversifies deposit and loan geography and stacks two factoring businesses (Northrim Funding Services + Steelhead). The deal is not done: regulatory approvals and closing conditions remain outstanding for a ~2027-01-01 target close. ## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Deal in one line.** Northrim BanCorp (NASDAQ: NRIM), the holding company of Alaska-based Northrim Bank (~$3.4B assets, 21 branches), is acquiring PBCO Financial Corp, parent of Oregon's People's Bank of Commerce, in an all-stock deal valued at ~$167.3M (~$32.36/PBCO share, 1.160 NRIM shares per PBCO share). Shareholder approvals (both sides) landed Oct 20, 2026; close is targeted ~Jan 1, 2027, still pending regulatory sign-off. This is Northrim's first out-of-state expansion, extending into Southern Oregon and the Willamette Valley. Pro forma scale: ~$4.2B assets, ~$3.0B loans, ~$3.5B deposits; PBCO holders end up with ~21.1% of the combined company.

**Sector context — the regional/community bank consolidation wave.** US bank M&A is clearly re-accelerating off its 2023 trough (~101-130 deals) toward ~179-188 deals in 2025, with ~83 deals in 1H26 and a widely expected H2-26/2027 pickup. The structural driver is scale economics: smaller banks are merging to spread rising compliance, core-platform modernization, and tech/AI spend over a larger revenue base, often catalyzed by aging leadership and activist shareholders. The $167.3M Northrim/PBCO deal is a textbook example of this cohort — sub-$5B combined, all-stock, in-market-adjacent geographic diversification rather than a transformational megadeal. Note the Alaska/Pacific-Northwest angle: Northrim's edge has historically been deep, concentrated knowledge of Alaska's commodity-sensitive economy; moving into Oregon is a deliberate attempt to dilute that single-state concentration risk, which KBRA flagged as a strategic positive (greater geographic diversification, enhanced scale, solid core funding).

**In-base comps (grep fallback; sem 402 down).** The vault shows this is part of a visible 2026 US regional/community-bank M&A stream: "Pinnacle Bancorp to acquire Southern Bancshares" (Texas SouthStar Bank, $1.35B assets), "Plains State Bank to acquire Texas's Austin Colony", "TowneBank to acquire blueharbor bank for $154M", and "Three Japanese regional banks begin $82bn merger talks" (same consolidation logic, different market). The TowneBank/blueharbor ($154M) and Pinnacle/Southern deals are the closest size/structure analogs — small, deposit-franchise-driven, in-footprint roll-ups. Collectively they corroborate that sub-$200M all-stock community-bank tie-ups are the modal 2026 deal, not the exception.

**Valuation read.** Per KBRA, the deal prices at ~1.73x PBCO tangible book value. For context, that is at the firmer end for a sub-$1B-asset community bank but not aberrant in a seller's-ish 2026 market where scarcity of clean, well-funded targets supports premiums. Economics lean on ~24% cost savings off PBCO's noninterest-expense base; NRIM models a $7.5M gross credit mark (1.26% of PBCO loans) and an $11.9M rate mark (2.0% of loans) accreting over ~3.5 years. The accretion of the rate mark will flatter reported NIM/EPS in the out-years — worth isolating from true core earnings when judging deal quality.

**Risk flags (skeptical).**
1. **Regulatory approval still outstanding.** Shareholder votes are done, but the deal is explicitly conditioned on bank regulatory approvals that are not yet in hand; the ~Jan 1, 2027 close is a target, not a certainty. Management's own forward-looking caveats list regulatory delay/denial as a top risk.
2. **First-ever out-of-state integration.** This is Northrim's first operation outside Alaska — a 1,500+ mile, cross-state (Oregon vs. Alaska) integration with no prior out-of-state execution track record. Management's risk disclosures explicitly flag integration difficulty, cost, and timeline slippage.
3. **Valuation / TBV dilution.** At ~1.73x TBV and an all-stock structure, NRIM issues ~21.1% new shares, diluting existing holders; forward-looking statements name dilution from the share issuance as a specific risk. Earn-back on tangible book should be scrutinized.
4. **Rate-mark-dependent accretion.** A meaningful chunk of post-deal "profitability" rides on the $11.9M interest-rate mark accreting over 3.5 years — a non-core, finite tailwind that can mask weaker underlying combined earnings.
5. **Geographic/credit concentration shift, not elimination.** Diversification into Oregon reduces single-state Alaska exposure but introduces a new, unfamiliar market (incl. People's niche in transportation-sector factoring via Steelhead Finance) where Northrim has no credit-underwriting history.
6. **Macro/rate sensitivity.** Both franchises are spread-dependent community banks; a sharp rate move or regional-economic downturn (Alaska commodity cycle, PNW) would pressure the deal's deposit-cost and credit-mark assumptions.
