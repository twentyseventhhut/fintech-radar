Euronet and Fireblocks Power the Next Era of Mainstream Payments with Stablecoin-Funded Card Issuing
Banks and fintechs will be able to launch white-label, stablecoin-funded card programs on Euronet's Ren and CoreCard issuing platforms, powered by embedded wallets from Fireblocks

NEW YORK, Oct. 19, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- Euronet (NASDAQ: EEFT), a global payments infrastructure company and leader in payments processing and cross-border transactions, and  Fireblocks , the digital asset infrastructure company, today announced an extension of their partnership to bring stablecoins into card issuing. Under the expanded collaboration, banks, fintechs and other financial institutions can seamlessly launch their own stablecoin-funded card programs on Euronet's Ren and CoreCard issuing platforms, with Fireblocks providing the embedded wallet infrastructure that holds and moves the underlying stablecoin balances, bridging the gap between traditional fiat networks and the digital asset ecosystem.
Stablecoins are becoming a mainstream way to hold and move money across borders, but spending them still usually means converting and moving funds between accounts first. Stablecoin-backed cards remove that step by connecting stablecoin balances directly to card networks. Consumers and businesses spend anywhere cards are accepted, and merchants continue to receive local currency.
Euronet brings global card issuing and processing capabilities through its Ren and CoreCard platforms, which serve leading financial institutions and fintechs worldwide. Fireblocks provides the wallet infrastructure underneath. Each cardholder receives an embedded wallet at onboarding, and fiat deposits convert to stablecoin before they are credited. End users will log in with credentials they already use and hold the balance their card spends from, with no seed phrase and no separate crypto app to manage. Fireblocks powers hundreds of payments companies and facilitates more than $300 billion in stablecoin volume each month on average. Together, Euronet and Fireblocks will provide a more integrated approach for financial institutions and fintechs to launch and scale stablecoin-powered card programs across markets, connecting the growing digital asset economy with the global infrastructure for everyday card payments.
Euronet began working with Fireblocks in October 2025 , starting with partner settlement and pre-funding across its Cross-Border Payments segment. Extending that partnership into issuing opens a fast, white-label path for banks, fintechs and other financial institutions to launch stablecoin-backed cards without having to build the underlying digital-asset infrastructure themselves. The timing reflects a broader shift already under way in the market: more than 160 stablecoin-linked card programs were live on the Visa network in Visa's fiscal second quarter of 2026, with payment volume on those programs up nearly 200% year over year.
Euronet plans to offer this as a white-label capability that banks and fintechs can embed directly into their own banking or wallet app, with Euronet and Fireblocks managing the underlying infrastructure in the background.
“Stablecoins have fundamentally changed how money can move around the world, but their true potential will be realized when they become as easy to use as the payment products consumers and businesses already rely on every day. With our stablecoin-backed card offering, we are bringing together the speed, flexibility and global reach of digital currencies with the familiarity and acceptance of traditional card payments. Our goal is simple: enable our customers to launch innovative card programs across markets while abstracting away the complexity of digital assets, payments infrastructure and local ecosystems,” said Vishal Pasari, Vice President of Products & Partnerships at Euronet. 
“A decade ago, stablecoins were just a trader's tool to exit Bitcoin. Today, combined with global cards, they are the ideal off-ramp for digital assets,” said Ran Goldi, Chief Payments and Network Officer at Fireblocks. “Euronet's Ren and CoreCard bring the issuing and processing expertise. As they advance their stablecoin strategy, Fireblocks provides the trusted infrastructure needed to deliver trust at scale.” Card issuing marks the first major milestone in this expanded collaboration, laying the foundation for a broader roadmap of borderless, digital-first payment solutions between the two industry leaders. Banks, fintechs and financial institutions interested in launching a stablecoin-backed card program on Ren or CoreCard can learn more by contacting Euronet at salesoperations@euronetworldwide.com .

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What changed (2026-10-19).** Euronet (NASDAQ: EEFT) and Fireblocks announced an *extension* of their existing partnership from cross-border settlement into **card issuing**. Banks, fintechs and other FIs can now launch white-label, **stablecoin-funded card programs** on Euronet's **Ren** and **CoreCard** issuing platforms, with **Fireblocks** supplying the embedded-wallet infrastructure that holds and moves the underlying stablecoin balances. Card issuing is framed as "the first major milestone" of the expanded collaboration.

**Mechanics.** Each cardholder gets an embedded wallet at onboarding; **fiat deposits convert to stablecoin before being credited**, and the card spends directly from that balance — no seed phrase, no separate crypto app. Merchants keep receiving local fiat. The pitch is a white-label capability banks/fintechs embed into their own banking or wallet app while Euronet + Fireblocks run the rails in the background.

**Prior tie-up (the baseline this extends).** Euronet began working with Fireblocks in **October 2025**, starting with **partner settlement and pre-funding in its Cross-Border Payments segment** — covered internally in [[Euronet selects Fireblocks for stablecoin cross-border payments]] and [[Euronet chooses Fireblocks for cross-border stablecoin payments]] (two near-duplicate reports of the same Oct-2025 deal). The new announcement moves the relationship from back-office settlement into customer-facing issuing.

**Why Ren + CoreCard.** Ren is Euronet's global issuing/processing platform; **CoreCard** is the modern credit-issuing platform Euronet agreed to acquire in a stock-for-stock merger in Aug 2025 ([[Euronet to acquire CoreCard in stock-for-stock merger]]). This release is an early monetization of that combined issuing stack. Euronet says it processed >20B transactions in 2025, operates in 200+ countries, 74 offices.

**Fireblocks scale claims (vendor-stated).** Fireblocks says it facilitates **>$300B in stablecoin volume/month** on average, secures **$16T** in digital-asset transactions across 200+ blockchains, and counts Worldpay, BNY, Galaxy and Revolut as clients. Related internal context on Fireblocks' payments push: [[Fireblocks launches stablecoin payments network]], [[Fireblocks launches Flow stablecoin acceptance product]], [[Fireblocks and Circle collaborate to speed stablecoin adoption]], [[Western Union launches USDPT stablecoin with Fireblocks]].

**Market backdrop.** Euronet cites Visa's data: **>160 stablecoin-linked card programs** live on Visa in its fiscal Q2 2026, with payment volume up **~200% YoY**; Visa separately reports stablecoin settlement running at a >$20B annualized rate and business/commercial cards at ~17% of stablecoin-linked card volume. Competitive field includes Mastercard (acquiring **BVNK**, reportedly up to ~$1.8B), Marqeta+BVNK, **Column**, **Rain** (Mastercard Principal Member), plus issuer-enablers covered internally (e.g. [[Binance and Mastercard launch stablecoin payment card in South Africa]]). Stablecoin-backed cards are becoming a crowded "crypto off-ramp" category.

**Quotes.** Vishal Pasari (VP Products & Partnerships, Euronet): goal is to let customers launch card programs "while abstracting away the complexity of digital assets." Ran Goldi (Chief Payments & Network Officer, Fireblocks): "combined with global cards, [stablecoins] are the ideal off-ramp for digital assets."

**Status note.** No named launch customers, no GA date, no economics/revenue-share disclosed — this is a capability announcement, not a live program count. Euronet Q3 2026 earnings land **Oct 27, 2026** (watch for any issuing/stablecoin commentary).

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is this a real new product or a re-announcement? It's an *extension* of the Oct-2025 settlement deal into issuing — directionally new, but the PR is capability/intent, not a shipped program with live cardholders.
2. Are there any **named launch customers**? None disclosed. "Banks and fintechs can launch" is future tense.
3. Is there a **GA date or timeline**? No. No availability window, no pilot markets named.
4. What are the **economics**? No revenue-share, pricing, fees, or interchange split between Euronet and Fireblocks disclosed.
5. Which **stablecoins / networks** are supported (USDC? USDT? which chains)? Not specified — "stablecoin" is generic in the release.
6. Who holds **custody / regulatory liability** for the embedded wallets and the fiat→stablecoin conversion? FX, MTL/EMI licensing, and who is issuer-of-record are unaddressed.
7. The "**fiat deposits convert to stablecoin before credited**" model adds a conversion leg — who bears **spread/FX risk and conversion cost**, and does it actually save vs. a plain fiat card?
8. Vendor-stated metrics (**$300B/mo stablecoin volume, $16T secured, >20B txns**) are self-reported and unaudited — treat as marketing.
9. The Visa "**160 programs / ~200% YoY**" stat is Visa's, cited to size the market, not evidence of Euronet traction. Zero Euronet/Fireblocks card programs are claimed live.
10. **Competitive moat?** Marqeta, Column, Rain, Lithic and Mastercard/BVNK all chase the same stablecoin-issuing enablement. What makes Ren/CoreCard+Fireblocks differentiated beyond Euronet's existing issuer base?
11. Is **CoreCard integration** actually done? The Aug-2025 acquisition is recent; "Ren and CoreCard" may overstate how unified the combined issuing stack is today.
12. **Regulatory risk**: stablecoin-card issuing faces GENIUS Act / MiCA / local-market rules; a white-label model pushes compliance onto the client banks — is that a selling point or a liability-shifting dodge?
13. **Demand question**: with merchants already receiving fiat, is the stablecoin leg solving a real consumer problem (cross-border/unbanked) or just repackaging crypto-to-fiat spending that crypto cards already do?
14. Does this materially move **EEFT financials**? Likely immaterial near-term; a strategic positioning move. Q3 earnings (Oct 27) unlikely to quantify it.
15. **Duplicate check**: distinct from the two Oct-2025 cross-border-settlement notes (those cover the prior phase), so fresh — but watch for same-day re-reports of this same PR across channels.

**Importance: 3/5** — Credible, well-known public issuer (Euronet) plus a leading digital-asset infra vendor (Fireblocks) moving stablecoin from settlement into mainstream card issuing, riding a fast-growing (~200% YoY) Visa-validated trend and leveraging the CoreCard acquisition. Capped below 4 because it's a capability/intent announcement with no named customers, no GA date, no economics, and a crowded competitive field — strategically interesting, not yet a market-moving event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Stablecoin-funded card issuing sits at the intersection of card networks and the digital-asset stack: a stablecoin balance is linked directly to a card so the holder spends anywhere cards are accepted while the merchant still receives local fiat. Momentum is real, not just announced: more than 160 stablecoin-linked card programs were live on the Visa network in Visa's fiscal Q2 2026, with payment volume on those programs up nearly 200% YoY, and Visa's stablecoin settlement volume crossed a ~$20bn annualized run-rate, up >15x YoY (per Visa, via crypto.news / KuCoin, as of fiscal Q2 2026). Structure: the value chain is layered — networks (Visa/Mastercard) on top, issuer-processors (Euronet Ren/CoreCard, Marqeta, Galileo) in the middle, and digital-asset infra (Fireblocks, Bridge, Zerohash, BVNK) underneath for custody/conversion. Barriers are regulatory (licensing, stablecoin rules) and integration depth rather than pure capital. "Why now": stablecoins have reached mainstream cross-border use, but spending still requires a convert-and-move step; connecting balances directly to card rails removes that friction, and a bank/fintech can now buy the whole white-label stack instead of building digital-asset infra itself.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for the issuer-processor, cards/accounts on platform, transactions processed and processing take-rate; for the digital-asset layer, stablecoin volume moved and institutional client count. Fireblocks cites >$300bn/month average stablecoin volume and $16tn lifetime digital-asset transactions across 200+ blockchains, with clients including Worldpay, BNY, Galaxy and Revolut; Euronet processed >20bn transactions in 2025 across 200+ countries. Key players / basis of competition: on the infra layer Fireblocks competes with Stripe-owned Bridge (which extended stablecoin card capability to 100+ countries), Zerohash (being acquired by Mastercard for ~$2bn — see below) and BVNK (Coinbase/Mastercard in talks to buy); competition is on blockchain coverage, security (MPC custody) and embedded-wallet UX. On the issuing layer Euronet's Ren + CoreCard compete with Marqeta and Galileo on platform breadth and global reach. Protagonist position: Euronet is a scaled incumbent processor extending an existing Oct-2025 Fireblocks relationship (which started with partner settlement/pre-funding in Cross-Border Payments) into issuing — catching-up-to-leading in crypto-card infra but with a genuine moat in global issuing scale, licensing and bank distribution `(analysis)`. The pairing is "announced as a capability," not yet disclosed as a live program with named bank customers — de-PR flag.

**Comps & multiples.**
- Protagonist — **Euronet (NASDAQ: EEFT)**: FY2025 revenue $4,244.2m, net income $309.5m, diluted EPS $6.84 (per Euronet FY2025 release). Market cap ~$2.4bn at 14 Oct 2026 close (via WebSearch). P/E = $2.4bn / $0.31bn ≈ 7.8x; P/S = $2.4bn / $4.24bn ≈ 0.57x. Cheap / low-multiple vs most payments names — reflects slow-growing legacy ATM/epay mix and cross-border remittance-tax headwinds, with the stablecoin/digital-accelerator layer (Q2 2026 digital-accelerator revenue +31% YTD, 26% of revenue) the growth option not yet re-rated.
- Infra partner — **Fireblocks** (private): last disclosed round $550m Series E at $8bn post-money, Jan 2022 (via CoinDesk/PR). Revenue not public → EV/Rev = no data; the $8bn mark is ~4yrs stale and pre-dates the current stablecoin cycle, so treat as a floor/round reference, not current value `[UNSOURCED]` for a 2026 mark.
- External comps: **Zerohash** — Mastercard to acquire for ~$2bn (see [[Mastercard to acquire crypto firm Zerohash for ~$2 billion]]); **BVNK** — Coinbase/Mastercard in buy talks (see [[Coinbase and Mastercard in talks to buy stablecoin firm BVNK]]). Both signal networks paying up to internalize the digital-asset layer Fireblocks occupies.
- Internal comps: [[Euronet chooses Fireblocks for cross-border stablecoin payments]] (the Oct-2025 origin deal this extends), [[Western Union launches USDPT stablecoin with Fireblocks]], [[Tether launches gold-backed Visa card with Fasset]], [[Binance and Pomelo partner on crypto cards in Brazil]], [[Coinbase expands Coinbase One Card to all US users]]. Distribution not computed (private/mixed figures) — qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
- **Disintermediation of the infra layer:** networks are buying their own digital-asset stacks (Mastercard→Zerohash ~$2bn; Coinbase/Mastercard→BVNK). If Visa/Mastercard push native stablecoin settlement, Fireblocks' middle-layer rent — and Euronet's reliance on it — could be squeezed.
- **Announced-vs-live gap / concentration:** no named bank/fintech customer or volume disclosed yet; a "capability" without a signed program is easy to announce and slow to monetize, and Euronet's crypto upside is a thin slice of a $4.2bn-revenue base.
- **Regulatory:** stablecoin-funded cards cross money-transmission, stablecoin-reserve and card-scheme rules across 200+ markets; a tightening (e.g. EU multi-issuance restrictions — see [[EU watchdog pushes multi-issuance stablecoin ban on crash fears]]) could gate the white-label rollout market-by-market.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is an incremental option-add, not a re-rating: Euronet is monetizing its CoreCard/Ren footprint as a white-label on-ramp for the stablecoin-card wave rather than defending against it, turning a crypto headwind into a processing-volume tailwind. Falsifiable thesis: if Euronet names ≥1 bank/fintech launching a live stablecoin-funded program on Ren/CoreCard within ~2 quarters (watch the 27 Oct 2026 Q3 call) and ties it to digital-accelerator revenue, the depressed ~0.6x P/S is too cheap for the embedded optionality; absent a named live program by FY2026, treat it as PR and the thesis breaks.
