Fuze Finance Partners With Modern Treasury's Payments Infrastructure for U.S. Expansion
The partnership gives Fuze's customers across 100+ countries Global USD Accounts, access to U.S. payment rails, instant settlement, and stablecoin orchestration through a single integration.
 Las Vegas, NV — Money20/20 USA — [October 19, 2026] — Modern Treasury today announced that Fuze Finance, a regulated digital asset payment and wealth infrastructure provider, has selected the company’s Payment Service Provider (PSP) to accelerate Fuze’s U.S. expansion.
Fuze operates a regulated infrastructure platform that lets banks, neobanks, marketplaces, payment service providers, and payroll platforms build stablecoin-powered payment products without managing the underlying compliance and engineering complexity themselves.
Through its stablecoin orchestration, cross-border payments, and crypto payment gateway offerings, customers can move money across countries on stablecoin and fiat rails, while Fuze's embedded trading and yield products give those same customers a way to offer digital asset trading and yield generation directly inside their own apps. Fuze currently processes $10 billion in annual volume.
Fuze was founded with a primary focus on the MENA region, of which it is now the champion, and has since expanded its footprint globally. With this partnership, Fuze is now deepening its connectivity in the U.S. with broader access to U.S. payments infrastructure as a foundation for its next phase of growth.
The Modern Treasury integration allows Fuze to:
Give individuals and businesses in 100+ countries access to named U.S. accounts with unique routing and account numbers
Move funds between USDG, USDC, USDT, and dollars for users globally and in all 50 U.S. states
Offer wholesale stablecoin liquidity services
Orchestrate payins and payouts via ACH debits, ACH credits, checks, and wires
Settle payments instantly using RTP, FedNow, and push-to-card
Scale into the U.S. market on infrastructure already trusted to move over $600 billion
For Fuze, whose customers span financial institutions, fintechs and marketplaces, remittance platforms and exchange houses, businesses, and individuals, transacting across some of the most complex corridors in the world, consolidating U.S. rails, accounts, and stablecoin orchestration under one integration is a key step in scaling into the U.S. market.
"Our customers need dollar access that works, no matter where they are in the world," said Mo Ali Yusuf, Co-Founder and CEO of Fuze Finance. "Modern Treasury's software has moved over $600 billion across a myriad of rails, and that battle-tested infrastructure gives us the confidence to accelerate our growth in the United States."
Rather than spending months waiting on a bank sponsor, stitching together vendors, and building the infrastructure themselves, Fuze can now go live on U.S. rails through a single API in a matter of days.
"Fuze is solving a real problem for people and businesses who need reliable, instant access to dollars, wherever they are," said Matt Marcus, Co-Founder and CEO of Modern Treasury. "We're proud to support Fuze's growth in the U.S. market by helping them save months on their roadmap and put that time back into building for their customers around the globe."
Explore Modern Treasury's stablecoin payment capabilities at moderntreasury.com/products/stablecoins.

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-10-19, at Money20/20 USA (Las Vegas), Modern Treasury announced that Fuze Finance — a regulated digital-asset payment and wealth infrastructure provider — selected Modern Treasury's Payment Service Provider (PSP) to accelerate Fuze's U.S. expansion. Through one integration Fuze gets: named U.S. accounts (unique routing/account numbers) for individuals and businesses in 100+ countries; movement between USDG, USDC, USDT and USD across all 50 states; wholesale stablecoin liquidity; pay-in/pay-out via ACH debits/credits, checks and wires; and instant settlement over RTP, FedNow and push-to-card. Framing: go live on U.S. rails "in days, not months," avoiding a bank-sponsor search and multi-vendor stitching.

**[1] Who the parties are.** Fuze (fuze.finance) is a UAE-headquartered, regulated digital-asset infra provider founded 2023 (Mo Ali Yusuf CEO/co-founder, plus Arpit Mehta, Srijan Shetty). It sells stablecoin orchestration, cross-border payments, a crypto payment gateway, and embedded trading/yield to banks, neobanks, marketplaces, PSPs and payroll platforms — originally MENA-focused (now the regional "champion"), active in 65+ countries, ~$10B annual volume, backed by Galaxy Digital and e& capital (raised a $12.2M Series A led by Galaxy + e& capital with Further Ventures; >$26M total). Modern Treasury (moderntreasury.com) is a U.S. money-movement infra firm (Benchmark/Altimeter/Salesforce Ventures-backed) that has powered $600B+ in payments; its PSP for fiat+stablecoins launched 2026-03, built on its 2025-10 Beam stablecoin acquisition.

**[2] Why it matters / deal scope.** Not an equity/M&A deal — it is a commercial infrastructure selection: Fuze is a PSP customer. For Fuze it collapses U.S. market entry (accounts + rails + stablecoin orchestration) into one API, removing the slowest part of U.S. expansion (sponsor-bank onboarding). For Modern Treasury it is a marquee non-U.S., crypto-native logo for its new PSP, validating the fiat+stablecoin "single API" pitch for inbound-to-U.S. flows. Strategically it stacks U.S. dollar access on top of Fuze's MENA/cross-border base, aimed at remittance platforms, exchange houses, FIs and marketplaces transacting complex corridors.

**[3] Dated timeline.** 2023 — Fuze founded (MENA). 2024 — Fuze $12.2M Series A (Galaxy + e& capital). 2025-09 — Dubai's VARA fined Fuze's entity (Morpheus Software Technology FZE) over AML-program gaps [[Dubai's VARA penalizes Fuze over anti-money laundering gaps]]. 2025-10 — Modern Treasury acquires stablecoin startup Beam [[Modern Treasury acquires stablecoin startup Beam]]. 2026-03 — Modern Treasury launches its fiat+stablecoin PSP ("Payments") [[Modern Treasury launches PSP for fiat and stablecoins]]. 2026-10-05 — Modern Treasury files OCC application for a national trust bank (stablecoin+fiat custody) [[Modern Treasury applies to establish national trust bank]]. 2026-10-19 — Fuze–Modern Treasury PSP partnership announced at Money20/20.

**[4] Live vs announced.** ANNOUNCED, not yet demonstrably live at volume: both quotes describe future capability ("gives us the confidence to accelerate," "can now go live … in a matter of days"), i.e., Fuze has selected the PSP but the U.S. flows are being stood up, not reported as processing material volume. The only hard volume figures are each firm's existing scale ($10B/yr Fuze; $600B+ cumulative Modern Treasury), not U.S. stablecoin throughput from this deal.

**Competitors (orchestration / Global-USD-accounts layer).** For Fuze's stablecoin-orchestration peers: BVNK (acquired by Mastercard for up to $1.8B, Aug 2026), Bridge (Stripe, conditional OCC approval Feb 2026), Zerohash (OCC trust charter refiled Aug 2026), Circle, Brale, Crossmint. For the "U.S. accounts + rails under one API" role Modern Treasury fills here, Fuze could alternatively have used BaaS/embedded-banking or direct sponsor banks — the pitch is that Modern Treasury's PSP removes that work.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
1. Is any U.S. flow actually live? Both CEO quotes are forward-looking ("can now go live … in days"); the PR announces selection, not production volume — treat U.S. throughput as 0 until confirmed.
2. The only concrete volumes ($10B/yr Fuze, $600B+ cumulative Modern Treasury) predate this deal and say nothing about U.S. stablecoin volume generated by the integration.
3. AML overhang: Fuze's entity was fined by Dubai's VARA (Sept 2025) for AML-program gaps — does onboarding a recently-sanctioned crypto firm onto U.S. rails raise compliance scrutiny Modern Treasury (and its OCC trust-bank ambitions) must absorb?
4. This is a vendor-selection PR, not an exclusive or equity deal — how sticky is it? Fuze could multi-home across PSPs; "selected" ≠ sole provider.
5. Named U.S. accounts "in 100+ countries" vs Fuze's own "65+ countries" footprint (from its About blurb) — the corridor numbers are inconsistent; which is real reach vs marketing?
6. Who holds the regulatory/compliance liability for a non-U.S. firm issuing named U.S. accounts to 100+ countries — Modern Treasury, a partner bank, or Fuze? The PR is silent on the sponsor-bank layer still underneath the PSP.
7. "Go live in days" is Modern Treasury's standard marketing line (identical to its March PSP launch copy) — is the speed claim deal-specific or boilerplate?
8. Stablecoin breadth (USDG, USDC, USDT) — USDG and USDT carry different U.S. regulatory/GENIUS-Act posture than USDC; are all three actually orchestratable for U.S.-domiciled users day one?
9. Crowded layer: BVNK (Mastercard), Bridge (Stripe), Zerohash, Circle all offer overlapping orchestration/accounts — what is Fuze's durable edge beyond MENA origination?
10. Modern Treasury's push-to-card/RTP/FedNow "instant settlement" depends on counterparties' reachability — "instant" is rail-conditional, not guaranteed for every payout.
11. Backers Galaxy Digital + e& — does Galaxy's U.S. presence quietly de-risk Fuze's U.S. entry independent of Modern Treasury, making the PSP more plumbing than strategy?
12. No pricing, no volume commitment, no timeline for U.S. GA disclosed — standard for a Money20/20 announcement, but limits any read-through to revenue for either party.
13. Modern Treasury's own OCC trust-bank application (filed 2026-10-05) is pending — if custody of stablecoins is central to serving firms like Fuze, is the full stack contingent on an unapproved charter?

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits at the stablecoin + cross-border payment-infrastructure layer: Fuze (digital-asset / stablecoin orchestration, $10bn annual volume) plugging into Modern Treasury's multi-rail PSP (fiat + stablecoin). Sizing: the stablecoin-infrastructure & cross-border-payment-platform market is put at ~$9.19bn in 2026 (up from ~$7.43bn in 2025), heading to ~$21.3bn by 2030 at ~23.4% CAGR (per The Business Research Company, via GII/EINPresswire, as of 2026). Cross-border B2B stablecoin flow is far larger and faster: ~$13.4bn in 2026 projected to $5tn by 2035 (per Juniper Research, 2026); stablecoins settled ~$7.2tn in Feb 2026, surpassing US ACH for the first time (per Reap, 2026) — note this is total transfer value, not payment volume. Structure: fragmenting into a thin "orchestration/rails" layer (Bridge, BVNK, Modern Treasury, Fuze) sitting between issuers (Circle/Paxos/Global Dollar) and front-end fintechs; barriers are regulatory (licensing, bank-sponsor access) and engineering (compliance, ledgering), not capital. Why now: the orchestration layer is consolidating fast — Stripe bought Bridge for $1.1bn (closed Feb 2025) and Mastercard bought BVNK for up to $1.8bn (closed Aug 2026) (per Cryptobriefing / TokenPost, 2026) — making a single-API, bank-sponsor-free entry into US rails the competitive table-stakes Fuze is now buying.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: annual stablecoin/payment volume (TPV), rail coverage (ACH/RTP/FedNow/wires + stablecoin pairs), corridor/country count, transaction count. Players & basis of competition: Bridge (Stripe-owned, orchestration layer), BVNK (Mastercard-owned, ~$30bn annualized volume across ~2.8m txns, up ~2.3x YoY — per StablecoinInsider, 2026), Modern Treasury (the rails/PSP supplier here, >$600bn lifetime payment volume, ~$183m raised — per Pitchbook/Forbes, 2026). Competition is on breadth of rails + speed-to-live + compliance, not price. Recent moves: Modern Treasury launched its integrated fiat+stablecoin PSP (Feb/Mar 2026 — [[Modern Treasury launches PSP for fiat and stablecoins]]), acquired Beam for instant/stablecoin payments ([[Modern Treasury acquires stablecoin startup Beam]], Oct 2025), and signed Depa Finance ([[Depa Finance selects Modern Treasury for stablecoin payments]], Jul 2026). Fuze's position: a MENA-origin niche champion catching up in the US — it is the *customer/distribution* side of this deal, not a rails competitor. Moat `(analysis)`: Fuze's edge is regulatory footprint across complex MENA/Turkey corridors + Galaxy/e& backing; the moat is switching-cost / distribution, not infrastructure (which it is renting from Modern Treasury).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Dependence on someone else's rails / disintermediation.** Fuze is renting US infrastructure from Modern Treasury (and Modern Treasury rents bank-sponsorship / ledgering below it). The orchestration layer Fuze occupies is exactly what Stripe and Mastercard just acquired away from independence — Fuze risks being squeezed between issuers above and a consolidating rails layer below.
2. **Compliance/regulatory concentration.** Fuze was penalized by Dubai's VARA over AML gaps in 2025 ([[Dubai's VARA penalizes Fuze over anti-money laundering gaps]]); scaling into US dollar rails + 50-state stablecoin movement multiplies compliance exposure precisely where its track record is thinnest.
3. **De-PR / announced ≠ live.** The PR is "selected" and "can now go live in days"; no US volume, revenue split, or fraud-liability allocation disclosed. Economics of the integration (take rate, who bears FX/settlement risk) are silent.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a distribution win for Modern Treasury (another orchestration customer migrating onto its PSP, validating the Feb/Mar 2026 fiat+stablecoin launch) more than a re-rating for Fuze. Watch for: (a) Modern Treasury disclosing cumulative volume past $600bn and a new round at a >$2.2bn mark as PSP customers compound; (b) whether Fuze reports actual US volume within ~2 quarters — if US flow doesn't materialize, the thesis that regulatory-footprint moats travel from MENA to the US is wrong.
