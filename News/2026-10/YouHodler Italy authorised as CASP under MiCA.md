YouHodler Italy Authorised by CONSOB as a Crypto-Asset Service Provider Under MiCA
13.10.2026 10:00:00 EEST  | Business Wire   | Press release

YouHodler Italy S.r.l., announces that CONSOB, after hearing the Bank of Italy, has authorised it as a crypto-asset service provider (CASP) under Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 (MiCA) and Legislative Decree 129/2024, by resolution no. 24156 of 8 October 2026.



The authorisation covers five services: custody and administration of crypto-assets on behalf of clients; exchange of crypto-assets for funds; exchange of crypto-assets for other crypto-assets; execution of orders on behalf of clients; and transfer services for crypto-assets on behalf of clients.



With the authorisation, YouHodler Italy serves clients in Italy as a supervised firm under MiCA's client protections: segregation of client crypto-assets, governance and security requirements, pre-contractual information and complaints handling. The company plans to extend its services to all 27 EU Member States through the procedure set out in the Regulation.



"The CONSOB authorisation is the result of long and rigorous work with the authorities, and we thank CONSOB and the Bank of Italy for the care with which they reviewed our project. MiCA has changed the Italian market: at the end of the transitional period, in June, nine firms were authorised to provide crypto-asset services, against some 140 operators that had been on the OAM register, while about 1.7 million Italians hold crypto-assets. Investors are looking for a supervised provider, and banks and institutions for an authorised partner through which to offer these services to their clients. From today, anyone who chooses YouHodler Italy will find a supervised provider, with the rules and protections MiCA sets for the custody and handling of crypto-assets," said Lorenzo Barbantini Scanni, Chief Business Officer of YouHodler Italy.



"We launched YouHodler in Switzerland to make crypto-assets accessible and useful, and the brand has since grown across Europe. A MiCA authorisation in Italy is an important step: it gives anyone dealing in crypto-assets in Italy, and in time across the rest of the Union, a supervised provider within the European framework," said Ilya Volkov, co-founder of YouHodler.



YouHodler Italy was advised throughout the authorisation process by the law firm Lener & Partners, with a team led by Salvatore Luciano Furnari.


 About YouHodler Italy 

YouHodler Italy S.r.l., based in Rome, is a crypto-asset service provider authorised by CONSOB under Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 for custody and administration of crypto-assets, exchange of crypto-assets for funds and for other crypto-assets, execution of orders and transfer services on behalf of clients.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** CONSOB — after hearing the Bank of Italy — authorised YouHodler Italy S.r.l. (Rome) as a crypto-asset service provider (CASP) under MiCA (Reg. (EU) 2023/1114) and Italian Legislative Decree 129/2024, by resolution no. 24156 of **8 October 2026** (press release dated 13 Oct 2026 via Business Wire). The licence covers **five services**: custody & administration of crypto on behalf of clients; exchange of crypto for funds; exchange of crypto for other crypto; execution of orders on behalf of clients; and transfer services. YouHodler Italy says it intends to **passport across all 27 EU member states** via MiCA's cross-border notification procedure. [Source: sttinfo.fi / Business Wire]

**[1] Who / company.** YouHodler was founded by **Ilya Volkov (co-founder/CEO)** in 2017, platform launched 2018, HQ in **Lausanne, Switzerland** (YouHodler SA — a financial intermediary via SRO Polyreg affiliation + a pawnbroker licence). Core business historically: **crypto-backed loans + crypto conversion** ("bridge between crypto and traditional finance"). The Italian entity is YouHodler Italy S.r.l., based in Rome; the authorisation was advised by law firm Lener & Partners (team led by Salvatore Luciano Furnari). Prior vault context: [[YouHodler extends partnership with Torino FC]] (Sep 2025) — brand/marketing push in Italy, i.e. YouHodler was already building Italian market presence before the licence. [Source: startupticker.ch, innovaud.ch, beincrypto.com]

**[2] Context — the Italian MiCA landscape.** MiCA's transitional period ended **30 June 2026**. A CONSOB/Bank of Italy joint press release (30 Jun 2026) listed **9 entities authorised in Italy** at that cutoff — 8 CASPs (CheckSig, Conio, CryptoSmart, Hercle, Hodlie, Olliv Italia, Riv Digital, Young Platform) plus 1 banking intermediary (Banca Sella). YouHodler Italy is therefore a **NEW, post-transition authorisation** (not one of the original 9). The company's own CBO (Lorenzo Barbantini Scanni) framed the contrast: ~140 operators were on the legacy OAM register, ~1.7M Italians hold crypto, yet only ~9 firms cleared MiCA at the deadline — a steep consolidation. [Source: consob.it 30 Jun 2026 release]

**[3] Context — EU-wide.** As of early Oct 2026, roughly ~386 CASPs are authorised across the EEA (Germany leading; Malta/Cyprus/Luxembourg/Ireland favoured by large platforms); major names include Kraken, Coinbase, OKX, Crypto.com, Bitstamp, Bitpanda, MoonPay. Related vault regulation notes for comparison: [[Crypto.com files for OCC National Trust Bank charter]] (US trust-charter route) and [[Bybit secures full UAE virtual asset license]] (non-EU licensing) — both illustrate the global "get-licensed" wave, though neither is MiCA. [Source: penning.io/ccn.com/paybis MiCA registers]

**[4] Why it matters / so-what.** For a mid-size Swiss crypto-lender, an Italian CASP licence is the cheaper entry door into the EU single market: authorise in one member state, then passport to 26 others. It upgrades YouHodler from a Swiss SRO/pawnbroker-licensed intermediary to a **MiCA-supervised EU provider** (client-asset segregation, governance, complaints handling). Caveats: the press release lists **no financial figures** (no AUM, no client numbers, no revenue), the passporting is a *stated plan* not an achieved status, and Italy adds domestic overlays (Legislative Decree 129/2024) beyond MiCA. Modest, incremental regulatory milestone rather than a market-moving event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Crypto-platform regulation in the EU is now a licensing market defined by MiCA (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114), whose transitional period ended 1 July 2026 — after that date any firm serving EU clients needs a full CASP authorisation, a passport, or must exit (per ESMA/MiCA framework, via AMLBot, Oct 2026). Size/structure of the licence pool: ~370–386 CASPs authorised across the EEA as of 7 Oct 2026, concentrated in a few home states — Germany 96, France 36, Netherlands 28, Cyprus 24, Malta 23 (per outrun.at MiCA tracker / ESMA interim register, as of late Sep–7 Oct 2026). Italy is a late, gated market: only **9** firms authorised by CONSOB/Bank of Italy at YouHodler's 8 Oct 2026 grant (8 prior + YouHodler), down from ~140 operators on the pre-MiCA OAM VASP register (per CONSOB/Bank of Italy joint release, 1 Jul 2026; bebeez.eu). Demand backdrop: ~1.7 million Italians hold crypto-assets (figure cited by YouHodler exec, echoing CONSOB context). **Why now:** the OAM→MiCA cliff-edge collapsed a light-touch registry into a supervised regime, so every incumbent either re-licenses or dies — regulation, not a tech shift, is the driver. Barriers are now capital + governance + custody-segregation requirements, i.e. a regulatory moat that structurally favours funded incumbents over the OAM long tail. Second-order effect: the Italian market is being re-intermediated toward a handful of supervised names, and bank/institutional distribution (explicitly flagged by YouHodler) opens only to the authorised set.

**Competitive landscape.** This is a licensing/regulatory race, not a comp-multiple market — the sector KPIs that would matter (AUM/custody balances, trading volume, active users, take rate) are **not disclosed** by YouHodler or most Italian CASPs → `[UNSOURCED]`. Basis of competition = scope of authorised services + passport reach + distribution, not price. YouHodler Italy's authorisation covers five services (custody/administration, crypto↔funds exchange, crypto↔crypto exchange, order execution, transfers) — a broad scope versus narrower players. Italian authorised peers (per CONSOB list, 1 Jul–8 Oct 2026): CheckSig, Conio, CryptoSmart, Hercle, Hodlie, Olliv Italia, Riv Digital, Young Platform, plus Banca Sella via notification. Broader EU MiCA-CASP peers on the "passport-across-the-Union" play: Triple-A (France AMF, Jun 2026), Crossmint (Jan 2026), Januar (Jun 2026), Fipto (dual PI+MiCA, Jan 2026). **Protagonist's position:** niche/catching-up — YouHodler is a Swiss-rooted (launched 2018) crypto-backed-lending + wallet + trading platform (supports 100+ assets per cryptoslate, Oct 2026) that is entering the EU regulated perimeter via Italy, not an established EU incumbent; its stated moat is brand + an EU passport base, not scale `(analysis)`. Note the gap: the CASP scope authorised is custody/exchange/execution/transfer — it does **not** on its face cover YouHodler's signature crypto-backed lending/"earn" products, which remain outside the MiCA CASP perimeter `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/revenue attaches to this news (a licence grant, not a raise), and YouHodler is private with no disclosed funding or valuation (web search returned none) → trading multiples **not computable; distribution not computed**. Internal comps are regulatory precedents, not valuation comps: [[Triple-A secures MiCA CASP licence from France's AMF]] · [[Crossmint secures MiCA authorization to expand across EU]] · [[Januar secures MiCA licence and raises €1M]] · [[Stablecoin player Fipto achieves dual authorisation status]] · [[BNY expands digital asset custody in EU under MiCA]]. These confirm the 2026 pattern: EU firms stacking MiCA CASP (often alongside a PI/EMI licence) to passport the EEA. EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E = no data (no public financials for YouHodler).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Passporting is announced, not live.** The release says YouHodler "plans to extend to all 27 Member States" — under MiCA Art. 65 passporting is a notification that takes effect ~15 days after filing, but no notification is confirmed filed. Treat EU-wide reach as intent, not fact; de-PR: "authorised in Italy" ≠ "operating EU-wide."
2. **Scope vs. business-model mismatch.** The authorised services (custody/exchange/execution/transfer) do not clearly cover YouHodler's core crypto-backed loans and yield/"earn" products. If those products stay outside the CASP perimeter, the licence legitimises the wallet/exchange leg but not the lending economics that differentiate YouHodler — a second-order risk to how much this licence actually unlocks in revenue terms `(analysis)`.
3. **Crowded, commoditising licence pool + single-market base.** With ~370+ CASPs EEA-wide and 9 in Italy already, authorisation is a ticket-to-play, not a moat; YouHodler is a late entrant in a concentrated field and currently supervised only in Italy, so execution risk sits in converting a single-market licence into real cross-border distribution and bank/institutional partnerships.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` For the sector this is one more data point that the OAM→MiCA transition is consolidating Italian crypto onto a supervised short-list and opening bank/institutional distribution to the authorised few — watch whether CONSOB's authorised count climbs past 9 and whether banks (e.g. Banca Sella's pending notification) actually route retail crypto through CASPs. Falsifiable thesis: YouHodler converts the Italian licence into EU passporting and a disclosed institutional/bank channel within ~12 months; trigger = a confirmed Art. 65 passport notification and a named bank/institution partner. What breaks it = the licence stays Italy-only and the crypto-lending products remain outside the authorised scope, leaving the grant more reputational than revenue-moving.
