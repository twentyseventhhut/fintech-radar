Russia’s largest bank, Sberbank, has received approval to exchange cryptocurrencies, advancing Moscow’s effort to bring crypto trading and custody into its regulated financial system.
The Bank of Russia added Sberbank to its register of cryptocurrency exchange providers, according to Interfax . The bank had already joined the digital depository register earlier this month, giving it a role in recording clients’ crypto holdings.
There are now nine organizations on the exchange register, including VTB and T-Invest Lab.
The approval marks another step away from the central bank’s 2022 proposal to ban domestic crypto exchanges and prohibit Russian financial intermediaries from handling cryptocurrencies. A law that took effect Sept. 1 instead creates a regulated market involving banks, brokers, exchanges and digital depositories.
The two registrations cover different parts of that market. Registered crypto dealers can buy and sell cryptocurrencies using their own funds outside organized exchanges, while depositories record ownership and transfers, according to the central bank .
The combination provides a route to offer both trading and custody-related services for Sberbank, having also started working on a crypto wallet earlier this year.
That opening comes with tighter control over how investors hold and move their coins. Domestic crypto payments remain prohibited, and the central bank says that from July of next year, crypto transactions will have to go through registered intermediaries. That would effectively shut out peer-to-peer transactions settled through Russian bank accounts.
Sberbank plans to debut its first products Dec. 1 through interfaces its customers already use, according to Interfax. The bank has been preparing crypto trading infrastructure .
The bank is already testing trades in BTC, ETH, USDT and other major tokens in its app, though the central bank will set the final list of permitted coins, Sberbank told Vedomosti .

# Context-enrichment: Sberbank approved to exchange cryptocurrencies by Bank of Russia
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5. Freshness: FRESH (genuine Sber-specific custody→exchange follow-up, a new 12 Oct registry entry; distinct from the 6 Oct registry notes)._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On **2026-10-12** the Bank of Russia added **Sberbank** to its **register of digital-currency EXCHANGE operators** (криптообменников), reported by Interfax and restated by CoinDesk on 10-13. This is on TOP of Sber's **digital-DEPOSITARY (custody) registration of 2026-10-06** (see [[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]], where Sber appeared ONLY as a depositary). So the news is **not** "Sber entered the crypto registers" (that was 10-06) — it is specifically **"Sber upgraded from custody-only to custody + exchange"**, becoming — alongside VTB — one of only **two** entities on **both** registers (per CoinDesk, Unchained, Interfax).
- Register math: the exchange list grew from **4 firms (6 Oct)** → **9 firms** now (Sber plus later additions; secondary sources name e.g. "Paradise Technology", "TT LLC" — `[low-confidence]`, not CBR-verified). The note's own "nine organizations" line is consistent.
- Scope of the "exchange" licence (de-PR'd, from CBR via CoinDesk): a **registered crypto dealer can buy/sell cryptocurrencies using its OWN funds OUTSIDE organized trading** — i.e. principal/dealer OTC, NOT an order-book venue. Depositaries record ownership/transfers. Sber holding both = it can both **trade against clients** and **custody** their coins.
- Launch: Sber plans to **open crypto trading to ALL clients on 2026-12-01** via existing interfaces (SberBank Online etc.); already **testing BTC/ETH/USDT** in-app. **The CBR, not Sber, sets the final permitted-coin list** (per Vedomosti) — a key control the PR glosses over.
- Hard limits the headline omits: **domestic crypto PAYMENTS stay banned**; from **July 2027** all crypto trades must route through **registered intermediaries**, which "effectively shuts out P2P transactions settled through Russian bank accounts" (CoinDesk). Retail (non-qualified) buyers are capped at **RUB 300,000/yr per intermediary** after a suitability test; qualified investors uncapped (regime context, [[Russia registers first crypto exchange operators and custodians]]).

**+ Why structured this way / what it reveals.** "Approved to exchange crypto" reads like market-opening, but the CBR deliberately built a **dealer/custody perimeter, not an open spot exchange** — Sber trades as principal against retail, with the CBR picking the coin menu and a 2027 chokepoint that criminalises bank-account-settled P2P. The real signal is **concentration**: the regulator is comfortable letting the single largest state bank hold BOTH the trading AND the custody licence — the OPPOSITE of Western segregation-of-duties — because the policy goal is control and onshoring, not competitive market structure.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Intra-Russia incumbent race (not a global-exchange story). Dual-register club = **Sber + VTB** only. Other named players: **T-Invest Lab** (exchanger, T-Technologies) + **Atomyze** (its depositary); **VTB** (both, trading via "ВТБ Мои Инвестиции" ~Nov); **MOEX** (1 Dec organised spot launch, partner depositary); **SPB Exchange** (building its own settlement depositary, year-end, [[SPB Exchange plans crypto spot trading and settlement depositary]]); plus opaque first-wave shells **Zefir / Sistema-Crypto** ([[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]] flags Zefir as a RUB 10k-capital LLC with no public product). Position: Sber is **not first** (missed the 6 Oct exchange wave, caught up 12 Oct) but is **best-distributed** — it converts the dual licence into the largest retail funnel.

**+ Why the lay of the land is this way / second-order.** Because the moat is **distribution + licence, not product**, Sber's ~100m+ retail base makes its late exchange-registration more valuable than an early slot held by a shell. Dual-register status lets Sber keep the client, the trade AND the coins in-house — vertical capture of the full fee stack. The counterintuitive effect: letting the two largest state banks own both links **concentrates counterparty/conflict risk** precisely where the sanctions exposure is also concentrated.

## [2] Company history / fit
Trajectory: Sber has pushed crypto adjacency for over a year — crypto-backed lending work with Cantor Fitzgerald (see [[Cantor Fitzgerald and Sberbank join crypto-backed lending push]], 2026-06), a crypto wallet earlier in 2026, a **digital-depositary application and ~1 Dec target** flagged in [[Финтехно banks race to control Russian crypto infrastructure]] (2026-10-06), then depositary registration 6 Oct, now exchange registration 12 Oct. The sequence is coherent: **custody first (record holdings) → dealer licence (trade them) → Dec 1 retail go-live**.

**+ Why the company acts this way.** A sanctioned (OFAC/EU since 2022) state bank needs an **onshore, supervised** venue to (a) capture oversight/tax, (b) retain deposit-adjacent flows that would otherwise leak to offshore/P2P, and (c) build a cross-border-settlement rail insulated from Western correspondent banking (the regime permits crypto for cross-border TRADE while banning domestic payments). Owning both links is the structural logic — Sber monetises its distribution monopoly without ceding the custody or trade margin to anyone else.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Genuinely new (vs corpus):** Sber crossing from depositary-ONLY (6 Oct) to **dual-register (12 Oct)** is a real new event — the canonical [[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]] explicitly had Sber as custody-only, so this is not a reprint. The "only two dual-register entities" framing (Sber + VTB) is the incremental fact.
**Traction de-PR'd = still ZERO live retail volume.** Sber is **testing** BTC/ETH/USDT internally; public launch is **announced for 1 Dec**, not live; the permitted-coin list is **not final** (CBR decides). "Approved/registered/testing" ≠ adopted.
**+ Why the value-add is real-but-bounded.** Who captures the margin: the distribution owner — Sber. But the market it captures is **structurally small and walled**: 3-asset retail menu, RUB 300k cap, domestic-payment ban, 2027 intermediary-only chokepoint, and — for a sanctioned bank — **no path to Western fiat liquidity**. So the licence is valuable *relative to rivals* yet the absolute pool may stay thin (cf. digital-ruble "high accounts, low activity", [[Russians open 220,000 digital ruble accounts in first month]]). What breaks the thesis: if onshore volumes never migrate from the offshore/P2P grey market that the 2027 rule is meant to kill.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch: (1) **Sber 1 Dec go-live** — the first REAL de-PR'd retail volume and the final CBR-approved coin list; (2) VTB Nov trading; (3) MOEX 1 Dec organised spot; (4) whether the July-2027 "registered-intermediaries-only" rule actually suppresses P2P or just drives it further offshore. Sentiment: regulator is **supportive-but-control-first** — channelling crypto into a bank-dominated, heavily-supervised perimeter. **+ Counterintuitive second-order:** formalisation under sanctioned state banks builds a **parallel onshore system deliberately unreachable by the global crypto stack** — legitimacy and tax-visibility at home, isolation abroad; and concentrating the rails in Sber/VTB makes the whole market a single sanctions-designation surface rather than a diversified one.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russia's crypto market moved from prohibition to a licensed regime in 2026. The law "On Digital Currencies" took core effect Sept. 1, 2026, creating five licensed participant types — exchanges, brokers, management companies, depositories and exchangers — all under Bank of Russia oversight, with phased operational requirements running to July 1, 2027 (per Cointelegraph/CBR, as of Sept. 2026). Access is tiered: non-qualified investors can buy the most liquid coins up to ₽300,000/year via one intermediary after a test; qualified investors trade without annual limits (per KuCoin summary of the law, Sept. 2026). The hard boundary that shapes everything: domestic crypto *payments* for goods/services remain banned — the regime is built for investment exposure and lawful cross-border settlement, not retail spending, which is a sanctions-era workaround lens rather than a consumer-payments play. "Why now": the framework is a reversal of the CBR's own 2022 ban proposal, driven by (a) a push to pull an estimated grey-market flow onshore under supervision and (b) demand for sanctioned-economy external settlement rails. No verifiable domestic-market TAM — Russian crypto holdings are frequently cited in secondary press but not from a primary source → no data.

**Competitive landscape.** This is a regulated-access race, so the KPIs are distribution (customer base funneled into trading) and register position rather than take rate. The register structure separates two permissions: crypto *dealer/exchange* (buy/sell on own account outside organized venues — quote prices to own customers) and *digital depository* (record ownership/transfers). Sberbank holds both, having joined the depository register earlier in Oct. 2026 and the exchange register ~Oct. 12 (per coindesk/Interfax). Nine organizations now sit on the exchange register, including VTB and T-Invest Lab (part of T-Technologies, alongside T-Bank). Recent peer/sector moves: Moscow Exchange launched crypto-linked (non-delivery) futures for qualified investors June 4, 2025, expanding to BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP/TRON perpetuals Sept. 22, 2025, with >72,000 qualified investors and ~₽600bn ($7.1bn) cumulative turnover by mid-Sept. 2025 (per crypto.news / bitcoin.com). Sberbank had already signalled intent via crypto-backed corporate lending plans (Feb. 2026, Reuters) — see [[Cantor Fitzgerald and Sberbank join crypto-backed lending push]]. Protagonist's position: **ahead/leading on distribution** — Sberbank reports 110.7m retail clients and 87.6m SberBank Online MAU (per TASS/Moscow Times on FY2025 results), and plans to open trading to all clients Dec. 1, 2026 via SberBank Online, SberInvestments and SberBusiness. Its moat here is distribution scale + the full trading-plus-custody stack; the register itself is the regulatory switching cost competitors must also clear.

**Comps & multiples.** No market-cap/EV multiple is computable — Sberbank's ADRs were delisted and Western IR coverage is sanctions-limited, so clean EV, EBITDA and consensus are not freely verifiable → `[UNSOURCED]`. Scale anchor only (not a multiple): Sber FY2025 IFRS net profit ~₽1.7tn (~$22bn, +7.9% y/y), ~22.7% ROE (per TASS/Moscow Times/investing.com, Feb. 2026; primary doc: Sber Summary Consolidated IFRS Financial Statements 12M 2025, https://www.sberbank.com/common/img/uploaded/files/info/reporting_q4_8a5ewlbo_2025_en.pdf). Internal comps (Russian regulated-crypto precedent, no valuation attached): [[Bank of Russia registers NAUFOR as first DFA-platform SRO]], [[Russian central bank open to stablecoins only for external settlements]], [[Russians open 220,000 digital ruble accounts in first month]]. Distribution not computed — qualitative comparison only; crypto trading is a marginal line vs a ₽1.7tn-profit bank, so near-term earnings impact is immaterial `(analysis)`.

**Risk flags.**
- **Regulatory reversibility & coin discretion.** The CBR sets the final permitted-coin list and already banned crypto in 2022 before reversing — the same regulator can re-tighten; a launch "approved" is not a launch that stays open. Second-order: product roadmap (BTC/ETH/USDT testing) is contingent on a list the bank does not control.
- **Narrow scope = capped upside.** Payments stay banned and from July 2027 all crypto transactions must route through registered intermediaries, shutting out P2P via Russian accounts. The addressable use is investment + cross-border, not a mass-market payments/spending flywheel — so the 110.7m retail base overstates the real crypto TAM `(analysis)`.
- **Sanctions / geopolitical.** The regime's cross-border-settlement rationale is widely read as sanctions-evasion infrastructure; Western secondary-market access (liquidity, stablecoin rails, exit venues) for Sber-intermediated crypto is constrained, raising custody/convertibility and reputational/enforcement risk that domestic approval does not resolve.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Russia is formalising a *domestic, intermediary-gated* crypto market where incumbent banks — not native exchanges — are the licensed on-ramps; this favours consolidation around a few large balance sheets (Sber, VTB, T) rather than a fragmented exchange landscape `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: by mid-2027, the bulk of Russian regulated crypto volume sits inside bank/broker apps (MOEX futures + bank dealers), not standalone platforms. Watch/trigger: the CBR's final permitted-coin list and actual Dec. 1 go-live (announced vs live), plus whether USDT survives the list — a narrow list or a slipped launch would break the thesis.
