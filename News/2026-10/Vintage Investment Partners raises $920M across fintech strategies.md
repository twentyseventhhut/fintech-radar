Fresh capital brings Vintage to over $4.8 billion in AUM, expanding its ability to partner with leading and capacity-constrained venture managers and invest across a growing universe of venture-backed companies. 
 TEL AVIV, Israel , Oct. 21, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Vintage Investment Partners ("Vintage"), a global venture capital platform founded in 2003, today announced it has raised $920 million in new capital bringing Vintage's assets under management to over $4.8 billion. The capital further expands the firm's ability to invest across leading venture funds and venture-backed companies on a primary and secondary basis at a time when more value is being created in private markets, portfolios are maturing, and demand for venture exposure and liquidity continues to grow.
The fresh capital comes at a pivotal moment for venture. Companies are staying private longer, and more of their value is being created before the public markets ever see it. Meanwhile, AI is accelerating innovation, helping create a new generation of high-growth technology companies that are reaching significant scale while remaining private. Capital is also concentrating at the very top of venture. According to the Q2 2026 PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor , just three firms accounted for nearly half of all venture capital raised in the first half of 2026. As capital concentrates among a relatively small number of firms, access becomes increasingly difficult to earn, and Vintage has spent more than two decades earning its place within that ecosystem.
 "More value is being created in private markets, over shorter timelines, than at any point in the history of venture,"  said Asaf Horesh, Co-Managing Partner. "In a moment like this, one thing hasn't changed, it's only become more decisive: many of the most successful founders continue to partner with a relatively small group of highly sought-after venture managers. That is exactly where we invest, and earning a seat with them is the hardest, most important work we do. With more than $1.1 billion raised over the past three years across primary, secondary and co-investment strategies, we can support fund managers, companies and investors in more ways, while helping address the growing demand for liquidity across the venture ecosystem." 
Focused exclusively on venture and with offices in Tel Aviv, London, and New York, Vintage has built long-standing relationships with many of the venture ecosystem's most sought-after managers in the U.S., Europe and Israel, while developing proprietary data and insights across thousands of venture funds and companies it tracks. The raise also marks another step for Vintage, as alongside current partners Asaf Horesh, Abe Finkelstein, Amit Frenkel, Iren Reznikov and Leyla Holterud, the firm has promoted Tzlil Kovetz to Partner, recognizing her progression from Associate to Partner and reflecting her contributions across the investment platform for the last five plus years. In addition, Ilan Leiferman, Vintage's Chief Value Officer was also promoted to Partner. Ilan leads Value+, Vintage's value creation platform, which connects portfolio companies with global enterprises from our network of hundreds, and has helped generate hundreds of millions of dollars in commercial opportunities across the firm's ecosystem. Vintage also recently appointed Brachie Sprung as Chief Investor Relations Officer, bringing two decades of global experience across venture capital, fundraising, investor relations and strategic communications.
 "We are deeply grateful to our LPs for the trust they continue to place in Vintage," said Abe Finkelstein, Co-Managing Partner. "We never assume our place in the room; we earn it, by doing the work, holding a very high bar for how and where we invest, and putting years of relationships and market understanding to work for our investors. In a market like this, that discipline matters more than it ever has. The promotions of Tzlil Kovetz and Ilan Leiferman to Partner, and the appointment of Brachie Sprung as Chief Investor Relations Officer, reflect our commitment to building a multi-generational institution and strengthening the team that will help lead Vintage's next chapter."

# Context-enrichment: Vintage Investment Partners raises $920M across fintech strategies
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Vintage Investment Partners (Tel Aviv-founded, est. 2003; offices Tel Aviv/London/New York) closed **$920M of new capital**, lifting AUM to **>$4.8B**. Press splits it as roughly **$580M new fund-of-funds + ~$340M secondary fund** (Globes/Calcalist). This is an **LP/allocator capital raise by a venture fund-of-funds + secondaries manager** — NOT a fintech operating-company round. The "fintech strategies" framing in our note title is misleading: Vintage is a **generalist venture allocator**; fintech is only part of the underlying exposure via the managers and companies it backs, not a dedicated vehicle.
- **Why structured this way:** two sleeves (primary FoF + secondaries) because Vintage's whole pitch is "access + liquidity" — the FoF buys into capacity-constrained top managers, the secondary fund buys maturing LP/employee stakes while IPO exits stay shut. The raise is deliberately timed to the liquidity drought.
- **What the PR reveals (and hides):** the release leans hard on a macro thesis ("more value created in private markets, over shorter timelines than ever") and the PitchBook-NVCA stat that **~3 firms took nearly half of all H1-2026 VC**. That stat is Vintage's reason-to-exist pitch — access to over-subscribed managers is the scarce good it sells. It also announces partner promotions (Tzlil Kovetz, Ilan Leiferman) and a new CIRO (Brachie Sprung) — institution-building optics for a multi-fund platform. No LP names, no target vs. hard-cap, no fund numbering, no DPI/IRR track record disclosed.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Venture-focused FoF + secondaries allocators: **Industry Ventures, StepStone, HarbourVest, Lexington, Ardian, Pantheon, Hamilton Lane, Top Tier, Cendana** (in the secondaries/FoF layer). In Israel specifically Vintage is the dominant local venture allocator. Direct-ish corpus peers on the private-markets-liquidity theme: [[Caplight raises $16M Series A led by BlackRock and Fin Capital]] (private-market data + secondary-trade marketplace), [[Flutterwave and Techdollar give staff liquidity against equity]] (employee-liquidity angle), [[Demand surges for private-credit secondaries]] (adjacent secondaries boom in credit), [[IDB Lab approves $10M investment in Mexican VC Cometa's fourth fund]] (LP-commits-to-VC-fund news, same week). $920M is **mid-sized** in this cohort — the global secondaries giants (Lexington, Ardian) raise multi-tens-of-billions; Vintage's edge is relationship access to the Israel/Europe/US top-manager set, not scale.
- **Why the landscape is this way:** secondaries are a scale + relationships game. The margin accrues to whoever has (a) proprietary access to over-subscribed managers and (b) cheap deal flow of maturing stakes. Vintage competes on (a) via two decades of GP relationships, not on pricing power.

## [2] Company history / fit
Trajectory (from PitchBook/PR): **Fund of Funds VI $550M (2021), Fund of Funds VII $631M (2022), Secondary V ~$312M, Growth IV $200M (2024, its 16th fund, AUM then ~$4B)**. The new $920M is cleanly on-strategy: same FoF + secondaries + growth playbook, bigger AUM (~$4B → >$4.8B). PR notes **>$1.1B raised over the trailing three years** across primary/secondary/co-invest. Fully consistent — no pivot, no new product line.
- **Why Vintage acts this way:** as an allocator its revenue is management fees + carry on committed capital, so growth = raising the next, bigger vintage while IPO-starved LPs crave both exposure to top managers AND liquidity. Standing up a larger secondary sleeve now is the rational response to the exit drought.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: low.** This is a routine next-vintage fundraise by an established 20+ yr platform, not a new capability. The only "new" is a larger AUM and partner promotions.
- **Traction is real and verifiable** (unlike a startup announcement): $4.8B of actual committed AUM, a 20-year track record, repeat LPs. But NO performance numbers (DPI/IRR/net returns) disclosed — the hardest number to get and the one that actually matters for a FoF is withheld.
- **Where the margin is:** FoF/secondaries managers earn a fee-on-fee layer; their value-add must exceed that double-fee drag. Vintage justifies it via (a) access to managers LPs can't reach directly and (b) Value+ (its 650-enterprise commercial network for portfolio cos). Access is a genuine moat IF the top-manager outperformance persists; it commoditizes if LPs get direct access or if top managers underperform.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Vintage will deploy into **fewer, larger funds** (Globes) — concentrating bets on the highest-conviction managers, mirroring the broader VC capital concentration. Sentiment tailwind is strong: **secondaries hit a record ~$226B in 2025 and $121B in H1-2026 (+19% YoY), with GP-led deals ~54% of volume** (Evercore/Bloomberg) — the liquidity drought is structurally feeding the secondaries bid.
- **Counterintuitive second-order:** the same capital concentration Vintage cites as its tailwind is also a fragility. If value and access concentrate in ~3 mega-managers, a FoF's "access" edge narrows to "can you get allocation in those 3?" — and if you can't, you're buying the long tail at a fee premium. The secondaries boom is partly a symptom of a broken exit market; a reopened IPO window would cool the very bid Vintage is positioning for.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Vintage sits in venture fund-of-funds (FoF) + secondaries — an intermediary layer on top of venture, not a direct fintech operator. Why now: value is increasingly created pre-IPO (companies staying private longer, AI-driven scale), while LPs need liquidity. Per PitchBook-NVCA Q2 2026 Venture Monitor (cited in the raise PR), just three firms took ~50% of all VC raised in H1 2026 — capital is concentrating at the top, so access/allocation to sought-after managers is the scarce good. The secondaries market is in a documented 2026 boom: global secondaries volume hit a record ~$118–121bn in H1 2026 (per Jefferies/Evercore, via valueaddvc/angelinvestorsnetwork), on pace to top 2025's ~$233–260bn; US venture secondary transaction value ran ~$112bn in Q1 2026 alone, with secondaries now rivaling IPOs/M&A as the default VC exit path (per PitchBook Q2 2026 US VC Secondary Market Watch, as of 2026). Specialist secondary buyers held ~$248bn dry powder at end-2025 (angelinvestorsnetwork). Structure: concentrated at the top of VC but fragmented among FoF/secondaries managers; barriers are relationship-access to top GPs and proprietary fund/company data, not capital.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for a FoF/secondaries platform: AUM, DPI/liquidity returned, net IRR/TVPI, NAV-discount at entry on secondaries. Vintage: $920M new ($580M FoF + $340M secondary fund, per Globes/Calcalist), AUM >$4.8bn, >$1.1bn raised over past 3yrs across primary/secondary/co-invest. Positioning signal: Vintage will back ~30 funds (down from 35 prior, and ~60 at peak — per Globes), leaning into the concentration thesis (~10% of companies drive ~80% of outcomes). Key players in venture secondaries/FoF: StepStone (VC Secondaries Fund VI final-closed $3.3bn, the largest ever; Fund VII filed ~$1.59bn as of Sep 1 2026 — transacted.io/angellinx), Industry Ventures (now Goldman-owned; Secondary XI), HarbourVest, Lexington, Coller. Basis of competition: GP access, data edge, and liquidity-solution breadth, not price. Position: Vintage is a mid-scale, relationship-led specialist (Tel Aviv/London/NY, US-Europe-Israel focus) — niche-but-established vs the $3bn+ US mega-platforms; moat is relationship + proprietary data across thousands of funds, plus its Value+ corporate-matching network (650+ enterprises) `(analysis)`.

- StepStone VC Secondaries VI: $3.3bn final close (largest venture-secondaries fund ever); VII filing ~$1.59bn interim (2026).
- Vintage: $920M new raise (this note), $4.8bn AUM.

- [[Notion Capital raises $130M for third growth fund]] (2025-10); [[Iliad Partners secures second close of $50 million fund]] (2025-10) — smaller direct-VC raises.

Read: Vintage's $920M is in-line-to-small for the venture-secondaries leaders (an order of magnitude below StepStone's flagship) but large for an Israel-rooted multi-strategy FoF; $4.8bn AUM is a credible mid-scale platform. Distribution not computed — comps differ in structure (FoF vs dedicated secondaries vs direct VC); qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. Concentration / access dependency — the whole thesis rests on securing allocations to a shrinking set of top GPs (3 firms = ~50% of H1 2026 VC); if Vintage loses its seat with sought-after managers, the FoF edge erodes, and backing only ~30 funds raises single-manager concentration.
2. Secondaries-cycle / pricing risk — Vintage is scaling secondaries into a record-hot 2026 market with ~$248bn of dry powder chasing deals; crowded buy-side can compress entry discounts, so returns depend on buying cheap at exactly the moment everyone else is buying.
3. Exit-dependence / mark-to-liquidity — FoF/secondaries returns (DPI) hinge on an eventual IPO/M&A reopening or continued secondary demand; the model assumes private-market value keeps compounding — a prolonged exit drought or AI-valuation reset would hit NAVs and the demand for its own secondary liquidity.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The raise is confirmation that the liquidity-intermediary layer — not direct VC — is where 2026 fundraising momentum sits, echoing [[Blue Dot and FT Partners report sees fintech liquidity supercycle]]. Thesis: as the IPO window stays narrow, FoF/secondaries managers keep out-raising; watch for whether Vintage's "fewer funds" concentration bet (30 vs 60) actually lifts DPI/IRR vs the mega-platforms' breadth. Falsifier: a reopened IPO market in 2027 that restores primary-exit liquidity would undercut the "secondaries as default exit" driver and slow this category's fundraising.
