Revolut is in active talks with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), the Philippines’ central bank, to obtain a digital banking license as it pursues a possible entry into the country’s market.
BSP Deputy Governor Lyn I. Javier, who leads its financial supervision department, said Revolut must pass the regulator’s assessment. The process includes requirements for establishing a foreign bank presence in the Philippines. Any approval timeline depends on Revolut’s compliance preparations.
Revolut has assessed digital banking and e-money licenses for the Philippines and discussed market entry with Trade Secretary Cristina Roque in London on Oct. 2.
The company began operating its Manila Global Technology Hub in July 2025, with plans for hundreds of new positions. Planned roles covered data analytics, financial-crime compliance, customer operations and software engineering.
Revolut’s bank licenses span the United Kingdom, Australia, Colombia and France. It also has conditional clearance to establish a bank in the United States and submitted an application for a Swiss banking license in September.

# Context-enrichment: Revolut in talks with Philippine central bank for banking licence
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Reported **26–27 Oct 2026** (tokenpost / BusinessWorld / philstar / Crowdfund Insider): **BSP Deputy Governor Lyn I. Javier** (head of the financial-supervision sector) confirmed Revolut is in **active talks** with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas for a **digital-banking licence**, and that Revolut "must pass the regulator's assessment," including the requirements for establishing a **foreign-bank presence**. Asked if a licence could land before year-end, Javier said **there is no fixed timeline** — it depends on both the applicant's and the BSP's readiness. The note also recalls Revolut's Oct-2 London meeting with Trade Sec. Cristina Roque and its Manila Global Technology Hub (opened Jul 2025).

De-PR'd: this is the **regulator's read-out on exploratory talks, NOT a filed application, shortlist, or approval**. The one genuinely new fact vs the earlier Oct-8 story is that the central bank itself (not just the trade ministry) has now publicly acknowledged the talks and explicitly flagged the **foreign-bank-presence hurdle** and the **absence of a timeline**. If anything this read-out is *cooler* than the headline implies — the BSP is managing expectations down ("no timeline," "must pass assessment"), not signalling imminent approval.

**+ Why this framing and what it reveals:** This is the SAME PH licence-intent story as the published 2026-10-08 note [[Revolut plans digital bank and e-money licences in Philippines]] — same Oct-2 Roque meeting, same digital-bank + EMI ambition, same Manila-hub backdrop — now re-voiced from the BSP's side. The incremental signal is small: a deputy governor confirming "active talks" with no timeline is roughly what you would expect three weeks after a government-affairs meeting. The structural constraint is unchanged: BSP lifted its moratorium on 1 Jan 2025 and raised the cap from 6 to 10 digital banks (4 new slots), but only three applicants filed before the Nov/Dec-2025 deadline, after which the Monetary Board imposed a **fresh moratorium** closing the window; MariBank became the 7th licensed digital bank in Jul 2026, leaving ~3 slots nominally but the application window shut. So a near-term digital-bank *filing* is not actually open; the realistic short-term path remains the uncapped **EMI (e-money) licence**.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Entrenched, wallet-led oligopoly (unchanged from the Oct-8 analysis):
- **GCash (Mynt)** — ~90m+ registered users; dominant wallet; prepping an ~$8bn IPO.
- **Maya (Voyager)** — ~50m+ users; holds both a wallet and a digital-bank licence (Maya Bank, largest by assets).
- **Tonik** — first PH neobank with a digital-bank licence (2021); first standalone digital bank to turn profitable.
- Plus UNO Digital, UnionDigital, GoTyme, SeaBank, OFBank, and **MariBank** (7th licence, Jul 2026).
GCash + Maya hold the vast majority of PH mobile-wallet activity.

**+ Why the lay of the land matters:** Revolut would arrive 5+ years after the incumbents, into a wallet duopoly with enormous installed bases — and crucially it must clear a **foreign-bank-presence** test the BSP specifically named, a hurdle local applicants did not face. An EMI-only entry pits Revolut against GCash/Maya on their core turf (wallet + remittances) with no structural edge; the full-bank path is gated by a closed slot window. This is the opposite of Revolut's winning early-mover/regulatory-arbitrage playbook — here it is the late follower.

## [2] Company history / fit
Revolut's 2024–2026 expansion is a licence-accumulation machine toward its **100m-user** (mid-2027) target: full **UK** bank licence (restrictions lifted ~2026), **Mexico** full bank live Jan 2026, **Colombia** operating licence 2026, **France**, **Australia**, plus a **Swiss** banking application filed **Sep 2026** and conditional US bank clearance. Its bank licences span UK, Australia, Colombia and France (per source). In the Philippines it opened the **Manila Global Technology Hub** in Jul 2025 (hundreds of planned roles: data analytics, financial-crime compliance, customer ops, software engineering) and in Apr 2026 was reported in talks to acquire a major Asian bank.

**+ Why Revolut acts this way:** It needs a long roster of licensed/launching markets to justify a ~$115–121bn valuation (2026 secondaries) on its user-growth-to-IPO narrative. The Manila hub gives it local staff and a political relationship; PH licence talk is the logical next PR beat on that base. But the Philippines fits the *geographic-coverage* logic more than a *unit-economics* logic — a flag-plant, not an obvious profit centre.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: low (and lower than the Oct-8 item).** Nothing filed, nothing launched; this is the central bank confirming the same exploratory talks the trade ministry already surfaced on Oct 6–8. The marginal new content is the **foreign-bank-presence requirement** and the explicit **"no timeline"** — both of which raise, not lower, the bar. **Zero traction**: no PH users, no product, no filing, no slot.

**+ Why the value-add is unconfirmed:** The binding constraint is regulatory supply (the slot moratorium + the foreign-presence test), which Revolut does not control. Even if it wins an EMI licence, margin in PH payments is captured by the entrenched wallet networks and their biller/merchant rails. The real question is not "can Revolut enter" but "is there a durable edge in being a late third wallet behind GCash and Maya in a market where it cannot yet take deposits" — on current facts, no.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Short term: possible EMI application; a digital-bank filing only if/when BSP reopens the window (uncertain, plausibly 2027+). Javier's "no fixed timeline" and the foreign-presence test signal a long, conditional path. Revolut will likely keep leaning on the Manila hub + government relationships to position for the next slot round.

**+ Second-order:** The real signal is *strategic breadth*, not *Philippine substance* — one more pin on the global map in service of the 100m/IPO narrative. The proliferation of "Revolut eyes country X" headlines dilutes each one's news value; a BSP confirmation of "active talks, no timeline" is a low-weight, directional update on an already-reported story.

# Market research: Revolut–BSP Philippine banking-licence talks

_Dated 2026-10-27. English, skeptical. In-base comps (semsearch down → grep) + web + Revolut's own filed FY2025 figures. No fabricated numbers._

## What is actually new here
This is an incremental, soft step — not a licence. BSP Deputy Governor Lyn Javier confirmed only that Revolut is "in active talks" and "must pass" the regulator's assessment, including requirements for establishing a **foreign bank presence**. No application confirmed as accepted, no timeline. It upgrades the June-2026 "early-access waitlist" note and the Oct-2026 "plans digital bank & e-money licences" note (talks with Trade Secretary Cristina Roque in London, Oct 2) from intent to regulator-acknowledged dialogue. Still regulator-gated and early.

## Sector context — Philippine digital banking is a capped, crowded market
- BSP runs a hard **cap of 10 digital-bank licences**. As of mid-2026 there are **7 licensed** (incl. MariBank's July conversion), leaving **3 slots**. Crucially, BSP **stopped accepting new digital-bank applications on 1 Dec 2025**; applications filed before the deadline continue processing. BSP said three applicants met the Nov-30 deadline and that an unnamed "European digital bank" had completed requirements — but has **not confirmed Revolut is among them**. So the headline path (a de-novo digital-bank licence) may already be administratively closed to a late entrant.
- Rules: min capitalisation **₱1.0bn**; a **qualified foreign bank may own up to 100%** of a digital bank (vs 40% cap for non-bank foreign corporates), so Revolut's structure/"foreign bank presence" framing matters — it likely needs a qualified-foreign-bank route or an EMI licence rather than a fresh digital-bank slot.
- Competitive field is already profitability-stressed: **Maya** leads with ~48.9% of digital-bank deposits (end-2025) and is profitable; **GoTyme** ~₱43.5bn deposits / ~9M customers (Mar 2026); **Tonik** became the first standalone (non-ecosystem) digital bank to turn profitable (Q1 2026); **UnionDigital** posted a ₱3.13bn net loss in 2024. A late foreign entrant faces entrenched, ecosystem-backed incumbents (GCash/Mynt, which just IPO'd).

## In-base comps (how this fits Revolut's APAC push)
- Revolut's Southeast-Asia hub is **Singapore** ($274M/5-yr commitment, 300+ staff target, 2027 permanent office; Singapore unit profitable two straight years). The **Manila Global Technology Hub** (opened July 2025, hundreds of roles in data/financial-crime/compliance/engineering) is an ops centre, not a licence.
- This is one node in a very broad 2026 licence-chase: full UK licence secured, Mexico bank live, Colombia/Peru approvals, US charter application, Swiss application (Sep), Brazil, plus talks to acquire an Asian bank. Pattern: Revolut prefers its own licence over local partnerships (it explicitly ruled out partnering with a local Philippine bank in the June waitlist story).

## IR grounding — Revolut's own filed FY2025 results (PRIMARY evidence)
Per Revolut Group Holdings Ltd **Annual Report 2025** (FY ended 31 Dec 2025), <https://assets.revolut.com/pdf/annualreport2025.pdf> and company release:
- Group revenue **$6.0bn, +46%** (from $4.0bn); profit before tax **$2.3bn, +57%**, PBT margin **38%** (fifth straight profitable year).
- Retail customers **68.3M (+30%)**; business customers **767K (+33%)**; total customer balances **$67.5bn (+66%)**.
- Stated **$13bn five-year investment** plan and a target of **100M retail customers by mid-2027** across 100+ countries.
Read-through: Revolut has the capital and profitability to clear a ₱1.0bn floor trivially and fund a Philippine build; the constraint is regulatory/structural, not financial. The Philippines (large, young, underbanked, remittance-heavy, ~9M+ GCash users already habituated to digital finance) fits the 100M-customer thesis — but is a marginal contributor near-term.

## Risk flags
1. **Slot/timing risk (primary):** BSP's 10-cap with only 3 remaining slots and the 1 Dec 2025 application cutoff means a late de-novo digital-bank licence may be unavailable; BSP has not confirmed Revolut filed in time. "Active talks" ≠ accepted application ≠ licence.
2. **Structure/foreign-presence risk:** BSP explicitly requires a "foreign bank presence"; Revolut likely needs a qualified-foreign-bank or EMI pathway and a wholly-owned PH entity — more legal/capital structuring and a longer runway than the headline implies.
3. **Execution-credibility risk:** Revolut's banking-licence track record is slow and uneven elsewhere (UK licence took years with risk-control setbacks; NZ, Israel, Morocco, Chile still pending/delayed). No timeline given here either.
4. **Competitive/profitability risk:** Entering a capped market dominated by profitable, ecosystem-backed incumbents (Maya 48.9% deposit share; GoTyme ~9M users; GCash IPO'd) — several peers still loss-making — implies heavy subsidy/CAC with uncertain unit economics for a late foreign entrant.
5. **Materiality/signal risk:** Even a win is small vs a $6bn-revenue, 68M-customer group near-term; this is a strategic APAC footprint play, not a 2026-27 earnings driver. Sourcing is single-channel (TokenPost relaying BSP comments) — treat specifics as regulator talking points, not committed plans.
