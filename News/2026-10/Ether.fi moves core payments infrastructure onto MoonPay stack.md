The partnership puts headless ramps, cross-chain trading, institutional-grade virtual accounts, and crypto deposits inside ether.fi, without giving up self-custody. 
 NEW YORK , Oct. 6, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Ether.fi , the crypto neobank built around a single account for saving, growing, and spending digital assets, has announced a partnership with MoonPay , the global financial technology company powering the movement of value across fiat and digital assets. Ether.fi is moving its core payments infrastructure onto MoonPay's integrated stack, which covers how users fund the app, move money inside it, and cash out.
Most neobanks source these functions from separate vendors. Each integration adds overhead and compliance surface area, and each usually brings its own identity check. A user ends up verifying for the card, again for a bank account, and again for an onramp, and gets handed to an outside provider in the middle of a transaction. In a self-custodial product this has been harder to fix than in a traditional app, which is a large part of why no one has delivered a seamless version over the past five years. Ether.fi and MoonPay are building one, where the user keeps control of their keys and the experience feels as simple as any mainstream neobank app.
"The bar for ether.fi isn't other crypto products," said Rok Kopp, CGO and Co-Founder of ether.fi. "It's whatever banking app a user opens every morning. Every integration we make is measured against that standard."
 Ether.fi is growing on spending, not speculation 
Ether.fi's card has become one of the largest onchain spending products in crypto . According to Paymentscan , which tracks card activity onchain, ether.fi card users spent $123.7M in September 2026 across 1.5M transactions and 48,162 active addresses. That is up from $24.1M in September 2025, more than 5x in twelve months. Since launching in April 2025, the card has processed $918.1M in spend across 11.6M transactions and 113K addresses.
This growth does not follow the crypto market. Over the past twelve months bitcoin fell roughly 27% , with double-digit drops in November 2025 and January, February, and June 2026. Over the same period ether.fi's monthly card volume rose in 13 of the last 15 months. In the two months it dipped, January and February 2026, the decline was about 2% each time, while bitcoin was down 10% and 15%. In June 2026, when bitcoin fell 20%, ether.fi's volume grew.
The reason is what people use the product for. Companies built around trading are tied to the market because their revenue depends on speculators trading large assets. When prices fall, activity falls with it. Ether.fi users are buying groceries, paying rent, and more. To them, the balance is just cash. Underneath, it is a stablecoin in a self-custodial wallet on blockchain rails, but that is invisible to the user, and it should be.
The experience matches what users expect from mainstream fintech services. The product underneath is different. A user's keys stay with the user, and ether.fi cannot move or seize the assets in their wallet . Funds move over crypto rails, which means they can travel faster and farther than through a chain of correspondent banks. And because the wallet is self-custodial, ether.fi does not have to be licensed as a bank in every market it enters. Traditional neobanks expand one licence at a time. Ether.fi can reach users in many more markets, with MoonPay's licensed infrastructure handling the fiat rails at the edges. Ether.fi goes further than other neobanks on ownership, reach, and speed.
 Four MoonPay products that will be live inside the ether.fi experience 
 MoonPay Headless Ramps  embed onramp and offramp flows natively in ether.fi. Users can save a card and fund their account in one tap. There are no web views and no redirects. Ether.fi controls the full UI, and MoonPay handles the payment rails, compliance, and global payment method coverage underneath.
 MoonPay Trade  handles cross-chain routing and powers crypto conversions inside the app. Users do not need to think about which asset they are moving or which chain it sits on. They get faster settlement, deeper liquidity, and access to more chains in the wallet they already use.
 MoonPay Enterprise  gives each ether.fi user a virtual account with account and routing numbers. Payment rails include ACH, Fedwire, SWIFT, Faster Payments, SEPA and Open Banking. A user's paycheck, savings, or business revenue lands directly in ether.fi, and money moves out just as easily to any linked bank, on local rails with settlement in seconds. This makes ether.fi a user's primary financial home, not just a place they visit.
 MoonPay Crypto Deposits  lets users bring money into ether.fi from wherever they hold it, whether that is Bitcoin or Solana . A user taps deposit, picks a token and a network, and either sends to a QR code or address or connects a wallet or centralized exchange. The deposited token is then swapped and delivered into the user's balance. Cashing out works the same way in reverse, with the user choosing the token they want to receive.
 One experience, verified once 
Because the stack is integrated, the card, the virtual account, the onramp, and the app are designed to work as one experience. A user completes verification once and that information carries across products, subject to local requirements, instead of starting over each time a new product is turned on.
"The platforms that win will be the ones that make money move simply," said Ivan Soto-Wright, CEO and Founder of MoonPay. "Ether.fi is exactly the kind of platform our infrastructure was built for and this partnership shows what's possible when a neobank chooses full functionality with no compromises for their users."
Headless Ramps, Trade, Enterprise Virtual Accounts, and Deposit will each roll out across ether.fi over the coming months to its users. To learn more about MoonPay's unified payments platform, visit moonpay.com/business

# Context-enrichment: Ether.fi moves core payments infrastructure onto MoonPay stack
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**FRESHNESS: FRESH.** This is a genuinely new deal, announced 2026-10-06 (MoonPay PRNewswire). It is NOT a re-run of the May-2026 [[MoonPay launches Headless Onramps for one-tap crypto checkout]] product launch (that was MoonPay shipping the Headless product; this is Ether.fi *adopting* it plus three other products as its consolidated backend), nor of [[Ether.fi allocates $100 million to Plume's real-world asset vault]] (a yield/RWA product, June 2026). No prior note covers an Ether.fi↔MoonPay tie-up. Not a duplicate.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Ether.fi (self-custodial "crypto neobank") is consolidating its payments backend onto **four MoonPay products**: Headless Ramps (embedded on/off-ramp), MoonPay Trade (cross-chain routing — the ex-Decent.xyz engine, see [[MoonPay launches MoonPay Trade digital asset platform]]), MoonPay Enterprise Virtual Accounts (ACH/Fedwire/SWIFT/Faster Payments/SEPA account+routing numbers), and Crypto Deposits.
- **Announced, NOT live.** PR language is explicit: products "will each roll out across ether.fi over the coming months." Treat as a signed integration roadmap, not shipped functionality.
- **No deal value / no financials disclosed** (confirmed across sources).
- **Why framed this way:** the PR leads with Ether.fi's card-spend traction ($123.7M Sept 2026, $918.1M cumulative since Apr 2025, per "Paymentscan") rather than the deal itself — because the deal is just plumbing. The traction stats are the real headline, used to position Ether.fi as the flagship logo that validates MoonPay's "full-stack" pitch. → why: a vendor-consolidation announcement has no inherent news weight, so both parties anchor to Ether.fi's counter-cyclical spend growth (volume rose in 13 of 15 months while BTC fell ~27%) to make the story about "crypto-as-cash is working." (analysis)
- **Caveat on the hero numbers:** the $6B+ AUM is a company self-claim; independent DefiLlama TVL is ~$5.2B (lower). Paymentscan's dashboard is JS-rendered and could not be independently re-derived — figures are confirmed only as "company citing Paymentscan" + secondary outlets.
- **Outdated present-tense to avoid:** Ether.fi's "restaking" origin — it was reported fully exiting EigenLayer restaking in Sept 2026 ([Blockhead 2026-09-29](https://www.blockhead.co/2026/09/29/ether-fi-to-fully-exit-eigenlayer-restaking-as-sector-profits-collapse/)) as sector yields collapsed. Its growth engine is now the Cash card, not restaking yield.

## [1] Competitors / peers
**Two distinct battlegrounds:**
- **Crypto-infra / stablecoin rails (MoonPay's side):** the heavyweights are now bank/processor-owned — **Stripe→Bridge ($1.1B, Feb 2025)** and **Mastercard→BVNK (up to $1.8B, Aug 2026)**; plus Circle (CPN), Fireblocks, Zerohash (~$1B, raising $1.5B+). MoonPay is still read by some analysts as *more onramp than rails* despite its Iron/Decent/DFlow/Sodot acquisition spree (see [[MoonPay launches enterprise stablecoin services]], [[MoonPay acquires DFlow for on-chain trading infrastructure]], [[MoonPay acquires crypto security startup Sodot for $100 million]]). Its "Stripe for stablecoins" framing is aspirational, not yet validated against Bridge/BVNK.
- **Crypto cards (Ether.fi's side):** fault line = who holds funds pre-swipe. Self-custodial: Ether.fi Cash, Gnosis Pay. Custodial: Coinbase Card, Crypto.com Visa (strongest rewards), Kraken "Krak" (US Aug 2026). **Key dependency:** Ether.fi's card is *issued by Rain* (Rain raised $250M at $1.95B, Jan 2026) — so this MoonPay deal does NOT reduce Ether.fi to one vendor; it now leans on **two** critical backends (Rain for issuing + MoonPay for ramps/accounts).
- **Position:** Ether.fi is a leader *within* the self-custodial-card niche on spend volume, but the niche is crowded (40+ stablecoin cards, per Foresight) and shares infrastructure. → why it matters: when every neobank runs on the same Rain/Visa/MoonPay plumbing, value accrues to the infra layer, not the consumer brand. (analysis, per Odaily/Foresight thesis)

## [2] Company history / fit
Ether.fi: Ethereum liquid-staking (eETH, Nov 2023) → liquid-restaking (EigenLayer) → **pivot to consumer neobank** (Cash card Apr 2025, now the growth driver) → RWA yield ([[Ether.fi allocates $100 million to Plume's real-world asset vault]], June 2026) → now consolidating payments backend. Series A $23M (Bullish Capital + CoinFund, Feb 2024, ~$570M val); founders Mike Silagadze (CEO), Rok Kopp (CGO — he is quoted here).
- **Why the structural pressure:** restaking yields collapsed → Ether.fi had to find a non-yield growth engine → the card (counter-cyclical, spend-based) became it → but a card business lives or dies on UX/fund-in-move-out friction. In a self-custodial product, stitching ramps + accounts + swaps from separate vendors (each with its own KYC) is painful. → so consolidating onto one integrated stack with KYC-once is the logical move to make a self-custodial app feel like a mainstream neobank. The fit is coherent.
- MoonPay side: its whole 2025–26 arc (Iron/Helio/Decent/DFlow/Sodot/Entendre) is a pivot from consumer onramp to full-stack B2B infra. Landing Ether.fi as a flagship, full-suite reference customer is exactly the proof point it needs for an IPO-track story (reportedly in talks with ICE at ~$5B, Dec 2025, unconfirmed).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty is modest.** None of the four products is new (Headless Ramps launched May 2026; Trade May 2026; Enterprise stablecoin/virtual accounts Nov 2025). The "new" thing is the *bundling* into one self-custodial neobank with verify-once — an integration milestone, not a technical breakthrough.
- **Traction of the deal itself = zero today** (nothing is live). Traction of Ether.fi's card (the thing being plumbed) is real: 5x YoY spend, counter-cyclical. That is the durable signal, not the MoonPay announcement.
- **Who captures the margin?** This is the central question, and it does NOT favour Ether.fi. The margin in on/off-ramp, virtual accounts, FX and card issuing sits with MoonPay and Rain (and Visa, >90% of on-chain card volume). Ether.fi owns the *brand and the user relationship* but outsources the economically dense layers. → so unless Ether.fi monetizes the deposit base/AUM directly (yield spread, interchange share), it risks being a "front-end" whose unit economics are set by its two backend vendors. (analysis — the real weight question is distribution-brand vs infra-margin, and the margin is upstream.)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Rollout "over coming months"; next real checkpoint is whether virtual accounts (paycheck direct-deposit → "primary financial home") actually ship and get adoption, since that is the stickiest claim.
- **Sentiment:** mostly promotional coverage with no risk framing. The sharpest skepticism (The Industry Spread, 2026-10-08): "one vendor as ramp, account number and compliance file is concentration risk," and self-custody does not shield the *fiat leg* — "a virtual account number is not a key; a paycheck is on a bank rail until it reaches the wallet." Foresight/Odaily: neobanks-on-shared-stacks are structurally fragile.
- **Risks:** vendor concentration (Rain + MoonPay); regulatory (MoonPay's Texas DOB consent order Dec 2024 for unlicensed money transmission 2020–23, $30,516 penalty; MiCA held); undisclosed fraud-liability allocation; stablecoin depeg risk unaddressed despite a stablecoin-heavy stack.
