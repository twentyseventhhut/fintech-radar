Teya and finmid partner on SME financing in Croatia and Hungary
Stefana Apetrei
22 Oct 2026 / 5 Min Read
Embedded lending provider finmid has partnered with Teya to offer financing to merchants in Croatia and Hungary.
The partnership gives local businesses using the Teya platform access to personalised, pre-approved financing. Offers are calculated using real-time performance data generated within the Teya platform, and the product operates as a merchant cash advance, with repayments linked to monthly revenue. According to the companies, funds are disbursed in an average of three minutes, and merchants can use the capital for purposes ranging from equipment purchases to marketing.
During the early stages of the partnership, more than 500 Teya merchants have received financing, with the product recording a Net Promoter Score (NPS) of 86.
Addressing financing barriers in CEE
The partnership targets a gap in access to capital for SMEs in Central and Eastern Europe (CEE), where businesses continue to face obstacles such as limited credit histories, insufficient collateral, and outdated bank processes. By basing financing decisions on performance data already held within the merchant’s platform, the companies aim to reach businesses that remain underserved by conventional lending channels.
 Sahithya Vemana , Head of Banking at Teya , said that access to capital at the right moment is central to the company’s goal of supporting local businesses. Vemana added that the merchant cash advance model, embedded in Teya and with repayments tied to sales, is being introduced to EU markets where it was not previously available, starting with Croatia, Hungary, and Portugal.
As part of the agreement, finmid joins Teya’s pan-European marketplace as a full-stack financing partner. In this capacity, the company covers underwriting, compliance, servicing, and capital provision for platforms and their merchants.
Founded in 2019, Teya serves more than 80,000 local businesses across nine European markets with payments, business accounts, and expense management tools. The company is regulated in the UK and the EEA.
Embedded lending across platforms
finmid provides an API that allows platforms to launch financing products for their business customers across 30 European markets through a single integration. Teya joins other platforms using finmid as an embedded lending partner, including Bolt, Wolt, Delivery Hero, Just Eat Takeaway, Guesty, and FREENOW.
According to finmid, merchants across its programmes typically see sales increase by up to 45%, with NPS scores above 80, while 80% of businesses renew their financing. Platform partners, the company states, record a 70% reduction in churn, pointing to the role of embedded financing as a retention tool for platforms as well as a source of capital for merchants.
 Max Schertel , co-founder and CEO of finmid , noted that small businesses in Croatia, Hungary, and Portugal show strong demand for growth capital, but have lacked the infrastructure to access it quickly and on fair terms.

# Context-enrichment: Teya and finmid partner on SME financing in Croatia and Hungary
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- **The deal.** Embedded-lending infra provider **finmid** (Berlin, ex-N26 founders, €52M total raised — see [[Finmid raises €17M extension, strikes Bolt financing deal]]) becomes a **merchant-financing partner on Teya's platform** for merchants in **Croatia and Hungary** (CEE). The product is a **merchant cash advance (MCA)**: pre-approved, personalised offers computed from the merchant's **real-time performance data inside Teya**, repayments **linked to monthly revenue**, funds disbursed in a claimed ~**3 minutes**. Use-of-funds: equipment, marketing, etc.
- **The traction figures (the one genuinely useful datapoint).** "During the early stages," **>500 Teya merchants** have received financing, with a self-reported **NPS of 86**. That is a small, early number — a pilot/ramp, not scale. NPS is a satisfaction metric, not a credit/economics metric (no loss rate, no funded volume, no take-rate, no average ticket disclosed).
- **finmid's role.** Joins **Teya's pan-European marketplace** as a **full-stack financing partner** — underwriting, compliance, servicing AND **capital provision**. This matters: in prior finmid framing capital came from "the platform, its bank, or finmid's refinancing partners"; here finmid is described as providing capital, implying it (or its refi partners) carries the MCA book, not Teya.
- **Why structured this way / what it reveals.** This is **not Teya's first SME-financing partner — it's at least its third**, each a different vendor for a different geography: **YouLend** for UK cash advances (Jan 2026, see [[Teya chooses YouLend to power UK SME cash advances]]), **Flowpay** for Czechia/Slovakia with Hungary+Croatia flagged "later in 2026" (Mar 2026, see [[Flowpay partners Teya to expand SME financing in Europe]]), and a Liberis+Teya tie-up also surfaced (CZ/SK). So Teya runs a **multi-vendor financing marketplace**, slotting the best-fit lender per market rather than committing to one rail. The PR frame ("Teya + finmid bring financing to CEE") hides that finmid **displaces/overlaps Flowpay's own stated Croatia/Hungary plan** — i.e. Teya picked finmid over (or alongside) Flowpay for exactly the markets Flowpay said it would enter. The real signal is **Teya's lender-agnostic marketplace strategy**, not the specific finmid pairing.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Two competitive planes here.
- **finmid's rivals (embedded-lending infra):** **YouLend** — the scaled leader (370k businesses, ~$230M FY revenue, powers Amazon/eBay; and already a *Teya partner in the UK*, so a direct rival inside Teya's own ecosystem); **Flowpay** (AI/data MCA, Teya's CEE incumbent partner, acquired Tapline); **Liberis** (now bought by Qred/Nordic Capital — consolidation); **Defacto**, **Banxware** (UniCredit-backed), **Wayflyer**, **Worldpay** embedded lending. finmid's edge is marquee mobility/delivery logos (Bolt, Wolt, Delivery Hero, Just Eat Takeaway, FREENOW) + 30-market single-API reach; its weakness vs YouLend is scale (orders of magnitude on revenue/businesses funded).
- **Teya's rivals (SME acquiring + embedded finance):** **SumUp**, **myPOS** (itself a finmid partner in Italy), **Adyen**, **Nexi/SIA**, **Revolut Business**. The MCA-on-acquiring playbook (advance against card takings, repay from sales) is the standard Square/SumUp/YouLend model — **nothing new mechanically**.
- **Why the lay of the land is this way + second order.** Everyone converges on "platform lends to its SMEs from its own transaction data." The differentiator is **(a) distribution** (whose platform you sit in) and **(b) cost/ownership of capital**. Teya has solved (a) — it owns 80k+ merchant relationships — so it commoditises the *lenders* by running a bake-off per market. **Second-order:** this makes the embedded-lender a **swappable supplier** to Teya; finmid winning Croatia/Hungary today can be replaced tomorrow (as Flowpay effectively was for these markets). The durable margin accrues to whoever owns the **merchant relationship + data (Teya)** or the **cheapest balance sheet**, not the interchangeable underwriting rail.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Teya:** founded 2019 (ex-**SaltPay**, StoneCo-linked founders), ~$760M+ raised; payments + business accounts + expense tools for **80k+ merchants across 9 European markets** (UK, Croatia, Czechia, Hungary, Iceland, Italy, Portugal, Slovakia, Spain); regulated UK + EEA; expanded to Spain/Italy Feb 2026 (see [[London fintech Teya launches in Spain and Italy]]).
- **Teya's financing timeline:** YouLend/UK (Jan 2026) → Flowpay/CZ-SK (Mar 2026, with CEE expansion promised) → **finmid/HR-HU (Oct 2026)**. A deliberate, market-by-market build-out of embedded credit on top of acquiring.
- **finmid:** founded 2021, Berlin, ex-N26; 30 EU markets, >€4B financing **offers** (not drawn); Oct 2026 extension pushed into vehicle finance (Bolt) and marketplaces (Skroutz) and Italy via myPOS.
- **Why the company acts this way.** Teya's core acquiring is a **thin, commoditised take-rate business**; to lift revenue-per-merchant and stickiness it layers **lending, accounts, expense mgmt** on the same relationship — classic acquirer→software/financial-services margin expansion (same logic as SumUp, Square). Using a **marketplace of external lenders** lets Teya monetise credit **without a banking licence, balance sheet, or credit-risk capital** — it takes referral/platform economics and offloads underwriting + capital to finmid/YouLend/Flowpay. finmid, conversely, needs platform distribution to deploy its rail; the fit is logical but **asymmetric in Teya's favour**.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Is it new?** Mechanically **no** — MCA against real-time acquiring data, repay-from-sales, is the standard embedded-SME-lending product (YouLend/Flowpay already do exactly this *inside Teya*). The novelty is narrow: **finmid as the vendor** and **Croatia/Hungary** as new geographies, plus finmid acting as **full-stack incl. capital** (vs pure orchestrator elsewhere).
- **Traction — real but tiny.** >500 merchants financed is a genuine, disclosed, drawn-down number (better than finmid's usual "€4B in offers" vanity metric), but it's **early-stage/pilot scale** against Teya's 80k base (<1% penetration). NPS 86 is satisfaction, not credit performance; **no funded volume, loss rate, average ticket, or renewal data** for the Teya book specifically (finmid's generic "80% renew / +45% sales / −70% churn" stats are **self-reported marketing**, not Teya-specific).
- **Who captures the margin (the real question).** Teya owns the merchant + the data + the distribution; finmid supplies swappable underwriting/servicing and (here) capital, bearing the credit risk. So **finmid eats the risk for a lender's spread; Teya takes platform/referral economics with little risk** — the durable, high-multiple position is Teya's. For finmid the Teya deal is incremental logo/volume but **structurally thin** (it's one of several interchangeable partners on the platform, directly alongside rival YouLend).
- **Value-add verdict.** Real, modest incremental distribution for finmid and a sensible CEE credit-access fill for underserved SMEs — but **not a category-defining event**; it's a routine partner-slot in Teya's multi-vendor financing marketplace.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Plans.** finmid framed as rolling the MCA into "EU markets where it wasn't previously available," starting **Croatia, Hungary, and Portugal** (Portugal overlapping Teya's home-ish CEE/Iberia footprint). Expect Teya to keep adding/swapping lenders per market.
- **Backdrop/sentiment.** European embedded lending is in a **consolidation + bank-tie-up phase** (Liberis→Qred/Nordic Capital; Banxware←UniCredit; Defacto chasing a banking licence; YouLend forward-flow facilities). CEE SME-credit gap (thin credit histories, weak collateral, slow banks) is a genuine, large structural opportunity — the demand side is real.
- **Risks.** (1) **finmid is disintermediable** — Teya can swap it (as it did Flowpay for these markets); concentration of value with the platform, not the rail. (2) **Credit cycle** — an MCA book priced off recent sales is pro-cyclical; CEE SME defaults in a downturn hit finmid's (or its refi partners') balance sheet. (3) **Channel conflict inside Teya** — finmid sits beside rival YouLend/Flowpay/Liberis, so pricing pressure is built-in. (4) **Regulatory** — consumer/SME-lending rules differ across Croatia/Hungary/Portugal; MCA is "introduced to EU markets where it was not previously available," so legal novelty = execution risk.
- **Why the market goes this way / second order.** Acquirers are racing to become **full financial-OS for SMEs**, and the fastest path is a **lender marketplace** rather than building a bank. **Counter-intuitive second-order effect:** the more lenders Teya onboards, the **more commoditised and fragile each lender's position** becomes — value concentrates in the distribution owner (Teya), which is exactly why the embedded-lending infra layer is consolidating.

### Red-team / challenge questions

1. **Is this a NEW event or a reprint?** FRESH. It is a new partner (finmid), new markets (Croatia/Hungary), with new drawn-down traction (>500 merchants, NPS 86) first announced 2026-10-22. It is NOT the same event as the Flowpay/Teya (Mar 2026, [[Flowpay partners Teya to expand SME financing in Europe]]) or YouLend/Teya (Jan 2026, [[Teya chooses YouLend to power UK SME cash advances]]) notes — those are different vendors/markets. Verdict: **fresh**.
2. **Is it mechanically new?** No. Merchant cash advance off real-time acquiring data, repay-from-sales, is the standard Square/SumUp/YouLend/Flowpay embedded-lending product. Novelty = vendor + geography only. Open/low.
3. **Is finmid really the first to bring financing to Teya in CEE?** No — Flowpay (Mar 2026) explicitly said it would expand into Hungary and Croatia "later in 2026." So finmid overlaps/displaces Flowpay's stated plan for these exact markets. The PR's "bringing financing to CEE" framing is overstated.
4. **How many financing partners does Teya run?** At least three-four: YouLend (UK), Flowpay (CZ/SK), Liberis (surfaced), now finmid (HR/HU). Teya runs a multi-vendor lender marketplace, not an exclusive. This is the real story.
5. **Who carries the credit risk / capital?** finmid is described as full-stack incl. "capital provision," implying finmid (or its refi partners) holds the MCA book, not Teya. So Teya takes near-riskless platform economics; finmid eats the credit risk for a lender's spread. Confirms margin asymmetry in Teya's favour.
6. **Is ">500 merchants" scale or pilot?** Pilot. <1% of Teya's ~80k merchant base. Explicitly "early stages." Not evidence of scaled adoption.
7. **Is NPS 86 a meaningful metric?** Partly — satisfaction only. No funded volume, loss rate, average ticket, APR/factor rate, or renewal data for the Teya book. Credit economics undisclosed.
8. **Are finmid's "+45% sales / 80% renew / −70% churn" stats verified?** No — finmid-sourced programme-wide marketing, not Teya-specific or independently audited. Treat as PR.
9. **Can finmid be swapped out?** Yes — demonstrably, since Teya slotted finmid into markets Flowpay had claimed. finmid is a disintermediable supplier; its position inside Teya is weak/contingent.
10. **How far behind YouLend is finmid?** Orders of magnitude (YouLend 370k businesses, ~$230M revenue vs finmid undisclosed on €4B *offers*). And YouLend is already a Teya partner — a direct rival inside the same ecosystem.
11. **Right finmid / right Teya?** Yes — Berlin embedded lender finmid (ex-N26) and ex-SaltPay Teya; "Bolt" in prior finmid notes = Estonian mobility, not the US checkout Bolt. No entity confusion here.
12. **Is the capital/funding model for the MCA book disclosed?** No forward-flow/warehouse facility named for the Teya book. finmid's general model (platform/bank/refi partners) applies; specifics open.
13. **Regulatory novelty risk?** finmid says MCA is "introduced to EU markets where it was not previously available" (HR/HU/PT) — so legal/structuring is new per market = execution/compliance risk. Open.
14. **Second-order downside trigger?** The more lenders Teya onboards, the more each is commoditised; value concentrates with the distribution owner (Teya), and the embedded-lending infra layer keeps consolidating (Liberis→Qred, Banxware←UniCredit). Fragility sits with the rail, not the platform.
15. **Does this change the central question?** Yes: not "did finmid win a partner" but **"in a platform-owned lender marketplace, who captures durable margin — the swappable underwriting rail (finmid) or the merchant-relationship/data owner (Teya)?"** Answer leans to Teya.

**Importance: 3/5 — rationale:** A real, dated partnership with genuine (if tiny) drawn-down traction (>500 merchants, NPS 86) between a credible acquirer (Teya, 80k merchants) and a well-funded embedded lender (finmid, €52M). More than noise, and a clean illustration of the acquirer-as-lender-marketplace trend. But mechanically unoriginal (standard MCA), pilot-scale (<1% penetration), with finmid in a structurally weak/swappable position (it overlaps/displaces Flowpay in the same markets and sits beside rival YouLend), and no credit economics disclosed. Fresh, mid-weight — not a category-defining or market-moving event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** European SME embedded lending — financing offered inside the software/payments platform a merchant already uses, repaid as a share of card/revenue flow. Market is small but growing fast: Europe's embedded-lending segment ~USD 2.87bn in 2026, ~25% of the broader embedded-lending market (per Global Growth Insights, as of 2026); the wider European alt-SME-lending pool is ~USD 151bn (+13.5% CAGR to ~USD 221bn by 2029, per useLuminix/market databooks, 2026). Demand pull is a persistent structural SME funding gap — ~EUR 39bn/yr of unmet SME debt demand in the EU (cited via EU Agenda/SME finance studies), worse in CEE where thin credit files, weak collateral and slow bank processes exclude otherwise-viable merchants (the note's own framing). Why now: acquirers/platforms already hold real-time cash-flow data, so performance-based underwriting is cheap at the margin and funds can be disbursed in ~3 minutes — a speed/cost edge legacy banks cannot match. Structure: fragmented — many embedded-lending rails (finmid, YouLend, Flowpay, Liberis, Wayflyer, Parafin) competing to plug into a smaller set of distribution platforms (acquirers, marketplaces, delivery apps). Value is migrating to whoever owns the merchant relationship + data; the balance-sheet/underwriting layer is increasingly a swappable supplier.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: financed volume / advance size, disbursement speed, approval/conversion rate, default & loss rate, renewal rate, platform churn reduction. finmid cites merchant sales +up to 45%, NPS >80, 80% renewal, and –70% platform churn (finmid, via The Paypers 2026-10-22) — retention metrics, notably NOT loss rates or take-rate. Basis of competition: distribution (how many platforms you embed into) + underwriting breadth + cost of capital. finmid = one API across 30 European markets, already live with Bolt, Wolt, Delivery Hero, Just Eat Takeaway, Guesty, FREENOW; it recently raised a EUR 17m Series A extension (total EUR 52m) and extended into 4-yr vehicle asset-finance for Bolt fleets ([[Finmid raises €17M extension, strikes Bolt financing deal]], 2026-10-06). Teya's position: a mid-scale pan-European acquirer — >80,000 merchants across nine markets (UK, Croatia, Czechia, Hungary, Iceland, Italy, Portugal, Slovakia, Spain), regulated in UK + EEA — acting as the DISTRIBUTION side, not the lender. Its moat is the merchant base + payment-flow data (switching costs once financing is bundled), not credit capability. Notably Teya is multi-sourcing its financing rails: YouLend for UK cash advances ([[Teya chooses YouLend to power UK SME cash advances]], 2026-01), Flowpay for parts of Europe up to EUR 100k ([[Flowpay partners Teya to expand SME financing in Europe]], 2026-03), now finmid for Croatia/Hungary/Portugal — a marketplace-of-lenders strategy rather than a single exclusive partner.

**Comps & multiples.** Internal comps (all embedded-lending rails behind platforms): [[Finmid raises €17M extension, strikes Bolt financing deal]] (EUR 52m total raised; valuation not disclosed), [[Teya chooses YouLend to power UK SME cash advances]], [[Flowpay partners Teya to expand SME financing in Europe]], [[Verifone launches Commander Capital with YouLend]], [[YouLend secures $225 million forward-flow facility from Värde Partners]] (the capital-stack layer behind these rails). Multiples: no revenue/EV multiple is computable — this is a commercial partnership, not a round; finmid's post-money is undisclosed and Teya is private. Teya IR grounding (Companies House / cityam, via ir_latest.json): Teya Europe FY2024 UK turnover ~GBP 19m (roughly doubled YoY) with pre-tax loss narrowing from ~GBP 110m to ~GBP 64m; these are UK-only statutory accounts, NOT group figures (the company declined to disclose group numbers), so they understate the business and cannot be annualised into a clean multiple. Third-party private-market trackers quote Teya valuations spanning ~USD 1bn to ~USD 2.4bn (Aug 2026, per multiples.vc/PitchBook-type sources) — too wide and unverified to anchor a comp. Distribution not computed; qualitative comparison only. [UNSOURCED]: financing GMV, loss rates, take rate on this programme.

**Risk flags.** (1) Supplier/disintermediation risk for the lenders, not Teya: finmid is one of at least three financing partners Teya runs in parallel, so each rail is replaceable and competes on price of capital — margin accrues to whoever owns the merchant, i.e. Teya. (2) Credit/cycle risk is unpriced in the PR: a merchant-cash-advance model underwritten on recent sales performs well in a benign cycle but is untested across a CEE downturn; the "500 merchants / NPS 86" framing discloses adoption and satisfaction but is silent on defaults, loss rates and who holds first-loss (finmid states it "covers... capital provision," implying balance-sheet/forward-flow exposure sits with finmid). (3) Concentration/early-stage scale: ">500 merchants" out of Teya's 80,000+ base is a pilot, in two to three small CEE markets — execution and unit economics are unproven at scale. (4) Regulation: MCA/revenue-based repayment sits in an evolving EU consumer-/SME-credit perimeter; cross-border scaling (Croatia, Hungary, Portugal) multiplies compliance surface.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Teya is deliberately becoming a lender-agnostic financing marketplace — stacking YouLend, Flowpay and finmid by geography/product rather than betting on one rail — which turns embedded SME credit into a commoditised input and concentrates bargaining power with the acquirer/data owner. Thesis: the durable value in European embedded lending accrues to distribution (merchant + cash-flow data), and the underwriting/balance-sheet layer compresses to utility economics. Watch: whether finmid/YouLend ever win exclusivity or attach-rate disclosure from Teya (would falsify the thesis), and the first disclosed loss/default or renewal figures beyond the pilot — the real test of whether 3-minute CEE underwriting is sound credit or just fast origination.
