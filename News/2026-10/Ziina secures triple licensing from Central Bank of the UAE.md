In a significant regulatory milestone for the Middle Eastern fintech ecosystem, consumer and enterprise financial platform Ziina has secured its Retail Payment Services Category III license and gained regulatory authorization to conduct Exchange Business Category IV activities from the Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE) . With these approvals, Ziina becomes the first homegrown UAE financial technology company to hold regulatory permissions spanning stored value facilities, retail payment services, and digital currency exchange. 
 The dual authorization marks the completion of Ziina’s phased licensing trajectory with the CBUAE, providing the company with an independent regulatory perimeter to operate a comprehensive financial platform . By combining these authorizations into a unified infrastructure, Ziina can offer an integrated suite of services—spanning consumer deposits, merchant acceptance, domestic real-time settlement, and cross-border money transfers—within a single proprietary application layer . 
 Rather than relying on third-party sponsorship or fragmented intermediary partnerships, Ziina’s newly expanded licensing structure gives the company direct regulatory oversight across three core operational pillars : 

 Stored Value Facility (SVF): Originally granted by the CBUAE in June 2024, the SVF license authorizes the firm to hold customer balances, operate digital wallets, and safeguard fiat funds . 


 Retail Payment Services (Category III): Expands the platform’s merchant acquiring, payment aggregation, and domestic fund transfer capabilities . The license provides regulatory authorization for Ziina’s commercial acceptance tools, including online payment gateways, customizable payment links, QR code transactions, and soft-POS Tap to Pay functionality across iOS and Android devices . 


 Exchange Business (Category IV): Formally authorized under its SVF framework in August 2026, this permission allows Ziina to facilitate international money transfers and multi-currency foreign exchange digitally, enabling users to remit funds cross-border directly through the Ziina app . 

 “We’re thankful for the continued support of the Central Bank of the UAE,” stated Faisal Toukan, co-founder and CEO of Ziina . “These licenses give us the regulatory framework to build faster and go further . When we launched Ziina in the UAE, we believed that we could build the next generation of financial products from this market, for the region . Today, people use different apps and providers for different parts of their financial lives . We want to bring more financial services into one platform, from spending and holding money to sending it abroad . Our ambition is to take what we build here to enable financial freedom across the Arab world .” 
 Founded in 2020 by Faisal Toukan, Sarah Toukan , and Talal Toukan , Ziina has evolved from a peer-to-peer mobile payments provider into a critical operational engine for the UAE business sector . The platform currently serves more than 500,000 active retail and enterprise customers annually and processes over $1.5 billion in annualized transaction volume . 
 Significantly, the platform is now utilized by one in ten small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) across the UAE, supported by its transparent pricing model and native merchant features such as the Ziina Card (compatible with Apple Pay and Google Pay ) and the Ziina Violet lifestyle membership offering fee-free foreign transactions . 
 Ziina’s expanded regulatory status coincides with rapid growth across the UAE’s domestic payment infrastructure . According to the CBUAE’s Annual Report 2025, retail fund transfers executed through the UAE Funds Transfer System (UAEFTS) reached AED 9.9 trillion across 114.9 million transactions in 2025 . This represents an annual increase of 19.9 per cent in total transaction value and 17.8 per cent in transaction volume, reflecting an accelerating regional migration toward cashless rails and instant digital settlement . 
 By combining direct regulatory licensing with full-stack merchant and consumer capabilities, Ziina is establishing a scalable blueprint to capture regional market share as digital payments, commercial acquiring, and international remittances continue to consolidate into unified platforms across the Arab world .

## Контекст

### [0] What happened
On 2026-10-22, Dubai-based fintech **Ziina** announced it had secured a **Retail Payment Services (RPS) Category III** license and authorization to conduct **Exchange Business (Category IV)** activities from the **Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE)**. Combined with its pre-existing **Stored Value Facility (SVF)** license (granted June 2024), this makes Ziina the first *homegrown* UAE fintech to hold regulated permissions spanning stored value, retail payments, and digital-currency/FX exchange in one stack. ([The Fintech Times](https://thefintechtimes.com/ziina-secures-triple-cbuae-licensing-across-payments-stored-value-and-exchange/), [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2026/10/uaes-ziina-secures-central-bank-approvals-retail-payments-exchange-services))

### [1] The three licenses (what each enables)
- **Stored Value Facility (SVF)** — granted June 2024. Authorizes holding customer balances, operating digital wallets, and safeguarding fiat funds. Ziina was the first VC-backed UAE firm to hold this (Aug 2024 milestone). ([CBUAE SVF Regulation](https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/en/rulebook/stored-value-facilities), issued Nov 2020; AED 15M min paid-up capital).
- **Retail Payment Services (RPS) Category III** — new. Covers merchant acquiring, payment aggregation, domestic fund transfers; underpins Ziina's online gateways, payment links, QR, and soft-POS Tap to Pay (iOS/Android). RPS falls under the CBUAE **RPSCS Regulation** (issued July 2021); categories scale by services/fund-holding, capital from AED 100K (Cat IV) up to AED 3M (Cat I). ([CBUAE RPSCS Regulation](https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/en/rulebook/retail-payment-services-and-card-schemes-regulation))
- **Exchange Business (Category IV)** — authorized Aug 2026 under the SVF framework. Enables digital international money transfers and multi-currency FX / cross-border remittance directly in-app.

### [2] Company context & scale
- Founded 2020 by **Faisal Toukan** (CEO), **Sarah Toukan**, and **Talal Toukan**; Y Combinator alum; evolved from P2P phone-number payments into an SME/consumer platform.
- Scale per the announcement: **500,000+** active retail+enterprise customers annually, **$1.5B+** annualized transaction volume; used by ~**1 in 10 UAE SMEs**. (Note: earlier 2024 Series A coverage cited ~AED 1.35B annualized volume — figures have grown materially since.)
- Funding: **$22M Series A** (Sept 2024), led by Altos Ventures; incl. Fintech Collective, FJ Labs, Avenir Growth, Y Combinator, Activant, MEVP, Jabbar Internet Group. ([Forbes Middle East](https://www.forbesmiddleeast.com/innovation/startups/uaes-ziina-secures-$22m-in-funding-to-shift-from-payments-to-full-financial-services))
- Products referenced: Ziina Card (Apple/Google Pay), Ziina Violet lifestyle membership (fee-free FX).

### [3] Why it matters / strategic read
The triple license gives Ziina an **independent regulatory perimeter** — it no longer depends on third-party sponsor banks/BIN sponsors or fragmented intermediaries. That means owning the full stack: deposits/wallets (SVF) → merchant acceptance + domestic real-time settlement (RPS) → cross-border remittance/FX (Exchange). CEO Toukan frames it as building "the financial platform for the Arab world." Timed against a cashless surge: CBUAE Annual Report 2025 reports UAEFTS retail transfers of **AED 9.9T across 114.9M transactions** (+19.9% value, +17.8% volume YoY).

### Red-team / challenge

1. **Is "triple licensing" genuinely three separate licenses, or marketing?** — PARTLY SPIN. It is two *new* CBUAE actions (RPS Cat III license + Exchange Business Cat IV authorization) layered on the pre-existing June-2024 SVF. The Exchange authorization is granted *under* the SVF framework, not a standalone license. "Triple" counts the activity scope, not three freshly-issued license certificates. The Fintech Times headline/body itself calls it a "dual authorization" completing a "phased trajectory." [Open — source wording inconsistent]

2. **Is "first homegrown UAE fintech" to span all three verifiable?** — ANSWERED (per sources, self-reported). Multiple outlets (Wamda, Mondovisione, Zawya release) repeat the claim; it is a company assertion, not independently audited. Plausible given CBUAE's granular regime, but "first" claims are marketing-grade. [Open]

3. **Are the scale numbers (500K customers, $1.5B volume) credible?** — PARTIAL. They are self-reported in the press release, unaudited. They jumped sharply from 2024 Series A figures (~AED 1.35B annualized). Direction is plausible but magnitude unverifiable externally. [Open]

4. **"1 in 10 UAE SMEs use Ziina" — real penetration or framing?** — OPEN. Self-reported; denominator (active vs registered SMEs) undefined. Treat as directional.

5. **Does the new license remove bank-sponsor dependency entirely?** — MOSTLY. SVF+RPS give direct wallet + acquiring rights, reducing BIN-sponsor reliance. But card issuance (Ziina Card) still runs on Visa scheme rails and likely a sponsor/issuer-processor relationship. "Independent regulatory perimeter" ≠ zero third parties.

6. **Capital requirements met?** — ANSWERED (implied). SVF carries ~AED 15M min paid-up capital; RPS Cat III also carries scaled capital. Having the license implies compliance, but Ziina's balance-sheet capitalization isn't disclosed. [Open]

7. **Timeline consistency?** — ANSWERED. SVF June 2024; Exchange Cat IV Aug 2026; RPS Cat III announced Oct 2026. Internally consistent across The Fintech Times and Wamda.

8. **Is this old news repackaged?** — NO. SVF (2024) was prior; the RPS Cat III + Exchange authorization are the fresh 2026 milestone. No existing vault note reports the triple license (prior notes cover card, Open Finance, Violet, Virtuzone). Fresh.

9. **Competitive threat realistic?** — PARTIAL. Competes with bank-backed wallets (e.g. Botim/e&), BNPL (Tamara), and foreign PSPs entering UAE (Wise, Airwallex). Full-stack licensing is a differentiator but incumbents + Aani/UAEFTS instant rails compress margins.

10. **Cross-border FX / remittance — how material vs incumbents?** — OPEN. UAE remittance is dominated by Al Ansari, LuLu, Western Union. Ziina's Exchange Cat IV enables digital remittance but market-share capture unproven.

11. **Any regulatory conditions/restrictions attached?** — OPEN. Press coverage is uniformly positive; CBUAE conditions, phased go-live, or volume caps (common with new RPS licensees) are not disclosed.

12. **Does this trigger near-term revenue?** — OPEN. Licensing enables products but doesn't itself monetize; execution on merchant acquiring + remittance determines upside.

13. **Source quality?** — MIXED. Primary driver is a company press release syndicated (Zawya, Mondovisione) + trade press (The Fintech Times "AI level 1", Wamda). No independent regulator confirmation link; CBUAE public register not cited.

14. **Does the vault note's title overstate?** — MINOR. "Triple licensing from CBUAE" is defensible shorthand but conflates one license + two authorizations under three activity pillars.

**Importance: 3/5** — A real, well-covered regulatory milestone for a notable homegrown UAE fintech (first full-stack domestic licensing), with concrete scale claims. Capped below 4 because figures are self-reported, "triple/first" is marketing-framed, SVF was prior, and there's no capital markets / M&A / systemic impact — it's a single-country platform-enablement story.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** UAE payments is a large, structurally expanding market: the broad UAE payments market was ~$201.8bn in 2025, projected to ~$213.4bn in 2026 and ~$274.8bn by 2031 at ~5.2% CAGR (per Mordor Intelligence, as of 2025); the narrower UAE fintech market is cited at ~$4.8bn in 2026 (per Markwide Research — treat as indicative, secondary estimate). Demand signal is in the note itself: CBUAE's Annual Report 2025 reports UAEFTS retail fund transfers of AED 9.9tn across 114.9m transactions in 2025, +19.9% value / +17.8% volume YoY — an accelerating cash-to-digital migration ("why now"). Structure: the acceptance/wallet layer is fragmenting fast with both global PSPs (Adyen, Ant International) and homegrown players entering under a single CBUAE perimeter, but the SVF leg carries a hard capital barrier — AED 15m minimum paid-up capital plus a bank guarantee and aggregate capital funds ≥5% of e-money float (per CBUAE RPSCS/SVF framework, via licensing advisories 2026). That capital/regulatory moat is the key entry barrier — distribution and merchant network are where value is actually won.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/annualized transaction volume, active customers, take rate (per-transaction margin), and SME penetration. Ziina discloses 500k+ annual active retail+enterprise customers, $1.5bn annualized TPV, and 1-in-10 UAE SMEs on platform; take rate not disclosed → `[UNSOURCED]`. Basis of competition = distribution + merchant feature breadth (soft-POS Tap to Pay, payment links, QR, card), not price alone.
- Key players / recent moves (dated): Ant International obtained CBUAE Retail Payment Services + Card Schemes + SVF licenses (2026-10-14) — Antom acquiring + WorldFirst cross-border; Adyen licensed by CBUAE for local operations (2026-06-30); Wise received CBUAE product approval (2025-10); Tabby secured a UAE wallet licence extending beyond Saudi BNPL; incumbents Network International/Magnati (merged acquirers) hold the legacy merchant base.

Moat = regulatory licenses + SME distribution/switching costs; scale is still small vs global entrants ($1.5bn TPV). Catching-up on balance sheet/scale, ahead on local SME embeddedness.

**Risk flags.**
1. Scale gap vs well-capitalized entrants — Ant International and Adyen hold the same CBUAE licenses with far deeper balance sheets and global merchant demand; Ziina's licensing-first "moat" is quickly becoming table stakes, so the differentiator reverts to local SME distribution, where a price/feature war could compress an already-undisclosed take rate.
2. SVF capital & float-safeguarding burden — AED 15m paid-up capital + bank guarantee + ≥5% capital-against-float means growth in stored balances directly consumes regulatory capital; for a Series-A-stage company this ties expansion to further fundraising (dilution/financing risk).
3. Single-market concentration — ~all TPV is UAE-domestic; the new Exchange (Cat IV) remittance leg pushes into cross-border, but execution against entrenched remittance incumbents (and FX compliance) is unproven ("announced," not yet shown as material volume).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a new-entry / "graduation" story, not a re-rating: Ziina converts from a sponsored wallet into a directly-licensed full-stack platform, the same template Tabby/Tamara used to expand beyond their first product into deposits + cards + FX. Falsifiable thesis: if the Exchange license turns into disclosed cross-border remittance volume (and TPV re-accelerates from $1.5bn) within ~12 months, Ziina is a credible homegrown consolidator of UAE SME finance; what breaks it — Ant/Adyen/Tabby capturing UAE SME acquiring faster, leaving Ziina sub-scale as licenses commoditize. Trigger to watch: next funding round valuation and any disclosed remittance TPV.
