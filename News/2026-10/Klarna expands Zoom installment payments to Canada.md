**New York - October 20, 2026** - Klarna, the everyday finance network, today announced the expansion of its integration with Zoom, making its flexible payments options now available on Zoom Workplace in Canada.

The expansion reflects growing demand for flexible payments in subscription models - a market set to hit $1.44 trillion by 2030. Starting today, Canadians can pay for Zoom Workplace subscriptions through Klarna, in full or in four interest-free installments. The launch extends a relationship already live in the U.S., UK, and Europe, deepening Klarna's ties with major global platforms.

"We're proud of the relationship we've built with Zoom, and bringing it to Canada is a natural next step," said Steven Clarke, Head of Klarna Canada. "Now more people can use Klarna to manage a tool they rely on every day, paying in a way that works best for them."

“Klarna has proven to be a reliable solution as we've rolled out flexible payments across the U.S., UK and Europe," said Alex Doroftei, Head of Global Payments & Fraud at Zoom. “We're excited to extend that same trusted experience to our customers in Canada."

Over 1.2 million retailers trust Klarna's innovative solutions to drive growth and loyalty, including Uber, H&M, Saks, Sephora, Macy's, Ikea, Expedia Group, Nike and Airbnb. ## [0] Что случилось (суть события)

On **2026-10-20** Klarna (NYSE: KLAR) announced it extended its existing integration with **Zoom** to **Canada**: Canadians can now pay for **Zoom Workplace** subscriptions via Klarna — either in full ("Pay Now") or in **four interest-free installments** ("Pay in 4"). This is a *geographic add-on*, not a new partnership: the Klarna–Zoom tie-up was already **live in the U.S., UK and Europe**. Steven Clarke (Head of Klarna Canada) and Alex Doroftei (Head of Global Payments & Fraud, Zoom) provided the quotes. Framing: demand for flexible payments in **subscription/SaaS** billing, a market Klarna pegs at **$1.44T by 2030**.
Source: <https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-and-zoom-expand-to-canada>

## [1] Предыстория / как мы сюда пришли

- The Klarna–Zoom partnership **launched globally on 2024-10-31 across 16 markets** (US, UK, Sweden, Germany, Finland, Denmark, Netherlands, Austria, Belgium, France, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Portugal, Spain, Poland). At launch, "Pay Now" was everywhere but **"Pay Later" was only US, Sweden, Germany** — so Canada getting Pay-in-4 is a real functional extension, not just a flag swap. (<https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2024/11/231914-klarna-zoom-unveil-16-country-payment-partnership/>)
- Klarna has been **systematically pushing BNPL into subscriptions/SaaS and B2B** through 2026: see [[Klarna expands Fiserv partnership to enterprise merchants via Commerce Hub]] (enterprise acquiring reach) and [[TAP expands Klarna installment payments to four markets]] (installments as a cross-market default). FreshBooks SMB partnership (US + Canada) also landed 2026-10-21.
- Canada is an active Klarna growth market: Klarna expanded there with "Pay in 4" + a shopping app and local investment; [[Klarna deepens Wayfair partnership with 0% APR financing]] explicitly spans **U.S. and Canada**.
- Macro backdrop: [[Klarna reports Q1 2026 revenue up 44% to $1 billion]] and the post-IPO lockup story [[Klarna post-IPO lockup expires for early investors]].

## [2] Почему это важно (значимость)

- Low-dollar, incremental: one merchant (Zoom), one new country, one product (Pay-in-4). It does **not** move Klarna's P&L. But it is a clean data point on Klarna's **subscription-billing / SaaS BNPL** thesis — recurring software is a stickier, lower-fraud use case than discretionary retail.
- Reinforces Klarna's "**everyday finance network**" positioning (120M active consumers, 1.2M merchants, 3.8M txns/day per the release) and its **enterprise/SaaS flank** vs. a pure fashion-retail BNPL image.
- For Canada specifically it adds to Klarna's footprint in a market where **Affirm leads** (via Shopify + legacy PayBright) — Klarna is accreting merchant logos (Walmart Canada, Wayfair, now Zoom-CA) to close the gap.

## [3] Контекст рынка / конкуренты

- **Canada BNPL market**: ~US$9.3B in 2026, forecast ~US$15.5–16.75B by 2031. **Affirm is the incumbent leader** (Shopify integration + former PayBright), Afterpay present, **PayPal launched Pay-in-4 in Canada Nov 2025**. (<https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/02/04/3231841/28124/en/>)
- **Regulation**: Canadian BNPL is **not yet regulated like traditional credit** — consumer-protection gaps remain; provincial rules apply piecemeal. A benign regime that lowers Klarna's expansion cost today but is a latent risk.
- **Klarna financial state (post-IPO)**: IPO'd on NYSE at ~$40; **Q2 2026 revenue $1.042B (+27%), GMV $36.6B (+18%), net income $9M, adj. operating income $91M**; stock fell ~19% after Q2 on a cut 2026 outlook and traded ~$12.5 in early Oct 2026 — i.e., **well below IPO price**. 120M active consumers, 26 markets. (<https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/klarna-reports-second-quarter-2026-113400699.html>)
- The $1.44T "subscriptions by 2030" figure is Klarna's own marketing framing — treat as directional, not independently verified.

## [4] Что дальше / на что смотреть

- Whether Pay-Later (not just Pay-Now) rolls out to **more of the original 16 markets** — Canada suggests Klarna is gradually turning on financing per-geo.
- More **SaaS/subscription merchant logos** (FreshBooks already signed) — the real signal is category expansion beyond retail.
- Klarna's **next quarterly print** and KLAR price recovery vs. the ~$12.5 trough; management guidance credibility after the outlook cut.
- Any **Canadian BNPL regulatory** movement that would raise compliance cost.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Klarna is extending an existing Zoom BNPL integration (live US/UK/EU) to Canada, letting Canadians pay for Zoom Workplace subscriptions in full or in four interest-free installments (klarna.com, 2026-10-20). Market size: the Klarna PR cites a subscription-payments market "set to hit $1.44 trillion by 2030" — that is a broad subscription-economy figure, not Canada BNPL, so treat it as marketing framing, not a BNPL TAM. For the actual addressable market: Canada BNPL is ~US$9.3bn in 2026 growing ~13-14% p.a. to ~US$14-16bn by 2031-2035 (per multiple syndicated reports via GlobeNewswire/Yahoo Finance, Oct 2026) — small vs Klarna's $149-151bn global GMV guide. Structure: BNPL is fragmented and competitive (Klarna, Affirm, Afterpay/Cash App, PayPal, Zip) with low switching costs; value concentrates at the merchant-integration and underwriting layer, barriers are capital (balance-sheet to fund receivables) + regulation. **Why now:** the Bank of Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act supervisory regime took effect Sep 8, 2025, adding operational/safeguarding obligations that favor larger, compliance-heavy players (per RPAA coverage, 2026) — i.e. incumbency now has a regulatory moat. Second-order: this is a geo-extension of a *named-merchant* deal (Zoom), not a new product — incremental, not transformational.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: GMV, net take rate (revenue/GMV), credit-loss rate (% of GMV), transaction-margin dollars (TMD). Key players in Canada BNPL: Affirm (reportedly leads via Shopify + travel integrations), Klarna, Afterpay (Cash App/Block), PayPal Pay Later, Zip. Basis of competition: merchant distribution and checkout placement, not price (four-installment / interest-free is commoditized). Recent moves: Klarna has been serially extending named-merchant deals through 2026 — Wayfair 0% APR, M&S, The White Company, TAP (four markets) [[TAP expands Klarna installment payments to four markets]], all Oct 2026 — a distribution land-grab playbook. Protagonist position: Klarna is a global leader by scale (120m+ active consumers, 1.2m+ merchants per PR) but in *Canada specifically* appears to be catching up to Affirm's Shopify-anchored lead (analysis). Moat = network effects (consumer app + merchant base) and scale in underwriting data; the Zoom tie-in itself carries no exclusivity signal — economics and fraud-liability split are undisclosed (de-PR flag).

**Comps & multiples.** Public BNPL comps (verifiable):
- **Klarna (NYSE: KLAR):** market cap ~$4.9-7.4bn range in Oct 2026 (sources disagree on date; investing.com / companiesmarketcap, Oct 2026). - Internal comps: [[Klarna reports Q1 2026 revenue up 44% to $1 billion]], [[Klarna stock tumbles after Q4 2025 results]].

Flag: Klarna trades at ~1-2x sales vs Affirm's ~5.8x — a 3-4x discount. Only partly growth-explained (Affirm +33% rev vs Klarna +27%); the gap mostly reflects Klarna's thin margin (FY25 adj. op margin 1.9%) and the post-IPO de-rating (KLAR fell from a ~$40 IPO price / ~$15bn to ~$13 / ~$5bn). Not an automatic "cheap" call — it is the market pricing lower profitability and guidance cuts. Distribution: 2 comparable public names → qualitative comparison, median not computed. EV/EBITDA, P/E = no data (Klarna GAAP-loss-making, EPS $(0.79) FY25).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Incremental, not needle-moving.** A single-merchant (Zoom) geo-extension into a ~$9bn Canada BNPL market adds negligible GMV against a $149-151bn global guide — reads as PR cadence, not strategy; watch whether it converts to disclosed volume.
2. **Margin > growth is the real story (de-PR).** Klarna *trimmed* FY2026 GMV guidance to $149-151bn on German consumer weakness + FX while raising TMD guidance to $1.62-1.65bn (Q2 2026). Expansion PRs mask a top-line that is being guided *down*; the Zoom-CA headline distracts from softening core demand.
3. **Regulatory + credit.** Canada's RPAA regime and rising consumer-credit scrutiny raise compliance cost; and the underlying product is still interest-free installment credit — provisions are low now (0.52% of GMV) but are cycle-sensitive. No data on Canada-specific loss rates.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector: nothing structural — it confirms BNPL's shift from checkout-only toward recurring/subscription SaaS billing (Zoom Workplace), a modest TAM extension that pits Klarna against Affirm/PayPal for subscription rails (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Klarna's next earnings (Q3 2026) does *not* re-accelerate GMV despite this drumbeat of merchant wins, the expansion PRs are confirmed as noise over a demand-softening core. Trigger to watch: Q3 2026 GMV growth vs the trimmed 18% Q2 pace and any Canada/subscription disclosure.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
> The Zoom-Canada news is a product/geo expansion with no financials. This review grounds it in Klarna's latest REPORTED quarter — **Q2 2026** (reported 2026-08-18, ~2 months old → fresh). Figures from Klarna's own Q2'26 press release/earnings release (NYSE: KLAR), cross-checked vs public consensus and the Q2 call. Note: the pipeline IR DB (`ir_latest.json[klarna]`) only indexes up to the Q1 2026 release (2026-05-14) and a 2026-06-23 AGM 6-K — it LAGS the Q2 print; Q2 figures pulled from the EDGAR 6-K + press release directly.

**Verdict (headline read).** **BEAT on the quarter, but the print was overshadowed by a guidance CUT.** Q2 2026 revenue $1.042bn (+27% YoY; vs public consensus ~$992.8m → beat +~$49m / +~4.8%) · GAAP net income $9m (diluted EPS $0.01 vs consensus ~$(0.05) loss → beat ~$0.06) · drivers: U.S. GMV +27% YoY and transaction margin dollars +42% YoY; first positive quarterly net income · guidance action: FY2026 GMV & revenue guidance CUT (German consumer weakness + FX + IFRS 9 presentation change), but FY transaction-margin-dollar and adj. operating income guidance RAISED. Market reaction: shares fell ~19.4% to ~$15.72 on the day — investors focused on the volume/revenue cut over the earnings beat.

**Key figures (Q2 2026, with growth).**
- GMV: **$36.6bn (+18% YoY)**; U.S. GMV +27% YoY (the growth engine).
- Revenue: **$1.042bn (+27% YoY)** — revenue grew faster than GMV, i.e. monetization per dollar of volume rose. Revenue/GMV (gross take rate) ≈ **2.85%** vs ~2.63% implied a year ago (analysis).
- Transaction margin dollars: **$446m (+42% YoY)** — margin $ growing ~2.3x faster than GMV → improving unit economics / operating leverage. Transaction margin = 42.8% of revenue.
- Adjusted operating income: **$91m (+214% YoY)** vs $29m in Q2 2025.
- GAAP net income: **$9m** vs $(53)m loss in Q2 2025 — a $62m swing; first positive quarterly net income as a public company (milestone).
- Consumer credit losses (provisions): **0.52% of GMV vs 0.56% in Q2 2025** — provisions grew slower than volume → credit discipline improving (mgmt cites better underwriting, forward-flow arrangements, maturing Fair Financing book, more off-balance-sheet receivables).
- Active consumers: **120m (+8% YoY)**; merchants **1.2m+ (+54% YoY)** — merchant count accelerating much faster than consumer count.

**By segment / driver.** Growth is led by the U.S. (GMV +27% YoY vs group +18%), while Europe — Germany specifically — is the drag: management flagged materially weaker German consumer discretionary spend and assumed NO near-term recovery. The quality of the beat is good: it's margin- and credit-led (transaction margin $ +42%, provisions/GMV down 4bps), not a one-off. Merchant expansion (+54% YoY) and product monetization are outrunning raw volume — consistent with the thesis that Klarna is shifting from a pure-BNPL volume story to a higher-margin "everyday finance network."

**vs expectations / prior period.** Revenue $1.042bn beat public consensus of ~$992.8m by ~+4.8% (consensus as of pre-print Aug 2026, free aggregators). EPS $0.01 vs consensus ~$(0.05) — a clear bottom-line beat. YoY momentum strong across the board (+27% revenue, +42% margin $, +214% adj. op income). No prior Klarna earnings note exists in the base to `[[wikilink]]`; corpus semsearch unavailable (irdb/semsearch down, OpenRouter 402), so trend is sourced from the IR filing series instead (Q1'26 release 2026-05-14; Q4/FY25 release 2026-02-19).

**Guidance / forward.** **Mixed — the core of the sell-off.**
- FY2026 GMV **CUT** to $149bn–$151bn (from prior ">$155bn").
- FY2026 revenue **CUT** to $4.08bn–$4.16bn (from prior ">$4.34bn").
- FY2026 adjusted operating income **$280m–$300m** — >4x the $65m reported for FY2025 (profitability trajectory intact / effectively raised).
- FY transaction-margin-dollar guidance **RAISED** despite the lower volume/revenue.
- Q3 2026 guidance (softer, investment quarter): GMV $35bn–$36bn, revenue $940m–$980m, adj. operating income just **$5m–$15m** — mgmt framed Q3 as its largest-ever product-launch/investment period with benefits landing in Q4.
- Cut drivers per mgmt: (1) softer German consumer discretionary spending (no assumed recovery), (2) FX, (3) an IFRS 9 presentation change. Management tone: confident on profitability/margins, cautious/defensive on top-line volume in Europe.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. **Guidance cut on volume, not on profit** — Klarna is trading top-line growth for margin and credit quality (revenue guide down, but margin-$ and adj. op income guide up/held). Why it matters: the equity story is pivoting to profitable-growth; second-order — a slower-GMV, higher-margin Klarna is less exposed to BNPL credit cyclicality but more sensitive to monetization (membership, ads, subscriptions like this Zoom deal) sustaining take-rate.
2. **Germany / European consumer softness** is the swing risk — mgmt assumes no near-term recovery. Why it matters: Europe is the mature base; if the weakness spreads beyond Germany, the FY guide could be cut again. The U.S. (+27% GMV) and new-market expansion (e.g. this Canada/Zoom launch) are the offset — geo diversification is now thesis-critical, not cosmetic.
3. **Credit discipline is improving into a soft macro** (provisions 0.52% vs 0.56% of GMV) — counter-cyclical positive; shows the off-balance-sheet / forward-flow funding model is working. Watch whether this holds if European delinquencies rise.
4. **Read-through to the Zoom-Canada news:** subscription/recurring-payment integrations (Zoom Workplace, now Canada; already US/UK/EU) are exactly the higher-margin, lower-credit-risk, sticky B2B2C volume that supports the margin-up / GMV-down guidance pivot.
