 Visa, BILL and Billtrust Launch First Live Pilot for Trusted AI Agent Payments Between Buyer and Supplier Platforms
 
LAS VEGAS — Visa (NYSE: V), BILL (NYSE: BILL) and Billtrust today announced they have launched the first live pilot to enable trusted AI agents to initiate and complete approved buyer invoice payments end-to-end through supplier portals.
Until now, many payment portals relied on human authentication requirements that limited how approved payments could be automated. Together, with leading Accounts Payable and Receivable solutions providers BILL and Billtrust, the Visa Intelligent CommerceTM portfolio helps bridge that gap. Leveraging offerings such as Visa Trusted Agent Protocol, AI agents can now be securely authenticated to credentialed portals. As a result, buyer payments that previously relied on manual entry can be completed by trusted AI agents, while preserving supplier control and oversight.
How trusted AI agents are overcoming one of payment automation’s biggest barriers
The flow begins when the authorized BILL Payment Agent — registered using Visa’s Trusted Agent Protocol and Agentic Directory — uses tokenized payment credentials and controls aligned with the buyer’s approved payment instructions to issue payments on behalf of BILL's customers. Supported by capabilities within the Visa Intelligent Commerce (VIC) portfolio, this enables BILL to securely automate card payments for supplier invoices.
Once the payment is initiated, Billtrust leverages Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol to recognize, authenticate, and grant access to the authorized agent enabling BILL to complete the invoice payment on the customer's behalf. This agent authentication can satisfy the site protections historically provided by CAPTCHA and other bot blocking mechanisms, without requiring an additional agent login. As a result, Billtrust customers can receive fast portal payments in accordance with their acceptance policy, which can help accelerate cash flow.
“Businesses should not have to choose between automation and control when making payments,” said Mark Nelsen, SVP and Head of Product, Commercial and Money Movement Solutions, Visa. “This pilot applies agentic commerce to a real B2B-payment problem, by having trusted agents complete payments across platforms. Working with BILL and Billtrust, we are testing how identity, payment controls and transaction visibility can make that model work.”
Building trust into agent-initiated B2B payments
Drawing on capabilities within the Visa Intelligent Commerce portfolio, including Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol, the pilot enables:
A recognized and authenticated AI agent acting on behalf of an identified buyer.
Tokenized payment credentials rather than shared supplier-portal logins.
Payment controls tied to the customer’s approved payment instruction.
A purpose-built payment path tied to the specific invoice.
Visibility into the authorization and completion of the payment.
"Nearly 500,000 businesses trust BILL to move, manage and maximize their money, and that trust depends on removing friction without sacrificing accuracy and security of supplier payments. For too long, supplier portals have been friction points, requiring a person to manually complete what should be an automated step,” said Archana Prasad, SVP of Payments and Financial Services at BILL. “With Visa and Billtrust, BILL’s AI-powered payments technology is closing that gap: the payment gets done, the supplier gets paid faster, and finance teams get their time back."
For suppliers using Billtrust, the pilot creates a way to recognize and accept payments initiated by trusted business agents, helping release trapped cash while retaining the information needed to process and reconcile the payment within their existing payment acceptance policy. For buyers using BILL, automating this last manual step can give finance teams back the time they'd spend manually keying information into supplier payment portals.
“Agentic commerce will only move forward if businesses can trust agents to operate across systems with the same clarity and accountability they expect from people,” said Kunal Patel, SVP Payments, Billtrust. “By connecting buyer-side invoice approval with supplier-side payment acceptance, this pilot shows how authorized agents can complete business transactions, releasing trapped cash faster while adhering to a complex acceptance policy and preserving visibility and control for both sides.”
The pilot is currently live and processing end-to-end transactions with participating buyers using BILL and Billtrust suppliers. As the pilot progresses, Visa, BILL, and Billtrust will continue exploring opportunities in agentic payments. To learn more about Visa Intelligent Commerce and how Visa is building trust into agentic commerce, visit Visa.com .

# Context-enrichment: Visa, BILL and Billtrust launch first live pilot for trusted AI-agent B2B invoice payments
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 4/5._

**FRESHNESS / DUPLICATE VERDICT: FRESH.** Announced 2026-10-20 at Money20/20 (Las Vegas). This is the first *live* pilot of agentic payments applied to a **B2B buyer→supplier invoice** flow across two separate platforms (AP = BILL, AR = Billtrust). It is distinct from — though built on — the prior consumer/merchant-checkout work: [[Visa launches Trusted Agent Protocol for AI commerce]] (2025-10-17, the TAP spec itself, consumer checkout), [[Nuvei supports Visa Trusted Agent Protocol]] (2025-10, acquirer enablement), [[Highnote works with Visa on agentic commerce payments]] (2026-05), and the consumer trial [[Visa and Bank of China (Hong Kong) complete AI agent payment trial]] (2026-05). None of those cover AP/AR supplier-portal invoice automation. Not a duplicate. Mastercard's parallel push (Agent Pay / Agent Pay for Machines, Santander live in Europe) is a *competitor*, not the same event.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- Visa (NYSE: V), BILL (NYSE: BILL) and Billtrust (private, EQT-owned since 2022) launched a **live pilot** letting a "trusted AI agent" execute **already-approved** buyer invoice payments end-to-end through supplier payment portals, without a human logging into the portal.
- Mechanism: the **BILL Payment Agent** is registered via **Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol + Agentic Directory** (both inside the Visa Intelligent Commerce / VIC portfolio). It presents **tokenized card credentials** + controls tied to the buyer's pre-approved payment instruction. On the AR side, **Billtrust uses TAP to recognize/authenticate the agent and grant portal access** — this satisfies the CAPTCHA / bot-blocking that historically forced a human login. Supplier gets paid by card; buyer skips manual keying.
- **De-PR'd reality — what is genuinely done vs claimed:**
  - It is **live and processing end-to-end transactions** (PR's own words) — stronger than "aims to / will explore." But: **no numbers** — no TPV, no count of buyers/suppliers, no ticket size, no conversion. "Participating buyers/suppliers" = small closed set. So: live ≠ at-scale.
  - The agent is explicitly **NOT deciding what to pay** — it executes payments the buyer already approved. This is RPA-with-identity, not autonomous spend. The "AI agent" framing is heavier than the actual autonomy delta.
  - **Why structured this way:** the real unlock is not "AI decides payments" but **killing the human-in-the-portal step**. Supplier portals (and CAPTCHA) were deliberately built to block bots; TAP gives the bot a *cryptographic identity + card token* so the AR side can safely let it in. That is the actual novelty — an **authentication/identity bridge**, dressed as agentic commerce.
  - **Why card rails:** BILL monetizes by pushing suppliers onto card acceptance (interchange-rich) vs ACH. "Automating the last manual step" conveniently funnels more spend onto Visa card rails → aligns all three parties' economics. The PR's silence on who bears interchange is telling.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Mastercard Agent Pay / Agent Pay for Machines (AP4M)** — launched 2026; first live end-to-end European agent payment with **Banco Santander**. Mastercard leans on Agentic Tokens + dispute mgmt. Direct rail competitor; Visa's differentiator here is a **named B2B invoice use case with two real software platforms**, not a consumer checkout demo.
- **Stripe / OpenAI (ACP), PayPal (Agentic Commerce Services), Coinbase (x402/MCP), Google (AP2), Anthropic MCP** — all mostly **consumer/merchant checkout** ([[Stripe enables payments in ChatGPT with OpenAI]], [[PayPal launches Agentic Commerce Services for AI checkout]], [[Coinbase launches Payments MCP for AI agents]], [[Affirm to support Google's Agent Payments Protocol]]). B2B AP/AR agent payments is a **thinner field** — Visa is early here.
- **Position:** Visa is **ahead on the specific B2B-invoice/supplier-portal vector** (first live), roughly at parity with Mastercard on overall agentic rails. **Why the field is thin in B2B:** consumer checkout is a single merchant endpoint; B2B needs *both* an AP platform and an AR/portal network to cooperate — which is exactly why Visa needed BILL **and** Billtrust, not just a protocol spec.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Visa:** TAP launched Oct 2025 (Cloudflare co-dev) for consumer checkout; VIC is the umbrella. 2026 cadence: BofA HK consumer trial (May), Highnote (May), Agentic Ready expansion to Canada. This pilot is the **logical B2B extension of VIC** — Visa pushing identity/token rails into commercial payments, a higher-margin, lower-penetration segment vs mature consumer cards.
- **BILL:** FY2026 revenue ~$1.7bn (+13% YoY; core +16%), ~500k businesses, 37m transactions and ~$98bn TPV in Q4, ~1% of US GDP. Launched BILL AI (AI agents) in FY26; **175k+ businesses using AI agents**, AI spend +50% YoY. **Structural pressure:** BILL's growth has decelerated and it's recast as a takeover target; it **needs an AI/payments-monetization narrative** and more card-funnel volume — this pilot serves both.
- **Billtrust:** largest B2B buyer network (20m+ buyers, ~$1tn annual trade-credit volume); taken private by **EQT in 2022**. Its AR/portal network is the scarce asset that makes the agent-authentication step meaningful.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new:** first time an authenticated AI agent completes a **cross-platform B2B invoice payment** through a supplier portal using a protocol-level identity (TAP) instead of shared logins / stored portal passwords. Solves a real, unglamorous friction (manual keying into supplier portals).
- **Vs prior art:** earlier VIC items were consumer/merchant checkout; this moves the same identity+token primitive into AP↔AR. Prior "automation" of supplier payments = virtual cards / screen-scraping bots with shared credentials — brittle and a security liability. TAP replaces the shared login with a verifiable agent identity.
- **Traction = announcement-grade only.** "Live and processing" but **zero disclosed volume/clients** → treat as pilot, not adoption. (analysis)
- **Who captures the margin:** Visa captures the **identity/token rail + interchange** on every card payment pushed through; BILL/Billtrust capture **software + transaction fees** and get stickier. **Second-order:** the agent-identity layer (TAP + Agentic Directory) is the choke point — whoever owns the directory that AR networks trust owns the toll booth. Visa is positioning TAP as that registry, which is the durable prize, not this one pilot. (hypothesis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Plan: expand the pilot, "continue exploring opportunities in agentic payments." Expect volume/partner disclosures to be the real signal.
- Sentiment: agentic payments is the dominant 2026 fintech narrative; networks (Visa/Mastercard/Stripe/PayPal) are racing to set the identity standard. B2B is the less-crowded, higher-value flank.
- **Risks / silence:** no fraud-liability framing (who eats a mis-paid invoice by an agent?), no interchange economics, no exclusivity, no scale numbers. Regulatory: agent authentication + payment-mandate questions are unsettled. **Counterintuitive second-order:** standardizing agent identity could *commoditize* the AP software layer — if TAP+Agentic Directory become the neutral rail, BILL/Billtrust's moat shifts from "we automate payments" to "we own the buyer/supplier network," raising the question of **who in the stack keeps the margin once agents are table stakes.**

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Agentic B2B payments sits at the intersection of two large, growing pools: the B2B digital-payment market (~$8.8tn in 2025, CAGR ~12.9% 2026–2035, per GMInsights via gminsights.com) and AP/AR automation, where AP automation holds the largest segment share within B2B payments (per Fortune Business Insights). Structure: highly fragmented on the rails/portal layer — the key friction is that supplier payment portals historically demanded human authentication (CAPTCHA, manual re-keying), which blocked straight-through automation. Value in the chain accrues to whoever owns (a) the trusted-agent identity/credential standard and (b) the buyer and supplier networks. "Why now": card networks are racing to set agentic-payment standards — Mastercard launched Agent Pay (Apr 2025), Visa launched Trusted Agent Protocol (Oct 2025), Amex shipped an agentic developer kit (Apr 2026), and Google's Universal/Agent Payments Protocol was endorsed by Visa, Mastercard, Amex, PayPal, Stripe, Adyen and Worldpay (per paymentsdive.com, digitalcommerce360.com, 2025–2026). This pilot is the first *live* B2B application of Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol across both buyer and supplier platforms.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for the networks — payments volume, cross-border volume, processed transactions, value-added-services (VAS) revenue; for AP/AR SaaS — TPV, transactions, core revenue, take rate. Key players and basis of competition (standards + network reach, not price): Visa (Trusted Agent Protocol / Intelligent Commerce), Mastercard (Agent Pay, embedded in PayPal's wallet Oct 2025), Amex (agentic dev kit), plus AP/AR fintechs — BILL, Billtrust, Ramp (added AI agents to Bill Pay, [[Ramp adds AI agents to Bill Pay platform]]), Brex. Recent moves: BILL launched AI agents to automate AP ([[BILL launches AI agents to automate accounts payable]], Oct 2025); Visa launched TAP ([[Visa launches Trusted Agent Protocol for AI commerce]]) with ecosystem support ([[Nuvei supports Visa Trusted Agent Protocol]]) and expanded its Agentic Ready program to Canada ([[Visa expands Agentic Ready program to Canada]], May 2026). Protagonist position: Visa is *ahead* on standard-setting — it supplies the identity/credential rail (TAP + Agentic Directory) that both BILL (buyer side) and Billtrust (supplier side) plug into; moat = network effects + intangibles (the protocol becoming a de-facto trust layer). BILL and Billtrust gain distribution: BILL serves ~500k businesses, ~9m-member network, ~1% of US GDP in annual volume; Billtrust claims >20m buyers and $1tn annual trade-credit volume (per the release — PR figures, unaudited).

**Comps & multiples.** Visa (NYSE: V), market cap ~$790bn, FY-TTM revenue ~$44.5bn → P/S ~17.5x; forward P/E ~24.6–25x (per stockanalysis.com / gurufocus.com, as of Oct 2026) — rich but in-line with its historical premium and ~14% top-line growth; agentic payments are a thin incremental driver inside its $3.8bn/quarter VAS line, not yet a needle-mover. BILL (NYSE: BILL), market cap ~$3.77bn (Oct 13 2026), FY26 revenue $1,653.2m → P/S ~2.3x; EV/Revenue ~2.3x; forward P/E ~11.7x; GAAP-unprofitable so trailing P/E n/a (per stockanalysis.com, macrotrends.net). Arithmetic: P/S = $3.77bn / $1.65bn = 2.3x. Billtrust is private (taken private by EQT in 2022) → no public multiple, "no data". Qualitative: BILL trades cheap vs the card networks (2.3x P/S vs ~17x) but that reflects SaaS-vs-network economics and decelerating same-store TPV (+6% y/y in Q4 FY26), not a cleanly comparable set — networks vs AP-SaaS are different models, so only directional. Distribution not computed (fewer than 3 comparable public pure-plays).

**Risk flags.**
1. *Pilot, not production.* The release repeatedly says "pilot," "testing," "exploring" — economics (interchange/take rate on agent-initiated card payments), fraud-liability allocation and ticket-size limits are all unstated. De-PR: "live and processing" ≠ scaled or monetized.
2. *Fraud / liability in agent authentication.* Replacing CAPTCHA and human sign-off with tokenized agent credentials shifts the attack surface; who eats a fraudulent or misdirected agent payment (Visa, BILL, Billtrust, or the buyer) is silent — a second-order regulatory/chargeback exposure.
3. *Standards fragmentation / disintermediation.* Competing agentic standards (Mastercard Agent Pay, Google's protocol, Amex kit) risk a format war; if a neutral protocol (e.g. Google's) wins, Visa's TAP moat thins. For BILL/Billtrust, the card network captures the identity layer — the SaaS players risk being commoditized plumbing if the trust standard, not the workflow, becomes where value sits.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is the first credible bridge of agentic commerce from B2C checkout into the stickier B2B AP/AR portal problem — if it scales, it re-rates the "last manual step" (portal re-keying) as automatable, pulling card volume onto rails that previously ran ACH/check. Watch: (trigger) whether Visa/BILL disclose live transaction counts or a GA (general-availability) date, and whether supplier acceptance economics are published. Thesis breaks if the pilot stays a pilot through FY27 or if interchange on agent-initiated B2B card payments proves uneconomic vs ACH.
