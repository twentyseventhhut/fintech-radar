# Context-enrichment: Reuters — 7,000 humanoid robots sold worldwide (2025)
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

A one-line Reuters wire datapoint relayed by the "42 секунды" channel. **Correction up front:** the headline/digest says "в 2026", but the actual figure is for **2025** — it is the International Federation of Robotics (IFR) *first-ever* official humanoid tally, released ~Sept 21 2026 as part of its annual World Robotics stats. So the "2026" in our title is a relay error; the number is a 2025 actual, not a 2026 forecast.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- **~7,000 humanoid robots sold worldwide in 2025**, per IFR — the first year IFR broke humanoids out as a separate category (previously only industrial + service robots). Reuters framed the number as *tiny* vs. the hype.
- **Scale check:** 7,000 humanoids vs. ~**542,000 conventional industrial robots installed in 2024**. Humanoids are ~1.3% of that base — still essentially a pilot/R&D category.
- **Use mix:** most 2025 units went to **AI R&D**; factory-floor pilots exist but are single- to double-digit per site. IFR/Reuters note most humanoids still rely on **human teleoperation or preset programs** (echoes [[The Fintech Blue mapping the AI-lab trade onto robotics]]).
- **The growth setup (why it still matters):** Bank of America Global Research projects **~90,000 shipments in 2026 → 1.2 million by 2030**. Goldman Sachs: ~75,000 in 2026 → 890k by 2030 → 6.5M by 2035 (~$38–138bn market). Morgan Stanley (China-only) lifted 2026 from 28k→50k; global ~$5tn / >1bn robots by 2050. So 2025's 7,000 is the "year zero" baseline against a forecast ramp of 2–3 orders of magnitude.

## [1] Competitors / makers (who's building the volume)
- **Tesla (Optimus)** — targets 100k units in 2026 (initially internal-use); Gen 3 pilot production Fremont, mid-2026. Funded internally; Tesla raised 2026 capex guidance to **$25bn** (AI/robotaxi/robotics), ~3x prior year.
- **Figure AI** — #1 by capital: Sept 2025 Series C **$1bn at ~$39bn valuation** (NVIDIA, Intel, Qualcomm, Brookfield, T-Mobile, Salesforce), ~$1.9bn cumulative; BotQ line target 12,000/yr; BMW Spartanburg deployment.
- **Unitree (宇树)** — the volume leader: ~5,500 G1/H1 units shipped 2025, 10k–20k target 2026; IPO'd Shanghai Aug 2026 at a ~$50bn valuation.
- **1X** (~$10bn, OpenAI/Tiger/Samsung), **Apptronik** (~$5.5bn), **Agility Robotics** (Digit, warehouse logistics).
- **China angle:** ships more humanoids than the rest of the world combined and supplies the components; see [[The Fintech Blueprint Monzo's low ROE and the robotics investment trade]] (robotics ≈ $3.8tn aggregate value, mostly inside big tech/industrials, not pure-plays).

## [2] Why this is in a fintech digest (the investment angle)
- **The capital/deployment gap is the whole story.** Private valuations ($39bn Figure, ~$50bn Unitree, ~$10bn 1X) and capex ($25bn Tesla) are being set against **7,000 actual units sold** and mostly-teleoperated robots. That gap is a classic capital-markets setup — exactly the "AI-lab trade mapped onto robotics" thesis Lex Sokolin/Fintech Blueprint has been running ([[The Fintech Blue mapping the AI-lab trade onto robotics]], [[The Fintech Blueprint Monzo's low ROE and the robotics investment trade]]).
- **Financing/IPO pipeline:** Chinese humanoid IPO rush (Unitree etc.), Beijing slowing it; Reuters separately flagged "hype outruns reality." This note is the *demand-side reality check* that bounds those valuations.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty = the IFR first-ever official count** — gives a hard, audited denominator to a sector that trades on forecasts. The value-add is the **reality check**: 7,000 units is the floor against which every $39bn valuation and 1.2M-by-2030 forecast is implicitly priced.
- Traction is real but thin: deployments measured in single/double digits per site; the signal is the *slope* (forecasts), not the 2025 level.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Watch 2026 actuals vs. the 90k (BofA) / 75k (GS) shipment calls — the first real test of the ramp thesis.
- Watch Tesla Optimus 2026 volume (the 100k target is the single biggest swing factor) and Figure/Unitree line ramps.
- Sentiment: bullish-long-term, skeptical-near-term; the Reuters framing and Beijing's IPO cooling signal "hype outruns reality."

## Challenge / red-team

1. **Year error:** the note title says "2026" but the IFR/Reuters figure is for **2025** (first-ever IFR humanoid tally, published Sept 21 2026). The digest relay ("за 2026") is wrong — this is a 2025 actual, not a 2026 count. Flag before any downstream use.
2. Is 7,000 "sold" or "installed"? Reuters/IFR language is sales for industrial + professional service use; it excludes consumer/toy and excludes units in pure internal R&D at makers — definitions matter when comparing to forecasts.
3. Is 7,000 comparable to the 542,000 industrial-robot figure? No — the 542k is a **2024** installation number (different year, different category). The ~1.3% ratio is indicative, not exact.
4. Forecasts diverge wildly: BofA 90k (2026), GS 75k (2026), MS 50k (China only). Which methodology, and do they count the same unit definition as IFR? Likely not — do not stack them.
5. Tesla's "100k Optimus in 2026" is a Musk target, repeatedly slipped historically. Treating it as supply is optimistic; actual 2025 Tesla shipments were negligible.
6. Figure's $39bn valuation (Sept 2025) is a private-round mark, not a public price — illiquid and possibly stale by Oct 2026.
7. Unitree's ~5,500 units are largely low-cost G1 research/education units — not the warehouse/factory labor the forecasts monetize. Unit counts conflate very different price points.
8. "Most humanoids still teleoperated/preset" undercuts the labor-replacement thesis the valuations price in — how much of 2025 volume is genuinely autonomous?
9. Does this note add anything beyond the existing [[The Fintech Blue mapping the AI-lab trade onto robotics]] and [[The Fintech Blueprint Monzo's low ROE and the robotics investment trade]]? Those already cover the investment trade and Chinese IPOs; this adds only the IFR demand-side datapoint.
10. Fintech relevance is indirect — this is an industrial/AI robotics stat. Justified only as a capex/financing/valuation reality-check, not a fintech event per se.
11. Source for our note is a single-line Telegram relay (42 секунды), 0 full source text loaded — all substance here is external WebSearch, not the note's own sourcing.
12. Chinese shipment dominance claims come from secondary write-ups (ai-supremacy, TMTPost) — verify before quoting "more than rest of world combined."
13. The $3.8tn / $5tn robotics TAM figures are bank estimates spanning big-tech + legacy industrials, not humanoid pure-plays — easy to misquote as "humanoid market size."
14. Is the forecast ramp (7k→90k→1.2M) supply-constrained or demand-constrained? The thin 2025 real deployment suggests demand/autonomy is the bottleneck, which would delay the curve.

Importance: 2/5 — A real, audited first-of-its-kind datapoint (IFR humanoid tally) with a clean investment-reality-check angle, but: (a) only tangentially fintech, (b) a single-line relay with a year error in our title, (c) the investment thesis is already covered by two richer same-month Blueprint notes. Useful as a supporting stat, not a standalone story.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** The IFR tally (reviewed by Reuters, published 2026-09-21) counts ~7,000 humanoid robots sold worldwide — and these are **2025** industrial/professional-service units, the first year IFR broke humanoids out as an official category (the note's title says "2026"; the underlying data is for 2025). Context on size: the humanoid market is tiny today (~$2.9–6.2bn in 2026 across research firms — wide spread, treat as order-of-magnitude, not a hard TAM) against aggressive forward curves: Goldman Sachs raised its 2035 outlook ~5x to 6.5m units / ~$138bn; Bank of America Global Research projects ~90,000 units shipped in 2026 scaling to 1.2m by 2030 (per Reuters/BofA, as of 2026). The "why now": a step-change in VLA/embodied-AI models (Nvidia GR00T) plus battery/actuator cost curves falling below ~$10k/unit, and China (world's largest industrial-robot market) subsidizing domestic supply. For a fintech audience the angle is pure **capex & venture financing**: this is a pre-revenue capital-formation story — the 7,000 real units vs. million-unit forecasts is the gap funding the raises.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs run on **units shipped, $/unit (BOM cost curve), and funding raised / post-money** — not revenue multiples (most makers are pre-revenue). Key players and the basis of competition (manufacturing scale + embodied-AI model quality, not price yet): Tesla Optimus (no standalone price; rides a ~$1.5T parent; ~$25bn 2026 robotics/AI/chip capex per reports, ~triple 2025; production slated to start mid-2026, sub-$20k target at scale); **Figure AI** (~$39bn post-money after a >$1bn Series C closed 2025-09-16, led by Parkway with NVIDIA/Brookfield/Intel Capital/Qualcomm et al.; ~$1.9bn raised total); 1X (~$10bn), Apptronik (~$5.5bn, ~$935m raised); and China's **Unitree** (~$1.3bn, IPO on STAR Market cited at ~$6.2bn valuation) — which reportedly out-ships the Western names on volume. Position: no clear winner on deployed units; demos lead adoption (de-PR: "announced/production-target" ≠ "live at scale").

**Comps & multiples.** Revenue multiples are **not computable** here — the leaders are pre-revenue, so valuations are round post-money, not market cap. Qualitative only:
- Figure AI — ~$39bn post-money on ~$1.9bn raised, essentially $0 commercial revenue → valuation is a bet on the 2030 curve, not current economics; the richest pre-revenue mark in the field. [UNSOURCED] for any EV/Rev.
- Unitree — ~$6.2bn IPO-level valuation while shipping the most units → cheapest on an implied price-per-shipped-unit basis (arithmetic not computable: unit split undisclosed).
- Internal comps (base): [[Hyundai to buy SoftBank's remaining Boston Dynamics stake for $325M]] (2026-06 — strategic/scale consolidation, a rare priced M&A mark in the space); [[Morgan Stanley raises forecast for China humanoid robot market]] (2026-06 — the China-forecast upgrade that feeds these raises); [[Kindred Ventures raises $355 million for AI and robotics bets]] and [[Menlo Ventures raises record $3 billion fund on Anthropic bet]] (2026-06 — the LP capital pooling into AI/robotics). Distribution not computed — too few comparable priced figures; qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Forecast-vs-reality gap / valuation air-pocket.** 7,000 real units (2025) vs. 90k (2026E) vs. 1.2m (2030E) vs. 6.5m (2035E) — every maker's valuation discounts the far end of that curve. If deployment slips (as demos routinely do), $39bn-type marks de-rate hard; second-order: the VC/LP capital (Kindred, Menlo) riding the theme takes the mark-down.
2. **China supply + policy concentration.** Unitree out-shipping the field plus China subsidies means the volume base — and the cost-curve that justifies forecasts — sits in a geopolitically exposed jurisdiction (export-control / "Chinese military companies" list dynamics already visible in the base). Western makers' sub-$20k targets depend on a supply chain they don't control.
3. **Pre-revenue / execution & funding-dependence.** None of the leaders has meaningful commercial revenue; they burn capital toward unproven mass production. A funding-market cooldown (rates, AI-capex fatigue) stalls the whole cohort at once — correlated, not idiosyncratic, risk.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a **capital-formation re-rating, not a revenue story** (analysis): the IFR number institutionalizes the category (official stats = legitimacy for allocators) even as it exposes how small actual adoption is — a classic "narrative ahead of fundamentals" setup. Falsifiable thesis: if 2026 shipments materially miss BofA's ~90k, expect the first down-rounds in the ~$10bn+ pre-revenue names within ~12 months. Trigger to watch: Tesla Optimus production start (mid-2026) and the next IFR annual tally — the cleanest independent check on whether units are catching the forecasts.
