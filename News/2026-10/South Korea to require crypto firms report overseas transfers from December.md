# South Korea to require crypto firms report overseas transfers from December

> [!info] 2026-10-26 · 1 упоминаний · 1 источника(ов) с текстом
> Каналы: Connecting the Dots in Fintech

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Connecting the Dots in Fintech] 🇰🇷 South Korea will require crypto firms to report overseas transfers from December. Rules taking effect on December 3, 2026 introduce registration for cross border crypto transfer businesses. Transfers between exchanges and private wallets will be reported to the Bank of Korea, and public comments close on October 26.

South Korea has moved closer to introducing mandatory registration and foreign exchange reporting for cryptocurrency transfers, with new rules scheduled to take effect on December 3, 2026.
South Korea’s amended foreign exchange law takes effect December 3, introducing registration for cross-border crypto transfer businesses.
The proposed rules cover transfers between cryptocurrency exchanges and private wallets, with reporting to the Bank of Korea.
Officials have not confirmed that USDT and USDC will become formally recognized payment instruments under this amendment.
Crypto businesses must prepare reporting systems and qualified staff, with public comments on the draft closing October 26.
Upbit, Bithumb and other service providers could face new obligations depending on their cross-border transfer activities.
According to the Ministry of Finance and Economy’s October 7 legislative notice , the proposed regulations will bring cross-border virtual asset transfers under a formal monitoring system, including transactions involving exchanges and private wallets. The government is accepting public comments until October 26 before finalizing the enforcement requirements.
Meanwhile, the changes follow amendments to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act passed by the National Assembly on May 7 and promulgated on June 2. They establish a new category of registered virtual asset transfer businesses, covering certain services that move cryptocurrencies between South Korea and overseas markets.
In an October 19 legal analysis, South Korean law firm Bae, Kim & Lee explained that the draft enforcement decree covers transfers between domestic and foreign virtual asset service providers. Transactions between exchanges and private, unhosted wallets are included because authorities consider them capable of producing similar cross-border effects.
The rules would require qualifying operators to register with the finance ministry after completing existing virtual asset reporting obligations. Businesses must connect their systems to the Bank of Korea’s foreign exchange information network and maintain records of relevant transfers.
Registration requirements include suitable computer systems and at least two qualified employees at each business office. Those employees must have two years of foreign exchange experience or complete government-approved training.
Unlike some foreign exchange business categories, virtual asset transfer businesses would not face a separate minimum equity capital requirement under the draft.
 Are USDT and USDC becoming official payment instruments? 
Reports circulating on social media have described the changes as South Korea formally recognizing dollar-backed stablecoins such as Tether’s USDT and Circle’s USDC as payment instruments.
Crypto analyst Ash Crypto welcomed the reported development, describing it as a major step toward stablecoin adoption in South Korea. In a post on X, he claimed the country would recognize cryptocurrency stablecoins as an official form of payment starting December 3, calling the development “absolutely massive” for dollar-backed stablecoins in one of Asia’s largest cryptocurrency markets.
However, the ministry’s official announcement describes a registration and reporting framework for virtual asset transfer businesses. Its published explanation does not establish a blanket reclassification of USDT or USDC as legally recognized payment instruments.
Stablecoin transfers could fall within the new monitoring framework when they involve activities covered by the amended law. That does not automatically grant either stablecoin domestic payment status or remove existing restrictions on foreign exchange transactions.
The distinction follows South Korea’s July roadmap for won stablecoins , which set out separate plans for digital currency legislation and foreign exchange reforms. The government has been considering how won-backed tokens could operate within the financial system, but those proposals remain distinct from December’s transfer registration requirements.
 Upbit and Bithumb face new reporting obligations 
The proposed framework could affect cryptocurrency exchanges such as Upbit and Bithumb where their activities meet the definition of virtual asset transfer business.
Registered operators would have to submit transaction information to the Bank of Korea’s foreign exchange information system. The information could then be shared with the National Tax Service, Korea Customs Service, Financial Supervisory Service and Korea Financial Intelligence Unit.
The Financial Services Commission would receive delegated authority over certain supervisory measures and sanctions, while the Financial Supervisory Service would handle inspections.
South Korea has already considered expanding crypto transfer licenses beyond exchanges to other financial technology businesses. The draft’s scope includes custody, brokerage and related transfer services, depending on how individual firms operate.
The monitoring requirements could affect transfers involving dollar stablecoins traded on Korean exchanges. However, the government has not announced separate USDT trading restrictions, a new tax on stablecoin transfers or specific limits on exchange premiums under this decree.
 South Korea strengthens oversight of overseas crypto transfers 
The finance ministry said the reporting system would help authorities identify unauthorized foreign exchange transactions and attempts to avoid existing controls. Its proposal includes stronger sanctions for certain money exchange businesses involved in illegal remittances, voice phishing or unauthorized trade payments.
The draft would permit registration cancellation after a first qualifying violation. Maximum administrative surcharges imposed instead of a four-month business suspension would rise to 100% of profits obtained through the violation.
Meanwhile, South Korean exchange operator Dunamu has been exploring international stablecoin payments with MoonPay. Their October 21 agreement covers potential cross-border financial services and corporate transactions, but does not establish an operational payment product.
Following the October 26 public comment deadline, the draft decree must undergo regulatory and legislative reviews before Cabinet consideration. The government expects the finalized provisions to take effect alongside the amended law on December 3, while separate transitional arrangements give certain existing remittance and money exchange businesses until June 30, 2027, to meet new requirements.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What's new.** South Korea is finalizing an enforcement decree that forces cross-border virtual-asset transfer businesses to register with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and report overseas crypto flows to the Bank of Korea (BOK). The draft stems from amendments to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act passed by the National Assembly on May 7, 2026 and promulgated on June 2, 2026; the MOEF issued its legislative notice on October 7, 2026, with public comments closing October 26, 2026. The amended law and decree take effect December 3, 2026 (Dec. 2 US ET), with BOK's operational reporting manual listing a start of Dec. 2 KST (Dec. 1 ET). ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/south-korea-crypto-transfer-rules-december-2026), [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/10/23/crypto-transfers-abroad-must-be-reported-to-central-bank))

**[1] Who it affects and the mechanics.** A new legal category of "registered virtual asset transfer business" covers firms moving crypto between South Korea and overseas — including transfers between a domestic VASP and a foreign VASP, and between domestic platforms and personal/unhosted wallets (because authorities judge these to have the same cross-border effect). Pure private-wallet-to-private-wallet transfers without an intermediary stay outside scope. Registration requires prior completion of existing VASP reporting, connection to BOK's foreign-exchange information network via an approved intermediary, suitable IT systems, and at least two staff per office with two years of FX experience or government-approved training — but, unusually, no separate minimum equity-capital requirement. Reporting is onerous: daily outflow/inflow records due to BOK by 11:00 the next business day, plus monthly user-holdings reports (including dormant accounts and staked assets) by the 10th of the following month at 17:30, carrying token quantity, KRW value, fees, and sender/recipient identity. BOK may share the data with the National Tax Service, Korea Customs Service, the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) and the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit; the FSC gets delegated supervisory/sanctions authority while the FSS runs inspections. Exchanges such as Upbit, Bithumb, Coinone and Korbit are the obvious targets. ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/south-korea-crypto-transfer-rules-december-2026), [Bloomingbit](https://en.bloomingbit.io/feed/news/122789), [TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/regulation/39369))

**[2] Scale and pushback.** Per a BOK operational manual, exchanges would have to hand over personal information on roughly 1,272,878 users with a cross-border transfer history — along with monthly balances and daily deposit/withdrawal data. The opposition party has criticized this as excessive collection of personal information, and firms are "chafing" at the compliance build-out on a tight timeline. The stated policy goal is detecting unauthorized FX transactions, illegal remittances, voice-phishing and disguised trade payments; sanctions tighten too, allowing registration cancellation after a first qualifying violation and surcharges up to 100% of illicit profits in lieu of a four-month suspension. ([blockchainreporter](https://blockchainreporter.net/bank-of-korea-cross-border-crypto-reporting/), [Seoul Economic Daily — firms chafe](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/10/25/crypto-firms-chafe-as-korea-mandates-cross-border-transfer))

**[3] The stablecoin red herring.** Social-media claims that the decree "recognizes USDT/USDC as official payment instruments" from Dec. 3 are not supported by the MOEF's own explanation, which describes only a registration-and-reporting FX framework. Stablecoin transfers can fall inside the monitoring net when they involve covered activity, but that confers no domestic payment status. This is distinct from South Korea's July 19, 2026 won-stablecoin roadmap (see [[Bank of Korea urges banks to lead won stablecoin issuance]]) and the pending Digital Asset Basic Act (DABA), which would require an FSC licence, KRW 1bn paid-in capital and 100%+ bankruptcy-remote reserves for won-backed issuers — and which remains stuck on the BOK-vs-FSC fight over a 51% bank-ownership threshold. Foreign stablecoins remain tradable on Korean exchanges under a "brokered transaction" model but cannot be used to pay merchants or settle invoices. ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/south-korea-crypto-transfer-rules-december-2026), [CryptoTimes](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/07/29/south-korea-fsc-moves-to-merge-crypto-bills-set-stablecoin-rules/))

**[4] Where it sits in Korea's 2026 crypto arc.** This is one more plank in a year where Seoul folded crypto into core financial strategy: the FSC lifting the nine-year corporate-trading ban, a Bithumb glitch prompting tougher exchange safety rules, and infrastructure moves such as [[South Korea blocks 27 unregistered crypto exchange websites]], [[Toss and Gwangju Bank complete stablecoin QR payment PoC]], [[Chainlink partners LG CNS on South Korea digital asset infrastructure]] and [[Ripple expands Korean partnerships to tokenized government bonds]]. Transitional relief gives certain existing remittance/money-exchange businesses until June 30, 2027 to comply. Separately, [[Dunamu]]-operator Upbit's parent was exploring cross-border stablecoin payments with MoonPay via an October 21 agreement — a sign exchanges are leaning into cross-border rails just as the reporting regime lands. ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/south-korea-crypto-transfer-rules-december-2026), [TradingView/Coinpedia](https://www.tradingview.com/news/coinpedia:7e7530f79094b:0-crypto-regulations-in-south-korea-2026/))

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is this actually "new law" or just the enforcement decree of an already-passed amendment? The Act was promulgated June 2, 2026 — the Oct. 7 notice is only the subordinate decree, so the headline risks overstating novelty.
2. Draft vs. final: public comments closed Oct. 26, 2026, and the decree still needs regulatory/legislative review and Cabinet consideration. Could scope, dates or the staffing/IT thresholds change before Dec. 3?
3. Which effective date is correct — Dec. 2 or Dec. 3? Sources split because of KST/ET timezone conversion and because the BOK reporting manual (Dec. 2 KST start) differs from the Act's Dec. 3 effective date. Verify before treating either as canonical.
4. Does the rule capture exchange↔private-wallet transfers but exempt wallet↔wallet? If so, does it meaningfully close the evasion channel, or just push flows to peer-to-peer and offshore mixers?
5. The 1,272,878-user figure comes from a BOK operational manual, not the statute — is it a hard reporting population or an estimate? How current is it?
6. Privacy/legal exposure: opposition calls the personal-data collection excessive. Could it face a constitutional or data-protection challenge (resident registration numbers, retroactive balance disclosure) that delays enforcement?
7. Is the "USDT/USDC become official payment instruments" claim debunked here, or does the decree quietly expand stablecoin status via the monitoring net? Confirm MOEF's wording vs. social-media hype.
8. Operational feasibility: can exchanges build daily 11:00-next-day reporting and FX-network connectivity by Dec. 3? What happens to firms that register late — are they barred from cross-border transfers?
9. No minimum equity-capital requirement is unusual for an FX business category. Is that a deliberate low barrier to encourage registration, or an oversight that invites thinly capitalized operators?
10. Does the June 30, 2027 transitional window apply to crypto transfer businesses or only to legacy remittance/money-exchange firms? The note should not conflate the two.
11. Competitive effect: do these obligations entrench incumbents (Upbit/Bithumb with compliance budgets) and squeeze smaller VASPs, custody and brokerage firms?
12. Enforcement teeth vs. reality: registration cancellation after a first violation and 100%-of-profit surcharges are severe — is there precedent for Korea actually applying first-strike cancellation, or is it a deterrent on paper?
13. Cross-agency data sharing (NTS, Customs, FSS, KoFIU) — is this primarily a tax/AML surveillance tool rather than FX stability, and does that reframe who the real "affected party" is (users, not just firms)?
14. Market reaction: any evidence of user outflows, delisting, or exchanges curbing cross-border features ahead of Dec. 3? The Dunamu–MoonPay Oct. 21 deal suggests the opposite — reconcile.

**Importance: 4/5** — A top-10 Asian crypto market bringing cross-border virtual-asset transfers under formal FX monitoring, with named effective date (Dec. 3, 2026), a ~1.27M-user reporting population, and material compliance/sanctions obligations on Upbit/Bithumb-tier firms. Precedent-setting for Travel-Rule-style surveillance and a bellwether for how Korea's broader DABA/won-stablecoin framework will treat cross-border flows. Not a 5/5 only because it is still a draft decree of an already-passed law and its direct global spillover is limited.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Asian crypto regulation is shifting from AML/licensing toward FX-and-tax surveillance of cross-border flows — the layer this decree occupies. South Korea's Dunamu/Upbit sits in one of Asia's largest retail crypto markets: Upbit holds ~71.6% of domestic volume (~$1.12bn/day) and with Bithumb ~96% combined (per Tiger Research / spotedcrypto, 2026) — a highly concentrated base that makes exchange-level reporting an effective dragnet. The decree stems from the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act amendment (passed May 7, promulgated June 2, 2026), enforced Dec 3, 2026, routing transfers into the Bank of Korea's FX information network with data shared to NTS, Customs, FSS and KoFIU (per crypto.news, Oct 23–24, 2026). "Why now": (1) regional convergence — Japan's July 15, 2026 FIEA amendment reclassified many tokens as financial instruments with real-time reserve reporting; Singapore's MAS (Sept 2026) and Hong Kong's stablecoin licensing (live Aug 2025, first licences Apr 2026) all tighten cross-border visibility (per The Diplomat / stablecoininsider, Oct 2026). (2) Korea's parallel won-stablecoin roadmap (July 2026) raises the stakes on monitoring dollar-stablecoin (USDT/USDC) outflows before any domestic-token regime launches.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: trading volume / market share, cross-border transfer volume, and (newly) compliance cost per registered transfer business. Players affected: Upbit/Dunamu, Bithumb, Coinone, plus custody/brokerage firms whose activity meets the "virtual asset transfer business" definition. Basis of competition shifts partly from fees toward compliance capacity — the decree mandates BoK-network integration and ≥2 FX-qualified staff per office (no minimum-capital floor), a fixed cost that favours scaled incumbents over small transfer operators. Recent moves: Dunamu–MoonPay cross-border stablecoin-payments agreement (Oct 21, 2026, exploratory, no live product); transitional relief for existing remittance/money-exchange businesses runs to June 30, 2027. Protagonist's position: the FSC/BoK as rule-setter is "ahead" on surveillance design; exchanges are "catching up" on reporting infrastructure — moat here is regulatory licensing + network integration (switching/compliance barriers), `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** This is a regulator story — no issuer, no deal valuation in the news itself, so market multiples are "no data". Scale anchor for the affected base: Dunamu (Upbit parent) implied valuation ~KRW 15.3tn (~$11bn) from 2026 stake sales — Hana Bank bought 6.55% for ~KRW 1tn ($670M) and three Samsung affiliates 4% for ~KRW 613bn ($408M) (per CoinDesk May 15 / TheBlock May 28, 2026). Profitability caveat: Korea's top-four exchanges booked ~$361M in 1H-2026 losses (Dunamu −$262M, Bithumb −$79M net) — EV/EBITDA and P/E not computable on negative earnings → `[UNSOURCED]`. Internal comps (base): [[Japan's regulator weighs letting banks hold and trade Bitcoin]] · [[South Korea's FSS urges caution on easing fintech rules]] · [[Bank of Korea urges banks to lead won stablecoin issuance]] · [[China asks Ant Group and JD.com to suspend HK stablecoin plans]] · [[Samsung affiliates buy Dunamu stake amid won stablecoin push]] — a cluster showing Asian regulators simultaneously opening (bank crypto, won stablecoin) and tightening (FX surveillance, HK stablecoin halts). No multiple distribution computed (qualitative only).

**Risk flags.**
1. Regulatory/interpretation risk: the decree is a reporting-and-registration regime, NOT stablecoin legalization — social-media claims that USDT/USDC become "official payment instruments" Dec 3 are unconfirmed by the ministry (per crypto.news). Mispricing this as a demand catalyst is the core trap; second-order, a misread could trigger a sell-the-news reversal.
2. Compliance-cost concentration: BoK-network integration + qualified-staff mandates raise fixed costs that small transfer/custody operators may not absorb, pushing cross-border activity toward Upbit/Bithumb and entrenching the ~96% duopoly — or offshore/unhosted-wallet rails outside the net (direct private-wallet-to-private-wallet transfers are explicitly excluded).
3. Enforcement severity: registration-cancellation after a first qualifying violation and surcharges up to 100% of illicit profits create step-function penalty risk; combined with 1H-2026 losses, compliance drag lands on already-unprofitable exchanges.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This marks Asia's pivot to treating crypto rails as FX-monitored channels rather than a licensing silo — a template neighbours may copy. Falsifiable thesis: if the Dec 3 decree lands as pure surveillance (no stablecoin reclassification), expect no sustained re-rating of Korean exchange value and a possible compliance-cost squeeze on 2H-2026 margins. Trigger to watch: the finalized enforcement decree post-Oct-26 comment deadline (Cabinet review) and any separate won-stablecoin legislation — the latter, not this decree, is the actual demand catalyst. Thesis breaks if the ministry formally recognizes USDT/USDC as payment instruments.
