## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Nothing new happened on 2026-10-29. The underlying facts — all previously reported:
- USAT is a US-only, dollar-backed stablecoin, structurally separate from the global USDT token.
- It is **issued by Anchorage Digital Bank** (the first and only federally chartered crypto bank), not by Tether directly. Tether deliberately avoided creating its own US issuing entity, instead white-labeling through a chartered bank. Reserve custody sits with Cantor Fitzgerald (and, per [[US Bank to custody reserves for Anchorage Digital stablecoins]], US Bank for Anchorage's stablecoin reserves generally).
- Bo Hines (ex-White House digital-asset policy director) heads Tether USA₮.
- The source itself concedes USAT "has not yet received official approval under the GENIUS Act" — i.e. the regulatory process is still pending even in this retelling.
- **Why structured this way:** Tether issues via a chartered bank rather than directly to inherit GENIUS-compliant issuance rails without building its own federal charter — a speed-and-compliance shortcut, not a novel 2026-10 move. The caveat "holders may not redeem directly with Anchorage unless approved clients" reveals USAT relies on secondary-market liquidity for retail, a limitation present since launch.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Circle (USDC):** the direct target. USDC ~$74.1B cap (Oct 2026), the incumbent "regulated/transparent" US dollar coin that USAT was built to attack.
- **Tether's own USDT:** ~$183.8B cap (Oct 2026), ~58% of the ~$316B total stablecoin market. USAT is a tiny US-compliance carve-out beside it.
- **PayPal (PYUSD), others:** sub-$10B field; no challenger above $10B after USDT/USDC.
- Position: USAT remains a **marginal entrant** — by Jun 2026 only ~$140M despite a 540% monthly jump, i.e. <0.05% of USDT. This note adds nothing to that standing.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** The total stablecoin market was ~$304bn in Oct 2026, off an all-time high of ~$322bn in May 2026 (per Stablecoin Beat tracker, via thefool.com/spark.money, as of Oct 2026). USDT leads at ~$183.8bn and USDC at ~$74–75bn — together ~82% of supply, i.e. a duopoly, not a fragmented market. Structure: concentrated at the issuance layer (two names dominate), but value is increasingly split across issuer (reserve carry), distributor/exchange and custody/banking rails. The secular "why now": the GENIUS Act (signed Jul 2025) created the first US federal framework for payment stablecoins, and the first operative rules only began to appear in late 2025 (FDIC proposed its first application rule Dec 2025 — see [[FDIC proposes first stablecoin rules under GENIUS Act]]). USAT is Tether's compliance vehicle for that regime: a US-focused coin issued by a federally chartered bank (Anchorage Digital Bank, N.A., OCC-regulated), ring-fenced from the offshore USDT franchise. Reserve custodian / primary dealer is Cantor Fitzgerald; initial supply was reported at only ~$10m (ERC-20 on Ethereum) at launch (per coindesk/pymnts/yahoo, Jan 2026). Note the de-PR point the pluang source itself flags: USAT was "not yet officially approved" under GENIUS at the time of this item.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: stablecoin market cap / circulating supply, reserve yield (Treasury carry), and distribution reach (exchange/wallet listings). Key players: Circle (USDC) as the compliance-first US incumbent already public; Tether (USDT/USAT) as the dominant offshore issuer now building a domestic arm; plus bank- and consortium-issued coins emerging under GENIUS. Basis of competition: regulatory legitimacy + distribution, not price (all peg to $1). Recent moves with dates: USAT launched via Anchorage Jan 27 2026, listing on Bybit, Crypto.com, Kraken, OKX, MoonPay (per coindesk/coinmarketcap, Jan 2026); Circle (public since Jun 2025) grew USDC ~19% YoY to ~$73–75bn and outpaced USDT growth for a second year ([[Circle's USDC outpaces USDT growth for second year]]). Protagonist position: Tether is the global leader but a **catching-up challenger inside the regulated-US niche**, where Circle has first-mover compliance credibility. Moat: Tether's is scale + reserve profitability (not US regulatory standing); USAT is an attempt to buy the latter via Anchorage's charter rather than its own license — a rented, not owned, regulatory moat (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.**
- **Circle (CRCL, public):** market cap ~$21.8bn (Oct 2026); TTM revenue ~$2.9bn (to Jun 30 2026). EV/Revenue proxy using market cap: `$21.8bn / $2.9bn ≈ 7.5x`; the cited P/S of ~7.1x on $2.9bn is in line. P/E ~56x on EPS $1.46 (per stockanalysis/yahoo/cnn, Oct 2026) — rich, but this is a regulated-issuer growth name, so a high revenue multiple is consistent with the ~19–72% USDC growth cited.
- **Tether (private):** explored a raise at a ~$500bn valuation, $15–20bn ticket ([[Tether explores fundraise at $500B valuation]], Sep 2025) — mark as round valuation, not market cap. Q1 2026 net profit ~$1.04bn and Q2 2026 ~$1.5bn (per tether.io / stablecoininsider attestations); reserves ~$187.8bn with ~75% in short-dated Treasuries. A crude P/E on a ~$500bn round vs ~$5bn annualized profit ≈ `$500bn / $5bn = 100x` — far above Circle's ~56x, reflecting round-valuation optimism, not public-market pricing [UNSOURCED on a clean comparable basis].
- **Anchorage (private, issuer):** [[Anchorage Digital eyes $400M raise ahead of IPO]] (Jan 2026) — valuation not disclosed in-base, "no data".
- Distribution not computed: too few clean public comps (effectively one — Circle) for a median. Qualitative read: Circle sets the only public benchmark; Tether's implied round multiple looks rich vs it.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory approval gap** — the source itself states USAT is "not yet officially approved" under GENIUS; a coin branded "GENIUS-compliant" that lacks final approval risks enforcement/relabeling and undercuts the whole premise (second-order: USAT's legitimacy moat is unproven).
2. **Rented charter / rails dependence** — Tether does not itself hold the US bank charter; issuance, redemption and reserve custody sit with Anchorage + Cantor Fitzgerald. Holders can't redeem directly with Anchorage unless they are approved clients (per pluang), so retail relies on secondary-market liquidity — a de-peg and redemption-friction risk the PR glosses over.
3. **Negligible scale vs the franchise** — ~$10m initial supply against USDT's ~$184bn means USAT is a strategic option, not yet a business; execution risk that it stays a token (pun intended) US beachhead while the profitable offshore franchise deliberately stays outside GENIUS (per Forbes framing: "USAT exists so USDT never has to comply").

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Structurally this bifurcates Tether into a regulated-US shell (USAT) and an unregulated-offshore cash machine (USDT), letting it contest Circle's home turf without subjecting its $180bn+ book to GENIUS (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: USAT is a defensive compliance hedge, not a genuine growth push — watch USAT circulating supply over the next 2–3 quarters; if it stays sub-$1bn while USDT keeps compounding, the "hedge" read holds. Catalyst that would break it: formal GENIUS approval + a major US distribution/payments integration pushing USAT supply materially higher.
