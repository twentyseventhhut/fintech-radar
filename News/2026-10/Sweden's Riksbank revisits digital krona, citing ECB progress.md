Sweden revisits digital krona as ECB advances digital euro plans
Sweden’s central bank is revisiting the possibility of introducing a digital krona, citing the European Central Bank’s (ECB) progress toward a digital euro and the growing use of stablecoins as reasons to reconsider a central bank digital currency (CBDC).
In an Oct. 7 letter to parliament’s Finance Committee, published Thursday, the Riksbank urged lawmakers to begin preparing legislation for a potential digital krona, three years after a government inquiry concluded there was insufficient need for one.
The letter pointed to the ECB’s planned digital euro pilot in 2027 and a potential public launch in 2029. According to the Riksbank, Sweden’s payment system could become less competitive without a comparable digital version of its own currency.
Know when your coins move
The central bank also identified the growing use of foreign-currency stablecoins, particularly those pegged to the US dollar, as a longer-term concern, though it sees the near-term risk as limited.
“If stablecoins pegged to foreign currencies are considered attractive as a means of payment, the use of the Swedish krona could come under pressure even within Sweden’s borders,” the letter read.
The Riksbank estimates that introducing a digital krona would take at least five years, excluding the time required for legislative changes. For this reason, it recommends beginning legal preparations now, even if Sweden ultimately decides against launching a CBDC.

# Context-enrichment: Sweden's Riksbank revisits digital krona, citing ECB progress
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**Freshness: fresh.** This is a genuinely new policy event — an Oct 7 Riksbank letter to parliament's Finance Committee (published Oct 15), formally reversing the posture set by the 2023 government inquiry (SOU 2023:16) that found "insufficient social need" for an e-krona. It is NOT a duplicate of the companion ECB note [[ECB sets 2027 digital euro pilot and possible 2029 issuance]] (that is a separate Oct 6 Cipollone speech) nor of prior Riksbank notes [[Sweden's Riksbank urges public to keep a week's cash]] (cash-preparedness) and [[Riksbank pressures Swedish banks to offer instant payments]] (instant payments). Those share the actor, not the event. The 2027/2029 ECB figures are quoted here only as the external trigger.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- On Oct 7, 2026 the Riksbank sent a letter to the Riksdag's Committee on Finance (published Oct 15) urging lawmakers to **begin preparing legislation** for a potential digital krona. It did NOT decide to build one, did NOT announce a new pilot, and explicitly took no position on whether an e-krona should launch. Under the Sveriges Riksbank Act the Riksbank cannot issue a digital means of payment without Riksdag consent — so this is a request for *legal optionality*, not a product.
- Stated rationale: (1) ECB's advancing digital euro (2027 pilot / possible 2029 issuance) means future European payment rails, infra and law will be built around the digital euro; without a comparable Swedish-currency instrument Sweden's payment system "could become less competitive"; (2) growth of foreign-currency (mainly USD) stablecoins is a longer-term threat to krona usage — but the Riksbank explicitly calls near-term risk **limited**; (3) deteriorated geopolitics → payments resilience as national preparedness.
- Key number that reframes the item: the Riksbank estimates a digital krona would take **at least five years to build, excluding** the time for legislative change — hence the "start the legal clock now even if we never launch" logic. **Why framed this way:** the ask is cheap and reversible (prepare a bill) while the alternative (being caught flat-footed when the digital euro goes live in ~2029) is not; it's an option-value play, not a commitment.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **ECB (digital euro)** — the pace-setter and the actual trigger ([[ECB sets 2027 digital euro pilot and possible 2029 issuance]], [[ECB outlines three models for central bank money on-chain]]). Sweden is reacting to Frankfurt, not leading.
- **Bank of England** — the mirror-image stance: [[Bank of England will not rush digital pound, eyes stablecoins]] — explicitly *in no rush*, betting on private sterling stablecoins + RTGS upgrade. UK leans private-rails; Sweden leans "keep the public-money option open." Same problem, opposite reflex.
- **Bank of Russia (digital ruble)** — the one that actually shipped: [[Russians open 220,000 digital ruble accounts in first month]], [[Финтехно Bank of Russia reframes digital ruble as consumer product]], salary payments live [[Russia's Finance Ministry pays salaries in digital rubles]]. Demonstrates "launched vs announced" — the gap Sweden has NOT crossed.
- **Sweden's own prior position** — once the global CBDC frontrunner (e-krona pilot phases 2020–2022 with Accenture), then cooled to "insufficient need" (2023). So this is a peer *re-entering* the race it had left, not a newcomer.
- **Position: catching up / defensive.** Why: cash is <8% of POS in Sweden (Riksbank Payments Report 2026) and Swish already solved domestic retail efficiency, so the e-krona case was never UX — it's monetary-sovereignty insurance against euro + USD-stablecoin encroachment.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Riksbank launched the e-krona project in 2017; pilot phase 1 (2020, Accenture) and phase 2 (2021–Feb 2022) tested a technical solution; work wound down toward "monitoring" by ~2023. The 2023 SOU inquiry recommended giving the Riksbank the mandate but judged there was no present need — while flagging that tech/economic/international shifts could justify revisiting. **This letter is exactly that revisit the 2023 inquiry pre-authorized.**
- Fits the Riksbank's consistent through-line: it is the Western central bank most anxious about a cashless society (see its cash-hoarding advice [[Sweden's Riksbank urges public to keep a week's cash]]). The e-krona, digital-cash resilience and instant-payment pressure on banks are three expressions of one structural worry: as private digital rails (cards, Swish, soon stablecoins/digital euro) displace cash, the central bank loses a direct public-money anchor. **Why act now:** the digital euro timeline (2029) gives a hard deadline; a 5-year build + multi-year legislation means starting later = missing the window.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty is modest.** Nothing is being built. The only new thing is the *policy posture flip* (2023 "no need" → 2026 "prepare the law"). No pilot, no vendor, no UX, no numbers on adoption — because there is nothing to adopt yet.
- **Traction = zero by design** (it's a legislative-prep request). The honest read: this is a central bank buying an option and hedging reputational/sovereignty risk, not a CBDC launch.
- **Second-order — who captures the value:** even if a digital krona ships, the binding constraint is the same one squeezing every retail-CBDC: with Swish + cards already near-free and ubiquitous, a public e-krona has no consumer pull; its only durable value is *sovereignty/resilience*, a public good banks won't fund. That is why the Riksbank frames it as national preparedness, not a product — the business case doesn't close on consumer utility. The real competitive threat it names (USD stablecoins, 99.5% of ~$301B vs 0.3% EUR) is a currency-substitution risk, and a retail e-krona is a weak tool against it; MiCA/euro-stablecoin policy matters more. **(analysis)**

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Next: the Riksdag's Finance Committee must decide whether to open a legislative process; nothing is automatic and the Riksbank itself hasn't endorsed issuance. Realistic path = multi-year legal groundwork running parallel to watching the ECB's 2027 pilot.
- Backdrop: EU digital euro regulation is still in trilogue; the digital euro's own 2029 "possible" launch is itself soft. So Sweden is hedging against a timeline that may slip.
- **Counterintuitive second-order:** the more credible the digital euro and USD stablecoins become, the *less* a standalone e-krona helps — if European rails standardize on the digital euro, a parallel krona token risks being a stranded domestic asset. The stronger case is interoperability with the digital euro, not competition. The letter's "competitiveness" framing may understate that the real choice is align-with-euro vs build-own. **(hypothesis)**
- Sentiment: treated by press as incremental/"back on the agenda," not a breakthrough — correctly.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits in the retail-CBDC / sovereign-money subvertical, not a commercial market — "size" is policy posture, not TAM. Sweden is the archetypal cash-decline case: cash fell from ~40% of retail payments in 2010 to ~15% in 2016 and under ~8% of POS transactions recently (per Riksbank e-krona pilot reports / IMF, as of 2021–2022). That decline is the structural driver behind a CBDC: if private digital rails dominate, the state loses a public money anchor accessible to all. "Why now" is explicitly imported from abroad — the Riksbank's Oct-7 letter (published Oct-15) cites two external triggers: (1) the ECB moving to a 2027 digital-euro pilot with possible 2029 issuance (contingent on EU co-legislators adopting the Regulation in 2026), and (2) the rise of foreign-currency (USD-pegged) stablecoins that could pressure krona usage "even within Sweden's borders." The structure is highly consolidated (one issuer, the central bank) and barriers are legislative, not capital: under the Sveriges Riksbank Act the bank may issue a digital means of payment only with the Riksdag's consent — hence the ask is for legal groundwork, not a launch.

**Competitive landscape.** The relevant "KPIs" for a retail CBDC are adoption/wallet penetration, active-wallet share, and transaction velocity — not revenue multiples. Key players are peer central banks, each at a different point on the same curve, competing on *stance* rather than price/product:
- **ECB (digital euro)** — furthest along on a concrete timeline: 2027 pilot, possible 2029 issuance, 36 PSPs selected; gated on 2026 EU legislation. [[ECB sets 2027 digital euro pilot and possible 2029 issuance]]
- **Bank of England (digital pound)** — explicitly in *no rush*; leaning on sterling stablecoins/tokenised deposits instead, with a temporary systemic-stablecoin issuance cap (~$52.9bn each, ~£40bn demand per name); design-phase decision due end-2026. [[Bank of England will not rush digital pound, eyes stablecoins]]
- **Bank of Russia (digital ruble)** — only one of the group *live*: ~220k wallets in the first month post Sep-1 launch (~4x the ~60k expected), but only ~150k operations — adoption likely employer/budget-driven, not organic demand. [[Russians open 220,000 digital ruble accounts in first month]]
- Wholesale-CBDC track (distinct from retail) is also moving: [[MAS plans to settle tokenised MAS Bills with wholesale CBDC]], [[ECB outlines three models for central bank money on-chain]].

Protagonist's position: Riksbank is a *first-mover-turned-laggard* — it ran two e-krona DLT pilots (Corda, with Accenture, 2020–2022) years before most peers, then a 2022–23 government inquiry concluded insufficient need. It is now **catching up / re-activating**, reacting to ECB momentum rather than leading `(analysis)`. Its moat is credibility/experience (existing pilot IP and a decade of study), not a time lead. Notably, the Riksbank has *not* taken a position on whether to issue — it is buying optionality on legislation.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/deal here — this is a central-bank policy letter, so trading multiples / arithmetic are **n/a (no data)**. The useful comparison is stance-vs-adoption, qualitative (distribution not computed — <3 comparable numeric figures):
- Live adoption benchmark: digital ruble ~220k wallets / ~150k ops in month 1 — a cautionary read that *launch ≠ usage* (de-PR: headline wallet count flattered by employer/budget enrollment).
- Timeline benchmark: digital euro 2027 pilot / 2029 issuance; digital krona would take "at least five years" per Riksbank, *excluding* legislative time — i.e. a realistic krona launch is ~2032+ even if legislation starts now.
- UK as the opposite pole: actively choosing *private* stablecoins over a retail CBDC.
Positioning flag: **in-line-to-behind** peers — Sweden's one-time technical lead has not converted into a launch commitment.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Legislative dependency / optionality cost** — the entire ask hinges on the Riksdag; a 2022 inquiry already said "no need." Starting legal prep for a CBDC Sweden may never issue risks sunk effort and a repeat rejection (why: ~5-yr build + legislative lag means a decision not taken now forecloses parallelism with the 2029 digital euro).
2. **Reactive / imported mandate** — the case rests on ECB progress and USD-stablecoin fear, not a domestic demand signal; the Riksbank itself flags near-term stablecoin risk as "limited." If the digital euro slips (EU legislation is the critical path and could miss 2026), the krona rationale weakens.
3. **Adoption risk (disintermediation by private rails)** — Sweden's cashless population already runs on Swish/cards; a CBDC risks the digital-ruble outcome (wallets opened, little use) while private stablecoins/wallets capture the actual payment flow — the central bank could win issuance and lose usage.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This marks a Nordic/European convergence toward *preparing* retail CBDC legislation as a sovereignty hedge against USD stablecoins — even where (BoE) the decision leans private. Falsifiable thesis: if EU adopts the digital-euro Regulation in 2026 and the pilot starts on time in 2027, expect more non-euro European central banks (Sweden, and watch Denmark/Norway) to fast-track enabling legislation within 12–18 months; the trigger to watch is the Riksdag's response to this letter and the EU co-legislators' 2026 vote. What breaks it: a digital-euro timeline slip or a soft public launch would remove the peer-pressure driver and leave the krona case stranded.
