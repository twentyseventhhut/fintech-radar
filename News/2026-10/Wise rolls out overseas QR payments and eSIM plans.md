 Wise  has expanded QR payments and added customisable eSIM plans to its app, with customers in Singapore and Malaysia among the first globally to access both features from today.
The QR payment service covers more than 50 destinations.
Customers can already use PayNow QR in Singapore and DuitNow QR in Malaysia.
The app also connects directly to QR Ph in the Philippines, Swiss QR-bill in Switzerland and Qvik in Hungary.
A partnership with Ant International’s Alipay+ also lets eligible customers scan supported merchant QR codes in markets including mainland China, Hong Kong, South Korea and Cambodia.
In mainland China, eligible travellers can pay at supported merchants without a Chinese bank card, local mobile number or separate app setup.
The eSIM service offers standard regional plans covering more than 150 destinations. Customisable plans let customers choose their data allowance and duration.
These customisable plans are available to customers in Singapore, Malaysia, the UK, the European Economic Area, the US, Canada, Brazil, Australia and New Zealand.
Customers install the eSIM once and buy subsequent plans through the app. Data use begins when the eSIM connects to a supported network at the destination.
Wise also offers refunds for purchased data plans that have not been used.
SK Saraogi, Asia Pacific CEO at Wise, said
 “Singaporeans are used to the ease of scanning a QR code to pay, but overseas, a different payment system in each destination can turn a simple purchase into a frustrating exercise. Travellers shouldn’t have to set up a new wallet or hunt for an ATM just to buy breakfast from a street food vendor. With Scan to Pay, we’re bringing these local QR networks together in the Wise app, helping customers pay like a local with an app they already use. Alongside our customisable eSIM, that means less time figuring out how to pay and stay connected, and more time enjoying the places they’ve come to discover.” 
Yen Ting Chiam, Malaysia Country Manager at Wise, said,
 “Malaysians want to travel for authentic local experiences, but juggling different e-wallets or searching for a money changer just to pay a local street vendor is frustrating. That’s why we’re building on the Wise Card to support global QR payments within the app. Combined with our customisable eSIM, travellers can explore with peace of mind, spending less time on logistics and more time enjoying the destination.”

# Context-enrichment: Wise rolls out overseas QR payments and eSIM plans
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On ~2026-10-06/07 Wise added, to its existing multi-currency app, (a) overseas "Scan to Pay" QR acceptance across 50+ destinations and (b) customisable eSIM data plans across 150+ destinations. SG + MY are the first markets for the combo; both features are **live today** in the named geographies, not a phased promise.
- QR runs on **two different rails**: (i) *direct local-scheme integrations* — PayNow (SG), DuitNow (MY), QR Ph (PH), Swiss QR-bill (CH), Qvik (HU); and (ii) *Alipay+ aggregation* (Ant International) to extend acceptance to mainland China, Hong Kong, South Korea, Cambodia etc. The "50+ destinations" number leans heavily on the Alipay+ gateway, not on Wise's own integrations.
- **Not genuinely new vis-à-vis Wise–Alipay.** TransferWise first partnered with Alipay back in **2020-03** (CNY remittances into Alipay wallets). The 2026 item is the *merchant-QR/Alipay+* extension — an expansion of a ~6-year relationship, not a debut. → The PR "partnership with Alipay+" framing overstates novelty.
- **eSIM provider undisclosed** (open). Wise did not name the MVNO backing the eSIM; it is a white-labelled telco resell, as with every other fintech-eSIM. Customisable plans (choose data + duration, refund on unused data) are in SG, MY, UK, EEA, US, CA, BR, AU, NZ.
- **Fees are not zero.** Wise's own QR-payments feature page confirms mid-market FX **plus a ~1.2%–2% payment fee** depending on country/QR type, plus conversion fee where applicable. "Pay like a local" ≠ free. Alipay+ QR is also **not offered to US/Canada-registered Wise customers** — a regulatory-friction tell.
- **→ Why structured this way:** bundling a commodity eSIM resell with QR is an engagement wrapper around the real margin pool (card + FX on the trip), not a new revenue line.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Both halves of the bundle are late, not first:
- **eSIM in a fintech app — Revolut did it first, 2024-02 (UK, via 1GLOBAL/ex-Truphone)**, since extended to SG, Poland, NL. The corpus shows a crowded field well before Wise: [[N26 adds travel eSIM plans and 1% cashback on travel spend]] (2025-07), [[Nubank Ultravioleta expands Travel eSIM with non-expiring 10GB]] (2025-07), [[YouTrip launches global eSIM service]] (2026-01), [[Brubank launches native eSIM BruFon in digital banking]] (2026-04), [[Monzo opens waitlist for eSIM mobile plans with loyalty discounts]] (2026-06). Wise is ~2.5 years behind Revolut on eSIM.
- **Overseas QR via Alipay+** already carries 25+ wallets (GCash, Kakao Pay, Toss, Touch'n Go, TrueMoney, AlipayHK) across tens of millions of merchants. Wise is simply the newest wallet on that gateway, not a new network.
- **Position: catching up / parity.** Wise's only defensible claim is the narrow "first to combine multi-market QR + customisable eSIM in one app" — a thin, unverifiable superlative. → Why the field looks this way: travel-fintech margin is in FX/interchange on the trip, so everyone bolts on connectivity + local-pay as retention bait; the features commoditize fast because the eSIM and the Alipay+ gateway are both bought-in.

## [2] Company history / fit
Wise's 2026 cadence is relentless Asia-Pacific + card expansion: [[Wise launches multi-currency travel card for 40+ currencies]] (2025-10), [[Wise secures five payment licences in Thailand]] (2026-03), [[Wise launches Young Explorer debit card in Singapore]] (2026-06), plus Japan Zengin go-live and Philippines rail integrations. FY2026: net revenue ~$2.5B (+19%), 19M active customers (+21%), cross-border volume $243B (+31%); primary listing moved to the US ([[Wise moves primary listing to United States]], 2026-05). → **Why Wise acts this way:** a commodity remittance take-rate needs a stickier, higher-engagement consumer surface to justify a software-style US multiple → hence "own the traveller" land-grab (card → QR → eSIM) that deepens trip-time usage without adding a real new P&L line. APAC customer/volume numbers for this launch: **not disclosed**.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Genuinely new *for Wise's own users*: scan-to-pay via local schemes beyond card rails, reducing reliance on card interchange abroad in markets where QR dominates (SG/MY/CN). But vs. a Wise card that already works at Alipay merchants, and vs. Revolut's existing eSIM, the incremental delta is modest. **Traction: zero adoption data (day-1; none expected).** → Why the value-add is thin: the eSIM is a resold commodity (supplier captures the telco margin), and the QR 1.2–2% fee is the only Wise margin — competable away. The durable value is retention of the FX/card relationship, not the new features themselves.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Rollout to more markets implied, not scheduled. No material market reaction (this is a feature release, not a results print). Risks: (1) thin/competed QR FX economics; (2) **Alipay+ single-gateway dependency** — geopolitical/China exposure, already excludes US/CA customers; (3) undisclosed eSIM supplier margins; (4) fraud/chargeback ownership on push-QR unstated. → Second-order: leaning on Alipay+ for the headline "50+ destinations" makes the flagship capability a dependency Wise doesn't control — a reseller, not an owner, of the overseas-pay graph.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Cross-border consumer payments + travel-fintech. Wise itself is the sizing anchor: FY2026 (ended 31 Mar 2026) cross-border volume $243.5bn, +31% (£181.7bn, +25%); active customers 18.9–19m, +21%; net revenue ~$2.5bn, +19%; underlying income ~£1.6bn, +18% (per Wise FY2026 results, SEC Form 6-K 25 Jun 2026 — PRIMARY; stocktitan/theindustryspread, as of Jun 2026). Structure: cross-border money-movement is consolidating around a few scaled rails (Wise, Revolut) while the consumer travel-payments/connectivity layer is fragmented across local QR schemes (PayNow, DuitNow, QR Ph, Qvik, Swiss QR-bill) and roaming/eSIM resellers — entry barriers are regulatory (50+ licences/approvals Wise has accumulated: Thailand, Nigeria IMTO, South Africa, UAE, Japan Zengin) plus network access to each domestic scheme. "Why now": (1) the Alipay+ tie-up makes mainland-China merchant QR payable without a Chinese bank card/app — removing a long-standing friction for inbound travellers; (2) super-app convergence — payments firms bolting non-financial "travel utility" (eSIM, connectivity) onto the account to raise engagement/ARPU (analysis).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: cross-border volume/TPV, take rate (bps), active customers, ARPU, cross-sell/attach rate on ancillary products. Wise take rate fell to ~0.52% FY26 from ~0.58% (51bps in Q4) — deliberate price-down to drive volume (per FY26 results). Key players & basis of competition: Wise (price + rails breadth + licences) vs Revolut (breadth/super-app — 50m+ customers, ~$75bn valuation Nov-2025, banking licence, trading/crypto/insurance). On this specific move the direct precedent is Revolut, not a payments peer: Revolut launched in-app travel eSIM in 2024 (now its #1 non-financial product by usage, millions of plans, 100+ locations) and a full Mobile network (Vodafone MVNO, UK/Germany, Jan-2026). Recent Wise moves: 40+ currency travel card (Oct-2025 [[Wise launches multi-currency travel card for 40+ currencies]]), India travel/forex card (Dec-2025 [[Wise launches 40-currency travel card in India]]), Young Explorer card Singapore (Jun-2026). Protagonist position on travel-utility: catching up / fast-follower to Revolut's eSIM, but ahead on QR-rail breadth (50+ destinations, Alipay+ into mainland China). Moat: network/licence access to domestic QR schemes + switching costs from the multi-currency account; the eSIM/QR features are convenience add-ons, not themselves defensible (analysis).

**Risk flags.**
1. Take-rate compression — FY26 take rate cut to ~0.52% from ~0.58%; QR/eSIM are near-zero-margin convenience features that raise engagement but not monetisation, so they do not offset structural price erosion on the core transfer business (margin risk).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is Wise closing a feature gap with Revolut (eSIM) while pressing its own edge (QR-rail breadth, Alipay+ China access) — an engagement/retention play in the travel wallet, not a new revenue line (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Wise starts disclosing ancillary/non-FX revenue or attach-rate uplift in future trading updates, the super-app pivot is real; absent that, treat QR+eSIM as defensive churn-reduction. Watch: whether Alipay+ China coverage is exclusive/durable and whether Revolut responds by widening its own QR-pay footprint in APAC.

**Latest reported (ground).** Wise's most recent print is **FY2026, year ended 31 March 2026** (results released 25 June 2026; next print H1 FY27 due ~Nov 2026) — verdict on that print: **IN-LINE / solid** — net revenue $2,502.8m (+19% YoY) at the top end of the 15–20% medium-term target; income before tax $660.4m at a 26% margin, above the 20–25% guided range.

**Key figures (FY26 vs FY25).**
- Revenue: $2,502.8m vs $2,098.9m (+19% YoY).
- Transaction/underlying income: $1,893.6m vs $1,546.3m (+22% YoY) — i.e. revenue ex-interest income is growing faster, a positive mix signal as rate tailwind normalises.

- Active customers: 19m (+21% YoY).

- Cross-border take rate: 0.52% (52bps) vs 0.58% (58bps) in FY25 — down ~6bps, management guides a further 1–2bps/quarter decline in FY27 (1bp already cut in Q1).

**By driver.** Growth is volume-led (+31%) against a deliberately falling take rate (−6bps) — the classic Wise flywheel: cut price, win share, monetise float (holdings +40%) and card (spend +37%). Underlying income (+22%) outpacing headline revenue (+19%) shows the engine is still working even as net interest income normalises off peak rates. This product story (Scan to Pay across 50+ QR destinations incl.
