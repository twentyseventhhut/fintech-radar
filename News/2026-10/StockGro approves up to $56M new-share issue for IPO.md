Exclusive: StockGro plans Rs 536 Cr IPO fresh issue, to appoint independent directors
 
Social investing and stock market advisory platform StockGro’s parent company, AssetGro Fintech, is set to raise Rs 536 crore through a fresh issue of equity shares as part of its proposed initial public offering (IPO). 
 
 Follow Us
 StockGro’s parent company, AssetGro Fintech, is set to raise Rs 536 crore through a fresh issue of equity shares as part of its proposed initial public offering (IPO).  

 According to its filing with the Registrar of Companies (RoC), AssetGro Fintech Ltd, the parent entity of StockGro, passed a resolution to approve the issue of up to Rs 536 crore in fresh equity and an offer for sale (OFS) by existing shareholders.  

 However, the size of the OFS has not been disclosed in the resolution. According to media reports, StockGro is looking to raise up to Rs 2,000 Cr-Rs 2,500 Cr through the initial public offering. 

 Notably, the company may raise up to Rs 107.2 crore through a pre-IPO placement before the offer. If completed, the amount raised through the placement will be deducted from the fresh issue, the filing further stated. 
 The company has yet to disclose the final IPO size, price band, valuation or listing timeline. In a separate filing ahead of its IPO, StockGro has passed a resolution to appoint Dinesh Chander Patwari, Yatrik Rushikesh Vin and Divya Dwivedi as independent directors for five-year terms. The company has also proposed appointing Diyanshi Agrawal as a nominee director. 

 Founded in 2020 by former venture capitalist Ajay Lakhotia, StockGro is building a full-stack ecosystem for smarter trading and investing through expert-driven insights and AI-powered research, delivered via its social-first, community-led platform. The company has raised over $100 million in funding to date, including investments from BITKRAFT Ventures, SBI Ventures, Root Ventures, and General Catalyst and others. 
 The company is yet to file its FY26 financials. Its operating revenue increased 30.99% to Rs 113.33 crore in FY25 from Rs 86.52 crore in FY24. It reported a profit of Rs 34.64 crore in FY25, compared to a loss of Rs 101.17 crore in the previous fiscal.

### [0] What happened
AssetGro Fintech Ltd, the parent of social-investing and stock-advisory platform **StockGro**, passed a Registrar-of-Companies (RoC) resolution to issue up to **Rs 536 crore (~$56M) in fresh equity** plus an undisclosed **offer for sale (OFS)** by existing shareholders, as part of a proposed IPO. The resolution also authorizes a **pre-IPO placement of up to Rs 107.2 crore (~$11M)**, which if completed would be deducted from the fresh-issue size. Separately, StockGro appointed three independent directors (Dinesh Chander Patwari, Yatrik Rushikesh Vin, Divya Dwivedi; five-year terms) and proposed a nominee director (Diyanshi Agrawal) — standard board-governance prep for a public listing. The company has **confidentially pre-filed its DRHP** with SEBI/BSE/NSE, so price band, final size, valuation and listing timeline remain undisclosed.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** India retail broking is deep and still expanding: demat accounts reached ~237.7M in Aug-2026 (+1.4% MoM) and NSE active clients ~46M (per BusinessToday/whalesbook, Aug–Sep 2026). The market is consolidated at the top — Groww ~29% share, Zerodha ~14.8%, Angel One ~14.6%; the top five (adding ICICI Securities, Upstox) held >67% of active clients in Sep-2026 (per startuptalky/lapaasvoice, Sep 2026). Barriers are regulatory (SEBI broker/RIA registration) plus network effects and acquisition cost in a saturating funnel. Why now: the 2025 listing wave (Groww IPO'd Nov-2025 at ~$9bn, stock ~doubled since) has reopened a public-market exit path and is pulling the next cohort — StockGro, and peers like Raise Financial — toward DRHP filings. StockGro's wedge is "social investing / advisory," not discount broking per se.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: active clients, market share of NSE active base, ARPU/revenue-per-user, and (for advisory) subscription/engagement. StockGro does NOT disclose active-client share and is not in NSE broker league tables — it sits in the advisory/community layer, not the execution-broking top five `[UNSOURCED active-client share]`. Players: discount-broking incumbents Groww, Zerodha, Angel One, Upstox; advisory/community adjacencies INDmoney, Smallcase. Recent moves: Groww completed its $150M Fisdom acquisition and launched its "GR 1" AI assistant (2026); Dhan turned unicorn on a $120M raise and Raise Financial hit unicorn on a $120M Series B (Oct-2025). StockGro's position: niche/catching-up — it monetizes advice and community rather than order flow, so its moat is content/community engagement and intangibles, not the scale/price moat of the discount brokers `(analysis)`. It must convert a social audience into paying/transacting users to justify broking-adjacent multiples.

**Comps & multiples.** StockGro FY25: revenue ₹125.5cr (~$15M; +26–31% YoY) and PAT ₹34.6cr (swing from a ₹101cr FY24 loss). Last private round ~$360M (Series B, round valuation not market cap). Targeted IPO ₹2,000–2,500cr (~$240–300M raise, fresh ₹536cr + OFS); final valuation undisclosed.
- [[Groww completes $150M acquisition of wealthtech platform Fisdom]] / Groww IPO: ~$9bn mcap, FY25 rev ₹39bn (~$440M), PAT ₹18bn → P/E 40.8x; implied P/S = $9bn / $440M ≈ 20.5x (per zerodha IPO page / Inspirepreneur, 2025–26).
- [[India's Raise Financial becomes unicorn after $120M Series B]] — ~$1bn round valuation (private comp).
- [[Dhan turns unicorn after $120 million fundraise]] — ~$1bn round valuation on a $120M raise (private comp).
Flag: at FY25 revenue ~$15M, even a conservative ~$360M last-round value implies P/S ~24x — above Groww's ~20x public P/S, and Groww is vastly larger and a profitable category leader. An IPO valuation materially above the $360M round would push StockGro to a rich/outlier multiple for a sub-$20M-revenue advisory name `(analysis)`. Public comp median not computed (only one listed broking comp with verifiable multiples; Zerodha/Angel One/Upstox market caps not pulled here → qualitative comparison).

**Risk flags.**
- Over-valuation vs comps: IPO target (₹2,000–2,500cr raise) against ~$15M FY25 revenue and a ₹34.6cr profit implies broking-leader multiples for a sub-scale advisory player — re-rating risk if the ₹2,000cr+ ambition isn't trimmed. Why: FY24 showed a 34% revenue drop + ₹101cr loss, so the FY25 turnaround is one year old and not yet proven across a cycle.
- Regulatory concentration on advisory: a "stock advisory / expert-insight" model is directly exposed to SEBI's tightening of registered-investment-adviser and finfluencer rules — a rule change can hit the core revenue line, unlike execution broking. Why: monetizing "tips"/advice is SEBI's primary enforcement target in 2024–26.
- Disintermediation by the top five: Groww/Zerodha already own the execution relationship and the active-client base (>67% combined) and are bolting on AI research and community features — StockGro risks being squeezed between free broker content and large wealth platforms. Why: if incumbents absorb the "community/advisory" layer, StockGro's differentiation collapses to a feature.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` StockGro's filing is less a sector re-rating than a second-wave IPO test: whether India's public market will fund an advisory/community broking-adjacent model at broking-leader multiples post-Groww. Falsifiable thesis: the book will force StockGro to anchor its ₹2,000cr+ target well below the implied >20x P/S, OR the raise shrinks toward the ₹536cr fresh issue. Trigger to watch: the DRHP/price band and disclosed active-client + transacting-user metrics — if those are thin, demand (and valuation) gets cut. What breaks the thesis: a confirmed, growing paying-user base and a second profitable year (FY26) would justify a premium.
