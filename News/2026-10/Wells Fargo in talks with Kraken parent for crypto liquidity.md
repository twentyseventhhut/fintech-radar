Payward, the parent company of crypto exchange Kraken, is in talks to become a crypto liquidity provider to U.S. financial giant Wells Fargo (WFC), according to two people with direct knowledge of the matter.
Under the potential deal, Wyoming-based Payward would supply liquidity for trading in crypto assets, the people said, speaking on condition of anonymity because the matter is private.
Talks are ongoing and may not result in a deal.
Payward and Wells Fargo both declined to comment.
Crypto exchanges often serve as gateways to digital asset liquidity for banks and institutional investors, providing access to trading venues and helping execute orders. For example, Coinbase Prime aggregates liquidity across multiple markets, while Kraken offers banks technology to integrate crypto trading into their own platforms, allowing them to serve clients without building the infrastructure themselves
The discussions suggest major banks are increasingly turning to established crypto companies to support their digital asset ambitions. A friendlier U.S. regulatory environment is also helping drive that shift. Under a more accommodating regulatory climate during President Donald Trump’s administration, major lenders are increasingly viewing established digital asset companies such as Payward as commercial partners, signaling the sector’s growing acceptance within traditional finance.
 The GENIUS Act , signed by Trump in July 2025, established a federal framework for payment stablecoins, providing clearer rules for a key link between crypto markets and the banking system.
During the industry’s banking squeeze, crypto firms struggled to secure basic banking services. Anchorage Digital CEO Nathan McCauley told the Senate Banking Committee in February 2025 that more than 40 banks rejected its requests for accounts despite its subsidiary holding a federal bank charter.
Wells Fargo already offers spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) to eligible wealth clients and has backed crypto compliance firm Elliptic and trading technology provider Talos. It has also announced plans for blockchain-based deposits and joined a consortium developing a dollar stablecoin, extending its digital asset activities into payments.
The California-based financial services firm strengthened its digital assets team earlier this year by hiring former Citi (C) banker Mark Gracia. The bank also served as Nasdaq’s exclusive capital markets adviser on the exchange operator’s September agreement to invest $100 million in Payward and deepen their collaboration on tokenized equities and market surveillance.
Payward is separately in talks with custody banking giant BNY over a broad financial-infrastructure partnership, CoinDesk reported last week. Those discussions could cover crypto products, custody, wealth management, trading and payments, further extending the company’s ties with established financial institutions.
Beyond operating Kraken, Payward offers trading, payments and financial infrastructure spanning spot crypto, derivatives, tokenized equities, custody, staking and traditional securities. Its Payward Services division provides infrastructure to banks, fintechs, brokerages and payment companies.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Wells Fargo (WFC) is reportedly **in early talks** for Kraken parent **Payward** to become a **crypto trading liquidity provider** — i.e. Payward would supply the liquidity/execution rail for WFC's digital-asset trading, with Kraken handling execution (and likely custody/compliance) behind the scenes while WFC keeps the client-facing relationship. Sourced to "two people with direct knowledge"; **both companies declined to comment; talks may not result in a deal.** This is an unconfirmed, pre-term-sheet report — no size, no economics, no timeline, no exclusivity disclosed.

**Why framed this way / what it reveals:** "Crypto liquidity" here does **not** mean WFC building an exchange. It means plugging into a wholesale execution venue so WFC can offer trading to wealth/institutional clients without standing up matching engines, market-making or 24/7 infra itself — the classic "exchange-as-plumbing" model (cf. Coinbase Prime, Kraken Prime, OTC desks like B2C2/Cumberland). The real question is not "is Wells Fargo doing crypto" (it clearly already is — see [2]) but **who owns the client and who owns the margin**: WFC wants the distribution and brand; Payward wants to be the invisible rail under TradFi. The leak itself is a signal — a pre-deal plant that advertises Payward's bank-grade credibility ahead of its IPO.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Direct pattern precedent:** [[Standard Chartered partners B2C2 on digital asset liquidity]] (Feb 2026) — a top-tier bank wiring an institutional crypto liquidity startup under the hood for its own clients. The WFC-Payward structure is the US mega-bank analog, with a larger, exchange-scale counterparty.
- **The rail rivals:** Coinbase Prime (smart order routing across venues), FalconX, Galaxy, Cumberland (DRW), Wintermute, B2C2 (web). Payward/Kraken competes here via **Kraken Prime** (launched 2025; "90%+ of market across 20+ venues," custody via state-chartered Kraken Financial).
- **The bank peers:** [[Citi]] plans crypto custody launch in 2026; [[Morgan Stanley files for Bitcoin and Solana ETFs]] + a Galaxy crypto-to-ETF bridge; JPMorgan tokenized MMF (JLTXX). WFC, JPMorgan, BofA, Citi are jointly building a **tokenized deposit network for 2027** ([[Top US banks plan tokenized deposit network for 2027]]).

**Why the landscape is this way / second-order:** Post-GENIUS Act (Jul 2025) + a friendlier Trump-era regulatory climate, banks have flipped from debanking crypto firms to **renting their infrastructure**. The scarce asset is a **regulated, bank-grade execution venue that a compliance team will sign off on** — which is why exchanges with charters (Kraken Financial, Coinbase) and licensed OTC desks win, not pure crypto-native shops. WFC choosing Payward over Coinbase is notable given WFC already *advised Nasdaq on its $100M Payward investment* — the relationship is pre-wired, lowering diligence friction (cf. [[Nasdaq partners with Kraken for tokenized stock trading]]).

## [2] Company history / fit
Wells Fargo's crypto push is **multi-year and accelerating**, so this fits cleanly:
- Offers spot Bitcoin ETFs to eligible wealth clients; backed compliance firm Elliptic and trading-tech provider Talos.
- Filed the **WFUSD** stablecoin/crypto trademark ([[Wells Fargo files WFUSD trademark for crypto]], Mar 2026); joined a dollar-stablecoin consortium; announced blockchain-based deposits.
- Hired ex-Citi banker Mark Gracia to build the digital-assets team (2026).
- Served as Nasdaq's exclusive adviser on its **$100M Payward investment (~$21B valuation)** — direct prior tie to this counterparty.

**Why WFC acts this way:** A universal bank facing wealth clients who now expect crypto access can't afford to be the laggard, but building execution infra is capital-intensive and off its core competency. Renting Payward's rail is the **capital-light, speed-to-market** move — it monetizes WFC's distribution without owning the riskiest, most commoditized part of the stack (matching/market-making).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty is modest.** The *structure* (bank + exchange liquidity rail) is not new — [[Standard Chartered partners B2C2 on digital asset liquidity]] did it, Coinbase Prime services banks, Kraken already sells "integrate crypto into your own platform" infra to banks. What's new here is the **name**: a US top-4 bank publicly linked to Kraken. **Traction = zero so far** — this is talks, not a signed deal, no live product, no numbers. Treat as a signal, not a shipment.

**Why the value-add is real-but-contested / who captures margin:** For Payward, every TradFi anchor (WFC, [[BNY in talks with Kraken parent over infrastructure deal]], SoFi) de-risks its pre-IPO story and shifts it from "retail crypto exchange" to "regulated financial infrastructure" — a higher multiple. **But** as the invisible rail, Payward captures thin wholesale/execution spread while WFC captures the client relationship and the richer wealth-management fee. The strategic risk for Payward is the same as any infra provider: it trains the bank, and the bank can later **in-source** or multi-home liquidity (Coinbase Prime smart-routes across venues precisely so no single exchange owns the flow). Durable value for Payward depends on exclusivity/switching costs that are **not disclosed** — likely because they don't exist yet.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect either a quiet signed agreement or quiet death — "talks may not result in a deal" is doing heavy lifting. Backdrop is bullish for such tie-ups: GENIUS Act framework, Fed/OCC thaw, and Payward's IPO timing (which has slipped toward 2027 per prior notes) create incentive to stack blue-chip bank relationships. **Regulatory risk:** the CLARITY Act stalled, so market-structure rules remain unsettled; a regime change could re-chill bank appetite.

**Why the market goes this way / counterintuitive second-order:** The visible trend is "banks embrace crypto." The less obvious effect is that banks embracing crypto via *rented exchange rails* **deepens exchanges' dependence on TradFi distribution while keeping exchanges in the low-margin plumbing tier** — the bank, not the exchange, ends up owning the profitable customer. Concurrently, as multiple banks adopt the same handful of liquidity venues, those venues gain systemic importance → inviting the very prudential scrutiny crypto firms sought to escape (cf. [[Bank groups voice concern over Kraken Fed master account]], [[Kraken's Fed master account raises financial system risk concerns]]).
