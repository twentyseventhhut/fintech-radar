EdfaPay and GoPravo Launch SDPay to Expand Digital Payments in Sudan

 
 F
 
 

 
 
 
 

 
 
 
 

 
 
 
 

 
 E
 
 
SDPay aims to enable merchants to accept payments and improve customer acquisition by providing easy payment options, particularly through QR codes, point-of-sale (POS) devices, and payment processing, alongside providing online payment gateways.
 EdfaPay,   the Saudi payment technology company, and GoPravo   , a Sudanese IT company, have officially launched the joint venture, SDPay, to provide and operate financial services and payments infrastructure in Sudan.
The new entity is a 50/50 joint venture between EdfaPay and GoPravo, with a focus on serving banks, telecom operators, merchants, government entities, and individuals across Sudan by harnessing the Saudi fintech developed by EdfaPay and the local expertise in the Sudanese market from GoPravo.
SDPay aims to enable merchants to accept payments and improve customer acquisition by providing easy payment options, particularly through QR codes, point-of-sale (POS) devices, and payment processing, alongside providing online payment gateways. It will also provide digital wallet, banking services, account aggregation solutions, and financial management for customers. Additionally, it will enable customers to issue payment cards and provide Processing-as-a-Service (PaaS).
The company will also provide bill payment and government collection services, integration with payroll disbursement platforms, and international cross-border remittance services, while enabling the government sector with various services ranging from digital payments infrastructure to embedded finance solutions and others.
 “Sudan has the human talent, widespread mobile penetration, and a need to build a modern payments ecosystem. We are working together, with GoPravo, to deliver infrastructure grounded in international standards, managed and operated from within Sudan. Our goal is to enter the market to offer integrated services leveraging technologies and expertise that have already operated under the supervision of a central regulator. This is to make a tangible impact on the lives of people and the business sector, from accelerating a merchant’s receipt of their payments to reducing the cost of remittances reaching beneficiary families.” 
 – Ghormallah AlGhamdi, Chairman of SDPay and CEO, EdfaPay 
 
 Enhancing the Banking Ecosystem and Payments Infrastructure 
SDPay seeks to empower clients with integrated and secure services to manage their businesses, offering integration via APIs, digital identity integration, and electronic Know Your Customer (eKYC) verification, alongside fraud monitoring and risk management systems, 3D Secure authentication solutions, financial settlement and reconciliation services, as well as financial analytics and reporting platforms.
 “Sudan has sustained demand for digital financial services, but the challenge lies in integration. Banks, e-wallets, telecom operators, and government collection systems often operate on separate systems, which highlights the need for a shared infrastructure that connects them. Here comes the role of SDPay to combine the technology platform and the regulatory and engineering expertise provided by EdfaPay with the local market knowledge, licenses, relationships, and execution capabilities provided by GoPravo. This integration forms the foundation for building a connected digital financial ecosystem through a registered and capitalized company headquartered in Khartoum.” 
 – Mohamed Elhag, CEO, SDPay

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened (2026-10-21):** EdfaPay (Saudi payment-technology firm) and GoPravo (a Sudanese IT company) officially launched **SDPay**, a 50/50 joint venture headquartered in Khartoum, to build and operate payments and financial-services infrastructure in Sudan. Ghormallah Al-Ghamdi (EdfaPay CEO) chairs SDPay; Mohamed Elhag is SDPay CEO. Planned stack: QR, POS, online gateways, digital wallets, card issuing, Processing-as-a-Service (PaaS), bill payment / government collections, payroll disbursement, cross-border remittances, plus eKYC, 3D-Secure, fraud/risk and settlement/reconciliation layers. Target clients: banks, telecom operators, merchants, government entities and individuals. (Sources: TechAfrica News, Finextra, Zawya, Africa Fintech Network.)

**Who EdfaPay is.** A Riyadh-based SoftPOS / merchant-acquiring enabler. It obtained a technical license as a soft-POS (mobile point-of-sale) technology provider from the Saudi Central Bank (**SAMA**) in Dec 2024, letting Android phones act as card terminals without dedicated hardware. It raised a **~$5M pre-Series A led by OmanTel Innovation Labs** (with Aljabr MENA, Waad Investment) and has pushed into Oman, Tunisia, Morocco, Pakistan and Brazil. SDPay is its first JV-structured market entry into a conflict economy. The SoftPOS angle fits the regional SoftPOS wave: cf. [[Arab Financial Services launches SoftPOS in Egypt]], [[CBE grants Arab Financial Services a SoftPOS license]], [[Egypt's central bank introduces soft POS contactless payments]], [[Tapaya raises €1 million pre-seed for SoftPOS platform]].

**Who GoPravo is.** A Sudanese IT company bringing local licenses, central-bank relationships, market knowledge and execution capacity — the "feet on the ground" half of the JV. Public profile is thin; no independent financials verified.

**Sudan macro context — this is the key framing.** Sudan has been in civil war since April 2023 (SAF vs RSF). The banking system is in crisis: up to ~70% of bank branches have closed, banks have lost roughly half their capital, industrial activity in urban centres has collapsed ~90%, average incomes have fallen to ~1992 levels, and the currency and inflation remain in distress (Al Jazeera, Sep 2026; MDPI bank-stress study; STPT/Dabanga). Critically, the monetary system is **fragmenting along the conflict line**: the central bank (CBOS) reissued banknotes that largely exclude RSF-held areas, while RSF zones use parallel tools such as the "Future Money" / El Mustaqbal Bank app. Any national payments rail is therefore being built on a partitioned, contested monetary base.

**Existing rails SDPay must displace or ride on.** Mobile banking is already concentrated: **Bankak** (Bank of Khartoum) has 10M+ subscribers and is the dominant transfer app, alongside Fawry (Faisal Islamic Bank), O-Cash (Omdurman National), Sahel (Nile Bank) and apps from Al Baraka/Mashreq/Al Tadamon. These apps suffer recurring outages (Bankak network failures reported Sep 2026). On policy, CBOS is pushing financial inclusion, USSD channels (for no-internet areas), agent banking and government-payment digitization (AFI; Developing Telecoms). So the demand narrative ("mobile penetration + need for modern rails") is real, but the incumbent and infrastructure gaps are severe.

**Saudi–Sudan fintech linkage precedent:** [[Network International partners Saudi Sudanese Bank]] shows cross-border Saudi/Gulf payment players already eyeing Sudanese banking. Broader Saudi outbound-fintech momentum: [[Fintech Saudi industry grew 30x to 301 players by 2025]], [[Ateon receives Saudi SAMA payment services license]].

**Bottom line:** A credibly-licensed Saudi SoftPOS vendor pairing with a local partner to stand up a full-stack payments company in a war-fractured, high-inflation, incumbent-dominated market. The ambition (QR→cards→PaaS→remittances→gov collections) is expansive for a brand-new entity; execution risk, regulatory-split risk and capital adequacy are the open questions.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. The announcement is a *launch of a JV entity*, not live volume. Is there any processed transaction, live merchant count, or go-live date — or is this an intent/registration milestone dressed as a product launch?
2. SDPay lists QR, POS, gateways, wallets, card issuing, PaaS, bill pay, gov collections, payroll, remittances, eKYC, 3DS, fraud, settlement. That is an entire national payments stack. Which modules actually exist on day one vs. roadmap?
3. Sudan's monetary system is fragmenting along the SAF/RSF line, with CBOS banknotes excluding RSF areas. Which territory does SDPay serve, and is it effectively only a SAF-controlled-area play?
4. What is SDPay's capitalization? "Registered and capitalized company headquartered in Khartoum" is asserted — but no figure is disclosed. Is EdfaPay's ~$5M pre-Series A balance sheet adequate to fund a de-novo payments company in a war zone?
5. Does SDPay hold (or only "relationships to obtain") the required CBOS licenses — payment institution, acquirer, wallet issuer, remittance? GoPravo's "licenses" are claimed, not evidenced.
6. Incumbency: Bankak alone has 10M+ users. What is SDPay's wedge, and does it compete with banks it also wants as clients (channel conflict)?
7. Infrastructure reality: electricity, telecom and internet are degraded by war; CBOS is pushing USSD precisely because internet is unreliable. How does a QR/SoftPOS/app model work where connectivity is intermittent?
8. Cross-border remittances into Sudan face FX controls, correspondent-banking de-risking and possible sanctions exposure (RSF-linked entities are sanctioned). What compliance stance covers this?
9. Is EdfaPay the technology licensor earning fees, or a true risk-bearing equity partner? A 50/50 split could be nominal if GoPravo carries operational/regulatory risk and EdfaPay just licenses software.
10. GoPravo has a minimal public footprint. What is its track record, ownership, and any political/military affiliation — material given Sudan's governance context?
11. Currency risk: the Sudanese pound is in freefall with high inflation. How are settlement, float and merchant payouts protected against devaluation?
12. "Operated and managed from within Sudan" plus "international standards" — can PCI-DSS/scheme certification and secure data operations realistically be maintained inside an active conflict zone?
13. Is this a genuine market-building move or primarily a flag-planting PR / optionality play for EdfaPay's MENA-expansion narrative (same framing as its Oman/Tunisia/Morocco announcements)?
14. Government-collection and payroll ambitions imply deep public-sector contracts. With contested state authority, which "government" is the counterparty?
15. Independent verification: nearly all coverage traces to the companies' own press release (TechAfrica/Finextra/Zawya/Africa Fintech Network reprints). Is there any third-party or regulator confirmation?

**Importance: 2/5 — rationale:** A real, credibly-licensed Saudi SoftPOS player (SAMA-licensed, $5M-backed, multi-market) entering Sudan via a local JV is a genuine datapoint for Gulf-outbound fintech and MENA-Africa payments corridors. But as news it is an early-stage JV *launch* — no volume, no disclosed capital, no confirmed go-live — into one of the most distressed, war-fragmented and incumbent-dominated payments markets on the continent, with heavy execution, regulatory-split, FX and connectivity risk. High thematic interest, low near-term materiality; sourcing is essentially a single press release.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** SDPay sits in two overlapping subverticals: soft-POS / mobile acquiring and payment-infrastructure-as-a-service (switching, gateway, wallet, PaaS). Global softPOS is small but fast: valued ~$429m in 2025, growing to ~$518m in 2026 and a projected ~$2.3bn by 2034, CAGR ~20.6% (per Straits Research, via grandviewresearch/straitsresearch, as of 2026). Africa's mobile-payments proximity segment is the fastest-growing slice at ~28% CAGR, with smartphone-based POS the fastest-growing POS segment in frontier markets (per marketdataforecast / indexbox, as of 2026). The Sudan market itself is nascent, not sized: <10% of adults hold an account against 33.3m mobile subscribers, card penetration negligible, e-commerce in infancy (per FSD Africa / UNCDF / IFC assessments, 2022-2023). Structure: highly fragmented and un-interoperable — banks, e-wallets, telcos (Zain ~48%, MTN ~28%, Sudatel ~24% subs) and government collection run on separate rails; the explicit SDPay thesis is to be the shared switching layer connecting them. Barriers are regulatory (Central Bank of Sudan licensing; CBoS ran a fintech sandbox from 2016) and capital — one assessment put the bar at ~$10.5m over three years to reach profitability for a Sudan fintech (per IFC, 2023). Why now: mobile penetration + a cash-heavy, post-conflict economy needing cheaper remittances and merchant digitisation; EdfaPay brings a regulator-supervised MENA stack, GoPravo brings local licenses/relationships.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for acquiring — TPV, take rate, active merchants, per-transaction margin; for a switch/PaaS — transactions processed, connected institutions, uptime. SDPay discloses none yet (pre-launch JV), so all `[UNSOURCED]`. Key players / basis of competition: incumbent bank-owned switches and telco mobile money (Zain/MTN/Sudatel wallets) compete on existing distribution and licenses; SDPay competes on product breadth (QR + POS + gateway + wallet + card issuing + PaaS + gov collection + cross-border remittance) and on interoperability as a neutral layer. Recent comparable peer moves: Arab Financial Services launched SoftPOS in Egypt after a CBE license (2026-01) — same soft-POS-into-a-frontier/MENA-market play, de-risked by a central-bank license first. Protagonist's position: niche / early-entrant in an underserved market, no incumbent all-in-one switch — first-mover optionality but unproven execution. Moat `(analysis)`: prospective regulatory licenses + local relationships (GoPravo) layered on EdfaPay's tech — a distribution/intangibles moat IF licenses and bank integrations actually land; today it is announced, not live/adopted.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation, round, TPV or revenue disclosed for the SDPay JV — multiples = **no data**. EdfaPay (the Saudi parent) has raised ~$9.8m total, incl. a $5m pre-Series A led by OmanTel Innovation Labs closing 2024-11 (per PitchBook/IBS Intelligence); no public post-money, so EV/Revenue and P/S are `[UNSOURCED]`. GoPravo is a ~5-person, unfunded Khartoum IT firm founded 2017 (per Tracxn, 2026) — no financials. Internal comps (base), qualitative only — distribution not computed (no figures):
- [[Colombia's Redeban enters Peru with merchant acquiring]] — regional acquirer cross-border market entry; same "established acquirer enters an adjacent underserved market" pattern.
- [[Arab Financial Services launches SoftPOS in Egypt]] / [[CBE grants Arab Financial Services a SoftPOS license]] — closest comp: MENA soft-POS launch gated on a central-bank license.
- [[VaultsPay and Mastercard partner to expand UAE digital payments]] — MENA payments infra expansion via partnership.
In-line/rich/cheap flag: not applicable — no priced metric on either side.

**Risk flags.**
1. Country / conflict risk (acute). Sudan is in active civil war; real GDP contracted 13.5% in 2024, FX reserves ~$1.17bn (~1.1 months of imports), NPLs ~16.5% (per AfDB, 2025-26). A payments build-out depends on functioning banks, power and telecoms that the conflict directly degrades — execution timeline and merchant base are hostage to the war.
2. FX / macro risk. The Sudanese pound hit a record ~10,000/USD in parallel trading (vs ~600 pre-war), inflation ~28-46% in 2026 (per Sudan Tribune / CNBC Africa, 2026). Revenue earned in SDP erodes fast in hard-currency terms, and cross-border remittance — a headline use case — is squeezed by parallel-rate spreads and sanctions-era correspondent-banking friction.
3. Regulatory / execution risk. The entire value proposition (switch, PaaS, card issuing, gov collection) is contingent on CBoS licenses and bank/telco integrations that are announced, not secured/live; the JV is newly "registered and capitalized" in Khartoum with no disclosed traction. Who's silent: capital committed, fraud/settlement liability split between EdfaPay and GoPravo, and merchant pricing — all undisclosed.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a frontier new-entry bet, not a re-rating: a Saudi acquirer using a 50/50 local JV to buy license/distribution access into a war-economy market no established switch serves. Falsifiable thesis: SDPay becomes Sudan's shared interbank/wallet switching layer. Trigger to confirm — a CBoS operating license plus a named bank or telco (Zain/MTN/Sudatel) live on the platform with disclosed volumes. What breaks it — the war disrupts banking/power, FX collapse makes SDP revenue uneconomic, or incumbents' own wallets keep rails siloed; a 5-person local partner is a thin execution base for this scope.
