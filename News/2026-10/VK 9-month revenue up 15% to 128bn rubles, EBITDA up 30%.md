## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)

On 2026-10-28 VK Company published Q3 and 9M-2026 IFRS results. The headline, de-PR'd:

- **9M-2026 revenue RUB 128bn, +15% YoY.** EBITDA RUB 20.2bn, +30% YoY; EBITDA margin ~16%.
- **Q3-2026 revenue RUB 47bn, +21% YoY; EBITDA RUB 6.2bn, +22% YoY.** Q3 growth (+21%) is faster than the 9M figure (+15%) — i.e. H1 was weaker and the top line is re-accelerating off online advertising.
- **Online advertising (VK Ads) revenue +18% to RUB 28.2bn in Q3**, with large advertisers +29% to RUB 18bn and in-stream video ads +48% to RUB 2.7bn. Advertising remains the engine; growth is concentrated in large brands and video.
- **VK Tech (B2B/cloud/SaaS) revenue +35% to RUB 14.4bn over 9M** — the fastest-growing segment and the stated diversification story.
- **EdTech for children +26% to RUB 6.8bn; social commerce RUB 2.1bn over 9M, +65% vs full-year 2025.**
- **Net operating cash flow RUB 17.4bn**, turning positive after a negative figure in the same period of 2025.

**Why structured / framed this way.** VK leads on revenue +15% and EBITDA +30% because the EBITDA line is the real turnaround proof point, not revenue. Context the PR under-weights: VK posted a **net loss of ~RUB 24.9-25.4bn in 2025** (its sixth straight annual loss), though that narrowed sharply from ~RUB 94.9bn in 2024. It carries heavy debt — net debt was cut by more than half to ~RUB 82bn in 2025, but only after shareholders recapitalised VK by ~RUB 112bn; 2025 interest expense (~RUB 23bn) essentially consumed all of adjusted EBITDA (~RUB 22.6bn). So the +30% EBITDA headline matters mostly because EBITDA still has to cover interest before anything reaches net income. The press release reports EBITDA, cash flow and segment growth — but **not a return to net profit**, which independent coverage says is unlikely near-term.

## [1] Competitors / peers

- **Yandex** — the dominant Russian internet/ad peer, far larger and profitable, and now pushing into payments (Yandex Pay) and fintech; VK competes with it in ads, cloud (VK Tech vs Yandex Cloud) and super-app ambitions. See [[Yandex Pay still down after Yandex data-center outages]].
- **Sber / T-Bank ecosystems** — the real "super-app + fintech" winners in Russia; they, not VK, own the payments/credit graph VK Pay would need.
- **Max messenger** — VK's state-backed national messenger push is the strategic bet to rebuild distribution; channel/user metrics ([[VK grows Max public channels to 530K in Q3 2026]]) are the leading indicator, but Max is not yet a material revenue line and MTS Bank, not VK, is embedding banking into Max chats ([[MTS Bank enables money transfers inside Max chats]]).
- **Product cadence** — [[VK launches Discovery AI neural search across products]], [[VK launches VK Data Catalog with AI-agent access]] show VK chasing an AI/B2B narrative that feeds VK Tech.

**Why the landscape is this way.** VK sits structurally behind Yandex and Sber: it has the social-graph/attention assets but not the payments rails or the balance sheet. Its growth is sectoral (ad-market recovery + VK Tech import-substitution tailwind), not a VK-specific moat. VK Tech's +35% is the one place VK may command a software multiple rather than a cyclical ad multiple — hence management spotlights it.

## [2] Company history / fit

VK (ex-Mail.ru Group) is a state-adjacent Russian tech holding (VKontakte, Odnoklassniki, VK Video, VK Tech/cloud, Dzen, Max, EdTech, VK Pay). After Western platform exits (2022+) it gained share by default in Russian social/ad, but mismanaged capital and accumulated losses and debt through 2024. 2025 was the stabilisation year (net debt halved via a ~RUB 112bn recap, EBITDA turned positive). 9M-2026 is the "proof of operating recovery" chapter: revenue growth re-accelerating, EBITDA +30%, operating cash flow positive.

**Why VK acts this way.** The structural pressure is the debt/interest load: VK must show expanding EBITDA and positive cash flow to service ~RUB 23bn/yr interest and justify the shareholder recap. That forces the dual narrative — milk the recovering ad business for cash, and build VK Tech + Max to earn a higher, less-cyclical multiple. The fintech/VK Pay angle is subordinate: it is a social-commerce/loyalty feature, not a licensed bank, so it rides social commerce (RUB 2.1bn, small) rather than being a standalone fintech driver.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction

Genuinely new: this is a **new reporting period (9M / Q3 2026), first disclosed 2026-10-28** — not a re-report. Real traction signals: EBITDA +30%, Q3 revenue +21% (re-acceleration), operating cash flow swinging positive, VK Tech +35%, large-advertiser and video-ad growth.

**Why the value-add is real-but-capped.** The operating turnaround is real at the EBITDA/cash line. But the margin is captured by lenders, not shareholders: interest expense ~= EBITDA, so net income stays negative and equity value depends on continued deleveraging. The fintech thesis is thin — VK does not own payments/credit rails (Sber/T-Bank/Yandex do), VK Pay is a commerce feature, and money-in-chat inside Max is being supplied by MTS Bank. So VK's durable value is advertising + VK Tech, not fintech.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment

- Management guided **2026 EBITDA to exceed RUB 20bn** (already RUB 20.2bn at 9M, so Q4 adds more). Watch: continued net-debt reduction, interest coverage, and whether VK Tech keeps a software-like growth/margin profile.
- Sentiment is cautiously positive on operations but skeptical on equity: analysts flag weak structural ad momentum, high borrowing costs and monetisation uncertainty on new products; a near-term return to net profit is seen as unlikely.
- **Second-order:** VK's recovery is heavily policy/state-driven (Max as the sanctioned national messenger, import-substitution demand for VK Tech). That makes the growth less a product win than a regulatory tailwind — supportive now, but it ties VK's fortunes to state priorities rather than market competitiveness, and leaves it exposed if ad spend or state backing softens.

**Sector & drivers.** VK is a Russian internet/ecosystem group (social, ads, VK Tech B2B cloud/SaaS, EdTech, the Max super-app). Its core P&L engine is online advertising. Russian internet advertising is large but decelerating: per ACAR/ADPASS citations, the "internet services" (contextual + targeted) segment reached ~RUB 510bn in 2025 (+9% YoY), and the broader online-ad market's growth collapsed from ~53% in 2024 to ~28% in 2025, with a 2026 forecast of only ~+12% (per ADPASS, Nov-2025, via iz.ru/caliber.az; figures vary by methodology, treat as directional). Structure: effectively a duopoly — Yandex + VK capture the bulk of domestic digital ad budgets after the exit of Google/Meta-era inventory, a structural tailwind for both from import-substitution. Why now: the slowing ad cycle is pushing VK to lean on faster non-ad legs (VK Tech, EdTech, the Max messenger) for growth — exactly the mix shift the 9M print shows. [Market sizes are third-party estimates; VK does not disclose its own ad-market share.]

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for this model: advertising revenue growth, VK Tech ARR/recurring revenue, Adj. EBITDA margin, net debt/EBITDA (VK is highly levered). Key players: Yandex (far larger — Q3 2025 revenue RUB 366bn, +32%; 9M 2025 >RUB 1trn; Adj. EBITDA RUB 78.1bn, +43% — per Reuters/AKM, so ~8x VK's revenue scale and growing roughly 2x faster) and VK. Basis of competition: distribution/network effects (VK's social graph + Max super-app) and, increasingly, cloud/B2B. Recent moves: (Oct-2026) Mintsifry confirmed Yandex and VK will combine cloud assets into a JV targeting ~⅓ of the Russian cloud market (per cnews.ru) — cooperation, not just rivalry, in B2B; VK is simultaneously pushing Max monetization (channel ads opened to >1K-subscriber accounts, native-ad tools). Protagonist's position: domestic #2 behind Yandex, catching up only in cloud; moat = network effects of the social/messaging base + regulatory favouring of domestic players `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** VK (MOEX: VKCO) 9M 2026 (news + VK press release): revenue RUB 128bn (+15% YoY), Adj. EBITDA RUB 20.2bn (+30%), EBITDA margin ~16%; VK Tech RUB 14.4bn (+35%), kids' EdTech RUB 6.8bn (+26%); Q3 EBITDA RUB 6.2bn (+22%); 9M operating cash flow +RUB 17.4bn (vs negative a year earlier). IR context: FY2025 revenue RUB 160bn (+8%), VK Tech RUB 18.8bn (+38%) — so 9M 2026's +15% top line is a re-acceleration vs the 8% FY2025 pace. Internal comps (base): [[Yandex Direct adds personalized ad targeting under Max posts]], [[VK launches VK Data Catalog with AI-agent access]], [[VK sells 40% stake in ticketing service Intickets]], [[VK grows Max public channels to 530K in Q3 2026]]. EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E = no data (MOEX market cap / EV / net debt not independently verified here; VK carries material net debt, so EV ≫ market cap). Peer Yandex trades at a far larger scale and faster growth; VK's low 16% EBITDA margin and leverage mean it screens as the lower-quality domestic internet name — qualitative only, distribution not computed (<3 verifiable multiples).

**Risk flags.**
1. Leverage/margin — only ~16% EBITDA margin on a heavily indebted balance sheet; EBITDA +30% is partly off a depressed base, and rising rates in Russia make debt service the swing factor on equity value. Why: interest cost, not revenue, likely governs the equity story.
2. Ad-cycle dependence into a sharp slowdown — core ad demand growth is decelerating to ~double digits market-wide; VK's +15% leans on VK Tech/EdTech, so a cloud stumble would expose the slowing ad base. Why: concentration of growth in a few non-ad legs.
3. Competitive asymmetry vs Yandex — Yandex is ~8x larger and growing ~2x faster; the planned cloud JV could entrench VK as junior partner, ceding B2B economics. Why: disintermediation of VK's best growth leg by a stronger partner.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Confirms the domestic-internet duopoly thesis: VK is re-accelerating off import-substitution and a maturing Max/VK Tech mix, but remains the structurally weaker, more-levered name beside Yandex `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: if VK Tech growth (+35% 9M) holds and EBITDA margin expands past ~20% while net debt/EBITDA falls, VK re-rates; watch the Yandex–VK cloud JV terms (Oct-2026) as the catalyst — if VK enters as minority, that caps the upside. Trigger to watch: FY2026 print and JV ownership split.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (headline read).** BEAT (vs prior-year trajectory; no public RU analyst consensus) · 9M 2026 revenue RUB 128.0bn (+15% YoY) · EBITDA RUB 20.2bn (+30% YoY), margin 16% (+2pp YoY) · Q3 alone re-accelerated to RUB 47.0bn (+21% YoY) · drivers: Social Platforms & Media Content advertising + a sharply profitable VK Tech/EdTech mix · guidance reaffirmed (FY 2026 EBITDA "more than RUB 24bn"). The real story is the profitability/deleveraging turn — operating cash flow swung to +RUB 17.4bn (vs negative in 9M 2025) and net debt fell 11% to RUB 73.3bn. But VK withheld a net-profit/loss figure in the release framing — the thesis still hinges on whether a company that lost RUB 25.4bn (FY 2025) can convert the EBITDA turn into net profit. Source: official VK press release [12477].

**Key figures (with growth).** 9M 2026 vs 9M 2025:
- Revenue RUB 128.0bn, +15% YoY (9M 2025: RUB 111.3bn). Growth RE-ACCELERATED — 9M 2025 was only +10% YoY, and Q3 2026 was +21% YoY, the fastest quarterly print in over a year.
- EBITDA RUB 20.2bn, +30% YoY (9M 2025: RUB 15.5bn); margin 16% vs 14% a year ago (+2pp). EBITDA growing ~2x revenue growth = genuine operating leverage, not just topline.
- Q3 2026: revenue RUB 47.0bn (+21% YoY), EBITDA RUB 6.2bn (+22% YoY).
- Net profit / loss: NOT disclosed in the release summary (de-PR flag — see below). FY 2025 net loss was RUB 25.4bn under IFRS (akm.ru).
- Operating cash flow 9M 2026: +RUB 17.4bn, vs negative in 9M 2025 — the single most important turn in the print.

**By segment / driver.**
- Social Platforms & Media Content (the core): revenue RUB 91.3bn (+18% YoY), EBITDA RUB 18.5bn (+19% YoY) — this segment is the profit engine (91% of group EBITDA).
- Online advertising: RUB 76.8bn (+11% YoY) — the largest line but the SLOWEST grower; a deceleration flag given ad is ~60% of revenue. In-stream/video advertising RUB 7.6bn (+44% YoY) is the bright spot inside it; large-business ads in Q3 RUB 18.0bn (+29% YoY).
- VK Tech (B2B cloud/SaaS): revenue RUB 14.4bn (+35% YoY), EBITDA RUB 1.8bn (+21% YoY). Fast but EBITDA grew slower than revenue here — margin dilution as it scales/invests (contrast FY 2024 when VK Tech EBITDA +138%).
- EdTech (children's education): RUB 6.8bn (+26% YoY), EBITDA RUB 0.9bn (+70% YoY) — small but the fastest margin expansion.
- Social commerce: RUB 2.1bn 9M (+65% vs full-2025 base), RUB 3.4bn cumulative since 2025 launch; 43,000+ Ozon sellers — early optionality, not yet material.
- MAX messenger: 140m registered / 109m MAU; monetization nascent — only "more than RUB 700m" of business/ad revenue Jul–Oct 2026. VK Video 82.1m MAU (+8% YoY); VKontakte DAU 64.2m (+4.7m YoY).

**vs expectations / prior period.** No public RU sell-side consensus is aggregated for VKCO (MOEX) → beat/miss assessed vs prior periods, not Street. YoY momentum is clearly positive and ACCELERATING: 9M revenue +10% (2025) → +15% (2026); Q3 +21% is the standout. Margin 14% → 16%. Against VK's own trajectory this is a beat. Internal trend (corpus): [[VK 9-month revenue up 15% to 128bn rubles, EBITDA up 30%]] is this note; prior reference points are 9M 2025 (RUB 111.3bn, EBITDA RUB 15.5bn, 14%) and FY 2025 (RUB 160bn revenue +8%, net loss RUB 25.4bn). The +21% Q3 vs +8% FY 2025 revenue growth is a sharp inflection worth watching. [UNSOURCED] for any quantified consensus beat — none exists publicly.

**Guidance / forward.** REAFFIRMED. Management maintains the FY 2026 EBITDA target of "more than RUB 24bn" and a net-debt/EBITDA target of 2–3x by year-end (currently ~2.7x). To hit >RUB 24bn FY after RUB 20.2bn in 9M implies ~RUB 3.8bn+ in Q4 EBITDA — consistent with the Q3 run-rate (RUB 6.2bn), so the guide looks conservative/achievable barring ad softness. Management tone: confident on the EBITDA/cash-flow turn and MAX user growth; notably quiet on the net loss and on MAX monetization economics.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. EBITDA turn looks REAL, not cosmetic — EBITDA +30% on revenue +15% AND operating cash flow swinging to +RUB 17.4bn (from negative) means the margin gain is cash-backed, not accrual engineering. Second-order: deleveraging (net debt −11% to RUB 73.3bn, 2.7x) reduces the equity-raise/dilution risk that has dogged VK. This is the strongest positive in the print.
2. Net profit still undisclosed → the headline hides the bottom line. VK lost RUB 25.4bn in FY 2025; RUB 20.2bn of 9M EBITDA must still clear heavy D&A and interest on RUB 73bn net debt before it reaches net profit. Why it matters: "EBITDA turned" is not "profitable." Flag: PR leads with EBITDA/cash, stays silent on net result — classic de-PR tell. Treat the turn as improving, not complete, until a net-income line is confirmed.
3. Advertising decelerating to +11% while it is ~60% of revenue — the group's growth re-acceleration (+15%/+21%) is carried by the smaller, faster segments (VK Tech +35%, EdTech +26%, video ads +44%). Second-order: if the macro ad market softens, the high-margin core could stall and the whole EBITDA bridge leans harder on sub-scale B2B.
4. MAX is the strategic call option but unmonetized — 140m registered / 109m MAU is a land-grab (state-backed messenger push), yet only ~RUB 0.7bn revenue Jul–Oct. It is a cost centre today; the bull case needs a monetization path management has not quantified.
