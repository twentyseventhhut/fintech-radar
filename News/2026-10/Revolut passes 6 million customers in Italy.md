Revolut’s customer growth in Italy passes 6 million, ranking third by users
Revolut’s customer growth in Italy has pushed it past six million users , making it the country’s third-largest bank by customer count, behind UniCredit and Intesa Sanpaolo. Its ambition is to take the top spot, even as the ECB has ordered it to slow new product launches over shortcomings in its approval process.
Summary
 Key takeaways 
Italy ranks third among Revolut’s European markets.
Under-35s account for 60% of its Italian customers.
Revolut’s Italian business customer base has reached 62,000.
Credit cards and mortgages are priorities for Italy.
According to Corriere della Sera , Revolut had five million Italian customers in May. Nicola Vicino, its general manager in Italy, said the company wants to become the country’s leading bank : “Our ambition goes well beyond: we want to become the first bank in Italy.”
 Revolut’s customer growth in Italy extends beyond younger users 
Italy is Revolut’s third-largest European market , after France and Germany, and is growing faster than Germany, Vicino said. The Italian customer base remains predominantly young, but older users with more complex financial needs are also increasing.
 60% of Italian customers are under 35 , with travel, trading and virtual SIMs among their uses of the platform. Vicino described Revolut as a “family bank,” saying it serves different usage profiles.
The expansion also reaches corporate banking. Revolut now serves 62,000 business clients in Italy , an increase of 36%.
 UniCredit’s warning and Revolut’s independent strategy 
UniCredit CEO Andrea Orcel has identified Revolut as one of the most formidable competitors to traditional banks, urging them to accelerate their technological transformation. “If you are not able to beat them, fintechs will end up eating you,” he said.
Vicino welcomed that assessment but acknowledged that an exclusively online offering cannot yet serve the entire Italian market. He nevertheless described the push toward digitalization as very strong.
Revolut does not intend to join Italy’s banking “Risiko.” Vicino said its platform does not integrate with other banks’ systems and described the company as a spectator. He also argued that the activity can distract competitors.
 Salary deposits rise as credit cards and mortgages take priority 
Revolut is developing credit cards and mortgages for Italian customers, following the introduction of Italian IBANs and F24 tax payments. Vicino called the two planned products strategic priorities.
The expansion faces a regulatory constraint: the ECB has ordered slower product releases after finding weaknesses in the approval process. Vicino said discussions with the regulator are ongoing.
Primary-account use is another point of competition. Orcel argued that digital banks still lack the full service range and customer trust needed to become the main account into which salaries are paid.
Vicino offered figures showing that Revolut’s customer growth in Italy includes greater everyday use: the number of customers receiving salaries directly through the platform has more than doubled in a year. He said many more customers use it for daily payments, while Italian users hold more than €1.2 billion in savings on Revolut.
 Postal Police investigate a false customer-data request 
Revolut is cooperating with the Postal Police over customer data it supplied following a request falsely presented as coming from the Prefecture of Reggio Calabria. Italian investigators are examining the incident.
Vicino said the investigation is ongoing and that Revolut had not suffered a hacker attack.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Revolut says its Italian base passed **6 million customers**, making it Italy's **third-largest bank by customer count** behind UniCredit and Intesa Sanpaolo. Supporting figures (from GM Italy Nicola Vicino, via Corriere della Sera / Cryptonomist, 2026-10-09): 60% under-35; **62,000 business clients (+36%)**; salary-deposit customers "more than doubled" YoY; **>€1.2bn in savings** held on the platform. Stated priorities: **credit cards and mortgages**, following earlier Italian IBANs and F24/I24 tax payments.

**What this really is (de-PR'd):** a **customer-count** milestone, not an accounts/revenue/profit one. "Third-largest bank" is by registered users, not by deposits, lending book or revenue — on balance-sheet metrics Revolut is nowhere near UniCredit/Intesa. The headline is a classic neobank vanity metric (registrations, many thin/secondary accounts: 60% under-35 using it for "travel, trading and virtual SIMs"). Vicino's own admission that primary-account/salary use only "more than doubled" (off a small base) and that "online-only can't yet serve the whole market" is the tell: **registered ≠ primary bank**. UniCredit's Orcel explicitly argues digital banks still lack the full range + trust to be the salary account — a point Vicino did not rebut with a primary-account share.

**Why framed this way:** the "third bank / ambition to be first" framing (a) rides a credible absolute number (Italy is Revolut's #3 European market, after France and Germany, growing faster than Germany) while (b) conveniently sidesteps that the ECB has **ordered Revolut to slow new product launches** over approval-process deficiencies — so the very credit-card/mortgage push being touted is **regulatory-gated**. The PR sells acceleration; the footnote is a brake.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Incumbents:** UniCredit, Intesa Sanpaolo (far larger by assets/lending). Orcel (UniCredit CEO) named Revolut among the most formidable competitors — "if you can't beat them, fintechs will eat you" — but also asserts neobanks lack full-service range + trust for primary accounts. Note the backdrop of Italian consolidation ("Risiko"): [[Intesa Sanpaolo launches €30.6B unsolicited bid for Monte dei Paschi]], [[UniCredit lifts Commerzbank stake to 37.7% in takeover bid]]. Vicino positions Revolut as a deliberate "spectator" of this M&A game — it grows organically while incumbents are distracted by mergers.
- **Neobank peers in Europe:** N26, and Revolut's own other-market milestones ([[Revolut surpasses one million customers in Belgium]], [[Revolut targets 10 million customers in Spain by next year]], [[Neobanks claim 27% of Spain's retail market as Revolut surges]]). Italy mirrors the Spain/France playbook: land young users with FX/trading, then push IBANs, tax, cards, mortgages toward primary-bank status.

**Position:** ahead of pure neobank peers in Italy by raw count, but still a challenger to incumbents on the metrics that matter (deposits, lending, primary-account share). The gap closes only if salary/mortgage attach rates rise — exactly what the ECB brake slows.

## [2] Company history / fit
Clean, documented trajectory in Italy: **4M (2025) → 5M (May 2026, [[Revolut reaches 5 million customers in Italy]]) → 6M (Oct 2026)** — ~1M in ~5 months. Supporting rails already shipped: [[Revolut launches F24 and I24 tax payment features in Italy]] (May 2026), local IBANs. Fits the global "become the primary bank" strategy ([[Revolut raises $3B at $75 billion valuation]] funds this; [[Revolut recruits John McFarlane to boost global expansion]]). **Why this structural push:** FX/trading is a thin, commoditized take-rate; to justify a $75bn valuation Revolut needs sticky deposits + lending (cards, mortgages) that earn a banking NIM and a software multiple — hence the Italy "primary bank" framing.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: low.** This is an incremental count update on a known trajectory; no new product, license or terms. The only genuinely new, non-PR data points vs the May 5M note: **62,000 business clients (+36%)**, **>€1.2bn savings**, salary-deposit customers "more than doubled," and the **ECB product-launch constraint** now explicitly binding the Italian roadmap. **Traction is the real story, and it's mixed:** raw growth is strong, but the value-add (salary/mortgage stickiness) is still "doubling off a low base," and the margin-earning products (cards, mortgages) are **not yet live** and are **regulatorily throttled**. Who captures the margin: until lending/cards scale, Revolut mostly captures interchange + FX + thin savings spread — not incumbent-grade banking economics.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Next: credit cards + mortgages in Italy, **pending ECB sign-off** (see [[ECB curbs Revolut's new product launches in Europe]] — product launches tightened, acquisitions/non-EEA onboarding curbed). Regulatory backdrop is heavier than the headline admits: [[Italy fines Revolut €11.5m over commercial practices]] (AGCM, Apr 2026, incl. unclear "commission-free" trading and IBAN-switch disclosure), plus an ongoing **Postal Police** inquiry into a customer-data request falsely presented as from the Prefecture of Reggio Calabria (Revolut cooperating; says no hack). **Second-order:** the ECB brake is the hinge — Revolut's Italy thesis (climb from #3 to #1) depends on shipping cards/mortgages, but those are exactly the launches the ECB has slowed. So the central question is not "how fast does the count grow" but **"can Revolut convert registrations into primary/lending relationships before the regulatory brake and incumbent catch-up close the window."**

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Europe neobanking is a mid-single-digit-billion revenue pool growing low-teens: ~$4.1bn in 2026, ~14% CAGR to ~$8bn by 2031 (per Mordor Intelligence, as of 2026). Structure is concentrated at the top (a handful — Revolut, N26, Monzo, Starling, Wise — hold the bulk of users) but fragmented against incumbents at the country level. In Italy specifically, Revolut at ~6m is now #3 by customer count behind UniCredit and Intesa Sanpaolo (per note / Corriere della Sera) — but customer count ≠ balances/revenue: €1.2bn Italian savings is small vs incumbent deposit bases. "Why now": the structural driver is the primary-account migration — Revolut says salary-receiving customers in Italy "more than doubled in a year," which is the metric that converts a free secondary app into a revenue-bearing main bank (interchange, deposits, credit). The headwind is simultaneous: the ECB in July 2025 restricted new EEA product launches pending a third-party risk/compliance/governance review (per TheNextWeb, Cryptobriefing, FT via 2026-06-10), directly gating the Italian credit-card and mortgage roadmap Revolut calls "strategic priorities."

**Competitive landscape.** Neobank KPIs: customers, % primary-account/salary deposits, deposit balances, ARPU, and PBT margin. Basis of competition in Italy = distribution and trust for the main-account slot, not price (UniCredit CEO Orcel: "if you are not able to beat them, fintechs will end up eating you," while arguing digital banks still lack the service range/trust to hold salaries). Recent moves: Revolut rolled out Italian IBANs and F24/I24 tax payments ([[Revolut launches F24 and I24 tax payment features in Italy]], 2026-05) — the plumbing that precedes credit cards/mortgages. Peer context from the base: in Spain, Revolut deposits surged 323% YoY to €3.1bn as neobanks hit 27% retail share ([[Neobanks claim 27% of Spain's retail market as Revolut surges]]); Portugal passed €600m deposits ([[Revolut passes €600 million deposits in Portugal, adds savings products]]). Position: **ahead on acquisition, catching up on depth** — Italy is the #3 European market (after France, Germany) and growing faster than Germany (Vicino), but 60% under-35 and only €1.2bn deposits signals a young, thin-wallet base. Moat = scale + cross-market network/product velocity; weak on local-trust/physical presence vs incumbents (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** Revolut is private — no public market cap; use last-round valuation, NOT market cap.
- **Revolut (IR, primary):** FY2025 revenue $6.0bn (+46%), PBT $2.3bn (38% margin, 5th straight profitable year), retail customers 68.3m (+30%), balances $67.5bn (per Revolut Annual Report 2025, filed 2026-04-29). Last priced: $75bn round ([[Revolut raises $3B at $75 billion valuation]], 2025-10) → P/S on FY25 rev = $75bn / $6.0bn ≈ **12.5x**. Secondary chatter of ~$115bn (July 2026, per MobiBank — secondary, treat as indicative) → ≈ **19x**.
- **Nubank (NU, public — best listed benchmark):** mkt cap ~$65–78bn, LTM revenue ~$21–22bn, net income ~$3.9bn → P/S ≈ **3.1–3.9x** (per Multiples.vc / public sources, Oct 2026).
- **N26 (private):** peaked ~$9bn (2021), unpriced since; 8m customers. **Monzo (private):** last completed ~£4.5bn, reportedly in talks up to ~£10bn autumn 2026; 12.2m customers.
Flag: Revolut's ~12.5x primary-round P/S is **rich vs Nubank's ~3.1–3.9x**, but the gap is partly justified by growth (rev +46% vs Nu +50% — comparable) and margin — so the premium rides on the private-round mark and the 100m-by-mid-2027 narrative, not on a demonstrable multiple discount. The ~$115bn/~19x secondary mark is the outlier to watch. Internal comps are country-share datapoints, not valuations — distribution not computed; qualitative only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory gate on the growth engine.** The ECB product-launch freeze (pending governance review) directly blocks the credit cards + mortgages Vicino named as Italy's priorities — i.e., the ARPU-deepening step. Second-order: acquisition may keep rising while monetization-per-user stalls, widening the customer-count-vs-revenue gap.
2. **Thin, young, low-balance base.** 60% under-35 and only €1.2bn Italian deposits means the "#3 bank" label is a vanity metric vs balance-weighted incumbents; primary-account conversion is still early (salaries "more than doubled" off a small base). Risk the top-spot ambition stalls at the secondary-wallet ceiling.
3. **Valuation stretch on private marks.** ~12.5x (and ~19x on secondary) P/S sits well above the only profitable listed peer (~3.1–3.9x); a public listing or down-round could re-rate the mark even as the operating business grows.
4. **Governance/trust overhang.** Postal-Police probe into a falsely-presented customer-data request (ongoing) compounds the ECB's governance findings — reputational drag precisely in the trust dimension incumbents exploit for the salary slot.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Italy is a test case for whether a neobank can convert raw customer count into #1-bank economics under a regulator actively throttling product velocity (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if the ECB review clears and Revolut ships Italian credit cards + mortgages within ~12 months, watch Italian deposit balances and salary-deposit share re-rate the market toward monetization; trigger = lifting of the product-launch restriction. What breaks it: the freeze persists into 2027 and acquisition plateaus while deposits stay <€2bn — then "#3 by customers" stays a headline, not a P&L.
