 Eligible MAJORITY members in the U.S. can now send money to family in Latin America 24/7, on zerohash’s regulated stablecoin infrastructure 
MIAMI, Oct. 26, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- MAJORITY, the mobile banking and international money transfer service for internationals, today launched cross-border stablecoin payments to Latin America, powered by zerohash, a leading onchain infrastructure provider. Built on zerohash’s trusted infrastructure and compliance capabilities, the integration lets eligible MAJORITY members send money to family in Latin America who are MAJORITY members, without leaving the MAJORITY app.
Through the integration, senders pay in U.S. dollars, and recipients receive USDC, a dollar-backed stablecoin, in their MAJORITY Global Wallet. zerohash is the licensed provider for the conversion and delivery of funds, and MAJORITY is the app members use to send and receive. Stablecoin payments let MAJORITY members send money across borders 24/7, including on weekends and holidays.
"Our members support family back home every week, and that shouldn’t depend on bank hours,” said Anders Rasmussen, Product Director at MAJORITY. "With zerohash, eligible members in the U.S. can now send money to family in Latin America any day of the week, right from the MAJORITY app. Senders pay in dollars, and their family receives USDC, a dollar-backed stablecoin, in their MAJORITY Global Wallet.”
By embedding zerohash’s regulated onchain infrastructure directly into its platform, MAJORITY abstracts away backend complexity to deliver intuitive, consumer-first payment experiences. The partnership shows how onchain payment networks can reduce financial friction for end users within the apps they already know.
"Sending money across borders shouldn’t wait for banks to open or take days to arrive,” said Adam Tesan, Chief Revenue Officer at zerohash. "MAJORITY deeply understands the needs of its members, and we’re proud to provide the onchain infrastructure that lets them send stablecoin payouts to Latin America, any day of the week.”
 About MAJORITY 
Founded in 2019 and headquartered in Miami, MAJORITY is a financial technology company that’s developing a mobile banking platform for ambitious global citizens navigating life across borders. Through the MAJORITY mobile app, members get access to a powerful financial toolbox, wherever they’re from and wherever they’re going. For a monthly fee, MAJORITY Plus members in the U.S. get a MAJORITY account and a MAJORITY Visa® GPR card. In the app, they can manage their money 24/7, make international money transfers, make low-cost national and international phone calls, and send mobile top-ups to a family member back home. Plus members can send payments to non-members using MAJORITY’s peer-to-peer payments service, MPay. Learn more at majority.com.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened (2026-10-26).** MAJORITY — the Miami-HQ'd (founded 2019), migrant-focused mobile banking and remittance app — launched cross-border stablecoin payments to Latin America, powered by onchain-infrastructure provider **zerohash**. Eligible U.S. members can send money to family in LatAm 24/7 (incl. weekends/holidays) from inside the app: the **sender pays in U.S. dollars** and the **recipient receives USDC** (Circle-issued, dollar-backed) in their **MAJORITY Global Wallet** (a self-custodial product of Majority Global, LLC). zerohash is the licensed provider handling conversion and delivery (NMLS #1699379, licensed across 51 U.S. jurisdictions; not available in New York; accounts not FDIC/SIPC-insured). Source: GlobeNewswire/Manila Times, Oct 26 2026.

**Not MAJORITY's first stablecoin move — this is the second act.** A year earlier MAJORITY had already launched USDC transfers across the Americas, but with a different stack: see [[MAJORITY launches Latin America stablecoin transfers with Privy]] (2025-10-28), which used **Privy** for embedded onchain wallets, **Circle** USDC, and **Solana** rails, promising *free, instant* transfers between the U.S., Colombia and Mexico, with cash-out into local currency "at near-zero cost." The 2026 announcement is notably quieter on fiat cash-out / local-currency delivery — the framing is sender USD → recipient **USDC in-app** (member-to-member), i.e. the recipient holds a stablecoin rather than necessarily pesos in a bank account. The partner swap (Privy/Solana → zerohash) and the narrower "both parties are MAJORITY members" wording suggest a re-platform or a parallel rail rather than a pure expansion.

**Who zerohash is.** Onchain infrastructure for crypto/stablecoins/tokenized assets via API + embeddable dev-kits + an MCP suite; regulated footprint across EU, LatAm, Bermuda and the U.S. Per zerohash, its payout network reaches 130+ countries / 200+ jurisdictions (Brazil, Mexico, Colombia included) and it screens each wallet address pre-payout. Since Aug 2026 zerohash has powered stablecoin prefunding/payouts for eligible **Visa Direct** clients, and **Gusto** uses it for non-U.S. worker payouts — context on why MAJORITY picked it.

**Market context / corridor economics.** U.S.→LatAm is one of the largest remittance corridors; ~258M people live outside their country of origin, ~50M in the U.S. MAJORITY's existing fiat remittance product is already among the cheapest (~$1.99 flat per transfer, or $0 with its ~$6.99/mo membership — vs Western Union agency cash-pickup fees of ~$15 on $500 to Mexico, and Wise bank-transfer fees ~0.5–1.5% to Colombia with 1–2 day settlement). The stablecoin rail's pitch is **24/7 settlement** (no bank-hour / weekend delays), not necessarily a dramatic fee cut versus MAJORITY's own already-cheap fiat product.

**Related internal coverage.** Adjacent LatAm-remittance / stablecoin-rail notes in the same vault: [[Colombian fintech Fronte raises preseed for transnational families]] (transnational-family banking, Colombia), [[Atum builds payments network connecting stablecoin providers]], [[Euronet and Fireblocks extend partnership to bring stablecoins into card issuing]], [[Tether signs stablecoin MoU with National Bank of Kazakhstan]] — the broader "stablecoins as remittance/payout rails" wave this launch sits inside.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. **Is this actually new?** MAJORITY launched LatAm USDC transfers in Oct 2025 via Privy/Solana/Circle ([[MAJORITY launches Latin America stablecoin transfers with Privy]]). Is the 2026 zerohash launch a genuine new capability, a partner replacement, or a re-announcement of the same product with new PR?
2. **Where did Privy/Solana go?** The 2025 note built on Privy wallets + Solana rails; the 2026 note names only zerohash and doesn't mention Solana. Did MAJORITY drop or supplement that stack? Which chain does the Global Wallet now settle on?
3. **Recipient holds USDC, not pesos.** 2025 promised cash-out into local currency "at near-zero cost"; 2026 says recipients "receive USDC in their MAJORITY Global Wallet." Can the LatAm recipient actually spend/withdraw as local fiat, or are they left holding a stablecoin they can't easily use at the grocer?
4. **"Both parties are MAJORITY members."** The 2026 integration requires the recipient to also be a MAJORITY member. How large is MAJORITY's installed base in Colombia/Mexico, and doesn't this closed-loop requirement sharply limit reach vs. Western Union's open cash network?
5. **Fee claim is unquantified.** No fee figure is given for the stablecoin rail. Is it cheaper than MAJORITY's own ~$1.99 / $0-with-membership fiat transfer, or just faster (24/7)? If no price advantage, what's the real user benefit?
6. **Corridor scope.** Press says "Latin America" generically. Which countries are actually live at launch — just Colombia/Mexico (as in 2025), or broader?
7. **Regulatory gaps.** zerohash is unavailable in New York and accounts aren't FDIC/SIPC-insured; funds held mid-transfer aren't FDIC-insured. How is consumer protection / de-peg risk communicated to migrant users?
8. **Eligibility fog.** Repeated "eligible members" language — what gates eligibility (state, KYC tier, membership level), and what share of U.S. members actually qualify?
9. **USDC de-peg / Circle dependency.** Entire value prop rests on USDC holding $1. What's the recipient's exposure if USDC de-pegs over a weekend when fiat off-ramps are the stated differentiator?
10. **Why zerohash now?** zerohash has been powering Visa Direct and Gusto payouts since Aug 2026. Is MAJORITY riding a broader zerohash distribution push rather than a MAJORITY-specific innovation?
11. **Competitive moat.** Comun, Remitly, Xoom, Wise and others also target U.S.→LatAm migrant remittances; several already offer near-instant delivery. What does stablecoin-in-app give MAJORITY that a fast fiat rail doesn't?
12. **Revenue model.** If sender fees are low/zero, does MAJORITY monetize via FX spread on USD→USDC→local currency, membership, or float? The release is silent.
13. **Source quality.** Nearly all coverage traces to one GlobeNewswire press release (Oct 26 2026) syndicated across outlets. Is there any independent reporting, volume data, or user uptake — or is this pure announcement?
14. **"Global Wallet" custody.** Self-custodial per disclosures — does the migrant recipient bear key-management responsibility, contradicting the "without any crypto complexity" promise?

**Importance: 2/5** — Incremental re-launch of a product MAJORITY already shipped a year earlier (new infra partner, same core idea), from a single press release with no disclosed volumes, fees, or independent uptake. Notable as another data point in the stablecoin-remittance-rail trend and zerohash's expanding distribution, but not market-moving on its own.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US→LatAm remittances are a large, structurally fragmented corridor: total remittances to LatAm reached ~$174bn in 2025, and the US→Mexico leg alone was $61.79bn (-4.6% YoY) per Banco de México (via coinspaidmedia/weex, Oct 2025). Stablecoins are a fast-growing but still-minority rail — estimated 18–22% of remittance volume in 2026 (~$25.5–31.2bn) (per tazapay/spark.money citations, 2026). "Why now": (i) regulated onchain infra (zerohash, Circle/USDC + SPEI last-mile into MXN since late 2024) lets a consumer app embed 24/7 settlement without building crypto rails; (ii) migrant neobanks need a differentiator vs incumbents on speed/weekend availability. Structure: value chain splits into app/distribution (MAJORITY), settlement rail (zerohash/USDC), and last-mile payout (local off-ramps) — value increasingly pools at the infra layer.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: transfer volume/corridor, full-cost-to-send (FX spread + fee), time-to-settle, active senders. Players by layer — distribution: MAJORITY, Remitly, Wise, Western Union, Félix Pago, Stori, Fronte (early-stage); settlement infra: zerohash, dLocal, Bitso (USDC-on-Stellar, >$6.5bn US-MX remittances in 2024 per coininsider), Circle. Recent moves: [[dLocal and Félix launch stablecoin WhatsApp remittances in LatAm]] (2025-11); [[Tether invests in EFY Finance for LATAM stablecoins]] (2026-10); zerohash–Marqeta stablecoin card deal (Jul 2026, GlobeNewswire). Protagonist's position: niche/catching-up (analysis) — MAJORITY is a migrant-focused neobank (founded 2019, Miami, ~$98m raised over 7 rounds per Tracxn/Bankingdive; valuation and user count undisclosed). Moat is distribution/switching costs within its migrant member base (members support family "every week"), NOT the rail — the rail is rented from zerohash. Note this is a repeat of [[MAJORITY launches Latin America stablecoin transfers with Privy]] (2025-10) with the infra swapped from Privy (wallet infra) to zerohash (regulated settlement) — an iteration, not a first launch.

**Comps & multiples.** No deal/valuation attached to this product launch → trading multiples not applicable; "no data" on MAJORITY valuation (undisclosed). Infra-partner reference point: zerohash reportedly discussed a ~$250m raise at a ~$1.5bn valuation (Jan 2026, Axios/pymnts) — a round valuation, not market cap; after dropping the ~$2bn Mastercard acquisition ([[Zerohash in talks to raise $250M after dropping Mastercard deal]]). Internal comps (products, not priced): [[dLocal and Félix launch stablecoin WhatsApp remittances in LatAm]], [[Tether invests in EFY Finance for LATAM stablecoins]], [[Stori launches US remittances in Mexico via Felix Pago]], [[BIS finds Wise, Remitly beat banks and stablecoins on transfer cost]]. Distribution not computed — qualitative only.

**Risk flags.**
1. Cost advantage unproven. BIS Working Paper 1385 (2026-10-19, [[BIS finds Wise, Remitly beat banks and stablecoins on transfer cost]]) found stablecoins show no durable full-cost advantage vs fintechs like Wise/Remitly — so the pitch here is 24/7 speed, not price; a recipient must receive and hold USDC in-app rather than get local-currency cash, which limits the addressable use vs cash-pickup rivals.
2. Closed-loop + rail dependence. Launch is member-to-member only (sender and recipient both MAJORITY members) and leans entirely on zerohash's licenses (NMLS #1699379; excludes NY) — concentration on one partner whose ownership/future is unsettled (dropped Mastercard deal, open funding round). Margin on the settlement leg accrues to zerohash, not MAJORITY.
3. Regulatory/custody exposure. Global Wallet USDC is self-custodial and explicitly not FDIC/SIPC-insured; stablecoin payout availability "varies by location" — compliance and consumer-protection risk if USDC de-pegs or a corridor regulator restricts stablecoin off-ramps.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Reinforces the pattern where consumer fintechs rent regulated stablecoin rails rather than build them, pushing durable economics to the infra layer (zerohash, Circle, Bitso). Falsifiable thesis: stablecoin remittance wins on availability, not cost, until last-mile fiat off-ramp fees compress — watch for MAJORITY adding local-currency payout (vs USDC-in-wallet) as the trigger that it's competing for mainstream remittance share rather than crypto-curious members; absence of that step would confirm it stays a niche feature.
