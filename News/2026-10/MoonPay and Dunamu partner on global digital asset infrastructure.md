 NEW YORK , Oct. 20, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- MoonPay , a global crypto payments and stablecoin infrastructure company, is pursuing a strategic collaboration with Dunamu in the field of global financial infrastructure built on digital assets.
The two companies will jointly identify new business opportunities across areas including stablecoin -based global payments and fund movement, and corporate finance and treasury management. By combining MoonPay's global financial services and network with Dunamu's technology and business capabilities, including its GIWA blockchain, they plan to explore the potential applications of digital asset -based financial infrastructure.
First, the companies will look for collaboration opportunities in global payments and fund movement using stablecoins . Drawing on both companies' technology and infrastructure, they will review a range of cases in which digital assets can be used for cross-border payments and fund transfers, and examine the business potential of new financial service models.
The companies will also pursue collaboration in global corporate finance and treasury management. They will jointly review ways to apply digital asset -based financial infrastructure to corporate finance and treasury operations, and identify related business opportunities and usage models.
Expanding Dunamu's GIWA blockchain ecosystem is another key area of collaboration. Leveraging their respective global ecosystems and partner networks, the two companies will explore ways to broaden access to GIWA-based services and digital assets and strengthen the GIWA ecosystem's global connectivity. In particular, by linking MoonPay's global services and network with Dunamu's blockchain infrastructure, they aim to increase overseas market access for services and assets within the GIWA ecosystem and jointly identify new business opportunities in global markets.
"For stablecoins to function as global financial infrastructure, it is important to have the technology and network to support not only cross-border payments and fund transfers, but also the real financial operations of businesses," said Bugeon Lee, Head of APAC at MoonPay. "By combining Dunamu's blockchain capabilities with MoonPay's global financial infrastructure, we will explore new possibilities for digital assets and work together so that the GIWA ecosystem can expand into global markets."
Going forward, MoonPay and Dunamu plan to firm up the technical and commercial potential in their main areas of collaboration, namely stablecoin -based global payments, corporate finance and treasury management, and GIWA ecosystem expansion, and to continue identifying new business opportunities that draw on both companies' strengths.
 About MoonPay 
MoonPay is a global financial technology company that provides infrastructure for moving value across traditional and digital financial systems. Founded in 2019, MoonPay serves more than 35 million customers in 180 countries and more than 1500 enterprise clients across crypto and fintech . Its products support payments, trading, commerce, stablecoin infrastructure and other services that connect conventional payment networks with blockchain-based financial rails. MoonPay maintains regulatory licenses and authorizations in multiple jurisdictions, including the United States and European Union. For more information, visit www.moonpay.com .

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened (one line).** On 20 Oct 2026 MoonPay (NY-based global crypto-payments/stablecoin infrastructure firm) and Dunamu — operator of Upbit, South Korea's largest crypto exchange — announced a **non-binding strategic collaboration** to explore stablecoin-based global payments, corporate finance/treasury use cases, and international expansion of Dunamu's **GIWA** blockchain. No binding deal, no dollar figures, no corridors, no launch dates, no named stablecoin ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/moonpay-and-dunamu-to-collaborate-on-global-digital-asset-financial-infrastructure-302914516.html); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-10-20-moonpay-upbit-dunamu-421476)).

**[1] Who the parties are.**
- **Dunamu** = operator of **Upbit**, Korea's dominant crypto exchange, and builder of **GIWA Chain**, an Ethereum L2 on the OP Stack (built with the Optimism Foundation) that uses ETH for gas — a compliance choice to sidestep Korean regulatory issues. Dunamu is the hub of Korea's won-stablecoin push: it formed a won-stablecoin alliance with **Hana Financial** (~KRW 900bn / ~$675M), and its cap table has drawn **Hana Bank** (~$670–749M stake), **Samsung** affiliates, and a planned **Naver** share swap ([Bloomingbit](https://en.bloomingbit.io/feed/news/96506); [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/07/22/hana-financial-dunamu-forge-won-stablecoin-alliance-aim-to); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/05/15/hana-bank-to-acquire-usd670-million-stake-in-upbit-operator-dunamu)). See [[Samsung affiliates buy Dunamu stake amid won stablecoin push]], [[Hana Financial, Dunamu]] / [[Kakao sells $670 million Dunamu stake to Hana Bank]], [[Naver eyes fintech IPO in Dunamu tie-up]].
- **MoonPay** = founded 2019; ~$3.4B valuation (Dec 2025), ~$107.6M 2025 revenue; 35M+ customers, 180 countries, 1,500+ enterprise clients. Licensed in US (NYDFS trust charter) + EU/UK/Canada/Australia ([getlatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/moonpay.com)).

**[2] Why it matters / strategic logic.** This is MoonPay buying optionality into Asia's hottest stablecoin market via its largest native gatekeeper, and Dunamu buying a licensed global distribution rail to take **GIWA + a future won stablecoin cross-border**. For MoonPay it extends a 2025–26 stablecoin-infrastructure land-grab (issuance, ramps, treasury) into APAC; for Dunamu it addresses GIWA's core weakness — no offshore reach.

**[3] How it fits MoonPay's trajectory.** MoonPay has aggressively built a full-stack stablecoin network via M&A and products: Helio (~$175M, Jan 2025), **Iron** (stablecoin infra, ≥$100M, Mar 2025), Meso, DFlow, Sodot, Glide, Entendre, Dawn Labs. Product line: [[MoonPay launches enterprise stablecoin services]] (M0 integration, issuance), [[MoonPay launches infrastructure platform for PYUSD-backed stablecoins]], [[MoonPay, M0 and PayPal launch PYUSDx stablecoin platform]], [[MoonPay introduces MoonPay Commerce crypto payments platform for merchants]], [[Ether.fi moves core payments infrastructure onto MoonPay stack]]. Treasury-mgmt framing here mirrors that enterprise pivot.

**[4] Regulatory backdrop (decisive here).** Korea's **Digital Asset Basic Act** (proposed Apr 2026; FSC merging ~10 bills into one framework, targeting passage by end-2026) would require won-stablecoin issuers to hold 100% reserves in bank deposits/government securities, segregate customer funds, and ban interest. Crucially, it adopts a **restrictive stance on foreign issuers — requiring a local branch/subsidiary and an FSC license** ([law.asia](https://law.asia/korea-stablecoin-regulation-framework/); [CryptoTimes](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/07/29/south-korea-fsc-moves-to-merge-crypto-bills-set-stablecoin-rules/)). Open questions — whether issuers must be bank-majority-owned and whether large exchanges face ownership caps — directly constrain what Dunamu can do domestically, making MoonPay's *overseas* rails the natural complement rather than a Korea-market play.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is this binding? No — the PR uses "exploring," "reviewing," "jointly identify." It's an MoU/intent, not a signed commercial deal. Treat as signaling.
2. Is there any concrete deliverable? None disclosed — no stablecoin named, no corridor, no launch date, no dollar figure, no revenue-share. "Plan, not product."
3. Does MoonPay already touch Korea/Upbit today? Unclear — no existing integration or volume cited; this is greenfield.
4. What's actually new vs prior coverage? Dunamu's won-stablecoin/GIWA ambitions and MoonPay's stablecoin build-out are both already documented; the *novel* element is only the cross-party MoU. Thin.
5. Why MoonPay and not an incumbent (Circle/Stripe-Bridge/Paxos)? Dunamu may be multi-sourcing; MoonPay's edge is multi-jurisdiction ramp licensing — but Circle/Tether have deeper stablecoin liquidity. Is MoonPay the lead or a hedge?
6. Does Korea's foreign-issuer rule (local branch + FSC license) actually block the won-stablecoin angle? Largely yes domestically — so is the real scope only *outbound* GIWA/remittance, i.e. narrower than "global infrastructure" headline implies?
7. GIWA uses ETH for gas and has limited ecosystem — what overseas demand does MoonPay's network realistically unlock? Unproven.
8. Is Dunamu's loyalty split? It already has a won-stablecoin alliance with Hana Financial; where does MoonPay sit relative to Hana's role? Potential channel conflict.
9. Could this be PR timing around Korea's end-2026 legislation rather than operational readiness? Plausibly.
10. MoonPay funding: ~$3.4B valuation, ~$108M revenue — does it have the balance sheet/standing to be a credible treasury-infrastructure partner for a $10B+ exchange group? Asymmetric.
11. Execution risk on APAC regulatory licensing for MoonPay itself — does it hold (or need) Korean VASP registration to operate there?
12. Corporate-treasury-on-stablecoins is still nascent/low-adoption — is the "treasury management" leg aspirational?
13. Any exclusivity? None stated — both sides keep all options open.
14. What would make this real? A named stablecoin, a live remittance corridor, or a disclosed pilot/commercial term. None exist yet.

**Importance: 2/5** — Two heavyweight names (Upbit's operator + a leading crypto-payments infra firm) in the strategically hot Korean won-stablecoin arena, which earns it above floor. But it is a non-binding MoU with zero concrete deliverables, no stablecoin/corridor/date/figure, and Korea's foreign-issuer rules likely cap the domestic upside. Signal of intent, not a market-moving event.

Driver: crypto trading-volume contraction; ~98% of revenue is Upbit transaction fees, so Dunamu is a near-pure levered bet on Korean crypto turnover. **MoonPay (private):** last self-disclosed 2024 net revenue +112% YoY, cash-flow positive and profitable; no formal results — not an earnings reporter.

**By segment / driver (Dunamu):** single-driver business — Upbit spot-trading fees (~98%). Revenue tracks KRW crypto turnover 1:1, so the 2026 slide is volume (not take-rate): investor sentiment weakened on global digital-asset liquidity contraction. Operating deleverage is severe — fixed cost base means op profit falls far faster than revenue (Q2 op profit -84.6% on revenue -39.2%). **vs expectations / prior period:** No public sell-side consensus for Dunamu (Korean private-ish reporter) → beat/miss assessed vs prior periods only. Clear sequential and YoY MISS every quarter of 2026 vs 2025 comps; Q2 the weakest point (op margin collapsed to ~13.5%). MoonPay: no consensus (private); 2024 +112% self-disclosure is a growth data point, not an earnings beat.

**Guidance / forward:** Neither party gives formal guidance. Forward catalyst outside the results: the **Dunamu–NAVER Financial share-swap** (Nov 2025; Dunamu to become NAVER Financial's wholly-owned subsidiary, KRW 10tn 5-yr AI/Web3 plan) reframes Dunamu from a standalone crypto-fee printer toward a strategically-backed Web3 platform — relevant to why it is now courting global infra partners like MoonPay. (analysis) The MoonPay MOU (stablecoin payments, corporate treasury, GIWA blockchain expansion) is strategy, not revenue — zero near-term P&L impact; it is Dunamu seeking non-fee, overseas growth to diversify off the ~98% fee concentration that just halved its earnings.

**Thesis-flags:**
1. **Revenue concentration is the whole story (Dunamu).** ~98% fees → earnings are a pure proxy for Korean crypto turnover; the 2026 miss is cyclical volume, and op deleverage amplifies it (-84.6% op profit on -39.2% revenue). Second-order: exactly why Dunamu is signing MOUs (MoonPay, ICEx/Indonesia) and the NAVER swap — to build non-fee, global, stablecoin/infra revenue. This partnership is a diversification tell, not a results event.
2. **MoonPay enters from strength but opacity.** Self-disclosed +112% 2024 net revenue and profitability are strong, but there is no audited print and no fresh valuation (last primary $3.4bn, 2021); treat growth claims as management-sourced. For a "global infrastructure" counterparty, the absence of current, third-party-verified figures is itself a flag.
3. **Cyclical, not broken.** Q3 2025's +300% YoY net-profit snap-back shows the franchise re-accelerates hard on volume recovery — the partnership's value is optionality on a turnover rebound plus a new stablecoin/treasury rail, not a fix for the current down-cycle.
