Flipkart super.money’s revenue triples to Rs 292 Cr in FY26; losses up 78%
 
super.money more than tripled its operating revenue in FY26 as it expanded its UPI payments and credit marketplace business. However, the growth came at a cost, as the losses for the firm widened by 78% during the fiscal year ended March 2026.
 
 Follow Us
 Flipkart-backed super.money more than tripled its operating revenue in FY26 as it expanded its UPI payments and credit marketplace business. However, the growth came at a cost, as the losses for the firm widened by 78% during the fiscal year ended March 2026. 
 Super.money's revenue from operations rose to Rs 292 crore in FY26 from Rs 89 crore in FY25, according to its annual financial statements sourced from the Registrar of Companies (RoC). 
 Super.money’s core business spans UPI payments and credit marketplace services. It facilitates digital payments and connects customers of merchant platforms with banks and NBFCs for loans, earning revenue through loan facilitation services. 
 The loan marketplace contributed Rs 248 crore, or nearly 85% of its operating revenue, while the remaining income came from other activities. 
 
 As a marketplace-led business, super.money’s key expense is customer acquisition and brand building. The firm spent Rs 341 crore on marketing in FY26, which accounted for around 61% of its total expenditure. 
 Employee benefit expenses also rose 75% to Rs 139 crore during the fiscal year. Other expenses, including marketplace, legal, telephone, postage and general overheads, pushed its total expenditure up 130% to Rs 557 crore in FY26 from Rs 242 crore in FY25. 
 The company’s marketing spend alone exceeded its entire operating revenue for the year. As a result, its net loss widened by 78% to Rs 264 crore in FY26 from Rs 148 crore in the previous fiscal year. 
 On a unit level, super.money spent Rs 1.91 to earn every rupee in FY26. Its EBITDA margin stood at -85.43% during the year. As of March 2026, the company had total current assets of Rs 136 crore, including cash and bank balances of Rs 63 crore. 
 According to NPCI data, super.money, the fifth largest UPI player by transaction volume, processed 405.14 million transactions worth Rs 19,613.29 crore in September. It accounted for 1.68% of total UPI transaction volume and 0.67% of transaction value during the month. 
 Beyond payments and credit, super.money has entered e-commerce with splitStore, an in-app marketplace that allows users to shop across categories and pay in zero-interest installments, with deliveries handled through Flipkart’s logistics network. 
 In September 2026, super.money also appointed former BharatPe chief product officer Rohan Khara to the same role as it looks to expand its offerings across payments, credit and commerce. Khara has previously held senior roles at FairMoney, Gojek and MobiKwik. 
 Super.money’s numbers show the cost of building a payments and credit business at scale, where customer acquisition remains central to growth. While its revenue has expanded sharply, marketing expenses exceeding operating revenue raise questions about the efficiency of its spending. Its expansion into commerce and installment-based shopping could open additional revenue streams, but the company will need to convert its growing user base into sustainable earnings. The key test will be whether scale can improve unit economics rather than deepen losses.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** Flipkart-backed super.money more than tripled its operating revenue in FY26 (year ended March 2026) to ₹292.4cr (~$31M) from ₹88.8cr in FY25 (+228%), per annual financials filed with the Registrar of Companies and reported by Entrackr. But net loss widened 78% to ₹263.8cr (~$28M) from ₹147.8cr, as total expenses jumped 130% to ₹557.1cr. Marketing alone was ₹341cr (~61% of total spend) — more than the company's entire operating revenue for the year. Unit economics: super.money spent ₹1.91 to earn every ₹1; EBITDA margin −85.4%. As of Mar-2026 it had ₹136cr current assets incl. ₹63cr cash. ([entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/flipkart-supermoneys-revenue-triples-to-rs-292-cr-in-fy26-losses-up-78-12689749), [medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-flipkarts-super-money-spent-rs-341-crore-on-marketing-to-earn-rs-292-crore-in-revenue-in-fy26/), [inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/flipkart-backed-super-moneys-fy26-revenue-triples-to-%E2%82%B9292-cr/))

**[1] What super.money is.** A Flipkart Group-backed UPI payments + credit-marketplace app, launched in 2024 after Flipkart's split from PhonePe. Its core business spans (a) UPI payments with cashback incentives (superUPI), and (b) a credit marketplace connecting users to banks/NBFCs for loans, earning loan-facilitation fees. The loan marketplace drove ₹247.8cr — ~85% of operating revenue — making this fundamentally a lending-distribution story dressed as a payments app. Flipkart also holds an RBI lending licence routed through the entity ([[Flipkart receives RBI lending licence via Super.Money]]), and the app has pushed into cards ([[super.money issues 470K RuPay secured credit cards on UPI]]) and BNPL ([[Flipkart's super.money preps buy-now-pay-later launch]]).

**[2] Scale / metrics.** Per NPCI data, super.money is the fifth-largest UPI app by volume, processing 405.14M transactions worth ₹19,613cr in September 2026 — 1.68% of UPI volume and 0.67% of value. It has processed 400M+ transactions/month for five consecutive months and overtook CRED to reach #5 ([bfsi.eletsonline](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/flipkarts-super-money-rises-to-fifth-place-in-upi-market-overtaking-cred/)). This sits inside India's broader UPI/UPI-credit boom — UPI is ~84% of India's digital payments ([[UPI accounts for 84% of India's digital payments in FY25]]) and credit-on-UPI is the new monetization frontier ([[Kiwi launches interest-back EMI on UPI]]).

**[3] Strategy / expansion.** super.money has moved beyond payments+credit into commerce with **splitStore**, an in-app marketplace offering zero-interest installment shopping with Flipkart logistics handling delivery. In Sept 2026 it hired former BharatPe CPO Rohan Khara as Chief Product Officer (prior roles at FairMoney, Gojek, MobiKwik) to expand across payments, credit and commerce. CEO Prakash Sikaria frames it as "a broader financial platform… across how they pay, borrow and shop." ([entrackr](https://entrackr.com/snippets/supermoney-appoints-former-bharatpe-cpo-rohan-khara-as-chief-product-officer-12539550), [peoplematters](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/appointments/supermoney-appoints-rohan-khara-as-chief-product-officer-to-strengthen-upi-and-digital-banking-play-52136))

**[4] Why it matters.** This is the canonical UPI-player economics problem: UPI itself is essentially unmonetizable (zero MDR), so super.money buys volume via cashback and tries to cross-sell lending. Revenue tripling is almost entirely loan-marketplace fees, but marketing (₹341cr) exceeding total revenue (₹292cr) means growth is wholly acquisition-driven and losses are widening faster than the top line normalizes. The real test is whether scale improves unit economics or just deepens losses — relevant given parent Flipkart is IPO-bound, which puts a spotlight on subsidiary burn. Context: Flipkart Group FY26 revenue rose ~18% to ₹1.46 lakh crore with a large net loss ([angelone](https://www.angelone.in/news/unlisted-companies/ipo-bound-flipkart-s-fy26-revenue-rises-18-losses-narrows)).

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
1. Is "tripled revenue" meaningful when 85% of it is loan-facilitation fees off a tiny base (₹89cr→₹292cr)? How much is durable vs. one-time incentive-driven loan origination?
2. Marketing (₹341cr) exceeded total operating revenue (₹292cr). What happens to volume and loan originations if cashback is cut — is any of the user base retained without subsidies?
3. ₹1.91 spent to earn ₹1, EBITDA margin −85%. Is there a credible path to unit-economics breakeven, or is this permanent cash burn underwritten by Flipkart?
4. Credit-marketplace model: super.money earns facilitation fees but banks/NBFCs hold the credit risk. Who eats losses if the thin-file UPI-sourced borrowers sour, and does that end the lender relationships?
5. Is the FY26 loss figure (₹263.8cr) the full economic picture, or are costs (capital, tech, Flipkart shared services) subsidized/absorbed by the parent and understated?
6. Only ₹63cr cash on hand as of Mar-2026 against ₹264cr annual losses — how dependent is survival on continuous fresh capital from Flipkart/Walmart?
7. NPCI #5 by volume but 1.68% volume / 0.67% value — low-value transactions. Does its user base skew to cashback-chasers with low monetization potential?
8. splitStore and BNPL expansion: is this genuine diversification or adding more subsidized, loss-making verticals while the core isn't profitable?
9. Hiring a BharatPe CPO — BharatPe itself has a troubled governance/profitability history. Signal of talent or of chasing the same unprofitable playbook?
10. RBI/regulatory risk: tightening on digital lending, FLDG, cashback-as-inducement, and UPI-linked credit could directly hit the 85% loan-marketplace revenue stream.
11. Flipkart is IPO-bound — is super.money's burn a drag that gets spun off/cleaned up pre-listing, and does that change its funding runway?
12. Entrackr/RoC figures are standalone annual filings; do they capture related-party revenue from Flipkart (e.g., loans on Flipkart checkout) that wouldn't exist independently?
13. Competitive moat vs. PhonePe/Google Pay/Paytm (far larger) and CRED (just overtaken) — is #5 position defensible or a cashch-fueled blip?

Importance: 3/5 — A fast-growing, well-backed top-5 UPI player's first real financial disclosure is a useful data point on India's UPI-credit monetization thesis and Flipkart's fintech ambitions, but it's a private subsidiary's standalone RoC filing (not a listed earnings print), the absolute numbers are small (~$31M revenue), and the "marketing > revenue" burn story is a familiar Indian-fintech pattern rather than a market-moving event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** super.money sits in India's UPI + credit-on-UPI stack — a near-free payments rail (NPCI/RBI) bolted onto a loan-distribution marketplace. UPI accounted for ~84% of India's digital payment volume in FY25 ([[UPI accounts for 84% of India's digital payments in FY25]]); the live headroom is credit layered on the rail. Per Navi/Karnataka Bank disclosures via Business Standard, credit-on-UPI transactions run ~Rs 10,000cr/month but the specific credit-line-on-UPI product is still only Rs 100–200cr/month (as of 2024) — early/immature (per Business Standard). Underlying NBFC retail-credit demand is strong: RBI data shows NBFC credit +15.8% YoY in Aug-2026, retail loans +21–22% (per RBI, via finin2min/kalkine). Structure: payments is a duopoly-plus (PhonePe + Google Pay = 77.8% of UPI volume in Sep-2026, down from 78.3% MoM; per medianama/NPCI); super.money is #5 at 405.1M txns (~1.68% volume, 0.67% value) — a distant challenger, not a scale player. Why now: with MDR on UPI zero, every app must monetise through credit/distribution, not payments — making super.money's 85%-of-revenue loan-marketplace tilt the sector-standard pivot, not a quirk.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: UPI txn volume/value share, loan-facilitation take, marketing-as-%-of-revenue (CAC proxy), EBITDA margin. Basis of competition = distribution + subsidy (cashback) + Flipkart ecosystem lock-in, not price (rail is free). Key players: PhonePe (45.6% share, targeting a $9–10.5bn IPO, revenue +22% but H1-FY26 loss Rs 1,444cr — per BW Disrupt/medianama), Google Pay (32.2%), Paytm (8.1%, now profitable: FY26 PAT Rs 552cr on Rs 8,437cr revenue — per Paytm IR), Navi (#4), super.money (#5). Recent moves: super.money issued 470K RuPay secured credit cards in 14 months ([[super.money issues 470K RuPay secured credit cards on UPI]]), prepped BNPL ([[Flipkart's super.money preps buy-now-pay-later launch]]), launched splitStore e-commerce + installments, and hired ex-BharatPe CPO Rohan Khara (Sep-2026). Position: niche challenger catching up on volume but structurally behind on monetisation. Moat `(analysis)`: thin on payments (commoditised rail), stronger via Flipkart distribution + its own RBI lending licence ([[Flipkart receives RBI lending licence via Super.Money]]) — intangible/ecosystem, not network-effect, since UPI interoperability neutralises app-level lock-in.

**Comps & multiples.** super.money is private (Flipkart unit), no disclosed valuation → EV/Revenue = no data. Growth: revenue Rs 292cr vs Rs 89cr = 3.3x (+228%); loss Rs 264cr (+78%); EBITDA margin −85.4%; it spent Rs 1.91 to earn Re 1; marketing Rs 341cr > full-year revenue Rs 292cr (per entrackr/medianama RoC filings).
- Internal: [[Razorpay revenue jumps 65% in FY25]] — Rs 3,783cr revenue (+65%), gross profit Rs 1,277cr, but Rs 1,209cr loss (redomicile-driven). Far larger, nearer breakeven on an operating basis.
- Paytm (listed): FY26 revenue Rs 8,437cr, PAT +Rs 552cr, EBITDA +Rs 502cr; market cap ~$10.6bn → P/S ≈ $10.6bn / ~$1.0bn (Rs 8,437cr ≈ $1.0bn) ≈ 10.6x (as of Aug-2026, per pitchbook/IR). Profitable, so no longer burning.
- PhonePe (private): IPO target $9–10.5bn vs annualised revenue ~Rs 7,836cr (2×H1) ≈ $890M → ~10–12x revenue as a round/IPO mark, not market cap.
Distribution not computed (only 1 directly comparable listed peer + 1 private IPO mark; models differ in scale/profitability). Flag: on growth super.money is rich (3.3x revenue), but its −85% EBITDA margin vs profitable Paytm/near-breakeven Razorpay makes it the clear outlier on unit economics — in-line with early-stage, cheap on scale, expensive on burn.

**Risk flags.**
1. Unit economics inverted — marketing (Rs 341cr) alone exceeds total revenue (Rs 292cr) and loss widened faster than peers normalised; growth is bought, not earned. Second-order: if Flipkart tightens funding (parent itself loss-making), subsidy-driven volume could reverse quickly.
2. Disintermediation / thin layer — 85% of revenue is loan-facilitation fees, meaning super.money captures a sliver while NBFCs/banks hold the balance sheet and economics; on a free UPI rail it owns neither the payment margin nor the credit margin. Second-order: any RBI tightening on first-loss-default-guarantee (FLDG) or marketplace-lending rules hits the core 85% directly.
3. Scale gap + regulatory concentration — #5 at 0.67% of UPI value vs a 78% duopoly; dependence on NPCI/RBI rails and Yes Bank sponsorship means policy (MDR, market-share caps) is an existential, exogenous risk it cannot control.

**What this changes (idea-lens) `(analysis)`.** Signals the UPI-app land-grab phase is maturing into a monetisation race: the question is no longer "who has volume" (Flipkart can buy it) but "who converts volume to profitable credit" — where Paytm has crossed over and super.money hasn't. Falsifiable thesis: super.money's loss ratio improves only if marketing/revenue falls below ~100% while loan-marketplace revenue keeps scaling; trigger to watch = FY27 marketing-to-revenue ratio and whether splitStore/BNPL lift take-rate. What breaks the thesis: a credit-cycle downturn or FLDG/marketplace-lending clampdown that compresses the 85%-of-revenue loan fees before the burn normalises.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**FY26 (year ended March 2026) annual results**, sourced from statements filed with India's Registrar of Companies (RoC). super.money is Flipkart's UPI payments and credit-marketplace app (private; no EDGAR filings).

**Topline — revenue more than tripled.** Operating revenue rose ~228% to ₹292.4cr (~$31M) in FY26, from ₹88.8cr (~$10M) in FY25. Growth was driven overwhelmingly by the loan/credit marketplace, which contributed ₹247.8cr — roughly 85% of total operating revenue. The remaining ~₹44.6cr came from other operating income (UPI/payments incentives and ancillary activities). super.money earns loan-facilitation fees by connecting merchant-platform customers to banks and NBFCs; its headline free-UPI product is a customer-acquisition funnel rather than a direct revenue line.

**Losses widened as spend outran revenue.** Net loss widened ~78% to ₹263.8cr (~$28M) from ₹147.8cr in FY25. Total expenditure jumped ~130% to ₹557.1cr from ₹241.8cr. The dominant cost was marketing/customer acquisition at ₹341cr — a >3x increase from ₹101.3cr in FY25, equal to ~61% of total expenditure and, notably, exceeding the entire year's operating revenue. Employee-benefit expense rose ~75% to ₹138.1cr (including ₹36.8cr of ESOP cost).

**Unit economics still deeply negative.** super.money spent ₹1.91 to earn every ₹1 of revenue in FY26; reported EBITDA margin was -85.43%. As of March 2026 it held total current assets of ₹136cr, including cash and bank balances of ₹63cr — a thin buffer against the ~₹264cr annual burn, implying continued dependence on Flipkart parent funding.

**Scale / market position.** Per NPCI data, super.money was the 5th-largest UPI player by volume, processing 405.14M transactions worth ₹19,613.29cr in September 2026 — ~1.68% of UPI volume and ~0.67% of value. It has extended beyond payments/credit into commerce (splitStore in-app marketplace with zero-interest installments via Flipkart logistics) and in September 2026 hired ex-BharatPe CPO Rohan Khara.

**Thesis risks.**
- *Marketing-dependent growth:* revenue trebled but acquisition spend alone (₹341cr) exceeded all operating revenue (₹292cr); the growth is effectively bought, and the key open question is whether scale converts acquired users into retained, monetizable lending customers rather than deepening losses.
- *Revenue concentration:* ~85% of revenue is loan-marketplace commissions — exposed to RBI/NBFC lending-regulation shifts and partner-bank/NBFC appetite; the free-UPI core is a cost, not a revenue line, so monetization leans almost entirely on credit.
- *UPI-incentive dependence:* payments-side "other" income relies on NPCI/government UPI incentive economics, which are policy-sensitive.
- *Path to profitability:* with -85% EBITDA margin, ₹1.91 cost per ₹1 earned, and only ₹63cr cash on hand against ~₹264cr annual loss, super.money remains reliant on parent capital; a credible unit-economics inflection (CAC payback, lending take-rate) has not yet been demonstrated.
