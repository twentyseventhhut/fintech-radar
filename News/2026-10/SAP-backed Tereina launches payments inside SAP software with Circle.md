Tereina, an SAP-backed company, and Circle Bring USDC and EURC into Enterprise Workflows, Starting with the SAP Ecosystem Behind 84% of Global Commerce
 Partnership  will integrate USDC and EURC into Tereina's platform, enabling businesses to send and receive stablecoin payments without leaving the applications they already use 
PALO ALTO, California and NEW YORK — October 7 2026 — Tereina, an SAP-backed financial services company built to embed agentic payments directly into the business applications enterprises already run, and Circle Internet Group (Circle) (NYSE:CRCL), one of the world’s leading internet financial platform companies and the issuer of USDC and EURC through its regulated entities 1 , today announced a partnership to integrate USDC and EURC into Tereina’s payments infrastructure, starting with SAP Cloud ERP. 
The collaboration is designed to make stablecoin-powered payments easier for enterprises to adopt by bringing USDC and EURC into existing business applications and financial workflows. Rather than requiring finance teams to introduce separate systems for stablecoin payments, the integration will provide eligible businesses with a path to access Circle's stablecoin infrastructure from environments they already use.
For eligible USD-denominated payment workflows, USDC will serve as the preferred stablecoin, while EURC will provide an option for euro-denominated activity. Together, they give participating businesses additional choice in how they move and settle value, with access to blockchain-based settlement and new options for managing global payments and treasury operations.
Through this partnership with Tereina, Circle's USDC and EURC integration is available to SAP customers via SAP Pay, addressing an ecosystem that generates 84% of global commerce and bringing digital currency infrastructure into an established enterprise payments environment at significant scale, without requiring businesses to replace the applications at the center of their existing operations. Over the coming months, Tereina and Circle plan to conduct joint proof-of-value programs with customers, train treasury and payments specialists and work with ecosystem partners to incorporate stablecoin-enabled payments into broader enterprise transformation initiatives. The companies also intend to develop insights around real-world enterprise stablecoin adoption as implementations progress. "Stablecoins are increasingly becoming a core infrastructure layer for global commerce. Working with Tereina to bring USDC and EURC into the applications that enterprises already use is an important step toward making internet-native money easier to integrate into everyday business operations. By connecting Circle's infrastructure with Tereina’s enterprise payment workflows, we can help businesses realize the benefits of faster, more programmable and more globally accessible money movement." - Jeremy Allaire, Co-Founder, Chairman and CEO of Circle 
 "Tereina is built on the core belief that the future of financial services is agentic, embedded, and digital. This partnership with Circle makes it easy to move into the future without changing how businesses already operate — stablecoin payments become a native feature of the applications they already trust." — Cedric Bru, CEO, Tereina

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)

Two adjacent announcements, one underlying event (SAP Connect, Las Vegas, ~Oct 6–7 2026):

1. **Tereina launched SAP Pay** — SAP's first native payment capability embedded in SAP Cloud ERP. Tereina is an SAP-backed financial-services company (majority SAP, per the Taulia playbook) led by CEO **Cedric Bru** — the former CEO of **Taulia** (supply-chain finance; SAP took a majority stake in 2022) — with CTO **Brady Cale**, an 11-year Taulia veteran who led Taulia's post-acquisition integration into SAP. Reuters (Oct 6): staff of "a few dozen" in San Francisco, built largely with AI coding tools over ~2.5 years (most in the last 18 months). SAP Pay handles payments inside software companies already use, across traditional and digital rails; claimed availability at launch in the **US and UK**, **89 payment corridors**, **40+ currencies**.

2. **Tereina + Circle partnership** (Palo Alto / New York, Oct 7) to integrate **USDC and EURC** into Tereina's payments infrastructure, "starting with SAP Cloud ERP," reachable by SAP customers via SAP Pay. USDC preferred for USD workflows, EURC for EUR. Secondary sources (Forkast, crypto.news, CoinMarketCap) report **Circle's Arc blockchain as the preferred settlement rail** for eligible SAP Pay stablecoin transactions — notable because Arc only launched ~Sep 16 2026, i.e. debut-to-enterprise-embed in ~3 weeks.

**De-PR'd reality — what is actually live vs announced.** The Circle piece is almost entirely forward-looking: "will integrate," "path to access," "over the coming months… joint proof-of-value programs… train specialists." So the stablecoin layer is an **announced integration + planned pilots, not confirmed production volume**. The genuinely shipped artifact is **SAP Pay itself** (a native payment capability in Cloud ERP); stablecoins are one declared payment type alongside ACH/wire/check. The repeated **"84% of global commerce"** figure is SAP's install-base TAM framing, not Tereina/Circle adoption. Tereina's **"up to 25% lower total cost of ownership"** is a vendor claim with no disclosed baseline. Who's paying stablecoin-rail fees, fraud/chargeback liability, FX spread capture, and merchant eligibility criteria are all unstated.

**+ Why structured this way:** SAP spun a separate, SAP-backed vehicle (Tereina) rather than build payments in-house — the exact Taulia template (independent-but-captive entity, same leadership). This lets SAP (a) move at fintech speed outside enterprise-software release cycles, (b) take regulated payment/stablecoin risk off the mothership's balance sheet, and (c) reuse the team that already knows how to graft a fintech product into the SAP core. The Circle tie-up is anchored to the install-base TAM ("84%") precisely because actual stablecoin traction is zero at announcement — distribution is the whole story.

## [1] Competitors / peers

- **Inside the SAP payments stack:** [[Adyen first to launch SAP's Open Payment Framework]] (Oct 2025) and [[Adyen and SAP launch SAP Unified Payment for Commerce Cloud]] / [[Adyen expands SAP partnership to launch SAP Unified Payment]] (2026) — Adyen is the incumbent card/e-commerce PSP layer for SAP Commerce Cloud. [[Checkout.com integrates with SAP Open Payment Framework]]. SAP Pay is a *different* layer: native, ERP-side, multi-rail incl. stablecoins — complement more than head-on rival, but it does encroach on "who owns the payment inside SAP."
- **SAP's own SCF lineage:** [[SAP Taulia partners with PayMate for business payments]] (2025-06) — same corporate family, same B2B-payments ambition; Tereina is effectively Taulia 2.0 pointed at agentic + stablecoin payments.
- **Circle enterprise/agentic rails:** [[Circle launches Agent Stack for autonomous AI agents]], [[Brex launches stablecoin payment platform supporting Circle USDC]], [[ClearBank to join Circle Payments Network for USDC and EURC]], [[Nium and Circle connect USDC settlement with global payouts]] — Circle's recurring move is "embed USDC/EURC into someone else's distribution." SAP is its biggest distribution surface yet.
- **B2B embedded-payments / trade-credit peers:** TreviPay, Balance, Resolve, Fundbox (compete on underwriting depth + vertical fit, not price); Oracle/NetSuite and Stripe also push embedded ERP payments. None has SAP-native, stablecoin-settling placement.

**+ Second-order dynamics:** SAP is deliberately layering — Adyen owns commerce-cloud card acceptance; Tereina/SAP Pay owns ERP-side AP/AR + treasury movement. The structurally interesting question is whether SAP Pay cannibalizes or federates these. If SAP Pay becomes the default settlement brain, Adyen risks being demoted to a rail among many.

## [2] Company history / fit

Tereina is a clean replay of the Taulia model: SAP backs an independent-looking entity staffed by the people who already integrated the last fintech acquisition (Bru, Cale). SAP bought Taulia (2022) to put working-capital finance inside its ERP; Tereina puts *payments + agentic execution + stablecoins* inside the same ERP. The logic is structural: SAP's core is high-multiple enterprise software, but payments is a flow/take-rate business with regulatory and balance-sheet baggage — so SAP externalizes it into a captive vehicle it can fund, control, and later re-absorb, while keeping the risk and the fintech cost structure outside the parent. "EPIC — Embedded Payments to Ignite Commerce" is the slogan; the real thesis is **monetizing the SAP install base's payment flows** without SAP itself becoming a payments company.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction

**What's genuinely new:** a *native* payment capability in SAP Cloud ERP (SAP has historically relied on partner PSPs and bank connectors, not an own payment product) that treats stablecoins as a first-class rail from day one. For Circle, embedding USDC/EURC directly into the ERP that touches ~84% of global commerce is its most strategically significant distribution deal to date — it moves stablecoins from crypto-native/fintech wrappers toward the system-of-record where corporate AP/AR actually lives. Using Arc as preferred rail ~3 weeks post-launch is a fast, if self-dealing, validation of Circle's own chain.

**Traction — thin.** No disclosed live enterprise, no GMV, no settled stablecoin volume. The concrete deliverables named are future "proof-of-value programs," training, and "insights." So the value-add is **potential distribution, not proven adoption.** 

**+ Who captures the margin:** the open question. If SAP Pay owns the payment initiation/settlement decision inside the ERP, Tereina/SAP capture take-rate + treasury/FX spread on enormous flow — a far better position than a PSP. But the actual money movement still rides Circle (USDC/EURC, Arc) and underlying banks/FX, so margin could leak to whoever controls settlement and liquidity. Stablecoin economics are also thin and under regulatory pressure (yield to issuers restricted), so the durable value is distribution and treasury optimization, not the token itself. The 25% TCO claim, if real, is the actual buyer hook — but it's unbaselined.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment

Near-term: joint Circle–Tereina pilots over "coming months," expansion beyond US/UK, more corridors/currencies, and an agentic-payments narrative (AI agents recommending/executing payments) that is roadmap, not product. Backdrop: post-GENIUS-Act US stablecoin clarity + MiCA in the EU (hence EURC, and Circle's France MiCA license) make enterprise stablecoin settlement newly defensible — see [[Connecting the Dots Stablecoin competition heats up post-GENIUS]].

**+ Why the market goes this way / counterintuitive:** the real battle is distribution into systems-of-record, and SAP is the ultimate one — so this is a land-grab for *where stablecoins get initiated*. Second-order: SAP-native placement could normalize stablecoins for conservative CFOs faster than any crypto-native effort, BUT it also hands Circle heavy dependence on a single gatekeeper (SAP/Tereina) that can swap issuers; and anchoring to the 3-week-old Arc chain introduces execution/credibility risk if Arc underdelivers. Importance is gated by the gap between the TAM framing and zero disclosed adoption.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Subvertical: embedded B2B payments inside ERP + stablecoin settlement for enterprise treasury. Stablecoins are shifting from crypto-native trading rails toward corporate flows: per Circle's own Q1 2026 filing, USDC onchain transaction volume was $21.5tn in Q1'26, +263% YoY, with USDC at 63% of stablecoin transaction volume (per Circle, Q1 2026 results, 2026-05-11). On market sizing, secondary estimates put cross-border B2B stablecoin payments at ~$13.4bn in 2026 rising toward ~$5tn by 2035 (per Juniper/industry forecasts, via phemex/venlyfinance, as of 2026 — forecast, treat as directional, not verifiable). Enterprise adoption is early but inflecting: ~34% of Fortune 500 treasury teams had piloted/deployed stablecoin infra by end-2025 vs ~8% in 2023 (per stablecoininsider, as of 2026 — secondary, unverified). **Why now:** the distribution bottleneck, not the token, is being solved — the news puts USDC/EURC *inside* SAP Cloud ERP via SAP Pay rather than asking treasurers to adopt a separate crypto stack. **Structure:** value chain splits between (a) stablecoin issuers capturing reserve income (Circle), (b) orchestration/embedding layers capturing per-transaction economics (Tereina, Bridge, Coinbase Business), and (c) the ERP/system-of-record that owns the enterprise relationship (SAP). Barriers are high at (a) (regulation/licensing, reserve scale) and at (c) (switching costs), but the embedding layer (b) is contestable.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for the issuer — USDC in circulation, reserve income, transaction volume; for the embedding layer — TPV routed, take rate, attach rate on ERP base. Key players: Circle (USDC/EURC issuer, NYSE:CRCL); Stripe/Bridge (acquired for $1.1bn, closed Feb 2025 — per Yahoo Finance/FintechMagazine) as the leading embedded stablecoin-payments stack; Coinbase Business (USDC global payouts/payment links, launched Oct-2025 — see [[Coinbase launches Coinbase Business stablecoin payment tools]]); bank-run rails like Citi Token Services (see [[Citi expands Citi Token Services into Japan and UAE]]); Tether (dominant by circulation but weaker in regulated enterprise/US channels). Basis of competition: distribution and regulatory standing, not token tech. Recent moves: SAP Pay launched 2026-10-06; Circle–Tereina partnership announced 2026-10-07; Tereina CEO is Cedric Bru, ex-Taulia (SAP took majority stake 2022) — i.e. the same SAP-aligned supply-chain-finance lineage (per Reuters/Yahoo Finance). **Protagonist position:** Circle is *ahead on distribution reach* here — embedding into an ecosystem SAP describes as 84% of global commerce is a distribution coup no rival issuer has matched. Moat for Circle = regulated-issuer status + reserve scale + now ERP-native distribution (network/switching-cost `(analysis)`). For Tereina the moat is almost entirely SAP backing/access, not proprietary tech.

**Comps & multiples.** Circle (NYSE:CRCL): market cap ~$22.0bn; TTM total revenue & reserve income ~$2.75bn (per CNN/Morningstar/Macrotrends, as of 2026-10-09) → **P/S = $22.0bn / $2.75bn = 8.0x** (revenue-multiple proxy; EV not cleanly computable from free sources → EV/Revenue `[UNSOURCED]`). Against revenue-multiple sanity range (~0.5–20x) this is in-line-to-rich; justified against +64% TTM revenue growth and +28% USDC circulation ($77.0bn at Q1'26 end, per Circle Q1 2026) — a high-growth name, so 8.0x is not an automatic red flag. P/E ~48.6x (per CNN, 2026-10-09) is rich but reflects reserve-income sensitivity to rates. Private-comp anchor: Bridge last priced at $1.1bn in the Stripe deal (~5.5x its $200m Series A, Mar-2024 — round valuation, not market cap; per seedtable/Yahoo). Tereina: no disclosed valuation/funding — "no data". Internal comps: [[Stripe to expand stablecoin cards to 100+ countries]], [[Citi expands Citi Token Services into Japan and UAE]], [[Coinbase launches Coinbase Business stablecoin payment tools]].

**Risk flags.**
1. **Adoption vs announcement gap.** The release is explicit that next steps are "joint proof-of-value programs" over "the coming months" — i.e. pilots, no live TPV, no committed enterprise volumes or go-live date disclosed. Why it matters: ERP-treasury sales cycles are long and the 84% figure is SAP's *addressable* base, not a converted pipeline; revenue impact to Circle is unquantified and likely immaterial near-term.
2. **SAP/Tereina single-channel dependence (for Circle's upside) and platform-capture risk (for Tereina).** The distribution runs entirely through SAP Pay / Tereina; SAP owns the enterprise relationship and the system of record. Why it matters: SAP could re-paper economics, add rival stablecoins, or in-house the rail — Tereina's moat is access that SAP controls, and Circle is one layer removed from the customer.
3. **Stablecoin regulatory / reserve-income sensitivity.** USDC/EURC are issued by regulated affiliates and economics hinge on reserve income (rates-driven: net income actually fell 15% YoY in Q1'26 to $55m despite revenue +20%). Why it matters: a rate-cut cycle compresses issuer economics, and cross-border enterprise use invokes EU MiCA (EURC) plus evolving US stablecoin rules — compliance drag on the very treasury flows being targeted.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a *distribution* event, not a product event: whoever embeds inside the enterprise system-of-record wins the B2B stablecoin flow, and SAP's ERP is the richest such surface — a new-entry/land-grab, not consolidation. Falsifiable thesis: if Circle converts SAP Pay into live, disclosed enterprise TPV within ~2–3 quarters, it re-rates the embedding layer and pressures Stripe/Bridge and bank rails; the thesis breaks if the next 2 quarters stay in "proof-of-value" with no volume disclosure, confirming the gap in flag 1. Trigger to watch: first disclosed SAP Pay / Tereina stablecoin volume or named enterprise go-live.
