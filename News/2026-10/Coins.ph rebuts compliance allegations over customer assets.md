Forecast Trend Report by Period
Philippine cryptocurrency exchange Coins.ph has issued a formal response to allegations related to offshore gambling transactions and the possibility of sanctions by financial authorities, underscoring its commitment to regulatory compliance.
In a statement released on Oct. 14, Coins.ph said it was aware of recent allegations involving offshore gambling-related transactions and discussions with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, or BSP. The company said it was explaining its position to users and business partners.
Coins.ph said it complies with requirements and standards set by the BSP and the Anti-Money Laundering Council, or AMLC. It also stressed that, as a fully licensed virtual-asset platform, it is focused on building a safe and reliable digital-asset ecosystem.
The company said it continues to strengthen its internal compliance framework and control functions while working closely with regulators to improve operations. Through those efforts, it aims to bolster user protection and respond to a changing regulatory environment.
Coins.ph also addressed the safety of customer assets and the continuity of its business. "Customer assets are safe, and we continue to operate normally as an institution licensed by the BSP," the company said.
It added that some affected services are expected to be fully restored soon and that updates on the matter will be provided through official channels. It did not provide a specific timetable for the recovery.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] TL;DR.** On 14 Oct 2026, Coins.ph — the Philippines' largest licensed crypto exchange — issued a formal statement denying it *knowingly* served offshore gambling operators, after CoinDesk reported its BSP license was at risk over alleged gambling-linked transaction flows. Coins.ph insists customer assets are safe, its license remains intact, and it complies with BSP and AMLC rules. The statement is damage-control: it follows a real, documented BSP action — a partial suspension (since 29 Sep 2026) of inbound InstaPay/PESONet cash-ins to DCPay Philippines, the peso e-wallet behind Coins.ph, under Monetary Board Resolution No. 839.

**[1] What actually happened (dated).**
- **Aug 2025** — BSP ordered supervised institutions to remove in-app online-gambling links within 48 hours. Per CoinDesk sources, some offshore gaming operators then migrated settlement flows to Coins.ph, reportedly using stablecoins to settle with offshore partners.
- **29 Sep 2026** — BSP partially suspended **DCPay Philippines Inc.** (the EMI running Coins.ph's peso wallet) under **Monetary Board Resolution No. 839**. Philippine Payments Management Inc. (PPMI) pushed this to banks/e-wallets via Advisory No. 2026-0929-029. Effect: **inbound** InstaPay/PESONet cash-ins blocked (users cannot top up from BDO, BPI, UnionBank, GCash, Maya); **outbound** transfers and QRPh payments continue. Crypto trading (run by separate entity **Betur Inc.**) unaffected.
- **~13–14 Oct 2026** — CoinDesk reported the license could be at risk; one source said the alleged infractions could warrant revocation. No formal enforcement order had been filed and no license revoked.
- **14 Oct 2026** — Coins.ph statement: aware of allegations re offshore-gambling transactions and BSP discussions; "firmly denies" knowingly serving gaming/gambling operators; customer assets safe; operating normally; some affected services to be "fully restored soon" (no timetable).

**[2] Who alleges what.** The *allegation* originates from reporting (CoinDesk, citing people familiar) that BSP is weighing punitive action — potentially license revocation — over transactions tied to restricted offshore gambling operators funneling money through Coins.ph/DCPay. Crucially, **no public BSP statement** confirms an enforcement decision; every outlet frames it as "reported"/"said to." The *documented* fact is narrower: the 29 Sep inbound-rail suspension of DCPay under Res. 839. Coins.ph's rebuttal targets the harsher framing (knowing complicity, revocation, unsafe funds) rather than denying the suspension itself.

**[3] Internal context (prior notes).** Coins.ph is a recurring, well-regarded Philippine crypto/payments name in the DB: [[Coins.ph launches stablecoin payments via national QRPh standard]] (Apr 2026 — QRPh stablecoin rail, the same QRPh channel BSP left untouched here), [[Coins.ph joins Circle network for peso payouts]] (Feb 2026), [[Coins.ph expands into all-in-one financial app]] (Jan 2026), [[Veem and Coins.ph expand cross-border payments partnership]], [[Coins.ph selects Clear Junction for European payment corridors]], [[Higlobe and Coins.ph launch stablecoin US-Philippines payment rail]], [[Coins.ph and HashKey launch Philippines-Hong Kong remittance corridor]]. This is the first *negative/regulatory-risk* Coins.ph item in the DB — a sharp turn from the prior build-out/expansion narrative.

**[4] So what / read.** Material but unresolved. The real bite is operational (frozen cash-ins for ~18M users since late Sep, dependent on InstaPay/PESONet top-ups) more than existential (no revocation order exists yet). The affected leg is the DCPay peso EMI, not the Betur crypto-trading entity — so "customer crypto assets are safe" is plausible even as fiat on-ramps are choked. The gap between "partial inbound suspension" (fact) and "license at risk / revocation" (unconfirmed report) is where the story lives. This fits a 2025–26 BSP crackdown on gambling-linked payment flows; watch for a formal BSP enforcement order or a Coins.ph service-restoration announcement as the next hard datapoint.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Philippine licensed VASP market: 9 active VASPs under the BSP as of 15 Jul 2026 (per BitPinas directory, via bitpinas.com). Entry is gated — the BSP froze new VASP licences in Sep 2022 and extended the moratorium indefinitely from 1 Sep 2025, so new entrants can only come via the SEC CASP track or by buying an incumbent licence (per cryptoslate/coingeek). Regulatory posture is tightening: memo M-2026-023 (eff. 5 Jun 2026) bars VASPs from listing privacy/anonymity-enhancing tokens (per cryptobriefing). "Why now": after the BSP tightened e-wallet access for gambling in 2025, offshore-gambling flows reportedly migrated onto other rails — the trigger for the current DCPay action (per agbrief, 15 Oct 2026). The licence moratorium is a double-edged moat: it protects incumbents' scarcity value but makes a licence revocation existential, since the franchise cannot simply be re-applied for.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: registered users, peso on/off-ramp volume (InstaPay/PESONet), VASP trading volume, AML/control adequacy (now a live gating factor). Players: GCash (~94M registered users, 2025) and Maya (>20M) dominate the peso e-wallet layer; Coins.ph (~18M registered users, per thecurrencyanalytics/the search cluster) is the crypto-native specialist, not an e-wallet leader. Recent moves: BSP partially suspended DCPay under Monetary Board Resolution No. 839 (late Sep 2026); inbound InstaPay/PESONet transfers to Coins.ph wallets are now rejected while outbound remains permitted (per cointelegraph/kucoin, 13–14 Oct 2026). Structure matters: Coins.ph runs TWO entities — DCPay (EMI licence, peso wallet) and Betur Inc. (VASP, crypto) — and the restriction hits DCPay's fiat rail, not Betur's crypto trading, which keeps operating. Position: niche crypto specialist, losing ground on its earlier crypto dominance after an unsuccessful pivot into broad e-wallet services (analysis). Moat = the scarce BSP VASP/EMI licence pair + 18M-user base; the moat is exactly what is now under threat.

**Comps & multiples.** No fresh round/valuation in this news — it is a regulatory/reputational event, so trading multiples are not applicable (no data). Last reference valuation: Wei Zhou / Joffre Capital acquired Coins.ph for ~$200M, completed 4 Apr 2022 (per coingeek/the-ken) — a private M&A price, not a current market cap; stale by 4.5 years and pre-dating this regulatory overhang. Internal comps (segregation / licence-action / compliance precedent in base):
- [[Central Bank of Kenya revokes PayU Kenya license]] — regulator pulling a payments licence outright (severe-case analogue for the revocation tail here).
- [[Bitso earns CCSS 2025 certification with AAA rating]] — LatAm exchange signalling custody/security assurance; the positive mirror of Coins.ph's un-evidenced "assets are safe" claim.
- [[Kraken reports Q3 2025 financial highlights]] / [[ESMA questions Binance over reverse-solicitation exemption]] — post-FTX norm that exchanges face proof-of-reserves and regulator scrutiny; Coins.ph offered neither PoR nor an attestation here.
Distribution not computed — qualitative comparison only; no verifiable revenue/EBITDA for Coins.ph.

**Risk flags.**
1. Licence/regulatory tail — in the most severe scenario BSP action could end in revocation of the operating licence (per cryptowisser/analyticsinsight); given the indefinite new-licence moratorium, revocation would be effectively terminal, not a fine the firm can absorb and move past.
2. Fiat-rail cut, not just reputational — inbound InstaPay/PESONet transfers are already rejected (per cointelegraph); a wallet that can take money out but not in bleeds balances and risks a slow-motion deposit flight even if technically "operating normally".
3. Disclosure gap / unverified assurance — "customer assets are safe" is an unaudited management assertion with NO proof-of-reserves, no attestation, no timetable for service restoration ("soon"); post-FTX this is the precise category of claim that markets should not take at face value.
4. Reputational contagion & silent economics — the company does not quantify how much of its flow was gambling-linked, nor name which services are restricted beyond generalities; AML-control inadequacy already acknowledged (BSP ordered control improvements) feeds contagion risk across its remittance/stablecoin partnerships (Circle, HashKey, Veem, Higlobe).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a licence-stress / potential-consolidation event, not a re-rating: a scarce-licence incumbent under AML pressure in a frozen-licence regime is a candidate to be remediated under BSP watch or, in the tail, to have its franchise forfeited and absorbed by another VASP (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if the BSP lifts the DCPay inbound-transfer block and Coins.ph publishes a dated restoration + an independent custody attestation within ~weeks, the "assets safe / licence intact" narrative holds; the thesis breaks if the inbound block persists without a timetable or BSP issues a formal adverse finding — watch the next BSP/PPMI advisory and any Betur-side (crypto) restriction as the escalation trigger.
