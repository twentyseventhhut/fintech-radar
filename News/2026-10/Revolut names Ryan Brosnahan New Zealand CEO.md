# Context-enrichment: Revolut names Ryan Brosnahan New Zealand CEO
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Two unrelated personnel moves bundled into one announcement (2026-10-12):
1. **Ryan Brosnahan named Revolut's first NZ CEO.** He runs the local team and sets NZ strategy. Georgia Grange, who led NZ from launch, is promoted to COO. Brosnahan is ex-ANZ (~15 yrs, incl. GM Services NZ, head of group payments management) and most recently Chief Transformation Officer at **Bendigo and Adelaide Bank** (tech/digital/data/cards/payments). ~30 yrs across banking/tech; board/advisory at EROAD and Banzpay; LBS MSc Finance, Otago BCom. (web)
2. **Piotr Bednarski and Radek Pluhar join Revolut Bank UAB's Supervisory Council** (the Lithuania-licensed European banking entity) — approved by the ECB and Bank of Lithuania. Bednarski: ex-PwC (18 yrs), NBP/KNF (16 yrs). Pluhar: ex-Home Credit Group CRO/CFO/CEO, ex-Equa Bank. This is EEA governance oversight, **nothing to do with NZ.** (web)

**Why bundled this way / what it reveals:** This is a corporate-comms packaging of routine org build-out, not a market event. The NZ CEO hire is the substantive half; the UAB council additions are standard board hygiene for a scaling European bank and are APAC-irrelevant. Being honest about scale: NZ is a ~5.3m-population market and this is an **executive appointment**, not a licence grant, product launch, or capital event. The genuine signal is sequencing — Revolut typically installs a senior, locally-credentialed CEO as it moves from "virtual accounts" toward a real bank, and hiring an ex-ANZ/Bendigo transformation exec is a tell that the RBNZ licence endgame is the priority (an insider who knows NZ prudential plumbing).

## [1] Competitors / peers
NZ retail banking is a concentrated oligopoly: **ANZ, ASB, BNZ, Westpac** (all Australian-owned) hold ~85% of banking assets, plus Kiwibank/TSB. Digital challengers: **Wise** (FX/multi-currency, exposing banks' 2-3% wholesale-rate margins), **Airwallex, Emerge** (web). Brosnahan is literally poached from two of the incumbents he will now attack (ANZ, Bendigo). Revolut is the **first fintech to apply to the RBNZ to become a registered bank** (Dec 2024 application, still under assessment).

**Why the landscape is this way / second-order:** NZ's duopoly-of-Australians structure means margins on FX and transaction fees are fat and politically contested — the Commerce Commission and government have publicly pushed for "more competition." That gives Revolut a regulatory tailwind an incumbent couldn't buy. But the same concentration means the Big Four have deep deposit books and switching inertia; a neobank wins the secondary spend/FX/travel wallet long before it wins the primary salary account. Position: **Revolut leads the challenger pack on brand and licence ambition but is sub-scale vs incumbents' deposit base** — the CEO hire is catch-up on local credibility, not a lead-extending move.

## [2] Company history / fit
NZ trajectory: launched July 2023 (virtual accounts) → [[Revolut launches RevPoints rewards scheme in New Zealand]] (Jan 2026) → [[Revolut targets New Zealand growth amid banking license wait]] (Mar 2026) → [[Revolut to launch small-business accounts in New Zealand]] (Mar 2026) → [[Revolut launches business platform for New Zealand SMEs]] (Jun 2026, targeting "north of 50,000" business customers in 5 yrs). Opened a Wellington HQ in 2026; committed ~NZD 120m and 90 new jobs by end-2027 (web). The RBNZ application (Dec 2024) remains under review; NZ licensing is slow (Heartland took ~2 yrs). 2025 NZ revenue reportedly +99% YoY, retail customers +96%, 6.5m+ transactions (web).

**Why Revolut acts this way:** Globally Revolut is in a "license-everywhere, localize-leadership" phase — cf. [[Revolut names Daisuke Aranami CEO of Japan unit]], [[Revolut names Santiago Perez Olano as Spain CEO]], [[Revolut appoints Yacine Faqir as Morocco CEO]]. Structural logic: its UK/EEA growth is maturing and its valuation ($75bn, cf. [[Revolut raises $3B at $75 billion valuation]]) demands a credible path to 100m+ customers, which forces entry into every mid-size English-speaking market. NZ is small but strategically a low-friction, English-language, pro-competition regulatory testbed adjacent to its larger Australia build-out ([[Revolut commits $400 million to expand Australian operations]], [[Revolut surpasses 1 million users in Australia]]). Installing an ex-regulator-savvy local CEO is the standard pre-licence move.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Low novelty. An **executive appointment** is the lowest-information class of fintech news — no new product, licence, or capital. Traction evidence is indirect: the NZD 120m/90-jobs commitment and a dedicated Wellington HQ show real intent and sunk cost, and the SME platform (50k target) is a concrete prior step. But nothing here is "live" that wasn't before; the material prize — the RBNZ bank registration — is **still pending**, so Revolut cannot yet offer interest-bearing deposits or lending at scale.

**Why the value-add is real-but-small / deeper:** The durable value in NZ comes only when the bank licence lands and Revolut can hold deposits and lend (margin product), displacing the FX/card-spend wallet it already owns. Until then it captures mostly interchange and FX spread — exactly the margin Wise is compressing. So the CEO hire's real function is **de-risking the licence application** (a credible, locally-known CEO reassures the RBNZ), not generating near-term revenue. Who captures the margin: today, still the Big Four on the deposit/lending core; Revolut nibbles the transactional edge. The appointment moves the licence-probability needle slightly, not the P&L.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch: (1) RBNZ decision on the bank registration (the only event that would re-rate NZ materially); (2) conversion of existing virtual accounts into bank accounts (flagged in prior coverage); (3) whether the 50k-SME and NZD 120m targets hit milestones. Regulatory backdrop is favorable — NZ government/ComCom actively courting banking competition — which is Revolut's biggest asset here. Risks: licensing delay (mirrors Revolut's chronic UK saga, [[Revolut's full UK banking licence held up by risk concerns]]), security/ops incidents (cf. [[Revolut suspends inbound PayTo top-ups in Australia after security incident]]), and thin deposit traction in a sticky-incumbent market.

**Why the market goes this way / second-order:** Revolut's playbook treats CEO appointments as licence-readiness signaling; expect the NZ noise to stay low until the RBNZ rules. Counterintuitive effect: a small market like NZ is attractive precisely because a fintech-first bank registration here is a **regulatory proof-point** Revolut can cite in larger jurisdictions (Australia, UK endgame) — the strategic value exceeds NZ's standalone economics. Net: a sensible, incremental build-out step; not a needle-mover on its own.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Global neobank / super-app expanding into APAC via local bank registration. NZ retail banking is a textbook concentrated oligopoly: the Australian-owned "Big Four" (ANZ, ASB, BNZ, Westpac) hold ~85–90% of registered-bank assets (per NZ Commerce Commission personal-banking market study, draft report 2024-03-21). The ComCom found "competition is sporadic rather than strong and sustained," with no "maverick" disruptor and high search/switching barriers — the exact wedge a foreign neobank targets. "Why now": the structure is being politically pried open — in Election-2026 campaigning Labour pledged new licensing rules to ease entry for challengers (NZ Herald, 2026), and RBNZ is actively assessing a bank-registration application (interest.co.nz) — understood to be Revolut's, filed Dec-2024; if granted, Revolut would be NZ's first global digital bank. Revolut already committed NZD 120m (~USD 67m) over five years to NZ and is scaling local headcount from ~35 toward ~90 by end-FY2026 (RNZ, businessdesk.co.nz).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: retail customers, deposit base, transaction volume / interchange, ARPU and product attach (super-app cross-sell). Players: incumbents = the Big Four (deposit-funded, lending-led, branch legacy); challengers = Kiwibank + small players (TSB, SBS, Rabobank); foreign digital = Wise (FX/transfers, no NZ deposit licence) and Revolut. Basis of competition: Revolut competes on product breadth and FX/cards, NOT yet on deposits/lending — that gap is exactly what the banking licence is meant to close (interest-bearing savings + credit). Recent moves: Revolut launched a NZ business platform in 2026 targeting "north of 50,000" SMEs over five years (thepaypers, mpamag), and the Brosnahan hire (ex-ANZ/Bendigo) signals it is buying incumbent-insider credibility ahead of registration. Parent momentum is real: group retail base 68.3m (+30% YoY) toward a 100m-by-mid-2027 goal; NZ revenue +99% YoY with 100k+ NZ customers (Revolut FY2025 annual report; itbrief.co.nz). Position: niche/catching-up in NZ (sub-scale vs incumbents, licence pending) but with a credible super-app moat (product breadth + global scale economics) `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Private company — valuation is last-round post-money, not market cap.
- Revolut: ~$75bn at the Aug/Oct-2025 secondary (internal comp [[Revolut raises $3B at $75 billion valuation]], a +66% step-up from the $45bn 2024 secondary). Group revenue FY2025 = £4.5bn (+46%); so P/S on last round ≈ $75bn / ~$6.0bn (£4.5bn) ≈ **~12.5x** revenue — rich on an absolute basis but paired with ~46% top-line growth, so growth-justified rather than an automatic flag.
- Wise (public, LSE/Nasdaq comp): ~$11bn mkt cap on ~$2.7bn revenue (as of 2026-07) → P/S ≈ $11bn / $2.7bn ≈ **~4.1x** (mature, profitable, FX-led).
- N26 (private): ~$3bn (May-2026 downround, from $9bn peak 2021) on ~$567m FY2025 revenue → ~5.3x on a round valuation that has compressed — illustrates neobank multiple de-rating Revolut is bucking.
Flag: Revolut trades at a large premium to listed Wise (~12.5x vs ~4.1x P/S) and to the de-rated N26; justified only if ~40%+ growth and super-app economics persist. Median not computed (mixed public mkt-cap vs private round valuations — not strictly comparable).
Internal comps: [[Revolut secures Mexico banking license to launch as bank]], [[Revolut wins approval to establish bank in Colombia]], [[Neobanks and payment platforms struggle to crack the Taiwan market]].

**Risk flags.**
1. Regulatory/execution: the whole NZ thesis hinges on an undelivered RBNZ bank registration (filed Dec-2024, still "under assessment") — until granted, Revolut cannot offer deposits/lending and competes only at the FX/cards edge. Second-order: multi-year licence timelines (cf. its drawn-out UK full-licence saga) delay monetization and ROI on the NZD 120m commitment.
2. Entrenched oligopoly + switching inertia: 85–90% incumbent share with low switching rates (ComCom) means customer acquisition is slow and marketing-heavy even with a superior product; new entrants in NZ have historically failed to reach scale.
3. Trust/conduct: Revolut's brand elsewhere carries fraud-complaint baggage (e.g. [[Revolut tops UK bank fraud complaints for third year]]); a conduct stumble during registration review could stall RBNZ approval. Second-order: deposit-trust is the gating variable in a conservative savings market.
4. Valuation: ~12.5x last-round P/S leaves little margin for growth deceleration; APAC entry (NZ, Taiwan, etc.) is slower/regulation-heavy than the headline 100m-customer target implies.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** The Brosnahan appointment is a staffing-up / go-to-market signal, not a licence — it tells us Revolut is positioning for the registration decision, not that disruption has arrived. Falsifiable thesis `(analysis)`: if RBNZ grants registration within ~12 months, Revolut becomes NZ's first credible digital-bank maverick and forces the first real deposit-pricing competition in a decade; trigger to watch = RBNZ registration decision + first interest-bearing savings product launch. What breaks the thesis: licence denied/deferred, or NZ customer growth stalls below the incumbents' switching friction — in which case NZ stays a low-margin FX/card beachhead.

_Financial-health backdrop. The news item is a country-CEO appointment (NZ), not a results release — so Revolut's own latest filed results (Group Annual Report, consolidated FY2025 ended 31 Dec 2025, published 2026-04-29) are used as the PRIMARY source to frame the market-entry story. Figures from the FY2025 Group release + prior-year (FY2024) release; no formal forward guidance is published by a private issuer._

**Verdict (headline read).** Not a results event — but the parent enters New Zealand from a position of financial strength. BEAT-trajectory on its OWN prior year: FY2025 Group revenue $6.0bn (£4.5bn), +46% YoY · profit before tax $2.3bn (£1.7bn), +57% YoY, 38% PBT margin (vs 35% FY2024) · 5th straight year of net profitability. A profitable, cash-generative parent is the backdrop against which the NZ unit pursues RBNZ bank registration and NZD 120m / ~90-jobs investment through 2027 — i.e. expansion funded off the balance sheet, not off fresh capital.

**Key figures (with growth, FY2025 vs FY2024).**
- Revenue: $6.0bn / £4.5bn, +46% YoY (FY2024: $4.0bn / £3.1bn, +72%). Growth is decelerating off a huge base (72% → 46%) but still high.
- Profit before tax: $2.3bn / £1.7bn, +57% YoY (FY2024 PBT $1.4bn / ~£1.1bn). Net profit ~£1.3bn ($1.7bn) (FY2024 net ~$1.0bn / £790m).
- PBT margin: 38%, up ~3pts from 35% — operating leverage, not just top-line.
- Retail customers: 68.3m, +30% YoY (FY2024: 52.5m, +38%). Business customers: 767k, +33%.
- Total customer balances / deposits: $67.5bn (£50.2bn), +66% YoY — the funding base that an interest-bearing savings + credit product set (what Revolut says NZ registration would unlock) is designed to monetise.

**By segment / driver.** Diversified, no single line dominant — 11 product lines each above ~$135m (£100m). FY2025 mix: card payments ~£1.0bn ($1.3bn, +45%); subscriptions £708m ($936m, +67%, fastest-growing); wealth £663m ($876m, +31%); FX £606m ($800m, +43%). Interest income was the FY2024 breakout line (+58% to ~$1.0bn/£790m) and remains a structural driver as balances compound +66% — relevant to NZ, where the stated prize of bank registration is exactly the ability to pay interest and lend locally.

**vs expectations / prior period.** No public analyst consensus — Revolut is private, so no Street beat/miss to compute; assessment is vs its own prior year. Two-year trajectory: revenue $4.0bn → $6.0bn; PBT $1.4bn → $2.3bn; customers 52.5m → 68.3m. Momentum is positive but second-derivative negative (revenue growth 72% → 46%; customer growth 38% → 30%) — maturation, expected at this scale. **Guidance / forward.** No formal guidance (private issuer). **Thesis-flags.**
1. Strong, self-funding parent → the NZ build-out is low financing-risk. Fact: +57% PBT, 38% margin, 5th profitable year. Why it matters: Revolut can absorb a multi-year, pre-revenue market entry without diluting or raising — a structural edge over loss-making neobank challengers. Second-order: competitors in NZ (the big-4 Australian-owned banks) can't out-wait it on capital.
2. Deposit base +66% to $67.5bn is the real engine — and the NZ thesis is a deposit/credit thesis. The appointment + licence pursuit is about converting a payments/FX footprint into a balance-sheet (interest-bearing savings, lending). Second-order: NZ only becomes margin-accretive IF the RBNZ licence lands; until then NZ is cost, not the deposit-engine economics the group print implies.
3. Growth deceleration (rev 72%→46%, customers 38%→30%) is the quiet counterweight. Why it matters: the 100m-by-2027 target needs new geographies like NZ/US to re-accelerate, raising execution and regulatory-approval dependence. Second-order: a leadership hire tied to an unapproved licence is a bet on regulatory outcome, not a booked result.
