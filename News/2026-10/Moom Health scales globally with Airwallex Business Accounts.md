Mili and Maya Kale didn’t set out to build another wellness brand. They started Moom Health after struggling to find products that addressed their own health needs. That experience continues to shape the way they’re building the business: they listen closely, personalise their solutions, and stay focused on what people really need.
As Moom grew from a two-person startup into an omnichannel brand, that same care became harder to support behind the scenes. But every supplier payment, overseas salary, and employee expense had to move through a financial setup that was slow, fragmented, and anything but tailored to the way Moom operated.
Instead of managing multiple banking relationships, Moom opened a single Airwallex Business Account to access local currency accounts, transfer funds, and earn yield. With Airwallex Spend, they can also manage company expenses across multiple currencies in the same place.
Today, Moom no longer toggles between banks and tools to move money – and the cost and time it recovers go straight back into its community and products. But getting there wasn’t easy. 
The challenge
Paying global suppliers and teams was slow and expensive 
Moom’s supply chain spans several countries: ingredients from North America, manufacturing in India, and packaging in China. Setting up bank accounts to facilitate supplier payments could take anywhere from 30 to 45 days. Product launches and market entries stalled from delays that came with bank approvals. 
The problems didn’t stop once accounts were created. Cross-border payments set the business back by hundreds of dollars every month in bank charges alone – and that was before conversion losses. 
Most of Moom’s overseas suppliers only release goods once payment is received. For a business offering tailored monthly subscription plans, keeping up with inventory restocks could mean the difference between a lost sale and a lifelong customer. 
Payroll came with a hidden tax on growth. Salaries for staff based in the Philippines, Indonesia, and India were sent in SGD and then converted to local currency at FX rates that cost Moom an extra 1.5% on every payment. Every dollar lost to fees and spreads was a dollar that could have gone into improving formulations and reaching more women.
Working capital sat idle between restocks
To keep up with demand across its retail stores, stockists, and website, Moom holds up to 180 days of inventory at a time. Between restock cycles, most of its working capital sat idle, earning next to nothing in fixed deposit accounts. Earning a return meant giving up access to cash Moom couldn't afford to be short on when it was time to send the next batch of supplier payments.

Expanding into new markets multiplied manual work for the finance team
As Moom expanded, the fragmented tools that worked for a smaller team started to strain with more employees, more markets, and more expenses every month.
Smaller purchases, like Grab rides and team lunches, were paid out of pocket by employees. Larger ones, like ad spend, subscriptions, and business travel, went on the founders' personal cards. 
What followed was a tedious reimbursement process that involved the finance team having to chase for receipts, manually record claims on Xero, match them to transactions, and track approvals across disconnected systems. Reimbursement cycles cost the team up to 48 hours a month – time that could have gone into planning Moom's next move.
The solution
With Airwallex, Moom finally had a financial setup tailored to how the business actually runs, freeing up time and capital for it to better serve its ever-growing customer base. 
Settle overseas transfers in hours at bank-beating FX rates
Not having access to local payment rails made it slow and costly for Moom to transfer funds globally. Airwallex Business Accounts solved this. 
With Borderless Cash, Moom can receive and send money like a local business in every market it operates in, without relying on local banks. Supplier payments are routed through Airwallex’s local rails at interbank rates, cutting out the marked-up fees Moom used to fork out for every transaction. Now, the team can track a payment status, confirm it with the supplier, and move on to the next operational decision.
The same local currency Moom collects from customers can also be used to pay its overseas staff – no forced conversions or additional fees. Instead of taking days to clear, both supplier payments and salaries now land within the same day. 
Grow capital without losing liquidity 
Airwallex Business Accounts also allow Moom to put its idle cash to work. With Yield, the team can earn returns on its USD and SGD balances without having to move funds across separate accounts or incurring conversion fees. Funds can be withdrawn within 48 hours for the next restock while returns get reinvested back into the balance, compounding the capital the business can put to work as it scales.
Reduce admin work and gain cash flow visibility 
Airwallex Spend lets Moom issue dedicated Visa debit cards with built-in spending limits to employees, so they no longer have to pay out of pocket or rely on founders’ personal cards to cover business expenses.
Employees upload receipts straight from the Airwallex mobile app, where they’re automatically matched to the right transaction before being pushed to Xero. This cuts out the manual re-entry step that used to eat into the finance team's time.
With payments, payroll, and spend running through one account, the finance team sees exactly what’s going out and what’s coming in at any point in the month, instead of piecing that picture together after the fact.

**[0] What happened.** Airwallex published a customer case study (en-SG) headlined "Moom Health expands globally and cuts manual work with Airwallex Business Accounts." Moom Health — a Singapore-based natural wellness/supplement D2C brand for Asian women (co-founders Mili and Maya Kale, ~10–50 staff) — consolidated cross-border payments, payroll, treasury and employee spend onto a single Airwallex Business Account plus Airwallex Spend. Claimed outcomes: up to S$36,000/yr saved on international transfer fees (eliminating hidden bank charges + up to ~4% FX markups), supplier payments and payroll settling same-day (vs 2–3 business days over SWIFT), reconciliation cut from up to 48h/month to ~half a working day (card transactions auto-syncing to Xero), and idle working capital earning >US$1,000/month via Airwallex Yield. Sources: <https://www.airwallex.com/en-sg/case-studies/moom-health>, <https://www.airwallex.com/global>.

**[1] Who the players are.** Moom Health = the customer: an omnichannel wellness brand (online D2C + subscriptions, physical retail in SG/MY/HK, marketplaces Shopee and TikTok Shop) with a multi-country supply chain (ingredients from North America, manufacturing in India, packaging in China) and staff in the Philippines, Indonesia and India. Airwallex = the infra provider: a global financial platform serving 675,000+ businesses (directly or via platform customers), ~$130B+ transactions annually, having recently raised a $320M round framed around "autonomous finance." Products used: Borderless Cash (local-currency accounts + interbank-rate transfers), Yield (returns on idle USD/SGD balances — explicitly flagged as an investment, not a deposit), and Spend (Visa debit cards + receipt capture + Xero sync). ## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a cross-border business-account adoption story: a Singapore SMB (Moom Health, 10–50 staff) replacing fragmented bank relationships with one multi-currency account + spend + treasury (Airwallex). Sector = cross-border B2B payments / global business accounts for SMBs & scale-ups. Market size: the B2B cross-border *flow* runs in the hundreds of trillions (per Straits/Mordor estimates ~$198tn in 2026, via researchandmarkets/mordorintelligence), but the *revenue pool* is the relevant lens — global cross-border payments revenue ~$397bn in 2026 → ~$728bn by 2034 at ~7.9% CAGR, with B2B ~52% of it (per Fortune Business Insights, as of 2026). Structure: fragmented — legacy correspondent banking (SWIFT, 2–3 day settlement, opaque FX markups) vs a layer of fintech rails (Airwallex, Wise, Revolut) owning local collection/payout networks. Barriers = payment licenses per jurisdiction (Airwallex is an MAS Major Payment Institution in SG), the proprietary local-rails network, and multi-currency balance-sheet capability. "Why now": SMBs are born-global (Moom sources ingredients in N. America, manufactures in India, packages in China, sells across SG/MY/HK + Shopee/TikTok Shop) yet legacy banks take 30–45 days to open an account and charge 1.5–4% on FX — the pain the rails layer monetizes.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/annualized transaction volume, FX take rate (spread + fee), net revenue, and attach (accounts → spend/cards → treasury/yield). Players & basis of competition:
- **Airwallex** (infra provider here) — global multi-currency accounts + Borderless Cash local rails + Spend (Visa cards/Xero sync) + Yield; competes on breadth of a single unified platform + APIs. ~$1.3bn annualized revenue (+74% YoY) and ~$287bn annualized transaction volume (+120% YoY) as of Mar-2026; 675,000+ businesses.
- **Wise Business** — strongest on transparent mid-market FX + real local account details (40+ currencies, 80+ payout countries); public, FY2026 net revenue ~$2.5bn (+19%).
- **Revolut Business** — bundled into a super-app, strong in EU freelancers/SMBs.
- **Mercury / Brex** — US-centric startup banking; less a true cross-border-rails play (Mercury focused on US banking + now its own charter; Brex now inside Capital One).
Recent moves: Airwallex raised $320M Series H at $11bn (Jun-2026), up from $8bn (Dec-2025); launched Billing (May-2026) and Yield in the US (Mar-2026). Wise moved its primary listing to the US (Mar-2026). Protagonist position: Moom is a *customer*, not a competitor; for the sector this is evidence Airwallex's SG/APAC SMB wedge is converting (same-day settlement vs SWIFT 2–3 days, S$36k/yr saved, >US$1k/mo Yield). Airwallex's position vs peers = ahead in APAC cross-border breadth; moat = proprietary local-payments network + platform attach (accounts→spend→treasury) raising switching costs (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.**
- **Airwallex (private):** $11bn Series H valuation / ~$1.3bn annualized revenue ≈ **8.5x** price/revenue (round valuation, not market cap) — rich vs public Wise, but revenue is growing ~74% YoY so the premium tracks growth (not an automatic flag).
- **Wise (public, LON:WISE):** mkt cap ~$10.6bn (19-Oct-2026) / net revenue ~$2.5bn ≈ **4.2x** P/S; reported EV/Rev ~3.9x, EV/EBITDA ~15.1x (via multiples.vc / Yahoo, Oct-2026) — in-line for a profitable scaled rails business.
- **Mercury (private):** $5.2bn last round (May-2026) / ~$1bn annualized revenue ≈ **5.2x** — but US-banking model, weaker comparability to cross-border rails.
- **Brex:** acquired by Capital One for $5.15bn (Jan-2026) — exit print, not a trading multiple.

Distribution: Airwallex 8.5x > Mercury 5.2x > Wise 4.2x — spread reflects growth + private-round premium vs public mark; qualitatively Airwallex sits at the rich end justified by ~2x the growth rate of Wise (analysis).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Source is a vendor case study** — the economics (S$36k/yr saved, same-day vs 3-day SWIFT, >US$1k/mo Yield) come from Airwallex's own marketing, a single sub-50-person customer. Treat as illustrative, not audited; Airwallex is silent on Moom's actual volume or fees paid.
2. **Yield is not a deposit** — the note's own disclaimer flags Yield as an at-risk investment product (fund-based, SGD 399/mo Accelerate tier, past performance not indicative). SMBs parking "idle working capital" there carry principal risk, and regulatory scrutiny of fintech "yield" products is rising (second-order: mis-selling / liquidity-mismatch exposure if a restock coincides with a drawdown).
3. **Disintermediation / margin to the rails layer** — the whole thesis is that Airwallex captures FX spread + fees that legacy banks used to earn; but the same logic means Airwallex itself is exposed to the next layer (stablecoin rails, e.g. Brex/Circle USDC, Revolut 1:1 stablecoin conversions) compressing cross-border FX take rates over time.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Falsifiable thesis: APAC SMB cross-border is where the rails layer's next leg of growth sits, and Airwallex's attach model (account→spend→yield) is converting faster than pure-FX peers (analysis). Watch: Airwallex transaction-volume growth re-accelerating/decelerating vs the ~120% YoY Mar-2026 print, and whether stablecoin rails start compressing the FX take rate — the latter would break the "rich multiple justified by growth" thesis. Trigger: next Airwallex funding mark or ARR disclosure vs Wise's public multiple.
