Pomelo y DiDi amplían su alianza y llevan sus tarjetas prepagas para repartidores a México
La alianza entre Pomelo y DiDi para emitir sus tarjetas prepagas para repartidores se expandió de Colombia a México. Su tarjeta powered by Pomelo fue diseñada para que sus couriers la usen en circuito cerrado y paguen los pedidos directamente en los comercios. Con esta solución, DiDi fondea las tarjetas en tiempo real y define exactamente dónde, cuánto y con qué frecuencia se pueden utilizar.
Para hacerlo posible, Pomelo puso a disposición su infraestructura de emisión y procesamiento multi-país, incluyendo licencias de BIN Sponsorship, procesamiento de las transacciones, controles de gasto configurables y embozado de las tarjetas físicas. A continuación, se explica cómo funciona.
El desafío de Didi: cómo pagar los pedidos sin un medio de pago propio
DiDi llegó a Pomelo con una necesidad concreta. Sus repartidores necesitaban una tarjeta para pagar en comercios y así poder completar los pedidos de los usuarios de DiDi Shop, pero la compañía no contaba con un medio de pago propio que le permitiera fondearlos de forma controlada, trazable y en tiempo real. Ese vacío genera fricción en toda la operación.
Sin una tarjeta propia, cada compra depende de reembolsos, adelantos, efectivo o instrumentos de terceros que la plataforma no puede auditar ni limitar. A escala regional, y con miles de compras diarias en supermercados, farmacias, tiendas de conveniencia y comercios especializados, el problema deja de ser administrativo y pasa a ser operativo.
 Lo que DiDi buscaba en el mercado era claro: un emisor de tarjetas con estabilidad operativa y herramientas de seguimiento de alto rendimiento, capaz de sostener un alto volumen de transacciones y acompañar la expansión del negocio a más mercados. 
 “Nuestra alianza con Pomelo es fundamental para las operaciones de DiDi Shop desde México y Colombia hacia más mercados. Las tarjetas permiten a nuestros repartidores realizar compras en cualquiera de las tiendas disponibles en la aplicación para los usuarios de DiDi, incluidos supermercados, farmacias, tiendas de conveniencia y comercios especializados. Esta colaboración fortalece nuestras capacidades operativas y nos ayuda a brindar una mejor experiencia al facilitar a nuestros usuarios el acceso a los productos que necesitan todos los días. Everyday Better For Everyone” - David Yu , General Manager DiDi Shop Global 
¿Qué es una tarjeta de circuito cerrado y por qué encaja en este caso de uso?
Una tarjeta de circuito cerrado, o closed loop, es aquella cuyo uso está restringido a un universo acotado de comercios, categorías o situaciones definidas por quien emite el programa. A diferencia de una tarjeta de consumo tradicional, no está pensada para el gasto libre del titular, sino para habilitar un tipo de gasto específico dentro de una operación.
En el caso de DiDi, la tarjeta corre sobre la red de pagos tradicional, pero se comporta como un circuito cerrado: el gasto queda acotado a los comercios y categorías que la plataforma habilita, con topes de monto y frecuencia definidos por la propia compañía. El repartidor no recibe un medio de pago de uso general, recibe una herramienta de trabajo. Este modelo resuelve tres cosas al mismo tiempo:
 Control del gasto: el dinero solo puede usarse para lo que fue destinado.
 Trazabilidad: cada transacción queda registrada y asociada a una operación concreta.
 Velocidad: el fondeo ocurre en tiempo real, sin adelantos ni reembolsos posteriores.
¿Cómo funciona la solución de tarjetas de Pomelo para DiDi?
 Pomelo acompaña el programa de punta a punta. Estos son los servicios que hicieron posible la operación:
 Licencias de BIN Sponsorship: DiDi emite tarjetas con las licencias de BIN Sponsorship de Pomelo, sin necesidad de contar con una licencia propia, lo que reduce su time to market y simplifica su operación en cada país.
 Procesamiento: Pomelo autoriza y procesa cada transacción en tiempo real, con la estabilidad que exige un volumen transaccional alto y creciente.
 Embozado: Pomelo gestiona la producción de las tarjetas físicas que llegan a manos de los repartidores.
 Bloqueo de MCC: se restringe el gasto a los rubros comerciales habilitados mediante el bloqueo de determinados Merchant Category Codes, los códigos que identifican la categoría de cada comercio dentro de la red de pagos.
 Control de límites y frecuencias de uso: DiDi define cuánto puede gastarse por transacción, por período y con qué frecuencia, según las necesidades de cada operación.
El resultado es una operación en la que la plataforma mantiene el control total del gasto y el repartidor puede resolver la compra en el punto de venta, sin fricción.
El programa arrancó en Colombia y luego escaló a México . Operar en dos países, sobre una misma infraestructura y con lógicas de producto consistentes, es una de las ventajas centrales de trabajar con un emisor multi-país como Pomelo: cada nuevo mercado no implica empezar de cero, sino replicar un modelo ya probado y adaptarlo a las particularidades locales.

Su liderazgo en delivery nos conecta con un mercado de alto movimiento y en constante crecimiento, y equipar a sus repartidores con una tarjeta que agiliza su trabajo diario y a la vez suma trazabilidad y configuraciones flexibles desde nuestra plataforma es un gran diferencial. Dar el soporte tecnológico que hace posible esa operación a gran escala es exactamente lo que nos propusimos construir." - Paul Cabrales , Account Manager Team Lead en Pomelo.

# Context-enrichment: DiDi and Pomelo expand prepaid card partnership to Mexico
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
DiDi extended an existing closed-loop courier-card program (already live in Colombia) to **Mexico**, issued on **Pomelo's** card-issuing rails. The card is NOT a consumer product: it is a **work tool for DiDi Shop delivery couriers** — verbatim from the source, "el repartidor no recibe un medio de pago de uso general, recibe una herramienta de trabajo." The courier uses it to pay merchants directly for the customer's order; DiDi funds it in **real time** and sets where/how much/how often via **MCC blocking** and amount/frequency caps. Pomelo supplies **BIN sponsorship** (so DiDi issues without its own card license), real-time processing, physical card embossing/logistics and the spend-rule config.
- **Why structured this way:** It is almost certainly an **open-network card (Visa/Mastercard) gated by MCC rules behaving like closed-loop** — the same default-deny + allow-list pattern as Ramp/Brex expense cards. In Mexico, non-financial issuers may only issue closed-loop, non-cash-redeemable prepaid; an open-loop/cash-redeemable card would be deemed deposit-taking (bank/IFPE territory). So "closed-loop" is likely a **regulatory design choice** that lets DiDi sidestep needing its SOFIPO or an IFPE license for this product. (analysis)
- **De-PR'd reality:** zero metrics disclosed — no card count, courier count, merchant count, volume, card network, or Mexico go-live date. The detailed trade write-up ([latamfintech.co], 2026-10-05) carries exec quotes (David Yu, DiDi Shop Global GM; Paul Cabrales, Pomelo) but **no go-live date and no official DiDi/Pomelo press release** — only Spanish-language trade press. Colombia's original launch date is also unstated. Verdict: **announced / claimed-live, operationally unverified.**

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Courier/driver cards (LatAm):** The DiDi–Pomelo card is the clearest *true closed-loop, spend-restricted order-funding* courier card found. **Uber Pro Card (Mexico, launched 2025-11-11, via Consubanco + Bkaya)** is the opposite — an open-loop earnings/banking debit card for drivers. **Rappi** issues a courier "tarjeta de compras" to pay for orders (closest functional analog; issuer/network/date unconfirmed). **iFood / Mercado Pago** route courier pay into ordinary accounts — no dedicated order-funding courier card found. So DiDi's mechanism (fund-at-point-of-purchase vs pay-out-earnings) is a genuine, if narrow, differentiator.
- **Card-issuing infra (Pomelo's rivals):** Dock/Conductor (Brazil BaaS), Galileo/SoFi (deepest in Mexico — Klar, Ualá-MX, Plata), Pismo (Visa-owned), Marqeta (LatAm ≈ Brazil only). Pomelo's claimed edge is **integrated BIN-sponsorship-with-local-licensing across multiple countries** — which is exactly what makes a two-country (Colombia→Mexico) replication cheap. (analysis)
- **Scale anchors (not product comps):** Nubank ~139M customers; Mercado Pago ~88M MAU, 2.6M cards issued in Q2 2026. Neither discloses a courier-card program — this is not a space they compete in.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Pomelo:** Buenos Aires, founded 2021 (CEO Gastón Irigoyen). Funding to ~$160M: Seed $9M → Series A $35M (Tiger Global) → +$18M (2022) → Series B $40M (Kaszek, 2024-01) → **Series C $55M (Kaszek + Insight, 2026-01)** — see [[Pomelo raises $55M Series C for payments infrastructure]]. Footprint across AR/MX/BR/CL/CO/PE; 150+ clients (Stori, PayJoy, AstroPay, Western Union/Pago Fácil — see [[AstroPay and Pomelo collaborate on regional card rollout]], [[Pomelo, Western Union and Pago Facil launch Argentina card]]). **Note the name-collision trap:** the San Francisco "Pomelo International" that Zepz bought in [[Zepz acquires Pomelo to expand into credit and cards]] is a *different* company — NOT this issuer.
- **DiDi in Mexico:** A genuine consumer fintech stack sits alongside this — DiDi Card (2023), DiDi Cuenta 15% yield ([[DiDi Mexico launches high-yield account offering 15% return]]), DiDi Préstamos 20M+ loans ([[DiDi Prestamos surpasses 20 million loans disbursed in Mexico]]), all on its acquired SOFIPO (JP Sofiexpress) — see [[DiDi expands Mexico financial services via SOFIPO license]].
- **Why act this way / fit:** The courier card is in a **separate bucket from the SOFIPO consumer play** — it is a DiDi Shop **logistics/ops-expense & fraud-control tool**, not customer acquisition. Replicating a proven Colombia model into Mexico on the same multi-country issuer is low-cost and low-risk; that is precisely why it reads as incremental rather than strategic. The structural driver: DiDi Shop had no owned payment instrument, so couriers relied on reimbursements/cash/advances that DiDi couldn't audit or cap — a float + fraud + traceability leak that a controllable card closes. (analysis)

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **New?** The *partnership* is not new (live in Colombia first); only the **Mexico geography** is new. So the item is a geographic extension, not a new capability.
- **Value-add (real but narrow):** Real operational value — removes courier float, adds per-transaction traceability, enforces category/amount controls in real time. But **who captures margin?** Pomelo earns interchange/processing on each courier purchase (one more "powered by" logo); DiDi saves on reimbursement friction/fraud. Neither disclosed a revenue or volume figure, so the magnitude is unknown and likely small relative to each company's core. (analysis)
- **Traction = zero disclosed.** No cards issued, no couriers enrolled, no merchant count, no volume. Present-tense marketing copy is the only evidence of "live." Treat as announced, not proven-adopted.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Both exec quotes frame this as a platform for **further country expansion** ("llevarla a más países" / "desde México y Colombia hacia más mercados"). The multi-country issuer model makes each next market cheap, so more LatAm geographies are plausible. (analysis)
- **Second-order:** DiDi's Mexico SOFIPO (JP Sofiexpress) reportedly posted a net loss (~MXN 381.5M) while scaling clients, per external research — so a tool that cuts reimbursement float and fraud on DiDi Shop is **margin defense dressed as product news**, not growth. (hypothesis — single-source, mark open)
- **Risk/open:** MCCs are network-assigned and coarse → leakage / false-decline risk; the legal issuing entity of record in Mexico is unstated; Colombia launch date and card network unconfirmed.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits in LatAm card-issuing-as-a-service (BIN-sponsorship + processing) riding embedded finance. Per a Nov-2025 ResearchAndMarkets/GlobeNewswire release, LatAm embedded finance is projected to pass ~$50bn by 2030; a separate estimate pegs the 2026 market at ~$13.2bn growing ~7-8% CAGR to 2030 (note: figures come from different vendor reports and are not reconcilable — treat as order-of-magnitude only, not a single TAM). The global embedded card-issuing market is put at ~$23bn in 2026 → ~$38bn by 2030 (~13% CAGR) per a mykxlg/GlobeNewswire syndication. Structure: fragmented on the enabler side (Pomelo, Dock, Marqeta, Galileo/SoFi, Kushki) but with high regulatory/capital barriers — a BIN sponsorship license is the real moat, letting non-licensed brands (DiDi here) issue without their own charter. Why now: Mexico banking penetration is still only ~46% of adults (facephi, 2026), super-apps are racing to monetize their user/driver base, and gig-economy delivery (DiDi Shop) creates a concrete closed-loop spend-control use case that generic consumer cards don't serve.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: cards-in-force/cardholders, transaction (TPV) volume, take rate / per-transaction processing fee, time-to-market per new country, markets live. Key players on the enabler side: Pomelo, Dock, Marqeta, Galileo (SoFi), Kushki, Liquido, Tpaga — basis of competition is multi-country reach + speed-to-launch + regulatory coverage (BIN) rather than price. Pomelo's disclosed position (vendor-sourced, de-PR'd): clients include BBVA, Santander, Bancolombia, PayJoy, Binance, Western Union, AstroPay, Stori; the note's own source cites Stori alone at 2.8M cards issued in Mexico; a web vendor figure of "50M+ cardholders / billions in annual TPV" is Pomelo marketing, not audited — `[UNSOURCED]` for the program-level DiDi numbers (cards issued, TPV). On the demand side DiDi competes with Mercado Pago (~20M users, moving toward a MX banking license) and Nu México (~12M clients, already approved to become a full bank) — but this specific product is a closed-loop *driver* payment tool, not a consumer card, so it is adjacent to, not head-to-head with, those neobanks. Protagonist position: Pomelo is a regional leader among independent LatAm issuers (catching-up vs global Marqeta/Galileo in scale); moat = BIN licenses + multi-country rails (switching costs once a program is live). (analysis) DiDi's position: it is the *buyer* of infrastructure, extending an already-live Colombia program to Mexico — incremental, de-risked execution, not a new bet.

**Comps & multiples.** No tradeable multiples computable here: DiDi Shop is an unquoted segment of DiDi Global (no segment valuation disclosed); Pomelo is private — its $55M Series C (Jan 2026, co-led Kaszek + Insight) and the Jan-2026 Zepz acquisition both closed with **terms undisclosed**, so EV/Revenue, P/S, price-per-card = **no data**. **Risk flags.**
1. **Single-rail dependence (both sides):** DiDi is fully dependent on Pomelo's BIN/processing stack for a now-operational driver-payment flow; concurrently Pomelo was acquired by Zepz (Jan 2026) — integration/strategic-focus shifts at the vendor are an execution/continuity risk DiDi cannot control.
2. **"Announced vs scaled" + silent economics:** the release de-PR's to an *expansion* of a live Colombia program, but neither party discloses cards issued, TPV, take rate, or who bears fraud/chargeback liability on the closed-loop cards — the unit economics that would make this material are entirely absent.
3. **Low-margin, narrow use case:** closed-loop prepaid for couriers is operationally useful but a thin, capped-ticket niche; it does not move DiDi's consumer-fintech P&L and is not defensible against DiDi building its own BIN once volume justifies it (disintermediation of the enabler over time).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is a deepening of the "super-app issues embedded cards via a specialist" pattern, not a re-rating — the signal is that multi-country BIN-sponsorship (one stack, many countries) is becoming the default way LatAm platforms add payments, favoring enablers with the widest license footprint. Falsifiable thesis: if Pomelo keeps winning multi-market super-app logos post-Zepz, it is the regional category-leader; it breaks if (a) DiDi or peers in-source BIN/processing as volume scales, or (b) the Zepz integration redirects Pomelo away from third-party enabler deals. Watch: next country added to the DiDi program, and whether any program-level TPV/card figures are ever disclosed.
