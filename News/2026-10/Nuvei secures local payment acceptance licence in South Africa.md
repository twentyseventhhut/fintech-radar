_Expansion into one of Africa's largest digital commerce markets advances Nuvei's Every Payment Everywhere strategy, extending its local acquiring infrastructure to 52 markets_

MONTREAL, Oct. 14, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Nuvei, the global fintech building the infrastructure for every payment, everywhere, today announced that it has secured authorization from the Payments Association of South Africa (PASA) to offer local merchant payment services in South Africa, expanding its ability to support global brands and local businesses through its global payments platform.

The authorization advances Nuvei's Every Payment Everywhere strategy, extending its presence within domestic payment ecosystems to help businesses optimize payment performance in every market they operate. By adding South Africa to its network, Nuvei is extending its local acquiring and payment acceptance infrastructure, transaction data, and operational controls to another major market, accessible through a single global platform.

**Phil Fayer, Chair and CEO of****Nuvei**, said: "Commerce is global, but payment performance is local. Our expansion into South Africa is another step towards giving businesses access to locally optimized payment capabilities at global scale. South Africa is one of Africa's most important digital commerce markets, and strengthening our presence here creates new opportunities for international businesses entering the region and South African companies expanding globally."

**A growing digital commerce market**

South Africa's B2C ecommerce market grew by an estimated 9.9% in 2025 to reach $8.48 billion, according to research published by PayNXT 360. The market is forecast to expand at a compound annual growth rate of 8.3% through 2029, reaching approximately $11.66 billion. This continued growth is increasing the importance of locally optimized payment performance for businesses competing in the market.

**Bringing global brands closer to the local market**

Processing transactions domestically can improve authorization rates compared with cross-border acquiring, reduce processing costs, and give businesses more complete visibility into transaction performance. Direct access to local transaction data also supports more precise fraud management and enables businesses to identify and address sources of payment friction more effectively.

Global brands entering South Africa will be able to access these capabilities through the same Nuvei integration they use across other markets. Businesses already serving South African consumers cross-border will be able to establish a more locally optimized payments operation without adding a separate gateway, acquiring relationship, and reporting environment. That gives them room to adapt the checkout experience to local expectations while managing authorization, fraud prevention, reconciliation, and reporting through one platform.

**About Nuvei**

Nuvei is the global fintech building the infrastructure for every payment, everywhere. Its flexible platform supports the full transaction lifecycle, giving the world's leading companies a single partner for local and cross-border commerce at scale, including the emerging wave of AI-driven agentic commerce. With reach in more than 190 markets, local acquiring in 50+ countries, 150 currencies, and over 720 alternative payment methods, Nuvei brings global infrastructure and local expertise to every market its customers serve.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] TL;DR.** On 14 Oct 2026 Nuvei (Montreal; private since the 2024 Advent International take-private) announced it secured authorisation from the Payments Association of South Africa (PASA) to offer **local merchant payment acceptance / domestic acquiring** in South Africa, extending its local-acquiring footprint to **52 markets**. Framed as advancing Nuvei's "Every Payment Everywhere" strategy. **Caveat (material): the regulatory authority is ambiguous** — PASA's licensing/authorisation role for payment institutions was formally transferred to the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) effective 11 Aug 2026, and Nuvei's release cites PASA, uses the word "license", but gives no approval date or reference number.

**[1] What the licence actually enables.** Local acquiring means Nuvei can process South African card/payment transactions **domestically** rather than via cross-border acquiring. Commercial value claimed: higher authorisation (approval) rates, lower processing costs, fuller local transaction-data visibility (better fraud management), and settlement/reconciliation inside one global platform — so merchants avoid bolting on a separate SA gateway + acquiring relationship. This is an infrastructure/market-access win, not a product launch; revenue impact is gradual and tied to merchant onboarding. Market sizing in the release (PayNXT360): SA B2C ecommerce ~$8.48B in 2025 (+9.9%), forecast ~$11.66B by 2029 at ~8.3% CAGR.

**[2] Regulatory context — the SARB handover (skeptic's core issue).** PASA (founded 1996, recognised as the Payment System Management Body / PSMB by SARB in 1999) was a self-regulatory body registering Third-Party Payment Providers (TPPPs: Beneficiary Service Providers and Payer Service Providers) via sponsoring banks. On 4 Jun 2026 SARB issued directives withdrawing PASA's PSMB recognition; from **11 Aug 2026** licensing/authorisation/registration of NPS payment institutions moved to SARB's National Payment System Department, with interbank rule-making/governance passing to the new industry body **PayInc** (functions transferred ~2 Sep 2026). Until SARB publishes its Authorisation Framework, new applicants must still meet PASA's legacy TPPP registration criteria — which plausibly explains the "PASA" wording for a permission now administered under SARB. Either way, Nuvei likely still needs a **sponsoring SA bank** for clearing/settlement into the national payment system; the release names none.

**[3] Competitive landscape.** SA has a deep local PSP/acquiring stack: **Peach Payments** (enterprise/subscriptions), **Stitch** (powers TFG, Vodacom, Takealot, MTN), **Yoco, iKhokha, Ozow, PayFast, Adumo, Netcash**, plus **PayShap/PayInc** real-time rails and legacy EFT. Global rivals: **Adyen** (already local-acquiring in SA), **Stripe** (lists SA only in "Extended network", routing to Paystack, which Stripe owns). Nuvei's edge is global-brand enterprise merchants wanting a single integration across 52 markets, not SMB share. Parallel same-week SA licensing moves (e.g. Wise obtaining SA authorisation) are **different companies/events** — remittance/money-transfer vs merchant acquiring — not duplicates of this story.

**[4] Why it matters / read-through.** Continues Nuvei's post-take-private expansion cadence alongside the pending [[Nuvei to acquire Payoneer for $2.75 billion]] (~mid-2027 close) and licence/network builds like [[Nuvei secures EU CASP licence under MiCA]], [[Nuvei and FreedomPay partner to unify enterprise payments]], and emerging-market plays like [[Colombia's Wasticredit partners with Nuvei on transaction traceability]]. Modest standalone materiality (one more acquiring market), but a credible marker of Africa/EM strategy under Advent ownership. Watch: (a) whether a sponsoring bank / SARB reference is ever disclosed; (b) go-live date and first named SA merchants; (c) take-rate vs Adyen on local acquiring.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. **Who actually granted the permission?** The release says PASA, but PASA lost PSMB recognition and authorisation functions moved to SARB on 11 Aug 2026. Is this a pre-handover approval, or SARB-era approval described with legacy PASA terminology?
2. **No date, no reference number.** The announcement gives no approval date or document ID (per FinanceFeeds). Can the permission be verified against any public register at all?
3. **"License" vs "authorisation."** TPPP status historically was a *registration/authorisation* via a sponsoring bank, not a standalone "license." Is "license" marketing overstatement?
4. **Sponsoring bank?** SA local acquiring/settlement into the NPS typically requires a sponsoring clearing bank. None is named — is settlement actually live, or only authorised?
5. **Go-live vs authorisation.** Is Nuvei processing domestic SA transactions today, or is this merely regulatory clearance with onboarding still ahead?
6. **"52 markets" counting.** Is 52 local-acquiring markets independently verifiable, or a self-reported figure? (About page says "50+".)
7. **Revenue materiality.** SA ecommerce ~$8.48B TPV; Nuvei's realistic merchant share is tiny near-term. Does this move the needle for a company doing the $2.75B Payoneer deal?
8. **Competitive reality.** Adyen already has SA local acquiring; Stripe routes via Paystack. What differentiates Nuvei beyond "single global integration" for enterprise clients?
9. **Local payment methods.** Does Nuvei support PayShap/EFT/Ozow/instant EFT — the methods SA consumers actually use — or mainly cards? Card-only local acquiring is a weak proposition in SA.
10. **FX / data residency / POPIA.** Local processing implies SA data-residency (POPIA) and PCI obligations. Are these addressed, and does domestic acquiring really cut merchant FX costs as implied?
11. **Timing optics.** Announced amid regulatory transition chaos (PASA→SARB→PayInc). Did Nuvei rush a release to claim a "first/milestone" during an authority vacuum?
12. **Duplicate check.** Same-week SA licensing headlines (e.g. Wise) are different companies/products (remittance vs merchant acquiring) — confirmed NOT the same event.
13. **Advent playbook.** Is this organic strategy or balance-sheet-light footprint expansion to dress up the private company's growth narrative pre-Payoneer integration?
14. **Authorisation Framework risk.** SARB has not yet published its new Authorisation Framework. Could Nuvei's permission need re-authorisation/migration once it does?

**Importance: 3/5** — Credible, verifiable-in-outline infrastructure expansion for a major global PSP into a sizeable African ecommerce market, consistent with Nuvei's documented post-take-private, pre-Payoneer growth strategy. But standalone materiality is modest (one more acquiring market, PR-driven), and the story carries a real, unresolved regulatory-attribution caveat (PASA vs SARB, no date/reference, no sponsoring bank), which caps its weight.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Global PSP/acquiring is a scale game built on local acquiring breadth: processing domestically (vs cross-border) lifts authorisation rates and cuts cost — per PaymentBrief/learnwithhasan, local acquiring can be "up to ~59% cheaper" and higher-approval (via article, 2026). Nuvei claims reach in 190+ markets with local acquiring in 50+ countries (now 52 incl. South Africa) and 720+ APMs (per note, 2026-10-14). SA e-commerce: $8.48bn in 2025, +9.9% YoY, ~8.3% CAGR to ~$11.66bn by 2029 (per PayNXT 360, via the Nuvei PR — single-sourced, treat as PR-cited). "Why now": SA is shifting off cards — account-to-account already ~30% of e-commerce value, PayShap live; card rails face margin pressure (per PaymentBrief/FinanceFeeds, 2026). So a card-centric acquiring licence enters a market already tilting toward A2A.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/processed volume, take rate (bps), auth-rate uplift, local-acquiring country count. Global peers: Adyen, Stripe, Checkout.com (combined ~$4.2tn processed/yr per TechBullion) compete on single-platform local acquiring + enterprise service; local SA incumbents Peach Payments, Adumo, Netcash, Ozow, Stitch, Yoco (200k+ merchants) own domestic distribution (per vektorindex/payatlas, 2026). Basis of competition: local-acquiring footprint + auth-rate/cost + one-integration-everywhere, not price alone. Recent Nuvei moves: EU MiCA CASP licence (2026-01), FreedomPay partnership (2026-01), advanced talks to buy Payoneer ~$2.7bn (2026-06) — i.e. buying cross-border B2B scale. Position: niche-to-catching-up in SA — a 52nd market is incremental footprint expansion for a global enterprise PSP, not a domestic challenger to Yoco/Peach on SME distribution (analysis). Moat = scale/breadth + single integration (intangible switching cost for multinationals), weak locally (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.**
- **Nuvei (protagonist, private):** Advent take-private closed Nov 2024 (announced Apr 2024, $34.00/sh) at ~$6.3bn EV on ~$1.3bn revenue = **EV/Rev ≈ 4.8–5.0x**, implied ~25.5x EBITDA (per SEC 6-K / multiples.vc, 2024). This is the last public mark; now opaque under Advent.
- **Adyen (public peer):** H1 2025 net revenue €1,093.5m (+20%), EBITDA €543.7m (50% margin), processed volume €649.0bn; mkt cap ~$31.3bn (Oct 2026, down from ~$45.7bn Aug 2025). On ~€2.2bn annualised net revenue, EV/Rev roughly **~13–14x** `(~$31bn / ~$2.3bn)` (analysis — EV≈mkt cap, net cash) — i.e. Adyen trades at a large premium to Nuvei's 2024 take-out multiple, reflecting higher growth/margin.
- **Internal comps:** [[Nuvei in advanced talks to acquire Payoneer for $2.7B]], [[Nuvei secures EU CASP licence under MiCA]], [[Adyen tops revenue estimates despite US tariff exemption end]], [[Adyen agrees to acquire loyalty platform Talon.One for €750 million]], [[Advent International completes tbi bank acquisition]] (Advent context).
- Flag: Nuvei's ~5x take-out looks cheap vs Adyen ~13x, but that gap is a growth/margin story (Adyen 20% growth, 50% EBITDA margin) and Nuvei's growth under Advent is undisclosed — **no data** on current multiple. SA licence itself carries no disclosed economics (TPV, revenue contribution, ticket size all silent).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory-sourcing red flag (material).** The PR credits PASA, but SARB withdrew recognition of PASA effective ~2 Sep 2026 — authorisation/card-interoperability moved to the SARB (and PayInc) from Aug 2026 (per Mahon Attorneys/FinanceFeeds, 2026). FinanceFeeds explicitly flags Nuvei's "PASA authorisation" claim as needing clarification. So the licensing basis as stated may be stale/mischaracterised — execution and legal standing of the "licence" is unclear.
2. **Scale/materiality undisclosed.** One of 52 markets, no TPV/revenue/merchant figures — low signal that this moves the needle for a ~$1.3bn-revenue (2024) group; pure footprint PR.
3. **Wrong-rail risk.** Entering a card-acquiring role in a market already ~30% A2A and tilting toward PayShap/A2A — card take-rate compression (per PaymentBrief) could erode the economics before they scale.
4. **Owner opacity.** Private under Advent since Nov 2024 — no filings, so no way to verify growth, margin or SA traction; claims rest on PR.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Incremental footprint land-grab, not a re-rating event — global PSPs racing to tick local-acquiring boxes in emerging markets to win multinational mandates (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: this licence is competitively meaningful only if Nuvei converts named global brands onto SA local acquiring within ~12 months; watch for a merchant/volume disclosure or an A2A/PayShap integration announcement. Breaks if the PASA-vs-SARB authorisation ambiguity forces re-licensing or if A2A keeps displacing cards faster than Nuvei onboards card volume.
