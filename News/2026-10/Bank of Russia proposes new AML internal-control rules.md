# Context-enrichment: Bank of Russia proposes new AML internal-control rules
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On ~2026-10-09 the Bank of Russia published for **public consultation** a draft "Положение" (regulation) setting internal-control rules (ПВК) for AML/CFT/counter-extremism-financing (ПОД/ФТ/ЭД) at the non-credit financial institutions (НФО) it supervises. Reported facts (Vedomosti is the originating, paywalled source; confirmed in substance by Kommersant, BFM, Interfax, Bits.media):
- The draft, once adopted, is the updated NFO internal-control rulebook succeeding **Regulation No. 445-П (2014)**. The "~102 pages", "consultation until 21 Oct 2026", "10-day entry into force" and "3-month transition" mechanics appear ONLY in the paywalled Vedomosti original and could NOT be independently verified — treat as reported-not-confirmed.
- **First time** the AML internal-control regime explicitly names the new crypto-market participants: **digital depositaries (цифровые депозитарии)** and **crypto-exchangers (криптообменники)**, plus existing brokers, trust managers and DFA (ЦФА) information-system operators. Banks enter only where they obtain digital-depositary status or act as delegated KYC/identity providers.
- Still must be coordinated with **Rosfinmonitoring** (the Federal Financial Monitoring Service) — plausible/standard, not independently confirmed.

**+ Why structured this way and what it reveals.** This is the "how" rulebook that closes a deliberately-left gap: the 282-ФЗ crypto framework went live 1 Sep 2026, the market-ENTRY rules (registers, min. capital 15M₽ exchangers / 50–250M₽ depositaries) took effect 5 Oct and the first participants were admitted 6 Oct ([[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]]). Russia stood up WHO may operate before HOW they must police money-laundering. This draft is step two — porting the banking-sector AML machinery (full KYC, beneficial-owner identification, "travel-rule" data on transfers, Rosfinmonitoring reporting >1M₽, the ЗСК "Know Your Customer"/traffic-light risk-rating platform) onto crypto infrastructure. Tellingly, Vedomosti/Kommersant note the draft still does NOT fully spell out crypto-exchanger operating principles under ПОД/ФТ/ЭД — the "how" is itself still partial.

## [1] Competitors / peers (regulatory, not corporate)
This is a regulation, so "peers" are parallel AML instruments and other jurisdictions:
- **860-П (18 Jun 2025)** replaced the banks' 375-П (2014), extending scope to ПОД/ФТ/ФРОМУ with a ~3-month alignment window (ПВК in force 13 Aug → align by 13 Nov 2025). This draft is the NFO-side mirror of that move — same template, now reaching crypto. **The regime is NOT consolidating banks + NFOs into one rulebook**: banks stay under 860-П, NFOs under this successor-to-445-П.
- **FATF "travel rule"** for virtual-asset service providers — the 60,000₽ (basic) / >60,000₽ (extended, incl. INN + DOB) and transfers-only-with-full-participant-data thresholds are Russia's domestic analogue, applied inside the "Russian contour".
- EU **MiCA** + the EU AML package (AMLR/AMLA) already impose AML on crypto-asset service providers; so does the US BSA on VASPs. Russia is **catching up / parity** on the principle (crypto must do AML) but on a distinctly state-controlled architecture — risk ratings pushed FROM the central bank via ЗСК, mandatory blocking, inter-agency appeal commission at the CBR.

**+ Why the lay of the land is this way.** Russia's AML build-out is reactive to a live problem: the [[Финтехно QIWI Bank collapse rooted in laundering-channel risk]] case showed e-wallets/terminals/payment agents becoming shadow-turnover rails (>30bn₽ exfiltrated via 24k drop-wallets, incl. crypto-exchange). The CBR's answer is to not let the new, state-blessed crypto infrastructure become the next QIWI — hence KYC/травel-rule rules land almost simultaneously with the licences.

## [2] "Company" history / fit (CBR crypto-regulation trajectory)
Trajectory: 221-ФЗ ЭПР (2024, crypto for cross-border trade) → stablecoin/DFA amendments (2026) → 282-ФЗ live 1 Sep 2026 → legal retail crypto purchase via licensed intermediaries from 1 Jul 2026 → entry-registers live 5 Oct, first names 6 Oct ([[Финтехно banks race to control Russian crypto infrastructure]]: Sber, VTB, Т-Технологии/Атомайз, Alfa all queuing) → **this AML ПВК draft 9 Oct**. It fits logically and is clearly sequenced.

**+ Why the CBR acts this way.** Structural logic: Moscow wants a sanctioned, bank-controlled crypto rail (for cross-border settlement + domestic investment) but cannot tolerate it as an AML hole. So it front-loads market entry to the large state/quasi-state banks (concentrated, controllable), then wraps them in the same ЗСК/Rosfinmonitoring surveillance it already runs on banks. Control, not liberalization, is the driver.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Genuinely new: for the FIRST time digital depositaries and crypto-exchangers are named AML-obligated subjects with concrete duties — refuse contracts, reject/block transfers lacking participant data, full KYC incl. beneficial owners (brokers/managers move from simplified to full ID for crypto), threshold monitoring (≥1M₽ fragmentation; mandatory Rosfinmonitoring reporting >1M₽), ЗСК risk-rating ingestion with restrictive measures on high-risk clients, and a client-appeal mechanism escalating to an inter-agency CBR commission.
**Traction = zero yet.** It is a DRAFT in public consultation, not in force; no adopted text, no compliance. The thresholds (60k₽/1M₽) are reported by secondary sources, not verified against primary text.

**+ Why the value-add is real but bounded.** Real: it removes legal ambiguity that would otherwise let crypto operators claim AML duties didn't formally apply to them. Bounded: (a) still a draft, subject to change and Rosfinmonitoring sign-off; (b) the draft reportedly leaves crypto-exchanger procedures under-specified — the operational "how" is incomplete; (c) effectiveness depends entirely on the ЗСК data pipe actually covering crypto flows, unproven. Who captures value in the stack: the CBR gains surveillance reach; compliant large banks gain a moat (small players can't bear the KYC cost); crypto users lose anonymity below even small thresholds.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
If the ~21 Oct consultation window and Rosfinmonitoring coordination hold, a final положение could enter force in Q4 2026 / early 2027, with a (reported) 3-month alignment period — pushing real compliance into early-mid 2027. Sentiment: participants (Sber, VTB, Т-Технологии, Alfa) are already building and will absorb this as cost of entry; the barrier further concentrates the market among large state-linked institutions.

**+ Why the market goes this way + second-order effect.** Counterintuitively, heavy AML + capital + KYC requirements make the sanctioned Russian crypto rail SMALLER and more concentrated, not broader — which is exactly the CBR's intent (a few controllable depositaries beat a diffuse ecosystem). Second-order risk: if legal rails are too costly/surveilled, genuine shadow flows migrate to unregulated P2P/foreign venues, leaving the regime policing the compliant while missing the illicit — the perennial AML failure mode.

## Red-team / challenge questions (answered or open)

1. **Is this a NEW event or a re-run of the 5–6 Oct crypto-register story?** NEW. The 5 Oct market-ENTRY rules (registers, min. capital) and the 6 Oct first admissions ([[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]]) are a separate regulation about WHO may operate. This 9 Oct draft is the AML internal-control (ПВК) rulebook — HOW they must police ML. Distinct instrument, distinct date → **fresh**.

2. **Does it really replace 445-П (2014)?** Plausible/effectively confirmed — it is the NFO internal-control регулирование; sources describe it as the updated 445-П edition. Whether legally a full-replacement положение vs. an amending указание is **open**.

3. **Does it consolidate banks + NFOs into one rulebook?** NO (common misconception). Banks stay under 860-П (which replaced 375-П in Jun 2025); this is the NFO track. Answered.

4. **Is it ~102 pages?** OPEN — only in paywalled Vedomosti; not independently verified.

5. **Consultation deadline really 21 Oct 2026 (a short ~12-day window)?** OPEN — reported only via Vedomosti; unusually short, treat with caution.

6. **10-day entry + 3-month transition confirmed?** OPEN — unverified for this draft; the 3-month figure is consistent with the confirmed ~3-month 860-П alignment window (banks), supporting plausibility only.

7. **Rosfinmonitoring coordination still pending?** OPEN — Rosfinmonitoring is clearly involved (adviser quoted by Interfax), but "still must be coordinated" is not confirmed in open sources.

8. **Did it actually appear on cbr.ru/project_na?** OPEN — media say so; direct CBR project page could not be loaded (404s via fetch). The note's own source text is a CBR project-list page, which lists the положение — partial confirmation.

9. **Does the draft specify crypto-exchanger procedures?** PARTIALLY NO — Vedomosti/Kommersant note the crypto-exchanger ПОД/ФТ/ЭД operating principles are not fully written out. Substantive gap: the "how" is incomplete.

10. **Are the 60,000₽ (basic/extended KYC) and 1,000,000₽ (fragmentation/reporting) thresholds in the draft or editorial interpretation?** Likely in the draft (Bits.media/www1.ru are specific) but NOT cross-checked against primary text → treat as reported.

11. **Is the ЗСК/traffic-light extension to crypto in THIS draft or a companion act?** Mostly answered — Interfax ties ЗСК risk-rating data-sharing to digital depositaries in this wave; whether codified in this exact draft vs. a separate instrument is open.

12. **Is the real headline crypto, or the ЭД (extremism-financing) scope expansion?** Analytical/open. Title is ПОД/ФТ/ЭД; the extremism-financing scope mirrors 860-П's ФРОМУ widening — potentially broader than just crypto, but crypto is the novel, newsworthy delta.

13. **Traction / adoption?** NONE — it is a draft in consultation, not in force; zero compliance yet. Real effect pushed to ~2027.

14. **Who captures value in the stack?** CBR gains surveillance reach; large state-linked banks (Sber, VTB, Т-Технологии, Alfa) gain a compliance-cost moat; small operators and crypto users lose (KYC burden, loss of anonymity). Second-order: illicit flows likely migrate to P2P/foreign rails, the classic AML leakage.

15. **English-language primary confirmation (CBR English)?** NOT found — all confirmation is Russian-language media/primary docs.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Subvertical: RU regtech / AML-compliance (ПОД/ФТ) tooling for CBR-supervised financial institutions. The draft is a new internal-control regulation that will replace the 2014 Statute No. 445-P (per note, cbr.ru draft; consultation open to 2026-10-21, must be cleared with Rosfinmonitoring, then +10 days to force and 3 months to comply). The structural driver is the 115-FZ AML/CFT regime layered on CBR's "Know Your Customer" (ZSK / "traffic-light") platform, live since 2022-07-01 and covering ~7m legal entities / sole traders; as of 2024-10 it rated ~96.8% low-risk, 1.9% medium, 1.3% high (per CBR, via cbr.ru). "Why now": the draft is the first AML rulebook to explicitly fold in the newly licensed crypto participants — digital depositories and digital-currency exchangers (per note) — so it hard-wires the just-created regulated-crypto perimeter (the first registries were named days earlier, see [[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]]) into the AML control stack. Global regtech is a useful backdrop (valued ~$19.7bn 2025, ~$24bn 2026, AML screening ~34.8% of 2026 share, per market-research vendors via straitsresearch/precedenceresearch) but there is no credible Russia-specific regtech TAM in-corpus or on open web — no data.

**Competitive landscape.** This is a regulation, not a company, so "competition" = the compliance-cost load on supervised entities and the regtech vendors serving them. Relevant KPIs for the affected institutions: number of in-scope entities, time-to-comply (here 3 months), and recurring AML-compliance headcount/opex (not disclosed → `[UNSOURCED]`). In-scope now explicitly includes crypto depositaries, crypto exchangers, and brokers/banks acting as digital depositaries (per note). Recent adjacent CBR moves: biometrics being pushed into onboarding for smaller players ([[Russian microfinance firms can connect to biometric system cheaply]], 2026-10; [[Sravni helps microlenders embed mandatory biometric verification]], 2026-06) and the central-bank fraud-victim support service ([[Russian central bank proposes Gosuslugi fraud-victim support service]], 2026-06) — a consistent pattern of CBR centralising KYC/AML plumbing. Protagonist (the regulation) position: it is the baseline the whole supervised sector must adopt; the moat is statutory (mandatory), not commercial.

**Comps & multiples.** No deal/round/valuation in this item — no trading-multiple arithmetic applies; comps are directional precedents only. Internal regulatory comparables: [[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]] (the perimeter this rule now covers), [[Russian central bank to overhaul bank economic-condition assessment]] (CBR tightening supervisory methodology), [[Russian central bank proposes Gosuslugi fraud-victim support service]], [[Russian microfinance firms can connect to biometric system cheaply]]. Valuation/multiple fields: not applicable → no data.

**Risk flags.**
- **Compliance-burden / cost escalation.** A 102-page rulebook replacing 445-P with only a 3-month adaptation window (per note) front-loads legal + systems spend on every supervised entity; smaller NBFIs and the brand-new crypto depositaries/exchangers (several of them tiny — e.g. "Zefir" with no public product, see [[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]]) are least able to absorb it, so second-order effect is attrition/consolidation at the small end of the new crypto perimeter.
- **De-risking amplification.** The ZSK "traffic-light" already red-flags ~1.3% of clients (2024-10, per CBR); a stricter internal-control baseline tends to push institutions to over-block to stay safe with the supervisor, so the second-order effect is more wrongful "red-zone" classifications and appeal load — CBR has already had to issue separate guidance on appealing high-risk ratings (per cbr.ru), a tell that false positives are a live problem.
- **Sanctions-era execution risk.** AML tightening on a freshly built, sanctions-driven regulated-crypto channel means the rules govern rails that exist partly to route around external restrictions; the control framework and Rosfinmonitoring sign-off (per note) raise the odds of further scope creep or delayed entry into force, so timelines and the final in-scope list are not yet settled.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is the AML "operating system" for Russia's new regulated-crypto market, not just a 445-P refresh — it binds crypto custody/exchange onto the same ПОД/ФТ spine as banks (analysis). Watch next: (a) whether Rosfinmonitoring sign-off slips the 2026-10-21→force timeline; (b) whether the 3-month window triggers early exits/mergers among the sub-scale crypto depositaries just named. Falsifiable thesis: the rule accelerates consolidation of the first-wave crypto participants — wrong if all nine named entities remain independently licensed 6 months after it takes force.
