Президент России Владимир Путин передал российские активы французского производителя напольных покрытий Tarkett во временное управление ООО «Управляющая компания "Центр Капитал»». Указ опубликован на официальном портале правовой информации, пишут " Ведомости ".
Согласно документу, под управление перешли акции АО «Таркетт», принадлежавшие сербской компании Tarkett из города Бачка-Паланка. Речь идет о 187 500 обыкновенных и 500 000 привилегированных акций. Аналогичный пакет акций, принадлежавший компании Tarkett, также передан «Центр Капиталу».
Кроме того, во временное управление переданы 100 обыкновенных акций АО «Таркетт Рус», принадлежавших российскому производителю напольных покрытий «Таркетт», а также 100% долей в ООО «Таркетт Соммер», которыми владело это акционерное общество.
Указ распространяется и на активы других иностранных компаний. В их числе – 12 565 акций АО «Каппа Рус», 100% долей в ООО «Онтэкс РУ», ООО «Эвиосис Пэкэджинг Кубань» и ООО «Менсен Пакажинг СНГ».
Документ вносит изменения в перечень активов, переданных во временное управление на основании указа президента № 302 от 25 апреля 2023 г. Он вступает в силу со дня официального опубликования.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened (one line).** On 2026-10-16 Putin signed a decree placing the Russian assets of French flooring maker Tarkett — plus four other European-owned firms — under the "temporary management" of UC Center Capital LLC («Центр Капитал»), expanding the asset-seizure list first created by Decree No. 302 of 25 April 2023.

**[1] The specific perimeter.** For Tarkett the decree covers: 187,500 ordinary + 500,000 preference shares of AO Tarkett held by the Serbian Tarkett entity (Bačka Palanka), an equivalent block held by the Tarkett parent, 100 ordinary shares of AO Tarkett Rus held by Russian "Tarkett", and 100% of OOO Tarkett Sommer. Tarkett runs two Russian plants (Moscow/Mytishchi and Samara/Otradny regions) and — contrary to many Western peers — never exited Russia after 2022, continuing to operate and pay taxes locally. The same decree swept in four other foreign-owned assets: 12,565 shares of AO Kappa Rus (paper packaging), and 100% of OOO Ontex RU (hygiene), OOO Eviosys Packaging Kuban (metal packaging) and OOO Mensen Packaging CIS. Combined Russian revenue of the five is reported at >RUB 50bn (~US$0.6bn).

**[2] Who the new manager is.** The manager, OOO UC "Center Capital", was registered only on 2026-02-03; its sole owner/CEO is reported to be Marat Mirkhaidarov, a former deputy PM of Transbaikal Region who volunteered for the war against Ukraine in 2024 (served in the "Akhmat" unit) and resigned in March 2025 citing a combat injury. Reporting notes the new manager asked journalists not to cover the transfer — a signal this is a politically sensitive discretionary allocation, not an open process.

**[3] Mechanism & why it matters (internal precedent).** This is the identical "temporary external management" tool used repeatedly in 2026: see [[Russia transfers Akzo Nobel local assets to state management]] (2026-07-13, same Decree-302 amendment pattern, assets to a newly-registered opaque manager AO "Razvitie stroitelnyh aktivov"). It sits in the same broad wave of state reallocation of Russian assets captured by [[Russian state sells 67% of zinc and lead processor Dalpolimetall]], [[Private investor consortium becomes new owner of Lesta Games]] and the nationalisation/auction cycle in [[PSB fails third auction for nationalized Vektor Rail]]. "Temporary management" is not nationalisation per se — legal title is not transferred — but in practice it strips the foreign owner of control, dividends and disposal rights, and historically precedes forced sale at a steep discount. Note: the Center Capital pattern here resembles earlier Sept-2026 placements (Globus, Metro, Auchan, Leroy Merlin, Nestlé Russia); >800 foreign-linked entities are reported under this regime by 2026.

**[4] Watch next.** (a) Whether a forced sale / discounted buyout of Tarkett Rus follows (the usual next step); (b) response from Tarkett SA (Paris) — impairment/write-off disclosure; (c) whether the "unfriendly countries" framing is used despite Tarkett's continued local operations; (d) precedent risk for remaining foreign manufacturers still in Russia.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team / second-order questions**

1. Is this nationalisation? No — "temporary external management" leaves legal title with the foreign owner; it transfers control, not ownership. Calling it a seizure in headlines is directionally right but legally imprecise.
2. Is the event genuinely new vs. the July Akzo Nobel item? Yes — different company (Tarkett + 4 others), different decree date (2026-10-16), different manager (Center Capital vs. Razvitie stroitelnyh aktivov). Same legal mechanism, new perimeter → fresh.
3. Why Tarkett now, after it stayed in Russia post-2022? Open — unclear whether this is punitive (reciprocity for EU seizures of Russian assets), a profit-extraction play, or allocation of a specific plant to a favoured insider. Sources don't give an official rationale.
4. Is the "French" framing accurate? Partly — Tarkett SA is Paris-listed/French-HQ, but the seized shares are held via Serbian (Bačka Palanka) and parent entities; Serbia is not an "unfriendly state", raising a legal question the sources don't resolve. Open.
5. How material to Tarkett SA financially? Low-to-moderate — Russia is 2 of 34 global plants; prior reporting already flagged a Russia/Ukraine revenue slump. Likely an impairment, not existential. Open on exact figure.
6. Is the RUB 50bn (~$0.6bn) revenue the five companies' combined or Tarkett alone? Combined across all five — do NOT attribute to Tarkett. Confirmed.
7. Does the new manager (Mirkhaidarov) have any flooring/industrial track record? None evident — a regional ex-official and war veteran, company registered Feb 2026 with no disclosed operating history. The "don't write about it" request underlines opacity. Confirmed opacity; competence open.
8. Could control revert if sanctions ease? Historically rare — assets under this regime have typically moved toward forced sale, not return. Open but skewed negative for the owner.
9. Is there double-counting with the Sept-2026 retailer/Nestlé placements? No — those were separate decrees/managers; this is a distinct October amendment. Confirmed.
10. Read-across for other foreign manufacturers still operating? High signal — demonstrates that continued local operation + tax payment does NOT protect foreign owners. Relevant for any remaining Western industrials in Russia.
11. Is "Center Capital" the same as any Cbonds/Investfunds "Центр Капитал" brand? Unverified — name collision risk; the manager here is OOO UC "Center Capital" registered 2026, not an established financial firm. Open.
12. Fintech/capital-markets relevance for this digest? Indirect — it is an M&A/regulation/asset-reallocation story (tagged capital-markets), not a payments/fintech-core event. Modest for a fintech radar.
13. Any cross-border litigation / ICSID exposure for Russia? Plausible — adds to a large stock of investor-state claims, but enforcement against Russia is weak. Open.
14. Is the timing linked to EU moves on frozen Russian assets? Plausible reciprocity narrative but not stated in sources. Open.
15. Could the figures (share counts) be mis-transcribed? Cross-checked Mergers.ru vs. TASS/Meduza — share blocks (187,500 / 500,000) consistent. Confirmed.

**Importance: 3/5 — rationale.** A clear, well-sourced, genuinely new data point in Russia's ongoing foreign-asset expropriation regime, with strong read-across for other foreign industrials and a vivid "war-veteran insider gets the plant" angle. Marked down because it is an M&A/geopolitics story rather than core fintech, the direct financial materiality to Tarkett SA is modest, and it is one more instance of a now-routine mechanism (>800 entities) rather than a regime change.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a capital-markets / forced-ownership-transfer event, not a flooring-industry story: Russia's regime for foreign-owned assets of "unfriendly" jurisdictions under Decree No. 302 (25 Apr 2023). The decree does not nationalize outright — foreign shareholders remain nominal owners but lose control (disposal, dividends, management) to a state-appointed manager (per CNN / Global Trade Alert, 17 Jul 2023). Scale of the regime: Russia has placed ~135 foreign-affiliated companies under temporary administration since 2022, the large majority EU-domiciled (per sirotapartners / Euromaidan Press, Oct 2026). "Why now": the 2026 wave is explicitly framed as leverage against EU governments over frozen Russian reserves — Nestlé's assets were seized Sep 2026 (CNN, 18 Sep 2026), and the Tarkett decree bundles five European firms (Tarkett, Kappa Rus, Ontex RU, Eviosis Packaging Kuban, Menshen Packaging CIS) generating together >RUB 50bn (~$596M) annual revenue (Pravda/UNN, 16 Oct 2026). The manager here, "Centre Capital," was registered only Feb 2026 — a vehicle built for this role (per EU Perspectives / globalsanctions).

**Competitive landscape.** The "market" is the pipeline of distressed foreign-exit assets being re-allocated to Russian buyers. KPIs that matter for this asset class: (1) discount-to-fair-value on forced sale, (2) time-under-administration before resale, (3) whether a "preferred buyer" / management-buyout is pre-arranged. Observed pattern: temporary administration is typically a waypoint, not an endpoint — assets are subsequently sold to Russian owners at steep discounts (mandated ~50%+ discount to valuation for unfriendly-country exits; Carlsberg's Baltika was approved for a ~$320M management buyout, per Reuters/Investing, and Veon exited to local management in 2023). Accelerated-privatization rules (Putin decree cutting pre-sale valuation to ~10 days) compress the cycle further. Protagonist (Tarkett) position: a forcibly-captured seller with no agency — it ranked #3 globally by flooring volume and #1 in vinyl in 2024, but corporate strength is irrelevant once control passes to Centre Capital; moat = none in this context.

**Comps & multiples.** No deal value / EV disclosed for the Tarkett transfer — multiples = no data (temporary management is a control transfer, not a priced sale). Internal comps from the base show the dense 2026 Russian forced-reallocation cluster this belongs to:
- [[Russian state sells 67% of zinc and lead processor Dalpolimetall]] — state exit of a seized industrial asset (priced disposal).
- [[Germany begins privatization of nationalized Gazprom unit]] — the mirror-image case: EU nationalized SEFE (~€6bn), the retaliation this regime answers.
- [[PSB fails third auction for nationalized Vektor Rail]] and [[RZD's fourth Riga Station auction fails without bids]] — resale-stage comps showing weak bid depth / repeated failed auctions for nationalized assets.
- [[LUKOIL-Carlyle foreign asset sale agreement expired in July]] — the symmetric Russian-asset-abroad exit also stalling.
- [[Russian prosecutors drop asset-seizure claim against Timur Turlov]] — rare reversal, useful as a counter-case.
External comps (control-transfer precedents, not priced): Danone/Carlsberg(Baltika) Jul 2023, Nestlé Sep 2026 — same mechanism. Distribution not computed; qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. Expropriation-in-fact for Tarkett SA (Euronext Paris): the French parent keeps title but loses cash flows and control, with a high base-rate of eventual forced sale to a Russian buyer at a mandated discount — likely a full write-off of the Russian CGU, not a temporary impairment.
2. Serbian/third-country exposure: the seized stake runs through Tarkett's Serbian subsidiary (Bačka Palanka), showing the decree reaches non-"unfriendly" holding structures too — contagion risk for any group routing Russian ownership through friendly jurisdictions.
3. Resale illiquidity: base comps (Vektor Rail, Riga Station repeated failed auctions) signal thin domestic bid depth, so "temporary" management can persist and value erode before any disposal.
4. Reciprocity/escalation: explicitly tit-for-tat with EU handling of frozen Russian reserves (SEFE) — the inventory of capturable EU-owned assets is the live bargaining chip, so more seizures are the base case, not the tail.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This marks a shift from the 2023 "temporary management as negotiation" posture to temporary management as a pre-sale pipeline feeding Russian "preferred buyers" at administratively compressed valuations. Falsifiable thesis: within ~6–12 months the Tarkett RU / Tarkett Sommer stakes are resold to a domestic buyer (likely a management buyout or politically-connected acquirer) at a steep discount, per the Carlsberg/Veon template. Trigger to watch: a resale/MBO decree naming the buyer, or a Decree-302 amendment adding more EU firms. Thesis breaks if assets are returned to foreign owners (as Putin has floated) — the Turlov claim-withdrawal is the only base-comp pointing that direction.
