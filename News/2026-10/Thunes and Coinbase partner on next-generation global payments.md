## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-19, at Money20/20 USA (Las Vegas), Thunes and Coinbase announced a strategic partnership (PR Newswire / Thunes newsroom). Mechanics, de-PR'd:
- **Coinbase → Thunes:** Coinbase integrates Thunes' Direct Global Network (DGN) to deliver payouts into local clearing, bank accounts and mobile wallets across 140+ countries / 90+ currencies, backed by 50+ payment licences.
- **Thunes → Coinbase:** Thunes will use Coinbase's stablecoin infrastructure ("broad suite of stablecoin solutions", plus a referenced network upgrade for transaction efficiency) for its own global network.
- **Use cases named:** cross-border B2B/supplier payments, gig-economy payroll, personal remittances.
- **What is NOT disclosed:** no go-live date (framed as "will integrate"/"aims to"), no pricing/economics, no named stablecoin beyond implied USDC, no volume/TPV targets, no exclusivity. The quote from Thunes Deputy CEO Chloé Mayenobe ("connecting digital currencies to fiat and local domestic financial systems remains essential for real-world utility") is thesis-level, not a metric. **This is an announced framework, not a live, metered rail.**
- **Why structured this way:** it is a reciprocal "rails swap" — Coinbase gets last-mile fiat distribution it does not own; Thunes gets a stablecoin settlement layer to compete with Circle/Mastercard-backed peers. Each covers the other's structural gap rather than building it.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Circle Payments Network (CPN):** Thunes already joined CPN for stablecoin settlement across 140+ countries (2026-04, [[Thunes partners with Circle Payments Network for stablecoin settlement]], [[Thunes joins Circle Payments Network Managed Payments]]). Circle = USDC issuer; Coinbase = USDC co-economics partner. So Thunes now straddles both USDC-aligned camps.
- **Mastercard Move × Thunes:** near-real-time payouts to stablecoin wallets via DGN (2025-11, [[Mastercard and Thunes bring stablecoin payouts to mainstream]]).
- **Coinbase's own last-mile moves:** Citi stablecoin checkout + fiat payouts (2026-10-12, [[Citi details stablecoin checkout with Coinbase]]); PPRO (2026-05, [[PPRO and Coinbase partner on stablecoin payments for US merchants]]); Coinbase Business stablecoin tools (2025-10, [[Coinbase launches Coinbase Business stablecoin payment tools]]).
- **Position:** parity-to-slightly-behind on novelty. Thunes has run this exact play (stablecoin-in / fiat-out) with Circle and Mastercard already; Coinbase has run the fiat-payout play with Citi already. This is a new counterparty pairing, not a new capability. **Why:** stablecoin distribution is consolidating into a small set of payout networks (Thunes, Circle, Mastercard Move) — issuers/exchanges must plug into one of them, so pairings proliferate while the underlying rail count stays small.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Thunes:** Singapore cross-border network, $150M Series D (2026-06, [[Thunes opens New York office in US expansion after $150M Series D]]), aggressive stablecoin push through 2025–26 — Pay-to-Stablecoin-Wallets ([[Thunes launches Pay-to-Stablecoin-Wallets solution]], [[Thunes launches Pay-to-Stablecoin-Wallets over SWIFT]]), Circle, Mastercard. Fit: logical — DGN monetises third-party stablecoin flows; adding Coinbase/USDC is incremental supply.
- **Coinbase:** diversifying off trading toward "stablecoins and payments" (Armstrong: "no longer a bet just on the price of Bitcoin"). USDC economics are a strategic subscription-revenue pillar (see earnings). Fit: Coinbase needs fiat off-ramps globally to make USDC a payment medium, not just a trading asset — it buys that via Thunes rather than building 140-country licensing.
- **Why both act this way:** Coinbase's trading revenue is cyclical and falling (Q2'26 net loss); it must convert USDC float into recurring payment volume. Thunes has a commodity payout take-rate and needs differentiated stablecoin supply to defend pricing.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Real novelty:** low. The "stablecoin-in, local-fiat-out over DGN" pattern is already live via Circle and Mastercard. The genuinely new element is the direct Coinbase↔Thunes link and the (vague) Coinbase "network upgrade for efficiency."
- **Traction:** none disclosed — no live clients, no corridors live, no volumes. Announced, not adopted.
- **Who captures the margin (analysis):** the last-mile payout rail (Thunes DGN) and the stablecoin issuer/economics (Circle/Coinbase on USDC reserve yield) capture value; the "partnership" layer is distribution. Coinbase's upside is USDC balance growth and transaction fees, not a new fee line. Risk: if multiple issuers all route through the same payout networks, the payout network (Thunes) gains pricing power and the exchange is commoditised to a USDC supplier.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Watch for a go-live date, named corridors, and whether USDC is the exclusive stablecoin. Catalyst: Coinbase Q3 2026 results on 2026-10-29 — any "stablecoin & payments" revenue/USDC-balance call-out would validate (or not) that these partnerships convert to revenue.
- Regulatory backdrop: US GENIUS-era stablecoin clarity is the "why now"; both cite "evolving global crypto policies."
- **Second-order (analysis):** as stablecoin payout rails consolidate, the strategic prize shifts from "who issues the stablecoin" to "who owns the 140-country last mile" — which favours Thunes-type networks over exchanges, making Coinbase's dependence on Thunes a structural, not incidental, feature.

## FRESHNESS / DUPLICATE VERDICT
**FRESH.** This is a genuinely new counterparty pairing (Coinbase × Thunes, announced 2026-10-19 at Money20/20) and NOT a reprint of the same-day/adjacent notes. It is distinct from [[Citi details stablecoin checkout with Coinbase]] (different partner, US-first merchant checkout) and from [[Thunes partners with Circle Payments Network for stablecoin settlement]] / [[Mastercard and Thunes bring stablecoin payouts to mainstream]] (different stablecoin counterparties). Novelty is modest (importance 3), but it is a new event, not a duplicate.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Cross-border payments + stablecoin settlement. Thunes network spans 140+ countries, 90+ currencies, 50+ payment licences (per deal PR, 2026-10-19) — a proxy for how fragmented local-rail licensing is and why distribution is the moat. Secular driver: US stablecoin legislation (GENIUS-era) + "evolving global crypto policies" (both parties cite) make regulated stablecoins viable as a payment medium, not just a trading asset — "why now." Second-order: as regulatory clarity arrives, the value in stablecoin payments shifts from *issuance* (crowded: USDC, USDT, bank coins) to *last-mile fiat distribution* (scarce: 140-country licensing), which is the scarce asset here. Latest reported period = Q2 2026 (ended 2026-06-30, released 2026-07-30); Q3 2026 reports 2026-10-29 (not yet out as of 2026-10-20).

**Verdict (headline read).** MISS · Q2 2026 revenue $1.2bn (-20% YoY, from $1.5bn; vs public consensus ~$1.3bn, a ~$0.1bn/~8% miss) · net loss $359.5m, EPS -$1.36 (vs consensus loss of ~$0.17; large miss) · driver: spot-trading softness (industry spot volumes down >20%) · guidance: no Q3 guidance given, FY26 adjusted-expense range narrowed.

**Key figures (with growth).**
- Total revenue: $1.2bn, -20% YoY (Q2'25 $1.5bn).
- Transaction revenue: $599m (under consensus and below year-ago).
- Subscription & services revenue: $555m (~48% of net revenue, up from ~29% in Q4'24 — structural mix shift toward recurring).
- Stablecoin revenue: $292m, -$17m vs Q2'25 (~$309m); came in below the ~$327m public forecast — a soft spot despite record balances.
- Average USDC held in Coinbase products: ~$20bn, all-time high, +44% YoY — balances up but revenue down (yield/rate drag; who's-silent flag).
- Net loss: $359.5m; EPS -$1.36 vs a $1.43bn profit / $5.14 EPS in Q2'25 (prior year lapped a huge crypto-up quarter).

**By segment / driver.** Decline led by transaction (cyclical spot-trading drop); subscription & services (incl. stablecoin, staking, custody) is the stabiliser and now near half of revenue. Stablecoin line under-performed vs forecast even with record USDC balances — the key read-across for the Thunes deal: more USDC distribution does not automatically lift stablecoin revenue if rate/yield and take economics compress.

**vs expectations / prior period.** Revenue ~$1.2bn vs public consensus ~$1.3bn (miss ~8%); EPS -$1.36 vs consensus loss ~$0.17 (large miss) — third consecutive quarter short of Street. Trend vs prior quarter: Q1 2026 also posted a steep loss and revenue miss ([[Coinbase posts steep Q1 2026 loss as revenue falls]], EPS -$1.49 vs +$0.27 expected; [[Coinbase reports record Q1 trading share, resumes service after outage]]). So deceleration/loss trend is sustained, not a one-off. Public consensus figures are free-aggregator sourced ("public consensus"), not paid Street.

**Guidance / forward.** No specific Q3 2026 guidance; FY26 adjusted-expense range narrowed (cost discipline). Public consensus for Q3 2026 (reports 2026-10-29): ~-$0.26 EPS on ~$1.19bn revenue (vs $1.44 EPS / $1.87bn a year earlier) — i.e. the Street models another loss and ~down-YoY quarter. Management tone (CFO Alesia Haas): "difficult operating environment," spot volumes down >20%; CEO Armstrong: "Coinbase is no longer a bet just on the price of Bitcoin," pushing payments/stablecoins/lending diversification — which is exactly what the Thunes tie-up serves.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. Revenue mix de-risking (S&S ~48%) is the positive structural story — but it is not yet offsetting trading declines; net losses persist. Second-order: diversification narrative needs the payments/stablecoin line (which the Thunes deal feeds) to actually grow.
2. Stablecoin revenue fell despite record $20bn USDC balances — balance growth ≠ revenue growth; rate/yield sensitivity is the silent risk. Direct caution on reading the Thunes partnership as near-term revenue.
3. Guidance absence + narrowed cost range = management managing a down-cycle, not calling a bottom. Q3 2026 print on 2026-10-29 is the near-term catalyst.
