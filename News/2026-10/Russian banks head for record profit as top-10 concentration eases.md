# Russian banks head for record profit as top-10 concentration eases

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] TL;DR.** Russia's banking sector is on course for a record 2026 net profit of ~4–4.2 trillion rubles (Expert RA; CBR range raised to 3.9–4.4trn), after earning 3.3trn in the first eight months (+36% y/y). The newsworthy twist is a reversal in profit *concentration*: the top-10 banks' share of sector profit fell to ~72% (Jan–Jul 2026) from 75% in 2025 — the first decline in three years — even as their share of assets held near a record ~82%. Mid-tier banks (roughly 11th–50th) are earning relatively more per ruble of assets.

**[1] Key figures (dated, sourced).**
- 4–4.2trn rub — Expert RA 2026 full-year net-profit forecast (press briefing 2026-10-15); a historic sector maximum.
- 3.9–4.4trn rub — CBR's raised 2026 forecast (up from an earlier 3.3–3.8trn, Mar 2026). Sberbank CEO Gref also guided ~4trn.
- 3.3trn rub — sector profit for 8M2026, +36% vs 8M2025; August alone ~440bn rub (slightly down m/m).
- Top-10 share of profit: 72% (8M2026) vs 75% (2025), 74% (2024), 73% (2023) — first drop in three years. Q1 2026 was as low as ~67% before recovering to 72%.
- Expert RA itself had forecast *76%* top-10 profit share for 2026 back in March 2026 — so the deconcentration is a surprise versus its own prior view.
- Top-10 share of assets: ~82% in 2026 (80.9% at 1 Jul 2025, ~81.5% projected end-2025) — concentration in *assets* keeps rising even as profit deconcentrates.
- Risky corporate-loan restructurings: 4.9% of the corporate book vs 4.3% at the start of the year — a rising asset-quality warning sign.

**[2] Drivers.**
- *Net interest income.* Sector NII grew ~a third in H1 2026. As the CBR key rate eased, large banks cut deposit rates faster than asset yields fell, widening net interest margin (e.g. VTB NIM reportedly ~2.6% vs ~1.0% a year earlier). This is the main profit engine.
- *Why deconcentration.* Growth in income at 11th–50th-ranked banks; in H1 2026 household deposits at top-10 banks fell ~1% on average, loosening their funding dominance. Big banks' outsized NIM recovery is partly a 2025-base effect.
- *Headwinds.* Reserve/provisioning expense rose (e.g. ~213bn rub in August, mostly corporate); unrealised negative revaluation of amortised-cost securities ~0.6trn rub (~3% of sector capital). Rising restructurings (4.9%) flag latent credit risk.
- *Source chain.* Primary = Expert RA sector review + CBR banking-sector monthly data; secondary = Kommersant, Vedomosti, Interfax/TASS, Prime.

**[3] Internal links (prior notes).**
- [[Russians withdrew 550bn roubles from banks in May]] — deposit outflows / funding dynamics feeding the top-10 deposit-share decline.
- [[Bank of Russia keeps Raiffeisen, UniCredit systemically important]] — the CBR's systemically-important (top-tier) bank list underlying "top-10" concentration framing.
- [[Mergers.ru Gref advises state companies on successful IPOs]] — Sberbank/Gref, the dominant top-10 profit contributor.

**[4] Numbers to watch / open questions.**
- Will the top-10 profit share keep sliding into 2026 full-year, or snap back as it did Q1→Jul (67%→72%)?
- Does the restructuring ratio (4.9% and rising) translate into a provisioning spike that dents the 4trn target?
- CBR 3.9–4.4trn vs Expert RA 4–4.2trn vs Gref ~4trn — tight cluster; a key-rate reversal or securities-revaluation hit is the main downside.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions.**
1. Is 72% really a three-year low, or just a mid-year reading that could revert? Q1 was ~67% then rebounded to 72% by July — so the "reversal of trend" headline may be premature until the full-year print lands.
2. Expert RA forecast 76% top-10 share in March 2026 and now reports 72% — is this a genuine structural shift or a forecasting miss being re-narrated as a "trend reversal"?
3. "Record profit" in nominal rubles ignores inflation and ruble depreciation — in real or USD terms, is 2026 actually a record vs 2024's 3.8trn? (CBR says 2025 was 3.5trn, down 8% from 2024.)
4. Does the profit deconcentration reflect mid-tier *strength* or top-10 *weakness* (e.g. a 2025 low base at big banks inflating their y/y recovery)?
5. Asset concentration is still rising to ~82% while profit deconcentrates — are these measuring different things (balance-sheet size vs. margin capture) rather than a coherent "concentration eases" story?
6. The NIM-widening driver depends on deposit rates falling faster than asset yields — reversible if the CBR hikes again. How durable is the H1 margin tailwind?
7. Rising risky restructurings (4.3%→4.9%) could force provisioning that erases the forecast beat — is the 4trn number pre- or post-adequate provisioning?
8. Unrealised securities losses (~0.6trn, ~3% of capital) sit off the P&L via amortised-cost accounting — is "record profit" flattered by not marking these to market?
9. Source concentration: the figures trace to one agency (Expert RA) cross-quoted by many outlets — are the top-10 profit-share numbers independently verifiable from raw CBR data?
10. "11th–50th banks earning more" — is this broad-based or driven by a few specific names (e.g. one-off gains, M&A, sanctioned-bank effects)?
11. How much of sector profit is Sberbank alone? If Sber's share moved, the whole "top-10 vs rest" story could be a single-bank artifact.
12. Does the 8M +36% y/y growth annualise cleanly to the ~4trn forecast, or is H2 expected to decelerate sharply (August already flat m/m)?
13. Any sanctions/geopolitical tail risk (secondary sanctions, frozen-asset actions) that could hit H2 2026 that the forecast ignores?

**Importance: 3/5** — a credible, well-sourced sector-level data point (record profit + a genuine first-in-three-years concentration shift) with macro relevance to Russian banking, but it is a forecast/mid-year read from a single agency, nominal-ruble "record," and the deconcentration may revert; informative but not market-moving on its own.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russia's banking sector is on track for a record 2026 net profit: Expert RA forecasts 4–4.2 trn rubles, with 3.3 trn already earned in 8M 2026 (+36% YoY) per the note citing CBR; the CBR itself raised its own forecast to 3.9–4.4 trn rubles on persistently high margins (per Interfax, as of 2026). This follows a *down* year — the sector earned 3.5 trn rubles in 2025, −8% vs 2024's 3.8 trn, with average ROE falling to ~18% (from ~23% in 2024) as cost of risk rose (per CBR, via Interfax). So 2026's "record" is a recovery-and-overshoot driven by net interest income, not a smooth trend. Structure is highly consolidated: top-10 hold ~82% of sector assets (note), an oligopolistic core (Sber, VTB, Gazprombank, Alfa, state agri/retail banks) with regulatory/capital/network barriers. Key driver + "why now": net interest margin is unusually wide — CBR improved its 2026 NIM expectation to 4.9–5.1% (via Interfax) — because high-yield assets booked during the tightening cycle still earn while the key rate, held at 14.00% since Sept 2026 (next meeting 2026-10-23, per CBR), has begun easing from its peak. Fact → wide NIM lifts NII → NII is the single largest profit lever for Russian banks → as the rate cycle turns, this tailwind is the thing most exposed to reversal (second-order).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: NIM, ROE, cost of risk / provisioning, NII growth, loan-book growth. The note's actual story is *deconcentration of profit*: top-10 share of sector profit fell to 72% (from 75% in 2025) even as asset share stayed ~82% — i.e. second-tier banks earned relatively more per ruble of assets in 2026. Expert RA itself had expected the opposite (forecast 76% in March); Q1 2026 came in at only 67%, recovering to 72% by July. The leader datapoint anchors scale: Sber posted 9M 2025 IFRS net profit of 1.307 trn rubles (+6.5%), ROE 23.7%, Q3 NIM 6.2% (per TASS/Interfax, 2025-10-28; IR: [Sber 9M 2025 IFRS results presentation](https://www.sberbank.com/common/img/uploaded/files/info/presentation_q3_z2lvsxdf_2025.pdf)) — i.e. the leader runs ROE well above the ~18% 2025 sector average, which is precisely *why* top-10 normally over-index on profit. Position: Sber/VTB remain the entrenched incumbents (moat = scale + deposit franchise + state backing), but the 2026 shift suggests mid-tier banks captured relatively more of the margin windfall this year `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** This is a sector-aggregate research-report item, not a deal/round — no transaction valuation to multiple out, so **trading multiples = no data / [UNSOURCED]** (Russian banks are largely delisted/sanctioned; no clean free public EV/EBITDA or P/E). Operating comparison instead:
- Sber 9M 2025: ROE 23.7%, FY2025 ROE 22.7% (per Investing.com slides) — leader.
- Sector 2025: ROE ~18%; profit 3.5 trn rubles (CBR).
- Sector 2026F: 4–4.2 trn rubles profit (Expert RA), NIM 4.9–5.1% (CBR).
Internal comps (in-base): [[Public sector banks post record ₹1.98 lakh crore net profit in FY26]] and [[Indian listed banks post record ₹4.11 lakh crore profit in FY26]] — same "banking-sector record-profit" pattern in another market; [[VTB takes large Ozon share stake as collateral]], [[Russian banks oppose shifting Pochta Rossii rescue costs]], [[PSB auctions Agropromkredit bank for 2.85bn rubles]] for RU-sector context. Distribution not computed (no comparable valuation figures); qualitative only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **NIM compression as the rate cuts land.** The record rests on a wide 4.9–5.1% NIM funded by legacy high-yield assets; once the key rate (14.00%, easing) feeds through to repricing, NII — the main profit driver — compresses. A "record" built on a cyclical margin peak is not a run-rate.
2. **Provisioning / asset-quality overhang.** The note flags risky corporate-loan restructurings rising to 4.9% (from 4.3% at start of year); 2025's profit *fall* was explicitly driven by higher cost of risk (CBR). Deferred provisioning can convert into charges that erase the 2026 beat.
3. **Sustainability / base effect.** 2026's +36% is partly a rebound off a depressed 2025 (−8%); the CBR's own range (3.9–4.4 trn) tops near the record only at the high end. Sanctions keep the sector capital-constrained (VTB CEO flagged capital-raising as the 2026 challenge, per MarketScreener) — a cap on balance-sheet growth as a profit source.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** The signal worth watching is not the headline record but the *deconcentration* reversal: for the first time in three years top-10 profit share fell. Falsifiable thesis `(analysis)`: if this is structural (mid-tier banks durably out-earning on a per-asset basis), expect sustained profit-share erosion of the top-10 through 2027; trigger/catalyst = full-year 2026 and Q1 2027 sector data — if top-10 share re-climbs toward 75%+, the 2026 dip was a cyclical/quarterly artifact (Q1 2026 was 67% then recovered to 72%), not a trend break.
