# Context-enrichment: Vest raises $13M for crypto and traditional asset trading
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

> Correction to the aggregated digest text: Vest (legally **Vest Labs**, New York) is NOT a retail "buy/sell crypto + equities" brokerage. It is a **profit-sharing proprietary-trading platform** for perpetual futures — qualified traders use *company capital* to trade perps across crypto (and planned equities/commodities/FX) and keep most of the profit. The "crypto and traditional asset trading" framing refers to the asset classes of the perps, not a consumer trading app.

## [0] What happened
- **Vest Labs** (NY) closed a **$13M round** (reported as pre-seed/seed; closed July 2026, announced ~2026-10-07) **led by Portal Ventures**, with angel checks from senior execs at **Citadel Securities, BlackRock, and KKR**. Valuation undisclosed.
- Founders: three UPenn dropouts — **Justin Ma (CEO)**, Rikuya Takatsu, Maximilian Tsiang. Ma previously built/sold trading app Berri; met Portal GP Catrina Wang at the 2024 Stanford Blockchain Conference.
- Use of funds: build a mobile app, hire (team ~22), expand asset categories (equities/commodities/FX perps beyond crypto).

## [1] The model (why it's novel)
- Traditional retail prop firms monetize by **selling simulated "challenge" accounts** and evaluation fees; critics argue many effectively profit when traders fail. Vest's pitch is the **inverse**: real capital on live markets, traders keep **up to 80% (marketed 80–95%)** of profit, firm takes the rest — "doesn't bet against its traders."
- VC backing for a prop firm is unusual (most bootstrap). The $13M reportedly sits in the ~97th percentile of US fintech seed rounds — signal of conviction, not scale.

## [2] Traction (fresh, late Sept 2026)
- **27,000 registered traders**; **~26% received cash payouts**; **>300% MoM** growth in active traders and volume. Early-stage numbers; "registered" != funded/active.
- Market context cited: retail prop-trading revenue ~**$850M in 2026**, +45% YoY.

## [3] Internal cross-links (News/)
- [[Robinhood launches 10x leverage on Bitcoin and Ether]] — adjacent US push into leveraged crypto perps for retail; different structure (brokerage margin vs prop capital) but same demand for high-leverage crypto exposure.
- [[Argentina's Cocos launches standalone Cocos Crypto app]] — contrasting model: retail buy/sell/custody crypto app (the kind Vest is NOT).
- [[SPB Exchange plans crypto spot trading and settlement depositary]] — exchange-side crypto buildout, illustrates the broader "crypto as a tradable asset class" wave.
- (Name note: unrelated to [[Vestwell raises $385M Series E, doubles valuation to $2B]] — that's a US retirement/401(k) infra firm.)

## [4] So what / watch-items
- Regulatory surface is the key risk: profit-sharing prop-trading on crypto perps sits in a lightly-defined US regime; "up to 80%" marketing + funded-account mechanics attract CFTC/SEC and consumer-protection scrutiny.
- Portal Ventures (crypto-native) + TradFi angels (Citadel/BlackRock/KKR) is an unusual coalition — read as a bet that the prop/funded-trader model migrates on-chain.
- Watch: conversion from 27k "registered" to funded traders, payout sustainability, and whether the equities/FX perps expansion actually ships.

# Challenge / Red-team: Vest $13M

1. Is the digest framing ("crypto and traditional asset trading") even accurate? (No — it's a *prop-trading* platform where traders use firm capital on perpetual futures, not a consumer multi-asset brokerage. Equities/commodities/FX are *planned* perps, not live.)
2. Is it a "pre-seed" or a "seed"? Sources disagree (financemagnates: pre-seed; kucoin/Fortune: seed). What's the registered round type? Does the distinction change the "97th percentile" claim?
3. Who actually led and co-invested? Confirm Portal Ventures lead + Citadel Securities/BlackRock/KKR execs as *angels* (personal, not institutional checks — easy to over-read as firm endorsements).
4. When did the money close vs announce? (Closed July 2026, announced Oct 7 — a 3-month gap; the "news" is PR timing, not a fresh event.)
5. How real is "27,000 traders"? Registered != funded != active. What share are funded accounts actually trading firm capital?
6. "26% received cash payouts" — of whom? All 27k registered, or only funded traders? Payout size undisclosed — could be trivial.
7. ">300% MoM growth" off what base? Early-stage percentages are nearly meaningless without absolute numbers.
8. Is "80–95% profit split" sustainable if the firm bears all downside on company capital? What's the risk-management / drawdown gate that qualifies a trader? (Classic prop-firm economics still rely on most funded traders losing/blowing accounts.)
9. Does "doesn't bet against its traders" survive scrutiny? If the firm nets trader flow or hedges, it may still profit from aggregate trader losses — verify the actual capital/P&L mechanics.
10. Regulatory exposure: crypto perpetual futures for US persons + funded-account marketing — CFTC/SEC/state posture? Is Vest offshore-structured for perps access?
11. Competitive moat: vs FTMO/other prop firms going on-chain, vs Robinhood/Hyperliquid-style perps, vs eToro/Revolut. What's defensible beyond the profit-split marketing?
12. Valuation undisclosed — is $13M large *relative to* dilution/stage, or just a big absolute number for a 22-person pre-revenue-model firm?
13. Founder track record: Ma "sold Berri" — at what outcome/price? Dropout founders + crypto-native lead — execution risk on a regulated-adjacent product.
14. Is this genuinely differentiated or a re-skin of the funded-trader model with a crypto wrapper and better PR?

Importance: 3/5 — Novel model (VC-backed prop firm flipping the funded-trader incentive on-chain) with a notable investor coalition (Portal + Citadel/BlackRock/KKR angels), which makes it a signal worth tracking. But it's a small pre-seed/seed at an early-traction startup with soft metrics and real regulatory overhang; not market-moving on its own. Thesis-interesting, not materially consequential yet.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** The aggregated one-liner ("trading platform covering crypto and traditional assets") is misleading. Per Fortune/Cryptobriefing (2026-10-07), the $13M seed (led by Portal Ventures, closed July; angels from Citadel Securities, BlackRock, KKR) funds Vest Labs — a **profit-sharing prop / funded-trader firm built on a crypto perps venue**, not a Robinhood-style retail super-app. Qualified traders deploy *Vest's* capital on perpetual futures (crypto, equity indices, commodities) ~24/7 and keep up to 80% of profits; Vest earns the rest, inverting the standard "sell challenges, profit when traders fail" model. Two sectors stack here: (1) retail prop/funded-trader — ~$850M in 2026, +45% YoY, ~2.1M funded traders / ~12M challenge purchases, top-5 firms (FTMO, FundedNext, 5%ers, Apex, TopStep) ~62% share (per track360, undated vendor estimate — treat as directional, `[UNSOURCED]` rigor); (2) on-chain perp DEX — where Vest Exchange is a long-tail venue, 0.7% of on-chain perp OI (~$91.4M) as of 2026-10-14 vs Hyperliquid's ~57% (per castlelabs/coingecko). Why now: prop-firm boom colliding with a 2024–early-2026 collapse of ~80–100 firms and a CFTC signal it may treat evaluation futures-prop firms as CTAs — a shakeout that rewards a differentiated, "aligned" model.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for prop — funded traders, payout rate, challenge→funded pass rate, net take after payouts; for the perp venue — OI, 24h volume, fees. Vest cites 27,000 traders with ~26% having received a cash payout by late Sept, MAU + volume +300% MoM, and daily revenue scaling $25k → $1M+ in two weeks in Sept (all **company-provided, de-PR skeptically — unaudited**). Players: on the prop side FTMO/FundedNext/Apex/TopStep (fiat/MetaTrader, upfront-fee model); on the venue side Hyperliquid (leader), dYdX, Aster, Lighter, Drift, Paradex, GRVT. Basis of competition: for prop — trust/payout reliability + economics alignment; for the venue — liquidity/OI depth + fees. Vest's position: **niche/sub-scale** on the perp-DEX axis (0.7% OI), but with a potentially differentiated distribution play (profit-share funded model) that could feed liquidity into its own book. Moat `(analysis)`: thin today — the perp tech is commoditized (Hyperliquid/dYdX far deeper), and the "we don't bet against you" promise is a positioning claim, not a structural barrier; any competitor can copy the split.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/post-money was disclosed for the round → **round valuation = no data**; price-per-user / EV multiples not computable → `[UNSOURCED]`. Internal comps (seed/early rounds for leveraged/crypto trading startups): [[Trading startup Liquid raises $18 million seed round]] (24/7 leveraged trading, $18M seed, Apr 2026 — comparable stage/theme), [[NAGA Group secures $8M via convertible bond at premium]] (listed multi-asset trading, 2026-10), [[India's Raise Financial becomes unicorn after $120M Series B]] (retail brokerage, different geo/model). Distribution not computed (no disclosed valuations for the private comps) → qualitative: $13M seed sits in-line to slightly below the $18M Liquid seed for a thematically similar 24/7-trading startup; nothing here reads rich given the traction claims — but with no valuation and unaudited metrics, no multiple can be anchored.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory / structural** — CFTC may reclassify evaluation-based futures-prop firms as CTAs (registration, capital disclosure); separately, offering perps on US-facing retail is a known derivatives-licensing minefield. A crypto-perps prop firm sits at the intersection of two regimes tightening at once → second-order: the "aligned" model doesn't exempt it from the same CFTC/securities overhang that just collapsed ~80–100 peers.
2. **Principal/balance-sheet & adverse-selection risk** — because Vest keeps ~20% and funds traders with *its own* capital on perps, it is effectively the house taking the other side of leveraged trades; a $25k→$1M daily-revenue spike in two weeks is as consistent with a favorable-variance streak as with durable edge → if its best traders are genuinely profitable, Vest's P&L is the mirror image, and one volatility regime shift can impair the book.
3. **Metrics quality / durability** — "+300% MoM" and the revenue ramp are self-reported, pre-audit, off a tiny base (~$91M OI, rank ~22 among perp DEXs); concentration in a handful of whales and reflexive crypto-volume cyclicality make the growth highly non-annualizable.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The interesting bet is convergence: a funded-trader *distribution layer* bolted onto an on-chain perp *venue* — turning prop-firm demand into captive exchange liquidity. Falsifiable thesis: if the model is real, Vest's share of on-chain perp OI should climb off 0.7% over the next 2–3 quarters *as* payout rate holds. What breaks it: OI stalls/falls, payout rate drops, or a CFTC/securities action lands — any of which signals the "alignment" story is marketing over structure. Watch: next OI/volume prints and whether a regulated entity/license emerges.
