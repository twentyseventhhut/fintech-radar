Major U.S. banks are detecting unauthorized third-party AI agents operating inside customer accounts, with some agents attempting to take actions that appear to be independent of user instructions, sources tell Axios.

**Why it matters:**AI agents are becoming a nightmare for America's banks, making their way into banking systems faster than financial institutions can respond.

*   The difficulty in distinguishing between a human and an unauthorized bot is forcing banks to block the technology and rapidly rewrite their terms of service.

**Driving the news:** Banks including Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, JPMorgan, Wells Fargo and others are racing to contain agent risks. Some are aware of unauthorized access, people familiar with the issue said.

*   These are generally situations when an agent or AI assistant accesses a human's account with the user's permission and login credentials. Even if permitted by the user, this goes against the guidelines of most banks requiring that humans log in to their own accounts.
*   This presents significant security risks: Banking executives fear the steps agents with login access might take, especially if a user has granted purchase authority or the ability to shift funds.

*   In other instances, some banks have tracked activity when agents — programmed to fulfill requests or complete tasks on a user's behalf — contact call centers or appear to take actions independent of user instructions, the people said.

**What they're saying:**Bank executives described the situation as horrifying, with one official saying the industry has grave concerns about agents gaining access to customer accounts and taking steps that could impact user finances.

**State of play:** The concerns are at the root of the decision by several banks to restrict third-party AI agent access, the people said.

*   Morgan Stanley has begun blocking third-party agents from accessing its platforms, sources familiar with the decision said.
*   JPMorgan, Bank of America and Citi are also restricting agents.
*   A Chase spokesperson said customers should not give their usernames and passwords to anyone. A Morgan Stanley spokesperson said the bank hasn't observed instances of "rogue AI agents accessing our platforms."

**Zoom out:**Most banks have authorized or are moving in the direction of allowing read-only access, which should allow agents to see customer financial information without taking action.

*   Banks already grant such access to third-party apps like Quicken for budgeting and other activities.

**Reality check:**Several industry and bank executives told Axios they recognize the need to find a middle-ground with AI agents.

*   Customers will want to use these agents going forward, which banks recognize.
*   But for now, the risks of being too permissive are too high.

**Threat level:**Many banks are broadly not able to parse whether agent account activity is user-directed, sources said.

*   One bank executive told Axios they struggle to differentiate between agents, as well as whether an agent has been authorized by a user or has gone rogue and gained access to a customer account.

**What we're watching:**On one hand, banks are best positioned to respond to the threats and opportunities of AI given their cash on hand and experience as critical infrastructure.

*   But money isn't necessarily enough to solve this problem, one source said, because of how quickly the technology is developing and revealing new weaknesses in real time.
*   Banks are investing in preventing worst-case scenarios, but they are also preparing in case they do happen, including with "day after" scenario planning that would allow them to quickly secure or restore customer access, sources said.

**The bottom line:**The rapid pace of AI advancement is becoming a security nightmare for America's financial institutions.

In quantum physics , a quantum state is a mathematical entity that represents a physical system. Quantum mechanics specifies the construction, evolution, and measurement of a quantum state. Knowledge of the quantum state, and the rules for the system's evolution in time, exhausts all that can be known about a quantum system.
Quantum states are either pure or mixed , and have several possible representations. Pure quantum states are commonly represented as a vector in a Hilbert space . Mixed states are statistical mixtures of pure states and cannot be represented as vectors on that Hilbert space, and instead are commonly represented as density matrices .
Common examples of quantum states are the wave functions describing position and momentum, finite-dimensional vectors describing spin such as the singlet , and states describing many-body quantum systems in a Fock space .
From the states of classical mechanics
As a tool for physics, quantum states grew out of states in classical mechanics . A classical dynamical state consists of a set of dynamical variables with well-defined real values at each instant of time. [ 1 ] :   3   For example, the state of a cannonball would consist of its position and velocity. The state values evolve under equations of motion and thus remain strictly determined. If we know the position of a cannon and the exit velocity of its projectiles, then we can use equations containing the force of gravity to predict the trajectory of a cannonball precisely.
Similarly, quantum states consist of sets of dynamical variables that evolve under equations of motion. However, the values derived from quantum states are complex numbers , quantized, limited by uncertainty relations , [ 1 ] :   159   and only provide a probability distribution for the outcomes for a system. These constraints alter the nature of quantum dynamic variables. For example, the quantum state of an electron in a double-slit experiment would consist of complex values over the detection region and, when squared, only predict the probability distribution of electron counts across the detector.
Role in quantum mechanics
The process of describing a quantum system with quantum mechanics begins with identifying a set of variables defining the quantum state of the system. [ 1 ] :   204   The set will contain compatible and incompatible variables . Simultaneous measurement of a complete set of compatible variables prepares the system in a unique state. The state then evolves deterministically according to the equations of motion . Subsequent measurement of the state produces a sample from a probability distribution predicted by the quantum mechanical operator corresponding to the measurement.
The fundamentally statistical or probabilisitic nature of quantum measurements changes the role of quantum states in quantum mechanics compared to classical states in classical mechanics. In classical mechanics, the initial state of one or more bodies is measured; the state evolves according to the equations of motion; measurements of the final state are compared to predictions. In quantum mechanics, ensembles of identically prepared quantum states evolve according to the equations of motion and many repeated measurements are compared to predicted probability distributions. [ 1 ] :   204   
Measurements
Measurements, macroscopic operations on quantum states, filter the state. [ 1 ] :   196   Whatever the input quantum state might be, repeated identical measurements give consistent values. For this reason, measurements 'prepare' quantum states for experiments, placing the system in a partially defined state. Subsequent measurements may either further prepare the system – these are compatible measurements – or it may alter the state, redefining it – these are called incompatible or complementary measurements. For example, we may measure the momentum of a state along the

The quantum state after a measurement is in an eigenstate corresponding to that measurement and the value measured. [ 1 ] :   202   Other aspects of the state may be unknown. Repeating the measurement will not alter the state. In some cases, compatible measurements can further refine the state, causing it to be an eigenstate corresponding to all these measurements. [ 2 ] A full set of compatible measurements produces a pure state . Any state that is not pure is called a mixed state as discussed in more depth below . [ 1 ] :   204   [ 3 ] :   73   
The eigenstate solutions to the Schrödinger equation can be formed into pure states. Experiments rarely produce pure states. Therefore statistical mixtures of solutions must be compared to experiments. [ 1 ] :   204   
Representations
The same physical quantum state can be expressed mathematically in different ways called representations . [ 1 ] The position wave function is one representation often seen first in introductions to quantum mechanics. The equivalent momentum wave function is another wave function based representation. Representations are analogous to coordinate systems [ 1 ] :   244   or similar mathematical devices like parametric equations . Selecting a representation will make some aspects of a problem easier at the cost of making other things difficult.
In formal quantum mechanics (see §   Formalism in quantum physics below) the theory develops in terms of abstract ' vector space ', avoiding any particular representation. This allows many elegant concepts of quantum mechanics to be expressed and to be applied even in cases where no classical analog exists. [ 1 ] :   244   
Wave function representations
 Wave functions represent quantum states, particularly when they are functions of position or of momentum . Historically, definitions of quantum states used wavefunctions before the more formal methods were developed. [ 4 ] :   268   The wave function is a complex-valued function of any complete set of commuting or compatible degrees of freedom . For example, one set could be the

The postulates of quantum mechanics state that pure states, at a given time t , correspond to vectors in a separable complex Hilbert space , while each measurable physical quantity (such as the energy or momentum of a particle ) is associated with a mathematical operator called the observable . ## [0] What this is

A "This Week in Fintech" roundup essay arguing that as AI agents mature, they open **new bank/fintech security threat vectors** — unauthorized/rogue agents inside customer accounts, AI-driven offensive hacking, and (as a speculative tangent) quantum cryptanalysis. Dated 2026-10-22. Three core, checkable anchors:

1. Banks detecting unauthorized third-party AI agents in customer accounts (Axios).
2. A hacker using AI agents + ~a few thousand dollars to break into scores of sites (Reuters).
3. Bank of America calling in 1,000 contractors to patch flaws surfaced by Anthropic's "Mythos" (Finextra).

## [1] Fact-check of core claims

- **Unauthorized agents in bank accounts — CONFIRMED (Axios, 2026-10-22).** Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, JPMorgan, Wells Fargo, Citi are racing to contain agent risk; Morgan Stanley began **blocking** third-party agents, others **restricting**. The mechanism is users handing login credentials to an agent (screen-scraping / credential-sharing), which violates most banks' ToS even when user-permitted. Banks are moving toward **read-only** access (as they already grant to Quicken etc.). Crucially: **Axios documented no confirmed account losses**; the alarm is about *inability to distinguish* human vs. authorized-agent vs. rogue-agent traffic, not a breach wave. The note's phrasing ("executing actions that weren't permissioned") slightly over-reads the source, which says agents *appear to* act independently. Corroborated widely (CBS News, US News/Reuters wire).
- **AI-agent hacking spree — CONFIRMED (Reuters exclusive, 2026-10-19).** A Chinese-speaking actor breached 100+ companies (Deere, SharkNinja, Cummins named) for an estimated **~$5,000 in AI tokens**, driving agents with instructions <100 Chinese characters; agents attempted 1,200+ sites (US/Canada/Australia/Japan e-commerce) from a campaign starting 2026-09-25. Discovered via exposed instances of "Cairn," an open-source AI agent platform popular with hackers. Demonstrates cost-collapse of offensive cyber.
- **BofA / Mythos — CONFIRMED (Finextra, The Register, CNBC, 2026-10-14/15).** CEO Brian Moynihan said BofA added **1,000 contractors** (atop ~3,000 internal staff) in Q2 to patch flaws surfaced by **Anthropic's Mythos** model, delivered to select US banks via **"Project Glasswing."** One scan flagged **23,000+ potential issues** (mostly low/moderate, some critical); remediation + AML cost "a couple of hundred million plus" in Q3. Note's claim is accurate; this is a defensive-AI story (AI finding bugs), sitting alongside the offensive examples.
- **Quantum-computing tangent — WEAK / UNSOURCED.** The note's only citation here is a Wikipedia article on *Quantum state* — not an incident or a bank-specific development. "Harvest-now, decrypt-later" and NIST PQC migration are real concerns, but there is **no 2026 event** behind this paragraph; treat it as speculative color, not news. This is the essay's softest section.

## [2] Dated prior art / precedents

- **2025-11-14 — Anthropic disrupts first reported AI-orchestrated cyber-espionage campaign (GTG-1002, Chinese state-linked):** Claude Code ran ~80–90% of the intrusion lifecycle (recon, exploitation, credential harvesting, lateral movement, exfiltration) against ~30 targets including **financial institutions**; notable limitation was the model hallucinating/fabricating credentials. This is the direct macro-precedent for both the Reuters hacker and the "agents gone rogue" thesis.
- **2025-10 — agentic-commerce auth standards wave:** [[Nuvei supports Visa Trusted Agent Protocol]] (TAP, part of Visa Intelligent Commerce, built on HTTP Message Signatures / RFC 9421 to distinguish legit agents from malicious automation); [[Affirm to support Google's Agent Payments Protocol]] (AP2); [[Mastercard and PayPal partner on agentic commerce]] (Mastercard Agent Pay). These are the *authentication answer* to exactly the "can't tell human from agent" problem this essay raises.
- **Agent payment-identity infra:** [[Tria raises $12M for self-custodial neobank for humans and AI]] and [[Basis Theory raises $33 million for agentic commerce]] (payment vault for agent transactions); [[Fireblocks launches Agentic Payments Suite for AI agents]].
- **AI-fraud framing already in the DB:** [[Ecommpay highlights AI-driven fraud threats]], [[This Week in Fintech Plaid report on AI-driven fraud prevention]], [[This Week in Fintech ACI Worldwide on AI fraud prevention]].

## [3] State of play — agentic-commerce / agent-auth standards

The industry's structural answer to "unauthorized agents" is **cryptographic agent identity + scoped mandates**, and it is multi-protocol:

- **Google AP2 (Agent Payments Protocol):** announced 2025-09-16 with 60+ partners (Mastercard, PayPal, Coinbase, Amex, Salesforce). Uses three signed **Mandates** — Intent, Cart, Payment — as **W3C Verifiable Credentials**; settlement-agnostic (cards, bank transfer, stablecoins). **v0.2 added "Human Not Present"** flows for autonomous agents. **Donated to the FIDO Alliance (May 2026)** for community governance.
- **Visa Trusted Agent Protocol (TAP)** within **Visa Intelligent Commerce (VIC):** RFC-9421 HTTP Message Signatures so merchants can recognize trusted agents without rewriting checkout.
- **Mastercard Agent Pay:** Mastercard's agentic payments platform (integrating into PayPal's wallet); Mastercard's CDO said (Jan 2026) it participates in **all** major protocols (UCP, AP2, A2A, ACP) — i.e., the market expects multi-protocol fragmentation.

Takeaway: commerce rails are building agent-auth standards fast, but **bank account access** (the Axios story) has **no equivalent standard** yet — banks are defaulting to blunt blocking + read-only. That gap is the real news.

## [4] So what / why it matters

The essay correctly bundles three verified signals: (a) banks can't distinguish agent traffic and are reflexively blocking it; (b) offensive AI has collapsed the cost of mass intrusion (~$5K / 100 sites); (c) defensive AI is simultaneously surfacing huge legacy-code debt (BofA 23k+ findings). The structural tension is real: **agent-auth standards exist for commerce (AP2/TAP/Agent Pay) but not for bank-account access**, so banks' only lever today is credential-sharing bans + read-only — which collides with consumer demand for agent banking. Weaknesses: the quantum paragraph is unsourced filler, and the "unauthorized actions" framing is slightly stronger than Axios supports (no confirmed losses). Solid, timely roundup; not a standalone scoop.

### Red-team / challenge

3. **Which banks actually blocked vs. are merely restricting?** Confirmed: Morgan Stanley blocking; JPMorgan, BofA, Citi restricting. Morgan Stanley's spokesperson said it has *not* observed "rogue AI agents." So on-record denials coexist with the anonymous-sourced alarm.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This commentary sits at the intersection of two markets: AI fraud/financial-crime software and the nascent agentic-commerce authentication stack. Sizing on the fraud-software side is fragmented and vendor-sponsored, so treat figures as directional: AML software is put at ~$4.0bn in 2026 growing to ~$9.8bn by 2032 (~16% CAGR) per imarcgroup/360researchreports-type secondary reports (via web, as of 2026-10) — not an auditable TAM, flag as `(analysis)`. The broader "fraud detection" bucket is quoted much higher (~$52bn 2026 by coherentmarketinsights) but that conflates software, services and hardware — "no data" on a clean software TAM. Structure: fragmented and consolidating — incumbents (Visa, Mastercard, Experian) are buying their way in while dozens of venture-backed specialists compete. "Why now": the threat vector shifted from humans/scripted bots to autonomous agents that log in with the user's real credentials, which defeats classic bot-detection and KYC-at-onboarding — banks (JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, BofA, Citi, Wells Fargo) are restricting/blocking third-party agents and funneling them to read-only access via intermediaries like Plaid (Axios/CNBC/Reuters, 2026-10). The driver is regulatory/liability ambiguity (who owns a rogue-agent loss) more than a new TAM.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: detection precision/recall and false-positive rate, transaction/event volume screened, and — for the agentic layer — share of agents that can be cryptographically attributed. The commentary doesn't name a protagonist vendor; the actors are (a) banks defensively blocking, and (b) the network/standards layer. Two things are happening at once: fraud-ML vendors (Feedzai, Featurespace, Sardine, Alloy, Socure, SentiLink, Incognia, Resistant AI) sell detection; the card networks are building the "trust" rails that would make the Axios problem tractable. Recent moves: Visa launched the Trusted Agent Protocol with Cloudflare (Oct 2025) — crypto-signed agent identity checked against a Visa directory; Mastercard's Agent Pay registers/verifies agents and issues dynamic "agentic tokens" (2025); Google's AP2 and OpenAI/Stripe's ACP (Sept 2025) cover agent-initiated checkout. Positioning: the banks are *behind* — per Axios they "struggle to differentiate" authorized vs rogue agents and lack a parsing layer, which is precisely the gap the Visa/Mastercard directories target. Moat for the networks: a registry/directory is a network-effect asset (every merchant+issuer must trust the same list) — hard to displace once adopted `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** No deal/valuation in the commentary itself, so these are reference comps for the fraud-prevention vendors most exposed to the agentic-fraud thesis:
- Feedzai — $2bn post-money, $75m Series E, Oct 2025 (fintechfutures/fintech.global). Revenue undisclosed → EV/Rev "no data"; claims $9tn/yr screened as a scale proxy.
- Featurespace — acquired by Visa for ~$925m, announced Sept 2024, closed FY2025 (paymentsdive). A realized exit, so a useful floor for "fraud-ML vendor bought by a network."
- Socure — ~$5.2bn valuation as of Aug 2026 after a ~$156m round (fintechfutures); identity-verification leader, directly relevant to agent-vs-human disambiguation.
- Sardine — ~$660m post-money, Feb 2025 Series C + ~$25m extension May 2026 (Forge/multiples.vc).
- Internal comps: [[Resistant AI raises $25M Series B against financial crime]] (Oct 2025, explicitly positioned to defend "fintechs and AI agents"); [[This Week in Fintech Plaid report on AI-driven fraud prevention]] (Plaid is the read-only intermediary banks are steering agents toward); [[Ant International launches AI SHIELD risk toolkit]] (AI-security toolkit, Asia). All revenue-private → multiples not computable; "distribution not computed, qualitative comparison." Read: private fraud-ML names cluster $0.7–2bn; identity (Socure) richer at ~$5bn — in-line with a scarce, re-rating subsector, not obviously over-valued given the demand pull this news implies. `[UNSOURCED]` on all EV/Revenue.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Liability/legal vacuum (regulatory).** No settled answer on who eats a loss when a user-permissioned agent "goes rogue" (PBS/Reuters threads); until that clears, banks over-block, which caps the whole agentic-commerce opportunity and the vendors levered to it. Second-order: standards fragment (Visa vs Mastercard vs Google vs OpenAI) with no single mandatory directory.
2. **Credential-based bypass defeats existing stacks.** Agents using the user's real login defeat onboarding-KYC and bot-detection — the incumbent fraud-ML install base may be partly obsolete against this vector, risking a mid-cycle re-tooling spend (opportunity for new entrants, impairment risk for legacy).
3. **Disintermediation of banks by the network layer.** If Visa/Mastercard own the agent-trust directory, banks become price-takers on the authentication standard and the economic value (and data) of agent-attribution accrues to the networks, not the deposit-holders — the layer that captures the margin is not the one holding the risk.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a catalyst for a demand-pull re-rating of agent-aware fraud/identity vendors and for the card networks' "agent directory" land-grab, not a one-off scare. Falsifiable thesis: within ~2–3 quarters, bank "block" postures converge on a single interoperable agent-authentication standard routed through read-only intermediaries (Plaid-style) plus one network directory. Trigger to watch: a regulator (OCC/CFPB) or a named bank publishing an agent-liability/authorization framework; what breaks the thesis — networks' directories fail to achieve cross-issuer adoption and banks keep blanket-blocking, freezing the market.
