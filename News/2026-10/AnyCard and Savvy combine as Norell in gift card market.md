LONDON--(BUSINESS WIRE)--AnyCard, Lifestyle, and Savvy today announced their agreement to come together as Norell. The three complementary businesses span multi-brand and global gift card processing, payment, and reward and recognition programmes, with operations in the UK, Europe and North America. The Group is backed by Codex Capital and existing investors from the founding businesses.

The strategic agreement enables each business to offer broader expertise, capability, and reach to customers, alongside established technology, processing and distribution partners. AnyCard, Lifestyle and Savvy will operate under the parent Norell group, maintaining each brand’s established identity, specialist capabilities, market focus, and talent.

As one of the UK’s early pioneers of multi-brand gift cards, Lifestyle enables consumers and corporate customers to give and redeem gifts, rewards and experiences at more than 750 brand partners. AnyCard and Savvy contribute complementary capabilities spanning advanced processing, international retail distribution, and product innovation. Together, they strengthen the range of solutions available to customers whilst continuing to deepen and build out their partnerships across international markets.

Collectively, the businesses serve more than 2,000 corporate clients, in partnership with over 1,300 brands. To date, the businesses have processed over US$8bn in card value, across more than 45 countries, and supported through 33 currencies. The agreement comes in the context of a global gift card market, including corporate rewards and incentives, valued at $1.5trn in 2026 and projected to reach $2.2trn by 2034 1.

New investment from Codex Capital will accelerate each business’s strategy and ability to serve partners and customers through enhanced technology, content, and service capabilities, across international markets.

**Alex Barseghian, Group CEO, Norell**

_“Customers want simpler access to a wide range of products and services, at home and abroad, in a market that remains fragmented, across branded payments and rewards and recognition. This agreement puts the scale, investment and reach of the group behind each of our brands, to serve customers and partners even better. This structure helps brands, retailers and businesses connect with consumers and employees through multi brand cards and stored value solutions. With Codex Capital's backing, alongside existing investors, founders and talented colleagues, Norell will help each business accelerate progress and bring more products, rewards and technology to our customers and partners.”_

**Jamie Lawson-Brown, Codex Capital**

_"Norell exemplifies the hallmarks we look for in our investments: a proven business model, an experienced and specialist management team, and exposure to large, growing markets in the UK and internationally. The stored value payments market is evolving rapidly, with gift cards increasingly used beyond traditional gifting as businesses seek to reward and build loyalty among employees and customers. This creates multiple avenues for growing the market, which Norell has the breadth of capabilities to serve. We are delighted to have worked directly with its owners and management team to secure the transaction and we look forward to continuing to build our partnership.”_

**NOTES TO EDITORS**

**About:**

**Norell**

Norell is a global branded payments, gift card and rewards group, bringing together the complementary capabilities of AnyCard, Lifestyle and Savvy. The individual companies help brands, retailers and businesses connect with consumers and employees through multi-brand gift cards, gift card processing, stored-value solutions, and employee reward and recognition programmes.

Norell works with more than 2,000 corporate clients and 1,300 brands, with products available online and across more than 13,000 merchant locations. Headquartered in the United Kingdom, Norell operates across North America and Europe, supporting clients in more than 45 countries and 33 currencies.

**AnyCard**

AnyCard is a Canadian gift card technology company connecting merchants, organizations and consumers across North America. Through its Gift Card Solutions Platform and Multi-Brand Gift Cards, AnyCard helps businesses launch and manage gift card programs and deliver flexible gifting, incentive, reward, engagement and retention initiatives. The company works with more than 550 brands across North America, distributes its products through more than 8,000 retail and B2B locations across Canada, and powers more than 1,000 gift card programs. AnyCard ranked No. 6 on The Globe and Mail’s Canada’s Top Growing Companies list for 2026.

**Lifestyle**

Since 2018, the Lifestyle Gift Card has grown to become a leading multi-retailer gift card across the UK and Ireland, serving the B2B, D2C and B2C markets. With millions of redemptions every year and hundreds of thousands of customers, Lifestyle has helped reshape the stored value and employee rewards markets, delivering greater choice, flexibility and value for businesses, consumers and employees alike. Offering access to a huge range of leading brands and experiences, Lifestyle combines choice with simplicity, creating a compelling gifting and rewards solution for almost every occasion. With a strong proposition across multiple channels, Lifestyle is well positioned to continue its rapid growth and further strengthen its position in the UK and Irish gifting and rewards markets.

**Savvy**

Savvy is a highly focused processor that powers brilliant gift card programmes for its brands through the advanced capabilities of Elevate, its globally available Azure cloud-based processing platform. Working in collaboration with partners and as an internationally recognised processor in the gift card industry, Savvy is perfectly positioned to support new international partners and brands with advanced processing and analytics expertise.

**Codex Capital**

Codex Capital is a London-based independent sponsor, founded in 2014. Its focus is on investing in proven, profitable, and growing UK-headquartered mid-market businesses.

# Context-enrichment: AnyCard and Savvy combine as Norell in gift card market
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 14–15 Oct 2026 three gift-card/rewards businesses — **AnyCard** (Toronto; Canadian gift-card tech, founded 2016, bought in 2021 by Alex Barseghian & Alex Davidkhanian), **Lifestyle** (London; UK multi-brand gift card, B Corp, launched 2018) and **Savvy** (Dublin; a processor running an Azure-based platform called "Elevate") — agreed to combine under a new UK-HQ parent, **Norell**. Backer: **Codex Capital**, a London independent sponsor (founded 2014) focused on profitable UK mid-market firms, alongside each business's existing investors. Group CEO: Alex Barseghian.
- **De-PR'd reality: this is a PE roll-up / buy-and-build, not a product launch or a true "merger of equals".** The brands keep their own names, teams and market focus; Norell is a holdco over three still-separate businesses. Codex is an "independent sponsor" (deal-by-deal capital, not a committed fund), consistent with a modest consolidation play, not a mega-deal.
- Hard numbers given: **combined revenue >£100m**; **>2,000 corporate clients**; **>1,300 brands**; **>US$8bn card value processed to date** (cumulative, not annual); **45+ countries, 33 currencies, 13,000+ merchant locations**. **Deal value / equity cheque / ownership split were NOT disclosed** — a notable omission that makes the economics unverifiable.
- The PR anchors to a **"US$1.5trn gift card, reward and incentive market"** (sourced to Fortune Business Insights, Sep 2026, growing to $2.2trn by 2034). **Why this framing:** a $100m-revenue group = ~0.007% of that TAM, so the giant-market anchor is doing rhetorical work to make a small roll-up sound strategically large. Other research houses peg 2026 "gift/incentive card" nearer **$600–825bn**, so the $1.5trn figure is at the high end of a very wide (and methodology-dependent) range.

## [1] Competitors / peers
The branded-payments / gift-card-processing / rewards space is already consolidated around scale incumbents:
- **Blackhawk Network** and **InComm Payments** — the two dominant US/global gift-card distributors and processors; both dwarf Norell.
- **Tillo**, **Runa** (ex-WeGift), **Tango Card**, **Reloadly**, **QwikCilver** — the newer API/digital-rewards-infrastructure cohort growing fastest in corporate incentives.
- Internal corpus peers in rewards/gifting: [[Kard raises $15M for rewards infrastructure]] (rewards-as-infra, US), [[Paramotor launches digital gifting platform Yayyy.shop]] (digital gifting, India), [[bunq partners with Ascenda for premium rewards]], [[Revolut launches RevPoints rewards in Australia]].
- **Position: catching up / sub-scale.** Norell's ~£100m revenue and $8bn *cumulative* processed value is small vs Blackhawk/InComm (billions in annual load) and the fast API players. **Why the land lies this way (analysis):** gift-card value accrues to those who own either (a) retail distribution shelf space (Blackhawk/InComm) or (b) a clean developer API into corporate HR/loyalty stacks (Runa/Tillo). Norell is a blend of mid-tier regional distribution + a processor, i.e. neither clear leader — hence the consolidation logic: bolt pieces together to reach minimum efficient scale.

## [2] Company history / fit
- AnyCard: founded 2016 as an SMB gift-card processor; acquired 2021 by Barseghian/Davidkhanian; #6 on The Globe and Mail's Canada's Top Growing Companies 2026 — the growth story that likely made it the "platform" asset in the roll-up.
- Lifestyle: UK multi-brand card since 2018, 750+ brand partners, B2B/D2C/B2C.
- Savvy: a processor (the "picks-and-shovels" leg) with an Azure platform.
- **Fit (analysis):** the three cover complementary gaps — distribution (Lifestyle UK), regional tech + retail reach (AnyCard North America), processing (Savvy). That is a coherent buy-and-build thesis. **Why structured this way:** an independent sponsor assembling a transatlantic group to sell/IPO later at a scaled multiple; keeping brands separate preserves customer relationships while back-office/processing is centralized on Savvy's Elevate platform (where the real cost synergy would sit — though none is quantified).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** No new product, rail or capability is announced — it is a corporate-structure event. The "innovation/content/technology" language is aspirational ("new investment will accelerate…"), not a shipped feature.
- **Traction is real but modest and mostly cumulative:** 2,000+ corporate clients and $8bn processed *to date* are genuine, but $8bn cumulative across three firms and multiple years is small in gift-card terms.
- **Who captures the margin (analysis):** gift-card economics = processing fees + breakage + float + corporate-incentive markups. The durable margin sits with whoever owns the corporate relationship and the processing ledger. Norell centralizing on Savvy's processing is the only lever here that could lift margin; distribution-only legs are commoditized and squeezed by Blackhawk/InComm on one side and API players (Runa/Tillo) on the other. **Value-add verdict: real but incremental** — scale and shared tech, not a moat.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Codex's stated thesis: gift cards moving "beyond gifting" into employee/customer reward & loyalty — a genuine secular tailwind cited across market reports (CAGRs 5–15% depending on source). Next steps implied: central tech investment, international cross-sell, likely further bolt-on acquisitions, and eventual exit.
- **Risks / silences:** no deal size, no debt structure, no synergy targets, no combined *annual* load; "independent sponsor" implies the capital base may be thinner than a traditional PE fund. Integration risk across three countries/currencies/platforms is real. Competition from scaled incumbents and faster API-native rivals caps upside.
- **Second-order (analysis):** the interesting question is not "is the gift-card market big" (it is, but fragmented) but **"can a sub-scale roll-up out-run both the distribution giants and the API disruptors before its float/breakage economics get commoditized?"** On current disclosure, unproven.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Norell combines AnyCard, Lifestyle and Savvy into a UK-headquartered branded-payments / gift-card / rewards-and-recognition group, backed by independent sponsor Codex Capital (announced 2026-10-15). The PR frames the opportunity as a "US$1.5trn gift card, reward and incentive market in 2026, projected to reach $2.2trn by 2034" (per Fortune Business Insights, "Gift Card Market", 14 Sep 2026, via the Business Wire release). Treat that TAM with caution: it is the broadest cut (gift cards + corporate rewards + incentives), and third-party estimates for the *gift-card* market alone vary widely for 2026 — ~$825bn (Persistence), ~$1.49trn (Fortune Business Insights), ~$1.6–1.7trn (Straits / Research and Markets) — while Klarna/BHN and On Me both size only the *US* gift-card market at ~$447–450bn ([[Klarna deepens Blackhawk Network gift-card partnership]], [[On Me raises $6M seed for digital gift card market]]). So the headline figure is a top-of-funnel marketing TAM, not Norell's addressable revenue. (analysis) **Structure:** highly fragmented — the CEO himself calls the market "fragmented, across branded payments and rewards and recognition"; value sits in two layers, distribution/aggregation (multi-brand card programmes, 1,300+ brand relationships) and processing (Savvy's Azure-based Elevate platform). Barriers are moderate: brand/merchant network relationships and processing scale, not capital or heavy regulation. **Why now:** the thesis (per Codex) is gift cards shifting "beyond traditional gifting" into employee/customer loyalty and corporate incentives — a secular B2B expansion of a historically consumer product; consolidation to add scale/geographic reach (UK+Europe+North America, 45 countries, 33 currencies) is the response.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: total card value loaded/processed (Norell cites >US$8bn processed to date, cumulative — not annual), number of brand partners (1,300+) and corporate clients (2,000+), and combined revenue (>£100m). Take rate / margin on processed value is not disclosed [UNSOURCED]. **Key players & basis of competition:** the segment leader is Blackhawk Network (BHN) — a global branded-payment incumbent whose US gift-card distribution reach dwarfs Norell (hundreds of brands, Giftcards.com, see [[Klarna deepens Blackhawk Network gift-card partnership]]); other comparables are Tillo (UK-based "global gift card platform", 3,000+ brands via plug-and-play API, [[Sientia partners with Tillo on payment-linked rewards]]), and regional/emerging players such as Gyftr/Vouchagram in India ([[LKP Finance acquires Gyftr stake, to surrender NBFC licence]]) and On Me in US consumer gifting ([[On Me raises $6M seed for digital gift card market]]). Competition is on brand network breadth, distribution footprint and processing capability rather than price. **Protagonist position:** mid-market consolidator / catching-up — at >£100m combined revenue and >$8bn cumulative card value it is a credible European-anchored challenger but well below BHN/Tillo scale in the key US market (AnyCard is Canada-focused; the "US$1.5trn" framing is aspirational, not a reflection of current US share). Moat (analysis): modest — brand-relationship network effects and Savvy's proprietary processing stack provide some switching cost, but no evident structural lock-in.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation, EV or enterprise multiple was disclosed for the Norell deal — Codex is an independent sponsor and the transaction terms/post-money are undisclosed, so EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA and P/E = **no data**. The only hard figure is combined revenue >£100m (~$134m at ~1.34 GBP/USD). Distribution not computed (no comparable disclosed multiples). Internal comps — all private rounds / stakes, none with a published revenue multiple:
- [[On Me raises $6M seed for digital gift card market]] — $6m seed (NFX-led, Dec 2025); early-stage, >$2.5m gifts facilitated in year 1 → not scale-comparable to Norell.
- [[Klarna deepens Blackhawk Network gift-card partnership]] — BHN is the scale benchmark; BHN revenue last publicly ~>$1bn (2017, stale) and valuation redacted [UNSOURCED], so no clean multiple.
- [[Sientia partners with Tillo on payment-linked rewards]] — Tillo, closest UK model comp, private, no disclosed valuation.
- [[LKP Finance acquires Gyftr stake, to surrender NBFC licence]] — LKP holds 22% of Mufin Pay (owner of Gyftr); stake value undisclosed.
Verdict: cannot assess rich/cheap — valuation undisclosed; only revenue scale ($134m-equivalent) is known.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Processing/take-rate opacity + "silent economics."** The PR headlines TAM, card value and client counts but discloses no take rate, margin, EBITDA or deal valuation — classic de-PR gap; "£100m revenue" on a reward/gift-card model could carry very different margins across the three units, and the market-size headline inflates perceived opportunity well beyond Norell's addressable share. Why: a reader can't size economics or the deal's value-for-money.
2. **US-scale gap vs BHN / disintermediation.** Norell is UK/Canada-anchored but pitches a "US$1.5trn" opportunity dominated in the US by Blackhawk Network and by wallet-native/digital-first entrants (On Me explicitly targets the "two controlling players" with decades-old plastic infrastructure). Why: distribution is the moat in this sector, and Norell's US distribution is thin — the growth thesis depends on winning a market where incumbents and digital challengers are both entrenched.
3. **Integration / "agreement" vs "live."** This is an agreement to combine three businesses that keep separate brands, teams and platforms (Savvy's Elevate processing, Lifestyle's UK multi-retailer card, AnyCard's Canadian platform) — the synergies (shared distribution, cross-sell, unified processing) are announced, not realised. Why: multi-brand/multi-geo integration under a sponsor's hold period is execution-heavy; failure to consolidate the processing stack erodes the scale rationale.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This reads as sub-scale consolidation in a fragmented gift-card/branded-payments market — a sponsor rolling up three complementary mid-market assets to build a sellable European-anchored challenger, not a re-rating event. Falsifiable thesis: if the sector's "beyond gifting" / corporate-incentive shift is real, Norell's next 18 months should show the three brands migrating onto Savvy's single processing platform and winning cross-border corporate reward mandates. Trigger/what would break it: watch for a disclosed valuation or follow-on Codex capital, and for US distribution wins; absence of processing consolidation (brands continuing to run separate stacks) would signal the "group" is a holding label, not an integrated platform.
