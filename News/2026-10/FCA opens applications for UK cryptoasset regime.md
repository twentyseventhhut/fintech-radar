# FCA opens applications for UK cryptoasset regime

> [!info] 2026-10-08 · 1 упоминаний · 1 источника(ов) с текстом
> Каналы: This Week in Fintech

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[This Week in Fintech] The FCAopened applications for the UK's new cryptoasset regime.

## Первоисточники

### fca.org.uk
<https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-opens-gateway-regulated-crypto>
*402 слов · direct*

From today, crypto firms can apply for authorisation from the FCA, marking a landmark moment as the UK takes a step closer towards becoming one of the most trusted places in the world to build and invest in cryptoasset businesses.
For the first time, crypto firms operating in the UK will be brought into full FCA regulation, with clear standards covering consumer protection, safeguarding, market integrity and financial resilience. This is a significant milestone, not just for the industry, but it signals to those who invest in crypto that the UK is serious in giving them protections they have never had before. It also gives the sector clarity and legitimacy that comes with a proper regulatory framework.
Firms that intend to continue operating in the UK should apply by 28 February 2027, ahead of the new regime coming into force on 25 October 2027.
Authorisation is not automatic. Firms will need to clearly demonstrate that they meet the FCA’s requirements, and those that cannot show the necessary standards will not be authorised to operate in the UK market. This reflects the government and FCA’s determination that the UK’s crypto sector should be defined by integrity and trust, along with growth and innovation.
Dominic Cashman, director of authorisation at the FCA said: 'The UK’s new crypto regime will give consumers greater protections and firms a clear framework to operate in. Firms can now apply for authorisation and start preparing for regulation.'
The FCA is supporting firms to prepare for authorisation including through pre-application discussions and webinars.
Notes to editors
In June 2026, the FCA published its final crypto rules and guidance.   
The FCA expects to determine applications submitted during the application period before the new regime comes into force. 
Existing firms that apply during the application period can continue providing cryptoasset services, including taking on new business, while their application is being assessed if a decision has not been made before the new regime comes into force. 
Firms are assessed against the FCA's standards on consumer protection, safeguarding of customer assets, market integrity and financial resilience. Firms that do not meet these standards will not be authorised and will not be able to continue offering regulated cryptoasset services in the UK. 
Firms can request a pre-application support meeting before applying for authorisation.  
The FCA has hosted webinars to help firms understand its rules and prepare for authorisation which are available on demand .

# Context-enrichment: FCA opens applications for UK cryptoasset regime
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-08 the FCA press-released that crypto firms can "from today" apply for authorisation under the UK's new cryptoasset regime — the authorisation **gateway is live**. Hard facts: apply by **28 Feb 2027**; regime commences **25 Oct 2027**; final rules were published June 2026. Firms are assessed on consumer protection, safeguarding, market integrity, financial resilience. Existing firms that apply in-window may keep operating (incl. new business) while assessed if no decision lands before commencement. Authorisation is explicitly "not automatic."

**What this actually is (de-PR'd):** this is the **opening of a pre-announced window**, not a new policy decision. The window dates (30 Sep 2026 → 28 Feb 2027) and the 25 Oct 2027 commencement were already confirmed in April/May 2026 ([[FCA confirms UK cryptoasset authorisation window]], [[FCA's new cryptoasset regime expected to take effect October 2027]]); the rulebook was finalised in June/July 2026 ([[FCA completes crypto roadmap with new rules]]). So the only genuinely new fact vs prior notes is "the door is now physically open and applications are being accepted." Note the one-week discrepancy: most external coverage dates the gateway opening to **30 Sep 2026**; the primary FCA release the note is built on is dated 2026-10-08 and says "from today." Likely the note captures a re-publication/re-circulation of the opening a week after the gateway technically opened (analysis) — which itself leans toward a stale, "re-report" reading.

**Why framed this way:** the FCA leads with "most trusted place in the world" and "legitimacy" language because the political goal is to attract crypto business post-Brexit while the regulator's own history (AML-registration approval rates were very low) signals it intends to **thin the field**. The "not automatic" line is the real message to firms.

## [1] Competitors / peers (regulatory regimes)
- **EU MiCA** — live since 2023 (phased, stablecoin titles from mid-2024, CASP from end-2024). The UK regime trails MiCA by ~3+ years on the clock. Mechanism delta: UK folds crypto into existing **FSMA + FCA Handbook** (SM&CR, conduct-of-business) rather than MiCA's bespoke standalone rulebook; UK explicitly captures **lending/borrowing and staking**, which MiCA largely left out. (analysis) The UK is betting that "same rules as banks/brokers" buys institutional trust; the cost is complexity and later go-live.
- **US** — moved to stablecoin legislation (GENIUS-style) and lighter-touch crypto framing in 2025–26; faster but narrower.
- **UK's own prior steps:** ETN retail ban lifted ([[FCA lifts retail ban on crypto ETNs]]), stablecoin sprint ([[FCA opens applications for stablecoin sprint]]), perimeter guidance ([[FCA publishes approach to UK cryptoasset regulatory regime]]). Position: the UK is **catching up, not leading** — this gateway is the operational step that finally makes the regime real.

## [2] Company/regulator history & fit
Clean, logical continuation of a multi-year roadmap visible in the corpus: consultation on applying Handbook rules (Sep 2025) → approach/perimeter guidance (Jan 2026) → window confirmation (Apr 2026) → final rules (Jun–Jul 2026) → **gateway opens (Sep/Oct 2026)** → commencement (Oct 2027). The FCA also hired crypto/payments leadership (Standard Chartered's Bajaj, Aug 2025) to staff this. The gateway opening fits exactly where expected; no surprise, no new direction.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Low novelty. Everything material (scope, dates, standards, the 1% stablecoin capital concession) was already public. **Scope = nine regulated activities:** issuing qualifying UK stablecoins; safeguarding; arranging safeguarding; operating a trading platform; dealing as principal; dealing as agent; arranging deals; making arrangements with a view to transactions; and **qualifying staking**. Traction to watch (not yet knowable): how many of the ~set of UK-operating firms actually apply, and the eventual **approval rate** — given the low historic AML-registration pass rate, the real story will be rejections/withdrawals, not the opening. No adoption numbers exist yet because applications only just opened.

## [4] What's next / sentiment
Next 4 months: application rush before 28 Feb 2027; FCA webinars and pre-application meetings; expect consolidation as marginal firms decide the UK compliance cost isn't worth it. The second-order effect: because transitional relief lets in-window applicants keep operating until a decision, the **real cliff-edge is whenever the FCA starts rejecting**, which could fragment the market well before Oct 2027. Sentiment is cautiously positive (clarity welcomed) but tempered by (a) the ~4-year lag behind MiCA, (b) uncertainty over how strict authorisation will be. Risk: firms that miss the 28 Feb window or fail authorisation must exit the UK.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a *regulatory-gateway* event, not a company deal — no valuation/round to anchor on. The FCA opened the authorisation gateway for UK cryptoasset firms (per FCA press release, this item dated 2026-10-08; secondary coverage dates the gateway opening to 30 Sep 2026). Timeline is the load-bearing fact: apply by **28 Feb 2027**, regime live **25 Oct 2027**, final rules published **June 2026** (FCA). Market structure of the *addressable base*: fragmented and largely unregulated today — the existing UK footprint is only the AML/MLR register, and sources conflict on its size (~42 firms per regulatorycounsel.co.uk, ~51 approved per IG/sumsub, ">60" per other coverage; call it **~40-60 MLR-registered firms** `[data quality: sources disagree]`). Demand side: ~**8% of UK adults (~4.5m) hold crypto** (per IG/sumsub citing FCA, as of 2026) — a real consumer base, which is *why now*: Westminster is converting a promotional-rules-only regime into full FSMA authorisation covering custody, trading, dealing, staking and stablecoin issuance. Barrier to entry rises sharply (capital, safeguarding, financial-resilience tests) — the gate is the moat the regime creates.

**Competitive landscape.** The "players" here are jurisdictions competing for crypto firms, and the firms choosing where to domicile. KPIs for a licensing regime: # authorisations granted, approval/rejection rate, time-to-decision, passporting reach. Benchmarks:
- **EU / MiCA** — live since Dec 2024; **~386 CASPs authorised as of 7 Oct 2026** (crypto-native ~60% / TradFi ~40%), led by Germany (96), France (36), Netherlands (29) (per outrun.at MiCAR dashboard). Decisive edge: **passporting** across 27 EEA states from one licence.
- **UK / FCA** — no passporting into the EU, separate authorisation; historically *harsh*: under the legacy MLR register **>85% of applications rejected, 12-18 month decisions** (per regulatorycounsel.co.uk / statebay). That track record is the competitive liability.
- **US / GENIUS Act** — narrower: payment-stablecoins only (signed Jul 2025), not a full CASP framework (per Morgan Lewis / SSGA).
Likely applicants named across coverage: Coinbase (CB Payments Ltd), Kraken (Payward Ltd), Bitstamp UK, Revolut (EMI-authorised) — all currently on the MLR/e-money register, none auto-converting. **UK's position: catching up, not leading** — it arrives ~3 years after MiCA, with no passporting and a punitive approval history; its pitch is "trust/legitimacy," not speed or reach `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation event → **EV/Revenue, P/E etc. not applicable** (regulator, not issuer). Comparable *regime/licensing* precedents in-base (closest analogues — other authorisation gateways and MiCA grants):
- [[Revolut secures MiCA license from Cyprus regulator]] — incumbent neobank taking the EU passporting route.
- [[Safello granted MiCA authorization in Sweden]] · [[Relai secures MiCA license to expand crypto access in Europe]] — crypto-natives using MiCA.
- [[BitGo receives BaFin licence for crypto trading in Europe]] — custody player picking an EU home state.
- [[HKMA receives 36 stablecoin licence applications]] — a comparable "gateway opens, measure the funnel" datapoint (36 applications → tiny approval set).
- [[FCA lifts retail ban on crypto ETNs]] · [[FCA approves crypto ETPs from 21Shares, Bitwise and WisdomTree]] — same regulator's recent liberalising arc.
Multiples not computed — distribution N/A for a regulatory event; the quantitative comp that matters is the **funnel ratio** (applications vs authorisations), and the FCA's historical ~85% rejection rate is the only hard number — a signal the gateway may be narrow in practice.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Approval-funnel / execution risk.** FCA's legacy register rejected >85% and ran 12-18 months per decision. If that rate persists against a Feb-2027 deadline, many firms miss the window → forced exit or offshore migration. Second-order: UK liquidity/users route to MiCA-passported or offshore venues.
2. **No EU passporting → structural disadvantage.** A UK licence buys one market; a MiCA licence buys 27. Marginal firms rationally pick the EU, so the UK regime risks attracting fewer/smaller applicants than its consumer base (4.5m holders) would justify.
3. **Continuity ambiguity / de-PR flag.** The PR frames trust and growth but is silent on capital-requirement calibration and on what happens to the large tail of MLR firms that don't convert — i.e. the attrition is unquantified. "Firms can keep operating while assessed" softens the cliff but doesn't remove rejection risk.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is the start of a UK crypto *consolidation/selection* event, not an expansion one: the authorisation bar culls the long tail and concentrates regulated activity in a few well-capitalised names (global exchanges + neobanks). **Falsifiable thesis:** by the 28 Feb 2027 deadline the UK authorises materially *fewer* firms than MiCA's ~386, confirming the EU as the EEA gateway of record. **Trigger/what breaks it:** FCA publishing a fast-track or markedly higher approval rate (vs the 85%-reject history), or a UK-EU equivalence/passporting deal — either would flip the UK from laggard to credible competitor.
