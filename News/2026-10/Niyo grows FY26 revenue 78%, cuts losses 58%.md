 Follow Us
 Travel fintech platform Niyo’s income grew 78% year-on-year to Rs 178 crore in FY26, compared with Rs 100 crore in FY25, according to the company’s press release. 
 The company also cut its losses by 58% to Rs 33 crore in FY26 from Rs 78 crore a year earlier. Niyo said the improvement came from better efficiency, including the use of AI in customer service, operations, marketing and product development. 
 Niyo’s operating expenses rose 20% to Rs 201 crore in FY26 from Rs 168 crore in FY25. Employee expenses increased 17% to Rs 92 crore from Rs 79 crore. According to Niyo, the increase in employee costs was mainly due to the expansion of Kanji Forex , which operates under Niyo’s own AD-II licence.  
 Despite the higher spending, Niyo’s EBITDA loss nearly halved to Rs 32 crore in FY26 from Rs 77 crore in FY25, the press release added. 
 Niyo is also expanding beyond its core travel business. The company said its revenue is now coming from forex cash, remittances, flights, hotels, experiences, international eSIMs and insurance, among other services. It is also selling more forex and travel products to its existing customers. 
 The company has set up Finnew Global Solutions IFSC in GIFT City and received approval to operate as a Payment Service Provider under IFSCA rules. 
 Niyo CFO Gourav Kumar said the company is now focused on getting more value from its existing customers, improving efficiency and moving towards profitability. Niyo expects to maintain over 50% annual growth in the coming years and become EBITDA positive. 
 Niyo has raised over $160 million in funding to date, including a $100 million Series C round in 2022. Its investors include Accel, Lightrock, Prime Ventures, Horizon, Tencent, and others.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What Niyo is.** Niyo is an India-based "travel/global banking" fintech (founded 2015/2016), historically a neobank issuing zero-forex-markup travel/forex cards in partnership with sponsor banks (SBM, DCB), plus payroll/savings accounts for salaried and blue-collar workers. It has pivoted its narrative from "consumer neobank" to a cross-border/travel money platform: forex cash, outward remittances, flights, hotels, experiences, international eSIMs and travel insurance, cross-selling to an existing base reported at ~2M+ customers across ~20 states (per its 2025 Kanji Forex disclosure). Legal parent: Finnew Solutions.

**The FY26 print (headline, from the press release via entrackr).** Total income +78% YoY to ₹178cr (~$18.5M) from ₹100cr in FY25; net loss cut 58% to ₹33cr (~$3.5M) from ₹78cr; EBITDA loss nearly halved to ₹32cr from ₹77cr. Opex +20% to ₹201cr; employee cost +17% to ₹92cr (driven by the Kanji Forex expansion under Niyo's own AD-II FX licence). Management attributes the efficiency gains partly to AI in support/ops/marketing/product. CFO Gourav Kumar: now focused on monetising the existing base, targeting >50% annual growth and EBITDA-positive. Note a source discrepancy: Inc42 reports FY26 "operating revenue" +80% to ₹158cr while entrackr/press release cites "income" +78% to ₹178cr — the ₹20cr gap is almost certainly operating revenue vs total income (incl. other/interest income); figures are not directly contradictory but headline framing differs.

**Strategic read.** This is a classic "efficiency-led narrowing to break-even" story for a mid-scale Indian consumer fintech that never reached neobank escape velocity. Two structural moves de-risk it: (1) the Aug-2025 Kanji Forex acquisition (1935-vintage FX house) gave Niyo an owned AD Category-II licence and offline FX distribution, reducing dependence on sponsor-bank BaaS economics — see [[Niyo acquires Kanji Forex and names new forex CEO]]; (2) a new Finnew Global Solutions IFSC entity in GIFT City with IFSCA Payment Service Provider approval, positioning for regulated cross-border/remittance rails. Revenue quality is shifting from card-interchange/float toward transaction-fee travel services and FX spread.

**Competitive set (internal comps).** Niyo sits at the intersection of India's travel-card and consumer-neobank cohorts, both under pressure:
- [[Scapia in talks with General Catalyst to raise $50-60 million]] — the closest direct comp; a co-branded travel credit card startup that grew revenue ~70% to ₹40.4cr and explicitly lists Niyo (alongside Uni Cards and Jupiter) as a competitor. Scapia is still raising growth equity; Niyo is instead optimising toward profitability on a much larger ₹178cr base.
- [[Neobank Fi pivots from B2C to B2B tech services amid layoffs]] — the cautionary counterexample: a well-funded Indian consumer neobank that abandoned B2C and laid off staff in Feb-2026. Underlines why Niyo's "existing-customer monetisation + travel vertical" focus is defensive positioning in a brutal B2C-neobank market.
- Broader Indian consumer-fintech cohort for context (Jupiter, Slice, Fi) — see e.g. [[Slice launches UPI credit card and first UPI bank branch]] and [[Jupiter and CSB Bank launch Edge+ RuPay credit card]]; most are chasing lending/credit-card monetisation because standalone neobanking economics in India are thin.

**Do NOT conflate:** [[Niyogin Fintech grows Q4 FY26 revenue to ₹72 crore]] is a SEPARATE, listed small-business lending company (Niyogin Fintech Ltd), unrelated to Niyo/Finnew despite the similar name.

**Funding/valuation history.** Niyo has raised ~$160M+ to date (the Aug-2025 note cited "$179M"), headlined by a $100M Series C in Feb-2022 led by Accel and Lightrock India (with Beams Fintech, Prime Venture Partners, JS Capital); other backers include Tencent and Horizon. A separate ~$30M tranche from Multiples Alternate Asset Management was reported in mid-2022. Valuation was not disclosed in the Series C. No new primary round is reported here — this is an operating-results story, not a funding event.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
Red-team questions (verify before amplifying):

1. **Revenue vs income gap.** Press release says income +78% to ₹178cr; Inc42 says operating revenue +80% to ₹158cr. Which base is the "real" growth number, and is the ₹178cr inflated by other/interest income? Headline growth could be overstated if non-operating income drove it.
2. **Self-reported, unaudited.** All figures come from a company press release, not filed MCA/audited accounts. Entrackr/Inc42 are relaying it. Have the audited FY26 financials been filed and do they reconcile?
3. **EBITDA loss vs net loss near-identical (₹32cr vs ₹33cr).** That implies negligible D&A and interest — plausible for an asset-light fintech, but worth confirming it isn't masking capitalised costs or one-offs.
4. **Opex +20% outpaced by income +78% — is this durable?** Much of the efficiency is attributed to "AI." How much of the loss reduction is structural (unit economics) vs one-time cost discipline that reverses as it scales travel bookings?
5. **Kanji Forex distortion.** Employee cost +17% is pinned on Kanji expansion. Does the acquisition inflate both revenue AND cost lines, making YoY comparability muddy (inorganic growth dressed as organic improvement)?
6. **Low absolute scale.** ₹178cr (~$18.5M) income after ~10 years and $160M+ raised is modest. Is 78% growth off a small base impressive, or a sign of a capped TAM in zero-markup travel cards?
7. **Margin pressure on the core product.** Zero-forex-markup cards are a loss-leader; RBI/regulatory tightening on forex markups and card BaaS (and TCS on outbound remittances) could compress the FX-spread revenue it's now leaning on.
8. **Sponsor-bank dependence.** Core cards still ride SBM/DCB. Any sponsor-bank BaaS clampdown (as seen with other Indian neobanks) is an existential single point of failure.
9. **"Path to profitability" is a forecast, not a fact.** CFO guides >50% growth and future EBITDA-positive — no date given. How many years of "narrowing losses" before actual profit, and is runway/cash sufficient without a new raise?
10. **GIFT City PSP approval — material or PR?** Does the Finnew Global IFSC PSP licence have real near-term revenue, or is it optionality being used to signal a growth story to investors?
11. **Competitive squeeze.** Scapia (raising $50-60M), Jupiter, Slice, Uni Cards all target overlapping travel/credit-card customers. Can Niyo hold share while cutting marketing spend to chase profitability?
12. **Stale/aging capital.** Last major raise was the 2022 Series C. Is the push to profitability genuine confidence or a forced response to inability to raise fresh growth equity at a flat/down round?
13. **Customer metric staleness.** "~2M customers / 20 states" is from the 2025 Kanji note; TechCrunch cited 4M in 2022. Did the base shrink or is the count definitionally inconsistent?
14. **Naming confusion risk.** Ensure no cross-wiring with Niyogin Fintech Ltd (separate listed SME-lender) in any downstream aggregation.

Importance: 3/5
(Mid-scale Indian consumer fintech with a credible efficiency-led turnaround toward break-even — a useful signal on the India travel-card/neobank cohort and the "AI cost-cutting to profitability" theme, but small absolute numbers, self-reported, and a non-event on funding/valuation keep it below high-priority.)

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Niyo sits in India's **outbound-travel forex / cross-border consumer-fintech** niche — distinct from the broader consumer-neobank pack. Market sizing is vendor-report-grade only: one India forex-cards estimate puts the market at ~$17.8bn (2025) growing to ~$106.8bn by 2032 at ~33% CAGR (per MetaStat Insight, via metastatinsight.com) — treat as indicative, not audited. More reliably, the regulatory tailwind is concrete: RBI issued the Draft Prepaid Payment Instruments Directions (22-Apr-2026) tightening PPI issuance/limits, and separately removed remittance limits for Form A2 online submissions (see [[RBI removes remittance limits for Form A2 online submissions]]) — easing outbound remittance friction that Niyo's AD-II forex book monetises. Structure: **fragmented** — legacy bank cards (HDFC/ICICI/Axis) + specialist forex houses (BookMyForex, Thomas Cook) + mobile-first challengers (Niyo, Scapia, Fi) — with value shifting from FX-markup spread toward zero-markup + ancillary travel attach (flights/hotels/eSIM/insurance). **Why now:** zero-markup is now table-stakes (Niyo Global, Scapia Federal, BookMyForex Wow all advertise ~0% FX markup, per happyfares.in, May-2026), so the margin has compressed out of the card and must be recovered via cross-sell — exactly Niyo's stated FY26 pivot.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: load volume / cross-border TPV, FX take-rate (collapsing toward zero on the card), cross-sell attach rate, cost-to-income. Players + basis of competition: **Scapia** (travel credit card + bookings, zero-markup, GC-backed) competes on rewards + travel OTA attach; **BookMyForex / Thomas Cook** on distribution + doorstep delivery; **Fi Money** has retreated — it wound down banking services and ended its Federal Bank tie-up (Mar-2026, see [[India neobank Fi winds down banking services]]) and earlier pivoted B2C→B2B — effectively vacating the consumer forex contest. Basis of competition is **distribution + travel-ecosystem attach + licence scope**, since FX markup is commoditised to zero. Niyo's distinctive move: it acquired 1935-vintage **Kanji Forex** (Aug-2025, see [[Niyo acquires Kanji Forex and names new forex CEO]]) to run FX cash/cards/remittance under its **own AD-II licence** — owning the licence rather than renting a partner bank's, which is a genuine differentiator vs rail-dependent neobanks. It also stood up Finnew Global Solutions IFSC in GIFT City (PSP approval under IFSCA). **Position: niche leader in outbound-travel forex, catching-up in breadth.** Moat (analysis): own AD-II licence + offline Kanji footprint = a licence/distribution moat most app-only peers lack; ~2M+ customers across 20 states (as of Aug-2025).

**Comps & multiples.** All private — valuations are last-round marks, not market caps; no EV/EBITDA computable (no public EV).
- **Niyo:** FY26 revenue ₹178cr (~$18.5M), net loss ₹33cr (~$3.5M), EBITDA loss ₹32cr (near-halved from ₹77cr); raised >$160M incl. a **$100M Series C (2022)** — no fresh FY26 round disclosed, so no current valuation mark (per entrackr.com). P/S not computable (last valuation undisclosed post-2022).
- **Scapia (closest external comp):** raised $63M May-2026, valuation **>$500M** (more than doubled in a year), ~250 employees (see [[Scapia raises $63 million for India travel payments]]). Travel-fintech, same outbound niche, but credit-card-led vs Niyo's forex/debit-prepaid-led. If one layered Niyo's ~$18.5M revenue against a hypothetical $500M mark it would be ~27x P/S — but Niyo has no such fresh mark, so this is **not a real multiple**, only a sizing of where a growth round might price (hypothesis).
- **Internal profitability benchmarks (same India consumer-fintech cohort):** [[Indian digital bank Slice swings to profit in Q4 FY26]] — FY26 rev ₹1,403cr (+132%), **net profit ₹48.4cr** (turned profitable, but via a bank licence, ~8x Niyo's revenue); [[FamApp turns profitable with INR 90-100cr revenue]] — FY25 rev ₹90–100cr, PBT ₹10–12cr (profitable at roughly half Niyo's revenue scale). Read-across: at ₹178cr revenue Niyo is **behind FamApp on the path to profit** (FamApp was already profitable at ~₹95cr), i.e. Niyo's ₹33cr loss at a larger top-line signals a heavier cost base (opex +20% to ₹201cr, employee +17% to ₹92cr from Kanji expansion). Median not computed — different business models (bank / teen-wallet / forex); **qualitative**: Niyo is scaling revenue fast (+78%) but is the laggard-to-breakeven of this cohort.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Margin compression on the core card.** Zero-markup is now universal (Niyo, Scapia, BookMyForex), so the FX spread that historically funded the card is gone — profitability now depends entirely on cross-sell (flights/hotels/eSIM/insurance/remittance). If attach rates disappoint, the "path to EBITDA-positive" (management guidance, de-PR'd: a target, not a result) slips. Why it matters: the whole FY26 loss-narrowing story rests on efficiency + cross-sell, not core-card economics.
2. **Regulatory exposure on two fronts.** Draft PPI Directions (Apr-2026) could tighten prepaid-card limits/features, and the AD-II/IFSCA forex-remittance book is directly RBI/IFSCA-governed — a licence-scope change hits revenue directly. Own-licence is a moat but also concentrates regulatory single-point risk.
3. **Single-use-case concentration + no fresh capital.** Revenue is heavily tethered to outbound international travel (cyclical, FX/visa/geopolitics-sensitive); last disclosed raise is the 2022 Series C, so a still-loss-making company is funding growth off an aging round — execution-to-breakeven must happen without obvious new runway (no FY26 round disclosed).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Not a re-rating event — a **self-funded grind toward profitability** by a niche leader while a weaker peer (Fi) exits the consumer-forex contest, leaving Niyo vs Scapia as the two credible mobile-first outbound-travel players. Falsifiable thesis (analysis): if Niyo hits the guided "EBITDA-positive + >50% growth" in FY27 while attach/remittance scales, it earns a Scapia-style >$500M growth round; it breaks if FY27 losses re-widen (cross-sell stalls) or a PPI/AD-II rule change clips the forex book. Watch: (a) any FY27 funding round + its valuation vs the stale 2022 mark; (b) Kanji/remittance revenue mix disclosure; (c) PPI Directions finalisation.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (headline read).** BEAT on the loss line, QUALITY-LIGHT on growth · total income ₹178.5 Cr (+78% YoY) · operating revenue ₹158 Cr (+80.1% YoY) · net loss ₹32.6 Cr (−58.1%) · no consensus (private co.) — read vs FY25 and vs mgmt's own prior framing. The loss cut is real and roughly tracks the revenue scale-up, but the top line is more concentrated (travel/forex) and less diversified than the "forex + lending + interest" neobank frame implies — the lending/interest leg barely moved.

**Key figures (with growth).**
- **Operating revenue: ₹158 Cr in FY26 (+80.1% YoY) vs ₹87.8 Cr in FY25** (Inc42, from MCA/ROC filing).
- **Total income: ₹178.5 Cr** = operating revenue ₹158 Cr + other income ₹16.2 Cr + other gains ₹4.2 Cr. The "+78% to ₹178 Cr vs ₹100 Cr FY25" headline is on TOTAL income; operating revenue grew faster (+80.1%). (Entrackr headline is total income; Inc42 splits it out.)
- **Gross revenue ₹187.6 Cr (+52% YoY) vs ₹123.4 Cr FY25**, with ₹29.6 Cr of customer consideration payable netted off to reach the ₹158 Cr operating figure (Inc42). Note the gross line grew far slower (+52%) than the net operating line (+80%) — the reported "+80%" is partly a function of a smaller pass-through deduction, not pure demand.
- **Net loss ₹32.6 Cr (−58.1% YoY) vs ₹77.9 Cr FY25** (Entrackr rounds to ₹33 Cr / ₹78 Cr).
- **EBITDA loss ₹32 Cr vs ₹77 Cr FY25 (≈−58%, "nearly halved")** per the press release (Entrackr). EBITDA loss ≈ net loss, i.e. finance/depreciation items are small — this is an operating-level loss, not a below-the-line artifact.

**By segment / driver.**
- **Network Partner Services (travel/forex rails): ₹132.4 Cr, ~nearly doubled YoY, ≈71% of gross top line** — this is the engine. Travel/forex is the growth, not a diversified neobank mix.
- **Technology-led financial services (2nd-largest segment): ₹46.9 Cr (+6% YoY) vs ₹44.2 Cr FY25** — essentially flat. This is the "fintech/lending/interest" leg, and it is NOT growing. The de-PR point: the press frames diversification (eSIMs, insurance, flights, hotels, remittances), but the financial-services segment that would make Niyo a "neobank" rather than a travel-forex distributor added only ₹2.7 Cr.
- **Forex handling / travel currency cards / telegraphic transfers: ₹2.7 Cr**; **other operating services: ₹5.6 Cr** (Inc42/Entrackr).
- Mgmt explicitly ties the uptick to cross-selling forex + travel products to the existing base and to the Kanji Forex expansion under Niyo's own AD-II licence.

**vs expectations / prior period.** No public consensus — Niyo is a private Indian neobank reporting via MCA/ROC filings + press release, so no Street estimate exists [no consensus]. vs FY25: revenue +80.1% (operating) / +78% (total), loss −58.1%. No prior-year earnings note in the corpus for a trajectory read [no prior note in base]. Management's own prior framing was a push toward profitability post the 2022 $100M Series C; FY26 is the clearest step-down in losses in that arc.

**Guidance / forward.** CFO Gourav Kumar guided (press release): **"maintain over 50% annual growth in the coming years"** and **"become EBITDA positive"** (no date given for break-even) [magnitude/timing of break-even UNSOURCED beyond "coming years"]. Plausibility: a >50% growth guide is a deceleration from the FY26 +80%, which is honest given the +52% gross-revenue print and the flat financial-services segment. EBITDA loss of only ₹32 Cr on ₹158 Cr operating revenue means break-even is within reach IF the next ~₹30–40 Cr of revenue carries incremental margin — but opex is still outrunning: total expenditure ₹212.7 Cr (+19.2%) vs ₹178.4 Cr FY25, with marketing & branding up 215.5% to ₹33.4 Cr (Inc42), so the path depends on marketing efficiency, not just scale. (Note: Entrackr cites "operating expenses" ₹201 Cr vs ₹168 Cr, +20%; Inc42's ₹212.7 Cr / ₹178.4 Cr is total expenditure — different scope.)

**Thesis-flags.**
1. **Quality of the loss cut: revenue-scaling, NOT cost-cutting.** Opex ROSE ~19–20% (₹201 Cr / ₹212.7 Cr depending on scope) while losses fell 58% — so the improvement came from the top line scaling faster than costs (operating leverage), not from austerity. That is the higher-quality driver. Second-order: it is durable only while travel/forex demand holds; a travel downturn would reverse the leverage fast.
2. **Concentration risk dressed up as diversification.** ~71% of the top line is travel/forex (Network Partner Services); the "diversification" list (eSIMs, insurance, hotels) is still tiny, and the financial-services segment is FLAT (+6%). The "neobank growing 78%" headline is really "a travel-forex distributor growing 80%." Second-order: Niyo's economics are leveraged to forex spreads and travel volumes + its AD-II/IFSCA licences (GIFT City PSP, Finnew Global Solutions IFSC), not to a lending/deposit book.
3. **Marketing spend is the swing factor on break-even.** Marketing +215.5% to ₹33.4 Cr is the single fastest-growing cost; if that is customer-acquisition for the travel push it must convert to repeat forex cross-sell (which mgmt is explicitly betting on — "more value from existing customers"). Second-order: if CAC does not amortise, the "EBITDA positive" guide slips.
4. **"AI efficiency" claim is unquantified.** Mgmt credits AI in customer service/ops/marketing/product for the efficiency gain, but gives no cost-per-unit or headcount metric — employee expense still rose 16.7% to ₹91.7 Cr. Treat the AI-efficiency narrative as PR colour [UNSOURCED on quantified savings], not a proven margin lever.
