## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened.** PalmPay (UK) Limited — the London-registered holding entity (Companies House no. 11099155, incorporated Dec 2017, 5 Merchant Square, W2) that sits above the PalmPay group — filed accounts for FY2025 showing a **£21.34m loss before tax** (~$21.3m). The red ink is almost entirely non-operating: a **£18.4m impairment** on the carrying value of its investments (the bulk attributed to a stake in Dexintec) plus a **£3.65m loan write-off** after borrower Giraffetech was written down to zero. UK-entity revenue is tiny and actually *fell* ~9% to £489,409 (from £538,578), consistent with this being a thin holding/IP-licensing vehicle rather than a trading fintech. Share capital of £41.82m and accumulated write-downs left net assets at £11.86m, down sharply from £33.20m in 2024. (Source: [thecondia.com](https://thecondia.com/palmpay-uk-posts-21-3m-loss-3-65m-loan/); [Companies House 11099155](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/11099155).)

**The framing gap.** The headline ("PalmPay UK burned through $21.3M... a window into what African fintech's international expansion actually costs") implies operating cash burn from overseas *expansion*. The filing says the opposite: this is a **balance-sheet impairment of a holding company's investments**, not operating burn from running a UK neobank. PalmPay does not operate a consumer UK product; the UK entity is a corporate-structure/holding layer. So the loss reflects write-downs of group-level equity/loan positions, not the day-to-day cost of international growth.

**Group scale dwarfs the UK entity.** The operating business is Nigeria-centred and large: **35M+ registered users** (reported 40M+ by Sept 2025), ~1M SME clients, revenue roughly **doubling from ~$64m (2023) to ~$128m (2024)**, and reported **group profitability in 2025**. PalmPay sits alongside OPay, Moniepoint and Kuda in Nigeria's fintech top tier (cf. [[African fintechs feature across CNBC's top 300 categories]]).

**Capital-raise / IPO context.** PalmPay was [[PalmPay in talks to raise Series B round|in talks for a $50–100M Series B]] (mid-2025, TechCrunch), and by Aug 2026 Bloomberg reported discussions to raise ~$200M at a **>$1bn valuation**, with a possible **Hong Kong IPO** (OASES agreement makes HK its non-Africa base). Against that, a £21.3m UK holding-company impairment is immaterial to the group's trajectory — but it does flag value destruction in two specific bets (Dexintec, Giraffetech).

**International footprint.** Expansion is real but Asia-led, not UK: PalmPay launched in [[Bangladesh approves PalmPay smartphone financing pilot|Bangladesh (smartphone-financing pilot)]] — its first market outside Africa — and is building out via Hong Kong. The UK entity is not a growth market; it is plumbing.

**Why it matters.** It is a reminder that African fintech "losses" headlines often come from UK/holding-company filings whose numbers (impairments, intercompany loans) say little about operating health. The genuinely interesting signals are the two specific write-downs and the gap between a loss-making holdco and a profitable, $1bn-valuation operating group.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** African remittance/diaspora fintech is a ~$100bn/yr inbound-to-Africa flow (per allBusiness Africa citing World Bank-style figures, as of 2026); the broader global remittance market is cited at ~$1.3tn by 2032 (per LemFi/industry framing, via TheNextWeb, 2025) — figures are directional, treat as "no data" for a precise UK-diaspora TAM. Structure: highly fragmented on the send side (LemFi, Nala, Chipper Cash, Sendwave, Grey, Leatherback all competing on price/corridor coverage), consolidating on the pan-African wallet side (OPay, PalmPay, Moniepoint). Barriers are regulatory (UK EMI/FCA + destination-market licensing) and network/liquidity on corridors, not technology. Why now: African-founded players are compressing send fees and routing more real value to recipients, while the Nigerian wallet giants push outward into the diaspora corridor — but PalmPay's UK entity, on the evidence below, is a holding/investment vehicle, not yet a scaled UK remittance operation.

**IMPORTANT de-PR — read the filing, not the headline.** The headline frames the £21.3m as "what African fintech's international expansion actually costs" (operational cash burn on a UK remittance/diaspora build-out). The Companies House accounts (FY to 31 Dec 2024) say otherwise: the £21,343,014 loss before tax is driven mostly by a non-cash impairment — PalmPay (UK) Ltd wrote down the carrying value of its investments by over £18.37m (the bulk attributed to Dexintec) and wrote off a £3.65m loan to Giraffetech after it collapsed to zero (per thecondia.com, 2026). So this is a holding-company / investment-writedown loss, NOT operational burn by a live UK neobank. PalmPay (UK) Ltd (Companies House 11099155, 5 Merchant Square, London) functions as a UK-registered holding/investment entity in the group, not the consumer wallet. The "cost of international expansion" read is only partly true — most of the number is impairment of soured bets, not customer-acquisition spend `(analysis)`.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for wallets — registered vs daily-active users, daily transaction count, take rate; for remittance — monthly transaction volume (TPV), corridor count, send-fee spread. Key players and positioning in the home (Nigeria) market where PalmPay actually operates: OPay (>50m registered / ~10m DAU mid-2025, per NotADeepDive via corpus), PalmPay (35m registered users, 15m daily transactions in Q1 2025, 80% retention, ~13m monthly actives — per PalmPay Q1 2025 report, drive_url below), Moniepoint (merchant-up), Interswitch. Basis of competition: distribution (Transsion handset pre-install is PalmPay's structural edge), fees, agent networks. Recent moves: OPay filed for a US/NYSE IPO targeting ~$4.42bn valuation and up to $366m raise (Oct 2026); PalmPay reportedly exploring a Hong Kong IPO at ~$1bn after a ~$200m target raise (Aug 2026, allAfrica/caproasia). Protagonist position: PalmPay is the clear #2 Nigerian wallet and profitable at group level (2025) — ahead on handset-distribution moat, catching up to OPay on scale/public-markets readiness. The UK entity specifically is niche / infrastructural, not a competitive front against LemFi/Nala `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Internal comps (base):
- [[OPay sets US IPO terms seeking up to $366M at $4.42B valuation]] — OPay H1'26 revenue $467.1m (+136.5% YoY), net income $90.9m; FY2025 revenue $536.3m, swung to profit from a $50.8m FY2024 loss. P/S on FY2025 rev = $4.42bn / $0.536bn ≈ 8.2x; on H1'26 annualized (~$934m) ≈ 4.7x — rich on trailing, more reasonable on run-rate, consistent with hyper-growth (not a flag given +137% growth).
- [[OPay hires banks for US IPO at about $4 billion valuation]] — earlier ~$4bn mark, confirms the $4-4.4bn band.
- [[Moniepoint raises $90M to complete $200M Series C]] — peer private round (Oct 2025), reportedly ~$1bn+ valuation territory.
- [[Chipper Cash CEO announced company's first-ever quarter of positive cash flow]] — diaspora/payments peer reaching cash-flow breakeven (Jan 2026), relevant to "burn vs scale".
- PalmPay own: ~$140m raised across seed/Series A, profitable group, Series B talks up to $100m at ~$1.5bn implied (per TechCrunch, Jun 2025, drive_url below).
PalmPay (UK) Ltd has no public revenue/EBITDA → no EV/Revenue or P/E computable for the UK entity = no data. Direct multiple vs OPay not meaningful (UK entity is a holding co, not operating). Qualitative: group is cheap-ish vs OPay's public ask if the ~$1-1.5bn private mark holds against 35m users, but unverified `(analysis)`.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Headline/economics mismatch.** The £21.3m is ~£22m of impairment + loan write-off (Dexintec, Giraffetech), not operational expansion burn — anyone reading it as "cost of going international" misjudges where the money went; the real signal is soured non-core investments inside the UK holding entity. Second-order: suggests PalmPay has made side-bets outside the core wallet that are impairing.
2. **Group-vs-entity opacity.** "PalmPay is profitable" (group, 2025) coexists with a loss-making UK holding co; consolidated economics and intercompany flows are undisclosed — investors sizing the HK IPO can't yet see clean segment P&L.
3. **Dependence on Transsion distribution + Chinese ownership.** PalmPay's moat (handset pre-install) and capital both lean on Transsion/NetEase; geopolitical/regulatory scrutiny of Chinese-owned African fintech is a latent risk ahead of a public listing.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is not evidence that diaspora/UK remittance expansion is structurally unprofitable — it's a holding-company writedown. The watch item is PalmPay's HK IPO prep against OPay's NYSE pricing: if OPay prints at ~$4.4bn on +137% growth, it re-rates the whole Nigerian wallet cohort and sets the benchmark PalmPay's ~$1-1.5bn ask will be measured against. Thesis: the UK loss is noise, not signal, for PalmPay's core; it would be wrong if subsequent filings show the UK entity is actually funding operational remittance build-out (not just parking impaired investments). Trigger to watch: PalmPay HK IPO prospectus (segment economics) and OPay NYSE final pricing.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict: miss (swing to a £21.3M pre-tax loss) — but note this is a UK holding-company result, not the operating African fintech.**

**What was filed.** PalmPay (UK) Limited (Companies House no. 11099155; formerly Transsnet Payment Limited, rebranded to PalmPay (UK) Limited in August 2025) filed FY2025 statutory accounts. The widely-reported "$21.3M" is the sterling figure converted: a pre-tax loss of **£21,343,014**, versus a small **£21,409 profit** the prior year.

**Headline figures (FY2025 vs FY2024):**
- Pre-tax result: **−£21.34M loss** vs **+£21.4K profit** — a ~£21.4M swing.
- Revenue: **£489,409**, down **~9.1%** from **£538,578**.
- Investment portfolio: **£25.29M**, down from **£40.11M**.
- Shareholders' funds: **£11.86M**, down sharply from **£33.20M**.
- Headcount: **0 employees** (down from 2), consistent with this entity being a holding company, not an operating business.

**What drove the loss (it is overwhelmingly non-cash):**
- **£18,376,487 asset depreciation/impairment** — a write-down of the value of investments/assets held by the holding company.
- **£3,650,000 loan write-off** — a loan extended to subsidiary **Giraffetech**, whose value "collapsed to zero" and became unrecoverable, written off in full.
- Together these two items (~£22.0M) exceed the entire £21.3M reported loss; the underlying trading revenue (£0.49M) is immaterial by comparison.

**Thesis flags:**
- The headline "PalmPay UK burned through $21.3M" overstates operating reality: this is a **holding/investment vehicle with zero employees and ~£0.5M revenue**, not a cash-consuming operating fintech. The "burn" is an **accounting impairment plus a bad intra-group loan**, not a product/marketing cash outflow.
- The underlying signal is a **failed subsidiary (Giraffetech → £3.65M written off)** and a **£14.8M reduction in portfolio carrying value (£40.1M → £25.3M)** — the group wrote down UK/international investments materially.
- This contrasts with the Nigerian operating parent, which PalmPay describes as **profitable** (35M users, 15M daily transactions per its own Q1 2025 release; in talks to raise up to $100M at a ~$1.5B valuation per June 2025 reporting). The UK entity's loss does **not** indicate the operating business is unprofitable — it reflects the cost/write-down of international-expansion holdings.
- No user, transaction, or forward guidance metrics were disclosed in the UK accounts.

**IR note.** PalmPay's own IR materials (ir_latest.json / irdb) cover only the Nigerian operating parent (user/KPI and funding press releases) and contain **no** PalmPay-UK FY statutory figures. Actuals above are sourced from the Companies House FY2025 filing as reported by The Condia; figures corroborated via web search.
