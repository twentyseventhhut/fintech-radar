### [0] Thesis (This Week in Fintech, citing Sentora's "Fintech Onchain" report)
This Week in Fintech's essay runs on one hook: the **average US savings account pays ~0.63%, while onchain dollar strategies pay up to ~8%**, and — per **Sentora's "Fintech Onchain" annual report** — balances that move into **onchain earn programs tend to stay** (high retention). The argument: onchain is now a *financial product layer* (savings, treasury, credit, payroll), not a trading toy, so fintechs should build savings products on blockchain rails.
**Why framed this way:** the 0.63% figure is the national-average savings rate (Bankrate ~Dec 2025 pegs the national average ~0.6% APY), deliberately contrasted against a *high-tier, risk-on* onchain yield (~8%) — not a like-for-like comparison. The honest comparison is tokenized T-bill funds (BlackRock BUIDL, Ondo USDY, Hashnote USYC) paying ~4–5% net, roughly matching high-yield online savings (~4.3–5%) at *lower* risk; the 8% number implies leverage/DeFi-vault/credit risk, not a free lunch over a bank. The essay is a re-surfacing of a report Sentora already pushed earlier in October — and Sentora is itself a **vault curator**, so the report is also marketing for its own book.

### [1] Duplicate / prior corpus
This is a **re-report of the SAME Sentora "Fintech Onchain" report** already enriched in [[Sentora onchain earn balances tend to stay put]] (2026-10-08, importance 3, same source URL `sentora.com/research/reports/annual-report-2026`, same "balances... tend to stay there / Read Fintech Onchain" digest text, same "This Week in Fintech" channel). The only delta here is the **0.63% vs 8% yield framing** layered on top — a new editorial hook, not a new event, dataset, or reporting period. For the underlying case figures (Kraken ~$800M DeFi Earn, Coinbase ~$2.3B crypto-backed originations, Deel payroll wallet in 80+ countries, 17-bank tokenized-deposit consortium targeting 2027) and Sentora's identity (May-2025 IntoTheBlock + Trident merger, $25M Series A, active Morpho-vault curator), see that prior note — all unchanged.

### [3] Novelty / value-add
Low. The essay repackages a two-week-old vendor report behind a punchy (and asymmetric) yield comparison. No new data, no new deployment, no new reporting period. Value to a reader is the one line that matters — **GENIUS Act bars payment-stablecoin issuers from paying yield**, so demand is being routed into *tokenized money-market funds and curated DeFi vaults* instead — but that regulatory mechanism is already well covered in the corpus (post-GENIUS stablecoin notes). "Balances tend to stay" remains Sentora's own data on its own curated vaults, measured in a rising market: sticky ≠ sticky through a drawdown or yield compression.

### [4] What's next / stakes
The real second-order debate the hook gestures at: **deposit disintermediation**. Payment stablecoins and onchain earn pull balances out of ~0.6% bank deposits; the banking lobby (ABA) has pressed exactly this concern around the GENIUS Act. Note the asymmetry industry commentary draws: *tokenized deposits* are a more-efficient version of existing bank deposits (little disintermediation), whereas *payment stablecoins / onchain earn* let balances leave the deposit relationship entirely — which is why 17 banks are building a tokenized-deposit network (2027) as a defensive counter. That tension is the substance; this particular essay only restates it.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** The pitch — avg US savings pays ~0.63% while onchain dollar strategies pay up to ~8% — is directionally real: Bankrate's national average savings APY was 0.64% as of 2026-10-24 (FDIC puts it lower, 0.38%), vs Aave V3 USDC ~3.29% (Aug 2026) and up to ~8–8.9% on looped/incentivised vaults like Fluid/Kraken DeFi Earn (per Bankrate / stablecoinbeat / theblock) — a ~5–9x gross gap before gas, smart-contract and de-peg risk. The supply backbone is tokenized Treasuries, which reached ~$14.9bn AUM by 2026-10-06, up from ~$5bn in early 2025 (per spark.money/stablecoininsider); leaders Circle USYC $2.41bn, Ondo OUSG/USDY $2.3bn, BlackRock BUIDL $2.25bn at ~3.6% 30-day APY (as of 2026-10). Structure: still small vs the ~$6.6tn US transactional deposit base the CEA's Apr-2026 report flagged as "at risk" (per congress.gov CRS). **Why now:** the GENIUS Act (P.L. 119-27) *bans* issuers from paying yield on payment stablecoins, which paradoxically pushed yield into *separate* onchain earn products (vaults, tokenized MMFs) that sit one layer away from the regulated coin — the exact "savings product on blockchain rails" Sentora's report is selling into.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: AUM/TVL, net APY vs the risk-free rate, and — Sentora's own thesis — retention/stickiness of migrated balances. Sentora is not a yield product; it is a risk/analytics + vault-curation layer. The Kraken DeFi Earn launch (2026-01) names Chaos Labs and **Sentora** as the risk managers operating the first three USDC vaults, allocating to Aave/Morpho/Sky — so Sentora sits in the "institutional DeFi plumbing" category alongside Gauntlet (powers [[Kast launches stablecoin yield product]]) and Chaos Labs, not alongside the issuers. Basis of competition: trust/risk-curation credibility and distribution partnerships, not rate. Recent peer moves: [[Kraken launches DeFi Earn vaults powered by Ink]] (2026-01, up to 8% APY, 48 US states), [[Ground raises $3.6M to pipe on-chain yield into fintech apps]] (2026-06, embedded yield infra), [[OpenTrade secures $17 million for stablecoin yield infrastructure]] / [[Osero raises $13.5 million for stablecoin yield infrastructure]] (2026-05). Position: niche but well-placed — a research report ("Fintech Onchain") is a lead-gen/thought-leadership play to funnel fintechs toward curated vaults; moat is credibility + exchange partnerships (switching costs, (analysis)).

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/revenue in the note — Sentora is a private risk-infra firm and the item is a research report, not a deal. **Multiples: no data / [UNSOURCED].** Internal comps by raise (infra layer, not a trading multiple): [[OpenTrade secures $17 million for stablecoin yield infrastructure]], [[Osero raises $13.5 million for stablecoin yield infrastructure]], [[Ground raises $3.6M to pipe on-chain yield into fintech apps]], [[Morpho raises $175M for onchain credit infrastructure]] (the credit layer these vaults route into). Product-scale references cited in the report itself — Kraken's $800M DeFi Earn, Coinbase's $2.3bn crypto-backed loans — are production-deployment markers, not Sentora's; Coinbase's $2.3bn outstanding is corroborated (per kucoin/cryptobriefing, 2026), the $800M Kraken figure I could not independently verify → [UNSOURCED].

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory arbitrage is the business model.** The yield lives in vaults *because* GENIUS bars issuers from paying it; the OCC's final rule (eff. Jan 2027) already moved to close affiliate/third-party reward workarounds (per spaziocrypto/onebullex). If regulators extend "any form" to curated earn programs, the deposit-migration thesis compresses fast.
2. **Apples-to-oranges yield comparison.** 0.63% is a FDIC-insured, instantly-liquid bank rate; 8% carries smart-contract, de-peg, liquidation and curator risk and no insurance. Sentora (as curator) carries reputational/liability exposure on any vault exploit — cf. [[Balancer Labs to shut down after $110m exploit]].
3. **Conflicted source.** The "balances stay once moved onchain" retention claim is Sentora's own proprietary data in a report it is marketing; de-PR this — it is a seller demonstrating stickiness, not independently audited churn data.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) The real contest isn't DeFi-vs-banks but *where the spread is captured*: tokenized-MMF issuers (BlackRock/Ondo/Circle) take the Treasury carry, exchanges/fintechs take distribution, and curators like Sentora/Gauntlet/Chaos monetise risk-management — the stablecoin issuer, barred from yield, is squeezed in the middle. Falsifiable thesis: if yield-bearing earn balances keep compounding into 2027 while small-bank deposit betas stay low, expect banks to lobby harder and/or launch their own tokenized-deposit consortia (the report's own "17-bank, 2027" reference). Trigger to watch: the OCC's 2027 rule and whether curated vaults get swept into the yield ban. Thesis breaks if onchain yields converge toward bank rates as the Fed cuts and the Treasury carry shrinks.
