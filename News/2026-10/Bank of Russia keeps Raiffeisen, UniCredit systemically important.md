# Context-enrichment: Bank of Russia keeps Raiffeisen, UniCredit systemically important
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

**Freshness verdict: FRESH.** This is the annual CBR systemically-important-banks (SIB) list refresh, published **2026-10-07**, keeping the roster unchanged for 2026. It is a distinct, new event (a yearly regulatory act) and arrives three days after the **2026-10-05 Putin decree** authorizing UniCredit's Russian spin-off/sale — the two together are what make the "no change" notable. No prior corpus note covers either the 2026 list or the decree; related notes are context, not duplicates.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-07 the Bank of Russia (CBR) **retained its SIB list unchanged** for 2026 — the SIBs still hold ~80% of Russian banking-sector assets. The newsworthy angle is pure continuity: for the fourth consecutive year the CBR keeps **Raiffeisenbank (RBI, Austria)** and **UniCredit Bank (Italy)** on the list even as both run down their Russian books.

- **Count correction (skeptical flag):** The Финтехно aggregate implies a routine list; several wire summaries say "13 banks" but those pull from **2021–2022 articles still listing Otkritie and Rosbank, which no longer exist as standalone entities**. The **correct current count is 12** — the roster has been identical since the **2025-10-07** revision that dropped Otkritie (merged into VTB) and Rosbank (absorbed into T-Bank) and added **Bank DOM.RF**. So 2026 = "retained 2025 list," not "13." (analysis of source conflict)
- **The wind-down numbers (verified, with caveats):** Raiffeisen's Russian loan book is down roughly **fourfold since Jan 2022, to ~245–255bn rub (~$3.2bn)** — i.e. **~75% (the note's "78%" is close but the best-sourced figure is ~75%; RBI's own group-FX framing is ~60%, the gap is currency/scope).** UniCredit's local loans fell **almost tenfold, from ~670bn to ~67bn rub (~$850m), i.e. ~90%** — loans now **<9% of its assets**.
- **Why this framing matters:** Both banks are now mostly **liquidity parked at the CBR**, not lenders — Raiffeisen ~839bn rub at the CBR (~40% of assets); UniCredit ~317bn rub (~42% of assets). Their "systemic importance" today is therefore about **deposits and payments plumbing**, not credit intermediation. (analysis)

**Why structured this way / what it reveals:** Inclusion is **formula-based with CBR discretion.** When asked in Jul-2024 why foreign subsidiaries stay, Nabiullina said there were **"no grounds for excluding Russian subsidiaries of foreign banks from it yet"** — "no grounds yet," not a mechanical score, signals judgment. The Gaidar Institute noted ~25 banks are formula *candidates* vs the 12 listed, again implying selection. Keeping two shrinking, exit-seeking foreign banks on the list subjects them to Russia's enhanced buffers and supervision **exactly as they try to leave** — a soft pressure/visibility signal more than a capital event.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Western banks that fully exited:** **Citigroup** completed the sale of its last Russian unit (AO Citibank → Renaissance Capital, approved Nov 2025) at a **$1.2bn loss** ([[Citigroup to sell remaining Russian operations at $1.2bn loss]]). Societe Generale exited outright in 2022 (Rosbank sold, now inside T-Bank). Against these, Raiffeisen and UniCredit are the **laggards** — the only two Western names still on the SIB list.
- **Russian SIB peers:** Sberbank, VTB, Gazprombank, Alfa, Sovcombank, MKB, DOM.RF, T-Bank, PSB, Rosselkhozbank — these dominate and are growing; the two foreign banks are a shrinking tail kept on for formula/optics.
- **Position:** On *strategic intent* Raiffeisen/UniCredit are behind peers (Citi/SocGen already gone). **Why:** trapped profits were extraordinarily lucrative (Raiffeisen's Russian unit was ~38% of RBI group profit in Q3-2025; NIM ~10% vs ~3% group), so the exit calculus was "stay and earn vs crystallize a loss and appease the ECB." The ECB forced the pace. (analysis)

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Raiffeisen:** Under ECB pressure since 2022; ordered to shrink the loan book (~65% target); says it is **ahead of the ECB schedule** and met 2025 targets; took a **€339m provision** (Q4-2025) on the Rasperia/Strabag litigation. Pursuing attritional run-off, **no confirmed sale** of the Russian bank.
- **UniCredit:** ECB ordered a scale-down in Apr-2024; UniCredit litigated, lost a Nov-2024 suspension bid, then **withdrew its EU appeal in Jan-2026**, pivoting to an orderly solvent wind-down. In **May-2026** it signed a **non-binding deal to sell most of its Russian business to a UAE private investor**, retaining only payments. On **2026-10-05 Putin's decree** authorized the reorganization: spin a "New Bank" (kept by UniCredit) and sell the "Remaining Bank" 100% — removing the Russian-side regulatory veto. Close targeted **H1-2027**.
- **Fit:** Staying on the SIB list is consistent with both banks still holding large deposit/payment franchises; it does not contradict their exit — it just means the exit is **slow and regulated on both sides** (ECB pushing out, Kremlin controlling the door). (analysis)

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty of THIS item: low.** It is a "nothing changed" regulatory notice. The genuine new developments are adjacent: the **Oct-5 Putin decree** (a real "talks → authorized" step for UniCredit) and the running-down numbers. The list itself is continuity.
- **What "systemically important" concretely buys/costs:** an **extra capital-adequacy buffer** (effective min CAR ~11.5% incl. the add-on per 2024 framing), **recovery-plan filing**, heightened supervision and Basel-III-style liquidity/disclosure scrutiny. A **new differentiated framework** (proposed May-2025) sets five surcharge groups (**2.5/2.0/1.5/1.0/0.5%**); first revision under it in **2027**, surcharges phased from **2028**.
- **Traction:** The substance is the **de-facto zombification** of two foreign banks — large balance sheets parked at the CBR, lending gutted, profits trapped, exits half-approved. That is the real story, not the list. (analysis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **UniCredit:** binding agreement, asset separation and approvals needed; UAE buyer unnamed; **H1-2027 close is a target, not done.** Potential loss framing circulated around €3.3bn in earlier reporting.
- **Raiffeisen:** continued forced run-off under ECB watch into 2026; no sale confirmed; dividend repatriation remains constrained/uncertain.
- **Second-order:** As long as both stay on the SIB list, Russia keeps a **supervisory lever** over two European groups and a **visibility tool** over their payment rails — useful geopolitically. Conversely, the moment the UniCredit sale closes, the list would finally change (New Bank may stay; Remaining Bank exits). **The central question is not "why keep them on the list" but "who controls the pace of the Western exit" — and the answer is: the ECB from outside, the Kremlin from inside, with the CBR list as a status marker.** (analysis)

## Red-team / challenge questions

1. **Is the list actually "13 banks"?** No — the correct count is **12**. The "13" in wire summaries comes from 2021–2022 articles still listing Otkritie and Rosbank (both since absorbed). The current roster has been unchanged since the 2025-10-07 revision (dropped Otkritie + Rosbank, added DOM.RF).

2. **Is this a fresh event or a reprint?** Fresh. It is the **annual SIB-list refresh dated 2026-10-07**, a distinct regulatory act. No prior corpus note covers the 2026 list. Adjacent, not duplicate.

3. **Is the "78%" Raiffeisen loan-cut figure right?** Approximately, but imprecise. Best-sourced ruble figure is **~75%** (fourfold, to ~245–255bn rub); RBI's group-FX framing is ~60%. "78%" is in range; the exact number depends on currency and date. (open)

4. **Is the "~90%" UniCredit figure right?** Yes — corroborated: local loans fell **almost tenfold, 670bn → 67bn rub**, i.e. ~90%, now <9% of assets.

5. **Does "systemically important" impose a real cost on these shrinking banks?** Marginal. It adds a capital buffer (~11.5% effective min CAR incl. add-on), recovery-plan filing and heightened supervision. Both banks are massively over-liquid (40%+ of assets parked at the CBR), so the buffer is non-binding. The real effect is supervisory visibility, not capital strain. (analysis)

6. **Is inclusion a pure asset-size formula or discretionary?** Formula-based with discretion. Nabiullina's Jul-2024 line "no grounds for excluding Russian subsidiaries of foreign banks **yet**" signals judgment; Gaidar Institute counts ~25 formula candidates vs 12 listed.

7. **Why keep two exit-seeking foreign banks on the list at all?** Likely a status/visibility lever: it keeps the CBR's enhanced reporting and supervision over two European groups' Russian payment rails precisely while they try to leave. (hypothesis)

8. **What is genuinely new here vs the prior year?** Almost nothing in the list itself — it is "retained." The actual new developments are adjacent: the **Oct-5 Putin decree** on UniCredit's spin-off/sale and the updated run-off numbers.

9. **Will the list change soon?** Plausibly on the UniCredit side: if the UAE sale closes (target H1-2027), the "Remaining Bank" exits foreign ownership and the composition could finally shift. Raiffeisen shows no confirmed sale — slow attritional run-off. (open)

10. **Who is the UniCredit buyer and is the deal done?** A "well-established private UAE investor" (unnamed); the May-2026 agreement is **non-binding**; Putin's decree removes the Russian-side veto but a binding deal, asset separation and approvals are still pending. Not done.

11. **Can Raiffeisen repatriate its trapped Russian profits?** Unclear — sources conflict (Russian restrictions on "unfriendly" owners vs one claim the unit may pay dividends to parent). (open)

12. **Did the ECB fine either bank?** Found supervisory orders/restrictions (scale-down mandates, UniCredit's lost Nov-2024 suspension bid, appeal withdrawn Jan-2026) but **no confirmed specific monetary fine**. (open)

13. **How material are the Russian units to the parents?** Very: Raiffeisen's Russian unit was ~38% of RBI group profit in Q3-2025 (NIM ~10% vs ~3% group); UniCredit Russia >8% of group returns in Q3-2025. That lucrativeness is exactly why exit has been slow. (analysis)

14. **Does staying on the list contradict "they're leaving"?** No — it reflects that they still hold large deposit/payment franchises; the exit is slow and controlled on both sides (ECB from outside, Kremlin from inside).

15. **What is the real central question?** Not "why keep them on the list" but **"who controls the pace of the Western exit"** — the answer being the ECB, the Kremlin, and the CBR list as a status marker.

**Importance: 2/5** — A no-change annual regulatory notice. Low novelty (continuity), no new terms, no traction event in the item itself. It earns a 2 (not 1) only because the "unchanged" framing is a legitimate signal about Russia's control over the slow Western-bank exit, and it sits beside the genuinely newer Oct-5 UniCredit decree. Net: useful context, not a standalone must-publish.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russian banking, systemically-important-bank (SIB) regulation sub-layer. The CBR SIB list stayed at 13 institutions for 2026 (unchanged since 2023); the 13 SIBs hold ~80% of Russian banking-sector assets (per Interfax/iz.ru, as of 2026-10-07). Structure is highly consolidated and state-tilted — Sberbank, VTB, Gazprombank, Alfa, PSB, Sovcombank, T-Bank, Credit Bank of Moscow, Rosselkhozbank, DOM.RF dominate, with only two foreign-owned names (Raiffeisenbank, UniCredit Bank) left on the list. "Why now": the newsworthy point is not a change but a non-change — the CBR keeps SIB status (with its higher capital buffer + liquidity/reporting requirements) on two banks that are actively winding down. SIB status is assigned on local size/interconnectedness/substitutability, not on parent strategy, so a shrinking foreign bank can still clear the threshold if its local payments/settlement role remains hard to substitute (analysis). Both parents are under separate ECB/EU pressure to exit (RBI targeting a ~60–65% loan cut; UniCredit carving out and selling most of its unit) — i.e. two regulators pulling in opposite directions on the same entities.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs here are prudential, not commercial: local RWA/assets, interconnectedness, substitutability, SIB capital buffer. Basis of "competition" is really basis of regulatory designation. The two protagonists are exiting, not competing: RBI's Russia loan book is down ~78% since Feb-2022 (per Bloomberg/marketscreener, Q1-2026); UniCredit's local lending down ~90% (per the note's channel text — not independently web-confirmed, treat as [UNSOURCED] on magnitude). UniCredit Russia has also been throttling retail cards since 2026-07 ([[UniCredit Russia tightens card, deposit limits amid pullback]]) — a soft client-base run-off. Recent moves with dates: 2026-10-05/06 Putin decree authorising UniCredit to spin off a new 100%-owned international-payments bank and sell the remainder, close targeted H1-2027 ([[Putin allows sale of UniCredit's Russian assets]], [[Финтехно UniCredit Russia sale requires asset-split first]]); UniCredit guided a €3.0–3.3bn cumulative earnings hit (per Bloomberg, 2026-05-07). Position: both are shrinking incumbents retained for substitutability/payments role, not growth — "run-off, still systemic" (analysis). Moat = none commercially; the only "stickiness" is the local payments plumbing regulators on both sides want preserved.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/deal metrics attach to this regulatory-list news, so trading multiples are not applicable (bank-of-russia is a regulator; the two banks are unlisted local subs of listed EU parents). "Distribution not computed, qualitative comparison." Internal comps — the foreign-bank Russia-exit precedent set:
- [[Citigroup to sell remaining Russian operations at $1.2bn loss]] (2026-01) — Citi exited at a disclosed ~$1.2bn loss; direct read-across to UniCredit's €3.0–3.3bn guided hit — exits crystallise large losses, not gains.
- [[Citi board approves sale of AO Citibank to Renaissance Capital]] (2026-01) — buyer was a local (Renaissance Capital); fits the pattern that exiting foreigners sell to RU/friendly-jurisdiction buyers (UniCredit → UAE investor).
- [[UniCredit Russia tightens card, deposit limits amid pullback]] (2026-07) — same-entity prior step.
RBI's non-Russia core generated €209m profit in Q1-2026 (per RBI IR), underscoring that Russia is a trapped-capital drag, not an earner, for the group now. No comparable EV/Rev or P/E meaningful here — "no data".

**Risk flags.**
1. Trapped-capital / forced-sale discount — Putin's decree green-lights UniCredit's sale but value and buyer are undisclosed; precedent (Citi −$1.2bn, UniCredit's own −€3.0–3.3bn guidance) shows exits realise steep losses; the carve-out + licensing of a new payments bank is an added execution step that can slip past H1-2027. Second-order: SIB buffer obligations persist on a shrinking balance sheet, raising the carrying cost of staying.
2. Dual-regulator squeeze — ECB wants faster shrinkage; CBR keeps SIB status (and its buffer/reporting load) in place. The banks optimise for neither; a further ECB acceleration order could collide with CBR's "you're still systemic" stance. Second-order: whatever "minimal" payments entity survives is exactly the part Moscow has most interest in retaining, limiting the parents' freedom to fully exit.
3. Disclosure/verification gap — the ~90% UniCredit-lending figure is channel-sourced, not independently confirmed; C-Account-type frozen balances (RBI: €2.178bn by Jun-2026) mean headline loan-book shrinkage overstates true de-risking. Second-order: reported "exit progress" may flatter the real residual Russia exposure.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a status-quo signal, not a re-rating: it confirms that even a near-complete commercial retreat does not remove SIB designation while the local payments/settlement role is deemed non-substitutable — Russia has an interest in keeping foreign-bank payment rails nominally "systemic" even as lending evaporates (analysis). Watch next: (a) whether the newly carved-out UniCredit payments bank is itself added to / kept on the SIB list once licensed; (b) disclosed sale price/buyer for the UniCredit remainder as the next hard loss-crystallisation datapoint; (c) any ECB acceleration order on RBI. Thesis would be wrong if the CBR drops either name from the list before the sales close — which would signal the payments role is no longer seen as systemic.
