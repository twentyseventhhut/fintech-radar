# NBFCs account for 47% of small new-to-credit loans in India

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened (dated).** On 2026-10-05 the Finance Industry Development Council (FIDC) — the RBI-recognised self-regulatory body for non-bank lenders — and credit bureau TransUnion CIBIL released a report titled "Bharat Nirman: NBFC Forming the Foundation of Credit Dispersion." Using data as of June 2026, it finds NBFCs account for ~47% of new-to-credit (NTC) consumer loan originations. In the small-ticket segment of loans up to ₹2 lakh (~$2.3K) — which is 82% of all consumer-credit originations by count — NBFCs supply 47% versus only 17% for banks. Headline 90+ DPD (balance-level) delinquency for the segment is cited at 1.1%, down from 2.7% in June 2019. (The item's note says "below INR 2 lakh … 47% … retail delinquency 1.1%"; figures reconcile with the report.)

**[1] Key facts / numbers (from the report).**
- NBFC NTC share: ~47% of new-to-credit originations (some outlets round to ~50%; TransUnion CIBIL's own newsroom headline says "~50%").
- Small-ticket (≤₹2 lakh): NBFCs 47% vs banks 17%; this bucket = 82% of consumer-credit originations by volume.
- NBFC retail-origination share rose 33% (Jun-2016) → 43% (Jun-2026) by volume; share by value lower at ~30% — i.e. NBFCs are volume-heavy / ticket-light.
- Credit-active NBFC consumer base grew ~7x over the decade, ~2.5x faster than the overall credit industry; ~46% of all credit-active consumers now hold an NBFC loan.
- Geography: ~58% of NBFC loan volume originates in semi-urban/rural (SuRu) markets; SuRu consumer base 31% (2016) → 59% (2026).
- Asset quality: 90+ DPD 2.7% (Jun-2019) → 1.1% (Jun-2026); above-prime consumer share 24% → 32%.
- Commercial lending: NBFC share of credit-active commercial entities 10% (Jun-2021) → 18% (Jun-2026).
- Original source URL in note: economictimes.indiatimes.com (ET) relay; primary = FIDC/TransUnion CIBIL press release (2026-10-05).

**[2] Why it matters.** Confirms a structural handoff: NBFCs (and the fintechs lending through them) are now India's default on-ramp to formal credit, especially in small-ticket and SuRu markets where banks stay selective. The "low delinquency" framing is partly a composition / selection effect and is reported by an industry body (FIDC) with an interest in a favourable narrative — see challenge. For fintech-lending, this is the macro tailwind behind UPI-linked credit, consumer-durable/BNPL, gold and two-wheeler NTC products, and co-lending/DLG arrangements.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. **Whose number is this?** The report is co-authored by FIDC — the NBFC industry's own SRO — which has a direct interest in a "NBFCs = financial inclusion, low delinquency" narrative. How much is advocacy vs neutral bureau analysis?
2. **47% of *what*?** NTC *origination share by count* in loans ≤₹2 lakh — not of credit *value*. NBFC value share is far lower (~30% retail; 36.6% of personal-loan value in the Q3 report). Headline overstates NBFC weight in rupee terms.
3. **Is 1.1% delinquency credible?** It is 90+ DPD *balance-level* for a snapshot in a benign, post-recovery window (June 2026). Business Standard/CRIF show small-ticket delinquency *rising* to 6.4% by Mar-2026 on other cuts. Which definition/denominator reconciles them?
4. **Selection / seasoning effect.** NTC books are young; low DPD can reflect loans not yet seasoned rather than durable quality. Is the 1.1% a vintage artefact?
5. **Does "47%" vs CIBIL's own "~50%" vs ET's "nearly half" signal rounding games?** TransUnion CIBIL's newsroom says ~50%; the note and most outlets say 47%. Reconcile.
6. **Duplication risk with [[India's new-to-credit borrower base grows to 4.4 crore]]** (May-2026, CRIF, "NBFCs >60% of NTC accounts"). Is this a genuinely new report or a re-slice of the same thesis? (Different bureau, author, metric and quarter → companion, not duplicate.)
7. **Co-lending / DLG optics.** A large share of "NBFC" NTC originations is bank money routed via co-lending/FLDG. Does the NBFC-vs-bank split overstate independent NBFC risk-taking?
8. **Regulatory reflexivity.** RBI eased risk weights in Feb-2025 (reversing Nov-2023). Is the NBFC share gain a durable structural shift or a policy-cycle rebound that reverses if RBI re-tightens?
9. **SuRu concentration risk.** 58% of volume in semi-urban/rural — exposed to monsoon/rural-livelihood shocks (MFIN itself flagged weak-monsoon risk). Is "low delinquency" robust to a bad crop year?
10. **Macro, not fintech, story?** Much NBFC NTC growth is consumer-durable, gold and two-wheeler loans at incumbent NBFCs (Bajaj, Shriram, gold-loan majors) — not digital fintech. How much is actually "fintech"?
11. **Figure mismatch in the note?** Note says "retail delinquency 1.1%" but the 1.1% is the *NTC/small-ticket 90+ DPD*, not total-retail PAR (CRIF puts total-retail PAR 31-180 at 3.1%). Label precisely.
12. **No URL to the primary report.** Note cites only an ET relay; the FIDC/TransUnion PDF/press release is the source of record. Verify figures against it, not the secondary.
13. **Representativeness of bureau data.** Does it capture only credit-bureau-reported NBFC lending, missing informal/unregulated small NBFCs whose quality is worse?

**Importance: 3/5** — A credible, well-sourced data point (FIDC/TransUnion CIBIL, Jun-2026) confirming the structural NBFC/fintech takeover of India's new-to-credit on-ramp — directly relevant to fintech-lending readers. But it is an industry-body report (narrative risk), a count-share not value-share figure, and largely corroborates prior coverage ([[India's new-to-credit borrower base grows to 4.4 crore]], [[India's retail loan portfolio grows 18% in Q3 FY26]]) rather than breaking new ground; "low delinquency" sits awkwardly against rising small-ticket DPD elsewhere. Solid context, not a standalone headline.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a sector-data item (no single protagonist company): the FIDC–TransUnion CIBIL report on India's retail/NTC credit market. NBFCs accounted for ~47% of new-to-credit (NTC) consumer loan originations as of June 2026, vs ~17% for banks; loans up to ₹2 lakh ($2K) were 82% of consumer-credit originations (per FIDC–TransUnion CIBIL, via APAC News Network / Elets BFSI, 2026-10). Note: the pick's own body and the ET link attribute the 47% stat to a generic "report"; the primary publisher is FIDC–TransUnion CIBIL, not ET — treat ET as a secondary citation. Asset quality has improved structurally: 90+DPD balance-level delinquency fell from 2.7% (Jun-2019) to 1.1% (Jun-2026), and above-prime borrowers rose 24%→32% over the same window (same report). Scale context: NBFC credit-active base grew ~7x over the decade, ~2.5x faster than the overall credit industry, with >58% of NBFC loan volume originated in semi-urban/rural (SuRu) markets. Structure — the market is a three-layer stack (banks / NBFCs / fintech-origination partners) that is fragmenting at the thin-ticket end: banks cede small-ticket NTC to NBFCs and fintech co-lenders because unit economics and underwriting speed favour digital-native players, while banks keep the balance-sheet. **Why now:** the RBI regulatory cycle turned supportive in 2025 — risk weight on microfinance-type consumer credit cut 125%→100%, and bank-to-NBFC risk weights (A-rated and above) cut 25pp, reversing the Nov-2023 tightening (per Outlook Business / Khaitan, 2026) — lowering funding cost for NBFCs just as thin-ticket demand accelerates.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: AUM growth, NTC origination share, 90+DPD / GNPA, net interest margin, and (for gold/MFI) LTV and collection efficiency. Key players by layer: (i) large retail NBFCs — Bajaj Finance (AUM ~₹5.1 lakh cr, +22.4% YoY), Shriram Finance (~₹3.0 lakh cr, +14.9%), Cholamandalam (~₹1.65 lakh cr); (ii) digital-first / fintech-NBFCs — Navi (lending AUM >₹130bn by mid-2026), KreditBee, Paytm (distribution + lending); (iii) banks, which hold only ~17% of thin-ticket NTC. Basis of competition at the NTC/thin-ticket end is distribution + underwriting data, not price — hence fintechs reportedly command ~52% of personal-loan origination share (per GlobeNewswire alt-lending report, 2026-01; `(analysis)` on exact share). Recent regulatory/market moves: ₹20,000 cr MFI credit-guarantee scheme approved Mar-2026 ([[India approves ₹20,000 crore microfinance credit guarantee scheme]]); new uniform gold-loan LTV framework effective 1-Apr-2026 (tiered 85/80/75%). Protagonist's "position": NBFCs collectively are ahead at the NTC frontier — the moat is intangible (alternative/bureau data + SuRu distribution + co-lending rails), not balance-sheet scale, which still sits with banks.

**Comps & multiples.** This is sector data, so trading comps are for the listed NBFC proxies, not a single issuer. Peers (public, verifiable):
- Bajaj Finance — P/B ~3.9x (per ForgeUp/EnrichMoney, 2026); richest, justified by scale + ROE.
- Shriram Finance — P/B ~1.8x; P/E ~13.8x trailing; post-merger ROE ~19.1% — cheap vs Bajaj on comparable AUM growth/asset quality (outlier-cheap flag; discount not obviously justified by fundamentals → re-rating candidate).
- Cholamandalam — mid-tier; clean P/B not from a free source → `[UNSOURCED]`.
Arithmetic shown where sourced: Shriram P/E = price / trailing EPS ≈ 13.8x; P/B figures are as-reported, not recomputed (book value per share not independently sourced → not recomputed). Distribution not computed (only 2 clean P/B figures, <3 → qualitative only). Internal comps: [[Bajaj Finance AUM grows 24% to $52bn in Q2 FY26]], [[India's retail loan portfolio grows 18% in Q3 FY26]] (retail book ₹163 lakh cr / $1.8tn, +18% YoY), [[Crisil gold-loan NBFC assets to grow at 40% CAGR]], [[Gold loan delinquency rises with larger borrower exposure]], [[RBI grants SRO status to FIDC for non-bank lending sector]].

**Risk flags.**
1. **Headline asset quality may understate forward risk (vintage/denominator effect).** The 1.1% blended 90+DPD is a through-the-cycle low measured against a rapidly growing, young NTC book — delinquency on fresh NTC vintages typically seasons 12–24 months later, so a falling blended ratio can mask deteriorating new-cohort behaviour. Corroborating signal: [[Gold loan delinquency rises with larger borrower exposure]] shows 1.5% delinquency (2.2x) at larger ticket sizes despite the benign aggregate.
2. **Regulatory reflexivity.** NBFC NTC dominance exists partly because RBI *eased* risk weights in 2025; the RBI has already (Jun-2024) red-flagged NBFC consumer credit and could re-tighten if unsecured/thin-ticket stress builds — the same lever that enabled the 47% share can reverse it, hitting funding cost and origination appetite.
3. **Dependence on others' rails / thin-ticket concentration.** 82% of originations are sub-₹2 lakh and 58%+ are SuRu — a concentrated, low-margin, income-fragile segment reliant on co-lending/FLDG partners (fintech default guarantees capped ~5%); a demand shock in SuRu households or an FLDG-partner failure transmits directly to NBFC books.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The data confirms a structural handoff of India's thin-ticket NTC market from banks to NBFCs/fintechs — not a cyclical blip — supported by the 2025 RBI easing and SuRu distribution economics. Falsifiable thesis: as long as risk weights stay eased and SuRu vintages hold ~1% range, NBFC NTC share holds/extends ~50%, and the Shriram-type P/B discount to Bajaj narrows (re-rating). What breaks it: a sharp rise in fresh-vintage 90+DPD (watch FY27 seasoning) or an RBI risk-weight reversal — either would compress NBFC NIM and re-expand the bank share. Trigger to watch: next FIDC–TransUnion CIBIL / RBI FSR delinquency print on NTC vintages.
