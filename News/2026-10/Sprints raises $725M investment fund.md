# Context-enrichment: Sprints raises $725M investment fund

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Sprints Capital — a London-based European growth/late-stage VC founded ~2015 (co-founder & managing partner **Henrik Persson**) — has closed its **fifth fund**. The headline in our note ("$725M") is a **currency mis-read**: the fund is **€725 million ≈ $810 million**. So the note title understates it and labels it a generic "investment fund"; it is specifically **Fund V of an established 20-year manager**.
- **Terms/traction (not just a close):** the raise took **~1 year**, and **more than half is already deployed** — i.e. this is a top-up of dry powder for an in-flight strategy, not a cold start. LPs are **endowments, foundations, pension funds and family offices** (classic institutional LP base; no named anchor disclosed).
- **Track record anchoring the PR:** early backer of **Revolut** (initial check 2018), **largest shareholder in Vinted** (also 2018), plus **Checkout.com**, Flightradar24, Hemnet, Vivino, Chrono24, Teamtailor. Firm cites **>€2.5bn invested over two decades**. Recent liquidity event: portfolio co **Fin** (AI customer service) acquired by **Salesforce for $3.6bn** this year; Sprints also ran **secondary equity sales** in the prior year.
- **Why structured this way / what it reveals:** the press frame leans on two famous logos (Revolut, Vinted) to sell a vanilla late-stage close. The genuinely informative detail the PR half-buries: Persson states Sprints has **deliberately NOT backed any "foundational AI" companies** despite record European AI valuations, preferring "solid, well-performing" fundamentals. That is the real editorial signal — a growth investor publicly fading the AI hype cycle while raising — more than the fund number itself.
- **Fintech relevance confirmed:** yes. Sprints explicitly targets digital/software/**fintech**/marketplace; Revolut and Checkout.com are core fintech holdings. Not a MENA/crypto entity (disambiguation resolved — not "Sprints" the agency/sprint accelerator, not the FCA "stablecoin sprint").

## [1] Competitors / peers
Direct comps are European growth/late-stage funds raising in 2025–26, several already in our corpus:
- [[Notion Capital raises $130M for third growth fund]] (Europe, Oct-2025) and [[Illuminate Financial closes $135 million Early Growth Fund]] (fintech-infra specialist, May-2026) — both an order of magnitude smaller; Sprints at €725M plays in the large-growth bracket alongside the likes of Index/Balderton growth vehicles.
- [[ParaFi raises $125 million venture fund for onchain finance]] and [[Air Street Capital closes third fund at $232m for AI]] — thematic (crypto / AI) funds; contrast sharply with Sprints' explicitly **anti-AI-hype, generalist-growth** posture.
- **Position:** Sprints is ahead on **scale and realized track record** (Fin→Salesforce $3.6bn exit, Revolut mark-ups) but is a **sector-agnostic growth** player, not a fintech-dedicated fund. **+ Why / second-order:** closing €725M in ~1 year with >50% deployed, while peers run thematic AI/crypto vehicles, signals LPs still fund disciplined generalist growth managers with DPI — i.e. the gap that matters now is **realized liquidity (DPI), not thesis novelty**. Against the macro base rate, [[Global fintech startups raised $52bn in 2025 venture funding]] shows funding recovering, so a large close is on-trend, not countertrend.

## [2] Company history / fit
Two-decade manager, >€2.5bn deployed, now on Fund V. The 2018 Revolut/Vinted entries are the franchise-making bets; the 2024–25 secondaries plus the Fin/Salesforce exit provided **demonstrable DPI** right before fundraising. **+ Why the firm acts this way:** a late-stage growth firm must periodically refresh dry powder to defend pro-rata in marquee holdings (Revolut's valuation has kept climbing) and to recycle realized gains — so raising Fund V with half pre-deployed is the structurally rational move: it funds follow-on into existing winners more than greenfield bets. The explicit "no foundational AI" stance fits a manager whose edge is **marking up proven consumer/fintech/marketplace names**, not underwriting pre-revenue model labs.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty is low** — this is Fund N of an incumbent, a capital-formation event, not a product, deal, or market-structure change. The value-add signal is **traction/liquidity**: >50% deployed and a $3.6bn exit are real numbers, not announcements. **+ Deeper / who captures margin:** for an LP, the durable question is **DPI vs TVPI** — Sprints can show a cash exit (Fin), which is scarce in 2024–26 and is what let it close in a hard fundraising environment. For the fintech ecosystem, the second-order read is capital-supply: more dry powder earmarked for European late-stage fintech (Revolut/Checkout.com orbit) means continued **follow-on availability for the already-large winners**, reinforcing concentration rather than widening early-stage access.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect continued follow-on into existing winners (Revolut, Vinted, Checkout.com) and selective new large-growth entries; the stated avoidance of foundational AI is a forecastable constraint. **+ Why / counterintuitive:** if the AI-foundation thesis compounds, Sprints' discipline could look like missed upside; if it corrects, Sprints looks vindicated — so this close is a **bet on fundamentals-mean-reversion** as much as a capital event. Second-order risk: a growth fund concentrated in a handful of mega-names is **fragile to a single mark-down** (e.g. a Revolut down-round), the opposite of how a large AUM reads as "safe." Regulatory/macro backdrop is neutral for the fund itself.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**What this is (de-PR).** Sprints (a London-based growth/crossover VC, early Revolut backer and largest shareholder in Vinted) closed its **fifth fund at €725m (~$810m)** on 2026-10-13. Note headline reads "$725M" but the raise is €725m; dollar-equivalent is ~$810m — the number in the title conflates currency. Not fresh dry powder: Sprints says the raise took ~a year and **over half is already deployed** into new and existing portfolio names (per Bloomberg / Dealroom). LPs: endowments, foundations, pension funds and family offices. This is a GP fund close, not an operating-company round — no revenue/valuation/multiple attaches to the firm.

**Sector & drivers.** European VC fundraising is in a late-downcycle rebound. Dry powder sat at ~€73bn ($86bn) at end-2024, but deployment pace fell from the historical ~37%/yr to ~18%/yr over the year to Sep-2024 (per PitchBook, via web). Fund close rates dropped to ~57% in 2025 from ~94% in 2020, and average fund sizes are shrinking even as fund counts rise — i.e. capital is spreading thinner and LP selection is bifurcating toward established brands. Against this, fintech venture funding rose: global fintech startups raised **$52bn in 2025, +27% YoY, on fewer-but-larger deals** ([[Global fintech startups raised $52bn in 2025 venture funding]]). Why now: a reopening exit window (Sprints cites portfolio co Fin acquired by Salesforce for $3.6bn in 2026, plus marks on Revolut/Vinted) is the proof-point LPs needed to re-up; a €725m fifth fund is a flight-to-track-record outcome, not a sign of a broadly easy market.

**Competitive landscape.** VC-fund KPIs run on DPI/TVPI (realized vs paper returns), fund size vs prior vintage, and deployment pace. Sprints is a concentrated, late-stage/growth crossover backer of European category leaders (Revolut, Vinted, Checkout.com, Flightradar24, Fin) — basis of competition is access to a short list of breakout companies, not deal volume. Peers competing for the same European growth/fintech tickets: Notion Capital (software+fintech, $130m Growth Opps III alongside a €300m venture fund — [[Notion Capital raises $130M for third growth fund]]), Illuminate Financial ($135m Early Growth Fund IV, Series B+ AI/fintech — [[Illuminate Financial closes $135 million Early Growth Fund]]), plus larger US crossover funds (Coatue/Tiger-style) at the top of the stack. Position: Sprints is a large-by-European-standards concentrated winner-backer — ahead on brand/access, but with a narrower portfolio than diversified software funds. Moat = reputation + cap-table access to proven European winners (intangible/relationship), easily eroded if marks compress.

**Comps & multiples.** Fund closes don't carry trading multiples — "no data" by design. Size comparison (European fintech/software fund closes in base): Sprints €725m / ~$810m (fund V) vs Notion $130m growth + €300m venture ([[Notion Capital raises $130M for third growth fund]]), Illuminate $135m ([[Illuminate Financial closes $135 million Early Growth Fund]]), ParaFi $125m ([[ParaFi raises $125 million venture fund for onchain finance]]), Air Street $232m for AI ([[Air Street Capital closes third fund at $232m for AI]]). Sprints is **3–6x the size of typical recent European fintech/software fund closes in-base** — consistent with a growth/crossover (larger check) mandate rather than early-stage, so "large" is a function of stage, not an outlier flag. Portfolio marks (Revolut, Vinted valuations) not independently verified here → `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Deployment/vintage risk.** "Over half already deployed" before the close means a large share went to work in 2025–26, a period of still-elevated private marks and a thin exit tape — if the 2026 re-rating stalls, this vintage carries entry-valuation risk that won't show in DPI for years.
2. **Concentration.** A winner-backer model (Revolut/Vinted dominate the story) means fund returns hinge on a handful of names and a few exits; one impaired marquee position (e.g. a Revolut down-round or a stalled Vinted listing) disproportionately swings TVPI.
3. **LP durability in a 57% close-rate market.** Sprints cleared the raise on reputation, but the same backdrop (pension/endowment VC allocations stuck near record lows, slow distributions) pressures re-up pace for fund VI — momentum depends on realizing the Fin-style exits, not just marking them.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` A €725m close with half pre-deployed signals that European growth capital is re-concentrating into a few brand-name GPs rather than broadening — a barbell, not a thaw. Falsifiable thesis: if Sprints' marquee names (Revolut, Vinted) print liquidity events (IPO/secondary) within ~18 months, expect Sprints back in-market with a larger fund VI and copycat large closes; the thesis breaks if the next 2–3 European growth closes stay sub-$200m and distributions stay frozen, implying this was an idiosyncratic track-record win, not a market signal. Watch: Vinted/Revolut liquidity events and Sprints' reported DPI.
