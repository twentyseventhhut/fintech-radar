India, UK agree to explore cross-border equity, bond listings through GIFT IFSC
The agreement was part of the joint statement adopted after the fourth UK-India Financial Markets Dialogue, held in London on 29 September.
New Delhi: India and the UK have agreed to explore ways to boost cross-border investment and Indian firms’ access to international capital, including through equity and bond listings via Gujarat International Finance Tec-City International Financial Services Centre (GIFT IFSC), according to a finance ministry statement.
The agreement was part of the joint statement adopted after the fourth UK-India Financial Markets Dialogue, held in London on 29 September.
The dialogue was led by senior officials from the finance ministry and HM Treasury, with participation from financial services regulatory authorities from India and the UK.
The Indian participants included the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Sebi), Reserve Bank of India (RBI), International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA), Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA), and the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (Irdai). The UK side included the Bank of England (BoE) and Financial Conduct Authority (FCA).
The finance ministry said participants exchanged updates on reforms in their respective jurisdictions and reaffirmed their shared ambition for sustainable economic growth, resilient and open financial markets, and increased bilateral trade and investment.
The discussions covered capital markets, insurance and pensions, asset management and fintech.
On capital markets
The finance ministry said Sebi shared how India is transforming global investor participation by streamlining market entry and operations, while RBI highlighted the significant ease of overseas access to sovereign and corporate debt.
The participants discussed the growing role of GIFT IFSC in mobilizing international capital into India and agreed to continue regulatory cooperation and knowledge-sharing to support its development as an international financial centre, the ministry said.
The two sides also agreed to identify priority areas to explore for future capital markets cooperation ahead of the next Economic and Financial Dialogue, according to the joint statement.
On insurance and pensions
The finance ministry said the UK outlined recent updates to its insurance regulatory framework as part of its Financial Services Growth and Competitiveness Strategy, as well as developments in its pensions and annuity markets.
India outlined its ambitions to expand coverage, investment and market capacity, the ministry said.
Both sides agreed on the importance of the Insurance and Pensions Workplan established in 2023 to support market development, bilateral trade and investment.
The participants recognized the value of competitive and resilient markets, access to specialist expertise, diverse pools of capital and reinsurance capacity in managing complex risks and supporting long-term domestic development, according to the finance ministry.
The two sides also discussed opportunities in reinsurance arising from India's evolving regulatory framework and from deeper cooperation between the London market and Indian insurers and reinsurers.
On pensions and asset management
Participants exchanged experiences on regulation, governance, coverage and long-term savings reform. They discussed measures to increase participation and support productive long-term investment and agreed to explore knowledge sharing in areas aligned with their domestic priorities, the ministry said.
The UK set out work on its regulatory framework for alternative investment fund managers, while India provided an overview of key reforms undertaken by Sebi and the IFSCA.
Both sides welcomed continued regulatory cooperation on asset management and discussed how mutual understanding of reforms, investor requirements and market conditions could support increased cross-border investment.
The two sides also welcomed further internationalization of the Indian rupee and recognized London’s role as the leading offshore rupee trading hub, the ministry said.
On sustainable finance
Participants discussed the importance of credible sustainable finance frameworks, high-quality disclosures, global standards and investor confidence in mobilizing private capital for the low-carbon and climate-resilient transition.
The finance ministry said participants also reviewed progress under the India-UK Financial Partnership asset management workstream and recognized the value of industry evidence in identifying practical opportunities for deeper participation by the UK and Indian asset managers in each other’s markets.
On fintech and innovation, India shared its experience on scaling fintech adoption and financial inclusion through digital public infrastructure, including digital identity, digital payments, central bank digital currencies and data exchange frameworks, according to the joint statement.
Both sides agreed on the importance of supporting responsible innovation and competition without compromising regulatory oversight. They also exchanged perspectives on the potential opportunities and the risks of artificial intelligence in financial services and agreed on the value of continued bilateral exchange and coordination through relevant international fora.
India shared its digital onboarding process for retail financial services and committed to further sharing its experiences, the finance ministry said.
The two sides also noted risks posed by the criminal adoption of new technologies to financial crime, including the use of artificial intelligence to commit fraud on an industrial scale in so-called “scam compounds” operating in foreign countries, as well as risks to cybersecurity, data protection and operational resilience.
Participants shared their experiences using technology and regulatory innovation to detect and prevent financial fraud and agreed to continue cooperation among the relevant authorities, according to the ministry.
Support for G20 Roadmap
On cross-border payments, both sides reaffirmed their support for the G20 Roadmap for Enhancing Cross-Border Payments. They discussed practical priorities for reducing frictions, improving transparency and facilitating greater efficiency in cross-border payments aligned to the G20 Roadmap.
The two sides also updated each other on developments in their retail payment infrastructures and recognised the work at the G20 to continue promoting interoperability and interlinkages among electronic payment infrastructures.
The finance ministry said India and the UK welcomed the constructive discussions and reaffirmed the important role of the Financial Markets Dialogue in supporting their shared ambitions for sustainable growth, deeper trade and investment, and resilient financial markets.
Both sides welcomed the contribution of the India-UK Financial Partnership in identifying practical and potential solutions to deepen financial services links.
The participants also reflected on a strengthened relationship, particularly in the context of the launch of Vision 2035, and the importance of driving implementation of the UK-India Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement, which entered into force on 15 July 2026, according to the joint statement.
The two sides agreed to drive progress across the workstreams, with updates to be reviewed through the relevant bilateral mechanisms and at the next UK-India Economic and Financial Dialogue, the Ministry of Finance said.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** At the **fourth India–UK Financial Markets Dialogue** (London, **29 September 2026**; joint statement published, picked up by Indian press ~7 Oct), India and the UK agreed to *explore* cross-border investment and ways for Indian firms to access international capital — including **equity and bond listings via GIFT IFSC** (Gujarat International Finance Tec-City International Financial Services Centre). This is an intent-level, government-to-government commitment, not a binding listing framework. Indian participants: Ministry of Finance (DEA/DFS), SEBI, RBI, IFSCA, PFRDA, IRDAI; UK side: HM Treasury, Bank of England, FCA.

**[1] Why it matters.** GIFT IFSC is India's offshore financial gateway and the policy channel through which Indian companies can raise foreign capital without a full overseas (e.g. US/UK main-market) listing. Linking it to the UK — London being the leading offshore rupee trading hub — gives Indian issuers a credible international venue and gives UK capital a regulated on-ramp into India. The Dialogue sits directly under the **UK–India CETA**, which entered into force **15 July 2026**, and the newly launched **Vision 2035** bilateral roadmap, so it is part of a broader, now-operational trade architecture rather than a standalone announcement.

**[2] Context / background.** Direct listing of Indian public companies on GIFT IFSC's international exchanges (**India INX** and **NSE IX / NSE IFSC**) was first unveiled in 2020, stalled over tax/FEMA concerns, and was formally permitted by the Finance Ministry in **January 2024** — but SEBI operating guidelines have remained pending (NSE IX has repeatedly pressed SEBI to finalize them). GIFT IFSC has scaled sharply: ~1,336 IFSCA registrations by mid-2026 (10x in five years), IFSC banking assets ~USD 107–121bn, fund commitments ~USD 45bn, 37 banking units. The India–UK capital-markets track is long-running: LSEG–NSE MoUs date back to 2018 (Masala-bond dual-listing route). See prior vault coverage: [[GIFT City launches real-time forex settlement system]] (FCSS cutting FX settlement to ~30s, Oct 2025) and [[IFSCA plans SPVs for insurance-linked securities]] (IFSCA building out GIFT product suite, Mar 2026).

**[3] Dialogue also covered** (beyond listings): reinsurance / London-market cooperation under the 2023 Insurance & Pensions Workplan; asset management (AIFM frameworks, cross-border funds); further **internationalization of the rupee** with London as the offshore hub; sustainable-finance disclosures; fintech/DPI knowledge-sharing (UPI, digital ID, CBDC); AI-enabled fraud / "scam compounds" risks; and support for the **G20 Roadmap on cross-border payments**. Next review point: the UK–India Economic and Financial Dialogue (EFD).

**[4] What to watch.** (a) **Concrete follow-through already appearing** — on **13 October 2026, NSE IX and London Stock Exchange signed an MoU** to explore dual/cross-listings, cross-border trading connectivity, ETFs, and FTSE Russell-linked derivatives on NSE IX; this is the operational counterpart to this government-level intent. (b) Whether **SEBI finally issues the pending direct-listing operating guidelines** — the binding constraint on actual issuance. (c) First real issuers / bond tranches via GIFT IFSC into UK capital. (d) Rupee-internationalization and reinsurance deliverables at the next EFD.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team / challenge questions**

1. **Is this actually news or just a communiqué?** The joint statement says "agreed to *explore*" — soft, non-binding language. Does this materially change anything, or is it standard bilateral-dialogue boilerplate repackaged by Indian press?
2. **What's the binding constraint?** Direct listing on GIFT IFSC was permitted in Jan 2024, yet SEBI's operating guidelines are still pending in Oct 2026. Does this Dialogue do anything to unblock SEBI, or does it sidestep the real bottleneck?
3. **Date integrity.** Note dates the event 2026-10-12; the Dialogue was 29 Sep 2026 and press carried it ~7 Oct. Is the 12 Oct date just the digest-capture date, not the event date? (Yes — worth flagging the lag.)
4. **Zero issuance to date.** Has *any* Indian company actually done a direct equity/bond listing on India INX or NSE IX since 2024? If the headline product has no takers, is "UK linkage" meaningful?
5. **Relationship to the 13 Oct NSE IX–LSE MoU.** Is this note double-counting the same storyline? (No — different date, exchanges vs. governments — but the MoU is the substantive event; is the government intent the weaker of the two?)
6. **Why GIFT, not a London main-market listing?** CETA could enable direct LSE listings; routing via GIFT adds a layer. Is GIFT a facilitator or a protectionist detour that keeps regulation under IFSCA/SEBI?
7. **Capital-flow direction.** Is this about UK capital into India, Indian issuers raising in GBP, or two-way? The incentives and FEMA/tax treatment differ sharply by direction — the note conflates them.
8. **Tax/FEMA overhang.** The 2020 plan stalled over tax-leakage fears. Have capital-gains/withholding and FEMA round-tripping concerns been resolved, or do they still deter issuers?
9. **Liquidity reality.** GIFT IFSC exchanges' traded value is dominated by derivatives (Nifty contracts migrated from SGX). Is there any genuine primary-equity or corporate-bond liquidity to support cross-border listings?
10. **UK-side appetite.** LSEG has said it's "open" to Indian dual listings — but is there real UK investor demand for GIFT-listed Indian paper vs. just ADR/GDR routes, or is this aspirational?
11. **Rupee internationalization claim.** "London as leading offshore rupee hub" — is that verifiable, or diplomatic framing? What volumes back it?
12. **Durability.** Dialogues produce workplans that frequently lapse. What accountability/deadline exists (EFD review) and has prior Dialogue output (2023 Insurance Workplan) actually delivered?
13. **Source concentration.** Core text is a single Livemint reproduction of a MoF statement. Any independent/UK-side (HMT/FCA) confirmation with different emphasis?
14. **Geopolitical framing.** Does GIFT-via-UK also serve as a hedge/alternative to US capital-market access amid trade friction, and is that an unstated driver?
15. **Fintech/AI sidebars.** The statement bundles UPI/DPI, CBDC, AI-fraud, G20 payments — is the "cross-border listing" headline overweighting one line of a broad catch-all communiqué?

**Importance: 3/5** — A substantive, well-sourced bilateral milestone on India's offshore capital-markets integration, reinforced within days by a concrete NSE IX–LSE MoU and sitting under the live CETA/Vision 2035 architecture. Downgraded from higher because the headline is "agreed to explore" (non-binding), the operative bottleneck (pending SEBI direct-listing guidelines) is untouched, and actual issuance via GIFT IFSC remains near-zero — real impact is contingent on execution still to come.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a regulatory/capital-markets-infrastructure story, not a deal: India and the UK agreed at the 4th Financial Markets Dialogue (London, 29 Sep 2026) only to *explore* cross-border equity/bond listings via GIFT IFSC — no binding mechanism, no issuer, no timeline. Sizing GIFT IFSC's capital-markets base: the debt side is mature — nearly $85bn raised cumulatively through bonds listed at GIFT IFSC (incl. ~$18bn green/social/sustainable), with Indian banks raising ~$11.12bn via IFSC bond listings Apr–Aug 2026 (per Cafemutual/India-Briefing, as of Sep–Oct 2026). Fund side: GIFT IFSC fund commitments topped $45bn in Apr–Jun 2026; 235 fund-management entities by end-June (per Cafemutual, as of Jun 2026). Equity side is nascent: India's *first* equity IPO from GIFT City launched only 6 Mar 2026 (XED Executive Development, a ~$12m USD-denominated offering on NSE IX, per BusinessToday/giftcfo). Structure: a state-sponsored offshore IFSC with two exchanges (India INX, NSE IX), single regulator (IFSCA), tax-advantaged SEZ. Barriers are regulatory, not capital: direct overseas listing for Indian cos has been permissible since the 2024 Direct Listing Scheme, but **only unlisted public companies** — SEBI's framework for *already-listed* Indian cos to dual-list overseas is still being finalised (per mondaq/EY, as of 2026). Why now: UK-India CETA entered into force 15 Jul 2026 and "Vision 2035" — the dialogue rides that political tailwind; London is recognised as the leading offshore-rupee trading hub (per the joint statement).

**Competitive landscape.** KPIs for an IFSC: capital raised (bond vs equity $bn), number of listings/fund entities, secondary-market liquidity/turnover (the binding constraint here). Players — GIFT IFSC competes with established offshore listing/funding venues: Singapore (SGX), Dubai (DIFC/Nasdaq Dubai), and London itself (LSE) for the same Indian-issuer flow; within GIFT, India INX vs NSE IX. Basis of competition: liquidity depth, investor access, cost/tax, regulatory credibility — not price. Recent moves: direct-listing-without-IPO framework being readied by IFSCA (Pradeep Ramakrishnan, ED IFSCA, auto-onboarding of listed cos, per inkl, Oct 2026); GIFT City's own broader buildout (USD debit card via ICICI/Visa [[ICICI Bank and Visa launch India's first USD debit card from GIFT City]] May 2026; real-time FX settlement [[GIFT City launches real-time forex settlement system]] Oct 2025; multi-currency ULIP [[Ageas Federal launches Aurum Advantage multi-currency ULIP at GIFT City]] Oct 2026). GIFT's position (analysis): dominant on debt/funds, a *fledgling* on equity — ahead of no one on equity liquidity yet. Moat (analysis): sovereign backing + tax regime + sole-regulator simplicity (intangible/regulatory moat), but no network-effect moat on equity because there is almost no float or secondary liquidity.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/deal in the news — no issuer, no multiple to compute. **Multiples = no data (not applicable: this is a bilateral MOU-style exploration, not a financing).** Internal comps (base precedents for the GIFT/IFSC capital-markets theme, all `[[wikilink]]`): [[SEBI proposes letting bond platforms offer GIFT City foreign debt]] (May 2026 — domestic access to GIFT debt), [[Decentro secures IFSCA payment licence at GIFT City]] and [[Skydo gets in-principle cross-border payment licence at GIFT City]] (May 2026 — IFSCA licensing momentum), [[ICICI Bank and Visa launch India's first USD debit card from GIFT City]] (May 2026), [[Ageas Federal launches Aurum Advantage multi-currency ULIP at GIFT City]] (Oct 2026), [[GIFT City launches real-time forex settlement system]] (Oct 2025). Pattern across the base: steady institution/product build-out at GIFT, heavily debt- and licence-led; equity-listing flow is conspicuously absent — consistent with the "early stage" read. Distribution not computed (no comparable figures).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Announcement vs reality gap.** "Agreed to explore" / "identify priority areas" — no mechanism, issuer or deadline. Second-order: easy to over-read as a cross-listing corridor when it is a workplan line item; execution risk is the dominant risk.
2. **Equity liquidity void.** Debt at ~$85bn vs equity essentially at one ~$12m IPO (Mar 2026). Why it matters: cross-border *equity* listings need a secondary market and a domestic investor base — yet resident Indians are barred from investing directly in GIFT IFSC equity listings (per IFSCA/getbelong), so float and price discovery stay thin; a listing venue without liquidity does not attract issuers.
3. **Regulatory incompleteness.** SEBI has not yet finalised rules for already-listed Indian cos to list overseas; scheme today is unlisted-public-cos-only. Second-order: the large, obvious candidates (listed Indian corporates) cannot use the GIFT/UK route until SEBI acts — gating any UK-India equity flow on a pending rulebook.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Incrementally positive for GIFT IFSC's *equity* ambitions — a political endorsement that could pull UK institutional demand and a London↔GIFT dual-listing track — but the thesis is falsifiable and gated on plumbing, not politics. Watch for: (a) SEBI's final framework for listed-company overseas listing, and (b) the *next* UK-India Economic & Financial Dialogue for a concrete mechanism or pilot issuer. Thesis breaks if, 6–12 months out, there is still no listed-company framework and no named cross-border issuer — in which case this remains a communiqué, not a corridor.
