Bitget Acquires Licensed Exchange To Expand into Thailand 
 Victoria, Seychelles, Octob er 20, 2026 — Bitget , the world's largest Universal Exchange (UEX), has announced the planned acquisition of GMO-Z.com Cryptonomics (Thailand) Co., Ltd., a Bangkok-based digital asset exchange and brokerage, through its interest in BTGT Management (Thailand) Ltd.
Under the proposed transaction, GMO Internet, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiary, GMO-Z.com Holdings (Thailand) Co., Ltd., amongst others, will transfer their entire shareholding in GMO-Z.com Cryptonomics (Thailand) to BTGT Management (Thailand) Ltd. and/or its designated entity at completion. The completion of the sale and purchase agreement is subject to applicable regulatory approval, among other conditions.
 GMO-Z.com Cryptonomics (Thailand) operates a licensed digital asset exchange and brokerage in Thailand. The proposed acquisition provides an opportunity for Bitget to establish a stronger operational presence in Thailand, drawing on the local company's existing business experience and market knowledge.
The transaction comes as Bitget continues to evaluate opportunities for expansion across Southeast Asia, where demand for digital financial services is developing alongside evolving regulatory frameworks. Bitget's approach focuses on establishing local operating capabilities that can support long-term market participation and respond to jurisdiction-specific requirements.
 “Building a presence in a market requires local expertise, an understanding of the regulatory environment and a long-term commitment to the market,” said Gracy Chen, CEO of Bitget. “Thailand is an important part of Southeast Asia's digital asset landscape, and this acquisition gives us a great opportunity to establish local operations in a thriving country. Our priority is to develop the right operational foundation while bringing Bitget's global experience and capabilities to the market.” 
The proposed acquisition will enable Bitget to establish a locally operated exchange in Thailand, leveraging the existing business's local market expertise and operational experience. Following completion of the acquisition and receipt of the necessary regulatory approval for the change of control, Bitget intends to introduce its trading platform to Thai users, bringing its world-class trading capabilities and experience to the local market while operating in accordance with applicable Thai regulations.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Deal in one line.** Bitget (Seychelles-registered "Universal Exchange", ~125M claimed users) is buying GMO-Z.com Cryptonomics (Thailand) — operator of the SEC-licensed *Z.com EX* exchange/brokerage — via its stake in BTGT Management (Thailand) Ltd. GMO Internet and GMO-Z.com Holdings (Thailand) transfer their entire ~99.94% holding; close targeted for Oct 31, 2026, subject to Thai SEC change-of-control approval. Terms undisclosed. (Bitget blog Oct 20; GMO Internet disclosure Sep 24; Blockhead, Cryptobriefing, Bitcoin.com — Oct 2026.)

**Why this is a buy-the-license deal, not buy-the-business.** GMO Internet announced in **March 2026** it would exit Thai digital assets, citing the Thai market environment; this sale completes that retreat. Multiple outlets describe the target as **loss-making** (Z.com EX launched 2021, never scaled against Bitkub/local incumbents). Bitget's own rationale ("local expertise, regulatory environment, long-term commitment") confirms the asset being bought is the **Thai SEC license + local operating entity**, not customer book or revenue. This mirrors the standard crypto-exchange playbook of acquiring a licensed shell to shortcut market entry rather than applying de novo.

**Sector context — license-acquisition is the SE Asia entry pattern.** Scarce national licenses + "must be a locally incorporated, SEC-supervised entity" rules make M&A the fastest legal on-ramp. In-base and web comps:
- **KuCoin → ERX (Thailand), 2025** — launched SEC-licensed Thai exchange *via acquisition of ERX*, the exact same structure Bitget is now using. Closest direct comp.
- **Binance → Gopax (South Korea), closed Oct 2025** — acquiring a licensed domestic exchange to enter a gated market; change-of-control approval was the long pole.
- **OSL Group → Koinsayang (Indonesia), Sep 2025** [[News/2025-09/OSL Group completes acquisition of Indonesian exchange Koinsayang]] — "gained regulatory approval," license-led entry.
- **Robinhood → Buana Capital + PT Pedagang Aset Kripto (Indonesia), Dec 2025** [[News/2025-12/Robinhood acquires Indonesian brokerage Buana Capital]] — dual license (securities + crypto) acquisition for Indonesia entry.
- **Paribu → CoinMENA ($240M), Dec 2025** [[News/2025-12/Paribu acquires crypto exchange CoinMENA for $240M]] — Türkiye exchange buying a VARA/MENA-licensed entity to expand region.
- **Bithumb + SSI Digital (Vietnam), May 2026** [[News/2026-05/Bithumb partners with SSI Digital on Vietnam crypto exchange]] — same thesis via partnership rather than buyout ("scarce licenses, scrutiny at home").
- **MEXC Ventures → Triv ($200M val, Indonesia), Aug 2025** [[News/2025-08/MEXC Ventures backs Indonesia's Triv at $200M valuation]] — invest-to-enter variant.

Thailand specifically: the market is dominated by **Bitkub** (weighing a Hong Kong IPO per Bloomberg Nov 2025), with KuCoin (via ERX) and others fighting for the #2–3 tier. Bitget enters as a late, licensed-but-sub-scale challenger.

**Thai regulatory texture.** Thai law requires a locally incorporated company holding an SEC digital-asset license; advisers note no mandated Thai shareholding ratio (so ~100% foreign ownership is practically achievable), but the Aug 2026 foreign-business reform did **not** cover digital-asset businesses, and the Ministry of Commerce / SEC are now looking through holding structures to ultimate control/financing (nominee shareholders ruled out). The deal's completion therefore genuinely hinges on SEC change-of-control sign-off — not a formality given the buyer profile below.

**Risk flags (skeptical read):**
1. **Timing vs. the $388M hack.** This is the dominant framing in nearly every outlet ("weeks after $388M hack"). Bitget suffered a **~$387.5M theft** via a third-party security product's zero-day (malicious activity from Aug 31, fraudulent withdrawals Sep 24, 2026); Bitget says it absorbed the loss, users unharmed, cold wallets/private keys uncompromised, and it rebuilt a ~$309M protection fund. Still, a regulator weighing change-of-control approval for a licensed local exchange, right after the acquirer's largest-ever security breach, is a material approval and reputational risk. Watch whether Thai SEC conditions or delays the transfer.
2. **Deal certainty / timeline risk.** Terms undisclosed, close contingent on SEC approval, Oct 31 is a *target* not a certainty. "Through its interest in BTGT Management" is a layered structure — the degree of Bitget control vs. a designated entity is not fully transparent, and Thai scrutiny of holding-company control could complicate it.
3. **Quality of the acquired asset.** Target is a **loss-making, sub-scale** exchange GMO is actively exiting; Bitget is buying regulatory optionality, not a franchise. Migrating/retaining Z.com EX users to Bitget's platform is unproven; incumbent Bitkub is far ahead.
4. **Counterparty/seller signal.** A diversified Japanese operator (GMO) concluded the Thai DA market wasn't worth staying in. Bitget is betting it can monetize where an incumbent couldn't — execution and SE-Asia-wide thesis remain to be demonstrated.
5. **Disclosure asymmetry.** No price, no expected contribution, no user-count for the target disclosed — hard to size value created; treat strategic ("foothold") narrative as the only concrete deliverable.

**Net.** Strategically coherent, low-disclosure, license-led entry into a Bitkub-dominated market, executed under the shadow of a nine-figure hack. The binding variable is Thai SEC change-of-control approval; everything else (price, synergies, user migration) is unquantified.
