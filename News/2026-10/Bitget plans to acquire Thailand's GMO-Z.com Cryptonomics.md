# Bitget plans Thai crypto exchange acquisition weeks after $388M hack

Latest NewsPublished Oct 20, 2026

The proposed acquisition would give Bitget a route into Thailand’s crypto market through a locally licensed operator.

Copy link Copied to clipboard Copy link

Crypto exchange Bitget has announced plans to acquire Thailand’s GMO-Z.com Cryptonomics, seeking to expand its Southeast Asia footprint weeks after a major hack impacting its hot and warm wallets.

The proposed transaction involves BTGT Management (Thailand), a company in which Bitget says it holds an interest, according to an announcement shared with Cointelegraph. The acquisition will allow Bitget to introduce its trading platform to Thai users.

“Thailand is an important part of Southeast Asia’s digital asset landscape, and this acquisition gives us a great opportunity to establish local operations in a thriving country,” said Bitget CEO Gracy Chen.

GMO Internet disclosed the proposed sale to BTGT Management in a Sept. 24 filing, targeting completion for Oct. 31. Bitget’s announcement identifies its involvement in the transaction.

The company declined to comment on how much interest in BTGT Management the company holds and the financial and ownership terms of the proposed acquisition.

GMO-Z.com Cryptonomics operates Z.com EX, which launched in February 2021. Thailand’s Securities and Exchange Commission lists the company among its licensed digital asset exchange operators.

_**Related:**__**Thailand crypto ETFs may see slow start despite new rules: CoinShares**_

Completion remains subject to regulatory approval and other conditions.

## **Thai expansion comes weeks after hack**

The proposed deal follows a Sept. 24 attack on Bitget’s hot and warm wallet infrastructure. In itsaccount of the breach, Bitget said approximately $388 million was affected after attackers exploited a vulnerability in a third-party security product and issued fraudulent withdrawal commands.

Bitget said customer account balances were unaffected and that it completed the phased restoration of withdrawals on Oct. 2.

On Oct. 17, Bitget alsoannounced plans to discontinue services for certain users, describing the move as a “one-off exercise.”

Bitget’s terms of use, updated Oct. 16, list Russia, Belarus and Kazakhstan among its prohibited jurisdictions. Thailand is also listed as a prohibited jurisdiction, although the proposed acquisition could provide Bitget with a route to serving the market through a locally licensed operator.

# Context-enrichment: Bitget plans to acquire Thailand's GMO-Z.com Cryptonomics
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On Oct. 20, 2026 Bitget confirmed it is the party behind **BTGT Management (Thailand) Co., Ltd.** — a vehicle "in which Bitget holds an interest" — that is buying **GMO-Z.com Cryptonomics (Thailand)**, operator of the SEC-licensed exchange/brokerage **Z.com EX** (live since Feb 2021). The seller, Japan's **GMO Internet Group**, disclosed the deal in a **Sept. 24, 2026 filing**; completion targeted **Oct. 31, 2026**, subject to a Thai SEC change-of-control approval. GMO + its Thai holding sub transfer their combined **99.94%** stake.

De-PR'd reality — several things the "Southeast Asia expansion" framing buries:
- **This is a licence acquisition, not a business acquisition.** GMO's own Sept. 24 filing shows the target did **~11M baht revenue (~$0.34M) and a net loss of 87.6M baht (~$2.7M) in 2025**, and GMO is "withdrawing from the business" given Thailand's market environment. Bitget is buying a distressed, sub-scale, loss-making operator — the asset of value is the **Thai SEC digital-asset exchange + brokerage licence**, not the user base or revenue.
- **Price undisclosed "at the buyer's request"**, and Bitget **declined to state its % ownership of BTGT Management**. For a shell with 2M baht (~$62k) registered capital, created **Sept. 22, 2026** (two days before GMO's filing), the opacity is the story: Bitget is structuring entry through a local vehicle it won't fully claim, likely to satisfy Thai foreign-ownership/change-of-control optics while keeping economic control.
- **Timing vs the hack is not incidental.** The deal lands ~4 weeks after the **~$388M Sept. 24/25 wallet breach** ([[SlowMist and Mandiant trace how Bitget $387.5M hack happened]]) and days after Bitget listed **Thailand itself as a prohibited jurisdiction** (terms updated Oct. 16) and exited Russia/Belarus/Kazakhstan ([[Bitget fully halts service to Russian residents]]). So Bitget is simultaneously (a) banned from serving Thais via bitget.com and (b) buying a Thai licence to serve them legally — i.e. the acquisition is the **compliance workaround** to the extraterritorial enforcement that pushed it out.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Thai licensed retail exchanges: **Bitkub** (dominant local leader, highest SEC-reported volume), **Gulf Binance** (Binance JV with Gulf Energy — the template for "global brand + local licence"), **Bitazza**, **Zipmex** (restructuring). GMO's Z.com EX was a marginal also-ran (hence the exit). Peer M&A: [[DV8 acquires Thai crypto custody firm Rakkar Digital]] (Mar 2026, custody, THB 100M) — same thesis of foreign/local capital buying regulated Thai crypto infrastructure.

- **Position: catching up via the Gulf Binance playbook, but from behind.** Binance already solved the Thai problem in 2023–24 through Gulf Binance; Bitget is doing the same move **two-plus years later and with a weaker asset** (buying a withdrawing operator vs. building a JV with a listed energy conglomerate). Bitkub keeps the local scale and trust moat.
- **Why the landscape is this way:** Thailand's April 2025 royal decrees + **June 28, 2026 extraterritorial enforcement** deadline forced every offshore exchange soliciting Thais to hold a Thai licence or exit. That turned licences into scarce, acquirable assets — which is why distressed licensees (GMO) find buyers (Bitget) and why the premium here is **regulatory-access, not franchise value**.

## [2] Company history / fit
Bitget's 2025–26 arc is relentless jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction licensing and local rails: Georgia licence (2025), Vienna MiCA hub ([[Bitget taps ex-Bitpanda Legal Chief Oliver Stauber to build Vienna MiCA hub]]), Mexico SAT/UIF registration ([[Bitget completes Mexico regulatory registrations with SAT and UIF]]), plus a large Bitget Wallet payments footprint across APAC/LatAm. Thailand fits that compliance-expansion logic cleanly.

- **Why act now, this way:** the structural pressure is **enforcement, not opportunity**. Bitget was effectively being locked out of Thailand (listed as prohibited); the only legal path is a local licensed entity. Buying a dormant licence is faster/cheaper than a de-novo application, so a distressed GMO unit is the path of least resistance. The hack adds a **reputational/regulatory-repair** incentive: demonstrating a "regulated, compliant local operator" narrative weeks after a $388M breach is a credibility hedge.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Nothing technologically novel — it is a **licence-and-market-access buy**. Real traction to date = near-zero: a loss-making, ~$0.34M-revenue operator the seller is abandoning.

- **Where value is (or isn't):** value-add is real **only if** Bitget actually migrates its liquidity/products onto the Thai licence and converts Thai users — i.e. uses Z.com EX as a shell to relaunch locally. Until the SEC approves change-of-control and Bitget deploys, this is an **announced option, not live traction**. The margin in the Thai stack is captured by whoever holds the licence + local banking/baht on-ramps; Bitget is buying the licence but the **open question is whether a post-hack, recently-banned foreign brand can win Thai trust vs Bitkub/Gulf Binance**.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch: (1) **Thai SEC change-of-control approval** — the binary gate; a post-hack applicant is not an easy approval; (2) whether completion actually hits **Oct. 31**; (3) Bitget disclosing its BTGT stake and whether it rebrands Z.com EX; (4) reconciling the "Thailand = prohibited jurisdiction" terms with a Thai consumer launch.

- **Why it may go slower than framed (analysis):** regulators approving a change-of-control to an entity that just suffered a $388M breach and is opaque about ownership is a genuine risk — the deal could slip or attach conditions. Second-order: if approved, this is a template signal that **distressed Thai licences are now the cheapest entry route** for sanctioned/banned offshore exchanges, which could draw SEC scrutiny of "licence-laundering" through local shells.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Thailand's regulated crypto-exchange market is small but growing: valued at ~$0.58bn in 2025 and projected to ~$3.18bn by 2034 at a ~20.8% CAGR (per TheReportCubes market report, via web, as of 2026 — secondary/paywalled vendor figure, treat as indicative, not audited). Adoption is high relative to market size: Thailand's SEC reported >7m domestic digital-asset holders in Q1 2026, ~1 in 10 Thai adults (per Thai SEC, via web). The structure is a licensed, concentrated regime — as of July 2026 the SEC listed only 7 licensed DA Exchanges (Bitkub, Gulf Binance, Orbix Trade, TDX, Upbit, WAANX, and Z.comex — GMO-Z's venue), so a license is itself the entry barrier. "Why now": Thailand is a *prohibited jurisdiction* in Bitget's own terms of use (updated Oct. 16, 2026, per Cointelegraph), so Bitget cannot serve Thai users directly — buying a licensed operator is the only compliant route to market. The deal thus buys regulatory access, not technology or liquidity. Headwind to note: the Thai market was *cooling* in mid-2026 — avg daily trading value fell -27% and active accounts -22% in July 2026 (per Cryptomind, via web), so Bitget is buying into a soft cycle.

**Competitive landscape.** Exchange KPIs: spot/derivatives volume & market share, number of licensed markets/jurisdictions, token listings (Thailand's licensed universe = ~698 tokens as of July 2026). Key licensed Thai players: Bitkub (dominant local incumbent), Gulf Binance (Binance's licensed local JV), Upbit, Z.comex (the target). Basis of competition locally = license + local liquidity + THB on/off-ramp, not global brand. Bitget's global position: pre-hack a top-5 global CEX, ~7.7% of top-10 spot volume in Q3 2026 and historically strong in derivatives/copy-trading (per CoinGecko/investingintheweb, via web) — but it *fell out of the top 10 by spot volume* in Q3 2026 after the Sept. 24 hack. Protagonist position: globally mid-pack and currently wounded; in Thailand a *new entrant* buying its way to incumbency via Z.comex's license. Moat (analysis): the acquired asset's moat is the license itself (regulatory scarcity — only 7 exist); Bitget's own global moat (derivatives depth, copy trading) does not transfer until the platform is localized and approved.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal terms undisclosed — GMO Internet and GMO-Z.com Holdings (Thailand) sell a combined ~99.94% stake to BTGT Management (Thailand) for an undisclosed sum, target close Oct. 31 (per Cointelegraph/Yahoo/crypto.news, via web). **EV/Revenue, P/S = no data** (no price, no Z.comex financials disclosed). Internal comps — crypto-exchange / licensed-local-access M&A in base:
- [[Binance completes acquisition of South Korea's Gopax]] — nearest comp: Binance buying a licensed local exchange purely for regulated market access (terms also undisclosed).
- [[DV8 acquires Thai crypto custody firm Rakkar Digital]] — same geography (Thailand DA), THB 100m / ~$3m for a custody firm — a loose read-across on Thai DA asset size (custody, not exchange; different model).
- [[Kraken acquires exchange in $100M derivatives deal]], [[Coinbase acquires onchain capital platform Echo for $375M]] — priced crypto-exchange/infra M&A, but different models/geos (comparability weak).
- [[Coinone may be up for sale as Coinbase targets South Korea]] — same "buy a licensed Asian local exchange for access" pattern.
Distribution not computed — too few comparable *priced* figures and the subject deal is undisclosed; qualitative read only: the Binance/Gopax and Coinbase/Coinone pattern (undisclosed or modest tags for a license-plus-local-book) suggests this is an access buy, not a scale buy — consistent with no financial terms disclosed.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory approval / change-of-control is the whole deal** — completion (targeted Oct. 31) is explicitly subject to Thai SEC approval of the control change. A Thai regulator may scrutinize a buyer that *just* suffered a ~$388m security breach (Sept. 24) and is simultaneously *exiting* users (Oct. 17 discontinuation, Russia/Belarus/Kazakhstan bans Oct. 16) — approval is not a formality; second-order effect: deal slip or conditions could strand the Southeast Asia entry.
2. **Hack-contaminated acquirer reputation & liability** — buying a licensed, verified-identity Thai venue weeks after a hot/warm-wallet exploit imports Bitget's security track record into a regulated market; if Thai users/regulators associate Z.comex with Bitget's breach, the license's value (trust) erodes — the opposite of what's being bought.
3. **Undisclosed terms + soft local market** — no price/ownership disclosed and Bitget "declined to comment" on its stake in BTGT Management; buying into a Thai market that was contracting (-27% daily value, July 2026) with opaque economics raises execution/overpay risk that can't be checked against comps.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is consolidation-by-license: global CEXs (Binance→Gopax, Coinbase→Coinone talks, now Bitget→Z.comex) are increasingly *buying* their way into gated Asian markets rather than applying organically, because the license — not the tech — is scarce (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if the deal closes on/near Oct. 31 and Bitget launches localized Thai trading within ~2 quarters, expect more prohibited-jurisdiction CEXs to pursue licensed-target buyouts in ASEAN. What breaks it: a Thai SEC rejection or extended conditions on the control change (watch for an SEC/GMO Internet filing after Oct. 31) — that would signal regulators are using M&A review to keep hack-tainted offshore players out, chilling the pattern.
