_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Headline is misattributed: it was **MTS**, not Yandex, that built the network — Yandex is the first customer/consumer. MTS deployed a GNSS RTK (Real Time Kinematic) high-precision positioning network along four federal highways — M-11 "Neva", M-4 "Don", M-12 "Vostok", TsKAD — totalling "over 4,000 km", plus five cities (Moscow, St. Petersburg, Nizhny Novgorod, Kazan, Voronezh). **Live today:** M-4 to Voronezh, M-12 to Kazan. **Announced/future:** M-4 to Novorossiysk by end-2026; M-12 to Tyumen by 2028; all million-plus cities + logistics hubs by 2030.
- Mechanism: reference (base) stations mounted on MTS telecom towers receive GLONASS/GPS/Galileo/BeiDou signals; corrections sharpen the raw 5–15 m fix to **2–5 cm**. On-board inertial systems (INS) carry the vehicle through connectivity gaps; map-matching against HD road maps gives orientation at "zero visibility".
- **Why framed this way:** the "first in Russia" claim is heavily caveated in a footnote — "first by coverage area **with open SLA** guaranteed by a telecom operator." Russia already has broad commercial RTK/CORS coverage (RTKNet since 2013; ~thousands of CORS stations), so the genuine novelty is a **carrier-grade SLA on a highway corridor**, not RTK itself. (analysis)
- The sovereign-stack framing ("fully domestic solution", "sovereign technology stack for driverless transport") is political positioning, not a technical claim.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Global AV positioning stack:** Waymo leans on lidar + HD maps for localization, treating GNSS as one input; Tesla rejects HD maps/lidar entirely (vision-only). RTK-GNSS vendors — **Swift Navigation** (ASIL-certified corrections, automotive-grade, used by OEMs), **Trimble/Applanix** (map-based localization fusing RTK-GNSS + IMU + SLAM), **Point One Navigation** (RTK correction networks) — sell positioning as a component, not bundled with a carrier. So MTS's edge is the **telecom-operator SLA + tower real estate**, not the RTK math, which is a commodity. (analysis)
- **Domestic:** RTKNet and Roscosmos/Rostelecom-adjacent CORS networks already blanket populated Russia. MTS's differentiation is corridor-focused, SLA-backed service pitched specifically at AV operators.
- **Known limitation across all RTK:** urban-canyon NLOS/multipath degrades RTK sharply — which is exactly why Waymo-class systems do not rely on GNSS alone and why MTS pairs it with INS + HD maps. Position: MTS is at **parity on the underlying tech, ahead on packaging/coverage-with-SLA** in Russia.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Yandex** runs the robotruck program: self-driving trucks on M-4/M-11/TsKAD since commercial hauling began ~May 2025 (~130,000 km covered per press), brand "Robotruck" patented Feb 2026, ~70 trucks planned for 2026, clients incl. Yandex Market and Magnit; robotaxi ambitions (>1,000 by 2030). Russia's experimental legal regime (EPR) for cabin-less trucks extended to 2028. The geopositioning layer fits directly — the exact same highways (M-4, M-11, M-12, TsKAD) carry the robotrucks. See [[Yandex Lavka expands robotic delivery to 130 dark stores]] for the adjacent last-mile autonomy push.
- **MTS** is pivoting from a telco into an infrastructure/B2B platform (see [[MTS bundles enterprise solutions under MTS CorpTech brand]]); it sold 49.9% of its tower company in 2026 ([[MTS sells 49.9% stake in its tower company]]) — yet the RTK play **monetizes those same towers** as reference-station sites, a logical way to extract new ARPU from legacy tower assets. (analysis)
- **Why MTS acts this way:** commodity connectivity ARPU is flat → MTS needs higher-margin platform/B2B revenue → RTK-as-a-service on existing towers is near-zero-marginal-cost infrastructure monetization. Yandex gets to avoid building its own CORS network.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Real and live, but narrow.** The deployed part (M-4→Voronezh, M-12→Kazan) is actual infrastructure, not a pilot — this is traction, not a press promise. But it is an **enabling layer** for a single anchor customer (Yandex), not a standalone product with multiple paying users yet. MTS says it "plans" to extend to other logistics players and "is considering" international replication — both unconfirmed.
- **Where margin sits:** the defensible asset is the tower footprint + SLA, not RTK corrections (commoditized globally and domestically). If Yandex's robotrucks instead lean more on lidar/HD-maps/INS (as Waymo does), the RTK layer becomes a redundancy/safety augment rather than the primary localizer — which caps how much MTS can charge. The Yandex quote ("MTS reference-station data **complements** our on-board localization") confirms RTK is a supplement, not the core. (analysis) That reframes the central question from "did Yandex get precise positioning?" to **"how much of the AV localization stack does MTS actually own vs. merely augment?"**

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Expansion roadmap is concrete to 2030 but back-loaded; near-term value hinges entirely on Yandex's robotruck fleet scaling (~70 trucks in 2026 is tiny). The EPR extension to 2028 is the real enabler — regulation, not positioning, is the binding constraint on Russian AV hauling.
- **Second-order:** a single carrier owning the SLA-backed positioning corridor for all domestic AV freight is a potential choke point / rent-extraction layer if the market scales — analogous to owning the toll road's nav layer. But concentration on one customer (Yandex) today makes the business fragile, not safe: if Yandex builds/buys its own corrections or shifts to vision-heavy stacks, demand evaporates. (hypothesis)
- International-replication talk should be discounted — Russian telecom + sovereign-stack framing limits export markets.

**Sector & drivers.** This is an infrastructure-enablement story for autonomous transport, not a payments/fintech deal: the actual builder is telco MTS, with Yandex as anchor customer for its self-driving fleet. The relevant market is high-precision (GNSS RTK, centimetre-grade) positioning for autonomous/ADAS vehicles. Sizing varies by scope: the broader automotive high-precision positioning market was ~$20.0bn in 2026, projected to ~$34.0bn by 2032 (~9% CAGR); narrower RTK-GNSS-for-vehicles was ~$3.2bn (2025) → ~$6.8bn by 2034 (~9.4% CAGR); the RTK-module-only slice is far smaller, ~$134m (2025) → ~$185m (2032, ~4.8% CAGR) — all per secondary market-research vendors (researchandmarkets / marketintelo / openpr), none independently verifiable, so treat as directional "no hard TAM". Structure: capital- and permission-intensive — a national RTK network needs a dense grid of reference stations (MTS cites 35 km coverage radius per station) mounted on existing telecom towers, which is MTS's structural edge (reuse of tower estate = low marginal cost vs a greenfield entrant). Why now: Russia is pushing a sovereign ("отечественный") autonomous-transport stack; Yandex plans ~70 self-driving trucks on roads in 2026 (per iz.ru/Rospatent filing, 2026-02-27) under its new "Robotruck/Роботрак" brand, and a national positioning layer is a precondition for scaling beyond pilot corridors.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for the infra provider: coverage (km of highway / # cities), positioning accuracy (2–5 cm claimed), SLA/uptime (MTS claims 24/7 with contractual SLA, not "best-efforts"), and eventually B2B ARPU from paid positioning. Current footprint: 4 federal highways (M-11, M-4, M-12, TsKAD), >4,000 km, 5 cities; live on M-4→Voronezh and M-12→Kazan; roadmap M-4→Novorossiysk end-2026, M-12→Tyumen by 2028, all million-plus cities by 2030 (CNews/comnews). Basis of competition: distribution + infrastructure reuse (telecom towers), not price. Players: MTS is first-mover on a nationwide open-SLA RTK network; Yandex (via Avride/SDG) is the leading Russian autonomy operator, with Cognitive Pilot (Sberbank/Cognitive Technologies) as the main domestic autonomy rival. Position: MTS is ahead on the positioning-infra layer (moat = tower estate + spectrum/telecom licences = scale + switching costs for any customer that standardises on its reference-station grid); Yandex is ahead on the vehicle/autonomy stack but is here a buyer of someone else's rails. **Risk flags.** (1) Dependence on someone else's rails — Yandex's truck autonomy now relies on MTS's RTK network for centimetre positioning on federal corridors; a single-supplier dependency with pricing power shifting to MTS once the planned paid B2B product launches (margin capture by the telecom/infra layer).
