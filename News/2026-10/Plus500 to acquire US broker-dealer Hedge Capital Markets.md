 Plus500 Ltd. 
 

 ("Plus500", the "Company" or, together with its subsidiaries, the "Group") 
 

 Acquisition of Hedge extends Plus500’s US offering into SEC-regulated securities 
 

 New York hub established to support US growth; US ‘super-app’ expected to launch in Q1 2027 
 

 Plus500, a global multi-asset fintech group operating proprietary technology-based trading platforms, is pleased to announce that it has conditionally agreed to acquire Hedge Capital Markets Inc., together with its subsidiaries (“Hedge”), including a US broker-dealer registered with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (“SEC”) and a member of the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (“FINRA”) (the “Acquisition”). Founded in 2021, Hedge offers US retail investors access to exchange-listed securities, including stocks and ETFs. The Acquisition marks Plus500’s entry into the SEC-regulated segment of the US market and strengthens the foundations for the expected launch of the Group’s US ‘super-app’ in Q1 2027. 
 

 Strengthening an established US franchise and opening a new market 
 

 Plus500 has built a substantial and fast-growing presence in the US, regulated by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (“CFTC”), providing clearing and execution services for futures and prediction markets to both retail and institutional clients. The Acquisition will complement the Group’s established US operations and will directly enhance client value by creating a truly integrated multi-asset ecosystem. Both new and existing US customers will benefit from a significantly broader suite of trading and investment solutions, while enabling the Group to drive cross-distribution synergies across a unified product offering. 
 

 Beyond reinforcing the current US business, the Acquisition creates new strategic and operational value for the Group and its customers by enabling direct access to SEC-regulated US securities markets, the world’s largest pool of retail investment activity. As a result, Plus500’s US product suite will expand materially, with stocks, options, ETFs and other exchange-listed securities, alongside futures and prediction markets, providing customers with a single, seamless destination for their trading and investing. 
 

 Disciplined M&A strategy and proven track record of execution 
 

 The Acquisition reflects the Group’s consistent strategy to generate tangible returns for shareholders through targeted bolt-on acquisitions. The Group identifies businesses that provide strategic regulatory licences, market access and specialist knowhow, then accelerates their development through its proprietary technology and operational efficiency, while keeping risk and capital exposure firmly contained. This strategy has a proven track record of success, most notably demonstrated by the Group’s CFTC-regulated US acquisition in 2021, which serves as the foundation for the business upon which Hedge will now build. 
 

 The Acquisition will be funded entirely from the Group’s existing cash resources, which stood at over $860m as of 30 June 2026, reflecting the strength of Plus500’s balance sheet. The closing of the transaction is subject to customary closing conditions, including the expiration of the required notice period and approval by FINRA under Rule 1017. 
 

 New York hub supports Plus500’s US growth strategy 
 

 To drive this next phase of growth, Plus500 has set up a new hub in New York to work alongside its established Chicago operations in expanding the Group’s operational capabilities and supporting its strategic expansion across the US business-to-consumer landscape. Headed by senior executives with deep knowledge of the US market, the New York team will be reinforced by the highly skilled Hedge personnel joining the Group, who bring specialist securities expertise and an established regulatory footprint. Working together, the New York and Chicago operations create a strong strategic foundation, driving operational synergies to support the planned launch of Plus500’s unified multi-asset platform in Q1 2027. 
 

 Plus500 to launch multi-asset US platform (‘super-app’) in Q1 2027 
 

 Today many US retail investors navigate between multiple providers, apps and accounts to trade across different asset classes. Plus500’s new multi-asset trading and investing platform is designed to remove that friction. Powered by the Group’s proprietary technology, it will consolidate the Plus500 US product suite, from futures and prediction markets to stocks, options, ETFs and other SEC-regulated products, into a single application, delivering a seamless user experience. 
 

 The development of the 'super-app' represents a core strategic priority for the Group, supported by significant dedicated R&D resources. For customers, it will deliver an intuitive, richer, and frictionless experience: a wider range of investment opportunities, more sophisticated trading capabilities and advanced technology, with new features rolled out to US users on an ongoing basis. 
 

 For Plus500, the ‘super-app’ is expected to drive deeper engagement, drive higher retention and monetisation, and open access to new segments of the US retail market, broadening the Group’s total addressable market. 
 

 The Acquisition provides the SEC-regulated foundation required to bring this integrated offering to US customers. Following the initial US launch, Plus500 intends to extend the ‘super-app’ to further geographies over time. 
 

 David Zruia, Chief Executive Officer of Plus500, commented: 
 

 “The Hedge acquisition marks a pivotal step in Plus500’s US journey. It strengthens the scaled, CFTC-regulated business we have built and provides, for the first time, direct access to the US securities market, the world’s largest retail investment market. With our new hub in New York and the experienced Hedge team joining us, we have the people, licences and technology to accelerate our growth and generate sustainable, long-term returns for our shareholders. 
 

 Above all, acquiring Hedge paves the way for the expected launch of our US ‘super-app’ in Q1 2027, one of Plus500’s most significant product developments. By bringing futures, prediction markets, stocks, options and ETFs together into one seamless experience, it will transform how our customers trade and invest, and represents a powerful new engine of value creation for the Group.” 
 

 About Hedge 
 

 Hedge was founded in 2021 by co-founders Kyle Al-Rawi and Yash Khandelwal to serve a new generation of US retail investors and to offer access to exchange-listed securities, including stocks and ETFs. It is a registered broker-dealer with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (“SEC”) and a member of the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (“FINRA”) and the Securities Investor Protection Corporation (“SIPC”).

# Context-enrichment: Plus500 to acquire US broker-dealer Hedge Capital Markets
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-13 Plus500 (LON: PLUS, FTSE 250) **conditionally agreed** to acquire Hedge Capital Markets Inc. — a US SEC-registered broker-dealer, FINRA member and SIPC member founded in 2021 (founders Kyle Al-Rawi, Yash Khandelwal). Hedge gives US retail investors access to exchange-listed securities (stocks, ETFs). This is Plus500's first move into the **SEC-regulated securities** segment of the US, layered on top of its existing **CFTC-regulated** US futures + prediction-markets business (built via the 2021 Cunningham Commodities acquisition).
- **De-PR'd core:** the headline event is a **licence/registration purchase**, not a scale play. Hedge is a tiny 2021 startup ("Fidelity for GenZ", group-investing gimmick); Plus500 is buying a working broker-dealer + FINRA licence + a small securities team to skip the multi-year de-novo registration path. The "super-app" (Q1 2027) is the actual product thesis; the acquisition is the regulatory enabler.
- **What PR won't say — price:** the consideration is **undisclosed**. For a group with >$860m net cash (30 Jun 2026) and no debt, funding "entirely from existing cash" is trivially true and reveals nothing. The silence on price strongly implies the ticket is immaterial to the group — consistent with a small bolt-on, not a transformational buy. **Why structured this way:** Plus500's explicit M&A playbook is to buy *small firms that hold a strategic licence/market access*, then scale them with its own proprietary tech — same template as Cunningham. The value is the regulatory key, not the target's revenue.
- **Conditionality:** closing needs FINRA approval under **Rule 1017** (change-of-control) plus expiry of a notice period — i.e. this is **announced, not closed**; completion timing is regulator-gated.

## [1] Competitors / peers
US retail multi-asset super-apps are a crowded field: **Robinhood** (stocks/options/crypto/futures, expanding into prediction markets and the EU), **Webull**, **eToro** (US), **Interactive Brokers**, and legacy **Schwab/Fidelity**. On the FX/CFD side Plus500's peers — **IG Group, CMC Markets, eToro, Capital.com, AvaTrade, Trade Nation** — are all reshaping via M&A (see corpus: [[AvaTrade to acquire majority of FXCM forex and CFD business]], [[Trade Nation acquires FXCM UK client business]], [[FxPro agrees to sell EU clients to Capital.com]]). Plus500's differentiator is the **CFTC futures + prediction-markets franchise** inside the US, which Robinhood is also chasing ([[Robinhood explores launching prediction markets outside US]], [[Robinhood targets UK platforms with prediction markets]]).
- **Position: catching up on securities, ahead on US prediction markets.** On plain-vanilla stock/ETF trading Plus500 is years behind Robinhood/Webull and will be a sub-scale new entrant. Its genuine edge is bundling **regulated US prediction markets + futures + equities** in one app — a combination Robinhood is still assembling. **Why the lay of the land is this way (analysis):** zero-commission equities is commoditised and margin-thin; the structural land-grab in US retail has shifted to *event contracts / prediction markets* (higher take-rate, novel). Plus500 bought its futures/prediction base in 2021 cheaply; equities is the missing shelf item, hence Hedge.

## [2] Company history / fit
Plus500 is an Israeli-founded, UK-listed CFD house (IPO'd LSE 24 Jul 2013). Historically ~100% OTC/CFD revenue, highly cyclical with trading volatility. Trajectory of deliberate diversification away from CFD dependence:
- **2021:** acquired **Cunningham Commodities (FCM) + Cunningham Trading Systems** for ~$30m → CFTC-regulated US futures foothold.
- **2026-02:** launched **US prediction-markets platform** ([[Plus500 launches US prediction markets platform]]).
- **2026-05:** appointed Merrill Lynch Int'l as joint corporate broker ([[Plus500 appoints Merrill Lynch International as joint corporate broker]]) — capital-markets signalling.
- **2026-10-13:** Hedge (SEC broker-dealer) + "super-app" + new **New York hub** alongside Chicago.
**Fit: logically consistent.** H1 2026: revenue +12% to **$462.9m**, customer income +24% to **$460.8m** (5-yr high), but **EBITDA only +1% to $187.5m**, margin compressing **45%→41%**. Non-OTC (US futures + prediction) is ~**30% YoY growth, ~15% of group revenue, ~$140m annualised 2026**. **Why the company acts this way (analysis):** the margin compression is the tell — CFD/OTC revenue is lumpy and the market prices Plus500 on a low CFD multiple. To re-rate toward a diversified-fintech multiple it must grow durable, non-CFD, US-regulated revenue. Equities + the super-app bundle is the vehicle to convert its US licences into recurring, re-rateable revenue.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
What's genuinely new: **direct SEC-regulated securities access** for Plus500 for the first time — meaningful because the US retail equity pool is the world's largest. What's NOT new/proven: the "super-app" itself (announced for Q1 2027 — **vaporware until shipped**), and Hedge brings a **licence + small team, not traction** (2021 startup, no disclosed client/AUM numbers; group-investing is a niche product).
- **Traction to date is in the futures/prediction leg, not equities** — the ~$140m annualised US run-rate predates Hedge and comes from Cunningham-era CFTC business.
- **Who captures the margin (analysis):** zero/low-commission equities make money on PFOF, spreads, securities lending, net interest on cash and cross-sell. Plus500's bet is that owning the *distribution funnel* (one app, cross-asset) lets it monetise via its higher-margin futures/prediction/CFD products — equities is a **customer-acquisition and retention loss-leader**, not a profit centre itself. **What breaks it:** if US equities stays commoditised and the cross-sell into futures/prediction underperforms, Plus500 ends up a sub-scale me-too broker spending R&D on a super-app that doesn't move the needle. The real question is not "can they add stocks" (yes, via the licence) but **"does the bundle lift lifetime value enough to justify the US build-out + R&D spend"** — unproven.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Timeline:** close subject to FINRA Rule 1017 (no date given); **US super-app targeted Q1 2027**; later geographic expansion of the super-app "over time".
- **Sentiment:** Plus500 shares rose modestly on the announcement; analysts broadly read it as a sensible, low-risk bolt-on. FY2026 consensus ~**$811.5m revenue / $365.1m EBITDA**.
- **Risks / why the market may go this way (analysis):** (a) execution — Plus500 has never run a de-novo US consumer equities product at scale; (b) regulatory — FINRA change-of-control approval is routine but gating; (c) competitive — entering US equities late against Robinhood/Webull's installed bases; (d) **second-order:** the >$860m cash pile funds buybacks ($182.5m shareholder returns H1) AND M&A — using cash for a tiny licence buy is low-risk, but the *super-app R&D* is the real, less-visible capex that could pressure the already-compressing EBITDA margin. The fragility is not the deal (cheap) but the product promise behind it.
