Acorns Acquires Frich, Expanding Its Financial Education Community
 Co-founders Katrin Kaurov and Aleksandra Medina bring a 1.6 million-person community and an organic creator network to Acorns.  
 IRVINE, Calif., October 14, 2026  — Acorns , the financial wellness company helping everyday Americans grow their money, today announced the acquisition of Frich, a community where young Americans learn about money from each other. Frich co-founders Katrin Kaurov and Aleksandra Medina join Acorns as Co-Leads of Education & Community. 
The deal reflects Acorns’ broader goal of giving everyday Americans a real chance to grow their money. The acquisition brings Acorns a community of 1.6 million people and an organic creator network producing financial education content, extending the company's approach to reaching new investors through creator-led education.
Many people avoid investing because they feel like it's a club they don't belong to, and they assume everyone else understands it and they don't. That feeling often forms years before someone opens an investing app. This acquisition meets people in the peer conversations where they're already shaping their money habits. It also continues a pattern for Acorns: bring in strong fintech builders and let them build.
“Investing is intimidating,” said Noah Kerner, CEO of Acorns. “Doing it with others can help people get past that. Katrin and Aleksandra helped a lot of people say ‘I don't know’ out loud, and that's a good place to start. We keep betting on builders like them.”
 
Kaurov and Medina founded Frich in 2021 to address a gap in financial education for young adults. The company was built around the premise that people learn how to manage money from each other, through real, diverse stories from people like them, shared in group chats, comment sections and creator videos, rather than from spreadsheets or formal instruction. Along with its active community and organic creator network, Frich also developed a peer-benchmarking feature that lets members compare their financial habits with their peers’, answering a question many young Americans ask: is my situation typical?
“We spent five years getting people to talk openly about money,” said Katrin Kaurov, co-founder of Frich. “Acorns is the natural next step, because it lets us pair that conversation with investing and saving.”
“Money feels like culture when it's honest, relatable, and social,” added Aleksandra Medina, co-founder of Frich. "That's what we built at Frich. It's what we get to keep building at Acorns.”
Acorns has spent more than a decade building products for financial wellness across life stages, including offerings for kids, teens, young adults, and families. The Frich acquisition adds an entry point built on peer conversation, reaching people where they are already talking about money, often before they have decided to invest. It also gives Acorns a way to start reaching specific communities more deeply, through the conversations those groups are already having with each other. 
Existing Frich community members will be welcomed to Acorns. Financial terms of the transaction were not disclosed. Kaurov and Medina joined Acorns this month.
 About Acorns Acorns is the financial wellness company helping everyday Americans grow their money for the long term. Since 2014, Acorns has grown into a global company with multiple life stage products serving the needs of kids, teens, adults and parents. Named one of America's Best Financial Services of 2026 by TIME , Acorns has served over 16 million people, and helped customers save & invest over $33 billion dollars, much of it from spare change and small amounts.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- Acorns acquired **Frich** ("Effing Rich"), a Gen-Z peer money-talk / financial-community app founded **2021** by **Katrin Kaurov** (ex-model) and **Aleksandra Medina** (met at NYU Abu Dhabi). Announced **2026-10-14** (Irvine, CA). Financial terms **not disclosed**.
- What Acorns actually bought: a **~1.6M-member community** + an **organic creator network** producing money-education content, plus the two co-founders, who join as **Co-Leads of Education & Community** (they joined "this month"). This is an **acqui-hire + audience/distribution buy**, not a product or balance-sheet buy.
- Frich's own scale (per 2024–25 reporting): **$2.8M seed** (TechCrunch, May 2024; led by Restive Ventures), grew from ~100k users (2024) toward the 1.6M community cited at acquisition, and was **"approaching ~$1M ARR"** on a **B2B** model (peer-benchmarking / anonymized Gen-Z financial-comparison data). Revenue was tiny.
- **Why structured this way / what it reveals:** undisclosed terms + "approaching $1M ARR" + acqui-hire framing imply a **small deal** (plausibly single-digit-to-low-double-digit $M — analysis, not disclosed). Acorns is paying for **top-of-funnel reach into pre-investors**, not Frich's P&L. The PR anchors on "community" and "peer conversation" precisely because the hard metrics (ARR, price) are unflattering. Note the Acorns CEO quote — "We keep betting on builders like them" — explicitly frames this as a talent/acqui-hire pattern.

## [1] Competitors / peers (Gen-Z / youth-finance M&A)
- **MrBeast / Beast Industries → Step** (Feb 2026, [[MrBeast's Beast Industries acquires Step banking app]]) — far bigger (Step ~7M users, ~$500M raised); also undisclosed. Distribution/brand player buying a Gen-Z finance audience.
- **Barclays ← GoHenry UK** (Jun 2026, [[Barclays acquires GoHenry's UK children's money business]]) — note the chain: **Acorns had owned GoHenry** (acquired 2023, CNBC) and **sold the UK unit to Barclays** in 2026. Acorns is simultaneously a serial buyer AND pruner in youth finance.
- **Cash App** teen savings (Nov 2025, [[Cash App launches high-interest savings for teens]]), **Revolut Kids & Teens** 75k→115k accounts (Oct 2025, [[Revolut Kids and Teens grew from 75,000 to 115,000 accounts in one year]]), **Greenlight** ~6.5M family members — the incumbent-scale competitors for Gen-Z/youth mindshare.
- **Experian × Snapchat** financial-literacy tie-up (May 2026, [[Experian and Snapchat partner on financial literacy for young users]]) — same "reach Gen-Z through the social/education layer" play from an incumbent.
- **Why the lay of the land is this way:** Gen-Z CAC via paid channels is brutal; incumbents increasingly **buy the community/creator layer** rather than build it. Frich's edge was *social/peer* top-of-funnel — the one thing a micro-investing app can't manufacture organically.

## [2] Company history / fit (Acorns)
- Acorns is a **serial acqui-hirer in family/youth finance**: GoHenry/Pixpay (2023, [[Pixpay targets EUR14M ARR by end of 2025]]), **Zeta** (couples/family planning, Jun 2025 — [[Acorns acquires Zeta to accelerate family strategy]]), and now Frich. Acorns positions all three as part of a multi-year "whole family / early financial life" strategy.
- Scale context: Acorns reports **16M+ all-time customers**, **$33B+ invested** (Aug 2026), and is reportedly **prepping a ~$2.2bn IPO** (industry reports, mid-2026). Named a TIME Best Financial Service 2026.
- **Why Acorns acts this way (structural logic):** Acorns' core is low-ARPU micro-investing; growth depends on **lowering the entry age** and **owning the moment before someone decides to invest**. Frich's pitch — "people avoid investing because it feels like a club they don't belong to" — maps directly onto Acorns' funnel problem. Buying a pre-investor peer community is cheaper than paid acquisition, and burnishes a "Gen-Z + community" narrative ahead of an IPO.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Not a novel product** — Frich is a content/community app; the "Scoop / salary-from-Instagram" AI tool (Jun 2025, [[Frich launches AI tool estimating salaries from social media]]) was a viral marketing stunt, not a durable product.
- **Real asset = the ~1.6M community + organic creator network** + the peer-benchmarking feature ("is my situation typical?"). That organic, non-paid reach is the only scarce thing here.
- **Who captures the margin:** Frich never monetized the community well (~$1M ARR). The open question is whether **Acorns** can convert peer-conversation traffic into funded investing accounts — i.e. whether community engagement survives absorption into a monetizing investing app. Communities often decay post-acquisition as the incentive shifts from "safe peer talk" to "convert to product".

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Expect Frich's community/creator content to become an **education / top-of-funnel surface inside Acorns**, with the co-founders running "Education & Community."
- **Risks:** (a) community authenticity erodes once owned by a product company with conversion KPIs; (b) the B2B comparison/data model carries **privacy/optics risk** under an investing brand; (c) tiny absolute scale → moves Acorns' numbers only marginally.
- **Second-order:** another data point that **standalone Gen-Z finance "community" apps don't reach escape velocity alone** — the exit is absorption by a monetizing platform. Frich joins Step and GoHenry on that path. The counterintuitive read: the more Acorns needs an IPO "Gen-Z community" story, the more likely it over-presents a sub-$1M-ARR tuck-in as strategically material.
