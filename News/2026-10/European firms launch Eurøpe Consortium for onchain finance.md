### dfns.co
<https://dfns.co/article/europ-support>
*941 слов · direct*

Today, European digital-finance companies, joined by Nasdaq-listed etoro, launch the Eurøpe Consortium, an industry-led coalition to accelerate the practical adoption of euro-denominated onchain finance. DFNS is a founding participant, providing the digital asset infrastructure through which organizations hold and move the euro onchain. And the asset at the centre of the consortium’s first programme, EURØP, is supported on DFNS.
For us this is the second chapter of a story that began in July, when Schuman Financial chose DFNS as the infrastructure behind the issuance and distribution of EURØP. Issuance was the first problem. Adoption is the harder one, and no single company solves it alone.
The imbalance the consortium exists to fix
Stablecoins have become a fundamental means of payment and settlement in digital-asset markets, and roughly 99% of their market value is denominated in US dollars, according to the European Central Bank’s November 2025 Financial Stability Review. Euro stablecoins account for less than 1%. Onchain, the dollar outweighs the euro by more than 300 to 1, for a currency that represents 20% of global foreign-exchange reserves.
That imbalance feeds itself. The currencies most used for payments, trading, and treasury attract more integrations, more liquidity, and more users, which attracts more of the same. Left alone, European businesses end up running euro economic activity on dollar-denominated rails, converting at both ends, simply because the infrastructure is there.
Europe did the hard regulatory work first. MiCA gives euro stablecoins the clearest rulebook in the world. What remains is everything regulation cannot create: distribution, liquidity, integrations, and daily use. That is a coordination problem, and the Eurøpe Consortium is the first European cross-company coalition built to solve it around a stablecoin that is already issued and already regulated, rather than one still awaiting launch.
What EURØP is
EURØP is a euro-denominated e-money token issued under MiCA by Schuman Financial, an electronic money institution authorised by the ACPR in France, with authorization across the EU and EEA. It is backed one to one by euro reserves and high-quality liquid assets held at Société Générale and other named credit institutions, with quarterly reserve attestations by KPMG. It is live on six public networks, Ethereum, Polygon, Avalanche, Solana, the XRP Ledger, and Plasma, available on Kraken, Bitvavo, Bit2Me, SwissBorg, and Bitpanda, and traded on Curve, Orca, and Pharaoh, with €116.8 million in transfers over the past thirty days. Not a token adapted to Europe, but a European instrument from the ground up.
Who is building the market
The consortium brings together the full value chain. Schuman Financial issues. SwissBorg, Coinhouse, Coinmerce, LCX, and BLOX provide the trading and distribution routes through which European customers already manage their money. etoro brings a global, Nasdaq-listed investment platform and mainstream investor distribution. Assetera connects euro digital money to tokenised assets and institutional market applications. XRPL Commons connects the initiative to developers and applications across the XRP Ledger ecosystem. RockawayX, an investor in Schuman Financial, brings capital and industry relationships. And DFNS provides the infrastructure through which organisations hold and move digital assets.
Participants will coordinate across four areas: access and distribution, making regulated euro stablecoins available on the platforms Europeans already use; infrastructure and integrations, with wallets, trading platforms, tokenised-asset infrastructure, and blockchain networks; practical applications across retail, institutional, payment, and tokenised-market use cases; and market development through education and engagement. Each participant keeps responsibility for its own commercial, technical, and regulatory decisions, and contributes according to its role.
“The EU established a regulatory framework for euro-denominated stablecoins, but regulation alone does not create a functioning market,” said Martin Bruncko, founder and CEO of Schuman Financial. “Adoption requires issuers, distribution platforms, infrastructure providers and broader financial and blockchain ecosystems to work together.”
What DFNS brings
Our role in the consortium is the role we play for every institution on the platform: the operating layer between a business and the networks its money moves on. For EURØP, that means a bank, fintech, or payment company on DFNS can hold the token in wallets and vaults across Ethereum, Polygon, Avalanche, Solana, and the XRP Ledger, move it with the full transaction lifecycle managed end to end, manage euro-denominated treasury alongside every other asset, and govern every movement through the Policy Engine, with approval quorums, limits, allowlists, and screening enforced before anything signs, and a complete audit trail behind it. For Schuman Financial itself, DFNS already provides the issuance and distribution infrastructure, with minting and burning run as policy-governed operations.
Within the consortium’s infrastructure and integration workstream, that is the contribution we intend to make: the connections that let euro digital money move between issuer, venues, tokenised assets, and institutions without each pair building its own bridge. A euro stablecoin becomes useful when the institutions that already run on common infrastructure can adopt it without a project. Many of them run on DFNS.
Why we joined
EURØP is issued in Paris, supervised by the Banque de France’s prudential authority, backed at European banks, and attested by KPMG. DFNS is headquartered in Paris, built for MiCA and DORA from the ground up, and runs beneath a growing lineage of European regulated institutions, including fellow consortium participant Coinhouse. Europe wrote the rules for onchain money before anyone else. It should not end up renting the rails. The euro’s share of onchain finance is going to change. Today, a coalition of the companies able to change it got to work. DFNS is proud to be one of them.

# Context-enrichment: European firms launch Eurøpe Consortium for onchain finance
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-06 (announced ~Oct 5), eleven European digital-finance firms launched the **Eurøpe Consortium**, an *industry-led, non-binding* coalition to promote adoption of euro-denominated onchain finance. Members: Schuman Financial (issuer), DFNS (wallet/custody infra), eToro, SwissBorg, Coinhouse, Coinmerce, LCX, BLOX, Assetera, XRPL Commons, RockawayX. Its first programme is **EURØP**, a euro e-money token issued by Schuman Financial under MiCA (ACPR-authorised EMI, France), 1:1 euro-backed (reserves at Société Générale and other named banks, +2% reserve fund per external research), quarterly KPMG attestations. Live on 6 chains (Ethereum, Polygon, Avalanche, Solana, XRP Ledger, Plasma — some outlets still list 5, i.e. ex-Plasma; "6" is current issuer claim).
- **De-PR'd reality:** This is a *coordination/marketing coalition around an already-live token*, not a new product, new capital, or binding commitments. The DFNS source itself frames it as "the second chapter" of a July 2025 relationship. Each member "keeps its own commercial, technical and regulatory decisions" — i.e. membership obligates nothing.
- **Why framed this way → what it reveals:** The announcement leans on the ECB Nov-2025 FSR stat (~99% of stablecoin value is USD; euro <1%; "300-to-1 onchain") to manufacture urgency. The "300:1" figure is DFNS framing, NOT an ECB verbatim number (ECB says euro stablecoins total ~€395M, ≈99:1 by supply share). Anchoring to a dramatic ratio distracts from the small absolute scale of EURØP itself.
- **Scale check (the number that matters):** EURØP supply is only ~€15.5M (as of Oct 1, external; live trackers diverged €7.8M–16M). The headline "€116.8M in 30-day transfers" is an issuer/DFNS-supplied, un-audited *transfer-volume* metric (not market cap); against ~€15.5M outstanding it implies very high turnover — plausible for a payments token but not independently verified. (analysis)

## [1] Competitors / peers
Euro-stablecoin landscape (mid–late 2026, approximate, external research):
- **Circle EURC** — dominant: ~$440–525M cap, ~50–63% share; ACPR EMI authorised Jul 2024 (first MiCA-authorised global issuer). See [[Deutsche Börse and Circle collaborate on stablecoins in Europe]].
- **Société Générale EURCV (SG-Forge)** — ~$143M; the main bank-issued euro stablecoin.
- **Banking Circle EURI** ~$51M; **Monerium EURe** ~$30M; **Quantoz EURQ** <$13M; **Stasis EURS** ~$8M (MiCA status contested).
- **ODDO BHF EUROD** — see [[ODDO BHF launches euro-backed stablecoin EUROD]].
- Bank-led rival consortium: **Qivalis** (9→37 banks, launching a euro stablecoin H2 2026) — see [[Euro stablecoin consortium Qivalis expands to 37 banks]] and [[European banks collaborate to launch euro stablecoin]].
- **Position of EURØP:** at ~€15.5M it is a *minor* player, well behind EURC, EURCV, EURI. The aggregate MiCA euro-stablecoin cap is ~$674M, +128% YoY — see [[MiCA-compliant euro stablecoins grow 128% ahead of deadline]].
- **Why this lay of the land → second order:** The constraint for euro stablecoins is NOT issuance (MiCA solved the rulebook; ≥8 tokens now exist) but *distribution/liquidity/network effects*, which dollar incumbents (USDT/USDC) already own 300:1. A coalition of mostly small crypto-native venues cannot manufacture the network effect that EURC gets from Coinbase/Circle's rails or that Qivalis will get from 37 banks' balance sheets and customer bases. So the real question shifts from "can Europe issue a euro stablecoin" (yes, many) to "who has the distribution to make one *used*" — and this consortium's distribution partners are the weakest link. (analysis)

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Schuman Financial:** founded by ex-Binance execs; EURØP unveiled ~Nov 2024; chose DFNS as issuance/distribution infra (DFNS frames this as July 2025 — exact month UNVERIFIED, mark hypothesis). Prior distribution tie: [[Wirex Pay partners Schuman Financial on EUROP stablecoin]] (Jul 2025) — a pattern of adding distribution partners one at a time; the consortium bundles that effort.
- **DFNS:** Paris-based wallets-as-a-service / MPC custody infra (founded 2020, $16M Series A), explicitly not a custodian/bank; 100+ chains. See [[Dfns launches payouts engine for global digital asset distribution]] and [[IBM launches Digital Asset Haven platform with Dfns]]. DFNS is the loudest promoter of the launch — fitting, since the consortium funnels EURØP flows onto its infra.
- **Why they act this way:** A sub-scale euro stablecoin's only path is to assemble a coalition — issuance alone is commoditised and cheap under MiCA; the scarce input is adoption. For DFNS, every regulated euro flow is infra revenue, so it has strong incentive to organise and publicise the coalition regardless of EURØP's ultimate success. (analysis)

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **What's genuinely new:** The *coalition structure itself* — a cross-company, crypto-native coalition built around an *already-issued, already-regulated* euro stablecoin (vs. Qivalis, still pre-launch). That framing ("regulated and live today") is the one defensible differentiator.
- **What is NOT new / weak:** No new money, no binding commitments, no new product. Critically, flagship member **eToro has made NO commitment to list EURØP** (external: LeapRate) — headlines implying eToro adoption overstate it. "Coordinate across four workstreams" is aspirational language, not traction.
- **Traction reality:** ~€15.5M supply is tiny; €116.8M/30d transfers is un-audited issuer data. Live on real venues (Kraken, Bitvavo, Bit2Me, SwissBorg, Bitpanda; Curve/Orca/Pharaoh) — that is real but small.
- **Who captures the margin → why value-add is thin:** In a euro-stablecoin stack, issuer economics (reserve yield) accrue to Schuman; infra fees to DFNS; distribution margin to exchanges. A non-binding coalition does not redistribute or guarantee any of this — members contribute "according to role" with no enforcement. The value-add is real only if it converts into listings/integrations with measurable volume; absent that, it is a press-release coalition. (analysis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Plans:** four workstreams (access/distribution; infra/integrations; applications; market development). Watch for *actual* new listings (esp. whether eToro ever lists EURØP) and EURØP supply/volume growth over the next 1–2 quarters as the only real scorecard.
- **Backdrop:** MiCA transitional window closed 1 Jul 2026 (see [[MiCA transitional window for crypto service providers closes 1 July]]); ECB *digital euro* in pilot-preparation (expressions of interest Mar–May 2026, pilot dev from Q3 2026, possible issuance ~2029) — a sovereign overhang that could crowd private euro stablecoins. Qivalis (37 banks) targets H2 2026 launch.
- **Why the market goes this way / counterintuitive second-order:** Europe has an *oversupply* of euro-stablecoin initiatives (EURC, EURCV, EURI, EURe, EURQ, EURS, EUROD, EURØP, + Qivalis + digital euro) chasing a <1% share. The binding constraint is consolidation of liquidity, not more issuers/coalitions. Counterintuitively, launching yet another coalition may *fragment* euro liquidity further rather than concentrate it — the opposite of the stated goal. The likely winners are whoever controls mainstream distribution (Circle/Coinbase for EURC; the 37 banks for Qivalis), not the issuer with the best coalition press release. ECB's Lagarde has also argued stablecoins should not be privately issued (see [[ECB's Lagarde argues stablecoins should not be issued privately]]) — a regulatory risk hanging over all of this. (analysis/hypothesis)

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Euro-denominated stablecoins are a sub-1% niche of a dollar-dominated market. Per the ECB's November 2025 Financial Stability Review (via dfns.co, as of 2026-10-06), ~99% of stablecoin market value is USD and euro stablecoins are <1% — onchain the dollar outweighs the euro >300:1, for a currency that is ~20% of global FX reserves. Absolute size is tiny but growing fast: per cryptobriefing/stablecoininsider web citations (as of Aug 2026), total euro-stablecoin market cap is only ~€600-620m (~0.2% of the ~$300bn global stablecoin market), with Circle's EURC the leader at ~$526m cap / ~63% share. EURØP itself is small — ~€15.5m supply and €116.8m 30-day transfer volume as of 2026-10-01 (per dfns.co / web). **Structure:** fragmented and sub-scale on the euro side; the real barrier is not issuance (MiCA already licenses issuers) but the coordination of distribution, liquidity and integrations — a classic network-effects flywheel the incumbent dollar rails already own. **Why now:** MiCA gives euro e-money tokens the world's clearest rulebook, while the non-euro €200m daily payment-cap pressure and 2026 delistings of non-compliant tokens open a vacuum; the consortium is the first cross-company European coalition built around an *already-issued, already-regulated* stablecoin rather than a still-pending launch. Driver → the imbalance self-reinforces (more usage → more liquidity/integrations → more usage), so Europe risks running euro activity on USD rails and converting at both ends (analysis).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: circulating supply / market cap, onchain transfer volume, number of supported chains and listing venues, and reserve quality/attestation. **Key players & basis of competition:** EURC (Circle) is the scale leader (~63% share, ~$526m) competing on liquidity and exchange distribution; bank-issued challengers compete on trust/reserves — Société Générale-Forge EURCV, ODDO BHF's EUROD (launched 2025-10-15, [[ODDO BHF launches euro-backed stablecoin EUROD]]), and the nine-bank Qivalis JV targeting a 2H-2026 launch ([[European banks collaborate to launch euro stablecoin]]); Deutsche Börse is building euro stablecoin/settlement rails with Circle ([[Deutsche Börse and Circle collaborate on stablecoins in Europe]]). **Recent moves (dated):** Eurøpe Consortium launched 2026-10-05/06 with 11 named members. **Protagonist's position:** EURØP is a niche challenger — a fraction of EURC's size — differentiating not on scale but on an end-to-end *European* value chain (issuer Schuman/ACPR, reserves at Société Générale + named banks, KPMG quarterly attestations, 6 chains, DFNS custody, Nasdaq-listed eToro distribution). Moat is a coordination/network play (analysis): the value chain assembled under one coalition lowers each member's integration cost. Live multiples / EURØP economics (fees, float yield split) are not disclosed → [UNSOURCED].

**Comps & multiples.** This is a partnership, not a round/deal — no valuation attaches to EURØP or the consortium, so market-cap multiples = no data. Comparison is on stablecoin scale (circulating cap / 30-day volume), where arithmetic is illustrative, not a trading multiple:
- EURØP: ~€15.5m supply; €116.8m 30-day transfers → velocity ≈ 116.8 / 15.5 ≈ 7.5x/month (high turnover, DeFi-liquidity-driven, not retail payments) (analysis).
- EURC (Circle): ~$526m cap, ~63% euro-stablecoin share → EURØP is ≈15.5/≈480(€) ≈ 3% of EURC's size — a ~30x scale gap (analysis).
- Internal comps (base): [[ODDO BHF launches euro-backed stablecoin EUROD]]; [[European banks collaborate to launch euro stablecoin]] (Qivalis); [[Deutsche Börse and Circle collaborate on stablecoins in Europe]]; sector backdrop [[Circle responds to European Commission MiCA review consultation]], [[ECB outlines three models for central bank money on-chain]].
- Distribution not computed (fewer than 3 comparable cap figures publicly verifiable); qualitative: EURØP is a small challenger well behind EURC. No P/S or EV multiple applies.

**IR grounding — eToro (Nasdaq: ETOR), founding member.** eToro is the one consortium member in the IR corpus (brokerage, quarterly cadence; 20-F + 6-K filings on file). Its latest filed results (Q2 2026, per etoro.com / investors.etoro.com) show Net Contribution of $229m (+9% YoY), funded accounts 4.28m (+18% YoY), adjusted EBITDA $78m (+9% YoY), GAAP net income $53m (+77% YoY), and AUA $19bn (+10% YoY) — with crypto activity shifting toward equities/commodities (Capital Markets net trading contribution +25% YoY). Read-through: eToro's contribution is "mainstream investor distribution," but euro-stablecoin flows are immaterial to its P&L today — participation is optionality, not a disclosed revenue line.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Scale / network-effects disadvantage.** At ~3% of EURC's size and a >300:1 USD onchain lead, the self-reinforcing liquidity flywheel favours incumbents; a coalition can lower integration cost but cannot manufacture liquidity — if EURØP doesn't cross a liquidity threshold, the coordination effort stalls.
2. **Announced ≠ adopted; no binding commitments.** Each member "keeps responsibility for its own commercial decisions" and contributes "according to its role" — notably eToro's own listing of EURØP is not promised (per leaprate). The de-PR read: this is a soft coalition, so distribution is aspirational, not contracted.
3. **Fragmentation within the euro camp.** EURØP competes not only with EURC but with bank-issued rivals (EUROD, EURCV, Qivalis) and ECB wholesale-CBDC / on-chain central-bank-money models; a crowded, sub-scale euro field risks splitting already-thin liquidity rather than consolidating it, delaying the escape from USD rails.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) The launch marks a shift in euro-stablecoin competition from *issuance* (solved by MiCA) to *distribution/coordination* — a potential consolidation/standard-setting moment for the European onchain-euro stack. Falsifiable thesis: if the coalition works, EURØP's circulating supply and multi-venue listings should climb materially (e.g. toward EURC's order of magnitude) over the next 2-4 quarters. Trigger/catalyst to watch: real listings on member platforms (esp. eToro) and rising 30-day transfer volume. What would make the thesis wrong: supply/volume flat or members declining to list EURØP, confirming it as PR-coalition rather than a functioning market.
