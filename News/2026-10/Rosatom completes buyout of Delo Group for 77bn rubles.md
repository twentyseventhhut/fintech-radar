 «Росатом» завершил выкуп 51% группы «Дело» за 77 млрд рублей у Сергея Шишкарёва 
Основатель ГК «Дело» Сергей Шишкарев и «Росатом» закрыли сделку по выкупу «Росатомом» 51% группы за 77 млрд руб. Об этом объявили стороны. Теперь «Росатом» консолидировал все 100% акций компании, отмечает " Коммерсант ". «С этого м
омента мы начинаем окончательную консолидацию всех логистических активов “Росатома”,— анонсировал Алексей Лихачев,— результатом которой станет создание крупнейшей национальной компании в сфере логистики. Наши стратегические цели — это укрепление транспортного суверенитета нашей страны, обеспечение устойчивых международных перевозок и развитие Северного морского пути, а вместе с ним и Трансарктического транспортного коридора».
ГК «Дело» — одна из крупнейших российских логистических компаний с контейнерными терминалами во всех основных морских бассейнах РФ, глубоководным зерновым терминалом РФ (КСК), владеющая также судоходными активами и железнодорожным оператором платформ «Трансконтейнером». В 2019 году в капитал ГК «Дело» вошел «Росатом» — сначала с 30%, а впоследствии через опцион ставший владельцем 49%. Отношения у партнеров не заладились, и даже попытка привлечь для урегулирования «Трансмашхолдинг» результата не принесла: машиностроители, подержав 1% в капитале группы, далее развивать свое акционерное присутствие не стали, и в итоге господин Шишкарев выкупил эту долю обратно.
Зимой «Росатом» и Сергей Шишкарев, желая прекратить свое партнерство, начали процедуру «корпоративной рулетки». В ее рамках одна сторона может предложить другой цену, по которой та может либо продать свою долю, либо выкупить долю партнера. «Росатом» 20 февраля сделал оферту господину Шишкареву, который решил покупать пакет. Оценен он был в 74 млрд руб., или в 77 млрд руб., если речь идет о варианте выкупа «Росатомом» доли Сергея Шишкарева. Господин Шишкарев, изначально планировавший выкупать 49% у «Росатома», в конце июня объявил, что не смог привлечь деньги на выкуп на приемлемых условиях. Инициатива перешла к госкорпорации, и в июле Алексей Лихачев объявил, что «Росатом» принял корпоративное решение выкупить у Сергея Шишкарева его 51% в ГК «Дело». 28 августа процедура, вопреки ожиданиям госкорпорации, стремившейся закрыть сделку в кратчайшие сроки, не была завершена: господин Шишкарев заявил, что соблюдены не все формальности, и сроки были сдвинуты на октябрь.
«За семь лет сотрудничества нам удалось немало сделать вместе, но в то же время непреодолимые разногласия о будущем группы привели нас в точку, где наши пути сегодня расходятся»,— резюмировал Сергей Шишкарев.
Как пояснил господин Лихачев, на сегодня логистический дивизион «Росатома» — безусловный лидер в своем сегменте. «Совокупная доля активов госкорпорации на рынке перевалки контейнеров составляет 50%, в железнодорожных контейнерных перевозках — 44%,— пояснил он.— Формирование общего центра управления группой “Дело”, FESCO и другими транспортными активами “Росатома” позволит выстроить единую систему мультимодальной логистики, включающую автомобильные, железнодорожные и морские перевозки, порты, экспедирование и бесшовный сервис. Консолидация активов позволит исключить дублирование инвестиционных вложений, что позволит в ближайшие десять лет сэкономить до 100 млрд руб.». При этом ранее «Росатом» анонсировал планы по созданию партнерства с DP World (ОАЭ), куда будет внесена его доля в FESCO.
О своих дальнейших планах Сергей Шишкарев заявил, что будущее по-прежнему связывает «с российской логистикой, спортом и, конечно, родным Новороссийском». «Сосредоточу внимание на проектах в сфере обеспечения транспортного суверенитета, поддержки гандбола, адаптивного спорта и демографии,— сообщил он.— Как обещал, значительную часть суммы, полученной от сделки, направлю на нужды национальной безопасности».

_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Rosatom (Russian state nuclear corp, already holder of 49% since 2019) **closed** the purchase of founder Sergei Shishkarev's **51%** of Delo Group for **77bn ₽ (~$900M)** on 2026-10-07, taking it to **100% ownership**. This is the terminal step of a multi-stage, 8-month exit, already tracked in-corpus: see [[Shishkarev drops plan to buy 49% of Delo Group from Rosatom]] (Jun 2026) and [[Rosatom decides to buy out Shishkarev's stake in Delo Group]] (Jul 2026). The vehicle was a rare "corporate/Russian roulette" clause: Rosatom's 20 Feb 2026 offer priced the stakes at **74bn ₽** (Shishkarev buys Rosatom's 49%) OR **77bn ₽** (Rosatom buys Shishkarev's 51%). The 74 vs 77 gap is **not an error** — it is the two sides of the same roulette offer. Shishkarev first intended to buy, signed an April memorandum with Rostec for financing, then in late June could not raise acceptable money; initiative flipped to the state.

**Why structured this way:** the "roulette" mechanism let a soured JV (partners publicly cited "irreconcilable differences" after 7 years) resolve without open-market price discovery — convenient for a state buyer. Rosatom paid with **borrowed funds**, to be refinanced by the planned DP World (UAE) partnership into which FESCO's stake is to be contributed. So the "77bn" is a roulette-derived valuation, not an independently audited market price **(open — neither party officially confirmed cash paid).** The Aug 28 planned legal close slipped ~6 weeks to Oct 7 (Shishkarev cited unmet formalities).

## [1] Competitors / peers
Russian container/rail logistics peers: **Globaltrans** (rail operator, ex-LSE), **RZD / RZD Logistics**, **UTLC ERA** (China–Europe transit), **NMTP** (Novorossiysk port), **Transmashholding** — which briefly held 1% of Delo (Dec 2024) with CEO-naming rights before agreeing to return it, restoring Shishkarev's clean 51%. Position: post-deal Rosatom is the **undisputed segment leader** — Likhachev claims **50% of container transshipment and 44% of rail container transport**. **Why this way:** sanctions (TransContainer and Global Ports' Primorye terminal are on the US list; NMTP under Canadian sanctions) have hollowed out private/foreign players and pushed Russian logistics toward state consolidation — the competitive field is narrowing structurally, not by market win.

## [2] Company history / fit
Rosatom entered Delo in 2019 (30%, then 49% via option) explicitly tied to Arctic-shipping ambitions. In parallel it absorbed **FESCO** (92%+ nationalized by court Jan 2023, transferred by presidential decree Nov 2023). Delo brings DeloPorts (NUTEP + **KSK deepwater grain terminal**, Novorossiysk), Global Ports (Baltic/Far East), Ruskon multimodal, and **TransContainer** (largest rail container operator). **Why Rosatom acts this way:** as the statutory operator of the **Northern Sea Route**, it needs owned, end-to-end multimodal rail-port-shipping assets to build the **Trans-Arctic Transport Corridor (~14,000 km)** and deliver "transport sovereignty" — a state-strategic, not commercial-return, logic.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
The novelty is **not** the price or even the buyout (both reported in July — this note is the *close*). The real new development is **100% consolidation unlocking the merger** of Delo + FESCO + other Rosatom transport assets under a single management center, with a claimed **up to 100bn ₽ of savings over 10 years** from eliminating duplicated investment. **Why value-add is partly real:** genuine synergy exists in unifying overlapping port/rail capex; but the figure is a self-reported state projection, and the economics depend on the DP World refinancing closing (delayed by FESCO sanctions) — so the capital structure, not the operational synergy, is the fragile link. Traction = control is now legally complete; the integration itself is still "announced."

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Likhachev: "final consolidation of all Rosatom logistics assets" into a national champion (aiming BRICS top-5). Watch: (a) the **DP World JV** ("Globalnaya Logistika") where Russia keeps 51% / DP World 49% of FESCO (~145–160bn ₽), delayed by US sanctions on FESCO; (b) integration execution of the single mgmt center. Shishkarev pledged a "significant portion" of proceeds to **national security/defense**, staying in logistics, handball, Novorossiysk. **Why/second-order:** state concentration of all container logistics under a sanctioned nuclear corporation makes the asset base strategically unified but **harder to partner internationally** — the very sanctions that cleared the private field also block the foreign refinancing the deal relies on. The counterintuitive effect: consolidation strengthens domestic control while increasing dependence on a single geopolitically constrained refinancing path.

## Top challenge / red-team questions (second-order)
1. **Is it really new vs the July note?** Yes — [[Rosatom decides to buy out Shishkarev's stake in Delo Group]] was the *decision*; this is the *legal close* to 100% and the explicit kickoff of FESCO integration. Fresh, but low incremental surprise.
2. **Was the 77bn ₽ actually paid in cash?** Open — neither party officially disclosed the paid price; 77bn is the roulette-offer valuation, widely reported but not independently audited.
3. **Why 74bn vs 77bn?** Answered — two sides of the same roulette offer (74 = Shishkarev buys 49%; 77 = Rosatom buys 51%). Not a discrepancy.
4. **Does the deal depend on the DP World refinancing?** Yes — Rosatom used borrowed funds to be replaced by partnership money; FESCO sanctions have delayed that JV. This is the deal's fragile link.
5. **Are the "50% transshipment / 44% rail" market shares independent or self-reported?** Self-reported by Likhachev; directionally plausible given TransContainer + FESCO + Global Ports, but not externally verified. Open.
6. **Is the "up to 100bn ₽ / 10yr savings" a real synergy or PR?** A state projection; real overlap exists (duplicated port/rail capex) but the number is unaudited. Treat as aspiration.
7. **Why did the Aug 28 close slip to Oct 7?** Shishkarev cited unmet formalities; the specific regulatory/financing cause is not spelled out. Open.
8. **What is the Transmashholding 1% story?** TMH bought 1% (Dec 2024) with CEO-naming rights, then agreed to return it pending FAS — Shishkarev regained a clean 51% before the buyout. Relevant to how the roulette had two clean blocks.
9. **Sanctions exposure of acquired assets?** TransContainer and Global Ports Primorye on US list; NMTP under Canadian sanctions — limits any future international monetization/partnering.
10. **Who needs whom more — state or founder?** Founder failed to raise financing (even with a Rostec memorandum); initiative flipped to the state. The state had the stronger hand and the roulette structure favored the deeper-pocketed buyer.
11. **Is this a forced/soft-forced exit?** Partners cited "irreconcilable differences"; the roulette is a mutual mechanism, but a private holder unable to finance a buyout of a state partner effectively has little choice. Analysis: structurally coercive.
12. **What is the fintech angle at all?** Thin — M&A event, borrowed-funds financing, a cross-border JV. Belongs in corpus as Russian infra/M&A, not a payments/fintech-product story.
13. **Second-order risk of consolidation?** Concentrating all logistics under one sanctioned nuclear corp unifies control but increases single-point geopolitical/financing fragility — the sanctions that cleared competitors also block the foreign refinancing.
14. **Where does Shishkarev's money go?** He pledged a "significant portion" to national security/defense — a wartime-corporate signal, not a commercial fact.
15. **Does any English-language primary confirm granular numbers?** No — only Moscow Times/Bloomberg/Interfax-EN at high level; 74/77 split, timeline, shares, asset detail are Russian-only (Kommersant/RBC/Vedomosti).

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russian transport & logistics — specifically container handling (port terminals), rail container operation, and multimodal freight. Not a fintech vertical; this deal sits in state-driven infrastructure consolidation, included here as an M&A/infrastructure data point. Size/structure: the Russian container-logistics market is consolidating under state control — per Likhachev's own quote in the note, Rosatom's assets already hold ~50% of container transshipment and ~44% of rail container transport, so the sector is moving from fragmented toward a single state-anchored champion. Drivers + "why now": (1) "transport sovereignty" / sanctions re-routing — Western sanctions since 2022 have forced Russian trade onto new corridors (Northern Sea Route, Trans-Arctic, Eurasian rail), and the state wants one controller; (2) the DP World (UAE) JV angle — Rosatom plans to fold its ~92.5% FESCO stake into a 51/49 JV with DP World to regain access to global port networks, a deal repeatedly delayed by EU sanctions on FESCO (sanctioned late Oct 2025, pushing the sale past its 2025 target and into renegotiation) (per portseurope / Interfax, as of 2026). "Why now": the Rosatom–Shishkarev "corporate roulette" (first-of-its-kind in RF) resolved in Rosatom's favour after Shishkarev failed to raise financing (incl. an approach to VTB) to buy out Rosatom's 49% — see internal notes below.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs (infra, not fintech): container throughput (TEU), rail container volume share, port transshipment share, terminal EBITDA. Key players: this is less "competition" than state consolidation — Rosatom is absorbing Delo (container terminals in all main RF sea basins, KSK deepwater grain terminal, TransContainer rail-platform operator) AND FESCO into one management centre. Basis of competition is therefore political/regulatory access and corridor control, not price/product. Recent moves (dated): 2019 — Rosatom entered Delo at 30%, later 49% via option; Feb 20 2026 — Rosatom made the "roulette" offer; Jun 2026 — Shishkarev dropped his plan to buy Rosatom's 49%; Jul 2026 — Rosatom's corporate decision to buy Shishkarev's 51%; Aug 28 2026 — closing slipped on formalities; Oct 7 2026 — buyout completed, 100% consolidated. Protagonist's position: dominant / monopolist-adjacent in domestic container logistics (analysis) — moat is almost entirely intangible/regulatory (state ownership, sovereign mandate, corridor concessions), not a commercial network-effect moat.

**Comps & multiples.** Headline: 51% stake = 77bn rubles (~$900M, per Moscow Times/Bloomberg, as of 2026-10-07). The "roulette" mechanics: Rosatom-buys-Shishkarev at 77bn vs Shishkarev-buys-Rosatom at 74bn — implying a rough 100% equity value of ~150bn rubles per Rosatom's offer framing [`per Interfax/portseurope`]. Delo 2026 EBITDA target reportedly raised to ~74bn rubles (from a 55bn-ruble draft) [`per Bloomberg, as of 2026`]. Arithmetic (treat as indicative, NOT a market-validated multiple): EV/EBITDA ≈ 150bn / 74bn = ~2.0x on the target-EBITDA; on the more conservative 55bn draft ≈ 150bn / 55bn = ~2.7x. Both are far below global container/port peers (typically EV/EBITDA ~8–12x) — but this is a captive, related-party, sanctions-constrained buyout at an administratively-set "roulette" price, not an arm's-length market clearing price, so the low multiple reflects illiquidity/forced-seller/sanctions discount, NOT genuine cheapness. Net debt is undisclosed → EV is approximated by equity value here, so the multiple is directional only; clean EV/EBITDA = [UNSOURCED].

**Risk flags.**
1. Sanctions / disintermediation of the exit story — Delo is being folded toward the FESCO–DP World JV, but FESCO itself is EU-sanctioned (since late Oct 2025), which already delayed and re-priced the DP World deal; the entire "global port access" thesis is hostage to secondary-sanctions risk on any foreign counterparty (incl. India NSR MoU exposure flagged in press). Second-order: if DP World walks, the consolidation delivers domestic scale but no international reach.
2. Related-party / administratively-set price — the 77bn "roulette" price is not an arm's-length market valuation; the implied ~2x EBITDA cannot be read as a sector re-rating signal and is non-transferable to any outside investor.
3. Concentration / regulatory monopoly — merging Delo + FESCO + TransContainer puts ~50% of port container transshipment and ~44% of rail container volume under one state owner; antimonopoly (FAS) conditions were attached to the earlier approval, and a single state operator concentrates execution and political risk (price-setting, cross-subsidy, investment-duplication claims of ~100bn-ruble savings are unverified projections, not realized synergies).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is consolidation, not a re-rating: Russia now has a single state logistics champion spanning sea/rail/road/ports, aligned to sanctions-era corridor policy (NSR, Trans-Arctic) (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: the real value inflection is NOT the Delo buyout but whether the FESCO–DP World JV actually closes and survives EU/US scrutiny — watch for the JV signing/completion and any further EU sanction listing of the JV entity.
