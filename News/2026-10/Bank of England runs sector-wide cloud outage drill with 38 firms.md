CMORG holds sector-wide cloud outage simulation exercise
News Release 
The Cross-Market Operational Resilience Group (CMORG) yesterday (8 October 2026) completed its biennial simulation exercise, known as SIMEX26, focused this year on a global cloud services disruption.
The group – chaired by the Bank of England (the Bank) and UK Finance, with participation from HM Treasury, the Financial Conduct Authority and the wider financial sector – met to undertake a wide-ranging crisis management exercise, with the 38 largest and most systemically important banks and market infrastructure operators involved.
The exercise, which was opened by the Economic Secretary to the Treasury and hosted by the London Stock Exchange Group, involved firms working alongside the financial authorities to respond to a scenario focusing on global disruption to cloud-based services. 

It also included a live meeting of the Bank-chaired Cross Market Business Continuity Group, which provides strategic direction to the sector’s collective response to systemic incidents. The Bank now expects firms to take action based on the exercise to further strengthen their resilience and ability to respond to severe operational and cyber scenarios.
Katharine Braddick, Deputy Governor of Prudential Regulation at the Bank of England and CEO of the Prudential Regulation Authority said: 

 "This was an important demonstration of the UK financial sector's commitment to operational resilience. The level of collaboration reflects the priority firms place on maintaining critical services in the face of disruption. That preparedness is essential to safeguarding financial stability." 
 
David Postings, Chief Executive of UK Finance, said:
“Maintaining resilience across the financial sector is critical to supporting confidence, stability, and growth in the UK economy. SIMEX 2026 enabled us to collectively strengthen preparedness and ensure the sector is better equipped to respond to significant disruptive events while continuing to deliver essential services to customers.”
Economic Secretary to the Treasury Lucy Rigby said:
 “The operational resilience of our world-leading financial services sector is core to maintaining financial stability. 
  
“I’m grateful to all participants in SIMEX26 as we look to collectively enhance our defences against disruption, and safeguard stability and confidence.” 
 
The SIMEX programme supports the financial sector’s operational resilience by rehearsing the response to the most challenging scenarios. Previous exercises, including those in 2022 and 2024, considered cyber-attacks against a major bank, as well as critical infrastructure disruption outside the financial sector.
SIMEX 26 builds on work done by the Bank of England, working in collaboration with the financial authorities and industry through CMORG, to boost operational resilience in financial services. In recent months, this has included strengthening third party engagement and resilience, enabling secure innovation in response to AI and other emerging technologies, and enhancing cyber preparedness, response and recovery for the most severe disruption scenarios.
Notes to editors 
1. SIMEX 26 and CMORG’s wider programme to develop financial sector capabilities supports the aims of both financial authorities and firms to ensure collective response tools are in place to respond to disruptive events. This improves the operational resilience of the financial sector and promotes a stable financial system that the public can depend on.
2. CMORG enhances the operational resilience of the UK financial sector through collective action, and is supported by specialist industry groups. It achieves this through the identification of systemic risks, the development of solutions to support sector-wide mitigation strategies and sharing capabilities and knowledge across the sector.
3. As part of this work, CMORG oversees the approach for sector-wide response to systemic incidents through the Sector Response Framework (SRF). The SRF is a key component exercised through SIMEX to ensure the UK’s financial services industry is ready to respond effectively and in a coordinated manner to severe disruption.
4. The scenario was not informed by any specific threat information but was developed alongside a range of industry experts.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened (dated).** On **8 October 2026**, the Bank of England–and-UK Finance-chaired **Cross-Market Operational Resilience Group (CMORG)** completed its biennial market-wide simulation, **SIMEX26**, this year themed on a **global cloud-services disruption**. The **38 largest and most systemically important** UK banks and financial market infrastructures (FMIs) took part, alongside HM Treasury, the FCA and the PRA. The exercise was opened by the Economic Secretary to the Treasury (Lucy Rigby) and hosted by the London Stock Exchange Group, and ran a live meeting of the Bank-chaired **Cross Market Business Continuity Group (CMBCG)** under the sector's **Sector Response Framework (SRF)**. The BoE announcement was dated 9 October; the scenario was not based on any specific threat. The Bank says it now **expects firms to take action** to strengthen resilience. (Source: bankofengland.co.uk CMORG release; Law360 "BoE Urges Banks To Act On Cloud Disruption Test Findings".)

**Why cloud, why now — the regulatory thread.** SIMEX26's cloud theme lands directly on top of the UK's new **Critical Third Party (CTP) regime**. On **13 July 2026**, HM Treasury designated four hyperscalers — **Amazon Web Services EMEA SARL, Microsoft Ireland Operations, Google Cloud EMEA and Oracle UK** — as CTPs, bringing them under **direct joint oversight** of the BoE, PRA and FCA (scenario testing, phased incident reporting, self-assessments, skilled-person reviews). Notably, **FSMA gives regulators no power to fine CTPs** — oversight, not enforcement teeth. In parallel, the **CMA's cloud-market investigation** found UK cloud heavily concentrated in a few hands and recommended Strategic Market Status (SMS) probes of Microsoft and AWS. SIMEX26 is the operational-resilience leg of that same cloud-concentration-risk policy push.

**Internal corpus — prior outages the drill is implicitly rehearsing.**
- [[AWS outage disrupts UK banks' online services]] (21 Oct 2025) — the live precedent: a single AWS region failure took down card payments and online banking across multiple UK banks. This is the closest real-world analogue to the SIMEX26 scenario and almost certainly part of its motivation.
- [[Coinbase suffers multi-hour trading outage after AWS failure]] (11 May 2026) and [[Worldpay outage leaves UK pubs and shops cash-only during England match]] (25 Jun 2026) — single-provider dependencies cascading into customer-facing payment failures.
- [[UAE central bank waives data residency rules after AWS outage]] (Mar 2026) — a regulator forced into reactive concessions by cloud/datacentre disruption; contrast with the UK's proactive drill-and-designate approach.
- [[Yandex Pay still down after Yandex data-center outages]] (12 Oct 2026, importance 3/5) — contemporaneous datacentre-outage story, same operational-resilience theme in a different jurisdiction.

**History of the programme.** SIMEX is biennial, run by CMORG (established by the BoE in 2015). **SIMEX 2018** reported publicly; **SIMEX 2022** and **SIMEX 2024** (2 Oct 2024) tested cyber-attacks on a major bank and a total sector shutdown-and-restart on major infrastructure failure. SIMEX26 is the first edition to centre the scenario on **cloud concentration** specifically — a thematic shift reflecting hyperscaler dependence.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team / challenge questions**

1. **What did firms actually fail at?** The release is self-congratulatory ("important demonstration"). The real signal is the *findings* — Law360 flags "act on findings," but neither the BoE note nor our source discloses specific gaps. Without the findings report, "success" is unverifiable. What were the weak points?
2. **Is a tabletop exercise a proxy for real resilience?** SIMEX is a coordinated, pre-scheduled crisis-management exercise with experts in the room — not a surprise failure. Does it test anything like the Oct 2025 AWS outage's actual chaos, or just the sector's *communication* choreography?
3. **"Global cloud disruption" — single provider or multi-cloud?** A true hyperscaler failure is usually regional/zonal, not global. Did the scenario model a realistic AWS/Azure region loss, or an implausibly total "all cloud down" event that no mitigation could survive anyway?
4. **CTP regime has no fine power.** If the BoE/PRA/FCA can't penalise AWS/Microsoft/Google/Oracle, what forces a hyperscaler to change behaviour based on a drill the *banks* ran? The leverage is on the regulated firms, not the concentration source.
5. **Does the drill address concentration, or just response?** Running a crisis exercise doesn't reduce the underlying dependence on 3–4 US cloud providers. Is this treating the symptom (incident response) rather than the disease (concentration)?
6. **Why only 38 firms?** Systemic risk propagates through the long tail of mid-size banks, PSPs and fintechs (cf. Worldpay, Coinbase) not in the room. Does excluding them understate contagion?
7. **Exit/portability untested?** The hard problem is that firms *cannot* fail over off a hyperscaler quickly. Did SIMEX26 test multi-cloud failover and data egress, or assume cloud comes back?
8. **Timing vs CTP go-live.** Drill is 3 months after 13 Jul 2026 designation. Is this substantive oversight or a PR beat to show the new regime "working" before it has teeth?
9. **No specific threat basis.** Scenario "not informed by any specific threat" — does an abstract scenario under-prepare for the real attack vectors (supply-chain, config error, as in CrowdStrike/AWS 2025)?
10. **Who bears the cost of "take action"?** BoE expects firms to invest in resilience. Multi-cloud + exit planning is expensive and anti-margin for smaller players — regulatory burden concentration risk?
11. **Does this change anything measurable?** After SIMEX 18/22/24, UK banks still went down in the Oct 2025 AWS outage. What is the evidence prior drills improved real-world uptime?
12. **Cross-border gap.** Hyperscalers serve EU/US too; a global cloud event is not UK-containable. Does a UK-only sector exercise matter if the providers and failure are global (cf. UAE forced to waive data-residency rules)?
13. **Transparency.** Will a public SIMEX26 findings report be published (as SIMEX 2018 was), or only a press release? Opacity limits market accountability.

**Importance: 3/5** — Materially relevant regulatory/operational-resilience signal: ties the new UK CTP regime (AWS/Azure/GCP/Oracle designated 13 Jul 2026) to a concrete sector-wide drill, against a backdrop of real 2025–26 cloud outages that hit UK banks. But it is a non-binding, no-new-rules event with no disclosed findings, no fine power over hyperscalers, and no change to underlying concentration — directional/thematic rather than deal- or P&L-moving. Not a 4: no enforcement, no numbers, no market-structure change announced.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Not a company deal — this is operational-resilience *regulation* in UK financial infrastructure. The subject is SIMEX26, CMORG's biennial sector-wide crisis exercise (completed 8 Oct 2026), this cycle scenario-testing a *global cloud services disruption* with the 38 largest UK banks and market-infrastructure operators, hosted by LSEG (per Bank of England news release, 2026-10). The "why now": the UK's Critical Third Parties (CTP) regime went live 13 Jul 2026, with HM Treasury making the first-ever designations under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2023 — AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft and Oracle — giving the Bank/PRA/FCA direct oversight powers (scenario testing, phased incident reporting, skilled-person reviews) over the hyperscalers themselves (per cloudswitched.com / operationalresilienceuk.co.uk, 2026). SIMEX26 is the live-fire counterpart to that rule-making. Sizing the underlying concentration risk: AWS ~28%, Azure ~21%, Google Cloud ~14% of a ~$129bn/quarter market — the big three = ~63% of global cloud infrastructure (per Statista/CloudZero, Q1 2026). Academic/regulatory estimate: a 3–6 day outage at one of the top-three US cloud providers could cause up to ~$15bn of total losses (per SSRN Gulliver/Nadler/Scott, via fintech.global/thefinanser.com, 2026). Structure: cloud infra is *consolidated* (oligopoly, massive capex barriers) while the financial sector consuming it is fragmented — the classic systemic-risk shape (every firm safer in the cloud, the system more fragile because they're all on the *same* clouds).

**Competitive landscape.** No commercial competition here — the "players" are regulators and the regulated. KPIs that matter for this theme: cloud market share / provider concentration (HHI-style), number of designated CTPs (4), recovery-time objectives, and incident-reporting SLAs. Regulatory moves on the clock: UK CTP designations live 13 Jul 2026 [[UK to oversee critical third-party cloud service providers]]; EU DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act) already in force since Jan 2025 with its own parallel Critical ICT Third-Party Provider oversight — the UK regime is the near-twin, though the two differ on scope and the UK is explicit that "designation is not authorisation" (per bratby.law, 2026). SIMEX precedent: 2022 and 2024 exercises rehearsed cyber-attacks on a major bank and non-financial infrastructure disruption — 2026 is the first cloud-specific scenario, signalling where the regulator's probability mass now sits. Protagonist (the Bank/CMORG) position: *ahead* of most jurisdictions on codified sector-wide rehearsal + a live CTP oversight regime; the US has FSB/EBA-style hand-wringing but no equivalent designation power yet (per diginomica, bankingjournal.aba).

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation subject — comps are *incident/regime* precedents in-base, not trading multiples, so EV/Revenue etc. = n/a. Internal comps grounding the concentration thesis: [[Worldpay outage leaves UK pubs and shops cash-only during England match]] (Jun 2026, single-vendor payments outage hit UK retail — exactly the real-world shape SIMEX rehearses); [[Amazon fixes bug that billed some AWS customers billions]] (Jul 2026, hyperscaler operational fragility, billing vector); [[Intesa Sanpaolo completes core IT migration to Google Cloud]] (Jul 2026, a G-SIB moving core banking onto a single hyperscaler — the migration trend that *drives* the concentration regulators are testing); [[UK to oversee critical third-party cloud service providers]] (Jul 2026, the enabling CTP regime). Distribution not computed — qualitative, no comparable financial figures. Trading multiples for the designated vendors (AMZN/MSFT/GOOGL/ORCL) exist but are not meaningful comps for a regulatory exercise — [UNSOURCED] here by design.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Concentration / single-point-of-failure risk** — 4 designated CTPs, big-three ~63% share; a multi-day outage at one hyperscaler is estimated at up to ~$15bn in losses. Second-order: correlated failure across the 38 systemic firms simultaneously, not idiosyncratic — the exact tail the drill exists to pre-empt.
2. **"Designation ≠ resilience / designation ≠ approval"** — regulators explicitly disclaim that oversight makes a provider safer; firms retain full accountability. Risk of false comfort / moral hazard: boards may treat CTP status as a safe-harbour it is not (per cloudswitched.com, 2026).
3. **Exercise-to-action gap** — the Bank "now expects firms to take action"; a tabletop/simulation surfaces gaps but does not fix them. Execution risk that findings aren't operationalised before a real cloud event; no public remediation deadline disclosed (de-PR: the release is silent on cost, concrete RTOs, and whether any firm failed the scenario).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Cloud concentration is crystallising from a policy talking-point into a *supervised, stress-tested* regime — UK CTP live + DORA + a cloud-scenario SIMEX together mark the transition. Falsifiable thesis: the next 12 months bring concrete prescriptive requirements on the 4 CTPs (mandatory multi-region/multi-cloud failover, incident-reporting SLAs) that raise compliance cost for banks adopting single-hyperscaler core migrations — watch the PRA/FCA CTP rulebook and any SIMEX26 published findings as the trigger. What breaks it: if the exercise stays purely voluntary with no enforceable follow-through, concentration keeps compounding unchecked. Beneficiaries to watch: multi-cloud/resilience tooling vendors and exit-strategy consultants.
