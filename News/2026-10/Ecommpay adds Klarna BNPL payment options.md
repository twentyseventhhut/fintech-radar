 Ecommpay Adds Klarna, Giving Consumers a More Flexible Way to Pay 
 Lending
 13.10.2026 10:17 am 
 Ecommpay, the inclusive global payments platform, has added Klarna, the everyday finance network, to its payment choices, giving merchants a simple way to offer millions of shoppers greater choice, transparency and control at checkout. 
 “Flexible payment options are fundamental to a strong checkout experience for e-commerce merchants and Klarna has been a popular choice for consumers and businesses,” explained Roman Tazetdinov, Head of Strategic Partnerships, Ecommpay.  “Used by millions of shoppers every month, we are confident adding Klarna to the Ecommpay payment platform will deliver real conversion gains for our merchants.” 
 "Shoppers today expect to pay their way: no faff, no friction, no nasty surprises," said Klarna Country Manager UK & Ireland Marc O'Fathaigh. "Ecommpay is a natural fit: we both believe good ways to pay should be available to everyone. Through a single integration, this partnership puts Klarna in front of merchants worldwide, expanding our reach and giving millions of shoppers more control over their money." 
 From small businesses to global enterprises across retail, travel and hospitality, fintech, mobility, digital goods and insurance, Ecommpay merchants gain access to Klarna’s flexible payment options including Pay in Full, Pay in 3 and Pay in 4 instalments, Pay in 30 days, and longer-term Financing, via one easy integration with no extra technical work or third-party systems needed. 
 “We know that many shoppers actively look for Klarna at checkout,” added Roman Tazetdinov. “Adding it to the Ecommpay payment platform gives merchants access to a large and engaged market, reducing hesitation at the point of purchase and giving customers another payment option at checkout. And it can be added to a merchant’s wider payment set-up in a single integration, eliminating unnecessary operational overhead.”

# Context-enrichment: Ecommpay adds Klarna BNPL payment options
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- **Ecommpay** (London-based full-stack PSP + direct card acquirer, founded 2012, FCA-authorised FRN 607597, Visa/Mastercard Principal Member, ~$361m revenue, 500–1,000 staff) has **added Klarna as a payment method** to its gateway. Announced 13 Oct 2026; press carried 14 Oct. Merchants on Ecommpay can now offer Klarna's **full suite — Pay in Full, Pay in 3, Pay in 4, Pay in 30 days, and longer-term (interest-bearing) Financing — through a single integration, "no extra technical work or third-party systems"**. Target sectors named: retail, travel & hospitality, fintech, mobility, digital goods, insurance.
- **This is a PSP-level distribution deal, NOT a merchant deal and NOT a bespoke build.** It is Klarna becoming selectable in Ecommpay's method catalogue — standard Klarna partner-onboarding. The substantive value-add is purely **one-integration reach**: any current/future Ecommpay merchant can switch Klarna on without its own Klarna integration.
- **De-PR'd:** no numbers whatsoever — no merchant count gaining Klarna, no GMV/volume target, no conversion-lift data, no fee/take-rate, no exclusivity, no live-merchant proof points. The quotes (Roman Tazetdinov, Ecommpay Head of Strategic Partnerships; Marc O'Fathaigh, Klarna Country Manager UK&I) are pure "more choice / conversion gains" boilerplate. The one asymmetry worth noting: the Klarna quote is UK&Ireland-scoped ("Country Manager UK & Ireland"), while the body claims "merchants worldwide" — the real initial footprint is almost certainly UK/EU, where both Klarna and Ecommpay are strongest, with "worldwide" aspirational.
- **Why framed this way:** both sides want the "reach / flexibility / conversion" story because the actual mechanism is unglamorous (a method toggle). Ecommpay foregrounds "single integration, no overhead" because frictionless method-addition is literally the PSP value proposition it sells; Klarna foregrounds "in front of merchants worldwide, expanding our reach" because post-IPO its equity story *is* distribution breadth (see [4]). Neither discloses economics because the PSP sits between Klarna and the merchant on the fee split — the silent part.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **The competitive truth that guts the novelty:** the big-four acquirers/PSPs **already** offer Klarna. Klarna is live/ramping as a **default** option with **Stripe** (new merchants get Klarna unless they opt out) and **Nexi**; set to go live with **Worldpay** and **JPMorgan Payments** during 2026; **Adyen and Checkout.com** have long carried Klarna. In the corpus: [[Worldline and Klarna partner on flexible online and in-store payments]] (May 2026 framework deal — Europe's largest card acquirer, Global Collect/GoPay + in-store POS) and [[Klarna and Worldpay bring installment payments to Italy's pagoPA]] (Oct 2025). Against that set, **Ecommpay is a mid-tier follower** adding a method every large rival already has.
- **Ecommpay's own BNPL/credit history:** [[Ecommpay partners humm for travel and retail finance]] (Oct 2025) — Ecommpay already bolted on *humm* for higher-value (up to £3,000) online finance in travel/retail, explicitly "beyond current BNPL". So Ecommpay was already building a multi-provider credit shelf; Klarna is an **addition to an existing credit strategy**, not a first move into BNPL.
- **Position:** behind/parity. Klarna via Stripe/Adyen/Checkout.com/Worldpay/Worldline is the established channel; Ecommpay adding Klarna is table-stakes catch-up so its merchants don't leak to acquirers with a richer method list. Second-order: in the PSP layer, **payment-method breadth is a defensive hygiene factor**, not a differentiator — not having Klarna loses deals, having it wins nothing special. That is exactly why this is a low-signal item.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Ecommpay's 2025–26 trajectory (corpus) is a steady drumbeat of **method/feature breadth + checkout-conversion messaging**: open-banking suite, Click to Pay with Mastercard, Google/Apple Pay express checkout, network tokenization, orchestration (BridgerPay), acquiring access (NORBr), humm finance, and multiple "checkout wins / conversion" reports. Klarna fits this pattern perfectly — one more high-demand method on the shelf.
- **Why Ecommpay acts this way (structural):** a mid-sized PSP/acquirer competes against Stripe/Adyen/Checkout.com/Worldline on **method coverage and conversion**, because card-acquiring take-rates are commoditised and the way to defend/grow merchant share is "we support every way your shoppers want to pay, in one integration". Adding the single most-asked-for consumer BNPL brand (Klarna) is the lowest-risk, highest-recognition move available. It is rational and expected — and therefore not newsworthy beyond confirmation.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** Klarna-via-PSP is a well-worn channel; Ecommpay is late to it relative to Stripe/Adyen/Checkout.com/Worldline. Nothing in the mechanism is new. The *only* genuinely useful fact is the breadth of the Klarna suite exposed (Pay in Full/3/4/30-days/Financing) through one integration — but that is Klarna's standard product set, not an Ecommpay innovation.
- **Traction: unproven.** Zero adoption data. No "X merchants now live", no conversion lift, no GMV. "Announced", not quantified. For a method-addition this is normal, but it means the item carries no evidence of impact.
- **Who captures the margin (the real question):** in a PSP-brokered BNPL deal the stack is merchant → Ecommpay (gateway/acquirer fee + its cut) → Klarna (its merchant fee + interest on Financing) → consumer. Ecommpay earns incremental processing/orchestration volume and stickiness; Klarna earns incremental GMV and reach into merchants it would otherwise have to sign one-by-one. The consumer gets free credit on Pay-in-3/4 and pays on Financing. **The durable value for Ecommpay is retention** (don't lose merchants to richer PSPs), not new revenue; the durable value for Klarna is **cheap distribution** (one integration = many merchants). Neither party discloses the fee split — the economics are deliberately silent.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **The real story is Klarna's, not Ecommpay's.** Post-NYSE IPO (Sep 2025; ticker KLAR), Klarna's equity thesis *is* distribution: >1m merchants (+~49% YoY), and an explicit strategy to be the **default** BNPL option inside every major PSP/acquirer (Stripe live, Nexi live, Worldpay + JPMorgan in 2026, Worldline framework, now Ecommpay). Adding mid-tier PSPs like Ecommpay is **volume filler that broadens the funnel**, consistent with the pattern captured in the same-day sibling [[TAP expands Klarna installment payments to four markets]] (a single-merchant travel geo-expansion) — together they evidence Klarna's **two-front land-grab: PSP/acquirer integrations + direct merchant/airline deals**.
- **Sentiment / numbers (Klarna):** the distribution push is happening against a *margin-over-volume* pivot. Klarna Q2 2026 (reported 25 Aug 2026): revenue $1.042bn (+27% YoY, a decel from +44% in Q1), GMV $36.6bn (+18%), swing to GAAP net income ($9m), credit-loss provision 0.52% of GMV (improving), **but FY2026 GMV/revenue guidance was CUT** while transaction-margin guidance was RAISED. Read-through: with same-store volume maturing, Klarna leans harder on distribution breadth (exactly these PSP adds) to defend GMV — which is *why* Ecommpay-type announcements keep coming.
- **Risks / regulatory:** EU BNPL is being pulled under the revised **Consumer Credit Directive (CCD2, ~2026 transposition)** — affordability checks + disclosure, biting the interest-bearing Financing more than Pay-in-3/4; raises cost-to-serve in Klarna's and Ecommpay's core EU markets. For Ecommpay specifically there is **no downside** to adding Klarna (liability and underwriting sit with Klarna); the risk is purely Klarna's.
- **Second-order:** because every major acquirer already carries Klarna, Ecommpay adding it **neither moves Klarna's thesis nor differentiates Ecommpay** — it is one more tick confirming that Klarna-at-the-PSP-layer is becoming ubiquitous/commoditised. The interesting forward question is not "who else adds Klarna" (everyone will) but "once Klarna is the default everywhere, does its per-transaction economics compress?" — ubiquity is reach today, commoditisation/price pressure tomorrow.

## FRESHNESS / DUPLICATE VERDICT
- **FRESH (narrowly), low weight.** This is a genuine new, specific event: Ecommpay (a named PSP) adding Klarna on 13–14 Oct 2026. The corpus has **no prior Ecommpay+Klarna note** — nearest Ecommpay-credit item is [[Ecommpay partners humm for travel and retail finance]] (a *different* provider, humm, not Klarna), and nearest Klarna-PSP items are [[Worldline and Klarna partner on flexible online and in-store payments]] and [[Klarna and Worldpay bring installment payments to Italy's pagoPA]] (*different* acquirers). They are complementary data points in Klarna's two-front distribution story, not restatements. No `duplicate_of`.

### Red-team / challenge questions

1. **Is this a PSP-level deal or a single-merchant deal?** Answered: PSP-level. Ecommpay (a payment gateway + direct card acquirer) adds Klarna as a selectable method for its whole merchant base via one integration — distinct from a single merchant (e.g. TAP airline) embedding Klarna.

2. **Is it actually new, or does every big acquirer already carry Klarna?** Answered: largely not new. Stripe and Nexi are live (Stripe as a default); Worldpay and JPMorgan go live in 2026; Adyen, Checkout.com and Worldline already carry Klarna. Ecommpay is a mid-tier follower — low novelty.

3. **Has Ecommpay done BNPL before?** Answered: yes. [[Ecommpay partners humm for travel and retail finance]] (Oct 2025) added humm for higher-value (up to £3,000) finance. Klarna is an *addition* to an existing multi-provider credit shelf, not a first BNPL move.

4. **Live or just announced? How many merchants actually have Klarna on now?** Open/unproven. Zero adoption numbers — no merchant count, GMV, or conversion-lift. "Announced", not quantified traction.

5. **"Merchants worldwide" — is the real footprint global?** Answered (analysis): likely UK/EU-first. The Klarna spokesperson is "Country Manager UK & Ireland"; "worldwide" is aspirational. Initial reach is almost certainly Klarna/Ecommpay's strong EU markets.

6. **Which Klarna products, and which is the money product?** Answered: full suite — Pay in Full, Pay in 3, Pay in 4, Pay in 30 days, Financing. Pay-in-3/4 are interest-free (the marketing hook); the long-term **Financing** is interest-bearing and the real economics.

7. **Who captures the margin in the merchant → Ecommpay → Klarna → consumer stack?** Answered (analysis): Klarna earns its merchant fee + interest on Financing; Ecommpay earns incremental processing/orchestration volume + merchant stickiness; the fee split between them is undisclosed — the silent economics.

8. **Why would Ecommpay bother if everyone has Klarna?** Answered (analysis): defensive hygiene. Method breadth is table-stakes for a mid-tier PSP competing with Stripe/Adyen/Checkout.com/Worldline; missing Klarna loses merchants, adding it wins little — hence low signal.

9. **Does this move Klarna's thesis?** Answered: no. One mid-tier PSP is volume filler in Klarna's post-IPO distribution land-grab (>1m merchants, +~49% YoY; PSP-default strategy). Marginal, not thesis-moving.

10. **Is it a duplicate of the same-day TAP×Klarna item?** Answered: no. Different actor (PSP vs airline), mechanism (whole-base method distribution vs single-merchant geo-rollout), and scale. Complementary evidence of Klarna's two-front distribution, not a restatement.

11. **Who bears fraud / credit liability?** Answered (standard, not disclosed): Klarna underwrites the consumer credit and takes default risk; Ecommpay/merchant pay fees. No downside risk sits with Ecommpay — the regulatory/credit exposure is Klarna's.

12. **Regulatory exposure?** Answered (partial): EU CCD2 (~2026 transposition) brings BNPL affordability/disclosure rules, biting the interest-bearing Financing more than Pay-in-3/4; raises Klarna's cost-to-serve in core EU markets. Ecommpay is insulated.

13. **Any exclusivity or volume commitment?** Open — none disclosed. Appears to be standard non-exclusive Klarna PSP onboarding; Ecommpay keeps humm and other providers in parallel.

14. **What's the second-order risk of Klarna being everywhere?** Answered (analysis): ubiquity = reach today, commoditisation/price pressure tomorrow. Once Klarna is the default across all PSPs, its per-transaction economics may compress — the forward question, not "who adds it next".

15. **Is the "no extra technical work / worldwide reach" framing substantive?** Answered: it's accurate but unglamorous — the whole value-add is a method toggle (Klarna's standard onboarding), which is literally the PSP value proposition. No bespoke build, no proprietary mechanism.

Importance: 2/5 — A genuinely fresh but low-weight item: a mid-tier UK PSP/acquirer adds Klarna as a method via one integration, catching up to Stripe/Adyen/Checkout.com/Worldline/Worldpay who already offer it. No disclosed traction, no novelty, no new economics. Matters only as one more confirmation of Klarna's post-IPO PSP-distribution land-grab (and of BNPL-at-the-PSP-layer becoming commoditised hygiene); by itself not thesis-moving.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is BNPL *distribution via a PSP/acquirer*, not a new consumer lender — Ecommpay (a global payments platform/gateway) adds Klarna as a checkout method behind its single integration, so the economically interesting layer is the *acquiring/orchestration* stack, not credit. BNPL is a mature, consolidating alternative-payment method: the public comparables (Klarna, Affirm, Zip, Afterpay/Block) are now growth-but-profitability names rather than land-grab start-ups, and distribution has shifted from direct merchant sign-ups to being *one toggle inside* acquirers/orchestrators (Worldpay, Worldline, Checkout.com, Ecommpay). Regulation is the "why now": the UK FCA brings BNPL under regulation (affordability checks, s.75-style protections) on a 2026 timeline, and the EU Consumer Credit Directive (CCD2) extends credit rules to BNPL — raising compliance cost and favouring scaled, licensed providers like Klarna over thin-margin new entrants (analysis). Second-order effect: regulatory burden pushes BNPL toward licensed banks/large platforms, exactly the direction Klarna (now a US-listed, deposit-funded "everyday finance network") is taking.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: GMV, take rate (revenue/GMV), transaction-margin dollars, credit-loss rate (% of GMV), active consumers/merchants. Klarna's position vs peers from primary IR (Q2'26, period to 30 Jun 2026):
- GMV $36.6bn, +18% YoY (US +27%);
- Revenue $1,042m, +27% YoY → **implied take rate 1,042/36,600 = 2.85%**;
- Transaction-margin dollars $446m, +42% YoY (42.8% of revenue);
- Net income $9m (vs −$53m Q2'25) — swung to profit;
- Credit losses **0.52% of GMV** (vs 0.56% Q2'25) — improving;
- 120m active consumers (+8%), 1.2m+ merchants (+54% — distribution expanding fast, which is exactly what deals like Ecommpay feed);
- FY26 GMV guide *cut* to $149–151bn (from >$155bn) on soft German consumer + FX.
(Klarna Q2'26 earnings release, SEC 6-K, 2026-08.) Basis of competition in *distribution* = breadth of PSP/acquirer coverage, not price. Recent peer/channel moves in the base: Klarna via acquirers/orchestrators — [[Worldline and Klarna partner on flexible online and in-store payments]], [[Klarna and Worldpay bring installment payments to Italy's pagoPA]]; direct-merchant wins [[Klarna deepens Wayfair partnership with 0% APR financing]], [[Klarna partners The White Company on BNPL homeware]]; and a parallel Klarna-on-PSP deal the same week, [[TAP expands Klarna installment payments to four markets]]. Ecommpay's position = niche PSP widening its APM shelf; moat is merchant switching costs + integration breadth (analysis), not credit.

**Comps & multiples.** For the *protagonist Ecommpay* there is no data — private PSP, no disclosed valuation/revenue → multiple = no data. For the BNPL method being distributed:
- **Klarna (KLAR, NYSE):** mkt cap ≈ $7.4–7.8bn (mid-Oct 2026); TTM revenue $4.04bn (to 30 Jun 2026) → **P/S ≈ 7.4 / 4.04 ≈ 1.8x**; EV/Rev ≈ 1.9x (per stockanalysis.com/wallstreetzen, Oct 2026).
- **Affirm (AFRM):** mkt cap ≈ $23bn; FY26 (to 30 Jun) revenue $4.26bn, GMV $50.2bn → **P/S ≈ 23 / 4.26 ≈ 5.4x**; EV/Rev ≈ 7.1x (EV ≈ $30.4bn).
- Internal round-stage BNPL comps from base: [[Gulf Startup Tabby nabs $4.5 billion valuation in secondary sale]], [[Zilch invites banks to pitch ahead of IPO]], [[Zip shares jump 12% on strong update and buyback]].
Flag: with only 2 clean public comps, **distribution not computed — qualitative**. Klarna trades *cheap* vs Affirm (≈1.8x vs ≈5.4x P/S) despite similar revenue scale — the gap reflects Affirm's faster GMV growth (+36% vs +18%) and cleaner US-growth story, while Klarna carries German-consumer softness and a *cut* FY guide. Neither is an over-valuation flag; the spread is growth-justified (analysis).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Zero visible economics for Ecommpay** — a "single integration, no extra technical work" PR says nothing about who bears fraud liability, what MDR/interchange Ecommpay keeps vs passes to Klarna, or ticket size; Ecommpay may simply be a pass-through pipe earning routing margin, not BNPL economics. Second-order: little durable value accrues to the PSP from an APM that merchants can also get via Worldpay/Worldline/Checkout.com.
2. **Disintermediation / commoditisation of distribution** — Klarna is simultaneously on TAP, Worldpay, Worldline and others the same period; "available everywhere" means no PSP has exclusivity, so Ecommpay's add is table-stakes catch-up, not differentiation.
3. **Regulatory + credit-cycle overhang on the method itself** — UK FCA / EU CCD2 bring affordability checks and consumer-credit obligations to BNPL; combined with Klarna's own FY26 GMV cut on soft German spend, demand for the distributed product could soften just as Ecommpay onboards it (announced, not yet volume-proven).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a distribution *catch-up*, not a re-rating event — it signals BNPL has fully graduated from a merchant feature to an orchestration-layer default that every mid-tier PSP must carry (analysis). Watch: whether Ecommpay discloses merchant uptake/attach or the economics of the deal (falsifiable thesis — if it stays silent on volumes within 1–2 quarters, treat it as pure shelf-space, value-neutral for Ecommpay). Catalyst to re-test: Klarna's Q3'26 print (does non-US GMV re-accelerate, validating the "expand reach via PSPs" thesis) and UK FCA BNPL rulemaking timing.

## Earnings Review

> Earnings layer on the IR-covered counterparty **Klarna** (NYSE: KLAR, IPO Sep-2025), the BNPL rail Ecommpay is distributing — not on Ecommpay (private, no financials). Anchored to Klarna's latest reported period: **Q2 2026** (reported 2026-08, SEC 6-K accession 000162828026057573). The Ecommpay deal is a PSP-distribution win; the question below is whether such wins move Klarna's needle.

**Verdict (headline read).** BEAT on the print, but a **MISS on the forward** — and the market traded the forward. Revenue $1.042bn (+27% YoY; vs public consensus ~$992.8m, a +4.75% beat); diluted EPS $0.01 vs consensus −$0.05 (+120% surprise). Transaction-margin dollars +42% and first GAAP net income ($9m) are the real beats. Yet management **cut full-year GMV/revenue guidance** (soft German demand + FX) while **raising** the margin-dollar guide — and the stock fell ~**-24.5%** on the day. Growth deceleration overshadowed the profitability inflection.

**Key figures (Q2 2026, with growth).**
- Revenue **$1.042bn, +27% YoY** (beat public consensus ~$992.8m).
- GMV **$36.6bn, +18% YoY** (U.S. GMV $7.9bn, +27%).
- Take rate (revenue / GMV) **≈2.85%** — revenue outgrowing volume by ~9pp, i.e. monetization up, not just volume.
- Transaction-margin dollars **$446m, +42% YoY** — well ahead of both revenue (+27%) and GMV (+18%); transaction margin **42.8% of revenue**, +4.5pp YoY.
- Consumer credit-loss rate **0.52% of GMV** (vs 0.56% in Q2'25) — losses improving even as volume grew.
- Adjusted operating income **$91m, +214% YoY**.
- Operating income **$27m** (vs **−$46m** loss in Q2'25); **net income $9m** (vs **−$53m**) — first GAAP-positive quarter.
- Active consumers **120m, +8% YoY**; revenue per active consumer **+24%** (monetization-per-user, not user-count, is the growth engine).
- Active merchants **1.2m+, +54% YoY** — the distribution-footprint metric most relevant to the Ecommpay thesis.

**By segment / driver.** Growth is quality-led: transaction-margin dollars (+42%) >> GMV (+18%), driven by U.S. (margin dollars +126% YoY there; U.S. transaction margin rose to 23% of revenue from 14%) and by revenue-per-consumer (+24%) rather than consumer adds (+8%). Fair Financing GMV +82% YoY to $4.7bn across 256k merchants — the higher-take long-tenor product is the mix-shift lever. The soft spot: GMV/volume growth is decelerating, concentrated in weak German demand.

**vs expectations / prior period.** Revenue beat public consensus by ~**+$49m / +4.75%**; EPS beat by **+$0.06** (−$0.05 expected → +$0.01). vs prior quarter [[TAP expands Klarna installment payments to four markets]] context: profitability trajectory is clearly inflecting (op income −$46m → +$27m YoY). The beat is sustainable in quality (margin + credit both improving), but the top-line beat is partly offset by the guide cut on the same metric. (Consensus from public aggregators as reported 2026-08; label "public consensus", not Street.)

**Guidance / forward.** Full-year 2026 (USD; filing states dollars, not SEK — a web aggregator mislabeled it): **GMV cut to $149–151bn** from >$155bn; **revenue cut to $4.08–4.16bn**; but **transaction-margin dollars raised to $1.62–1.65bn** and adjusted operating income guided **$280–300m**. So: volume/revenue down, profitability up — management is prioritizing unit economics over GMV-at-any-cost, blaming German demand and FX. Tone: confident on margin ("transaction margin dollars grew 42%, well ahead of revenue and volume"), quieter on the volume cut. The market read the GMV cut as the signal (-24.5%).

**Thesis-flags (does a PSP-distribution win like Ecommpay move the needle?).**
1. **Reach is already saturated at the top; the margin is in monetization.** Merchants +54% to 1.2m and consumers 120m — Klarna's constraint is not distribution breadth but revenue-per-consumer (+24%) and take-rate. A single PSP integration (Ecommpay) adds incremental merchant reach but is immaterial against a 1.2m-merchant base and a $36.6bn-quarterly-GMV rail. Second-order: these deals matter as cheap, low-CAC volume top-ups, not as needle-movers.
2. **Quality-over-volume is now the explicit strategy** (guide: GMV down, margin up). PSP wins that bring high-conversion checkout volume feed transaction-margin dollars (the metric they *raised*), so Ecommpay is directionally aligned — but one PSP won't offset a German-demand air-pocket.
3. **Credit discipline holding (0.52% loss rate, improving)** de-risks the "BNPL = bad loans" bear case; distribution growth is not coming at the cost of underwriting. Watch next quarter for whether the GMV deceleration is German/FX (cyclical) or structural.
4. **De-PR:** the Ecommpay/TAP-style "added Klarna" releases are partner-marketing, not revenue events — Klarna's own disclosures tie growth to monetization and the U.S., not to new PSP logos. Treat "Ecommpay adds Klarna" as reach confirmation, not a catalyst.
