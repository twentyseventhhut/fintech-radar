Arf and Ripple bring RLUSD settlement liquidity to the XRP Ledger
Sinziana Albu
23 Oct 2026 / 5 Min Read
Switzerland-based Arf will supply short-duration working capital in RLUSD, a regulated US dollar stablecoin, to licensed payment, credit card, and remittance companies on the XRP Ledger (XRPL). The capital pre-funds cross-border transactions and is repaid as those payments settle. Under the agreement with Ripple, the company plans to route part of this activity through XRPL’s native lending functionality once that infrastructure becomes available.
The working capital gap in cross-border payments
Licensed firms that move funds across borders must hold working capital in multiple markets so they can pre-fund payment flows while they wait for settlement. According to the companies, the issue is not only whether a firm has capital overall. It is whether the capital is in the right market at the right time.
Traditional financing has not always matched the speed of modern payment flows. Bank credit lines can take months to establish and generally require substantial collateral. That ties capital to a single location, which suits payment companies poorly because their liquidity needs to move between markets.
PayFi and Arf’s operating model
Arf operates in payment finance, or PayFi, a category that builds treasury, FX, custody, and investment products around real-time payment flows and the time value of money. PayFi covers settlement liquidity, receivables financing, and treasury management across fiat and stablecoins. Arf and Huma Finance were early developers of the category. At the same time, Arf provides the regulated infrastructure, and Huma converts that regulated activity into onchain yield assets. The companies identify settlement liquidity as PayFi’s main use case at present.
Ali Erhat Nalbant, Co-founder and CEO at Arf, said the partnership brings stablecoin working capital to the XRPL to help payment firms cover the gap before funds settle and to support higher payment volumes. In addition, Jasmine Cooper, Head of Product for RippleX at Ripple, described settlement liquidity as the type of use case XRPL and RLUSD were designed for. In that view, the ledger provides fast settlement, and RLUSD provides a regulated dollar stablecoin.
Phased rollout and native lending
In the first phase, Arf will provide RLUSD settlement liquidity directly on the XRPL. This liquidity is typically repaid within one to five days as cross-border payments settle, so the same capital can be redeployed repeatedly against payment flows.
Once XRPL’s native lending infrastructure goes live, Arf intends to move more of its settlement liquidity activity onto the protocol. Under this model, the capital behind the liquidity would be managed onchain and deployed through native lending functionality. Arf would continue to make underwriting and working capital decisions offchain.
The arrangement links onchain lending to volumes from traditional payment companies rather than to activity that originates on the blockchain. For the XRPL, it adds a PayFi use case built around licensed payment firms. Moreover, for those firms, it offers short-duration working capital that can move across markets as an alternative to collateral-heavy bank credit lines.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened (2026-10-22/23).** Ripple and Switzerland-based **Arf** announced a partnership to bring **RLUSD settlement liquidity** to the **XRP Ledger (XRPL)**. Arf will supply short-duration working capital in RLUSD — Ripple's regulated USD stablecoin — to licensed payment, credit-card and remittance companies. The capital pre-funds cross-border transactions and is repaid as those payments settle, typically **within one to five days**, so the same dollars can be redeployed repeatedly against payment flows.

**Who Arf is.** Arf is a **PayFi** (payment finance) provider that supplies on-chain settlement/working-capital liquidity to licensed money-transfer operators (MTOs). Arf **merged with Huma Finance in April 2024**: Arf provides the regulated, off-chain underwriting and working-capital infrastructure, while Huma converts that activity into on-chain yield assets (Huma has run "Arf Cross-Border Payment Financing" pools on Solana). Co-founder & CEO is **Ali Erhat Nalbant**; Arf raised a $13M seed in 2022 (Circle Ventures, Stellar Development Foundation). Reported scale: **~$10B in annual settlement volume**. Note Arf is NOT a Ripple acquisition — this is a liquidity/distribution partnership, distinct from Ripple's GTreasury (2025) acquisition.

**The problem it solves — the pre-funding/working-capital gap.** MTOs and cross-border payment firms must hold capital in *each* market so they can pay out before the corresponding funds settle. The constraint isn't total capital but having it **in the right market at the right time**. Traditional bank credit lines take months to arrange, demand heavy collateral, and lock capital to one geography — a poor fit for liquidity that needs to move between corridors. Arf's RLUSD line replaces that with short-duration, redeployable stablecoin working capital settling on XRPL rails.

**Phased rollout.** Phase 1: Arf provides RLUSD settlement liquidity directly on XRPL. Phase 2 (once XRPL's **native lending** protocol goes live): Arf moves more of the activity on-chain, managing the backing capital through XRPL native lending while keeping underwriting/working-capital decisions off-chain. The arrangement notably ties on-chain lending to **volumes from traditional (off-chain) payment companies** rather than to crypto-native activity — i.e., real-world payment flows feeding on-chain credit.

**RLUSD scale / context (Oct 2026).** RLUSD market cap ~**$2.38B** (8th-largest stablecoin), with ~**$1.01B of it on XRPL**; Ripple's Oct-1 transparency report showed ~$2.51B in circulation against ~$2.63B reserves. RLUSD was the third-fastest-growing stablecoin of 2026. This deal deepens the on-chain *use case* for XRPL/RLUSD beyond holding — turning the stablecoin into live settlement liquidity.

**How it fits Ripple's RLUSD playbook.** This extends a run of RLUSD distribution/settlement deals: [[Ripple and Bitso expand stablecoin settlement in Latin America]] (US–Mexico corridor, MXNB+RLUSD, 2026-06), [[Ripple and SBI launch RLUSD stablecoin in Japan]], [[Ripple and Unloq test RLUSD payments on Singapore's BLOOM]], [[AMINA becomes first bank to support Ripple USD]], and the institutional off-ramp [[Ripple makes RLUSD off-ramp for BlackRock, VanEck funds]]. Where those target corridors, banks or tokenized-fund rails, the Arf deal targets the **MTO working-capital layer** — arguably the stickiest real-world PayFi use case for a settlement stablecoin.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. **Is this committed liquidity or an MOU?** The release describes a partnership and a phased model but gives no committed facility size, corridor count, or launch-client names. "Will provide" is forward-looking — how much RLUSD is actually deployable at launch?
2. **Phase 2 depends on vaporware.** The bigger on-chain-lending vision is gated on XRPL's **native lending** going live — not yet in production. If that slips, the deal is just Arf extending RLUSD credit off a product it could already run on other chains.
3. **Why XRPL over Solana?** Huma/Arf already ran cross-border financing pools on Solana. What does XRPL/RLUSD add beyond Ripple distribution and a regulated-dollar stablecoin? Is this incremental or a genuine migration of volume?
4. **Credit risk ownership.** Arf makes underwriting decisions off-chain; if an MTO fails to settle in 1–5 days, who eats the loss — Arf's balance sheet, Huma's on-chain investors, or RLUSD holders? The red-team concern is off-chain default risk dressed as on-chain yield.
5. **Counterparty concentration.** Arf's ~$10B annual volume claim is self-reported; how many MTOs, and how concentrated? A few large counterparties defaulting could impair the pool.
6. **RLUSD liquidity depth on XRPL.** With only ~$1.01B RLUSD on XRPL, can it absorb redeployable settlement liquidity at scale without strained mint/burn and secondary-market slippage in payout markets?
7. **FX leg unaddressed.** Settlement liquidity solves the dollar pre-funding gap, but MTOs still need local-currency payout. Does RLUSD actually reach the last mile, or does a fiat off-ramp reintroduce the pre-funding problem the deal claims to kill?
8. **Regulatory exposure.** Providing short-term credit/working capital to licensed payment firms across jurisdictions invites lending-license and stablecoin-reserve scrutiny (MiCA, US frameworks). Is Arf licensed to lend in each corridor?
9. **Is "repaid in 1–5 days" best case?** Settlement delays, failed payments and corridor outages extend tenor and tie up capital — eroding the "redeploy repeatedly" economics.
10. **Not a Ripple acquisition.** Easy mis-framing: this is a liquidity partnership, not an M&A event. Does Ripple have any equity/exclusivity, or can Arf run the same RLUSD model with competitors?
11. **Duplicate check.** Is this materially new vs prior RLUSD settlement notes (Bitso/LatAm, SBI/Japan, Unloq/Singapore, AMINA, BlackRock off-ramp)? Yes — distinct partner (Arf), distinct mechanism (MTO working-capital pre-funding + future XRPL native lending), not a re-announcement.
12. **Reflexivity / XRP price.** Does more RLUSD settlement volume actually benefit XRP the asset, or (as commentators note) is RLUSD growth decoupled from XRP price? Narrative risk vs real utility.
13. **Measurable outcome.** What KPI proves success in 6–12 months — RLUSD-on-XRPL supply, number of live MTO corridors, settlement volume financed? Absent metrics, this stays an announcement.

**Importance: 3/5 — rationale.** A credible, concrete use case that plugs a stablecoin into the single most defensible real-world PayFi problem (MTO settlement working capital), from a partner (Arf, ~$10B volume, Huma-backed) with genuine distribution. It meaningfully advances the thesis that RLUSD/XRPL can host live settlement liquidity rather than passive balances, and it previews XRPL native lending. But it is early-stage: no committed facility size or named clients, Phase 2 depends on unshipped XRPL lending infra, the FX last-mile and credit-loss ownership are unaddressed, and it is one of several 2026 RLUSD distribution deals rather than a step-change. Solid directional signal, not a headline mover.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Stablecoin B2B cross-border payments. Per Ripple's Oct-2026 transparency report, RLUSD circulation was ~$2.5bn ($2,509.8m vs $2,633.8m reserves, as of 2026-10-01), up ~86% YTD from ~$1.34bn — but still an order of magnitude behind USDC ($74.1bn) and USDT ($183.8bn) (per coindesk/KuCoin citations, Oct 2026). The relevant pool is the stablecoin B2B/cross-border settlement lane: total stablecoin payment volume ran ~$401–527bn Jan–Aug 2026 (+42–63% YoY), with B2B now ~60% of flows (per blindpay/barchart citations, 2026); longer-term projections put cross-border B2B stablecoin flows on a multi-trillion trajectory by 2030–35 `(analysis — forecast, treat as directional)`. Structure: the settlement layer is fragmented and still forming — Ripple/RLUSD compete with Circle/USDC, PayPal/PYUSD, Tether/USAT plus bank rails; the *financing* sub-layer (PayFi: funding the pre-settlement working-capital gap) is thin and concentrated. Barriers are capital + licensing (money-transmission/EMI licences), not technology. Why now: post-GENIUS-Act regulatory clarity for regulated USD stablecoins, plus XRPL moving to add native lending (XLS-65/XLS-66, pending validator approval) that would let the financing run on-protocol.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: settlement volume, capital turnover/velocity (same dollar redeployed per month), repayment tenor, default rate, take on financed volume. Players: on the financing side Arf is the regulated-infrastructure arm of the PayFi category it co-built with Huma Finance — Huma reports >$17bn cumulative PayFi transaction volume growing >$1bn/month with zero credit defaults to date, ~$160m active liquidity and deployed liquidity of $508.5m by 2026-06-30 (per Huma/tipranks citations). Arf itself cites ~$10bn annual settlement volume, with working capital turning over ~4x/month (per crypto.news/Arf, Oct 2026) — i.e. a small financing book cycled many times. On the stablecoin side RLUSD is a challenger behind USDC/USDT; on rails, XRPL competes with Solana (where Huma/Tala already operate — see [[Tala partners with Huma to fund tokenized loans on Solana]]), Stellar (PYUSD), and card/bank networks (Visa Direct stablecoin prefunding, [[Visa Direct taps stablecoins for faster business funding]]). Recent peer moves: Bitso using RLUSD+XRP for US–LatAm settlement (2026-02, [[Bitso uses Ripple RLUSD and XRP for US-LatAm settlements]]); Stable Sea/dLocal on B2B stablecoin prefunding reduction (2026-02/03, [[Stable Sea and dLocal partner on stablecoin B2B cross-border payments]]); Coinbase/MassPay USDC payouts with Coinbase Prime prefunding (2026-06, [[Coinbase and MassPay enable cross-border stablecoin payouts in USDC]]). Protagonist position: niche leader in PayFi settlement financing, catching up on the stablecoin/rails choice — this deal is Arf diversifying its rail/stablecoin mix (XRPL+RLUSD on top of existing Solana/USDC Huma activity). Moat `(analysis)`: licensing + underwriting track record (zero defaults claimed) + two-sided network (payment firms on one side, on-chain liquidity depositors on the other) — switching costs are moderate; the rail/stablecoin layer is substitutable, so Arf's moat sits in the regulated-underwriting layer, not the chain.

**Comps & multiples.** No deal value or RLUSD liquidity amount was disclosed for this partnership (per announcement) — "no data" on transaction economics. Ripple itself: $500m strategic investment led by Fortress/Citadel Securities at a **$40bn valuation** (2026-01, [[Ripple raises $500m at $40bn valuation]]); this is a private round valuation, not a public market cap, and no public revenue figure is disclosed, so EV/Revenue = `[UNSOURCED]`. Arf (private, Huma-affiliated) and Huma (token HUMA) — no clean comparable public revenue/EBITDA to compute trading multiples; distribution not computed, qualitative comparison only. One sizing anchor: Arf's ~$10bn annual settlement volume at ~4x monthly turnover implies an average financing book on the order of ~$10bn / (12×4) ≈ **~$0.2bn outstanding** `(analysis — rough, from disclosed velocity; not a reported figure)`, i.e. the balance-sheet-light model is the point. RLUSD at ~$2.5bn vs USDC $74.1bn / USDT $183.8bn = roughly a **1.3% / 3.4%** share of those two — in-line "small challenger" positioning, not over-valued on size.

**Risk flags.**
- *Announced ≠ live.* No launch date, no committed RLUSD liquidity amount, and the on-protocol phase depends on XLS-65/XLS-66 validator approval that hasn't happened — the headline is a signed intent, not deployed volume; execution/timing risk.
- *Rail/stablecoin substitutability & RLUSD dependence.* Arf's moat is underwriting, not the chain; RLUSD is a distant #3 stablecoin, so adding XRPL+RLUSD is a distribution bet whose upside depends on Ripple growing RLUSD liquidity and XRPL depth — if RLUSD stays sub-scale, the lane adds little. Conversely for Ripple, settlement-financing volume is being borrowed from traditional payment firms, not generated on-chain — demand is exogenous.
- *Credit/liquidity concentration in a thin book.* "Zero defaults to date" (Huma/Arf claim) is untested through a stress cycle; 1–5 day tenor relies on continuous redeployment — a settlement-chain disruption or a single large counterparty failure in a small (~$0.2bn-scale) book could impair it. PayFi sits structurally between regulated payment firms and on-chain yield depositors, a layer exposed to both liability-side runs and asset-side credit.

**What this changes (idea-lens) `(analysis)`.** Signals the next PayFi battle is for *which rail/stablecoin* captures settlement-financing flow, not whether stablecoins win cross-border — Arf is deliberately multi-rail (Solana/USDC + now XRPL/RLUSD). Falsifiable thesis: if XRPL native lending (XLS-65/66) ships and Arf routes a disclosed, growing RLUSD book through it, RLUSD gains a real B2B utility leg beyond exchange listings — watch for (a) validator approval of XLS-65/66, (b) a first disclosed RLUSD settlement-liquidity figure, and (c) RLUSD circulation inflecting above its ~$2.5bn trend. Thesis breaks if the partnership stays an announcement with no deployed volume or RLUSD circulation flatlines.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (RLUSD scale read).** Ripple is private — no quarterly P&L — so the "results" here are RLUSD's circulating scale, the asset the Arf deal is built on. Trajectory: **IN-LINE / supportive** of the settlement-liquidity thesis. RLUSD market cap ~$2.40bn (circulating supply 2,404,105,889 RLUSD) as of 2026-10-25, vs ~$1.0bn when it "crossed a $1 billion market cap" in the week of 2025-11-05 — roughly **+140% over ~11 months**. The partnership adds a concrete PayFi demand sink (cross-border pre-funding repaid in 1–5 days) on top of an asset that is still sub-scale vs the >$220bn total stablecoin market Ripple itself cited.

**Key figures (RLUSD, dated).**
- Market cap / circulating supply: **~$2.40bn** (2,404,105,889 RLUSD), 2026-10-25 (CoinGecko). Ripple's own transparency report puts circulation at **$2,509.8mn** vs **$2,633.8mn** reserves as of 2026-10-01 — i.e. ~105% reserve coverage.
- Growth: from **~$1.0bn** (week of 2025-11-05, Ripple) → ~$2.4bn (Oct 2026), ≈ **+140% / ~2.4x**. From a standing start at the Dec-2024 global launch.
- Context: RLUSD "grew faster than almost any stablecoin before it" per Ripple's 2025 Impact Report (2026-06-01). Total stablecoin market was ">$220bn" at Ripple's last framing (Q4 2024 XRP Markets Report) — RLUSD remains ~1% of the category, so the growth is off a small base.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. **Scale is real but small.** ~$2.4bn of RLUSD float is enough to seed Arf's short-duration (1–5 day) revolving settlement liquidity — the capital recycles repeatedly against flows, so a modest float can support far larger annualized volume. But at ~1% of stablecoins, RLUSD is a challenger, not the rail; USDC/USDT still dominate the cross-border stablecoin plumbing Arf's clients could alternatively use. *Why it matters:* the deal's upside depends on Ripple converting regulatory wins (national trust bank approval 2025-12; MiCA/EMI/FCA licenses through 2026) into actual payment-firm adoption, not just float.
2. **Doubling pace + reserve transparency = credible supply side.** ~2.4x growth in ~11 months with ~105% disclosed reserve backing supports RLUSD as a usable settlement asset — the supply exists to be lent. *Second-order:* if XRPL native lending goes live as planned, Arf moving liquidity on-protocol turns RLUSD float into recurring on-chain lending volume, a demand driver that compounds the market-cap trend rather than just riding it.
3. **No disclosed Arf/RLUSD settlement-volume figure.** The announcement quantifies nothing deal-specific — no committed facility size, no projected TPV, no fee economics. *De-PR:* "will provide," "once infrastructure becomes available," "plans to route" — this is a phase-1 intent, not live at-scale volume. Treat traction as a product launch, not a results print. [UNSOURCED on deal size]
