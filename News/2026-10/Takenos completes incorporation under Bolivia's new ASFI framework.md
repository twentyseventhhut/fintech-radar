La billetera digital Takenos anunció que es la primera fintech constituida desde cero como Empresa de Tecnología Financiera (ETF) bajo el nuevo marco regulatorio de la Autoridad de Supervisión del Sistema Financiero (ASFI) de Bolivia. Completó la etapa de constitución, pero todavía no tiene su licencia de funcionamiento: según la compañía, está dentro de los plazos establecidos para ese trámite. Takenos ha trabajado con las autoridades desde 2025 para constituirse bajo la nueva norma.

El marco nació con el Decreto Supremo 5384, del 7 de mayo de 2025, y con el Reglamento para Empresas de Tecnología Financiera que la ASFI aprobó el 3 de julio de ese año. El decreto obliga a las fintech a constituirse como entidades financieras y a obtener de la ASFI dos autorizaciones: el permiso de constitución y la licencia de funcionamiento.

Las fintech que ya operaban tenían hasta el 31 de diciembre de 2025 para iniciar su adecuación, plazo que la ASFI extendió luego al 30 de abril de 2026. El 21 de octubre, el regulador informó que incorporó 36 ETF a su proceso de regulación y supervisión: 23 ya operan con un Certificado de Adecuación y 13 obtuvieron la No Objeción para iniciar su constitución.

Takenos tiene un centro de atención al cliente en Equipetrol y oficinas en la Green Tower, en Santa Cruz de la Sierra, y equipo en Cochabamba y La Paz. Según la empresa, ya movió más de US$50 millones en remesas desde Europa hacia Bolivia. En medio de una escasez de dólares, el país abandonó el 29 de junio de 2026 el tipo de cambio fijo de 6,96 bolivianos por dólar que sostenía desde 2011. El uso de activos virtuales pasó de US$46,5 millones en junio de 2024 a US$294 millones en junio de 2025, según el Banco Central de Bolivia.

Takenos fue fundada en 2022 en Argentina por Lucas Posada (CEO), ex-director de producto de IOL invertironline; Joaquín Herrera (COO), con experiencia legal en el Ministerio Público Fiscal; Francisco Goulu (CPO), ex-analista de producto de IOL, y Simón Bouché (CMO), ex-gerente sénior de proyectos en Amadeus. Según la compañía, supera el millón de usuarios y opera en Bolivia, Argentina, Perú, Ecuador y Colombia. En octubre de 2025, cerró una ronda semilla de US$5 millones coliderada por los fondos Variant y Lattice.

> Constituirnos bajo el marco de la ASFI implicó trabajar en la adecuación de nuestra operación, nuestros controles y nuestros procesos a los requisitos de la normativa. Este compromiso continúa mientras avanzamos en el proceso de emisión de la licencia de funcionamiento.
> 
> Joaquín Herrera, COO y cofundador de Takenos

# Context-enrichment: Takenos completes incorporation under Bolivia's new ASFI framework
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Takenos — an Argentina-founded (2022) cross-border / USD-stablecoin consumer wallet — announced on 21 Oct 2026 that it is the **first fintech incorporated "from scratch"** as an *Empresa de Tecnología Financiera (ETF)* under Bolivia's new ASFI regime. Crucially, this is a **constitution permit, NOT an operating licence**: the company "completed the incorporation stage" and *still awaits* its *licencia de funcionamiento*, claiming it is within the regulatory timeline. So the headline "first fintech constituted from scratch" is a **procedural milestone dressed as a market-defining event** — it can incorporate as a regulated entity but cannot yet operate under the licence.
- **Why framed this way:** Bolivia's ETF regime (Supreme Decree 5384, 7 May 2025; ETF Regulation approved by ASFI 3 July 2025) demands two separate ASFI authorizations — permit of constitution + operating licence. On 21 Oct 2026 ASFI reported 36 ETFs in its regulatory process: 23 already operating with a *Certificado de Adecuación* (pre-existing fintechs being grandfathered in), and 13 with a *No Objeción* to begin incorporation. Takenos's angle — "first built from scratch" — is a **semantic carve-out**: the 23 adapted fintechs were already running, so Takenos claims the narrow title of first *greenfield* ETF incorporation. That is a PR distinction, not a competitive moat.
- **Why it matters (2nd order):** Being first to *formally incorporate* signals compliance-first positioning in a market where most crypto flows are informal/grey. The real prize — the operating licence — is still pending; until then, this is a regulatory-posture play, not a product or revenue event.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Bolivia is suddenly a contested stablecoin-rails market after the dollar peg collapsed:
- **Peso** (Bolivian fintech, pre-seed led by Tether, 21 Oct 2026 — see [[Tether leads pre-seed round in Bolivian fintech Peso]]): USD₮ account + POS + virtual accounts wired into Bolivian banking via QR/ACH; already live with Yango food delivery. Tether-backed and infrastructure-deep.
- **Tala + El Dorado wallet** embedding credit in Bolivia ([[Tala brings embedded credit to El Dorado wallet in Bolivia]]).
- Regional USD-stablecoin wallets for freelancers/contractors: **Belo** (Argentina, Tether-led $14M Series A — [[Argentina's Belo raises $14 million Series A led by Tether]]), **Mural** ($15M Series A led by Tether), **Rain** (card issuance — Takenos's own TakeCard partner), and broader LatAm stablecoin plays ([[Squads, Belo and Segura raise capital for stablecoin platforms]], [[Bitget Wallet launches Scan to Pay for stablecoins in Latin America]]).
- **Position:** Takenos is **ahead on regulatory formality** (first greenfield ETF), but **behind Peso on capital + Bolivian infrastructure depth** (Peso has Tether's balance sheet and bank-rail integration; Takenos has no Tether tie and is still pre-licence). Takenos's edge is its multi-country footprint (AR/PE/EC/CO/BO) and ~1M user base vs Peso's Bolivia-first depth.
- **Why the landscape is this way:** Tether is aggressively buying distribution across LatAm's dollar-starved corridors (Peso, Belo, Mural, Noah). Takenos is one of the few sizeable regional wallets NOT in Tether's orbit — which is strategic independence but also a capital/distribution disadvantage.

## [2] Company history / fit
Founded 2022 in Argentina by Lucas Posada (CEO, ex-IOL invertironline), Joaquín Herrera (COO), Francisco Goulu (CPO, ex-IOL), Simón Bouché (CMO). Trajectory in the corpus:
- Oct 2025: **$5M seed** co-led by Variant & Lattice (Reverie, North Island, Refract, Gumi Cryptos, Nascent, Polaris) — [[Takenos raises $5 million seed for stablecoin wallet]]. (Note: some third-party trackers cite a "$10M" figure; the primary source and corpus say **$5M** — treat $5M as canonical.)
- Apr 2026: appointed Gonzalo Ostria as **Bolivia Country Manager** ([[Takenos appoints Gonzalo Ostria as Bolivia Country Manager]]); **committed $4M to expand into Peru** ([[Takenos commits 4M to expand into Peru]]); **Honda Autobol x Takenos** payment-discount launch ([[Honda Autobol and Takenos launch Bolivia payment discounts]]).
- Oct 2026: ASFI incorporation. Built on Solana; partners include Mastercard, Visa, Fireblocks, Bridge, Rain, Coinflow.
- Self-reported traction: >1M users; >US$500M (one source US$560M) cumulative volume; >US$50M in remittances Europe→Bolivia; "most-downloaded wallet in Bolivia."
- **Why this move fits:** Takenos has leaned into Bolivia since early 2026 (country manager, Honda deal). Incorporating formally is the logical next step to convert grey-market usage into a defensible, bank-connected position before the licence opens up regulated services. **Structural pressure:** as Bolivia formalizes (and as Tether-funded rivals arrive), an unlicensed incumbent risks being shut out — so compliance is now a survival requirement, not a nicety.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new?** The regulatory *first* is narrowly true (first greenfield ETF) but low-substance until the operating licence lands. The product (stablecoin wallet + card for freelancers/remittances) is NOT new — it existed pre-ASFI and competes with many peers.
- **Traction is the real story:** >US$50M Europe→Bolivia remittances and "most-downloaded wallet in Bolivia" are concrete adoption, not announcements. Bolivia's macro tailwind is severe: the country abandoned its 6.96 BOB/USD peg on ~29 Jun 2026 (BOB fell ~40%, settling ~9.7–9.8/USD); virtual-asset use jumped from US$46.5M (H1-2024) to US$294M (H1-2025) per the central bank (~530% YoY). Dollar scarcity → stablecoins as a dollar proxy → Takenos's core product is demand-pulled.
- **Who captures the margin?** Takenos sits on Solana + third-party card/custody rails (Rain, Fireblocks, Bridge, Mastercard/Visa). Its value-add is **compliance + distribution + UX**, not infrastructure ownership — meaning take-rate is squeezed between the networks above and local banks below. The ASFI licence, once granted, is the thing that could let it move up the stack (hold balances, connect to banking rails directly) and defend margin.
- **What breaks it:** if Tether-backed Peso wins the bank-rail + USD₮ integration race, Takenos could be relegated to a front-end wallet while rivals own the rails.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Immediate:** the operating licence is the gating event — watch for ASFI granting Takenos's *licencia de funcionamiento*. Until then, regulated operation is pending.
- **Macro backdrop:** Bolivia is mid-transition — post-peg float, central-bank plans to reopen USD accounts/return ~$933M frozen deposits, and a July-2026 move to consider recognizing USDT alongside the boliviano and USD for payments/savings/trade. This is an unusually fast formalization of a crypto-reliant economy.
- **Risks:** (1) licence delay/denial; (2) capital disadvantage vs Tether-funded rivals; (3) regulatory whiplash — ASFI tightening could raise compliance cost or restrict stablecoin use; (4) FX/boliviano volatility post-float hitting user economics.
- **Why the market goes this way (2nd order):** Bolivia's dollar crisis makes stablecoins structurally sticky (not speculative — a dollar-access utility). Formal ASFI incorporation lets compliant players capture flows that informal P2P can't serve (merchants, salaries, banked remittances). Counterintuitively, regulation is a **moat accelerant** here: it raises the barrier against unlicensed grey-market rivals and legitimizes the category — favoring whoever gets the licence first and has capital to scale. Takenos is first on paper; the open question is whether it converts that into the operating licence and defends against better-funded Tether-aligned rivals.

**Sector & drivers.** LatAm cross-border/stablecoin payments. Remittances to LatAm & the Caribbean hit a record ~$174bn in 2025 (World Bank-type figure, per cryptonews/Bybit citation, via cointelegraph, Oct 2026); stablecoins are projected to carry ~18–22% of the global remittance market (~$25–31bn) by 2026, and 71% of regional financial institutions already use them for cross-border payments (per Bybit report, via cointelegraph/cryptonews, Oct 2026). Bolivia is the acute end of the thesis: facing a severe USD shortage and >22% inflation, the government lifted its crypto ban (2024) and on-chain volumes jumped from $46.5m (H1 2024) to $294m (H1 2025) per the Banco Central de Bolivia — a 630% rise (per BCB, via crypto.news/bitget, 2025). On 29 Jun 2026 Bolivia abandoned the 6.96 Bs/USD peg held since 2011 (note text). Structure: fragmented and nascent — the sector is being formalised top-down by regulation rather than consolidated by scale. Value sits in two places: distribution (owning the end-user wallet) and compliant on/off-ramps into local banking rails. Barrier to entry just shifted from pure capital/tech to *regulatory licence* — the ASFI ETF regime (Decreto Supremo 5384, 7 May 2025; ASFI reglamento 3 Jul 2025) requires both a permiso de constitución and a licencia de funcionamiento (note text; confirmed via ASFI/Infoleyes). Why now: the peg break + dollar drought make a USD-denominated stablecoin wallet a genuine substitute for scarce physical dollars, and the regulator is actively pulling fintechs in-perimeter (36 ETFs as of 21 Oct 2026: 23 with Certificado de Adecuación, 13 with No Objeción — note text).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/remittance volume, take rate/flat fee per transfer, active users, and on/off-ramp coverage (corridors + local rails). Takenos' position: niche-but-early-mover. **Risk flags.**
1. **Licence still pending — moat is announced, not live.** Takenos has only *completed incorporation*; the licencia de funcionamiento is not yet granted. Until it is, the "first regulated fintech" claim is a constitution milestone, not permission to operate under the regime — a delay, denial, or added conditions would strip the headline advantage.
2. **Single-country regulatory & macro concentration.** The thesis hinges on Bolivia's dollar drought + broken peg persisting and on ASFI's regime staying favourable. Bolivia is tightening crypto oversight under an IMF-backed program (per crypto.news/blockonomi, 2026); a policy reversal, FX stabilisation, or USDT restriction would compress the core value proposition at the same time it raises compliance cost.
3. **Distribution/scale disadvantage vs capitalised peers.** On ~$5m seed capital Takenos faces Tether-backed Peso in the same market and $200m-funded regional rails; if the moat is distribution (per emergingfintech), a thinly-capitalised wallet risks being out-spent on user acquisition and disintermediated by rails players that bundle the Bolivian corridor into a larger stablecoin stack.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) ASFI's regime is converting Bolivia from a crypto-grey-zone into a licensed stablecoin-banking market, and first-mover licence-holders could enjoy a short regulatory-scarcity window. Falsifiable thesis: being first-constituted translates into a durable edge *only if* Takenos converts incorporation into an operating licence ahead of Peso and before foreign platforms are brought in-perimeter.
