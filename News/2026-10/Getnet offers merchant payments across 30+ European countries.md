Getnet simplifies European expansion for merchants with one payments integration

 Getnet will enable e-commerce businesses to accept, manage and move payments across Europe through a single integration. 
 Madrid (Spain), 6 October 2026. 
 Expanding across Europe just became simpler for merchants. Getnet, Santander's global merchant payments platform, now enables multinational merchants, marketplaces and e-commerce companies to access payment services in over 30 countries across Europe through a single integration. The expansion broadens Getnet's footprint beyond Latin America, Spain and Portugal and reinforces its role as a strategic payments partner for businesses operating across Europe.
As European commerce becomes increasingly cross-border, businesses face the challenge of navigating multiple payment methods, regulations and technical integrations. Getnet's European platform brings these capabilities together through a single infrastructure called Getnet Single Entry Point (SEP). SEP allows merchants to connect to multiple markets through one integration while accessing leading local payment methods, including Bizum in Spain and MB WAY in Portugal, where Getnet operates as a direct member, enabling a stronger value proposition for merchants.
The model is already proven at scale in Latin America, where Getnet processes billions of transactions across Brazil, Mexico, Chile, Uruguay, Colombia and Argentina.
In addition to payment acceptance, Getnet will enable merchants, platforms and payment service providers to collect funds, reconcile balances, convert currencies and make payouts through the same infrastructure. The solution initially supports 20 currencies and payouts to 140 countries.
 Rubén Justel, CEO of Getnet Europe , said: "This expansion marks a new chapter for Getnet in Europe. Businesses can access multiple markets through a single integration, with transparent pricing and dedicated support. Our goal is simple: help companies grow internationally while improving payment performance." 
Getnet is also building an open partner ecosystem with payment service providers, technology companies and software providers to help businesses scale more efficiently. Getnet is investing in new payment models, including instant payments, account-to-account payments and AI-powered commerce. The company recently completed Latin America's first agentic transaction using Mastercard Agent Pay technology.
 Juan Franco, CEO of Getnet , said: "The future of commerce is increasingly connected, intelligent and international. Europe plays a key role in connecting businesses across regions, including Latin America, North America and Asia. By expanding our capabilities in Europe, we are creating new opportunities for merchants, platforms and partners to grow across borders." 
 
 About Getnet 
 Getnet is the leading fintech provider of payment solutions in Latin America and Europe. We offer robust, omnichannel solutions that cater to the unique needs of our customers, integrating seamlessly with existing ecosystems while delivering powerful proprietary capabilities. In 2025, Getnet processed over €238 billion across 10.5 billion transactions, serving 1.2 million clients. The company ranks as the largest acquirer in Latin America by number of transactions and as one of the largest acquirers globally. Getnet operates across Brazil (as an e-commerce leader), Mexico, Chile, Argentina, Uruguay, Spain, and Portugal. Getnet is built to serve merchants, ISVs, PayFacs, orchestrators, platforms and the next generation of commerce enablers, connecting technologies and markets through a unified, omnichannel payment experience that boosts performance and drives growth.

# Context-enrichment: Getnet offers merchant payments across 30+ European countries
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5. Freshness: fresh._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Santander's merchant-acquiring arm **Getnet** announced (Madrid, 6 Oct 2026 — note: some aggregators republished 8 Oct) that its **Single Entry Point (SEP)** now lets merchants, marketplaces and e-commerce firms accept/manage/move payments across **30+ European countries via one integration**, with local methods **Bizum (Spain)** and **MB WAY (Portugal)** where Getnet is a direct member; **20 currencies initially; payouts to 140 countries**.

De-PR'd:
- **Not a from-scratch launch.** "SEP / one integration" is reused framing: Getnet launched **Getnet SEP on 25 Oct 2024** — but for LatAm (Brazil, Argentina, Mexico, Chile). The genuinely new part is extending that architecture/brand **beyond its long-standing Iberia-only acquiring footprint** to 30+ European markets.
- **"30+ countries" ≈ announcement + phased rollout, not 30 fully-live staffed markets on day one.** Reporting (el Economista summary) indicates local teams stand up first in **France and Italy** (priority), then Belgium/Nordics. Treat continent-wide as positioning.
- **No Europe-specific proof points.** Every big number (€238B TPV, 10.5B transactions, 1.2M clients in 2025 — see [[Connecting the Dots Getnet processed €238B across 10.5B transactions]]) is **group/LatAm-weighted**. The release discloses **no European merchant count, named EU client, or Europe GMV**. The "proven at scale in LatAm" line is doing the credibility work.

**Why framed this way:** Getnet reuses a battle-tested LatAm narrative ("unified platform, local methods, one integration") to enter crowded European acquiring with instant credibility — while avoiding the harder metric (actual European traction), which does not yet exist.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Single-integration pan-European acquiring is a contested field:
- **Adyen** — the benchmark single global platform + local methods; H1 2025 net revenue ~€1.09B (+20%), ~€649B processed H1; profitable. Getnet is well behind on European net revenue/volume.
- **Stripe** — ~$1.4T TPV 2024, strongest developer/single-API pitch.
- **Worldline** — in crisis: shares crashed ~41% on 25 Jun 2025 after the EIC "Dirty Payments" fraud-processing allegations; >90% off 2021 peak. Credibility damaged.
- **Nexi** (SIA+Nets, Europe's largest by volume) — under pressure (target cuts, share weakness Sep 2025).
- **Global Payments / Worldpay / Fiserv** — post-consolidation, near Adyen/Worldline scale.
- **Checkout.com** — enterprise single-API rival (and a co-enabler with Getnet on Mastercard Agent Pay LatAm).

**Position:** On raw processing Getnet is large (€238B) but LatAm-dominated; its European acquiring outside Iberia is nascent. Real edges: **Santander bank backing**, **direct Bizum/MB WAY membership**, and timing — **Worldline is scandal-hit and Nexi/Worldline are seen as perennial strugglers**, opening room for a bank-backed challenger. Weakness: no proven pan-European single-platform track record vs Adyen/Stripe, and zero disclosed EU wins. **(analysis)** The Worldline opening is a one-time tailwind, not a moat.

## [2] Company history / fit
Getnet: founded Brazil 2003, Santander took control 2013. In Europe since **July 2021** (PagoNxt launched Getnet merchant payments built partly on **Wirecard tech/Munich assets acquired 2020–Jan 2021**), but limited to **Spain + Portugal**, where it is the **#2 acquirer with >20% client share** in each. Brazil IPO **18 Oct 2021** (B3) → **taken private by PagoNxt** (announced May 2022, delisted B3 **29 Dec 2022**). It ranks **#1 LatAm acquirer, top-10 global** — see [[Getnet ranks top 10 global and #1 LatAm acquirer]]. It sits inside **PagoNxt** (Santander payments holding: Getnet + Ebury + PagoNxt Payments; revenue €1,373m 2025, +16.2%).

**Why Getnet acts this way (analysis):** A commodity acquiring take-rate pushes Getnet to scale geography + attach software/FX/payouts (the "collect, reconcile, convert, payout in one infra" pitch) to win a software-like multiple and defend against A2A disintermediation. Europe is the logical next leg after saturating LatAm/Iberia — and lets Santander monetize a pan-European corporate client base it already banks.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Really new:** pan-European *acquiring reach* beyond Iberia through one integration + direct local-method membership. **Reused:** the SEP "single integration" concept (LatAm, Oct 2024) and the "unified platform across LatAm + Iberia" line (Oct 2025, see [[Getnet ranks top 10 global and #1 LatAm acquirer]]).
- **Traction = announcement-grade, not adoption.** No European merchant/GMV/client numbers. Instant/A2A capabilities described as **in development**, not shipped.
- Adjacent credibility prop: the **Mastercard Agent Pay "first live LatAm agentic transaction" (Getnet+Mastercard+Neivor, Mexico)** — real but **LatAm-specific**; see [[Getnet and Mastercard run first live AI-agent payment in Mexico]]. The release folds it in to signal innovation, but it does not evidence European traction.

**Who captures the margin (analysis):** In European e-commerce acquiring, card schemes (Visa/Mastercard) and A2A rails (SEPA Instant, Wero) increasingly compress acquirer economics. Getnet's durable value depends on the software/orchestration + FX/payout attach, not the commoditizing acceptance leg. If A2A/pay-by-bank scales, the acquiring take-rate thins — so the real question is whether Getnet captures orchestration/FX margin, not acceptance volume.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Plans:** phased market staffing (France/Italy first), open partner ecosystem with PSPs/ISVs, instant + A2A + AI-agent commerce "investing in."
- **Regulatory backdrop:** EU Instant Payments Regulation (receive by 9 Jan 2025, send + Verification of Payee by 9 Oct 2025); **A2A/pay-by-bank surging** (~17% of EU e-commerce value 2024; projected >€850B 2026; boosted by SEPA Instant + Wero); **PSD3/PSR** extends VoP.
- **Risks:** late entry into a saturated/consolidated market outside Iberia; A2A disintermediating card acquiring; must build local acceptance/support in ~28 new markets vs entrenched incumbents; execution unproven (no EU clients disclosed); the Worldline-scandal tailwind is transient.

**Why the market may tilt Getnet's way (analysis):** A wounded Worldline and strained Nexi, plus merchants wanting one bank-grade pan-European + LatAm bridge, create a genuine opening — but structurally the value is migrating to A2A/orchestration, so a bank-backed acquirer entering on *card acceptance reach* may be scaling into a thinning-margin leg unless it wins the orchestration layer.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** European merchant acquiring is a mid-single-digit-to-9% grower on a large base: the global merchant acquiring market is ~$31.7bn in 2026 growing ~9.1% CAGR to ~$48.9bn by 2031, and the Europe payment-processing-solutions segment is ~$22.5bn in 2026 (per Mordor Intelligence / MarketDataForecast, via web, as of 2026 — secondary analyst estimates, treat as directional). Structurally the market is *fragmented*: international card schemes coexist with entrenched domestic rails (iDEAL in NL, Bizum in ES, MB WAY in PT, Girocard in DE, Cartes Bancaires in FR) that dominate local volume but lack cross-border interoperability. That fragmentation is exactly the moat Getnet is attacking — "one integration for 30+ countries" monetizes the pain of stitching together per-country methods, regulation and settlement. **Why now:** PSD2/SCA, SEPA Instant and the rise of account-to-account (A2A) and wallet volume are pushing multinational merchants toward single-integration pan-European PSPs rather than per-market bank acquirers (per BNY EMEA 2026 trends, via web).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: processed/total payment volume (TPV), take rate (net revenue / TPV, bps), and EBITDA margin. Getnet disclosed scale for its base: €238bn processed across 10.5bn transactions, 1.2m clients in 2025 (per Santander PR) — but this is overwhelmingly LATAM + Iberia; the European 30-country launch is *announced/expanding*, not yet at that scale. Key players and basis of competition: (i) **Adyen** — single-platform pan-European acquirer, the direct architectural analogue to Getnet SEP, competes on technology/single-integration; (ii) **Nexi** / **Worldline** — incumbent European acquirers competing on distribution/installed base, both under structural pressure; (iii) **Stripe / Checkout.com** — software-first global PSPs; (iv) local bank acquirers. Recent peer moves: Nexi — CVC weighing a ~€9bn take-private (2026-05, [[CVC Capital Partners weighs €9 billion bid to take Nexi private]]); Worldline — selling its North American business to Shift4 ([[Shift4 to acquire Worldline's North American business]]) and exiting the ANZ JV ([[ANZ to acquire Worldline's stake in ANZ Worldline joint venture]]); Adyen — €750m acquisition of Talon.One (2026-05, [[Adyen agrees to acquire loyalty platform Talon.One for €750 million]]). **Getnet's position (analysis):** a *new entrant catching up* in Europe — proven at scale in LATAM (largest acquirer there by transactions) but sub-scale in European acceptance vs Adyen/Nexi/Worldline. Moat leans on (a) Santander's balance-sheet/distribution and (b) direct scheme membership for local methods (Bizum, MB WAY) rather than reselling — a switching-cost/integration play rather than a price play `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round attaches to Getnet (it's a Santander unit; not separately listed — standalone multiple = no data). External peers (via web, 2026):
- **Adyen** — mkt cap ~€32–34bn; 2025 net revenue €2,364m; EV/Revenue ~11x on TTM (arithmetic: ~€26bn EV / ~€2.57bn ≈ 10x, per web); take rate ~17bps (€2,364m / €1,394bn); EBITDA margin ~55% (H2'25). *Premium/rich* vs peers — but justified by ~18–21% net-revenue growth and 55% margins (growth-multiple correlation).
- **Nexi** — ~€3.6bn 2025 revenue; CVC exploring ~€9bn take-private (incl. ~€6bn debt). Implied EV/Revenue ≈ €9bn / €3.6bn ≈ 2.5x — *cheap*, reflecting a ~65% four-year share decline and fee compression.
- **Worldline** — 2025 revenue €4,030m (−2.7% YoY), EBITDA margin collapsed to ~19.3% (from 23.3%); market cap distressed (~€0.7–1bn range, per web). EV/Revenue well below 1x on an equity basis — *distressed/cheap*, the cautionary tale of the incumbent-acquirer model.
- **Stripe** (private, software-first) — ~$1.9tn TPV 2025 (+34%); last secondary ~$159bn (Feb 2026). Round valuation, not market cap; not revenue-comparable without disclosed net revenue → P/S = no data.
Distribution: with a 10x–0.5x spread across Adyen/Nexi/Worldline the comp set is *bimodal* (tech-platform premium vs legacy-acquirer discount), so no meaningful median — qualitative comparison only. The read-across for Getnet: which bucket its European build lands in depends on whether it's a single-platform tech acquirer (Adyen-like) or a repackaged incumbent (Nexi/Worldline-like).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Fee compression / incumbent de-rating** — Nexi (−65% over 4yr) and Worldline (margin 23%→19%, negative FCF) show European acquiring economics are under structural pressure; a late entrant competing on "transparent pricing" (Getnet's own framing) risks entering into a shrinking-take-rate market.
2. **Execution / sub-scale in Europe** — the €238bn/1.2m-client scale is LATAM+Iberia; the 30-country European offer is *announced*, direct scheme membership exists only in ES/PT (Bizum, MB WAY), the rest implied via SEP — "announced" ≠ "live at scale across 30," and the PR is silent on European volume, merchant count and per-country go-live.
3. **Platform disintermediation** — against Adyen's single-platform tech and Stripe's software distribution, a bank-owned acquirer can be relegated to a balance-sheet/licensing layer while the merchant relationship and margin sit with the orchestration stack above it.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Getnet's move signals LATAM acquirers exporting a single-integration model *into* a fragmented, de-rating European market — a new-entrant thesis, not consolidation. Falsifiable thesis: if Getnet discloses European TPV/merchant counts within 2–4 quarters and they scale materially, it validates the SEP platform as an Adyen-style challenger; **what breaks it:** continued silence on European volume + further sector fee compression (watch the Nexi/CVC outcome as the sector's valuation signal) would mark this as a PR-led footprint claim rather than a live, scaled acquiring business.
