Dutch central bank grants Qivalis e-money license for MiCA-compliant euro token
Backed by BBVA, BNP Paribas, and ING, Qivalis secured Dutch regulatory authorization to offer onchain euro settlement infrastructure.
Qivalis, a consortium backed by 37 major European banks, received a Dutch electronic money institution license, bringing it closer to issuing a euro-denominated stablecoin.
The company must still meet European Union requirements, including publishing a white paper, before launching the token through banks, cryptocurrency exchanges and payment providers in the coming months.
Under the European Union’s Markets in Crypto-Assets regulation, holders of electronic money tokens can redeem them at face value at any time, but the tokens cannot pay interest.
Qivalis, the consortium backed by 37 major European banks, will soon be able to issue euro-denominated electronic money tokens (EMTs), a regulated form of stablecoin.
The Amsterdam-based company said that the Bank of the Netherlands (DNB) had granted it an electronic money institution license. Qivalis must still meet the remaining requirements under the European Union’s Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation, including publishing a white paper, before issuing its token, which it plans to do in the coming months.
The Dutch license brings Qivalis closer to offering banks and payment companies a way to transfer euros using blockchain technology. The consortium’s size gives it access to a large banking network, although Qivalis has yet to reveal which members will use the token or how much money they expect to move through EMTs.
Under MiCA, all EMTs are stablecoins, but not all stablecoins are EMTs, as the U.K. parliament has explained . EMTs are pegged to a single official currency, such as the euro or U.S. dollar, while other types of stablecoins can be linked to baskets of currencies or other assets. EMTs must be issued by authorized credit institutions or electronic money institutions, which means holders have a legal claim against the issuer and the right to redeem their tokens at face value at any time. EMTs cannot pay interest under MiCA.
Dollar-backed stablecoins such as USDC can also qualify as EMTs under MiCA if they meet the EU's requirements. Issuers that do not meet the applicable rules cannot lawfully offer their tokens to the public in the EU.
"Europe's currency deserves Europe's rules, onchain," said Jan-Oliver Sell, Qivalis CEO. "We believe Europe should have a euro-denominated onchain settlement asset issued under European law and subject to European regulatory standards."
Qivalis plans to distribute its token through banks, cryptocurrency exchanges and payment providers rather than directly to consumers.
The consortium has expanded to 37 European banks , including BBVA, BNP Paribas, ING and DZ Bank, since its founding in September 2025. It has also selected Fireblocks to provide digital asset infrastructure.

# Context-enrichment: Qivalis secures Dutch EMI licence to launch bank-backed euro stablecoin
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 4/5._

**FRESHNESS: FRESH.** This is a genuine NEW milestone — the EMI licence was actually GRANTED by De Nederlandsche Bank (DNB) on 2026-10-14, the regulatory gate the consortium had been chasing since inception. It is NOT a re-run of the prior Qivalis notes, which are distinct earlier stages of the SAME multi-step story: formation ([[European banks collaborate to launch euro stablecoin]], 9 banks, 25 Sep 2025, "will apply for MiCAR licence"), naming/governance ([[European banks back Qivalis euro stablecoin]], 2 Dec 2025, company incorporated, CEO/chair named), and the 25-bank expansion to 37 ([[Euro stablecoin consortium Qivalis expands to 37 banks]], 20 May 2026, EMI "still being sought"). So the consortium WAS already noted — three times, same slug `company/qivalis` — but each marks a different, non-duplicative step. The "talks/applied → licence granted" transition is exactly the kind of real follow-up that qualifies as fresh.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- DNB granted Qivalis B.V. (Amsterdam) an **Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence** on 2026-10-14. This authorises it to issue/distribute/redeem e-money generally.
- **It is NOT yet authorised to issue the stablecoin and the token is NOT live.** Two gates remain under MiCA: (1) **publish a white paper** (DNB has a ~20-working-day review window per Ledger Insights), and (2) complete the EMT (electronic money token) requirements. Only then can it issue. Management guides issuance "in the coming months" — consistent with the long-standing **H2 2026** target, now at the very tail of that window (mid-October).
- **De-PR'd:** every outlet's headline says "to launch" / "closer to issuing" — the licence is a necessary-but-not-sufficient condition, not a launch. No token name, no committed volume, and crucially **no disclosure of which of the 37 member banks will actually use or distribute the token, or expected EMT flow** (CoinDesk explicitly flags this gap). The consortium's headline asset remains its *size*, not any live traction.
- Structure reveals intent: Amsterdam domicile + DNB supervision is a deliberate single-issuer, bank-owned JV design — one regulated issuer, banks as distributors/wallet+custody providers (B2B2X), **distributed via banks/exchanges/PSPs, not direct to consumers**. Fireblocks supplies the digital-asset infrastructure. **Why this way:** EMTs under MiCA must be issued by a licensed EMI/credit institution, holders get a par-value redemption claim, and **interest is banned** — so the consortium pools the regulatory lift into one vehicle rather than 37 separate licences, and the economics for member banks are distribution/float-adjacent, not interest-yield.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Circle EURC** — the dominant euro EMT, ~€474M cap, ~58-63% of the ~€810-900M euro-stablecoin market (late Sep 2026). First-mover, deep exchange distribution, already MiCA-authorised via France.
- **Société Générale-FORGE EURCV** — ~€188M, the incumbent *bank-issued* euro stablecoin and Qivalis's closest structural analogue (a systemically-important bank issuing under EU law). Proof a bank can do it; also proof that bank-issued ≠ instant scale.
- **Banking Circle EURI** — ~€37-51M, went from zero to third-largest in months; shows a new entrant *can* gain share quickly but the absolute numbers stay tiny.
- **Internal corpus comps:** [[ODDO BHF launches euro-backed stablecoin EUROD]], [[Italy's Bancomat announces EUR.bank stablecoin]], [[AllUnity preps Swedish krona-backed stablecoin for June launch]] — a wave of European-bank/fiat stablecoins. On the dollar side USDT+USDC are ~99% of the ~$300B+ global market ([[Euro stablecoin consortium Qivalis expands to 37 banks]]).
- **Position:** Qivalis is **behind on traction (zero live supply) but uniquely ahead on backing breadth** (37 banks / 15 countries vs single-issuer rivals). **Second-order:** the euro-stablecoin pie is still <0.5% of the global market; EURC already owns most of it. Qivalis is not racing to invent a product — it's betting that *bank-distribution reach* beats *crypto-native head start* once institutional/tokenised-settlement demand materialises. The open question is whether 37 banks cooperate or quietly free-ride (SG, which has its own EURCV, is a reminder that big banks may prefer their own rails).

## [2] Company history / fit
- 25 Sep 2025: 9 banks (Banca Sella, CaixaBank, Danske Bank, DekaBank, ING, KBC, Raiffeisen Bank International, SEB, UniCredit) announce the JV, DNB-supervised ([[European banks collaborate to launch euro stablecoin]]).
- 1 Dec 2025: BNP Paribas joins; 2 Dec 2025 the entity is named **Qivalis**, incorporated in Amsterdam, governance set — CEO **Jan-Oliver Sell** (ex-Coinbase Germany MD who won BaFin's first crypto-custody licence; ex-Binance), CFO **Floris Lugt** (ex-ING digital assets), Chair **Sir Howard Davies** (first FSA chair, ex-RBS/NatWest chair) ([[European banks back Qivalis euro stablecoin]]).
- 20 May 2026: expands to 37 banks across 15 countries (adds ABN AMRO, Rabobank, Intesa Sanpaolo, Nordea, Erste, NBG, DZ Bank, BBVA, etc.); EMI licence still being sought ([[Euro stablecoin consortium Qivalis expands to 37 banks]]).
- 14 Oct 2026: EMI licence granted. **Why the consortium acts this way:** incumbent European banks face strategic-autonomy pressure (US dollar stablecoins = ~99% of supply; the digital euro CBDC won't arrive before ~2029) → they need a defensive, MiCA-native euro settlement rail *now*, and no single bank wants to shoulder the issuer risk/cost alone → hence a shared JV. The leadership hires (a licence-winning crypto operative + a heavyweight regulator chair) signal the plan is "pass regulators, then scale distribution," matching exactly how the milestones have unfolded.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new:** the first EMI licence actually in hand for the largest bank-backed euro-stablecoin consortium — a concrete regulatory de-risking, not another "we plan to."
- **What's NOT new / not yet real:** no live token, no white paper, no disclosed volume, no named participating-bank list. Traction is still **zero issued supply**. Bank-issued euro EMTs already exist (EURCV), so the *product category* isn't novel; the *scale of backing* is.
- **Value-add, one level deeper:** the real asset is the 37-bank distribution+custody network, not the token tech (Fireblocks is off-the-shelf). **Who captures the margin:** MiCA bans interest on EMTs, so the issuer can't monetise holder yield; value accrues from reserve float (at the issuer) and from banks' transaction/FX/settlement fees on top. **What breaks the thesis:** if member banks treat Qivalis as optional and keep routing euro flows through their own products or through EURC's exchange liquidity, Qivalis becomes a well-governed token with no volume — "consortium theatre." The breadth that is its strength is also its coordination risk.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Next concrete steps:** publish MiCA white paper → DNB review (~20 working days) → first issuance "in coming months" (H2 2026 target, now days from expiring; slippage into early 2027 is plausible — "analysis"). Watch for: token name, launch chains (reports mention Ethereum), and the first *named* distributing banks + any volume commitment — those, not the licence, will decide weight.
- **Backdrop:** S&P projects the euro-stablecoin market could grow from ~€770M to as much as €1.1T by 2030 ([[Euro stablecoin consortium Qivalis expands to 37 banks]]); the digital euro is a parallel, slower (2029) public-sector track ([[Digital Euro clears key hurdle in EU parliament]]); the ECB has resisted loosening euro-stablecoin rules ([[ECB rejects proposals to relax euro stablecoin rules as too risky]]); ESMA is forcing exchanges to drop non-compliant stablecoins ([[ESMA gives crypto exchanges three months to drop unauthorized stablecoins]]) — a regulatory tailwind that *clears the field* for MiCA-compliant issuers like Qivalis and EURC.
- **Counterintuitive second-order:** the same MiCA regime that legitimises Qivalis also caps its economics (no interest, strict reserves) — so success depends on *settlement/payments utility and bank adoption*, not yield. And the field-clearing against non-compliant tokens helps EURC (already live) at least as much as Qivalis (not yet live). The licence is a real step; the differentiator — actual bank flow — is still entirely unproven.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Qivalis sits in the euro-denominated EMT ("stablecoin") subvertical under MiCA. The whole euro-stablecoin pool is tiny: ~$810–900M total as of late Sept/Oct 2026 (per Bitcoin World / Gokhshtein citing market trackers, Sept–Oct 2026), vs a ~$316bn total stablecoin market that is ~99–99.5% USD-denominated (per Coindesk/ECB Nov-2025 FSR, via in-base notes). Onchain the dollar outweighs the euro by >300:1 for a currency that is ~20% of global FX reserves (ECB FSR, via [[European firms launch Eurøpe Consortium for onchain finance]]). Structure: nascent and concentrated — EURC alone is ~58–63% of euro supply (~$474–526M; Circle/CryptoBriefing, Oct 2026). Barriers are overwhelmingly regulatory + distribution, not tech: MiCA forces EMT issuers to be authorised EMIs/credit institutions, hold 30% of reserves at banks (60% for "significant" issuers), redeem at par, and pay no interest (ECB, via [[ECB rejects proposals to relax euro stablecoin rules as too risky]]). "Why now": MiCA gives Europe the clearest EMT rulebook globally and has pushed USDT off EU venues ([[Tether exits EU market as MiCA blocks USDT from exchanges]]), opening a compliance-shaped hole; the EU strategic-autonomy / de-dollarisation thesis (Davies: "preserving its strategic autonomy") is the political tailwind. S&P projected the euro-stablecoin market could grow from ~€770M to as much as €1.1tn by 2030 (S&P Global Ratings, via in-base Coindesk note) — a vast-but-speculative TAM, treat as scenario not forecast.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: circulating supply (market cap = float), 30-day on-chain transfer volume, number of venues/chains live, and share of euro supply. Qivalis discloses NONE of these yet — it has only an EMI licence (DNB, granted 13 Oct 2026), no white paper, no live token, no named members committed to use it, no stated expected EMT flow (de-PR: economics and actual demand are entirely silent). Key players: (1) incumbent issuer Circle EURC — ~$474–526M, the share leader; (2) bank-/asset-manager JVs — AllUnity EURAU (DWS/Flow Traders/Galaxy, BaFin EMI, live but tiny ~$1.2M Jul 2026) and Société Générale-FORGE's EURCV (multi-chain, MetaMask/XRPL — [[Societe Generale-FORGE launches EUR stablecoin on XRP Ledger]]); (3) crypto-native EMIs — Monerium EURe (~$28M), Schuman Financial's EURØP (ACPR EMI, live on 6 chains, ~€116.8M 30-day transfers, now anchoring the rival [[European firms launch Eurøpe Consortium for onchain finance]]); (4) others: ODDO BHF EUROD ([[ODDO BHF launches euro-backed stablecoin EUROD]]), Banking Circle ([[Banking Circle launches stablecoin clearing service]]). Basis of competition: distribution + liquidity + integrations, NOT product — MiCA commoditises the token itself. Qivalis's position: **behind on product, uniquely ahead on distribution potential** — 37 banks across 15 countries (BBVA, BNP Paribas, ING, UniCredit, DZ Bank), Fireblocks + the DNB licence. Moat (analysis): network effects via the bank consortium's balance sheets and client reach; but moat is latent until a token ships and members actually route euros through it.

**Comps & multiples.** Qivalis is pre-revenue, pre-token, privately held — no round/valuation disclosed, so EV/Rev, P/S, price-per-user = **no data / [UNSOURCED]**. Comparison is necessarily qualitative on supply/share:
- EURC: ~$474–526M supply, ~58–63% euro share (Circle/CryptoBriefing, Oct 2026) — the benchmark incumbent.
- AllUnity EURAU: ~$1.2M (Jul 2026) — closest structural comp (EMI-licensed, institution-backed JV) and a sobering base rate: licence + bank backing ≠ volume.
- Monerium EURe: ~$28M (Oct 2026); Schuman EURØP: ~€116.8M 30-day transfers (Oct 2026) — crypto-native EMIs already live and distributing.
Internal comps (base): [[Euro stablecoin consortium Qivalis expands to 37 banks]], [[European banks back Qivalis euro stablecoin]], [[European banks collaborate to launch euro stablecoin]], [[Societe Generale-FORGE launches EUR stablecoin on XRP Ledger]], [[How European banks are embracing stablecoins]]. Distribution not computed (multiples not applicable to a pre-launch issuer); qualitative read: whatever Qivalis's eventual supply, it enters a market where the entire euro pool (~$0.9bn) is <1/300th of the dollar pool — the prize is market *creation*, not share capture.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Launch-to-adoption gap (execution).** Licence ≠ live token ≠ usage. White paper still pending; no member has committed to route flow; AllUnity's ~$1.2M shows bank/institution backing does not auto-create liquidity. Second-order: a consortium of 37 competing banks risks governance paralysis over who distributes, who earns float income, and who bears fraud/redemption liability — none disclosed.
2. **Economics gutted by MiCA + ECB stance.** EMTs pay no interest and must park 30–60% of reserves at banks; the ECB explicitly rejected easing these rules and prefers tokenised bank deposits / a 2029 digital euro (via [[ECB rejects proposals to relax euro stablecoin rules as too risky]]). Second-order: the reserve-yield business model that makes USDC/EURC profitable is capped, and a future digital euro could disintermediate private euro EMTs outright.
3. **Structural demand deficit.** With euro stablecoins <1% of supply and the dollar winning on network effects (liquidity attracts liquidity), Qivalis must overcome a self-reinforcing dollar incumbency, not just competitors. Second-order: even a well-distributed euro EMT may stall at single-digit-% of a sub-$1bn pool unless real-economy / tokenised-finance settlement demand materialises.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is the bank-led euro-stablecoin bet reaching regulatory escape velocity — the first consortium-of-incumbents to clear DNB, converting the "strategic autonomy" narrative into a licensed vehicle (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Qivalis is the one euro EMT whose bank-distribution flywheel finally moves the needle, its live-token supply should exceed EURC's within ~18 months of launch; trigger to watch = white-paper publication + first named member committing live euro flow + 30-day transfer volume. What would break the thesis: token ships but consortium members keep settling on USD rails / EURC (as the ~$1.2M EURAU precedent warns), or an ECB digital-euro timeline that crowds out private issuers. Also watch the rival [[European firms launch Eurøpe Consortium for onchain finance]] (EURØP, already live) — adoption, not issuance, is now the contested ground.
