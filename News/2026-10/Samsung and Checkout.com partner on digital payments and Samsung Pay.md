Samsung Electronics and Checkout.com partner to support high-performance digital payments and expand Samsung Pay

 Samsung Electronics and Checkout.com announce a global partnership across Samsung’s digital commerce ecosystem 
 AI-powered insights and data-driven optimizations from Checkout.com to improve payment performance and reliability at scale across Samsung Electronics’ global payments infrastructure 
 Samsung Pay will be made available to existing Checkout.com merchants through Flow, starting October 31 
 LONDON, UK — 19 October 2026 — Checkout.com, a leading global digital payments provider, today announced a global partnership with Samsung Electronics to support its digital commerce ecosystem with flexible, high-performance payment capabilities, and bring Samsung Pay to more merchants.
Samsung Electronics is a globally recognized technology leader, with 221 operational sites across 76 countries. Its broad portfolio spans mobile devices, consumer electronics, software, connected experiences and digital commerce, including Samsung.com, Galaxy Store, Samsung Wallet and Samsung Pay. 
Checkout.com currently processes payments for Samsung Gulf Electronics, supporting Samsung.com across the MENA region. Under the global partnership, Checkout.com will extend its support to additional Samsung Electronics’ digital-commerce environments and markets in the EU and North America across both the Galaxy Store and Samsung.com.
The partnership will bring together Checkout.com’s global and local acquiring capabilities, payment-processing platform and payment optimization technology to support Samsung Electronics’ payment flows as it expands across markets. Samsung Electronics will use Checkout.com’s Intelligent Acceptance, an AI-powered optimization engine that uses real-time data from Checkout.com’s global network to help intelligently optimize transactions, reduce payment failures and improve performance.
Samsung Pay will also launch on Flow, Checkout.com's low-code payments integration, on October 31. This gives Checkout.com merchants a simple way to add Samsung Pay as a digital wallet option for online and in-app checkout and tap into over 1 billion Samsung Galaxy phone users, providing consumers more choice in how they pay.
Together, the partnership will support more flexible payment routing, stronger payment performance and smoother digital checkout experiences across Samsung’s ecosystem, while bringing Samsung Pay to more merchants through Flow.
 Woncheol Chai, Executive Vice President, Samsung Electronics, said: “As Samsung continues to build connected digital experiences for customers around the world, we need payment infrastructure that is flexible, reliable and ready to scale across markets. Our partnership with Checkout.com will support that ambition, helping us create a more consistent foundation for digital commerce and bring Samsung Pay to more merchants through Flow.”
 Antoine Nougué, Chief Revenue Officer at Checkout.com, said: “Samsung is one of the world’s most innovative technology companies, and we’re proud to support its digital commerce ecosystem through this global partnership. By combining our acquiring capabilities with Intelligent Acceptance and Flow, we’re helping Samsung build greater flexibility into its payments infrastructure while giving merchants more choice in how they accept payments.”
The Samsung partnership builds on Checkout.com’s momentum with enterprise merchants worldwide. The company now serves more than 1,700 enterprise merchants and has announced recent partnerships with Microsoft, Spotify and Best Buy. It has reached $750 million in annualized net revenue, growing 28% year over year. The US is its fastest-growing region, with processing volumes growing over 120% year to date.

# Context-enrichment: Samsung & Checkout.com partner on digital payments and Samsung Pay
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Announced **19 Oct 2026** (London). Two distinct, unequal things bundled under one "global partnership" headline:
1. **Checkout.com becomes an acquirer/processor for more of Samsung Electronics' own storefronts.** It already processes payments for **Samsung Gulf Electronics (Samsung.com across MENA)** — that relationship is NOT new. What's new: extending acquiring + payment-processing + optimization (incl. its AI engine **Intelligent Acceptance**) to Samsung.com and the **Galaxy Store in the EU and North America**. This is a merchant-acquiring contract expansion, region/platform by region/platform.
2. **Samsung Pay launches as a wallet option on Checkout.com's "Flow"** (its low-code checkout UI) from **31 Oct 2026**, so any Checkout.com merchant can offer Samsung Pay alongside cards.

**Why structured this way / what it reveals:** The PR frames this as Samsung picking Checkout.com, but the mechanics are two-sided and self-serving for both. (1) For Samsung, consolidating its fragmented global storefront payments onto one modern acquirer with AI auth-rate optimization is a cost/conversion play, not a strategic bet. (2) The "1 billion Galaxy phone users" number is a **TAM/reach figure, not Samsung Pay actives** — Samsung Pay's actual installed base is far smaller (see [1]). The reach claim conflates "phones that could run Samsung Pay" with "people who use it." The genuinely load-bearing fact is #1 (another enterprise acquiring logo); #2 (wallet-on-Flow) is incremental distribution with uncertain pull.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Acquiring/processing side:** Checkout.com competes with **Adyen, Stripe, Worldpay/FIS, Fiserv, PayPal (Braintree)** for enterprise acquiring. Adyen is the closest "single-platform global acquirer + optimization" peer and the benchmark Checkout.com is chasing on multiple and growth. Winning Samsung alongside Microsoft, Spotify, Best Buy, Uber, eBay, Vinted (2026 wins) is Checkout.com rebuilding enterprise credibility after its 2022→2025 valuation reset.
- **Wallet side:** Samsung Pay (~9% mobile-wallet share, ~45M actives, ~31 countries) trails **Apple Pay (~39%) and Google Pay (~44%)** badly. Adding Samsung Pay to Checkout.com's Flow is defensive distribution for Samsung, not a category move.

**Why the landscape is this way / second order:** Acquirers now compete on **auth-rate uplift via AI/network data**, not price — Intelligent Acceptance mirrors Adyen's RevenueAccelerate and Stripe's optimized acceptance. The moat is data scale on the network; Samsung's global volume feeds Checkout.com's model, a flywheel. On the wallet side, Samsung Pay's weakness means merchant demand to add it is thin, so the Flow launch is low-stakes for Checkout.com and a reach grab for Samsung.

## [2] Company history / fit
Checkout.com in 2026: **$750M annualized net revenue (+28% YoY), >1,700 enterprise merchants, >$300B/$480B volume, returned to full-year profitability, ~$150M adj. EBITDA target**, valued ~$12B (down from $40B in 2022 — see [[Checkout.com now valued at $12B, down from $40B]]). The year has been a march of enterprise logos: [[Checkout.com selected by Microsoft for EMEA digital payments]], Spotify (Feb), Best Buy (Sep), Uber, plus agentic-commerce positioning ([[Checkout.com prioritizes agentic commerce and stablecoins]], [[Checkout.com named a leader in Forrester agentic commerce evaluation]]) and US expansion ([[Checkout.com secures Georgia bank charter to accelerate US expansion]]).

**Why it acts this way:** A commodity acquiring take-rate caps the multiple; to justify a software-like valuation and recover from the $40B→$12B markdown, Checkout.com must (a) stack marquee enterprise logos to prove it's not just MENA/mid-market, and (b) sell AI optimization (Intelligent Acceptance) as a differentiated, stickier product. Samsung is a trophy logo that does both. The US (fastest-growing region, +120% YTD volume) is the strategic prize; the NA leg of this deal fits that.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Genuinely new:** EU + NA acquiring for Samsung.com/Galaxy Store, and Samsung Pay on Flow (31 Oct). **Not new:** the Samsung relationship itself (MENA, pre-existing). Traction is **announced, not yet live** — go-live is 31 Oct 2026, and no volume/share-of-Samsung-wallet figures are disclosed.

**Why the value-add is real / where it isn't:** Real for Checkout.com = recurring enterprise acquiring volume + proof point for Intelligent Acceptance. Thin for the wallet narrative = Samsung Pay's weak actives mean the Flow integration adds optionality, not conversion. **Who captures the margin:** Checkout.com captures acquiring economics on Samsung's own storefronts (the durable piece); on the wallet side the economics are trivial. The durable value is the acquiring contract, not the headline wallet reach.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch for: Samsung Pay on Flow going live 31 Oct and whether any merchants actually enable it; expansion of Checkout.com acquiring into more Samsung markets; whether this feeds the agentic-commerce/stablecoin roadmap. Sentiment on Checkout.com is recovery-mode: profitable, growing, no IPO plans, rebuilding enterprise trust after the markdown.

**Why / second order:** Each enterprise logo compounds the auth-rate data moat and the "we win flagships" narrative that supports re-rating toward Adyen-like multiples. Counterintuitive risk: leaning on marquee logos and AI-acceptance claims invites scrutiny — if volume/auth-rate uplift numbers don't surface, the logos read as PR, not durable revenue.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Global payment-gateway/online-acquiring market, where this deal sits, is estimated at ~$33–36bn for 2026 across most trackers (ResearchAndMarkets $35.55bn / Fortune Business Insights $34.49bn / Precedence $33.29bn), with a ~13–16% CAGR into the early 2030s (Grand View's $58.8bn/22.7% is an outlier on wider scope) — per secondary market-research citations, as of 2026, treat as order-of-magnitude, not a hard TAM. Structure: fragmented at the merchant level but consolidating around a handful of global acquirer-processors (Stripe, Adyen, Checkout.com, Worldpay, PayPal), with barriers = global acquiring licences, scheme/PSP integrations, scale on fraud/optimization data. "Why now": two secular drivers converge — (1) enterprises consolidating fragmented payment stacks onto one global acquirer for routing/auth-rate optimization, and (2) AI/"agentic commerce" reframing authorization as a data problem (Checkout.com's own 2026 FY release leads with "agentic commerce"). Second-order: the value in acquiring is migrating from pure processing to data-driven optimization (Checkout.com's Intelligent Acceptance), where incumbents with network-wide transaction data have an edge.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/processing volume, net take rate (net revenue / TPV), authorization/auth-uplift rate, enterprise-merchant count. Key players and basis of competition: Stripe (developer platform + distribution), Adyen (single global platform, enterprise), PayPal (consumer wallet reach), Checkout.com (global+local acquiring, optimization) — competition is on auth performance, geographic acquiring coverage and enterprise relationships, not headline price. Recent peer moves: Adyen H1 2026 processed €803.8bn volume (+24% YoY), net revenue €1,302.9m (+19%), FY guidance lifted to 21–23% cc (H1 2026 results, per Adyen/Finance Magnates); Stripe repriced to $159bn in a Feb 2026 tender (up from $91.5bn a year earlier) on ~$1.9tn 2025 volume (+34%) (CNBC, Feb 2026). Checkout.com's position: a clear #3/#4 global challenger, catching up — the Samsung win (extending from MENA-only to EU + North America across Samsung.com and Galaxy Store, plus the Microsoft/Spotify/Best Buy references) signals enterprise traction, but at smaller absolute scale than Stripe/Adyen. Moat (analysis): switching costs once embedded in a merchant's payment routing + the network-data flywheel behind Intelligent Acceptance; thinner brand/distribution moat than Stripe.

**Comps & multiples.** Checkout.com figures are company-reported, not market-priced; peers are a mix of public and private.
- **Checkout.com (note):** $750m annualized net revenue, +28% YoY; US volumes +120% YTD; FY2025 $300bn TPV (+64% YoY), net revenue >30% growth for a 2nd year, FY2025 EBITDA-profitable, >1,000 enterprise merchants (24 Feb 2026 FY release). Internal valuation marked to $12bn in Sep 2025 (down from $40bn in the 2022 round). P/S on $12bn / $0.75bn net rev = **16.0x** on an internal, stale (Sep-2025) mark — rich on an absolute basis but on a hyper-growth, newly-profitable base; treat as round-mark, not market cap. - **Adyen (public, external):** mkt cap ~$30–34bn on LTM revenue ~€2.57bn → EV/Rev broadly ~11–13x (analysis; exact EV/clean figures [UNSOURCED] without a free filing-grade source) — in-line-to-cheaper than Checkout.com's internal mark, but Adyen is larger, slower-growing and fully profitable.
- **Stripe (private, external):** $159bn Feb-2026 tender valuation on $1.9tn volume; revenue $6.9bn (per 2026 round) → ~23x on revenue (round mark, not market cap).
Read: Checkout.com's 16x internal P/S is defensible only if the >28–30% growth holds; relative to Adyen (~11–13x, slower) it looks full, so the Sep-2025 $12bn mark is the binding uncertainty here.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Partnership is "announced," not yet proven economics.** Samsung Pay goes live on Flow only 31 Oct 2026 and the Samsung.com/Galaxy Store EU+NA expansion is a rollout — the PR is silent on take rate, exclusivity, volume commitments and fraud-liability split, so revenue contribution is unquantified (could be a logo win more than a P&L event).
2. **Wallet-side value is weak for Samsung Pay.** Samsung Pay is a distant #3 wallet (~3–9% US share vs Apple Pay ~40–57%, Google Pay ~15–44% by source; strong only in Korea at ~42%), so "1bn Galaxy users" overstates realistic checkout conversion — the merchant-adoption upside is modest outside Korea.
3. **Disintermediation / stale valuation.** As an acquirer, Checkout.com captures basis points while Apple/Google/Samsung own the wallet rails; margin can be squeezed by both schemes above and wallet owners below. Combined with an internal $12bn mark already cut from $40bn, any growth deceleration re-opens the down-round question.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector, this is incremental consolidation of enterprise acquiring onto global challengers plus another push to make Samsung Pay competitive at checkout — not a re-rating event (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if the Samsung relationship materially lifts Checkout.com's EU/NA volumes, it supports the next internal valuation mark-up toward Stripe/Adyen multiples. Trigger to watch: Checkout.com's next FY (Feb 2027) release for Samsung-era volume/net-revenue acceleration, and whether the internal $12bn mark is raised. What breaks it: Samsung Pay adoption via Flow stays niche and the deal remains a logo, not a volume driver.
