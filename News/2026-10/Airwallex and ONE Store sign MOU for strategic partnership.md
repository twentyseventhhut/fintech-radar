 Airwallex and ONE Store Sign MOU to Advance Strategic Partnership 
 Infrastructure
 19.10.2026 10:26 am 
 Airwallex, a leading global financial platform for modern businesses, and ONE Store, a leading independent app marketplace in Korea, signed an MOU to explore a strategic partnership supporting ONE Store’s international expansion and its global developer and publisher ecosystem. 
 The MOU signing ceremony took place on October 15 in Seoul, bringing together senior representatives from Airwallex and ONE Store to mark the beginning of their strategic  partnership.  
 ONE Store has established a strong position in Korea’s gaming ecosystem and is expanding its reach into international markets. The partnership brings together ONE Store’s Korean gaming ecosystem and Airwallex’s global financial infrastructure, with the aim of supporting ONE Store as it scales its platform and serves developers, publishers and players across markets. 
 Game developers and publishers operating internationally often navigate different platform fee structures, payment requirements and local payment systems across markets. Working with multiple gateways and intermediaries can add operational complexity and increase the overall cost of managing international payments, potentially affecting margins and the resources available for game development and operations.  
 Through the partnership, Airwallex and ONE Store will explore ways to simplify global payments and international financial operations for game developers and publishers. By leveraging ONE Store’s gaming ecosystem and Airwallex’s global financial infrastructure, the collaboration aims to provide developers and publishers with a more scalable foundation for entering and growing in international markets.  
 As the partnership develops, it is intended to support smoother, more consistent and convenient payment experiences for players around the world. 
 “Korea is one of the world’s most dynamic markets for games and digital entertainment, with developers and publishers building ambitious businesses for players around the world,” said Keun Bin Kim, Head of Enterprise for South Korea at Airwallex. “Through our partnership with ONE Store, we're excited to work together to simplify global payment experiences for Korea's gaming developers as they reach new international audiences.” 
 “ONE Store has always aimed to create more choice and opportunity for developers and publishers,” said Henry Chang , CEO at ONE Store. “As Korean game developers and publishers increasingly look to global markets, we see an opportunity to work with Airwallex to simplify international payments and operations, helping our developer ecosystem reach more players around the world.” 
 Airwallex will also participate in G-STAR in November, where it plans to engage with game companies, developers and industry partners on the opportunities and challenges of global expansion.

# Context-enrichment: Airwallex and ONE Store sign MOU for strategic partnership
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Airwallex and ONE Store (Korea's homegrown app marketplace) signed a **non-binding MOU** on **2026-10-15 in Seoul** (publicly announced ~Oct 19–20) to "explore" simplifying cross-border payments for game developers and publishers on ONE Store's platform. De-PR'd: there is **no binding commitment, no product, no pricing, and no go-live date** — the language is "will explore ways" throughout. The stated intent is to bring Airwallex card + card-based simplified payment methods across ~200 countries to "ONE Store Global" and "ONE Shop" so devs avoid wiring up a separate processor per market (per en.fnnews.com). Airwallex provides payment acceptance / local payment methods / cross-border rails; FX and payouts are its general capabilities but are **not** spelled out as MOU line-items.
**Why structured this way / what it reveals:** An MOU is the cheapest possible signal — it lets both sides issue a press release and show up at G-STAR (Busan, Nov 2026) without balance-sheet or integration commitments. For Airwallex it is a low-cost beachhead into Korea's gaming payments vertical; for a newly-private ONE Store it is a credibility prop for its "ONE Store Global" international push. The PR anchors to Airwallex's scale numbers (675k+ businesses, $287B+ processed) to lend the thin MOU weight — the tell that there is little concrete to announce yet.

## [1] Competitors / peers
The gaming cross-border payments niche is already occupied and further along:
- **Xsolla** — the gaming-native merchant-of-record incumbent; **partnered with Adyen (Aug 2025)** to power global game payments across 200+ markets / 160+ currencies. This is the exact template Airwallex–ONE Store is reaching for, delivered a year earlier and live.
- **Adyen, Stripe, PayPal, Nuvei, Worldpay, Paymentwall, Rapyd, dLocal, Thunes** — all offer cross-border acceptance/payouts relevant to game devs and marketplaces.
- ONE Store's own marketplace rivals: **Google Play, Apple App Store, Samsung Galaxy Store** — against which ONE Store is a distant domestic challenger.
**Why the lay of the land / second-order:** The win in app-store payments is the merchant-of-record + global-tax + local-methods bundle (Xsolla's moat), not raw FX. Airwallex is strong on cross-border accounts/FX but has to prove it can match MoR/dev-facing tooling. Position: **catching up to a proven Xsolla–Adyen template**, not breaking new ground.

## [2] Company history / fit
Airwallex trajectory: APAC-rooted cross-border payments platform; **$287B+ annualized volume (+120% YoY, Mar 2026), ~$1.3B annualized revenue (+74%), 675k+ businesses, 85+ licenses**; **Series H $320M at ~$11B (June 2026)** — Axios had earlier reported a ~$12B mark (May 2026). Korea/Asia is core to its identity (president Lucy Liu, Aug 2026: "not the best time" for an IPO). The ONE Store tie fits a steady drumbeat of regional B2B partnerships — cf. [[Circles partners Airwallex for telco digital banking in 70+ countries (2)]] (Mar 2026, embedded finance) and [[OPTRA Labs and Airwallex partner to support cross-border SME growth]] (May 2026, SME cross-border). See also [[Airwallex raises new funding at $12 billion valuation]] and [[Airwallex hires Alin Dobrea to drive Southeast Asia growth]].
**Why the company acts this way:** Airwallex's core take-rate on cross-border is a commoditizing business; it needs vertical/embedded-finance land-grabs (telco, SME, now gaming) to defend growth and justify a software-like multiple. Gaming is a high-volume, FX-heavy, multi-market vertical — a natural adjacency for its rails.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: low.** Nothing new is live — it is an intent to explore. The underlying capability (global game payments with local methods) already exists at Xsolla/Adyen. **Traction: zero so far** — no merchants, no GMV, no integration date; the only "deliverable" is a joint appearance at G-STAR in November.
**Why the value-add is (not yet) real:** For devs the value is MoR + tax handling + local methods bundled, which captures the margin in the stack. If Airwallex only supplies acceptance + FX while ONE Store keeps the merchant relationship, Airwallex risks being a commoditized rail behind ONE Store's storefront rather than the margin-capturer. Value-add is unproven until a signed commercial agreement with pricing and a go-live exists.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch for: (1) a binding commercial agreement converting the MOU, with scope/pricing/go-live; (2) G-STAR (Busan, Nov 19–22, 2026) engagement; (3) whether "ONE Store Global" (~120+ countries) actually ships Airwallex-powered payments. **Key risk — counterparty in flux:** ONE Store was taken ~**96% private by NEXUS in mid-2026** (share exchange resolved ~Sep 29, 2026) and is pivoting toward a "Web3 game store" vision — a newly-controlled, strategically unsettled partner, which adds real execution risk to any MOU.
**Why the market may go this way / second-order:** Korea is a large, export-oriented games market, so the logic is sound — but the thin MOU + an unsettled, Web3-pivoting counterparty means the probability of this becoming a material live deal on any near horizon is modest. The second-order read: for Airwallex this is cheap optionality and PR; for ONE Store it is validation signaling during an ownership transition.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** The deal sits in cross-border B2B payments infrastructure — Airwallex providing its global financial rails to ONE Store's Korean developer/publisher ecosystem for international game monetisation. Market sizing varies wildly by firm and definition: the broad "cross-border payments" market is put at ~$371bn (2025) growing to ~$727bn by 2034 at ~7.9% CAGR (per Fortune Business Insights); narrower cross-border B2B flows are estimated in the multi-trillion-dollar range at ~9.8–10.8% CAGR (per Straits/Global Growth Insights) — treat the headline TAM as directional only, not a clean figure. Structure: fragmented at the long-tail (many gateways/intermediaries per corridor) but consolidating around a few full-stack infra players (Airwallex, Wise, Nium, Rapyd, Adyen). Value accrues to whoever owns the local-payments network + licensing stack across many jurisdictions — a high capital/regulatory barrier (Airwallex holds licences across 60+ markets, recently added UAE in-principle licences, per [[Airwallex receives UAE in-principle payment licenses]]). Why now: (1) Korean game publishers pushing abroad amid a saturated domestic market, with ONE Store launching "ONE Store Global" across 122 countries in Aug 2026 (per The Elec/EDAILY); (2) app-store fee pressure (Apple/Google ~30%) driving D2C and alt-marketplace monetisation — ONE Store's own Web Shop charges ~8% all-in, so cross-border collection/FX cost is now a visible margin line for developers (analysis).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: annualised transaction volume (TPV), revenue/ARR growth, take rate (revenue ÷ TPV), and number of markets/licences. Key players and basis of competition: Airwallex and Nium compete on B2B/platform infrastructure and API breadth; Wise leads on consumer+SMB transfer volume and price transparency; Rapyd on acquiring+payout breadth; Adyen as the enterprise acquirer moving into embedded flows. Basis of competition = licensing footprint + FX/network cost + API/platform depth, not headline price alone. Recent moves: Airwallex raised $320M Series H at $11bn (Jun 2026, per Airwallex/BusinessWire), acquired Leapfin (Jun 2026, IR) and OpenPay for US billing ([[Airwallex acquires OpenPay to build US billing platform]]), and has leaned into embedded/platform partnerships ([[Mellow launches Airwallex-powered global contractor management platform]], [[OPTRA Labs and Airwallex partner to support cross-border SME growth]]). Protagonist position: ahead on scale and growth among private peers — ~$287bn annualised TPV (+120% YoY) and ~$1.3bn annualised revenue (+74% YoY) as of Mar 2026 (per Sacra/Quantlogix), targeting ~$2bn ARR by year-end. Moat: licence portfolio + proprietary local-payments network (scale + regulatory intangibles) with developer switching costs once embedded (analysis).

**Risk flags.**
