SBI, BlackRock Pursue Linking Yen Stablecoin With Tokenized MMF
Japanese financial group SBI Holdings is teaming up with BlackRock and Startale Group to explore linking a yen-denominated stablecoin with tokenized money market funds (MMFs). The companies said on the 27th that they, together with BlackRock Financial Management and BlackRock Japan, signed a memorandum of understanding (MOU) for business cooperation on the initiative.
At the center of the collaboration is JPYSC, Japan's first trust-bank-based yen stablecoin, launched by SBI and Startale in June. SBI Shinsei Trust Bank oversees issuance, while crypto exchange SBI VC Trade handles distribution. The plan is to connect this to BUIDL, the tokenized fund BlackRock introduced in 2024. BUIDL manages roughly $2.17 billion (about 2.9 trillion won) in assets, centered on U.S. Treasuries, repurchase agreements (repos), and cash.
The four companies will examine the scope of cooperation along three axes: technology and infrastructure, business and distribution, and regulation and market development. They will first identify the technical requirements needed to connect tokenized MMFs with stablecoins, and explore business models and distribution plans that leverage each company's financial networks. They will also jointly monitor changes in digital-asset regulations in Japan and other major markets. The cooperation is intended to extend beyond Japan to overseas financial institutions and companies.
Yoshitaka Kitao, chairman of SBI Holdings, said, "The shift to on-chain finance is becoming an unavoidable global trend," adding, "For Japan to keep pace with and lead this change, it must build the foundations for the next-generation financial market early." Jonathan Steele, a senior managing director at BlackRock Financial Management, also emphasized that digital market infrastructure can serve as a bridge between existing financial services and the blockchain ecosystem.
Japan established a regulatory framework for stablecoins through its 2022 amendment to the Payment Services Act, with the related rules taking effect in 2023. The law classifies fiat-linked stablecoins as electronic payment instruments and restricts issuers to banks, trust companies, and registered funds transfer operators. However, critics note that regulatory groundwork alone does not provide the systems needed for tokenized assets and stablecoins to actually work together in financial markets. Connecting different products requires technology, distribution channels, and institutional arrangements that support their use.
The collaboration has not yet reached the stage of completing technical integration or finalizing the launch of a joint financial product. No commercialization timeline has been provided either. Still, observers say it establishes a framework for examining how regulated yen stablecoins and tokenized investment products could operate together in the future. As global financial institutions race to make existing investment products compatible with blockchain technology, the move is seen as an attempt by Japan's financial sector to go beyond the legal foundations of digital assets and build the infrastructure needed for institutional adoption.
The market interprets the MOU less as an immediate source of revenue than as a bid to secure an early lead in the standards and distribution networks of Japan's tokenized asset market over the medium to long term. SBI brings group-wide capabilities spanning banking, securities, and crypto exchanges; BlackRock brings product-design experience as a major asset manager; and Startale brings blockchain technology. Analysts say that if the three forces combine, they could wield considerable influence over the early shape of a yen-based on-chain financial ecosystem.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-10-27, SBI Holdings, Startale Group and BlackRock (via BlackRock Financial Management and BlackRock Japan) signed a non-binding memorandum of understanding (MOU) to explore linking tokenised money market funds (MMFs) with JPYSC, the yen-denominated stablecoin SBI and Startale launched in June 2026. The intended connection is between BlackRock's BUIDL tokenised Treasury fund (~$2.1–2.9B AUM, launched March 2024 via Securitize) and JPYSC. The parties framed cooperation across three axes: (1) technology & infrastructure, (2) business & distribution, (3) regulation & market development. SBI's own release notably did not name a specific BlackRock fund, a blockchain, or a timeline — "BUIDL" is the market's inference from BlackRock's existing product. No commercial product, launch date, or technical integration exists yet; this is an exploratory framework only.

**[1] Who the players are.**
- **SBI Holdings** — Japanese financial conglomerate (banking, securities, crypto exchange SBI VC Trade, trust bank SBI Shinsei Trust Bank). Chairman Yoshitaka Kitao: "The shift to on-chain finance is becoming an unavoidable global trend." SBI brings group-wide distribution across banking/securities/crypto.
- **Startale Group** — Singapore-based Web3 infrastructure firm founded Jan 2023 by Sota Watanabe (also founder of Astar Network, and co-builder of Soneium, the Sony/Startale Ethereum L2 JV "Sony Block Solutions Labs"). Raised ~$63M Series A in March 2026 (SBI $50M, Sony Innovation Fund $13M). Brings blockchain technology. See [[SBI Holdings forms tokenized stock venture with Startale]].
- **BlackRock** — world's largest asset manager; runs BUIDL (BlackRock USD Institutional Digital Liquidity Fund), the leading/co-leading tokenised US-Treasury MMF across 8–9 chains. Senior MD Jonathan Steele cited digital market infrastructure as a "bridge" between TradFi and blockchain. BlackRock filed with the SEC in May 2026 to launch two new tokenised funds and on-chain share classes for its ~$7B MMF.
- **JPYSC** — Japan's first trust-bank-based yen stablecoin, launched June 24 2026. Issued by SBI Shinsei Trust Bank, distributed exclusively via SBI VC Trade, pegged 1:1 to yen, classified as an *electronic payment instrument* under the Payment Services Act — crucially with no per-transaction cap, unlike earlier fund-transfer-type yen stablecoins subject to a ¥1M ceiling.

**[2] Why it matters.** This is the first time BlackRock has publicly joined a yen-stablecoin / tokenised-MMF linkage in Japan. The economic prize is on-chain cash management and 24/7 settlement: a regulated yen stablecoin (payments leg) plumbed into a yield-bearing tokenised Treasury fund (collateral/yield leg) could let institutions move between yen liquidity and USD-Treasury yield on-chain. SBI gets a flagship anchor tenant for JPYSC and its tokenised-asset ambitions; BlackRock gets a regulated Japanese distribution beachhead for BUIDL; Startale supplies the rails. The market reads the MOU less as near-term revenue and more as a land-grab for standards and distribution in Japan's nascent on-chain finance market.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions (skeptical):**

1. This is an MOU — non-binding, no named chain, no named BlackRock fund, no timeline. SBI's own release reportedly did *not* name BUIDL. How much of the "BUIDL ↔ JPYSC" framing is market inference rather than stated commitment? Is the headline over-claiming?
2. MOUs in crypto/TradFi frequently expire without product. What is the base rate of SBI/BlackRock MOUs that actually ship a commercial product within 18 months?
3. What is the concrete use case that needs *both* a yen stablecoin *and* a USD-Treasury MMF? If BUIDL is USD-denominated and JPYSC is yen, who bears the FX leg, and does that reintroduce the very friction tokenisation is meant to remove?
4. JPYSC is distributed *exclusively* through SBI VC Trade and was deliberately capped pending tax/regulatory clarity. Can an institutional MMF-linkage product even function at meaningful scale under those distribution constraints?
5. Which blockchain? JPYSC sits in Startale's Soneium/Astar orbit; BUIDL is native to Ethereum/Avalanche/Solana/etc. Bridging (Wormhole-style) adds custody and systemic risk — is there any evidence a native solution is planned?
6. Is BlackRock the lead here or a logo? "BlackRock Financial Management + BlackRock Japan" signing an MOU is a low-commitment posture; no capital, no product SKU. Is this strategically material to BlackRock or a cheap option on Japan?
7. Regulatory: foreign tokenised MMFs (US Treasuries) distributed to Japanese institutions touch securities, banking, and crypto regimes simultaneously. Is the Payment Services Act framework actually sufficient, or does this need FSA securities-side approval that doesn't exist yet?
8. Competitive moat: Franklin Templeton (BENJI ~$2.0B), Circle/Hashnote (USYC ~$3.0B), Ondo (OUSG) could strike parallel JPY deals. What stops SBI/JPYSC from partnering with multiple MMF issuers, diluting BlackRock's "early lead" narrative?
9. JPYC (the rival fund-transfer-type yen stablecoin) already launched and markets itself as "Asia's only truly global" yen token. Does JPYSC's trust-bank model + this MOU actually leapfrog JPYC, or is this catch-up PR?
10. Demand side: is there verified institutional appetite for yield-bearing tokenised Treasuries settled against yen, or is this supply-led infrastructure chasing a thin market? What AUM/volume target has anyone disclosed? (None found.)
11. Counterparty/trust-bank risk: JPYSC redemption depends on SBI Shinsei Trust Bank. How is the reserve backing audited, and does linking to BUIDL create cross-contagion if either leg de-pegs?
12. "Extends beyond Japan to overseas institutions" — is there any named overseas partner, or is this aspirational boilerplate?
13. Timing: announced same week as a wave of tokenised-RWA headlines. Is this incremental signal or noise amplified by the BlackRock name?

**Importance: 4/5 — rationale.** First public BlackRock involvement in a Japanese yen-stablecoin + tokenised-MMF linkage is genuinely notable: it pairs the world's largest asset manager and its flagship tokenised Treasury fund (BUIDL, ~$2–2.9B) with Japan's first trust-bank stablecoin (JPYSC) and SBI's full financial stack — a credible attempt to set standards for yen-based on-chain finance. Marked down from 5 because it is only a non-binding MOU with no named chain, no named fund in SBI's release, no product, and no timeline; real-world impact is contingent and unproven. High strategic signal, low near-term execution certainty.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** The deal sits at the intersection of two fast-growing on-chain segments: tokenised money market funds (MMFs) / US-Treasury products and fiat-linked stablecoins. Total tokenised RWA (ex-stablecoins) reached ~$31.4bn by mid-May 2026, with Treasuries/MMFs ~80% of that — the dominant category (per RWA.xyz data, via sqmagazine/spark.money, mid-May 2026). BlackRock's BUIDL crossed ~$3bn AUM in May 2026 (note text cites ~$2.17bn at the time of the MOU; the gap reflects different cut-dates), Franklin Templeton's BENJI topped ~$2.5bn, and Circle's USYC ~$3.0bn (per spark.money / eco.com, Q1–May 2026). Structure: issuance is concentrated among a few TradFi-grade sponsors (BlackRock, Franklin, Circle/Hashnote, Ondo) — high barriers (regulatory licensing, custody, distribution rails, brand), so consolidated at the top rather than fragmented. "Why now": the yield gap is the driver — compliant payment stablecoins pay holders ~0% (GENIUS Act in the US, PSA in Japan keep reserve income with the issuer), while tokenised Treasuries deliver ~4–5.25% APY on the same dollar; linking an MMF to a stablecoin lets holders capture yield + stablecoin-grade settlement/collateral utility (per eco.com/circle.com, 2026). Japan's PSA (2022 amendment, in force mid-2023) classifies fiat stablecoins as electronic payment instruments restricted to banks/trust companies/registered transfer operators; the FSA removed the ¥1m per-transaction cap in Aug 2026, unlocking institutional-scale yen stablecoin flows (per fystack/techtimes, Aug 2026).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: AUM/TVL on-chain, net yield passed to holders (APY vs T-bill minus fees), number of supported chains (distribution reach), and secondary-market/collateral utility. Key players and basis of competition: BlackRock BUIDL and Franklin BENJI compete on brand + distribution breadth (BENJI on 8 chains); Circle USYC and Ondo USDY compete on collateral utility and yield (USYC overtook BUIDL on AUM via derivatives-collateral use). Competition is shifting from "who has the biggest fund" to "who controls the rails that connect funds to stablecoins/payments." Recent regional moves: DBS + Franklin Templeton + Ripple MOU to merge tokenised MMFs with RLUSD (Sep 2025, see internal comp); OCBC/Lion Global/DigiFT tokenised gold fund (Apr 2026); JPMorgan tokenised MMF on Ethereum (Dec 2025). Protagonist position: BlackRock is ahead globally on product, but in Japan it is the entrant — SBI supplies the moat (group-wide bank+securities+crypto-exchange distribution, JPYSC issuance via SBI Shinsei Trust Bank + SBI VC Trade) and Startale supplies the blockchain stack. Combined, the trio is positioned ahead of any single-firm yen on-chain effort `(analysis)`; moat = regulatory/distribution scale + first-mover on a trust-bank yen stablecoin.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation, round or deal economics in the news (MOU only, no commercialisation timeline) — trading multiples not applicable; "no data" on EV/Revenue, P/E etc. for this transaction. Comparable strategic linkups (framework comps, not valuations):
- [[DBS, Franklin Templeton and Ripple to merge tokenized collateral with stablecoins]] — same MMF↔stablecoin-collateral thesis, Asia, Sep 2025.
- [[JPMorgan launches tokenized money market fund on Ethereum]] — incumbent tokenised MMF, Dec 2025.
- [[OCBC, Lion Global, and DigiFT launch tokenized gold fund]] — Asia TradFi tokenised fund, Apr 2026.
- [[World's first yen-pegged stablecoin JPYC debuts in Japan]] — rival yen stablecoin (JPYC, fund-transfer licence) vs JPYSC (trust-bank model), Oct 2025.
- [[DigiFT raises $25 million for tokenised RWA exchange]] — SBI-backed RWA infra round, Aug 2025.
AUM reference points (not multiples): BUIDL ~$2.17–3.0bn, BENJI ~$2.5bn, USYC ~$3.0bn, Ondo USDY ~$2.1bn (2026). Flag: in-line with a crowded field of ~$2–3bn tokenised-MMF products; the differentiator is the yen leg, not scale.

**Risk flags.**
1. Execution / "MOU-ware": purely exploratory — no fund named, no chain, no timeline, no commercialisation date. Second-order: announcement value may fade if no live product follows within 12 months; precedent (DBS/Franklin/Ripple MOU, Sep 2025) shows these often stall at the framework stage.
2. Regulatory fit of yield pass-through: JPYSC is a regulated payment instrument that by design does not pay holders yield; wiring it to a yield-bearing MMF risks regulatory reclassification or friction under the PSA (who captures the T-bill yield, and does that make JPYSC look like a security/investment product?). Second-order: FSA could constrain the economics that make the link attractive.
3. Rail/distribution dependence & concentration: BlackRock's Japan on-chain access runs through SBI's stack (issuance, VC Trade distribution, Startale tech) — single-partner concentration; if SBI's rails or JPYSC adoption lag, BlackRock has no independent yen distribution. Mirror risk for SBI: disintermediation if BlackRock later partners with a megabank (MUFG/SMBC/Mizuho, live FY2026) stablecoin instead.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a bid to set the standard for Japan's yen-denominated on-chain MMF/stablecoin stack before the megabank consortium (MUFG/SMBC/Mizuho) goes live in FY2026 — a land-grab for rails and distribution, not near-term revenue. Falsifiable thesis: if a live BUIDL↔JPYSC (or a joint product) ships within ~12 months, the trio locks early standards for yen RWA; trigger to watch = a named fund + chain + launch date, or JPYSC AUM/integration milestones. What breaks the thesis: the MOU lapses with no product, or a megabank stablecoin wins distribution first.
