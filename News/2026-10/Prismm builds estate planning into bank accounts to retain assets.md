# Prismm builds estate planning into bank accounts to retain assets

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[TechCrunch — Fintech] Meet Prismm, a startup that helps banks keep families’ money in-house after an account holder dies. It does this by building beneficiary and estate planning into accounts from day one, which makes the transfer of assets to heirs less complicated. Prismm is presenting its mission today on the Startup Battlefield stage at TechCrunch Disrupt 2026.

### [0] What happened (dated)

On **2026-10-14**, startup **Prismm** (getprismm.com) presented on the Startup Battlefield stage at **TechCrunch Disrupt 2026**. Prismm sells **"estate orchestration infrastructure" to banks and credit unions**: it embeds beneficiary designation and estate planning into deposit accounts from day one so that, when an account holder dies, heirs can view assets/documents and initiate eligible beneficiary transfers digitally — keeping the money in-house rather than letting it leak to a rival institution ("estate attrition"). This was a **product/mission presentation, not a funding announcement**. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/14/prismm-helps-banks-keep-inherited-money-in-house/))

### [1] Company background

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Estate/inheritance infrastructure sits at the intersection of the "Great Wealth Transfer" and bank deposit retention. Per Cerulli (via CNBC, 2026-03) ~$124tn is set to pass from Boomers/Silent Gen through 2048, with heirs already inheriting ~$2.5tn/year, projected to top $4tn around 2036 (via Forbes, 2026-08). The structural problem Prismm targets: when an account holder dies, assets typically freeze and route through probate, and heirs frequently move the money to their own (often different) institution — a leakage point for the incumbent bank's deposits/AUM. The segment is early and fragmented: estate-planning tech to date has been sold mostly to RIAs/advisers (Wealth.com, Vanilla, Trust & Will), NOT embedded at the bank-core/day-one-account level, which is Prismm's wedge. Why now: the transfer wave is cresting in the 2026–2036 decade (~55% of activity, via Forbes 2026-08), and banks face deposit-cost pressure, making retention-at-death a board-level issue (analysis).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs run on AUM/deposits retained per inheritance event, number of accounts with beneficiaries designated, advisory firms/B/Ds integrated, and (for the SaaS layer) seats/advisors on platform. Key players by positioning: estate-planning SaaS for advisers — Wealth.com (platform supports firms managing >$15tn client assets, +664% YoY AI workflows, per company via FinTech Global 2026-04), Vanilla, Trust & Will; adjacent wealthtech in-base — [[Farther raises $150 million Series D for wealth management]], [[Wealth.com launches integrated tax planning platform]]. Prismm's differentiator vs these: it sells to the BANK and embeds beneficiary/estate setup into the account "from day one" (products Prismm Designate, Prismm Anchor, upcoming Prismm Continuity, via TechCrunch 2026-10-14) — i.e. B2B2C distribution through the deposit-holder rather than the adviser. Position: niche/early entrant, not catching an incumbent — the "embed at bank" lane is relatively uncontested (analysis). Moat candidates: core-banking integration switching costs + distribution lock-in if a bank standardizes on it (analysis); none proven, company is pre-scale.

**Comps & multiples.** No round, valuation, revenue or customer count disclosed for Prismm (founded 2020 by Martha Underwood & Shella Sylla; TechCrunch Disrupt 2026 Battlefield semifinalist, Enterprise & Security track). So **no multiple can be computed — no data**. Reference points for the adjacent estate-planning-SaaS lane (round valuations, NOT market caps, and NOT direct comps since Prismm's buyer is the bank, not the adviser): Wealth.com — $65M Series B (2026-04, backers incl. Schwab, GV, Citi Ventures; via FinTech Global); Vanilla — $35M raise (2024, via Yahoo Finance); earlier Wealth.com $30M Series A led by GV (via Axios). In-base: [[Wealth.com raises $65M Series B for AI wealth platform]], [[Farther raises $150 million Series D for wealth management]]. Distribution of multiples not computed (no comparable disclosed figures); comparison is qualitative only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Pre-revenue / zero disclosure** — no round, metrics or named bank customers; a Battlefield pitch is not traction. Thesis currently rests entirely on the macro transfer narrative, not proven adoption (execution/validation risk).
2. **Long, procurement-heavy sales cycle into banks + core-banking integration** — embedding "from day one" means touching the deposit core, KYC and legal/beneficiary workflows across 50 states' probate regimes; adoption is slow and incumbent-core-vendor-gated (disintermediation risk: core providers like FIS/Fiserv/Jack Henry could bundle the same feature and capture the value layer).
3. **The claimed benefit (retained deposits) is hard to attribute and the behavioral premise is unproven** — heirs may still move inherited funds regardless of a smoother transfer; no public statistic was found quantifying how much a bank actually retains via day-one beneficiary setup [UNSOURCED], so the ROI pitch to banks is assertion, not evidence.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** If it works, Prismm reframes estate planning from an adviser-side product into deposit-retention infrastructure sold to banks — a new entry point rather than a re-rating of the adviser-SaaS cohort (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: watch for a named bank/credit-union deal with disclosed account coverage or a priced seed/Series A within ~12 months; absence of either by mid-2027, or an incumbent core vendor shipping native beneficiary-at-onboarding, would break the standalone thesis.
