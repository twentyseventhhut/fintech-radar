Building a financial network for global money: A conversation with Plasma CSO, Zaheer Ebtikar
Managing global payments requires predictable fees, fast finality and broad asset choice, without the overhead of maintaining dozens of separate blockchain integrations. At BVNK, our vision is to deliver one trusted network for every form of money, abstracting complexity so businesses can access the payment rails that best fit their needs.
That’s why we continue expanding our platform, adding native support for payment-first infrastructure like Plasma . Plasma is designed for high-frequency USDT* transfers. We sat down with their Chief Strategy Officer to discuss global dollar movement, solving the "gas token" headache, and their work supporting global payroll with platforms like Deel.
What kind of person and business are BVNK and Plasma  building for? And what's broken about how they move money today?
People whose lives do not fit in one country. They earn in one place, live in another, and often have a company in a third. Today they run that life through five or six providers and do the joining up themselves. They move money, convert it, keep spare balances in case a transfer is slow, and chase support when a payment fails. Each provider does its part fine. Nobody owns the whole thing. I know because I am that customer, and so are many of us on the team. We are building the infrastructure we wanted and could not find. 
Sending a stablecoin used to require buying and holding a separate native token just to pay "gas"– the transfer fees. How does Plasma remove that friction for users?
You choose to be paid in stablecoins and give a Plasma address. On payday the money is there in seconds, and the transfer carries no network fee. There is no waiting on a wire. With Plasma One, you can spend it on your card the same day, anywhere cards are accepted. That is the part I care about. Getting paid is only useful if the money lands somewhere you can use it. 
Many people paid this way also run their own companies. What changes for a founder or agency owner paying themselves and a team across borders?
A lot of our customers run a company, often more than one. Revenue lands in one account, costs leave another, and they pay themselves across a border. Paying a team in four countries usually means four sets of fees, cut-off times and delays. When the payout is a stablecoin, a contractor in Dubai and one in Singapore are paid the same way at the same moment. We are building for the business side too, because it's the same person. 
Most businesses don’t want to manage separate blockchain integrations. How does access through BVNK make it easier to use Plasma for global payments?
BVNK already sits in the payment flow for companies that pay people around the world. Plasma is now one of the rails behind the API those companies already use, so they can pay someone on Plasma without building anything new. Credit to the BVNK team for the work that went into it. 
At Split Capital, you approached crypto as an investor. What did that experience teach you about demand for digital dollars, and what conviction brought you to Plasma?
Running a fund, my job was to find where the real demand was. Stablecoins kept being the answer. They were the one product in crypto that people outside crypto used every day without caring how it worked.  Plasma is being built to fix that problem. 
What does the future hold for Plasma and your customers over the next few years? 
The test is simple. Do the people we built this for use it more, stay, and bring the people around them? If in a few years someone is paid into Plasma, spends from it and holds their money in it, and never once thinks about what it runs on, we did our job. ‍ *USDT on BVNK is supported for customers outside the EU/EEA.

### [0] What happened (one line)
On ~2026-10-12/13 BVNK added **native support for Plasma**, a Tether/Bitfinex- and Founders Fund-backed stablecoin-first Layer 1, so businesses using BVNK's API can pay out **USDT on Plasma with zero network fee and sub-second finality** — no XPL "gas" token required. Global payroll platform **Deel** is cited as an early live use case. (Source: BVNK blog Q&A with Plasma CSO Zaheer Ebtikar.)

### [1] What is Plasma (external)
- **Plasma (XPL)** = a Bitcoin-anchored, EVM-compatible Layer 1 purpose-built for stablecoin payments, backed by Tether sister-company **Bitfinex** and Peter **Thiel's Founders Fund**.
- Mainnet beta + XPL token went live **2025-09-25**; raised ~**$373M** in an oversubscribed public sale (7x the $50M target), launched with 100+ DeFi partners (Aave, Ethena, Fluid, Euler) and ~$2B stablecoin liquidity, hitting ~$5.6B TVL in week one. (Internal: [[Plasma launches Layer 1 mainnet with $2B stablecoin liquidity]], [[Thiel-backed Plasma debuts $373M stablecoin neobank]].)
- Key mechanic: a **protocol-level paymaster sponsors gas** for simple USDT transfers, so users send stablecoins without holding XPL — removing the classic "need a native gas token" friction. Plasma later launched **Plasma One**, a consumer stablecoin neobank/card product. (Internal: [[Plasma launches Plasma One stablecoin banking product]].)
- Plasma is positioned as a challenger to TRON for high-frequency USDT settlement.

  - https://thedefiant.io/news/blockchains/plasma-stablecoin-chain-mainnet-beta-tge-launch-date

### [2] What is BVNK + why this matters (external + internal)
- **BVNK** = UK-founded (2021) stablecoin payments infrastructure operating in 130+ countries across major chains; sits in the payment flow for firms that pay people cross-border via one API.
- Adding Plasma as "one of the rails behind the API those companies already use" lets BVNK clients pay on Plasma **without building a new integration** — BVNK abstracts chain complexity ("one network for every form of money").
- **Why native Plasma support matters:** zero-fee + instant USDT payouts materially improve unit economics for high-volume, low-value cross-border flows (payroll, contractor/gig payouts). It plugs Plasma's rails into a distribution layer that already reaches enterprise customers, and gives Plasma a flagship enterprise reference (Deel).
- Note: BVNK states **USDT on BVNK is supported for customers outside the EU/EEA** — a regulatory caveat (MiCA).

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
### Red-team questions
1. Is this a real *new integration* or a marketing Q&A? The only primary source is a BVNK-owned blog interview with Plasma's CSO — promotional, not an independent/technical announcement.
2. Is Plasma support actually **live and in production**, or announced/preview? Secondary coverage says "adds native support," but scale, volume limits and availability by market are unspecified.
3. "Zero network fee" — sponsored by whom, and for how long? Protocol paymasters subsidize gas; is this sustainable, or does it shift cost to BVNK/Plasma and risk fee reintroduction?
4. Is **Deel** genuinely live on *Plasma specifically*, or just live on BVNK (which may route via other chains)? The May 2026 Deel-BVNK launch predates Plasma support, so "already using it" may conflate BVNK payroll with Plasma rails.
5. Geographic/regulatory scope: USDT on BVNK excludes EU/EEA (MiCA). How much of the addressable payroll market is actually served?
6. Counterparty concentration: Plasma is Tether/Bitfinex-aligned. Does routing payroll through a Tether-centric L1 add single-point-of-failure / reserve-quality risk vs USDC chains?
7. XPL token context: XPL has reportedly had a volatile/declining price post-launch. Does token/validator-economics risk affect chain security or paymaster funding?
8. Competitive differentiation: TRON already dominates cheap USDT transfers; Stripe/Circle have their own L1s (Tempo/Arc). Why is Plasma the pick, and is it durable?
9. Does Mastercard's ownership of BVNK constrain or accelerate this? A Mastercard-owned entity promoting a Tether-linked chain is strategically notable but under-examined.
10. Volume evidence: no numbers on throughput, corridors, or how many BVNK clients have opted in — can't size impact.
11. Finality claim ("sub-second"/"in seconds") — is that chain finality or BVNK-side UX? Reorg/settlement-risk unaddressed.
12. Is this duplicative of the Deel/BVNK or Plasma One stories already in the DB? (Verdict: fresh — this is a distinct BVNK↔Plasma rail integration event, not previously covered.)
13. Reversibility/lock-in: how easily could BVNK drop Plasma if XPL economics or Tether regulatory status deteriorate?

Importance: 3/5 — A concrete, directionally meaningful integration: it connects a fast-growing stablecoin-first L1 (Plasma) to an enterprise payments distribution layer (BVNK, now Mastercard-owned) with a real anchor use case (Deel payroll). But it is a rails-expansion announcement sourced only from a promotional BVNK Q&A, with no disclosed volumes, limited EU/EEA availability, and Tether/XPL counterparty risk — incremental rather than category-defining.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Stablecoin payments infrastructure — the "rails + orchestration" layer between fiat on/off-ramps and settlement chains. Market structure: four sub-models — issuer networks (Circle, Paxos), custody-first (Fireblocks), payment-API/orchestration (BVNK, Bridge, Zero Hash, Conduit), and the newer stablecoin-native L1s (Plasma, Stripe's Tempo, Circle's Arc, Stable, Codex) that compete to be the settlement chain itself (per stablecoininsider.org / frame.xyz, 2026). Size/growth: stablecoin settlement volume grew 91% YoY to $10.9tn (per [[Stablecoin volumes grew 91% to $10.9 trillion last year]]); total USDT float ~$183bn as of Sep 2026 (DeFiLlama, via eco.com). "Why now": the layer is consolidating fast — Stripe bought Bridge (~$1.1bn, 2024), Mastercard agreed to acquire BVNK itself for up to $1.8bn in Mar 2026 and closed Aug 2026 (~$1.5bn upfront + up to $300M earn-out, per onebullex.com) (analysis). So this integration ships while BVNK is now a Mastercard subsidiary — strategically it extends Mastercard's reach onto a new settlement rail.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/processing volume, take rate per transfer, chains/rails supported, licensing footprint (US MTL states), settlement finality/fees. Players and basis of competition: BVNK (~$20bn+ annualized TPV, [[BVNK surpasses $20bn in processing volume]]; 50-state MTL; now Mastercard-owned) competes on distribution + rail-neutrality; Bridge/Stripe competes on merchant distribution + reserve-yield sharing (3–4% APY via BlackRock/Fidelity) and self-serve dev access; Zero Hash on US-regulated B2B2C licensing (clients Stripe, Morgan Stanley, Interactive Brokers) — raising above $1.5bn ([[Zerohash pursues funding above $1.5 billion valuation]]); Fireblocks on MPC custody for 2,400+ institutions (payment logic left to the client). BVNK's position: rail-neutral orchestrator — its stated moat is abstracting "dozens of separate blockchain integrations" behind one API (from the note). Adding Plasma is pure rail expansion: Plasma becomes "one of the rails behind the API those companies already use." Position: a leading independent orchestrator, now with Mastercard scale — ahead on distribution, undifferentiated on the underlying rail.

**Comps & multiples.** This is a product/integration, not a round — no transaction multiple to compute for the news itself. Reference valuations (all private-round / deal values, not public market caps):
- BVNK: Mastercard deal up to $1.8bn (Mar 2026); prior talks $1.5–2.5bn ([[Coinbase and Mastercard in talks to buy stablecoin firm BVNK]]); against ~$20bn TPV that is deal value / TPV = $1.8bn / $20bn = 0.09x TPV (analysis; TPV is gross flow, not revenue — treat as directional only, not a revenue multiple).
- Zero Hash: >$1.5bn round valuation (2026); earlier M&A talks up to $2bn.
- Plasma: raised ~$373M to launch ([[Thiel-backed Plasma debuts $373M stablecoin neobank]]); XPL token market value is NOT a comparable to a payments-infra equity — different asset class.
Revenue/EBITDA for all names: not publicly disclosed → EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E = no data / [UNSOURCED]. Distribution not computed (fewer than 3 comparable revenue multiples); qualitative only: the orchestration layer is clearing ~$1.5–2bn private valuations on undisclosed economics — rich looks plausible but unverifiable.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Rail is a weak settlement choice (adoption risk).** Plasma's own network has de-rated hard post-launch: stablecoin TVL fell ~72% from a ~$6.3bn peak to ~$1.8bn, XPL token ~-85% from ATH, and in Oct 2025 Plasma saw ~$996M stablecoin outflows while Tron took ~$1.1bn inflows (per 99bitcoins.com / blockeden.xyz). Much early TVL was mercenary yield-farming (~65% in lending for XPL rewards). Second-order: BVNK can offer the rail, but payroll counterparties may still prefer Tron/Ethereum where USDT liquidity and acceptance are deepest — integration ≠ volume.
2. **Rail disintermediation / zero-fee pressures take rate.** Plasma's pitch is zero-fee USDT transfers (protocol paymaster). If "no network fee" becomes the norm across stablecoin L1s, the value captured at the settlement layer compresses toward zero, pushing all margin to orchestration/compliance — exactly where BVNK and Bridge then fight on price. Dependence on someone else's rails cuts both ways.
3. **Announced vs live (de-PR).** Source is a BVNK blog Q&A with Plasma's CSO, not a usage disclosure. No volume, no customer count on-Plasma, and USDT support is explicitly EU/EEA-excluded (from the note) — the addressable base for this specific rail is narrower than the PR implies; "who's silent" = economics and actual throughput.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Confirms the orchestrator thesis: as stablecoin L1s proliferate (Plasma, Tempo, Arc, Stable, Codex), the durable value sits with the rail-neutral API that can add or drop any chain, not with any single chain — BVNK/Bridge are a bet against chain-level lock-in (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if, over the next 2–3 quarters, Plasma's share of BVNK-routed USDT payroll stays negligible versus Tron/Ethereum, the integration is a logo, not a rail shift. Trigger to watch: any BVNK/Mastercard disclosure of on-Plasma volume, or Plasma TVL/outflow stabilizing — absent either, treat as optionality, not traction.
