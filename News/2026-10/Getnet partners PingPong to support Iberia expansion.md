Getnet, the leading AI-payments company and part of Santander Group, partners with PingPong, the intelligent payments infrastructure for global growth, to support its expansion into Iberia and provide international businesses with access to local payment capabilities in Spain and Portugal
 
The collaboration brings together two established payments businesses to deliver a stronger payment capability in the region and a simple, streamlined solution for global businesses looking to expand operations in Iberia. By combining PingPong’s global cross-border payments infrastructure with Getnet’s European acquiring capabilities and access to leading local payment methods, including Bizum in Spain and MB WAY in Portugal, the partnership aims to simplify market access, reduce technical complexity and provide  premium support to merchants expanding into the region. 
“This partnership represents another important milestone in Getnet's strategy to expand our open-market business across Europe while strengthening the connections between Iberia and one of the world's fastest-growing commerce ecosystems. By combining PingPong's strong presence across Asia with Getnet's leadership in Europe and Latin America, we are creating new opportunities for international merchants to grow with a seamless and trusted payment experience.”
 Fabrice Mendez , Global Head of Partnerships at Getnet
With a network of more than 750,000 businesses across over 200 countries, PingPong brings an established international footprint to the partnership. Through this collaboration, Getnet complements PingPong’s global offering with local payment capabilities and connectivity, helping international merchants navigate the Iberian payments landscape and access new growth opportunities through a streamlined integration and dedicated support.
“Iberia is one of Europe's most valuable commerce markets, and the 750,000+ businesses in our network want to operate there on payment infrastructure they already trust. Our move into Iberia is the latest step in an ongoing global expansion, driven by rising customer demand and sustained investment in our international growth”
 Aaron Xu, General Manager, Europe and Americas at PingPong
 About PingPong 
Founded in 2015, PingPong is a global cross-border B2B payments platform supporting over 750,000 businesses across more than 200 countries. The company annually processes over $300bn and holds 80+ financial licences worldwide. PingPong operates from 44 offices across 18 countries.
For more information, visit www.pingpongx.com .

# Context-enrichment: Getnet partners PingPong to support Iberia expansion
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

**FRESHNESS: fresh.** First Getnet x PingPong tie-up (no prior note). Press release dated 2026-10-19 (PR Newswire / Santander). Not a reprint of the LatAm-framed Finextra retelling — same deal, but no existing note covers it. Note it sits directly downstream of Getnet's 2026-10-06 "Single Entry Point" Europe launch, so this is the first named merchant-channel plug into that new rail.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- A commercial partnership (not M&A, no equity, no disclosed terms/fees): PingPong — a China-founded cross-border B2B payments platform — resells / plugs into Getnet's European acquiring so PingPong's merchants can collect in Spain & Portugal via local methods (Bizum, MB WAY) plus cards. Getnet = Santander Group's merchant-acquiring arm.
- The PR's own numbers describe PingPong, not the deal: 750k+ businesses, 200+ countries, $300bn+ processed annually, 80+ licences, 44 offices/18 countries, founded 2015. Zero deal-specific metrics (no merchant count onboarded, no TPV target, no go-live date). This is an announcement, not traction.
- **Why structured this way:** PingPong needs *local acquiring + local rails* (Bizum/MB WAY) it cannot economically build/license itself in two mid-size markets; Getnet needs *merchant flow* onto the "Single Entry Point" (SEP) rail it just extended to 32 European countries (announced 2026-10-06). So this is a classic "specialist cross-border platform rents local acquiring from a bank-owned processor" structure — the real asset being monetized is Santander's direct-member status in Iberia, not PingPong's brand. The PR anchors to PingPong's flattering global scale because the Iberia-specific substance is thin.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- Direct acquiring/local-rail peers in Europe: Adyen (direct licences in 40+ markets — the real moat), Stripe (largely indirect acquirer), Checkout.com, Worldpay, Nexi/Nets in Iberia. Getnet's edge is narrow and specific: direct-member access to Bizum (ES) and MB WAY (PT), which Adyen/Stripe reach but which a bank-owned acquirer offers with first-party economics.
- PingPong's cross-border peers: Airwallex, Payoneer, Wise Platform, Nium — all racing to bolt local acceptance onto remittance/collection rails. PingPong is "catching up" on acceptance/acquiring vs Airwallex/Adyen; the Getnet deal is how it closes that gap in Iberia without a licence.
- Getnet itself is serially doing these plug-in partnerships: [[Aevi and Getnet launch global merchant payments partnership in Mexico]] (2026-05, orchestration + local acquiring, one integration — same template), plus DEUNA and Nayax tie-ups. PingPong-Iberia is the European instance of a repeatable playbook.
- **Why the lay of the land:** acquiring is a scale/licence game. Bank-owned Getnet (top-10 global acquirer, #1 LatAm, €238bn / 10.5bn txns / 1.2m clients in 2025) wins on licence+balance-sheet, loses on developer/API velocity vs Adyen/Stripe — so it competes by becoming the *wholesale local-rail supplier* to platforms rather than winning the merchant directly. Second-order: the margin on this flow is thin and shared; Getnet captures interchange-adjacent acquiring economics, PingPong keeps the merchant relationship.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Getnet: Santander's acquiring vehicle, built partly on ex-Wirecard assets (2021 acquisition closed), organized under PagoNxt, now pushing "open market" (third-party, non-Santander-customer) revenue — ~30% of Payments revenue, with TPV growth from Mexico, Brazil and Europe. Fabrice Mendez (Global Head of Partnerships) fronts both this and the Aevi deal — same person, same playbook.
- PingPong: founded 2015 (Hangzhou), e-commerce seller payouts → broadened to B2B collection, licences, InvestXB fund ops, Visa card-to-account ([[Visa partners with PingPong for B2B card-to-account payments]]), bought out Xinhang for its China domestic licence ([[PingPong acquires remaining 49% of Xinhang Payment]]). Europe push is deliberate and multi-step.
- **Why the company acts this way:** Getnet is a commodity take-rate business inside a bank; to lift its multiple and justify the "open-market franchise" story it must add distribution volume cheaply → partnerships (Aevi, PingPong, Nayax, DEUNA) are low-capex volume. PingPong faces rising-cost local build and regulatory barriers in Europe → renting rails is faster than licensing. Both pressures point to exactly this deal.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- Novelty: low. The *capability* (PingPong merchants accepting Bizum/MB WAY via a local acquirer) is not new technology; it is a distribution plug into Getnet's just-launched SEP. The genuinely new thing underneath is Getnet SEP itself (32 countries, one integration, 20 currencies, payouts to 140 countries, 2026-10-06) — this PingPong deal is its first visible customer narrative.
- Traction: **none disclosed** — no merchants live, no volume, no date. "Supports expansion into Iberia" = intent. Treat as announced, not live.
- **Why value-add is real-but-shared:** For merchants the value is real (one integration, local rails, trusted Santander acquiring). But who captures the margin? Getnet gets the acquiring/local-rail economics (the durable piece); PingPong keeps the client and the cross-border FX spread. The risk for PingPong is becoming a reseller whose local economics are set by the bank — i.e. the "dashboard vs the-one-who-settles" problem. The real prize sits with Santander's rails.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Watch for: a go-live date, named merchant logos, TPV/merchant count, and whether the deal extends beyond Iberia onto the full 32-country SEP footprint (the logical next step, and the only thing that would move importance up).
- Sentiment: routine paytech partnership, no market-moving signal for Santander (immaterial to a group doing €238bn Getnet TPV). Regulatory backdrop: EU PSD3/instant-payments and local-scheme (Bizum/MB WAY) membership requirements are precisely why renting Getnet's direct membership is attractive.
- **Why the market goes this way:** cross-border platforms are converging on "local acquiring everywhere" and bank-owned acquirers are converging on "wholesale rail to platforms"; expect more Airwallex/Nium/Payoneer-style firms to sign Getnet/Nexi/Worldpay for exactly these rails. Counterintuitive second-order: each such deal commoditizes the cross-border platform's local layer, so differentiation migrates up to FX, treasury and credit — not acceptance.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Cross-border B2B payments sits atop a very large flow base but a modest revenue pool: a representative sizing puts B2B cross-border payments revenue at ~$48.6bn in 2025 rising to ~$112.4bn by 2034 (~9.8% CAGR) — per Market.us-type industry research, via openPR syndication, as of 2025 (directional; TAM estimates vary widely and some conflate trillions of *flow* with *revenue*, so treat as order-of-magnitude). Structure: the chain splits between acquiring/local-acceptance (Getnet's layer — capital- and licence-intensive, bank-owned incumbents) and cross-border orchestration/FX/global collection (PingPong's layer — licence-heavy, 80+ licences, network-driven). Value accrues to whoever owns the merchant relationship and the local payment method rails; neither side alone covers both. Why now: global merchants want single-integration access to local methods (Bizum in Spain, MB WAY in Portugal) rather than stitching country-by-country — the partnership is a de-fragmentation play, pairing Asia-origin demand (PingPong's 750k+ businesses) with European acceptance (Getnet). Second-order: acquirers increasingly monetise *distribution* to foreign PSPs rather than only own-merchant volume.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/processing volume, take rate, local-method coverage, licence breadth. Key players — acquirers/incumbents (Adyen, Worldpay, Nexi, Getnet/Santander); cross-border B2B specialists (PingPong, Airwallex, Payoneer, Wise Platform, Nuvei). Basis of competition: local-acceptance depth + FX/pricing + integration simplicity + regulatory licence coverage. Recent peer moves: Vivid picked Wise Platform for international SME payments (2026-10, per corpus [[Vivid selects Wise Platform for international SME payments]]); PingPong partnered Visa on B2B card-to-account (2026-06, [[Visa partners with PingPong for B2B card-to-account payments]]) and acquired the remaining 49% of Xinhang Payment (2026-03, [[PingPong acquires remaining 49% of Xinhang Payment]]). Getnet's own scale: €238bn TPV / 10.5bn transactions / 1.2m clients in 2025, +14% YoY constant-FX, #1 LatAm acquirer and top-10 globally (per Santander, via latamfintech, as of 2025) — see [[Connecting the Dots Getnet processed €238B across 10.5B transactions]]. Position: Getnet is *ahead* in European/Iberian acceptance and local-method connectivity (the thing PingPong lacks locally); PingPong is *ahead* in cross-border collection footprint. Each is complementary, not competing — moat is Getnet's bank-backed acquiring licences + local rails (switching costs/scale), PingPong's licence network and merchant base (network effects) (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** Private/no-standalone-disclosure names; compute where both numbers are public.
- PingPong: ~$5bn estimated valuation, ~$226.6m total funding, last round PE Jan-2022 (per Tracxn/Caplight secondary data — treat as stale round/secondary mark, not market cap). Processes >$300bn/yr. Rough price-to-flow = $5bn / $300bn = ~0.017x flow (flow, not revenue — illustrative only; revenue undisclosed, so EV/Revenue = no data).
- Airwallex (closest listed-scale private peer): ~$11bn valuation (Series H, Jun-2026), ~$1.3bn annualised revenue (Mar-2026, +74% YoY), so P/S = $11bn / $1.3bn = ~8.5x; ~$287bn annualised TPV, so price-to-flow = $11bn / $287bn = ~0.038x (per Airwallex newsroom / Sacra / Axios, as of 2026).
- Payoneer (public): traded ~1.9x revenue mid-2025 (per Sacra, 2025) — a sobering public-market anchor for the subsector.
- Getnet: not separately listed; embedded in Santander, so standalone multiple = no data.
Read: the private cross-border set (Airwallex ~8.5x P/S) carries a large growth premium vs the public anchor (Payoneer ~1.9x); PingPong's ~$5bn mark looks modest on flow vs Airwallex (~0.017x vs ~0.038x) but the 2022 vintage and undisclosed revenue make the gap uninterpretable — distribution not computed, qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. Rails dependence / disintermediation — PingPong relies on Getnet for Iberian acceptance and local methods; Getnet relies on PingPong for the foreign-merchant pipeline. If either builds or buys the other's layer (PingPong already holds 80+ licences and acquired Xinhang), the partner's economics get captured by the other stack layer.
2. Economics opacity — the announcement is silent on revenue split, FX margin, and who holds fraud/chargeback liability on cross-border acquiring; "announced" is not "live", so merchant-adoption pace is unverified.
3. Concentration/geography — value hinges on two local methods (Bizum, MB WAY) and two markets (Spain, Portugal); regulatory or scheme-pricing changes to those domestic rails directly hit the value proposition.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Signals the acquirer-as-distribution-platform model: incumbents monetising foreign PSPs' merchant demand rather than only own volume — a consolidation/partnership pattern vs build-your-own-local-acceptance (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if the tie-up produces disclosed Iberian volume/merchant counts within ~2-3 quarters, it validates the "acceptance-as-a-service" wedge; trigger to watch — a reciprocal deal (Getnet riding PingPong rails into Asia) or PingPong announcing its own Iberian acquiring, which would break the complementarity thesis.
