 «Атолл Капитал» стал новым акционером оператора гибких офисов Aspace 
Новым акционером оператора гибких офисов Aspace стал институциональный инвестор — фонд «Атолл Капитал», который получил до 25% долей в компании, об этом “ Коммерсанту ” сообщили стороны сделки. Директор по стратегии ФГ «Финам» Ярослав Кабаков считает, что 25% акций в компании могут стоить 330–515 млн руб. Собеседник издания, близкий к Aspace, отмечает, что сумма сделки составила 1,65 млрд руб.
Решение приобрести доли в организации связано с потенциалом масштабирования бизнеса Aspace, поясняют в «Атолл Капитал».
Основатель и гендиректор Aspace Иван Гуськов считает, что сделка поможет улучшить корпоративное управление и повысить инвестиционную привлекательность компании.
До этого 72,25% долей в компании контролировало АО «Мера недвижимости», 15% долей принадлежали Сергею Седову, остальные 12,75% — Ивану Гуськову. Вхождение нового инвестора в капитал приведет к пропорциональному сокращению долей текущих акционеров, считает начальник аналитического отдела инвесткомпании «Риком-Траст» Олег Абелев.
За счет сделки Aspace получит необходимые для расширения сети финансы, сможет разделить риски и не полагаться только на заемные средства, отмечает директор направления рынков капитала IBC Real Estate Николай Горюнов. Новые площадки требуют значительных вложений в аренду, отделку и запуск, а долговая нагрузка при высоком темпе роста становится серьезным ограничителем, поясняет гендиректор Umbrella Consulting Group Кирилл Агапов.
Сейчас под управлением Aspace находится более 49 тыс. кв. м сервисных офисов. По объему управляемых коворкингов компания находится на втором месте по объему портфеля, уступая Space1, по данным CMWP. Ранее сообщалось, что Aspace рассматривает возможность приобретения сети гибких офисов Multispace. Речь идет о четырех площадках в Москве, их общая площадь составляет 18,2 тыс. кв. м. Сделка оценивалась в 800–950 млн руб.
Выручка АО «А Спэйс», управляющего сетью коворкингов Aspace, за 2025 год увеличилась почти вдвое, до 1,5 млрд руб., чистая выручка — на 52%, до 1,1 млрд руб., следует из данных СПАРК.
«Атолл Капитал» — российская частная консалтинговая и инвестиционная компания, владелец — Сергей Фрейдин. Ее выручка по итогам 2025 года составила 323 млн руб., увеличившись на 23%, чистая прибыль достигла 19 млн руб., по данным СПАРК.
Интерес инвесторов к гибким офисам объясняется довольно активным развитием этого рынка. Только за три квартала 2026 года в Москве открылось коворкингов на 29 тыс. кв. м, что на 12% больше год к году, по данным Николая Горюнова. К концу текущего года объем рынка гибких офисов в Москве может достигнуть 454,5 тыс. кв. м, увеличившись за год на 8%, подсчитали в Remain. Интерес крупных инвесторов к сегменту может способствовать дальнейшей консолидации рынка, считают в CORE.XP.
Однако сейчас ситуация на этом рынке складывается не очень благоприятно для собственников из-за общего замедления деловой активности. Объем сделок с сервисными офисами в Москве по итогам 2026 года сократится на 25,4% год к году, до 7 тыс. рабочих мест, подсчитали Remain.

# Context-enrichment: Atoll Capital becomes new shareholder in flex-office operator Aspace
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Institutional fund **Atoll Capital** (LLC, 100% owned by Sergey Freydin; GD on file Liliya Kazantseva; registered Feb 2023) bought **up to 25%** of Aspace (legal entity AO "A Space"), the operator of the Aspace serviced-office / coworking network. The parties confirmed the deal to Kommersant.
- **Price is contradictory and unverified.** Finam's Yaroslav Kabakov *estimates* 25% could be worth **330–515M RUB**; a source close to Aspace says the **transaction totalled 1.65bn RUB**. That 3–5x gap is the whole story: at 1.65bn for 25%, Aspace is valued at ~6.6bn RUB, vs ~1.3–2.1bn implied by the Finam estimate. One of these is wrong, or the 1.65bn includes more than the equity stake (e.g. a primary capital injection, not just share purchase). **(analysis)** The PR frame ("scaling potential", "better corporate governance") sidesteps this.
- **Mechanics:** pre-deal cap table was AO "Mera Nedvizhimosti" 72.25%, Sergey Sedov 15%, founder/CEO Ivan Guskov 12.75%. New investor entry dilutes all three proportionally (per Rikom-Trust's Abelev). Atoll gets a **board seat** and input into long-term strategy.
- **Why structured this way:** per IBC Real Estate's Goryunov, the deal lets Aspace fund network expansion without leaning only on debt — new sites require heavy upfront spend (rent, fit-out, launch) and high debt load is a hard ceiling at a fast growth rate (Umbrella Consulting's Agapov). So this is a **growth-equity recap to de-lever**, not a strategic/trade sale.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Space1** — #1 Moscow flex-office operator by portfolio; Aspace is **#2** by managed volume (per CMWP), managing **>49k sqm** of serviced offices.
- Other flex operators: Multispace (Aspace's own acquisition target — see [[Aspace eyes acquisition of Multispace flexible-office chain]]), Apollax Space, and the broader Moscow coworking field.
- **Position:** Aspace is a clear #2 trying to consolidate (Multispace bid) while raising growth equity (Atoll). **+ Why:** the Moscow flex market is cooling (see [4]), which favours well-capitalised consolidators who can buy distressed/sub-scale networks cheaply — exactly the two-pronged move (equity in + M&A out) Aspace is running. The scarce resource is balance-sheet capacity, not demand; whoever can fund fit-out without over-levering wins share in a down cycle. **(analysis)**

## [2] Company history / fit
- Aspace revenue 2025 **~1.5bn RUB (nearly 2x YoY)**; net revenue +52% to 1.1bn RUB (SPARK). Strong top-line growth but growth funded increasingly by debt.
- Atoll Capital 2025 revenue only **323M RUB (+23%)**, net profit 19M RUB (SPARK) — a small consulting/investment shop, not a large PE fund. **+ Why it matters:** a 1.65bn-RUB cheque is many multiples of Atoll's own revenue, which again makes the 1.65bn figure suspect as a pure equity outlay from Atoll alone; the lower 330–515M estimate is more consistent with Atoll's size. **(analysis / hypothesis)**
- Fit: logical — Aspace is scaling aggressively (Multispace bid, new 850-seat Novoslobodskaya site) and needs equity to avoid a debt wall. Bringing in an institutional minority + board seat ahead of further M&A is a standard pre-consolidation governance step; Guskov explicitly frames it as improving corporate governance / investment attractiveness (i.e. prepping for future capital events).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **What's new:** a *new investor entering Aspace's cap table* — distinct from the 2026-10-12 note where Aspace is the *acquirer* of Multispace. Real, dated, confirmed by both parties; not a reprint.
- **Value-add (real?):** the capital de-risks expansion and the board seat adds governance — genuine, but modest. This is fintech/proptech only loosely (a real-estate-operator equity recap). No disclosed product, tech, or financial-infrastructure innovation.
- **Traction:** the operating traction is Aspace's (1.5bn revenue, #2 position, 49k sqm), not anything the deal itself created. **+ Who captures the margin:** in a serviced-office model the operator captures the spread between long lease-in cost and short-term seat rental; in a cooling market with +31% rents and falling deal volume that spread compresses, so the equity is partly defensive. The multiple rests on continued network growth; it breaks if occupancy/pricing deteriorate. **(analysis)**

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Plans:** use proceeds for network expansion; the Multispace acquisition (4 Moscow sites, 18.2k sqm, valued 800–950M RUB) is the near-term use of firepower.
- **Market backdrop — mixed/cooling:** Moscow coworking ~454.5k sqm by end-2026 (+8% YoY, Remain); Q1–Q3 2026 saw +29k sqm opened in Moscow (+12% YoY, Goryunov). BUT serviced-office **deal volume to fall ~25.4% YoY in 2026** to ~7k workplaces (Remain); other sources report even sharper drops (deal count/volume down 2.6x; new supply down ~44%). Rents up ~31%.
- **+ Why / second-order:** investor interest + a cooling market = **consolidation wave** (CORE.XP view). Counter-intuitively, large-investor entry signals opportunism in a *weak* market, not a hot one — capital is flowing to #2 so it can roll up distressed sub-scale operators at low prices. Risk: if business activity keeps slowing, the levered-expansion thesis that justifies the equity stake weakens, and the 1.65bn/6.6bn valuation (if real) looks rich.

## FRESHNESS / DUPLICATE VERDICT
**FRESH.** This is a DISTINCT event from [[Aspace eyes acquisition of Multispace flexible-office chain]] (2026-10-12): that note = Aspace as *acquirer* of Multispace; this note (2026-10-19) = Atoll Capital taking a *new equity stake in Aspace*. Different parties, different transaction direction, different figures. Not a reprint. Related, cross-linked, but not a duplicate.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russian flex-office is a Moscow-centric, fast-growing but now-cooling niche. Operating flex-office stock in Moscow reached ~426k sqm by H1 2026 (154 sites, 62.2k workplaces; per CMWP via Kommersant) and may hit 454.5k sqm by end-2026, +8% y/y (per Remain, via mergers.ru). YTD through 3Q 2026 Moscow added ~29k sqm of coworking, +12% y/y (per Goryunov/IBC Real Estate, via mergers.ru). *Structure:* fragmented, capital-intensive — each new site needs rent, fit-out and launch capex, so operators scaling fast lean on debt; the market is consolidating around a top tier (Space1 #1, Aspace #2 by managed portfolio per CMWP). *Why now / headwind:* the cycle is turning against operators — vacancy rose to ~16% in May 2026 (+11.1pp y/y, CMWP+Space1; IBC puts it at 20.6%, NF Group 14.6%), demand fell >50% y/y to ~20.3k sqm, and 2026 service-office deal volume is seen down 25.4% y/y to ~7k workplaces (per Remain). Offsetting: avg rate +42% y/y to ~63.3k RUB/workstation/month (May 2026, via Kommersant). So an equity injection into a fast-grower during a demand air-pocket is the core story — capital to keep scaling without over-levering (analysis).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: managed GLA (sqm), workplaces, occupancy/vacancy %, rate per workstation/month. Key players: Space1 (#1), Aspace (#2), plus Workki, SOK, Business Club, Multispace and others — competition is on location, fit-out quality and net rate, not technology. Recent moves: Space1 resumed leasing ex-WeWork coworkings (2026, rb.ru); Aspace is in parallel studying the acquisition of Multispace's four Moscow sites (18.2k sqm) valued at 800–950mn RUB (per Burmistrov/Infoline — see [[Aspace eyes acquisition of Multispace flexible-office chain]]). Aspace position: clear #2, >49k sqm under management, 2025 revenue ~1.5bn RUB (nearly 2x y/y), net revenue +52% to 1.1bn RUB (SPARK via mergers.ru) — i.e. real top-line momentum into a softening market. Moat: location/scale and brand in a service-heavy product; switching costs for corporate tenants are modest, so the moat is thin `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** *The deal itself:* up to 25% to Atoll Capital. Two conflicting price signals — a source close to Aspace cites a deal sum of 1.65bn RUB, while Finam's Kabakov values 25% at 330–515mn RUB (both via mergers.ru). If 1.65bn RUB is the 25% consideration, that implies a ~6.6bn RUB post-money equity value (`1.65bn / 0.25 = 6.6bn`) and ~4.4x 2025 revenue (`6.6bn / 1.5bn`); on Kabakov's 330–515mn RUB range for 25% the implied equity is ~1.3–2.1bn RUB (`0.33–0.515bn / 0.25`) and ~0.9–1.4x revenue — a ~3–5x gap between the two reads, so a single clean multiple is **no data / contested** (the 1.65bn figure may be a total capital commitment, not pure equity price `(hypothesis)`). *Internal comp:* [[Aspace eyes acquisition of Multispace flexible-office chain]] — Multispace's 18.2k sqm valued 800–950mn RUB implies ~44–52k RUB/sqm; at >49k sqm Aspace's implied asset base on that yardstick would be far below a 6.6bn RUB equity tag, i.e. the 1.65bn-based read looks rich on an asset basis, in-line to cheap on the Kabakov basis. *External trading comps:* no public RU-listed pure flex-office peer with verifiable EV/Revenue — IWG/Regus are foreign and not comparable in scale/geo; multiples `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Risk flags.** (1) *Demand air-pocket* — vacancy 16–20%+ and demand down >50% y/y mean new sites funded by this capital could open into weak absorption, pressuring occupancy and the rate gains; second-order, if scaling outpaces demand the equity cushion erodes fast. (2) *Valuation ambiguity / disclosure gap* — the 3–5x spread between the 1.65bn source figure and Finam's 330–515mn estimate means the price (and whether it is equity vs commitment) is unverified; a minority 25% stake also carries no control, so governance "improvement" is a claim, not a guarantee. (3) *Leverage/capex dependence* — the sector's growth engine is debt-funded fit-out; if the raise substitutes for rather than complements debt, Aspace still faces high fixed rent/fit-out costs against softening demand (margin captured by landlords, not the operator).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Reads as consolidation capital: a #2 operator taking institutional money mid-downturn to buy share (Multispace) while weaker players stall `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: if Aspace closes Multispace and keeps occupancy near sector-leading levels through 2027, the raise re-rates it as the consolidator; the thesis breaks if vacancy stays ≥16% and the Multispace deal lapses or Aspace's occupancy tracks the 20%+ tail — then this is defensive, not offensive. Trigger to watch: confirmation of the Multispace acquisition and Aspace's 2026 occupancy/revenue print.
