## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 21 October 2026, ASX-listed Australian non-bank lender MONEYME Limited (ASX: MME) executed the MME Autopay ABS 2026-1 Trust, a A$413.5 million term securitisation of auto-loan receivables originated through its Autopay car-loan product. Settlement was scheduled for 28 October 2026. The Class A and Commission notes achieved expected AAA (sf) ratings from both Fitch and S&P Global. This is MONEYME's first public capital-markets transaction of FY27 and brings its rolling 12-month term issuance to ~A$1.234 billion. (Primary: [australianfintech.com.au](https://australianfintech.com.au/asx-listed-moneyme-executes-413-5-million-autopay-abs-transaction/))

**[1] Who MONEYME is.** MONEYME is an ASX-listed Australian consumer non-bank lender (secured car loans via Autopay, personal loans, and a credit-card product). It funds itself not with deposits but through warehouse facilities and public ABS term securitisations — the standard non-bank model where loans are originated in bank-funded warehouses and then termed out into capital markets to recycle capital. The group's total loan book crossed A$1.75bn in H2 CY25 ([[MoneyMe grows loan book 26% to $1.75bn in H2 CY25]], [[MoneyMe grows loan book to $1.75B, cuts credit losses]]) and reportedly reached ~A$2.24bn by FY26, with a strategic tilt toward secured (auto) lending (secured was 61% of the book at Dec-2025).

**[2] What an auto-loan ABS is / deal mechanics.** In an ABS, MONEYME pools Autopay car loans into a bankruptcy-remote trust that issues rated notes to institutional investors, secured by the loan cashflows. The senior Class A notes carry the AAA rating (lowest cost of funds); junior/subordinated tranches absorb first losses and are typically retained or sold at wider spreads. On the prior A$455.4M deal (Nov 2025), Class A1 was ~78% of the pool priced at ~110bps over 1-month BBSW, with rates spanning ~5.41% (Class A) to ~6.21% (Class D) — a useful reference point for this deal's likely structure. Dual AAA ratings from two agencies (Fitch + S&P) broaden the investor base and support tighter pricing. (Refs: [tradingview](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025:newsml_FWN3WW1K1:0-moneyme-executes-a-455-4m-autopay-abs-deal/), [kalkine](https://kalkine.com.au/news/announcements/moneyme-sets-interest-rates-for-4554m-auto-loan-asset-backed-securities-following-november-2025-settlement))

**[3] How it fits the funding program.** This is the latest in a cadence of Autopay term deals: debut MME Autopay ABS 2024-1 (A$517.5m), 2025-1 (A$455.4m, settled Nov-2025), and now 2026-1 (A$413.5m). MONEYME reported ~A$2.9bn total funding capacity at Dec-2025 (including a new A$300m credit-card warehouse) and ~A$3.0bn at Jun-2026, with the Autopay warehouse previously doubled to A$750m. Each term ABS frees warehouse capacity to re-originate, extends funding duration, and — if priced tighter than the warehouse — lowers blended cost of funds. The Autopay portfolio reportedly reached ~A$1.25bn at 30 Sep 2026 (up ~28% YoY), so this deal directly refinances that growth. ([MONEYME 2Q26 trading update PDF](https://investors.moneyme.com.au/DownloadFile.axd?file=%2FReport%2FComNews%2F20260129%2F03050111.pdf), [FY26 results](https://www.marketindex.com.au/data-api/api/v1/announcements/XASX:MME:2A1690335/pdf/inline/fy26-results-announcement))

**[4] Why it matters.** For MONEYME, repeat AAA-rated issuance validates the Autopay collateral performance (credit losses trending down to ~2.9%) and keeps the funding engine turning for FY27 growth targets. For the market, it signals continued healthy investor appetite for Australian prime/near-prime auto ABS and demonstrates a sub-scale non-bank successfully accessing term markets at AAA on the senior tranche. The slightly smaller size vs. the prior two deals is worth watching but is consistent with a programmatic issuer sizing deals to its origination pipeline, not a sign of demand weakness. Related: [[MoneyMe selects SEON for fraud prevention and credit decisioning]] (underwriting/credit-quality angle underpinning the collateral).

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is the A$413.5M the total deal size (all tranches) or just the senior/Class A? The headline "$413M" and the primary source say total; confirm vs. the ASX announcement.
2. The story says "bringing 12-month issuance to $1.2bn" — does that reconcile? 413.5 + 455.4 = 868.9; add the ~A$360m+ of other FY26 warehouse/ABS activity to reach ~A$1.234bn. Which specific deals are counted in that rolling figure?
3. Exact pricing/spread over BBSW for Class A1 on THIS deal is not yet disclosed in the aggregated source — is it tighter or wider than the Nov-2025 ~110bps? Cost-of-funds direction is the real signal, not the headline size.
4. Why is 2026-1 (A$413.5m) SMALLER than 2024-1 (A$517.5m) and 2025-1 (A$455.4m)? Is this prudent sizing to pipeline, or softening collateral generation / investor demand?
5. Which tranches were actually SOLD vs. RETAINED (subordinated/equity)? Retained junior notes mean MONEYME still holds first-loss and hasn't fully de-risked.
6. "Expected" AAA ratings — are these final, or indicative pre-settlement? Expected ratings can be withdrawn before close.
7. Both Fitch and S&P rated it (vs. Moody's+Fitch on 2024-1). Agency switch can reflect ratings-shopping or methodology fit — does it change comparability of the AAA?
8. What is the arrears/loss performance of the Autopay collateral pool backing THIS trust? Headline group net losses (~2.9%) may not equal the specific pool's delinquency.
9. Is Autopay prime or near-prime? AAA on senior notes with a near-prime pool implies heavy subordination/credit enhancement — what is the actual enhancement level and weighted-average rate on the loans?
10. Macro risk: Australian used/new-car values and rate path — a term ABS locks funding but the underlying loans still carry residual/credit risk if the car market or consumer deteriorates.
11. Does this deal materially lower MONEYME's blended cost of funds, or just extend duration? Without the margin, "strengthened funding" is a volume claim, not a profitability claim.
12. Is there any warehouse step-down or facility expiry this deal was timed to refinance? A forced term-out is different from opportunistic issuance.
13. Source concentration: the aggregated text comes from one secondary outlet (australianfintech.com.au); has the underlying ASX announcement/MME investor release been independently confirmed?
14. Does the ~A$1.25bn Autopay portfolio figure (30 Sep 2026) reconcile with group-level FY26 disclosures, or is it a cherry-picked segment number?

**Importance: 3/5** — Material, confirmatory funding execution by a mid-cap ASX non-bank lender: repeat AAA-rated auto ABS validates the Autopay growth strategy and keeps the funding runway open for FY27. Not market-moving beyond MME and not novel (fourth deal in a programmatic series), and smaller than prior tranches, so it reads as routine capital-markets plumbing rather than a strategic inflection.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Australian non-bank lenders fund loan books primarily off-balance-sheet via warehouse facilities + term ABS (asset-backed securities); recurring, well-rated public ABS is the primary lever for lowering cost-of-funds and freeing warehouse capacity to grow. Structure is fragmented at the non-bank/fintech tier (MONEYME, Plenti, Wisr, Harmoney) sitting under scaled auto incumbents — per Ken Research the Australian car-finance market lists Toyota Finance, Angle Auto Finance, Allied Credit, Pepper Money and Plenti as the major players (via kenresearch.com, 2025). Entry barrier is capital-markets access itself: you need rating-agency acceptance, a seasoned receivables pool and warehouse lines before you can term-out. "Why now": with rates past their peak, auto ABS demand is strong — Plenti reported its 2026-1 deal priced at "the tightest pricing since 2021" with demand "exceeding expectations" (via kalkine.com.au, May 2026) — so issuers are terming out aggressively to lock cheaper funding.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for a non-bank auto lender: loan-book size + growth, net interest margin (NIM), net credit-loss rate / arrears, secured-mix, and cost-of-funds (the ABS margin). Basis of competition = cost-of-funds + credit quality (cheaper, better-rated funding → finer customer pricing → better originations). Recent peer moves: Plenti priced a record **$660.5M** Plenti Auto ABS 2026-1 (its 7th auto ABS, ~$5.3bn total program), settling ~30 Jul 2026, after a $559M auto ABS in Nov 2025 (via kalkine.com.au / asreport.americanbanker.com). MONEYME's position: a smaller, catching-up issuer. This $413.5M MME Autopay ABS 2026-1 (priced 21 Oct 2026, settling 28 Oct 2026) is its **first public deal of FY27**, taking trailing-12-month issuance to **$1.234bn**; Class A notes earned AAA from both Fitch and S&P (via australianfintech.com.au). Autopay portfolio reached **$1.25bn at 30 Sep 2026, +28% YoY** (via australianfintech.com.au). Moat: AAA ratings + a repeat issuance cadence lower marginal funding cost `(analysis)`; but MONEYME remains sub-scale vs Plenti's larger, more established auto program.

**Comps & multiples.** Comparable: ASX-listed Australian non-bank auto/consumer lenders funding via ABS.
- Internal comps: [[MoneyMe grows loan book 26% to $1.75bn in H2 CY25]] (prior $455.4M Autopay ABS; loan book $1.75bn, NIM 6.8%, net credit losses 2.9%, 61% secured, funding capacity $2.9bn); [[MoneyMe grows loan book to $1.75B, cuts credit losses]].
- External: **Plenti** — $660.5M (2026-1) and $559M (Nov-2025) auto ABS, ~$5.3bn cumulative program — i.e. Plenti's single recent deal (~$660M) exceeds MONEYME's entire TTM issuance ($1.234bn) relative to program maturity.
- Equity multiples: MONEYME (ASX: MME) market cap ≈ **A$50m** (stockanalysis.com, 19 Oct 2026; ~A$0.061/sh, 25 Oct 2026), down ~42–46% YoY. Against gross revenue of ~A$208m FY25 (via FY25 results), that implies a P/Revenue well below 1x — but revenue here is gross interest income on a leveraged book, so a sales multiple is not meaningful for a balance-sheet lender; EV/EBITDA / P/E "no data" (FY25 was a statutory net loss of A$66.6m driven by non-cash items, per FY25 reporting). Qualitative read: the equity is priced for distress/turnaround (sub-A$50m cap on a >A$1.75bn book), **in stark contrast** to the debt side executing AAA-rated ABS — the market trusts the collateral far more than the equity.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Funding-market dependence / rollover risk** — the whole model rests on continuous access to warehouses + term ABS; if auto-ABS spreads widen or demand softens, the cheaper-funding thesis reverses and growth stalls. Second-order: MONEYME is a smaller, later issuer than Plenti, so in a risk-off window it would likely pay up or get crowded out first.
2. **Credit-cycle / arrears risk** — net credit losses improved (4.5% FY24 → 3.4% FY25 → 2.9% 1H26) on a rising secured mix, but that trend is unproven through an Australian unemployment/auto-repayment downturn; AAA senior notes are protected by subordination, so losses land on the equity/residual (MONEYME) first.
3. **Equity fragility** — a ~A$50m market cap (−42% YoY) on a statutory FY25 net loss limits MONEYME's ability to raise equity to support warehouse first-loss/subordination as it scales, making it structurally more fragile than better-capitalised peers `(analysis)`.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is scaling/normalisation, not a re-rating: repeat AAA ABS at finer spreads is exactly the flywheel a non-bank needs, and the debt market is validating the Autopay collateral even as the equity trades near distress. Falsifiable thesis: if MONEYME sustains quarterly/half-yearly ABS issuance at tightening margins AND net credit losses stay ≤3% through FY27, cost-of-funds compression should drive operating-cash profit up and eventually re-rate the equity. Trigger to watch: FY26 full-year results (NIM, net credit losses, operating cash profit) and the pricing margin on the next Autopay ABS vs this one. What breaks it: arrears tick back up or the next deal prices wider.
