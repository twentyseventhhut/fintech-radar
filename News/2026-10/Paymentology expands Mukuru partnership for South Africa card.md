_Longstanding partnership evolves as Mukuru launches new Account & Card, bringing payments, salary deposits and cross-border money movement together for South African customers_

**Johannesburg, South Africa – 26 October**– Paymentology, the leading global issuer-processor, is expanding its partnership with Mukuru as the African fintech takes its next step beyond remittances with the launch of the new Mukuru Account & Card in South Africa.

The launch marks an important evolution for Mukuru, bringing everyday financial services together with the cross-border money movement capabilities that have been at the heart of its business for more than two decades.

Supported by Paymentology’s issuing and processing platform, the new Mastercard-enabled proposition is offered in partnership with Bank Zero as the licensed sponsor bank. Through the Mukuru Account & Card, customers can receive salaries and other EFT payments, make payments to South African bank accounts, shop online and in-store, withdraw cash and continue to send money across borders, bringing more of their everyday financial needs into a single proposition.

The launch builds on a longstanding relationship between Paymentology and Mukuru. Paymentology has previously supported Mukuru’s prepaid card programme in South Africa, helping the fintech extend digital financial services to customers who have traditionally relied heavily on cash.

The latest development significantly expands what customers can do. Around 500,000 Mukuru customers already hold a prepaid card, creating an established customer base from which the new Account & Card can grow. Existing customers can now move onto the new proposition, which provides broader functionality and increased monthly spending limits, EFT’s and immediate payments to South African Banks.

For Paymentology, the launch also demonstrates the opportunity created through its partnership with Bank Zero. Paymentology became Bank Zero’s first alliance partner earlier this year, combining Bank Zero’s digital banking capabilities and local market expertise with Paymentology’s card issuing and processing technology. Together, the model enables businesses such as Mukuru to bring tailored financial propositions to market without having to build the underlying banking and payments capabilities themselves.

**Kesheni Moodley, Regional Director for Africa at Paymentology, said:**“Working with Mukuru over the years has given us a front-row seat to its evolution and the impact it has had on how people across Africa access and move their money. This latest launch is particularly exciting because it brings together two important parts of people’s financial lives: how they manage money day to day and how they support family and communities across borders.

“Our role is to give partners the technology and flexibility to turn ideas into products that work at scale. By bringing together Mukuru’s deep understanding of its customers, Bank Zero’s banking capabilities and Paymentology’s processing expertise, we are helping to connect everyday payments with the cross-border services Mukuru’s customers already rely on.”

**Andy Jury, CEO of Mukuru added:**“The Mukuru Account & Card represents an important next step in our journey. Our customers have trusted us for more than 20 years to help them move money across borders, and we now have the opportunity to support much more of their everyday financial lives.

“Paymentology has been a longstanding partner to Mukuru and understands both our business and the customers we serve. As we expand our proposition, having partners that can support the scale, flexibility and experience we want to deliver is incredibly important.”

Mukuru has built an extensive physical and digital network across Africa, with more than 320,000 locations offering its services or cash-in and cash-out capabilities. The combination of physical access and digital services allows customers to move between cash and digital finance according to their individual needs.

The partnership reflects a broader shift underway across African financial services, as fintechs increasingly expand beyond individual products such as remittances or prepaid cards to build more connected financial experiences around their customers.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team Q&A**

1. *Is this a brand-new deal or an expansion?* Expansion — Paymentology already powered Mukuru's SA prepaid card programme; this adds a full Account & Card. Not a new logo win.

2. *Is Mukuru actually becoming a bank?* No. Bank Zero is the licensed sponsor bank; Mukuru is the brand/distribution front end. SARB licence/liability sits with Bank Zero.

3. *Is the 500,000 figure the whole customer base?* No — it's SA prepaid cardholders eligible to migrate. Mukuru reports 17M+ customers group-wide. Easy to conflate; the 500K is the near-term conversion pool, not TAM.

4. *Does Paymentology earn more per user now?* Plausibly yes (account + EFT + card vs prepaid top-ups), but no economics disclosed. Open.

5. *Is migration guaranteed?* No. "Can move onto" ≠ will. No migration timeline or conversion target disclosed. Open.

6. *Is Bank Zero a material/stable sponsor?* Bank Zero is a small SA digital bank (founded by ex-FNB execs); Paymentology became its *first* alliance partner in Apr 2026. Concentration risk — this product depends on one small sponsor's licence and uptime.

7. *New vs the April Bank Zero note — is this duplicate?* No. April = the Paymentology↔Bank Zero alliance itself. This = first named fintech (Mukuru) product shipped on it. Distinct event. Fresh.

8. *Is Mastercard a new relationship?* No — Paymentology has a longstanding Mastercard tie in SA (see PayoCard 2025-08). Expected, not news.

9. *Any numbers unverified by the press release?* R150k limit, R85 fee, 60+ countries, 17M customers come from Mukuru's own site / secondary press, not the Paymentology PR. Directionally consistent; treat fee/limit specifics as marketing figures.

10. *Competitive threat?* Mukuru now overlaps SA incumbents + Capitec/TymeBank on everyday accounts — a crowded, thin-margin segment. Differentiator is the remittance network + cash-in/out footprint (320K locations), not the account itself.

11. *Does this move the needle for Paymentology financially?* Hard to say at this scale (500K potential users) vs its $175M raise and global ambitions. Strategically a reference case more than a revenue event. Open.

12. *Is cross-border still core?* Yes — the card preserves "send abroad"; the pivot is additive, not a replacement. Reduces a key risk (losing the remittance moat).

13. *PR vs substance?* It's a vendor (Paymentology) press release amplifying a partner launch. Real product exists (live on Mukuru's site), but framing is promotional. Credible, low-to-mid materiality.

14. *Regional read-through?* Reinforces the African "remittance fintech → connected neobank on third-party rails" pattern (Chikwama, M-Pesa). Thesis signal more than a singular catalyst.

Importance: 3/5 — Real, live product and a clean proof-point for the Paymentology+Bank Zero BaaS model converting a 500K prepaid base into transactional banking; but it's a partner-PR expansion of an existing relationship in a small-sponsor, crowded SA account market with no disclosed economics or migration targets.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector.** This is a card-issuing / "remittance-to-everyday-banking" play in South Africa, where three distinct layers stack: a remittance fintech owning the customer (Mukuru), a licensed sponsor bank renting out its banking permissions (Bank Zero), and a cloud-native issuer-processor supplying the card rails (Paymentology). The strategic thesis — remittance players bolting on accounts, salary deposits and everyday spend to monetize an existing base rather than just per-transaction send fees — is now the dominant Africa fintech pattern, not a differentiator. Mukuru's own CEO frames the Account & Card as the "next step beyond remittances," which is the same arc Mukuru's regulated peers are on (deposit-taking microfinance licence in Zimbabwe, e-money licences in Botswana/Zambia/Malawi per Wikipedia/press).

**Scale, grounded.** Mukuru is large: ~17M+ customers and ~320,000 cash-in/cash-out locations across 60+ countries, processing >$2bn/yr; it handled ~52% of Zimbabwe's ~$2bn diaspora remittances in 2024 (per techcabal/Wikipedia, Oct 2026). But the number that matters for this deal is far smaller: the ~500,000 *existing South African prepaid cardholders* who can migrate onto the new proposition. The headline "expands partnership" is a base-migration + feature-upgrade, not a net-new 17M TAM — so model this as an upsell of an already-carded cohort, not greenfield acquisition.

**Paymentology position.** Paymentology is a credible, cloud-native issuer-processor operating across ~70 countries; it raised $175M (co-led by Apis Partners and Aspirity Partners, announced May 2026) to push beyond core issuer-processing into credit, stablecoin, tokenisation and AI, and claimed 117% YoY sales growth and 65% transaction-volume growth for FY2025 (fintech.global / fintechfutures, May 2026). Note the self-reported, un-audited nature of those figures and that no valuation/revenue was disclosed. In-base, Paymentology recurs heavily in this exact Africa card niche — PayoCard South Africa launch (Aug 2025), Chikwama Pay WhatsApp neobank (Mar 2026), the Mastercard expansion — confirming Africa card-issuing is a core franchise, not a one-off.

**Bank Zero angle.** The genuinely new structural element is the Bank Zero sponsor-bank model: Paymentology became Bank Zero's "first alliance partner" earlier in 2026, and Mukuru is an early tenant of that BaaS arrangement. This is the reusable asset — Bank Zero's licence + Paymentology's rails let other fintechs skip building banking infra. Watch whether this becomes a repeatable channel (more tenants) versus a bespoke Mukuru deal.

**In-base comps.** Closest adjacencies in the vault: Western Union + Sasai (SA cross-border app, Mar 2026), Onafriq + Visa (DR Congo, Sep 2025), botim + TerraPay (Africa remittance rails, Feb 2026), Yuno + Flutterwave (African payments access, Apr 2026). The competitive read: Mukuru's edge is its 320k-location physical cash network bridging cash↔digital, which pure-digital rivals and remittance apps (WU/Sasai) can't easily replicate. Issuer-processor competition (Marqeta, Thredd, Galileo, i2c, NymCard) is intense globally but Paymentology's Africa footprint and existing Mukuru/Bank Zero relationships give it incumbency on this specific program.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Promotional framing / scope inflation** — "leading global issuer-processor" and 117%/65% growth stats are company-sourced and un-audited; the deal itself is a base-migration + feature-upgrade of ~500k already-carded users, materially smaller than Mukuru's 17M headline suggests.
2. **Execution/migration risk** — converting prepaid cardholders to a full account (salary deposits, EFT, higher limits) requires KYC uplift, Bank Zero onboarding and behavior change; migration numbers, not sign-ups, are the real KPI and aren't disclosed.
3. **Dependency / concentration** — value hinges on an untested three-party stack (Mukuru customer-owner, Bank Zero licence, Paymentology rails); Bank Zero is a small SA digital bank and is Paymentology's first alliance partner, so sponsor-bank capacity, resilience and regulatory standing are single points of failure.
