 Funding will support Saber’s expansion across Asia-Pacific and the Gulf region, including new local licenses and B2B payment products. San Francisco, USA, October 2026: Saber Money, a payment infrastructure company enabling financial institutions, fintechs and businesses to move money across key emerging-market corridors, today announced strategic funding from Tether , the world’s largest stablecoin issuer and Hong Kong based CMCC Global’s Titan Fund . With the new capital, Saber plans to deepen its presence in existing corridors while expanding into additional emerging markets in Asia-Pacific and the Gulf region. A key focus will be securing local licenses and banking relationships that will enable Saber to build deeper payment capabilities and improve settlement economics within each market. "Emerging markets are where the next chapter of global payments is being written, and stablecoins are the infrastructure that makes it possible. Saber has built real regulatory depth and market access across Asia and the Middle East, and we're proud to support a team that understands how to translate that into products businesses can actually use. This is exactly the kind of use case where stablecoin settlement changes the economics for everyone involved." — Paolo Ardoino, CEO of Tether . “Saber Money sits at the convergence of a vast market opportunity and a generational shift in financial infrastructure. Asia is home to some of the world’s largest cross-border payment corridors contributing to about 53% of global remittances. Yet moving money across the region remains costly, fragmented, and reliant on outdated rails. With cross-border B2B stablecoin payments projected to reach $5 trillion by 2035, we believe Saber Money is well positioned to become a category-defining global payments company and a trusted infrastructure layer through which money moves as seamlessly as information” — Shiau Sin Yen Co-founder and Managing Partner of Titan Fund. Saber currently holds licenses, registrations and authorizations in Canada, EU, India and Australia. The group also operates Mudrex, one of the largest crypto exchanges in India. Over the past nine months, Saber’s payment volumes have grown over 6x, processing billions of dollars across 40+ countries . More than 90% of payments are processed in under one minute , enabling faster and more efficient cross-border transactions. “Saber gives businesses stablecoin-powered payment rails into markets like the Asia-Pacific and the Gulf region, bringing near instant, 24/7 and cost-effective settlement infrastructure available in developed markets to the regions driving the next wave of global cross-border volume.” — Edul Patel, CEO, Saber Money. The company also plans to expand into B2B payments, where businesses continue to face significant friction when paying suppliers, managing treasury and moving working capital internationally. Asia-Pacific and the Gulf region sit at the centre of some of the world’s largest and fastest-evolving cross-border payment corridors. Asia already accounts for roughly 35% of global payment flows, while South Asia and East Asia & Pacific together receive more than $1+ trillion annually. The GCC is one of the world’s most important sources of outbound payments; the UAE alone sent approximately AED 188 billion in remittances in 2025. “As trade, migration and digital commerce expand, these corridors represent a significant opportunity for payment infrastructure that can provide faster settlement, deeper local connectivity and more efficient movement of liquidity across borders. Stablecoins fundamentally change what is possible in cross-border payments,” Patel added. The investment from Tether and Titan fund marks the next phase of Saber’s ambition to build a payment network connecting emerging-market financial systems with global stablecoin liquidity. About Saber Money Saber Money is a payment infrastructure company that enables financial institutions, fintechs and businesses to move money across key global and emerging-market corridors using stablecoin-powered payment infrastructure. The company is backed by tier 1 investors like Y-Combinator, Nexus Ventures, Tether and CMCC Global’s Titan Fund. Saber supports businesses in sectors such as remittances, payroll, and fintech with easy-to-use APIs, strong compliance systems, and round-the-clock support. Learn more at https://saber.money/

# Context-enrichment: Saber Money raises strategic funding from Tether and CMCC Global's Titan Fund
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Saber Money (SF-HQ'd, parent of India crypto exchange **Mudrex**; CEO Edul Patel) took a **strategic investment from Tether and CMCC Global's Titan Fund**, announced 2026-10-15. **Round size and valuation are undisclosed** — a notable silence that caps how much weight the deal can carry. There is no lead/round-stage label ("Series A/B"); it reads as a strategic/extension cheque, not a priced primary raise. Prior backers named: Y Combinator, Nexus Ventures (plus Tether/Titan now).
- **What's real vs framed:** Disclosed traction is specific and not nothing — payment volume up "6x over the past nine months", "billions of dollars across 40+ countries", ">90% of payments settle in under one minute". But "billions across 40+ countries" is an aggregate with no GMV/revenue/take-rate/net-revenue figure, so the economics stay opaque. Licences claimed: Canada, EU, India, Australia — real regulatory surface, but none yet in the headline **Asia-Pacific + Gulf** corridors this raise is meant to open (so the capital is to *buy* that reach, i.e. local licences + banking relationships, not to scale something already licensed there).
- **Why structured this way:** Tether is deploying surplus into a *portfolio* of regional last-mile USD₮ rails (see [1]) rather than one build; keeping the ticket undisclosed is consistent with that — these are distribution/reach buys, not flagship stakes. The PR anchors to macro TAM numbers (Asia ≈53% of global remittances, cross-border B2B stablecoin flows "projected to reach $5T by 2035", UAE outbound ≈AED 188B in 2025) precisely because deal-specific numbers (ticket, valuation, revenue) are withheld.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Two frames. (a) **Tether's own EM stablecoin-rails portfolio** — the most informative peer set, since this is one more node in it: [[Tether invests in LemFi to expand USDT settlement in Africa and Asia]] (Africa/Asia remittances), [[Argentina's Belo raises $14 million Series A led by Tether]] (Titan Fund *also* participated — same co-investor pairing, LatAm), [[Tether invests in EFY Finance for LATAM stablecoins]] (seed, 17 countries), [[Tether invests in Axiym for global payment infrastructure]], [[UAE's Utexo closes $7.5 million seed led by Tether]], [[Tether invests in Kenya's Kotani Pay]]. Saber is the **India/APAC+Gulf** entry in a deliberate regional map. (b) **Independent stablecoin cross-border B2B infra:** Conduit (~$53M raised, $10B+ annualized, deep Africa/LatAm/APAC payout), Bridge (acquired by Stripe; deepest API/licensing), BVNK, Thunes (140+ countries, Circle/USDC settlement), [[Noah and Nala launch instant stablecoin settlement network]], [[Thunes launches instant transfers to China via UnionPay]].
- **Position:** Catching up / parity at best on volume (Conduit already cites $10B+ annualized vs Saber's unquantified "billions"). Saber's differentiator is the **India + Gulf corridor plus the Mudrex exchange** giving it on/off-ramp liquidity — but that is corridor-specific, not a technology moat.
- **Why this lay of the land / second order:** The premium in this category is *not* the rails tech (commoditized — anyone can move USD₮) but **regulated local last-mile + liquidity in hard corridors**. That is exactly what the capital targets. The risk: Tether is simultaneously funding *multiple* overlapping EM rails (LemFi also does Asia), so portfolio companies may end up competing for the same USD₮ settlement volume Tether itself wants to grow — reach for Tether, not necessarily a durable moat for Saber.

## [2] Company history / fit
Saber is the payments arm of the group that runs **Mudrex** (one of India's larger crypto exchanges). Trajectory: YC + Nexus backing → built licensed footprint (CA/EU/IN/AU) → 6x volume growth in 9 months → now raising to push into APAC + Gulf. The fit is logical: an India-rooted exchange operator extending into cross-border B2B/remittance rails where it already has liquidity and compliance muscle.
- **Why the company acts this way:** A crypto exchange is a commodity take-rate business under fee/regulatory pressure; bolting on stablecoin payment *infrastructure* (APIs, settlement, corridors) is the classic move to a higher-multiple, stickier B2B revenue line. India's own crypto tax/regulatory drag gives extra incentive to build the dollar-denominated cross-border layer offshore and sell it regionally.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Not novel as a *concept* — USD₮-settled EM cross-border rails are a crowded 2025-26 theme (Conduit, Bridge, BVNK, LemFi, Noah/Nala). Saber's claimed edge is corridor depth (India + GCC) plus the Mudrex liquidity engine and sub-one-minute settlement at scale across 40+ countries.
- **Traction is partially real:** 6x/9-month growth and 40+ countries are concrete and above pure-announcement grade; but with no GMV, no revenue, no take-rate, and no disclosed APAC/Gulf licences yet, the "category-defining global payments company" claim (Titan Fund's Shiau Sin Yen) is aspiration, not demonstrated.
- **Who captures the margin (1 level deeper):** In this stack the margin sits with (i) the stablecoin issuer earning float on reserves (Tether) and (ii) whoever owns the *regulated local payout + FX spread*. Saber is trying to own (ii). But Tether — its new investor — structurally captures (i) regardless of who wins the rails layer, which is why Tether can afford to back several competing rails. Saber's durable value depends on locking exclusive banking/licence relationships in hard corridors before peers; absent that, it is a thin-margin reseller of USD₮ settlement.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Stated plan: deepen existing corridors, secure **local licences + banking relationships** in APAC and the Gulf, and expand B2B payment products (supplier payments, treasury, working capital). Backdrop is favorable for the theme — stablecoin regulatory clarity (US GENIUS Act era, HK/UAE licensing regimes) and heavy VC/strategic flows into EM stablecoin rails.
- **Why the market goes this way / second order:** TAM is genuinely large (Asia ≈53% of remittances; GCC a top outbound source), so capital will keep flowing. Counterintuitive risk: the *same* tailwind (Tether funding many rails + issuer float economics) means the rails layer commoditizes fast and consolidates — an undisclosed, un-priced strategic cheque into a sub-scale player is as likely to be an **acqui-reach option for Tether** as the start of an independent category winner. Watch for: disclosed APAC/Gulf licence wins, an actual priced round with a number, and real B2B product launches (vs "plans to expand").

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Saber sits in cross-border B2B / remittance payments on stablecoin (USDT) rails into emerging-market corridors (Asia-Pacific, Gulf/GCC). Market sizing: cross-border B2B stablecoin transactions projected to reach **$5tn by 2035** from **$13.4bn in 2026** (per Juniper Research, via CoinDesk/GlobeNewswire, 27 Apr 2026), with ~85% of 2035 stablecoin value expected to be B2B. Structure: highly fragmented — long correspondent-banking chains, wide FX spreads, slow/costly settlement are the inefficiency stablecoins attack; value accrues to whoever holds local licences + banking relationships per corridor (the hard, defensible layer), not to the rail/token itself. "Why now": (1) the $5tn/37,000% growth narrative + Tether's push to build distribution for USDT demand; (2) correspondent banking's structural fit is weakest exactly in the inflation/FX-volatile recipient corridors Saber targets (Asia ~53% of global remittances per company/Titan; GCC a top outbound source — UAE alone ~AED 188bn remittances 2025, per PR). Second-order: if licences are the moat, this is a regulatory-arbitrage land-grab, not a technology race.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: payment volume/TPV, number of live corridors, settlement speed, and settlement economics (FX spread + fee take vs correspondent rails). Saber's disclosed traction (de-PR'd, unaudited, company-reported): payment volumes "grown over 6x" over nine months, "billions of dollars" across 40+ countries, >90% of payments settle in under one minute; licences/registrations in Canada, EU, India, Australia; also operates Mudrex (Indian crypto exchange); backed by Y-Combinator, Nexus Ventures. Key players & basis of competition (distribution + licence depth, not price): [[Noah and Nala launch instant stablecoin settlement network]] (Africa/Asia settlement, $850bn EM-payments target), [[Onafriq partners Conduit to scale stablecoin cross-border payments]] / Conduit (LatAm+Africa B2B stablecoin rails), [[BVNK secures direct SEPA access for instant euro payments]] (B2B stablecoin infra, Mastercard-adjacent), [[Zerohash in talks to raise $250M after dropping Mastercard deal]] (embedded crypto/stablecoin APIs), plus Bridge (Stripe). Recent peer moves: Stripe closed its ~$1.1bn Bridge acquisition (Feb 2025); Mastercard acquired BVNK (~$1.8bn, per 2026 press); Zerohash pursuing >$1.5bn valuation (2026). Saber's position: **niche / catching-up** — differentiated by EM-corridor licence depth + Tether endorsement, but smaller and later than the US/EU-infra incumbents `(analysis)`. Moat = per-market licences + banking relationships (switching/regulatory barriers) rather than technology `(analysis)`.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Tether / USDT dependence.** Settlement economics and liquidity hinge on USDT; Tether is both investor and rail. Concentration on a single issuer with ongoing reserve-transparency and regulatory scrutiny (US GENIUS-style / MiCA treatment) means a USDT shock would hit Saber's core settlement layer directly — this is correlated, not diversifiable, risk.
2. **Undisclosed economics (de-PR flag).** No round size, valuation, revenue, take rate, or margin disclosed; "6x volume" and "billions across 40+ countries" are unaudited vanity/volume metrics that say nothing about unit economics or whether per-corridor FX/fee spread survives at scale. Who's silent on economics = the key tell.
3. **Regulatory / licence execution.** Thesis rests on winning local licences + banking relationships corridor-by-corridor (Asia-Pacific + GCC); each is slow, idiosyncratic, revocable, and exposed to de-risking by correspondent banks — execution risk compounds across markets and is the gating constraint on the whole expansion.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Signals Tether moving from pure issuer to vertically integrating distribution — backing corridor-level payment companies to seed organic USDT demand in EM rails rather than relying on speculation; expect more Tether-anchored strategic rounds and eventual consolidation (Stripe/Bridge, Mastercard/BVNK templates) as rails commoditize and licence-holders become acquisition targets. Falsifiable thesis: EM-corridor licence depth is the durable moat, so Saber's value compounds with each new authorization. Trigger to watch: next 12 months — actual new licence grants (not just "focus on") in Asia-Pacific/Gulf and a disclosed round size/valuation. Thesis breaks if volumes keep growing but no new licences land or if a rail incumbent (BVNK/Bridge/Zerohash) enters the same corridors with cheaper distribution.
