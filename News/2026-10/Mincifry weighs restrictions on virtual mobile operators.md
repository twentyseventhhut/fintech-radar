# Mincifry weighs restrictions on virtual mobile operators

> [!info] 2026-10-28 · 1 упоминаний · 1 источника(ов) с текстом
> Каналы: Финтехно

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Финтехно] Минцифры хочет ограничить виртуальных операторов связи, работающих на инфраструктуре крупных телеком-компаний, причём обсуждаются сразу несколько ограничений, пишет «Коммерсант». Среди них минимальный тариф в 250 рублей, повышение отчислений в резерв универсального обслуживания с 2% до 4%, запрет на обслуживание критической информационной инфраструктуры и использование сетей двух инфраструктурных операторов в одном регионе.

# Минцифры намерено ограничить работу виртуальных операторов связи

27.10.2026, 06:05

В ходе реформы связи виртуальным операторам (MVNO, работают на инфраструктуре и частотах опорных операторов) готовят ужесточение условий деятельности. В частности, им планируется ввести минимальный тариф на связь, запреты на работу с критической информационной инфраструктурой (КИИ) и использование сети более чем одного хост-оператора. Также у них будет повышенная до 4% ставка отчислений в резерв универсального обслуживания. При этом отдельных лицензий для MVNO не планируется. Об этом “Ъ” рассказал источник на телеком-рынке и подтвердил еще один собеседник в отрасли.

В Минцифры “Ъ” сказали, что конкретные механизмы по изменению законодательства в деятельности MVNO еще прорабатываются. Там отметили, что на рынке MVNO большую долю занимают проекты крупнейших банков, которые в рамках своих экосистем «компенсируют клиентам расходы на связь, что позволяет их операторам предлагать тарифы по себестоимости или ниже рыночных». Для граждан такие предложения выгодны, объясняют в Минцифры, но переход абонентов к виртуальным операторам может влиять на доходы опорных операторов и их возможности инвестировать в развитие сетей: «Важно сохранять баланс между доступностью тарифов и развитием инфраструктуры, от которой зависят качество и надежность связи для всех абонентов».

«Большая четверка» операторов считает, что нынешние условия работы MVNO создают «дисбаланс на рынке». Виртуальные операторы уверены: ограничение их работы приведет к росту цен на связь.

# Context-enrichment: Mincifry weighs restrictions on virtual mobile operators
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 27 Oct 2026, Kommersant (sourced to two unnamed telecom-market insiders, with Mincifry — Russia's Ministry of Digital Development — confirming only that "mechanisms are still being worked out") reported a package of tightening measures being prepared for MVNOs (virtual operators that ride the networks/spectrum of the "Big Four" host operators — MTS, Beeline, MegaFon, T2). The discussed measures:
- **A minimum tariff floor of ~₽250/month** per subscriber (≈$3) — i.e. banning the ultra-cheap/free plans.
- **Raising the Universal Service Reserve (резерв универсального обслуживания) levy from 2% to 4%** of revenue. (Caveat: the generic statutory base rate is often cited as 1.2%; the 2%→4% figures here are the specific numbers Kommersant/Mincifry attribute to MVNOs — treat as the reported figures, not the universal statutory rate.)
- **A ban on serving Critical Information Infrastructure (КИИ/CII)** clients.
- **A ban on using more than one host operator's network in a single region** (today many bank MVNOs multi-home, e.g. T-Mobile on T2+MTS, SberMobile on T2+MegaFon — multi-homing gives them coverage/price arbitrage).
- **No separate MVNO licence** is planned (so the regime stays sub-licensing, just with tighter economic terms).

**Why framed this way / what it reveals.** This is explicitly an **economic-squeeze** play dressed as "market balance," not a security/fraud/SIM measure (despite the regtech tag and the broader 2025-26 Russian telecom-security backdrop). Mincifry's own stated rationale is revenue protection for host operators: it acknowledges bank MVNOs "compensate customers' connectivity costs within their ecosystems, letting their operators price at or below cost," which is good for citizens but erodes the Big Four's revenue and thus their network-investment capacity. The minimum-tariff + multi-homing ban + higher levy all directly attack the exact mechanism (cross-subsidy + network arbitrage) that makes bank-MVNO plans cheap. So the "central question" is not "is Russia securing SIMs" but **"is the state reallocating rents from bank ecosystems back to the infrastructure incumbents?"**

## [1] Competitors / peers
Russian MVNO market (end-2025): ~25.5M SIMs, +51% YoY, ~9.4% of the mobile market; market value ~₽111bn; bank-MVNO combined 2025 revenue ~₽33bn.
- **Yota** (MegaFon-owned) — market leader, ~41% of MVNO subs. As an incumbent subsidiary it is *insulated* from much of this (same corporate group as a host).
- **SberMobile** (Sberbank; T2+MegaFon) — ~25% / ~4M subs. Loudly opposed; argues restrictions reduce competition and consumer choice.
- **T-Mobile / T-Mobayl** (T-Bank, ex-Tinkoff; T2+MTS) — ~21% / ~4.6M subs. Says restrictions will raise prices and slow innovation.
- **VTB Mobile**, **Alfa-Bank MVNO** (launched 2025) — smaller bank entrants, directly in the blast radius.
The **"Big Four" host operators** publicly argue current MVNO terms create a "market imbalance." **Why the lay of the land is this way:** the split is structural — *captive* MVNOs inside a host's group (Yota, Rostelecom) survive; *independent/bank* MVNOs that arbitrage across hosts and cross-subsidise from banking are the target. Second-order: this is the same cleavage seen in the Sep-2026 5G decision (only Yota + Rostelecom got 5G rights; all bank MVNOs stay 4G) — a consistent policy direction of fencing bank ecosystems out of frontier telecom.

## [2] Company history / fit
Russian banks built MVNOs as **ecosystem loyalty/retention tools**, not standalone telco P&Ls: a cheap SIM bundled with the bank app deepens engagement, raises switching costs, and feeds data/notifications (OTP delivery, push) into the super-app. T-Bank/Tinkoff pioneered this (Tinkoff Mobile launched 2017 on Tele2), Sber followed with SberMobile, then VTB and Alfa. **Why banks act this way:** connectivity is a low-margin commodity, but *inside an ecosystem* a below-cost SIM is a customer-acquisition/retention subsidy paid out of the far-higher-margin banking relationship — the telco line is a loss-leader funded by lending/payments economics. That is precisely the cross-subsidy Mincifry now names and wants to cap.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Is it new?** The *proposal* is new (Oct 2026) and not previously noted in corpus — **fresh**. But it is the economic sequel to the Sep-2026 5G exclusion of independent/bank MVNOs ([[Russia Bars Independent Virtual Operators From Offering 5G]] — external), i.e. part of a continuing 2025-26 arc of tightening around bank-run telecom. **Traction of the underlying trend (not the proposal):** bank MVNOs are genuinely large and fast-growing (+51% YoY, ~9.4% share, ~₽33bn revenue) — this is a real market, which is *why* incumbents lobbied for restrictions. **Stage:** proposal/consultation only — Mincifry confirms only that mechanisms are "being worked out"; no draft law cited, no effective date. So: real policy signal, but "announced/explored," not enacted. **Where the margin sits:** today bank MVNOs capture ecosystem value by externalising telecom cost onto the host network + their own banking P&L; the measures try to force the economics back toward the host operators (minimum price = a price floor transferring consumer surplus to incumbents; +levy = a direct tax transfer; multi-homing ban = removes the arbitrage).

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Next: Mincifry to formalise mechanisms into draft legislation; expect lobbying both ways (Big Four for; banks + MVNOs against; and a consumer-price angle that regulators dislike politically). Bank MVNOs warn of **higher consumer prices and slower innovation**; the Big Four frame it as fairness/investment capacity. **Why the market may go this way / second-order effects:** (i) If enacted, a ₽250 floor kills the free/near-free bank plans → slows MVNO sub-growth and dents a cheap ecosystem-retention lever for banks → marginally weakens bank super-app stickiness. (ii) Counterintuitively, protecting incumbent revenue "for network investment" concentrates the market around the Big Four right as the same operators are being handed exclusive 5G — compounding incumbent power rather than funding broad investment. (iii) For fintech specifically the hit is modest: MVNO is a retention garnish, not a revenue engine (~₽33bn across all bank MVNOs is tiny next to their banking P&Ls), so importance to the fintech thesis is real but second-order. Regulatory/geopolitical backdrop (VPN crackdown, CII controls, 5G rationing) suggests the trajectory is toward *more* state control over who runs telecom and on what terms.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Importance: 3/5.** Real, well-sourced policy signal materially targeting the economics of bank-owned MVNOs — a genuine fintech-ecosystem angle — but still only a proposal ("mechanisms being worked out"), and MVNO is a retention garnish, not a core revenue line for Russian banks (~₽33bn total across all bank MVNOs). Fresh (no prior corpus note on this proposal), region/ru, directly tied to Sber/T-Bank/VTB/Alfa ecosystems.

## Red-team / challenge questions

1. **Is this fraud/SIM-security regulation or pure economics?** → Economics. Despite the regtech framing, the four measures (price floor, +levy, CII ban, single-host rule) all attack the cross-subsidy/arbitrage that makes bank plans cheap. The CII ban is the only security-flavoured item, and it's narrow. Central question = rent reallocation to incumbents, not SIM fraud.

2. **Is the ₽250 floor and the 2%→4% levy confirmed, or a leak?** → Reported by Kommersant from two unnamed telecom insiders; Mincifry confirms only that mechanisms are "being worked out." Numbers = reported, not legislated. **Open** whether final figures hold.

3. **Does the 2%→4% levy square with the statutory Universal Service Reserve rate (often cited ~1.2%)?** → Discrepancy flagged in [0]. The 2%→4% are the MVNO-specific figures in the Russian reporting; the 1.2% is the generic public-network base rate from an English legal source. **Open** / likely different bases; do not conflate.

4. **How big is the actual revenue at stake?** → Bank MVNOs = ~₽33bn combined 2025 revenue; whole MVNO market ~₽111bn, ~25.5M SIMs, ~9.4% share. Material to telecom, marginal to the banks' own P&Ls.

5. **Who is insulated vs exposed?** → Insulated: captive MVNOs in a host's group (Yota/MegaFon ~41%, Rostelecom). Exposed: independent/bank MVNOs — SberMobile (~25%), T-Mobile (~21%), VTB, Alfa — exactly the fintech-ecosystem plays.

6. **Is this a one-off or part of a pattern?** → Pattern. Follows the Sep-2026 decision barring independent/bank MVNOs from 5G (only Yota + Rostelecom got rights). Consistent arc: fence bank ecosystems out of frontier telecom + squeeze their economics.

7. **Who benefits?** → The "Big Four" host operators (price floor transfers consumer surplus to them; levy is a tax transfer; multi-homing ban removes bank-MVNO arbitrage). Mincifry's stated rationale = protect host revenue/network investment.

8. **What's the real second-order effect of "protecting investment"?** → Concentrates the market around the Big Four right as they also get exclusive 5G — compounds incumbent power rather than demonstrably funding broad network investment. (analysis)

9. **How much does this dent bank super-app stickiness?** → A below-cost SIM is a retention/engagement lever (deepens app usage, OTP/push channel). A ₽250 floor removes the "free" hook, modestly weakening stickiness — but banks can still bundle discounts, just not sub-cost pricing. (hypothesis)

10. **Does the multi-homing ban hit coverage or price more?** → Both: single-host reduces coverage/quality redundancy AND removes the ability to arbitrage the cheapest host per region, raising wholesale cost → higher retail price. Directly undercuts the "at or below cost" model.

11. **What stage is the proposal and when could it bite?** → Pre-draft consultation; no bill, no effective date cited. **Open.**

12. **Could banks exit MVNO if economics break?** → Possible at the margin (telco is non-core), but ecosystem/retention value and sunk brand investment argue for staying and repricing rather than exiting. (hypothesis)

13. **Consumer-price politics — does that stall it?** → Bank MVNOs/consumers lose cheap connectivity; regulators dislike visible price hikes. This is the main political friction that could soften the final measures. **Open.**

14. **Why does this matter for the fintech thesis at all?** → It's the clearest regulatory signal that Russian bank ecosystems' telecom leg is being deliberately constrained — a structural limit on the "super-app bundles everything" strategy, even if the direct revenue hit is small.

15. **Any chance it's protectionism for state-adjacent incumbents (Rostelecom/Yota) specifically?** → The carve-out pattern (Rostelecom + MegaFon's Yota survive both 5G and likely this squeeze) is consistent with favouring state-adjacent/large incumbents over private bank MVNOs. (analysis, not proven)

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russia's MVNO (virtual-operator) segment reached ~25.5m SIMs at end-2025, +51% YoY, roughly ~10% of the mobile market, with a forecast of 28–30m by end-2026 (per Sostav/Inc./Forbes citing market data, as of 2025–26). The structure is highly concentrated AND vertically dependent: MVNOs ride the Big Four hosts' (MTS, MegaFon, T2, Beeline) radio networks with no spectrum of their own, so value is created at the distribution/CRM layer, not the infrastructure layer. Bank ecosystems dominate the segment — ~93% of MVNO subscribers and ~76% of the independents' financial turnover sit with bank-owned brands (SberMobile, T-Mobile, VTB Mobile, Alfa-Mobile, GPB Mobile), per ComNews (as of 2026-04). **Why now:** the proposed Mincifry package — min. tariff RUB 250, universal-service-fund (USF) levy doubled from 2% to 4%, ban on serving critical information infrastructure (KII), and a ban on using >1 host network per region — is explicitly aimed at the bank-subsidized "at-or-below-cost" tariffs that Minister Shadayev called a "pain point" for the industry (per Kommersant/1Prime, as of 2026-10-27). This is classic incumbent-vs-entrant regulatory capture: the Big Four argue bank MVNOs create a "market imbalance" and erode the cash flow that funds network capex.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: subscriber base, ARPU/min-tariff floor, revenue, and (for bank plays) in-base attach / cost-of-acquisition offset by ecosystem cross-sell. Market share by subscribers (mid-2025): Yota ~41%, SberMobile ~25%, T-Mobile (ex-Tinkoff Mobile) ~21% — note Yota is itself owned by host MegaFon, so the independent-bank threat is really SberMobile + T-Mobile. Within the bank-MVNO sub-segment, T-Mobile + SberMobile hold ~95% of revenue. Recent moves: SberMobile grew its base ~160% in 2025 to ~6.3m subscribers; T-Mobile's paying base +32% in 2025. The basis of competition is distribution and bundling, not price-per-GB in isolation — telecom is a retention/engagement hook inside the super-app, cross-subsidized by banking margins. Protagonists' position: the two bank leaders are the *target* of the rule, not a challenged incumbent; their moat (ecosystem switching costs + captive distribution) is exactly what the regulation attacks. Marketplaces (Ozon, Wildberries, Yandex) are reportedly preparing a "second wave" of MVNO launches — a draw for the same rule. (analysis)

**Comps & multiples.** Bank-MVNO economics (2025, per Tadviser/ComNews): T-Mobile revenue RUB 22.1bn (+32.8% YoY); SberMobile revenue RUB 9.3bn (+117.8% YoY) but a net loss of RUB 3.3bn; total bank-MVNO revenue ~RUB 33bn. Loss-ratio arithmetic for SberMobile: `−3.3bn / 9.3bn ≈ −35%` net margin — i.e. the model is run deliberately below breakeven as a customer-acquisition/retention spend, which is precisely what the RUB 250 floor + 4% USF levy would raise the cost of. No listed pure-play RU MVNO exists, so EV/Revenue multiples are **no data**; qualitatively, Sber and MTS are the only scaled public reference points and only at the group level (telecom MVNO is immaterial to Sber's banking P&L). Internal comps (bank/fintech-as-telco playbook): [[Nedbank readies public launch of MVNO platform]], [[Revolut launches Dutch mobile network on KPN 5G]], [[Nubank launches NuCel mobile plan for under-18s in Brazil]], [[Klarna launches mobile phone plans via Gigs]], [[Zip partners with TPG Telecom to launch ZMobile in Australia]] — same global thesis (fintech super-app bolts on connectivity as a low-churn engagement layer); none carries a standalone MVNO valuation, so the comparison is strategic, not multiple-based.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory/margin risk (primary).** The RUB 250 floor + USF levy 2%→4% directly attacks the below-cost pricing that is the bank MVNOs' acquisition engine; second-order effect — if the subsidy loop is capped, the ecosystem's cheap-SIM customer-acquisition channel loses its edge and the in-base cross-sell flywheel slows.
2. **Rail dependence / disintermediation.** MVNOs have no spectrum and the ban on using >1 host per region removes their ability to arbitrage host operators; the Big Four (who are both suppliers and competitors) would gain pricing power over the banks' telecom input cost — a structural dependence on a rival's rails.
3. **Concentration.** With ~95% of bank-MVNO revenue in two names (T-Mobile, SberMobile), the rule is effectively bespoke to Sber and T-Bank; execution risk is binary on the final text (whether banks can still subsidize via ecosystem "compensation" vs a hard tariff floor).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a re-rating of the "bank-as-telco" bundle in Russia from a cheap growth channel to a regulated, cost-floored line — watch whether the marketplace entrants (Ozon/WB/Yandex) still launch once the subsidy loop is capped; the falsifiable thesis: if the RUB 250 floor passes as drafted, bank-MVNO net subscriber adds decelerate sharply in 2027 (vs the +51% segment growth of 2025). Trigger/catalyst: the actual draft legislation text from Mincifry and the Big Four's negotiating stance. (analysis)
