Hong Kong Government Selects Unlimit as a Strategic Enterprise
Unlimit joins a select group of companies under OASES, a government initiative established to attract leading international businesses in sectors of strategic importance to Hong Kong, including financial technology and artificial intelligence. The programme provides tailored government support to help selected companies establish and expand their operations in the city.
From Hong Kong, Unlimit plans to expand its presence across Asia-Pacific, helping businesses enter new markets and move money across borders through its proprietary payment network spanning more than 180 countries. The company obtained its Hong Kong Money Service Operator (MSO) licence in August 2026.
Michele Fung, Head of APAC at Unlimit, said: “Being selected as a Strategic Enterprise by the Hong Kong Government is a strong recognition of Unlimit’s role in the region and our long-term ambitions. Hong Kong is one of the world’s most important financial centres, and we see it as a key base for the next phase of our growth.”

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-10-26 Unlimit announced that the Hong Kong government has selected it as a "Strategic Enterprise," admitting it to the OASES programme. Unlimit was one of 18 companies welcomed in OASES' seventh batch at a 22 October 2026 signing ceremony (the government press event). Unlimit frames Hong Kong as its principal Asia-Pacific hub for cross-border payments and multi-currency settlement, building on the Money Service Operator (MSO) licence it obtained in August 2026. Michele Fung, Head of APAC (Hong Kong-based, ex-LianLian Global), is the named spokesperson. Sources: [unlimit.com press release](https://www.unlimit.com/blog/press-release/hong-kong-government-selects-unlimit-as-a-strategic-enterprise), [thepaypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/hong-kong-government-selects-unlimit-as-a-strategic-enterprise), [fintechnews.hk](https://fintechnews.hk/41460/payments/unlimit-hong-kong-strategic-enterprise/), [ibsintelligence](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi-news/hong-kong-selects-unlimit-for-regional-payments/).

**[1] What OASES is.** The Office for Attracting Strategic Enterprises (OASES), created under the Hong Kong Financial Secretary, recruits leading international/mainland businesses in strategic sectors (fintech, AI, advanced manufacturing, life/health tech, new energy) with tailored, case-by-case government support (land, talent/visa facilitation, funding introductions) in exchange for investment and jobs. This is a partnership/MoU-type designation, not a licence or subsidy grant; Unlimit's actual regulated permission comes from the separate MSO licence. OASES' seventh batch (announced 22 Oct 2026) collectively pledged ~HK$10bn (~US$1.3bn) and 1,600+ high-end jobs; cumulatively OASES partners have pledged ~HK$84bn and ~26,700 jobs. Sources: [HK gov (info.gov.hk)](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202610/22/P2026102200546.htm), [news.gov.hk](https://www.news.gov.hk/eng/2026/10/20261022/20261022_180153_453.html), [The Asian Banker](https://www.theasianbanker.com/press-releases/hong-kong-s-oases-announces-new-batch-of-18-strategic-enterprises).

**[2] Who Unlimit is.** Global payments / financial-infrastructure firm founded 2009, HQ'd across San Francisco and London with ~17 offices, proprietary payment network spanning 180+ countries and 150+ currencies; clients cited include Xiaomi, Telegram, Convera and inDrive. The company has been expanding its licensing footprint market-by-market — e.g. its Brazil Pix payment-institution licence ([[Unlimit obtains Brazil payment institution license for Pix]]) — of which the HK MSO licence and now the OASES designation are the APAC leg. Source: [unlimit.com](https://www.unlimit.com), [fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/10/unlimits-hong-kong-licence-sharpens-its-apac-edge/).

**[3] Prior art / peers (internal).** Hong Kong's OASES pipeline for payments/fintech is a recurring theme in the vault: [[SUNRATE signs agreement with Hong Kong's OASES]] (2026-04, cross-border + Web3 hub), [[KN Group and AlloyX tokenize consumer loans on-chain]] (2025-07, KN Group named an OASES "key enterprise"), and the broader HK-as-APAC-HQ trend in [[Lianlian DigiTech sets up overseas HQ in Hong Kong]] (2025-11) and [[Aspire launches international payment services in Hong Kong]] (2025-12). Unlimit joins a crowded field of payments players anchoring APAC in Hong Kong.

**[4] Why it matters / stakes.** For Unlimit this is primarily a credibility/government-relations win and a planting of its APAC flag — useful for merchant acquisition, bank/partner introductions and talent visas, layered on top of the hard regulatory asset (the MSO licence). For Hong Kong it is one more fintech name in a batch meant to reassert the city's post-pandemic competitiveness versus Singapore. The designation is non-exclusive (18 firms this batch alone) and carries no disclosed Unlimit-specific investment/jobs figure, so its standalone commercial weight is modest; the real test is whether Unlimit converts the status into live APAC cross-border payment volume.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is "Strategic Enterprise" a licence or binding commitment? — **No.** It is an OASES partnership designation offering tailored government support; the binding regulatory asset is the separate MSO licence (Aug 2026). Don't conflate the two.
2. How exclusive is the designation? — **Low.** Unlimit is 1 of 18 in the seventh batch alone; OASES has signed dozens cumulatively. Prestige, not scarcity.
3. Is there an Unlimit-specific investment or jobs number? — **Open / not disclosed.** Batch aggregate is ~HK$10bn and 1,600+ jobs; no per-company breakout for Unlimit found. Treat batch figures as collective, not Unlimit's.
4. Did this actually happen and when? — **Confirmed.** OASES ceremony 22 Oct 2026 (HK gov press release); Unlimit's own 26 Oct release. Multiple independent trade outlets corroborate.
5. Is this just a re-announcement of the August MSO licence? — **No, distinct.** MSO = regulatory permission; OASES = government-relations designation ~2 months later. Related but separate events.
6. Does OASES give Unlimit any exclusive market access? — **No evidence.** Support is facilitation (talent/visa, introductions), not market exclusivity or a payments monopoly.
7. Any financials / valuation implications? — **None disclosed.** Unlimit is private; no deal value, funding, or revenue figure attached.
8. Is the "180+ countries / 150+ currencies" network claim verifiable? — **Partially.** Self-reported by Unlimit across releases; plausible for its profile but un-audited here.
9. Who benefits more — Unlimit or Hong Kong? — **Both, marginally.** Unlimit gets credibility + facilitation; HK gets another fintech name for its Singapore-rivalry narrative.
10. Duplicate of an existing OASES note? — **No.** SUNRATE (2026-04) and KN Group (2025-07) are different companies/batches; Unlimit is a fresh, distinct designation.
11. Competitive read-through? — Reinforces HK's pull on cross-border payments players ([[SUNRATE signs agreement with Hong Kong's OASES]], [[Lianlian DigiTech sets up overseas HQ in Hong Kong]]); not zero-sum for Unlimit.
12. Execution risk? — **Open.** Status is only as valuable as the APAC volume Unlimit actually books; conversion from designation → live merchants is unproven and unmeasured as of this date.
13. Geopolitical exposure? — HK as a base carries US/China friction and sanctions-compliance considerations for a global payments network; not addressed in the release.
14. Is the spokesperson/role real? — **Yes.** Michele Fung, Head of APAC, ex-LianLian Global, corroborated by trade press.
15. Any negative angle the PR omits? — The release is purely promotional; omits costs/obligations Unlimit takes on under OASES and gives no KPI to judge it against.

**Importance: 2/5** — A credible, well-corroborated government-relations milestone that cements Unlimit's APAC/Hong Kong base atop its MSO licence, but it is a non-exclusive designation (1 of 18), carries no disclosed Unlimit-specific investment/jobs or financials, and adds no new regulatory capability beyond the August licence. Incremental expansion news, not a market-moving event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Cross-border payments is a large, structurally-growing pool: secondary estimates cluster at ~$190–240bn in 2026 revenue with a ~7–8% CAGR to the early 2030s (range across Grand View $193.5bn/7.1%, Mordor $238bn/7.2%, Polaris $201.6bn/7.5%, Fortune 7.9% — all vendor reports, treat as directional, not precise; as of Oct 2026). The PSP / merchant-acquiring layer Unlimit sits in is **fragmented**: global card networks (Visa/Mastercard) own the rails, while value in cross-border is captured by the orchestration/local-acquiring layer that licenses locally and nets FX — a high-barrier game (per-market licensing, scheme membership, compliance capital). "Why now": APAC regulators are courting licensed international PSPs to deepen their hubs — Unlimit's Hong Kong MSO licence (Aug 2026) and now OASES "Strategic Enterprise" status is HK positioning itself as a cross-border settlement hub vs Singapore. Second-order effect: a licence + state endorsement is a distribution and trust shortcut, but it is announced entry, not yet proven volume.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/processing volume, take rate (net revenue / TPV), net revenue retention, number of local licences/currencies. Key players in emerging-market / cross-border acquiring: dLocal (listed, EM-focused), Airwallex (private, APAC-rooted), Ebury, Nuvei/Payoneer, Checkout.com, plus local acquirers. Basis of competition: breadth of local licences + payment methods + FX/settlement economics, not price. Recent moves: dLocal FY2025 TPV $40.8bn (+60% y/y), revenue $1.1bn (+47%); Airwallex ~$287bn annualized TPV (Mar 2026), ARR ~$1.5bn (May 2026). Unlimit's position: **niche / catching-up** in APAC (analysis) — it advertises 180+ countries and 150+ currencies and a proprietary network, but discloses no TPV, take rate or NRR, so scale vs dLocal/Airwallex is unverifiable. Moat (analysis): accumulated local-licence stack (Brazil Pix [[Unlimit obtains Brazil payment institution license for Pix]], Mexico card issuance [[Unlimit adds card issuance to Mexican portfolio with Visa]], now HK MSO) = regulatory switching cost, but no network-effect or scale edge is evidenced. **Comps & multiples.** Unlimit is **private and does not disclose financials**; public profile aggregators (Crunchbase/Tracxn) return a wrong-entity homonym (17 employees, £1–10M) — disregard, no Unlimit valuation exists → "no data". Comparison is qualitative against disclosed peers:

- Airwallex (private): last round ~$11bn (May 2026) on ARR ~$1.5bn → implied ARR multiple = `$11bn / $1.5bn ≈ 7.3x` (round valuation, not market cap) — richer than dLocal, justified by ~120% TPV growth (growth-multiple correlation).
- Internal comps: Ebury revived a ~£2bn London IPO target on SMB cross-border FX [[Ebury revives London IPO targeting £2B valuation]]; Nuvei–Payoneer ~$2.7bn deal talks [[Nuvei in advanced talks to acquire Payoneer for $2.7B]]; Sunrate acqui-hired into global acquiring [[Sunrate acqui-hires payments team to enter acquiring market]].
Distribution not computed (Unlimit figure missing); the peer set spans ~3x (listed, profitable) to ~7x (private, hyper-growth) revenue — Unlimit cannot be placed on it without disclosed TPV/revenue.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Opacity / unverifiable scale** — no disclosed TPV, take rate or revenue; "180+ countries / proprietary network" is a marketing claim, so execution and actual share in APAC are unknowable. Why: the headline is a government label, not a volume metric, and may overstate traction.
2. **Single-market dependence on policy favour** — OASES/Strategic-Enterprise status is discretionary government support; concentration on HK's hub ambitions means a policy shift or licence-condition change directly hits the APAC build-out. Why: state endorsement is a reversible asset, not a contractual moat.
3. **Disintermediation / margin pressure** — cross-border acquiring value is squeezed between scheme rails above and local real-time rails (Pix, HK FPS, stablecoin settlement) below; incumbents dLocal/Airwallex are larger and already profitable, so a late APAC entrant risks sub-scale economics. Why: take rate compresses as local rails commoditize cross-border flow.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a **new-entry / land-grab** signal, not a re-rating — APAC cross-border acquiring is becoming a licensing race, and HK is explicitly recruiting PSPs to compete with Singapore (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Unlimit converts the HK licence into disclosed APAC TPV/merchant wins within ~12 months, it's a credible fourth name behind dLocal/Airwallex/Ebury; watch for the first disclosed APAC volume or a funding round that finally prints a valuation. What breaks it: continued silence on volumes through 2027 = the licence was a PR milestone, not a growth inflection.
