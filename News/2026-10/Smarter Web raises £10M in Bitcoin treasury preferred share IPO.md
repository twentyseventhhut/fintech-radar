# Smarter Web raises £10M in Bitcoin treasury preferred share IPO

The Smarter Web Company, a Bristol-based digital services business that holds Bitcoin as its primary treasury reserve asset, has conditionally raised £10 million through the initial public offering (IPO) of its new MORE perpetual preferred shares. The amount meets the minimum required for the deal to proceed but sits below the £15 million to £25 million range the company targeted when the offer opened.
The company disclosed the result in a Regulatory News Service (RNS) announcement on October 12, 2026. Net proceeds are expected to be approximately £8.35 million after expenses, compared with the £13.1 million to £22.7 million in net proceeds the company had projected for a fully subscribed offer.
Retail Investors Supply Most of the Capital
Of the £10 million raised, £7.6 million came from retail investors and £2.4 million from institutional investors, meaning individual investors accounted for about 76% of total demand. Retail participation was routed through the Winterflood Retail Access Platform’s partner network of intermediaries, with a minimum subscription of £500.
Smarter Web will issue 111,112 preferred shares at the £90 offer price in connection with the IPO. A further 100,000 preferred shares will be issued into an at-the-market (ATM) facility held by or on behalf of Tennyson Capital Partners LLP, taking the total number of preferred shares to 211,112. An ATM facility allows a company to sell new shares gradually into the open market at prevailing prices rather than through a single fixed-price offering.
Trading in MORE is scheduled to begin at 8:00 a.m. UK time on October 14 on the Main Market of the London Stock Exchange (LSE) under the ticker MORE, with the International Securities Identification Number (ISIN) GB00C0HJ0D12. The company said all other IPO conditions have been satisfied and admission remains subject only to final formalities. Smarter Web’s ordinary shares will continue to trade under the ticker SWC.
Revised Use of Proceeds
Because gross proceeds fell below the £15 million level referenced in the prospectus , Smarter Web adjusted how it will deploy the net funds:
 £4 million (about 48%) for additional Bitcoin purchases
 £3.85 million (about 46%) for working capital and general corporate purposes, primarily to build cash reserves for dividend payments
 £0.5 million (about 6%) for the operating business
The weighting toward cash reserves reflects the fixed income commitment attached to the new security. At the time of launch, the company had said proceeds would go toward working capital and reserves, further Bitcoin acquisitions and growth of the operating business, without the specific split now disclosed.
How MORE Preferred Shares Work
MORE is a sterling-denominated perpetual preferred share, meaning it has no maturity date , and it carries no voting rights at general meetings. Holders receive a cumulative, variable-rate preferential dividend paid weekly, at an initial rate of 12% per annum on a £100 reference amount per share. “Cumulative” means any dividend the board does not pay accrues and must be settled before ordinary shareholders receive distributions. The board retains the ability to suspend weekly payments.
At the £90 issue price, the 12% rate on £100 equates to a running yield of about 13.3% if the rate is maintained. The shares also carry a liquidation preference that ranks ahead of ordinary shares, and the company holds the right to redeem them.
The first weekly dividend has been set at £0.2301 per share, with an ex-dividend date of October 22, a record date of October 23 and payment expected by November 9, 2026. The company described this as the first weekly dividend paid by a London-listed commercial company.
The shares are being admitted to the non-equity shares and non-voting equity shares category of the Official List maintained by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), the UK’s financial services regulator. According to Orrick, which advised financial adviser Strand Hanson, this is the first preferred share IPO into that category under the FCA’s new prospectus regime, which took effect in January 2026.
Road to Listing
Smarter Web first proposed the preferred share class in an RNS announcement on September 11, 2026. Shareholders approved the three required resolutions at a general meeting on September 28, each with more than 99.8% of votes cast in favour.
The FCA approved the prospectus the following day, and the company opened the £15 million to £25 million offer with up to 277,777 preferred shares available. The IPO was conditional on raising at least £10 million, a threshold the final result cleared by £80, based on 111,112 shares at £90 each.
Bitcoin Holdings and Balance Sheet
As of September 30, 2026, Smarter Web held 2,747 BTC at a net average cost of £82,562 per coin, down from 2,878 BTC on June 30. The decline reflects the sale of 177.89 BTC in July to repay its Smarter Convert instrument early, partially offset by 46.89 BTC bought during the quarter. The company reported a BTC Yield of minus 4.35% for the quarter, a metric that tracks the change in Bitcoin held per fully diluted share.
During the same quarter, the company cut borrowings under its Coinbase credit facility from about £20.8 million to £19.0 million and completed a £210 million capital reduction. A capital reduction converts share premium into distributable reserves, which UK companies need to pay dividends lawfully.
Chief Executive Officer Andrew Webley described the £10 million result as being at the lower end of the targeted range in the October 12 announcement, while highlighting the scale of retail participation. In the company’s quarterly update, he said: “We believe it will be the first preferred share of its kind in the UK.”
Analysis: What the Smaller Raise Means
The structure follows a model used in the United States by Strategy, formerly MicroStrategy, whose STRC perpetual preferred stock also pays a variable dividend, set at 12% on a $100 stated amount as of August 2026. Smarter Web’s offering tests whether that income-focused approach can attract capital in the UK market.
Annual dividends at the initial 12% rate would total about £1.33 million on the 111,112 IPO shares, or about £2.53 million across all 211,112 preferred shares. On the lower figure, the £3.85 million reserve allocation would cover roughly 2.9 years of payments before operating cash flow, Bitcoin holdings, or new capital would be needed. These figures assume the rate stays unchanged, which the variable-rate terms do not guarantee.
The £4 million Bitcoin allocation is modest relative to the company’s existing treasury, which cost more than £226 million at its average acquisition price. Investors will now watch MORE’s first weeks of trading against its £90 issue price, the use of the Tennyson ATM facility, and any disclosures of Bitcoin purchases funded by the new proceeds.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
The Smarter Web Company (SWC, LSE Main Market; Bristol web-design firm turned UK's largest public Bitcoin-treasury company) **conditionally raised £10M** via the IPO of a new perpetual preferred share class, **MORE**, admitted to the FCA Official List's non-equity/non-voting category and trading on the LSE from 2026-10-14 (ISIN GB00C0HJ0D12). Ordinary shares stay under SWC.

De-PR'd reality: this is a **miss, not a win**. The £10M only cleared the £10M minimum (by ~£80) and **fell well short of the £15–25M target**; net proceeds ~£8.35M vs a projected £13.1–22.7M. **76% (£7.6M) came from retail**, £2.4M institutional — a thin institutional bid for a 12%-coupon instrument is itself a signal. Because the raise undershot, SWC **re-cut use of proceeds**: only ~£4M (48%) to buy BTC, ~£3.85M (46%) parked as cash reserves to fund the dividend, ~£0.5M to the operating business. **+ Why structured this way:** the reserve-heavy split is forced — the instrument carries a fixed, cumulative, weekly 12%/yr dividend on a £100 reference (≈13.3% running yield at the £90 issue price), so a company with negligible operating cash flow must pre-fund coupons from the raise itself. That is circular: investors are partly paid back with their own capital. The board can suspend weekly payments and redeem at will; "cumulative" means skipped dividends accrue ahead of ordinary holders.

Genuine firsts (real, if narrow): first preferred-share IPO into the FCA's new non-equity category under the Jan-2026 prospectus regime (per Orrick, adviser to Strand Hanson); first weekly dividend by a London-listed commercial company (first payment £0.2301/share, ex-date 2026-10-22, pay by 2026-11-09). An ATM facility via Tennyson Capital adds up to 100,000 more preferred shares (211,112 total) for gradual issuance.

## [1] Competitors / peers
The template is **US Strategy (ex-MicroStrategy) STRC** — a variable-rate cumulative perpetual preferred, also 12% on a $100 stated amount (as of Aug 2026), the explicit model SWC copied. Also in the preferred-income cohort: Strive's **SATA** (higher yield, daily dividends, debt-free). European BTC-treasury peers in the corpus: [[Amdax plans Bitcoin treasury listing on Euronext Amsterdam]] (NL, equity listing to grow BTC-per-share), [[France's Capital B raises €15.2 million to expand bitcoin treasury]] (€15.2M private placement, Adam Back). The Amdax note already name-checks SWC as a European BTC holder alongside Germany's Bitcoin Group and France's The Blockchain Group. **+ Why the lay of the land:** SWC is a scale minnow — 2,747 BTC (~£226M at cost) vs Strategy's ~640k BTC. It is a **follower cloning a US structure 15 months after STRC's July-2025 debut**, and importing it precisely as the US version broke (see [4]). Position: catching-up / me-too on structure, first-mover only on the UK regulatory wrapper.

## [2] Company history / fit
Founded 2009 by Andrew Webley as a web-design/marketing shop; IPO'd on **Aquis in April 2025** adopting a digital-asset treasury policy, becoming the UK's best-performing equity of 2025; **uplisted to LSE Main Market Feb 2026**. Holdings: **2,747 BTC at 2026-09-30**, net avg cost £82,562/coin, **down from 2,878 BTC at June-30** — it **sold 177.89 BTC in July to repay its "Smarter Convert" instrument early**, offset by 46.89 BTC bought; **BTC Yield of −4.35%** for the quarter (BTC-per-diluted-share fell). It cut Coinbase credit-facility borrowing £20.8M→£19.0M and did a **£210M capital reduction** (converting share premium to distributable reserves — legally required to pay dividends). **+ Why it acts this way:** a tiny operating business cannot organically fund a BTC-accumulation thesis, so it must tap capital markets repeatedly; the capital reduction and reserve-funded coupon are plumbing to make a dividend-paying preferred *legally possible* for a company that doesn't generate the cash to pay it. A negative BTC Yield quarter + selling BTC to retire debt is the opposite of the "stack grows per share" story these vehicles sell.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Real novelty = the **regulatory first** (UK/FCA non-equity category) and the weekly-dividend mechanic — not the economics, which are a direct STRC import. Value-add is thin: £4M of fresh BTC buying is **~1.8% of an existing £226M treasury** — immaterial to the stack. **+ Why value-add is weak, deeper:** the preferred only creates value for ordinary holders if SWC's equity trades **above 1.0x mNAV** so new capital buys *more* BTC-per-share than it dilutes — but here the capital is a fixed liability (12% cumulative), not accretive equity. It is **leverage dressed as income**. Who captures the margin: retail yield-chasers take on equity-like downside (perpetual, suspendable, redeemable, junior to the Coinbase facility) for a bond-like coupon the company must pre-fund from their own money. On the arithmetic in the primary source, the £3.85M reserve covers only **~2.9 years** of coupons at 211,112 shares before BTC sales/new capital are needed — a short runway if the premium closes.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch MORE vs its £90 issue price, Tennyson ATM usage, and whether promised BTC buys actually appear. **+ Why the market backdrop matters (the central-question shift):** the question is not "did SWC pull off a UK first" but **"is it importing a model at the exact moment that model is failing?"** In **June 2026 the US blueprint cracked**: Strategy's STRC de-anchored from $100 par, hitting an intraday **~$71** low; falling below $95 triggers contractual +0.5% coupon step-ups, Strategy **paused its ATM**, and ultimately **sold ~3,588 BTC (~$216M), its largest-ever disposal, to cover Q2 preferred dividends** — the first real stress test of the digital-credit structure, and it failed the "stable value" promise. Sector-wide, **~37 of the 100 largest treasury companies (~40%) traded below NAV in early Jan 2026**; as of Sept-2026 most trade below 1.0x on an enterprise basis, draining the issue-shares-above-NAV growth engine ("spiral of doom"). SWC's own mNAV reportedly swung to **~0.75x (a discount)** at one 2026 quarter-end before recovering to **~1.60x (~60% premium) by Oct-9** — extreme volatility that makes the coupon-coverage math fragile. **+ Second-order:** a weekly, cumulative, suspendable 12% coupon pre-funded from the raise is a self-liquidating structure if BTC stalls or the equity premium collapses; the "UK first" is as likely to be remembered as a late-cycle top signal as a milestone. Backdrop: FCA's new 2026 prospectus regime made the wrapper possible, but regulators are the risk, not the catalyst, for retail-heavy crypto-leverage products.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Smarter Web is a UK Bitcoin-treasury / digital-asset-treasury (DAT) company — a public vehicle whose equity thesis is a leveraged proxy on BTC, templated on Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy). The structure runs on one mechanism: the flywheel works only when the stock trades *above* the net value of its coins (mNAV > 1x), so issuing new shares at a premium is accretive (buys more BTC per share than it dilutes). "Why now": in 2026 that premium regime has broken sector-wide. Per K33, ~40% of the top-100 BTC-treasury firms traded below NAV as of Sept 2026; per DWF Ventures, only 4 of the 20 largest DATs had mNAV > 1x (i.e. 16 of 20 below NAV); on 6 Oct 2026 (BTC ~$86k) even Strategy sat at ~1.06x and had authorized selling up to $1.25bn of BTC as a funding lever (via The Block, Sherwood, TradingKey). Structure: no revenue moat — these are capital-markets vehicles, not operating businesses; entry barrier is access to cheap capital, which is exactly what evaporates below NAV.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: mNAV (market cap ÷ BTC NAV), BTC Yield (change in BTC-per-fully-diluted-share — Smarter Web disclosed **-4.35%** for the quarter, i.e. treasury-per-share *shrank*), and cost of capital on new instruments. Peers / basis of competition (access to premium capital, not product): Strategy (STRC perpetual preferred, 12% on $100 par, rate reset monthly to trade near par; ~$96.18 / 12.48% effective yield 21 Aug 2026), Strive (SATA preferred, 13% daily-paid), Twenty One (~0.62x), Metaplanet (~0.80x) — via Strategy.com, TipRanks, Cryptopolitan. Smarter Web's position: a small niche follower (2,747 BTC at 30 Sep 2026, ~£82,562/coin avg cost) importing Strategy's preferred-share playbook into the UK — genuinely *first* UK preferred-share IPO into the FCA's new non-equity category (per Orrick), but a structural latecomer with no moat beyond regulatory novelty. `(analysis)`

**Comps & multiples.** Internal comps: [[Barclays initiates Strategy coverage at overweight, $130 target]] (Strategy mNAV compressed to ~1.16x, as low as ~0.68x on some feeds, Jul 2026) · [[France's Capital B raises €15.2 million to expand bitcoin treasury]] (European BTC-treasury private placement, May 2026). Multiple that matters here is mNAV, not EV/Rev. Smarter Web (SWC) arithmetic: fully-diluted market cap ~£103.1m ÷ NAV ~£116.9m = **~0.88x mNAV** (an Apr-2026 update put it at 0.97x) — below 1x (via stockanalysis.com, theblock.co). So the issuer is itself in the below-NAV cohort. Running yield on MORE: 12% on £100 ÷ £90 issue price = **~13.3%** (company-stated). Dividend load arithmetic: 12% × £100 × 211,112 shares ≈ **£2.53m/yr**; the £3.85m reserve allocation ≈ 2.9 years of cover before operating cash, BTC sales or new capital are needed. Flag: the cost of capital (~13.3% sterling) is *high* and fixed, layered senior to ordinary shares. Distribution across comps not computed (STRC/SATA are different currencies/structures) — qualitative only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Raising at sub-NAV cost of capital.** With SWC itself at ~0.88x mNAV, ordinary-equity issuance would be openly dilutive — the preferred route sidesteps that but locks in a ~13.3% fixed sterling claim that ranks ahead of ordinary holders. Why it matters: on a BTC book that yielded **-4.35%** BTC-per-share last quarter, a 13% senior cash obligation is a second-order drain on the very per-share BTC the equity thesis depends on.
2. **Dividend-coverage / refinancing cliff.** Reserves cover only ~2.9 yrs of payments and the split already skews 46% to "building cash reserves for dividends" rather than BTC — a self-funding income instrument whose cash ultimately leans on either BTC sales (Smarter Web already sold 177.89 BTC in Jul to repay Smarter Convert) or fresh capital. The board can suspend weekly dividends, but cumulatively; arrears compound.
3. **Undersubscription / demand-quality signal.** The raise cleared the £10m minimum by just £80 and missed the £15–25m target; 76% came from retail via an intermediary platform, not institutions. Why it matters: thin institutional appetite for a novel UK BTC-preferred at a 13% yield is a weak read on the sector's cost-of-capital in a below-NAV regime, plus retail concentration raises conduct/suitability scrutiny for a 12%-yield perpetual.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Smarter Web is testing whether Strategy's "Digital Credit" preferred-share model travels to the UK once the equity flywheel is dead — i.e. whether DATs can refinance via fixed-income claims when their stock trades below NAV `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: if MORE trades at/above its £90 issue price and the ATM facility places the remaining 100k shares smoothly, it validates preferred stock as the DAT survival mechanism for below-NAV names; it's wrong if MORE breaks par, the ATM stalls, or dividends get suspended within the first year — any of which would signal the UK won't fund this structure at a sustainable rate. Trigger to watch: first weeks of MORE trading vs £90 and disclosure of BTC bought with net proceeds.
