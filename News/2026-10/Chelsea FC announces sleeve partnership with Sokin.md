Chelsea Football Club has today announced a multi-year partnership with Sokin, the embedded finance and payments company used by businesses worldwide. Sokin will be named a Principal Partner and Official Business Payments and FX Partner of Chelsea FC and Chelsea FC Women. 
As a Principal Partner, Sokin will be represented on the sleeves of the Chelsea FC men’s, women’s and academy home, away, and third kits from the 2026/27 season. 
The partnership brings together two ambitious London-headquartered organisations with worldwide reach – a club with millions of fans across the world and a fintech platform through which businesses move more than $11 billion a year. A club of Chelsea’s scale works in many currencies every day, with partners, competitions and supporters around the world, and needs a financial partner built to handle that. 
Sokin gives finance teams business payments, multi-currency accounts, foreign exchange, checkout and treasury in one place, so they spend less time juggling banks and providers and keep more of what they move. 
As Official Business Payments and FX Partner, Sokin will simplify how Chelsea FC sends, receives and manages money in the currencies the club operates in. 
The Sokin logo will feature on the sleeve of the Chelsea shirt for the first time when the men’s team host Tottenham Hotspur at Stamford Bridge on Saturday 24 th October.
‍
 Jason Gannon, President at Chelsea FC said: 
“At Chelsea, we seek to partner with organisations that share our ambition and innovative mindset. Sokin’s expertise in business payments, and their focus on making both simpler for the businesses they serve, makes them a natural fit for the Club. We are pleased to welcome Sokin to the Chelsea family and look forward to working together in the years ahead.”

 Vroon Modgill, CEO and founder at Sokin said: 
“Chelsea plays on the biggest stage in world football, and its business runs in dozens of currencies. Sokin was built for that with regulated, multi-currency payments and foreign exchange for organisations that operate internationally. We’re proud to be the platform behind the business payments and FX of one of the most iconic teams in football. Sokin’s platform serves businesses in more than 170 countries. We’re thrilled to be partners for the season ahead. An organisation as sophisticated as Chelsea doesn’t leave its finances to chance.”
   
 About Sokin 
Sokin is the financial layer global business runs on. Businesses can transact, convert and hold money in 70+ currencies across 170+ countries. Some businesses use Sokin to manage global finance in-house. Others build it into their products to power financial experiences for their customers. Businesses move more than $9 billion a year on licenses Sokin holds in 36+ countries and infrastructure Sokin owns. Founded in 2019, Sokin has raised over $100 million from investors including Morgan Stanley Expansion Capital and Prysm Capital. 
   
 About Chelsea Football Club 
Chelsea Football Club is one of the biggest, most successful football clubs globally and our men’s team are reigning world champions, having won the four-yearly FIFA Club World Cup in 2025. Our women’s team, Chelsea Women, are also multiple trophy winners. 
Founded in 1905, Chelsea is London’s most central football club, based at the iconic 40,000-capacity Stamford Bridge stadium. Nicknamed the Blues, the club lifted the Champions League for the first time in 2012 and domestically has won the Premier League five times, the FA Cup eight times, the Football League Cup five times and the Football League Championship once, in 1955. 
The Chelsea Women’s team have enjoyed a huge amount of success and in 2025 won the en’s Super League for a sixth consecutive year and the eighth time overall. The Women’s FA Cup has been won on six occasions. We have also captured the FA Women’s League Cup four times as well as reaching the UEFA Women’s Champions League final in 2021. In two seasons, 2020/21 and 2024/25, a domestic treble of trophies was secured. 
The Chelsea Foundation boasts one of the most extensive community initiatives in sport, helping to improve the lives of children and young people all over the world.

## [0] What happened

On 2026-10-24 (announced/retrieved 2026-10-27), **Chelsea FC** named London fintech **Sokin** a **Principal Partner** and **Official Business Payments & FX Partner** of both Chelsea FC and Chelsea FC Women under a **multi-year** agreement. As a Principal Partner, the Sokin logo appears on the **sleeve** of the men's, women's and academy home/away/third kits from the **2026/27 season**, debuting on the men's shirt vs Tottenham Hotspur at Stamford Bridge on Sat 24 Oct. Beyond branding, Sokin will handle Chelsea's international payments, multi-currency accounts, FX, checkout and treasury.

**Commercial terms (value and duration) were not disclosed** by either party ([financefeeds.com](https://financefeeds.com/chelsea-fc-names-sokin-principal-partner-in-multi-year-payments-deal/), [insideworldfootball.com](https://www.insideworldfootball.com/2026/10/27/chelsea-sign-sokin-sleeve-deal)). Treat any specific figure as unverified.

## [1] Who / what is Sokin

Sokin is a UK-headquartered **global business payments / embedded-finance fintech**, founded **2019** by CEO **Vroon Modgill**. It offers multi-currency accounts, cross-border business payments, FX, checkout and treasury in one platform; it claims coverage of **170+ countries** and **70+ currencies**, operating on licenses in **36+ countries**. The press release cites businesses moving **"more than $11 billion a year"** through Sokin in the headline, while the "About Sokin" boilerplate in the same release says **"more than $9 billion a year"** — an internal inconsistency worth flagging (volume claims are self-reported and unaudited).

Funding: Sokin has raised **$100M+** total from investors including **Morgan Stanley Expansion Capital** and **Prysm Capital**. Prior rounds include a **$50M Series B led by Prysm** (reported valuation ~**$300M**) and a Morgan Stanley-led **~$31M** investment; it also drew a **$100M debt facility from Oxford Finance** (see [[Sokin secures $100M debt facility from Oxford Finance]]). Notable backers/figures include ex-PayPal execs Gary Marino and Mark Britto, and footballer Rio Ferdinand.

## [2] Why it matters / context

This is a **brand-visibility + commercial-services** deal, not a capital or product event. For Sokin it is a marketing escalation: it already held a **B2B payments partnership with Manchester United** (Aug 2025 — [[Manchester United names Sokin official B2B payments partner]]) and a **payments-partner tie with the Professional Triathletes Organisation** ([[Professional Triathletes Organisation names Sokin official payments partner]]). Chelsea moves Sokin from a behind-the-scenes B2B supplier to a **front-of-audience sleeve sponsor** at a globally-marketed club (reigning 2025 FIFA Club World Cup winners) — a bet on brand awareness to feed its B2B sales funnel.

Fintech-on-football-shirts is now a crowded playbook: see [[Manchester City names Revolut official back-of-shirt partner]], [[Zilch becomes official payment partner of Arsenal FC]], [[Liverpool FC names PayPal official digital payments partner]], [[Ebury renews fintech partnership with Southampton FC]], and [[dLocal becomes global sponsor of Uruguay national football team]]. Sokin is thus following a well-worn fintech brand-marketing route rather than breaking new ground.

## [3] Dynamics / what to watch

- **Sokin's traction**: recent activity includes a $100M debt facility (Oxford Finance, Jan 2026), an **Adyen US payments/treasury partnership** ([[Sokin and Adyen partner on US payments and treasury]]), stablecoin capabilities ([[Sokin launches stablecoin capabilities for hybrid finance platform]]), a deposit product paying up to 4.15% APY ([[Sokin launches Balance Boosts paying up to 4.15% APY]]), and a new CFO ([[UK paytech Sokin names Tom Steer as CFO]]). The Chelsea deal fits a push for scale and visibility ahead of a likely future raise.
- **Undisclosed economics** make ROI unverifiable; sleeve deals at big clubs typically run in the low-to-mid single-digit £M/year range, but no public figure exists here.
- **Execution risk**: delivering live payments/FX/treasury for a club operating in "dozens of currencies" is operationally harder than a pure logo placement; the B2B service component is where real value (and risk) sits.

## [4] Bottom line

A multi-year Chelsea sleeve + business-payments/FX partnership gives Sokin high-profile brand exposure to feed B2B sales, extending its football strategy from Manchester United into a Principal Partner role. Financial terms undisclosed; volume claims self-reported and internally inconsistent ($11B vs $9B in the same release). A routine, well-trodden fintech sponsorship — modest strategic significance, mainly a marketing/credibility signal rather than a funding, product, or regulatory milestone.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Sokin sits in B2B cross-border / multi-currency payments & FX. Market context (secondary analyst estimates, so treat as directional): the cross-border payments market is sized at ~$221.6bn in 2025 rising to ~$237bn in 2026 (per Fortune Business Insights, via web, as of 2026), with the B2B slice ~$117.6bn in 2026, i.e. ~half the pool (per Mordor/Fortune citations, via web) — attribute with caution, estimates diverge widely across firms. Structure: fragmented at the mid-market tier Sokin targets (business multi-currency accounts + FX + checkout + treasury in one place), but converging on a handful of scaled platforms (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Nium) at the top; entry barriers are regulatory (Sokin cites licenses in 36+ countries) and network/liquidity scale on the FX side. "Why now": Premier League's 2026 front-of-shirt gambling-ad ban is pushing betting brands off shirts and pulling finance/tech brands in (per SponsorUnited/Traders Union, via web) — the structural tailwind that made a club like Chelsea an open slot for a payments fintech, not a one-off.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/cross-border volume, take rate, # currencies/countries, FX spread. Sokin's disclosed scale: >$11bn/yr business payments flow (note text) — though the same Sokin "About" text separately says businesses move ">$9bn a year" on its own licenses/infrastructure, a notable internal inconsistency in the PR itself; 70+ currencies, 170+ countries, holds balances in 26 currencies (per web, Sokin IR). Peers & recent moves: **Wise** net revenue $2.5bn, +19% YoY, cross-border volume $243.5bn in FY2026 (per American Banker, via web); **Airwallex** ~$1.3bn revenue, $11bn valuation at June 2026 Series H (per Sacra/TFN, via web); **Payoneer** $1.05bn 2025 revenue, $12.3bn B2B volume (per web). Position: Sokin is a **niche challenger** — $300m post-money (2025 Series B, see below) vs multi-billion peers; it is ~2 orders of magnitude smaller than Wise/Airwallex on both valuation and flow. Moat `(analysis)`: owned licenses/infrastructure in 36+ countries is a real regulatory switching-cost barrier, but it has no scale, brand or liquidity moat vs incumbents — the Chelsea deal is a distribution/brand play to close that gap.

**Comps & multiples.** Sokin is private; the relevant anchor is its own last round, not a trading multiple:
- **Sokin** — $50m Series B (led by Prysm Capital; Morgan Stanley Expansion Capital, Watershed, ex-PayPal execs) at **$300m post-money**, 2025; total raised ~$96m; cited 100% YoY revenue growth / 8x revenue since 2022 (per TechFundingNews / Fintech Magazine / Finextra, via web). No revenue figure is disclosed, so **P/S = no data / `[UNSOURCED]`**; valuation stated only as a round valuation, not market cap.
- **External public comps (EV/Rev, round numbers, arithmetic shown):** Airwallex ≈ $11bn / $1.3bn = **~8.5x** revenue (private round); Wise ≈ listed, ~$2.5bn revenue, +19% growth; Payoneer ≈ $1.05bn revenue. 2–3 comparable figures but on much larger, differently-staged companies — so **distribution not computed; qualitative comparison only.** At Airwallex's ~8.5x, Sokin's $300m would imply ~$35m revenue — plausible but unverified, so `[UNSOURCED]`.
- **Internal comps** (base, cross-border/multi-currency peers): [[Pleo launches multi-currency accounts to cut FX fees]], [[PayPal launches PayPal World cross-border wallet platform]], [[Standard Chartered partners with Dandelion for cross-border payouts]], [[MassPay adds 35+ local fiat funding currencies for payouts]], [[Remitly launches stablecoin-powered cross-border wallet]].

**Risk flags.**
1. **Marketing spend vs scale / cash burn.** A multi-year Premier League sleeve deal (SumUp's Man Utd sleeve was reported >£20m/season as a reference point, via web) is a large fixed commitment for a company last valued at $300m with ~$96m raised — terms undisclosed; the second-order risk is runway consumed on brand before unit economics are proven.
2. **Self-reported, inconsistent metrics.** The PR cites both ">$11bn" and ">$9bn" annual flow, with no revenue, take rate or margin disclosed — "announced" scale that can't be de-PR'd; who's silent about what (economics) matters here.
3. **Disintermediation / scale disadvantage.** Sokin competes head-on with Wise/Airwallex/Payoneer that are 10–40x its flow; without a price, liquidity or distribution edge, FX-spread margin is the layer most exposed to being competed away by larger rails.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a distribution/brand bet, not a business-model shift: Sokin buys top-tier global visibility (Chelsea's international fan base + a real FX client in the club itself) to punch above a $300m challenger's weight. Falsifiable thesis: if the deal converts to disclosed client/revenue growth at next raise, it validates sponsorship-as-CAC for mid-market B2B fintech; trigger to watch — Sokin's next funding round valuation and whether it discloses revenue. What breaks it: terms/burn leak out as disproportionate to flow, or no revenue disclosure at next round (signalling the spend didn't convert).
