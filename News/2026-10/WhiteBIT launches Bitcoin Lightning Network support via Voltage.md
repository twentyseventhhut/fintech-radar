ZUG, Switzerland, Oct. 08, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- 
 WhiteBIT , a global crypto exchange serving 10 million users, launches support for the Bitcoin Lightning Network, giving customers a faster and more efficient way to move Bitcoin across deposits, top-ups, withdrawals, send-and-receive flows, and QR payments. The launch is powered by Voltage, a Bitcoin and Lightning infrastructure provider that helps businesses bring reliable Lightning payments and liquidity operations to production.
The Lightning integration delivers on a simple goal: Making Bitcoin feel instant and practical inside the WhiteBIT experience. For users who still associate Bitcoin with slow settlement or high-friction transfers, Lightning makes Bitcoin easier to use for everyday movement of funds, exchange top-ups, trader transfers between platforms, and cross-border payment use cases. 
WhiteBIT now supports Lightning across user-facing and infrastructure-level flows, including Bitcoin deposits and withdrawals, fast account top-ups, QR payment experiences, and send-and-receive functionality. Together, these features empower customers to move value more quickly while giving WhiteBIT another payment rail inside a broader ecosystem that already supports trading, stablecoin access, earning-oriented products, and card-based spending. 
For customers in regions where traditional payment rails can be expensive, slow, or limited, Lightning offers another path for smaller transactions and cross-border money movement. The launch supports a faster Bitcoin rail for remittances, exchange funding, merchant-style QR payments, and interoperability with Lightning-enabled wallets and applications.
"WhiteBIT’s mission is to make blockchain technology accessible and widely adopted by delivering practical, user-friendly solutions for digital assets" said Volodymyr Nosov, Founder and CEO of WhiteBIT and President of W Group , which WhiteBIT is a part of. "Adding Lightning support brings us closer to this goal as we are making Bitcoin faster and more useful for customers who want to top up accounts, send and receive funds, and use Bitcoin across more real-world flows." “Lightning becomes powerful when it disappears into a customer experience people already trust. WhiteBIT is bringing that experience to millions of users, and Voltage is proud to support the infrastructure, liquidity, and operational reliability needed to make Bitcoin faster and more practical at scale.” said  Graham Krizek, CEO and Founder of Voltage. 
Voltage's infrastructure helps businesses launch and operate Lightning payments without having to manage the full complexity of liquidity, channels, routing, and payment reliability on their own. By partnering with Voltage, WhiteBIT can focus on the customer experience and product rollout while using a dedicated Lightning infrastructure partner for production-grade operations. 
WhiteBIT users can now choose Lightning as a faster way to move Bitcoin into, or out of their WhiteBIT account. The result is a more practical Bitcoin experience for customers who want speed, lower-friction transfers, and another way to use Bitcoin across the WhiteBIT ecosystem. 
Lightning is available on WhiteBIT, supporting Bitcoin deposits and withdrawals:  whitebit.com

 WhiteBIT is a leading global crypto exchange by user traffic, offering over 1,020 trading pairs, 360+ assets, and supporting 8 fiat currencies. With a strong focus on regulatory compliance, WhiteBIT serves 10 million users across six continents and more than 150 countries. ## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Lightning is maturing from an experiment into a payment rail: per Spark/CoinLaw-cited metrics (via web, as of early 2026) the network holds ~5,400 BTC capacity (+384% vs 2020), ~18,000 active nodes / ~75,000 channels, processes 12M+ tx/month and crossed $1bn/month in payment volume (public Lightning volume +266% y/y per bex.co, Mar 2026) — a shift where payment utility decouples from BTC price speculation. No single credible TAM for Lightning payments is publicly verifiable — TAM: no data. Structure: the stack is splitting into (a) exchanges/wallets at the customer edge (WhiteBIT, Kraken, Bitget, Coinbase, Cash App) and (b) managed infra/liquidity providers underneath (Voltage, Lightspark, Strike) — WhiteBIT sits in (a) and rents (b). Entry barrier is operational, not capital: running liquidity, channels and routing reliably — exactly the complexity WhiteBIT offloaded to Voltage. Why now: remittance/cross-border friction + Lightning's growth being fastest in high-fee corridors (Nigeria, Philippines, Vietnam, US-Mexico, per web) makes an "instant BTC" rail a defensible UX feature for a 10M-user exchange, not a differentiator anymore.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for an exchange adding a rail: deposit/withdrawal volume on the rail, routed-payment success rate, and (undisclosed here) take/fee on transfers — all `[UNSOURCED]` for WhiteBIT. Players at the customer edge already on Lightning: Kraken, Bitget, Coinbase (deposits/withdrawals), Cash App/Strike (merchant+consumer). Basis of competition is UX + breadth of supported flows, not the protocol. WhiteBIT's position: catching up / at-parity, not ahead — it is a late-ish adopter adding table-stakes (deposits, withdrawals, QR, send/receive) that peers shipped earlier; the earlier same-pattern in-base precedent is Square's 2025 merchant launch [[Square launches Bitcoin payments for merchants]] and BitGo's infra play [[BitGo adds Lightning Network support to crypto-as-a-service]]. Moat (analysis): WhiteBIT's edge is distribution/scale (10M users, 150+ countries, Visa/FC Barcelona/Juventus brand reach) and switching costs of an existing exchange relationship — NOT the Lightning tech itself, which is rented and commoditised.

**Comps & multiples.** This is a product launch, not a deal — no valuation attaches to WhiteBIT and no public financials, so EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E = no data for the protagonist. WBT token exists but token price ≠ equity multiple; excluded. Infra-partner / adjacent fundraise comps (web, mark as round valuations not market caps): Lightspark — ~$175M raised to date, Series A post-money ~$849M (May 2022, per web); Strike — $80M round (Ten31-led, per web); Voltage — no disclosed valuation/round, private, no data. Internal comps (same "exchange/firm adds Lightning or BTC payment rail" pattern): [[SoFi taps Lightspark Bitcoin Lightning network for remittances]], [[BitGo adds Lightning Network support to crypto-as-a-service]], [[Square launches Bitcoin payments for merchants]], [[Lightspark acquires Estonia's Striga]]. Distribution not computed — fewer than 3 comparable equity multiples; comparison stays qualitative. Flag: nothing to call rich/cheap — no price, no valuation disclosed by WhiteBIT.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Dependence on someone else's rails** — WhiteBIT offloaded liquidity, channels and routing to Voltage; reliability, uptime and economics of the rail now hinge on a single private infra vendor (Voltage, no disclosed financials). Second-order: a Voltage outage, price change or failure becomes WhiteBIT's customer-facing failure, and the margin on transfers is captured partly by the infra layer.
2. **Commoditised / late-mover feature** — Kraken, Bitget, Coinbase and Cash App already offer Lightning; this is catch-up table-stakes, so it is unlikely to move user acquisition or fees materially (analysis). The PR is silent on fees, limits and routing success rate — the economics that would make it more than a checkbox.
3. **Regulatory / compliance exposure on an instant cross-border BTC rail** — WhiteBIT markets Lightning for "remittances" and "cross-border" across 150+ countries; an instant, QR-based BTC payment rail raises AML/travel-rule and sanctions-screening surface (cf. in-base enforcement precedent [[Binance among crypto firms hit by French money-laundering checks]]). Second-order: faster settlement compresses the window for transaction monitoring.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector this is confirmation, not inflection: Lightning-at-exchanges is becoming default plumbing, which re-rates the value toward the infra layer (Voltage/Lightspark/Strike) that every exchange must rent (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if the "announced → adopted" gap closes, WhiteBIT (or a peer) discloses Lightning deposit/withdrawal volume or a fee on it within ~2-3 quarters; trigger to watch = any volume/fee disclosure, or a Voltage funding/valuation round that would anchor the infra-layer value. What breaks the thesis: Lightning volume at exchanges stays a silent, zero-fee convenience feature with no disclosed traction — in which case it stays a UX checkbox, not a monetisable rail.
