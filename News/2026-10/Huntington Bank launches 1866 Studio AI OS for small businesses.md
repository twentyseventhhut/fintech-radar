New connected experience designed to help small business owners build and scale their businesses with access to financial, operational and business management AI tools and expertise
 COLUMBUS, Ohio , Oct. 19, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- The Huntington National Bank ("Huntington") today announced The 1866 Studio, a new AI-powered operating system built with Gemini platform, Google Cloud's AI platform. Named for the year Huntington was founded, The 1866 Studio honors the bank's legacy of empowering businesses by bringing together business guidance, financial intelligence, digital tools and human expertise into a single, connected experience for aspiring and existing small business owners.

 Experience the full interactive Multichannel News Release here: https://www.multivu.com/huntington/9347252-en-huntington-bank-announces-the-1866-studio  
The 1866 Studio is designed to simplify the complex and time-consuming demands of starting and running a business. The operating system will provide agentic AI workflows across the financial, operational and management needs of business owners that streamline complex work and help them move from information to intelligence to action. By retaining relevant business context across tasks, The 1866 Studio enables business owners to orchestrate decisions across disparate tools while maintaining control as their needs evolve.
"Small businesses are essential to strong communities and a strong economy, and helping businesses thrive has been central to Huntington's purpose for more than 160 years," said Brant Standridge, president of Huntington Bank. "We are continually looking for new ways to help business owners navigate challenges, seize opportunities and spend more time focused on the financial success and growth of their businesses. The 1866 Studio is the next evolution of that commitment, bringing together Huntington's expertise and Gemini platform to help owners make more informed decisions and spend more time building the businesses they imagined."
The 1866 Studio will leverage Huntington's extensive small business and financial expertise and an AI-powered experience built using Google Cloud's comprehensive platform to build, scale, govern and optimize agents. Combined with Google's multimodal Gemini models, Gemini platform is enabling Huntington to offer business owners an intelligent, connected operating system that helps them navigate decisions with greater confidence and efficiency.
"Small business owners shouldn't have to become experts in every function or in AI to build a successful business," said Vijay Konduru, head of innovation and AI products at Huntington. "The 1866 Studio is an AI-powered platform designed around the small business jobs to be done. As the product evolves, it's intended to increasingly support the full range of those jobs across the business lifecycle, from turning an idea into a business to running and growing it, and ultimately preparing for succession, sale or exit."
"The future of small business banking is about delivering expertise and guidance in the moments customers need it most," said Rohit Bhat, general manager and managing director, Financial Services, Google Cloud. "By using Gemini platform and Gemini frontier models to build The 1866 Studio, Huntington is creating an innovative experience that helps small business owners move from information to action."
Initial AI capabilities of The 1866 Studio, including agentic workflows, equip entrepreneurs to turn business ideas into actionable plans, navigate startup requirements, strengthen their brand, identify funding sources, enable funding readiness and plan for growth. For example:
 Business launch support: With built-in AI agents, owners can navigate the steps required to start a business, from entity selection and registration to licensing and operational setup, while generating a customized action plan to help move from idea to launch.
 Marketing & campaign creation: Leveraging multimodal Gemini models, owners can instantly generate tailored marketing collateral, digital ad copy and social media campaigns.
 Loan readiness assistance: AI-powered agents help owners evaluate financing readiness by identifying potential gaps, explaining lender expectations and recommending actions that can strengthen eligibility for future financing opportunities.
"Agentic AI workflows that help carry out tasks are one part of this end-to-end operating system," Konduru added. "Alongside them are relevant, personalized expert content and an AI assistant that helps owners both monitor their business operations and finances and manage and orchestrate daily work. Together, these capabilities help owners move from advice to action with support that evolves with the business, while they remain in control."
The 1866 Studio builds on Huntington's multi-year strategic partnership with Google Cloud to modernize technology, advance governed AI capabilities and accelerate innovation. Through the partnership, Huntington continues to explore new ways to use technology built with Google Cloud to enhance experiences for customers, colleagues and the communities and businesses it serves.
Interested small business owners and entrepreneurs, not limited to Huntington customers, can sign up for early access to The 1866 Studio beginning Oct. 19.
 Visit Huntington.com/The1866Studio to sign up for early access to The 1866 Studio. 
 About Huntington Huntington Bancshares Incorporated is a $284 billion asset regional bank holding company headquartered in Columbus, Ohio. A top 10 U.S. commercial bank, The Huntington National Bank and its affiliates provide consumers, small and middle-market businesses, corporations, municipalities, and other organizations with a comprehensive suite of banking, payments, wealth management, and risk management products and services. Founded in 1866, Huntington operates over 1,400 branches in 21 states, with certain businesses operating nationally. Visit Huntington.com for more information.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
### [0] What happened
On 2026-10-19 The Huntington National Bank announced **The 1866 Studio**, an "AI-powered operating system" for small-business owners, built on **Google Cloud's Gemini platform** (multimodal Gemini frontier models). Named for Huntington's 1866 founding year, it bundles agentic AI workflows, personalized expert content and an AI assistant into one "connected experience." Early access opened the same day at Huntington.com/The1866Studio — and is explicitly **not limited to Huntington customers**. At launch it is an early-access/announcement product, not a GA rollout; concrete capabilities are three agentic workflows: (1) business-launch (entity selection, registration, licensing, setup, action plan), (2) marketing/campaign creation (Gemini multimodal ad copy + social content), (3) loan-readiness assessment (gap analysis, lender-expectation coaching). Source: [PRNewswire / Google Cloud press](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/huntington-bank-introduces-the-1866-studio-an-ai-powered-small-business-operating-system-built-with-google-cloud-302912579.html), [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48601/huntington-bank-partners-google-cloud-for-ai-powered-small-business-operating-system).

### [1] Who is Huntington & why it matters
Huntington Bancshares (Nasdaq: HBAN), Columbus OH, is a **~$284B-asset regional bank holding company**, a top-10 US commercial bank, ~1,400 branches across 21 states. Its scale and footprint jumped in 2025-26 via two deals: the **Cadence Bank** merger (closed Feb 2026, ~$279B combined, +$36.9B loans incl. $26.4B commercial — 8th largest in Texas, #1 in Mississippi) and the **Veritex** acquisition (Texas). See [[Huntington completes Cadence Bank merger into $279 billion bank]] and [[Huntington and Veritex secure all merger approvals]]. Combined revenue synergies are now guided to ~$600M through 2028. Small/middle-market business banking is a core franchise, so an SMB-facing AI product aligns with the deepened commercial base this geographic expansion created. Spokespeople: Brant Standridge (president, Huntington Bank), Vijay Konduru (head of innovation & AI products), Rohit Bhat (GM/MD Financial Services, Google Cloud).

### [2] The Google Cloud relationship
1866 Studio builds on Huntington's pre-existing **multi-year strategic partnership with Google Cloud** to modernize infrastructure and stand up governed AI. This is one of a wave of banking x Google Cloud/Gemini deals in the news DB: [[Commerzbank and Macquarie adopt Google's Gemini Enterprise]], [[Klarna and Google Cloud enter strategic AI partnership]], [[TransUnion advances AI credit intelligence with Google Cloud]], [[WeLab and Google form AI partnership for digital banking in Asia]]. Pattern: Google positions Gemini/Vertex as the agent-building platform ("build, scale, govern, optimize agents") for FS incumbents; Huntington is a US-regional showcase. Note also Huntington's separate fintech partnership [[Payabli and Huntington Bank partner on digital banking payments]].

### [3] Competitive landscape
SMB banking/finance AI is crowding fast. Megabanks: BofA's Erica (>2.5B interactions, Agentforce to advisers) and JPMorgan (#1 AI-banking visibility, ~$1B+ AI run-rate; also [[JPMorgan pledges $80 billion small-business lending in American Dream push]]). Fintech challengers targeting the same SMB owner: [[Relay raises $50 million for small business banking platform]], Mercury, Brex, plus [[Gusto launches business formation products with managed bank accounts]] (direct overlap with 1866's entity-formation workflow) and card/agent plays like [[Mastercard launches Agent Suite for small businesses]] / [[Mastercard launches Small Business Navigator in Canada]]. Also [[Lama AI raises $12 million Series A for AI agents in banking]]. Huntington's differentiator is bundling bank-grade loan-readiness + a real lending balance sheet with a non-gated funnel (open to non-customers) as a top-of-funnel acquisition channel.

### [4] Why it's significant / open questions
Significance: a mainstream ~$284B regional bank branding a full "operating system" (not just a chatbot) for SMBs, built natively on Gemini agents, and using it as a **customer-acquisition funnel** (open to non-customers) rather than a gated feature. It signals the "AI-as-SMB-front-door" strategy among US regionals and deepens Google Cloud's FS agent franchise. But: it is early-access/announcement-stage with three narrow workflows; no pricing, user numbers, GA date, or independent efficacy evidence; agentic-AI-in-banking is simultaneously drawing security scrutiny (unvetted agents accessing bank accounts, per Axios Oct 2026). Treat impact as potential, not yet proven.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
### Red-team questions

1. **Is 1866 Studio actually live, or just announced?** Announcement + **early-access sign-up only**, opened 2026-10-19. No GA, no user count, no production metrics. Treat as a launch of early access, not a shipping product.
2. **Is it a real "operating system" or marketing framing for a chatbot?** Largely framing. Concrete scope = three agentic workflows (launch, marketing, loan-readiness) plus an AI assistant and expert content. "Operating system" is aspirational ("as the product evolves... the full business lifecycle").
3. **How much is Huntington vs. just Google's Gemini?** Built on Google Cloud's Gemini platform/models; Huntington supplies SMB/financial expertise, data context and the loan-readiness logic. Platform risk: core AI capability is Google's.
4. **Does the loan-readiness tool actually lend?** No — it coaches on financing readiness (gaps, lender expectations). It is a top-of-funnel qualifier, not a credit decision or committed capital. Open: conversion to actual Huntington loans.
5. **Why is it open to non-Huntington customers?** Customer-acquisition funnel — classic top-of-funnel play to capture aspiring/existing SMB owners before they bank elsewhere. Monetization path (cross-sell to deposits/loans) is implied, not disclosed.
6. **Is this a first-of-kind?** No. SMB AI tooling is crowded (BofA Erica, JPMorgan, Relay, Mercury, Brex, Gusto formation, Mastercard Agent Suite). Differentiation = bank balance sheet + non-gated funnel + "OS" bundling, not novel tech.
7. **What's the data/privacy/security exposure?** Open/elevated. Agentic AI touching business finances; contemporaneous Axios reporting (Oct 2026) flags unvetted AI agents accessing US bank accounts; megabanks are restricting agents. No detail on 1866's data governance or agent permissions.
8. **Does the Cadence/Veritex expansion actually feed this?** Plausibly — larger commercial/SMB footprint (TX, South) = bigger addressable base. But no stated linkage; speculative connection.
9. **Any revenue/P&L impact disclosed?** None. No pricing, cost, or financial targets for 1866 Studio itself.
10. **Hallucination/accuracy risk on regulatory guidance (entity selection, licensing)?** Open and material — giving legal/compliance-adjacent startup guidance via LLM carries liability/accuracy risk; disclaimers and human-expert fallback mentioned ("human expertise") but not detailed.
11. **Is "160+ years" / "$284B asset" / "1,400 branches / 21 states" accurate?** Consistent with Huntington's own release and 2026 filings post-Cadence/Veritex. Verified.
12. **Competitive moat durability?** Weak-to-moderate. Gemini is available to any bank; moats are distribution (branches, existing SMB relationships) and the lending balance sheet, not the AI layer.
13. **Does it differentiate from Google's own agentic SMB push?** Google supplies the platform across many FS clients; Huntington's version is one instance. Risk of commoditization if every regional ships a Gemini SMB assistant.
14. **Measurable adoption signal to watch?** Early-access conversion, GA date, disclosed user/SMB counts, and whether loan-readiness feeds reported SMB loan growth — none available yet.

**Importance: 3/5** — A credible, strategically-framed move by a genuinely large US regional bank (~$284B assets) and a notable Google Cloud/Gemini FS showcase, with a distinctive open-funnel, "AI-as-SMB-front-door" angle. But it is early-access/announcement-stage with three narrow workflows, no disclosed metrics/pricing/GA, rests on Google's AI (low tech moat), and enters a crowded SMB-AI field. Directionally meaningful for incumbent-bank AI strategy; not yet proven in impact.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US regional-bank SMB banking, overlaid with the "GenAI copilot for business banking" wave. Market anchor: the US neobanking market is projected at ~$48.5bn in 2026, with the business-account segment the largest slice at ~68% share (per Fortune Business Insights, as of 2026) — treat as a vendor-report TAM, directionally indicative not audited. Structure: fragmented and barbelled — on one side money-center/regional incumbents (JPMorgan, BofA, Huntington) with deposits, SBA franchises and compliance scale; on the other a crowded SMB-neobank cohort (Relay, Mercury, Bluevine, Novo, Found) competing on UX and software. The GenAI layer is a new axis of competition on top of deposits/lending. "Why now": frontier multimodal models (here Google's Gemini) make agentic workflows — business formation, marketing collateral, loan-readiness — cheap to productize; incumbents are racing to graft AI onto existing distribution before neobanks capture the SMB software surface. Note Rohit Bhat's own framing ("expertise and guidance in the moments customers need it") is PR positioning, not evidence of adoption.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: deposits, SBA/7(a) origination volume, SMB customer count, and (for the AI layer) activation/adoption vs. sign-ups. Key players and basis of competition: incumbents compete on balance-sheet + trust + SBA franchise; neobanks on product velocity and embedded software; the new front is AI copilots. Recent dated moves: BofA shipped AskGPS, an in-house GenAI assistant for its ~40k transaction-banking clients (Sept–Oct 2025, see [[Bank of America rolls out AskGPS GenAI assistant for payments]]); JPMorgan runs 400+ AI use cases on an ~$18bn tech budget and pledged $80bn in SMB lending ([[JPMorgan pledges $80 billion small-business lending in American Dream push]], May 2026); Gusto launched AI-assisted business formation with managed bank accounts (Oct 2026, [[Gusto launches business formation products with managed bank accounts]]) — a direct functional overlap with 1866 Studio's "business launch" agent. Huntington's position: a credible SMB incumbent, not a laggard — #1/#2 nationally in SBA 7(a) origination (record >$1.8bn SBA 7(a) in 2025, +20% YoY; ~$2.09bn across ~6,998 loans per SBA/industry trackers, as of 2026) and a $284bn-asset top-10 US commercial bank per its own release. Moat (analysis): SBA distribution + existing SMB relationships + a multi-year Google Cloud partnership — distribution moat, not an AI moat (the model is Google's, not proprietary).

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/revenue attaches to this launch itself — bank-internal product, so revenue multiples are not computable here; "no data" on 1866 Studio economics. Public read-across: Huntington Bancshares (HBAN) is listed; EV/EBITDA and bank-style multiples exist but are not cleanly computable from a free source here → [UNSOURCED]. Private SMB-neobank comps as a positioning gauge (round valuations, not market caps): [[Mercury raises $200 million at $5.2 billion valuation]] — ~$5.2bn post-money (May 2026) against >$1bn annualized revenue ([[Mercury crosses $1 billion in annualized revenue]]), i.e. P/revenue ≈ $5.2bn / ~$1.0bn ≈ 5.2x (round, not public-market); [[Relay raises $50 million for small business banking platform]] — $50m raise, ~200k SMB customers, valuation not disclosed → "no data" on multiple; Bluevine — 1M+ lifetime businesses, $2bn+ deposits, private, valuation not disclosed. Distribution not computed (only one revenue-backed comp); qualitative read: SMB-neobanks carry mid-single-digit revenue multiples on a software/growth story, whereas Huntington's value here is defensive (retaining SMB primacy), not a re-rating catalyst. In-line/no-flag on valuation given no number disclosed.

**Risk flags.**
1. Announced ≠ adopted. The release describes agents in future tense ("will provide", "early access" from Oct 19); no users, no retention, no economics disclosed. The tell: silence on activation, pricing and whether non-Huntington sign-ups convert to banking relationships — the actual monetization thesis.
2. Dependence on someone else's stack. 1866 Studio is built on Google Cloud/Gemini; Huntington owns distribution but not the model. Margin and roadmap control sit partly with Google, and the differentiator is replicable — any Google Cloud bank client can build a similar copilot, so the moat is the SBA franchise, not the AI.
3. Agentic-AI governance / liability. Regulators are hardening on bank AI agents (FINRA's 2026 report declares the GenAI "honeymoon over"; ~18 supervisory documents now address AI agents, though no formal rule yet). Loan-readiness and business-formation guidance from a hallucination-prone agent creates fair-lending, suitability and reliance-liability exposure that sits on Huntington, not the vendor.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is incumbent defense, not a new entry: Huntington is using AI to wrap its SBA/SMB franchise and keep the software surface from drifting to neobanks (Mercury, Relay) and SMB platforms (Gusto). Falsifiable thesis: if 1866 Studio drives measurable SMB deposit/loan growth (watch for disclosure of activated users or SBA-pipeline lift in H1 2026 earnings), the bank-as-AI-platform model validates; the thesis breaks if it stays a sign-up funnel with no reported conversion by mid-2027 — i.e. a marketing veneer on unchanged economics. Trigger to watch: whether peers (other Google Cloud/regional banks) ship copycat copilots within 2-3 quarters, confirming it's table stakes rather than differentiation.
