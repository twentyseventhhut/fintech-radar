## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
The Финтехно channel publishes an explainer of the **2026-10-05 presidential decree** permitting the sale of AO UniCredit Bank ("ЮниКредит Банк"). Content, de-PR'd:
- **It is NOT a full exit.** Before any sale, UniCredit first carves out ("выделят") a **new bank** that will host international (euro/USD) payments and stay **100% owned by the Italian group** — framed as "the minimal Russian business European regulators are willing to tolerate." The **remaining** business goes to the buyer. This is identical to the structure in [[Putin allows sale of UniCredit's Russian assets]].
- **Timeline per the channel:** the split/sale scheme was first announced by UniCredit in **May 2026** (the non-binding agreement); the group targets a **H1-2027 close**; buyer and price are **undisclosed**. The decree green-lights BOTH operations at once — the reorganization-by-spin-off AND the subsequent purchase of 100% of the remaining bank — with "no further approvals required."
- **What is still NOT done (de-PR'd):** the channel concedes the new bank still has to be **separated into a standalone credit institution and granted its own banking licence** — i.e. permission ≠ completion. (The channel aggregate is truncated mid-sentence at "пока до конца неп[онятно]".)

**Why this item exists / what it reveals:** It is a secondary-source Russian explainer riding the Oct-5 decree news cycle. ## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Theme: the controlled, dual-regulator exit of the last Western banks from Russia — and, specifically, the *carve-out-then-sell* engineering that governs how value is extracted. Structure of the exit market is not price-driven but permission-driven: every share deal in a "strategic" credit institution needs (a) a Russian presidential decree, now granted (2026-10-05), and (b) separate Bank of Russia (CBR) approval of a sale to a Western-owned seller, still pending; on the EU side the ECB forces the shrinkage. So the binding constraint is regulatory choreography on both ends, not a buyer or a valuation. "Why now": the president's decree converts the May-2026 non-binding UAE agreement from blocked to executable, clearing only the Russian-side veto; a binding deal, physical asset separation and a new banking licence for the spun-off entity remain (per Meduza/Euronews/Global Banking & Finance, 2026-10-05/06). Two secular drivers: ECB scale-down pressure (the stick pushing parents out) and Moscow's "unfriendly-seller" exit regime (mandatory ~60% discount + ~35% exit levy, approximate, per prior corpus) that makes a *clean* full-bank sale uneconomic — which is exactly what forces the split design. (analysis)

**Competitive landscape.** KPIs here are prudential/structural, not commercial: local loan-book run-off %, share of assets parked at the CBR, trapped non-repatriable profit, and the realised P&L hit on crystallisation. "Competition" = speed and cleanliness of exit, not market share. This note's specific angle — the asset split — is the mechanism: UniCredit keeps a 100%-owned "New Bank" confined to EUR/USD cross-border payments for non-sanctioned corporates (the strategically scarce, sanctions-robust rail) and sells only the gutted "Remaining Bank" (local lending down ~90% per channel text → [UNSOURCED] on exact magnitude). Peers by exit *progress* (dated): Citigroup — fully out, AO Citibank → Renaissance Capital, closed Jan-2026 at a ~$1.2bn loss; Societe Generale — clean full exit 2022. Raiffeisen (RBI) is the direct laggard-peer and the sharpest contrast: unlike UniCredit it has NO authorised deal — Russian authorities previously *blocked* a local-buyer sale over sanctions-transfer fears (Reuters/Moscow Times, 2025-10), and RBI now pins hopes on a €3.15bn Rasperia court windfall (Vienna default judgment) to fund an exit (Bloomberg, 2026-10-05). RBI's AO Raiffeisenbank also runs a strategic rail (TurkStream fuel-payment flows), which is precisely why Moscow resists letting it go — a direct parallel to why UniCredit is only *allowed* to leave once it agrees to KEEP the payments piece itself. Position: on exit progress UniCredit is now ahead of RBI (authorised split-sale vs blocked/attritional run-off) but behind Citi/SocGen (done). Moat: none commercial; the residual "stickiness" is the payments plumbing both Moscow and the parent want preserved — the split formalises that each side keeps the rail it values. (analysis)

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation metric attaches to this decree: price, buyer identity and the "Remaining Bank" book value are all undisclosed, so EV/Rev, EV/EBITDA and P/E are **"no data"** (an unlisted local sub governed by a sale price, not a market cap). "Distribution not computed, qualitative comparison." The relevant internal comps are exit-loss precedents, consistently negative:
- [[Citigroup to sell remaining Russian operations at $1.2bn loss]] (2026-01) — ~$1.2bn pretax loss on AO Citibank; direct read-across that foreign-bank exits crystallise losses, not gains.
- [[Citi board approves sale of AO Citibank to Renaissance Capital]] (2026-01) — sold to a local/friendly-jurisdiction buyer; UniCredit → UAE investor mirrors the pattern.
- [[Putin allows sale of UniCredit's Russian assets]] (2026-10) and [[Bank of Russia keeps Raiffeisen, UniCredit systemically important]] (2026-10) — the same-event decree and the adjacent CBR SIB-list continuity.
UniCredit's own guided figure: cumulative P&L hit ~€3.0–3.3bn, of which ~€1.6–1.8bn is a non-cash FX-reserve recycle through P&L (no capital drain), with the deal expected to add ~35bps to CET1 (Bloomberg, 2026-05-07). So the "€3bn hit" overstates the economic loss — roughly half is accounting. A multiple cannot be computed absent a disclosed price → the next hard datapoint is the sale price at binding signing.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Carve-out execution is the binding path, not the decree.** The headline is "sale allowed," but the real gating step this note surfaces is that a *new standalone credit institution must first be spun out AND separately licensed* before anything sells; CBR approval of the onward sale is then still required. Second-order: each incremental step (spin-off → licence → binding deal → CBR nod) is an independent slip-risk against the H1-2027 target, and until it closes UniCredit keeps carrying a Russian balance sheet (plus SIB-buffer load) it wants off the books.
2. **Forced-seller discount hidden behind the split.** The split is explicitly designed to "reduce the discount / ease getting the funds" (channel text) — a tell that a large (~60%) mandatory discount + ~35% levy otherwise bites; precedent (Citi −$1.2bn, UniCredit's own −€3.0–3.3bn) confirms steep realised losses. Second-order: by shrinking the sold perimeter to the gutted lending shell, UniCredit narrows the base the discount bites on, but value for shareholders still leaks to Moscow via discount/levy.
3. **Retained payments bank undercuts the "exit" and carries a sanctions tail.** UniCredit does not actually leave — it keeps a 100%-owned Russian EUR/USD payments franchise, and sells to an unnamed UAE investor (reportedly tied to a Russian group; treat as reported, not confirmed). Second-order: a retained Russian rail plus a politically-connected buyer invites continued ECB/EU and sanctions scrutiny, so the "overhang removed" read is only partial.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Not a re-rating — it is a procedural unlock that moves UniCredit from "stuck" to "cleared to engineer and transact," and it sets the template for how a Western bank can leave Russia *at all*: split off and keep the payments rail Moscow wants preserved, sell the rest at a discounted shell price. Falsifiable thesis: the Russia exit is paced by regulators, so the decree's value is optionality (a path to close), not realised value — which only lands at binding signing with a disclosed price. Triggers to watch: (a) the spun-off bank's licensing and whether it is itself kept on the CBR SIB list; (b) the first disclosed price/loss at binding signing (next Citi-style crystallisation datapoint); (c) read-across pressure on RBI, whose own exit now hinges on the Rasperia windfall rather than any Kremlin green light. Thesis is wrong if a binding deal closes at/above book with no incremental loss — which the Citi precedent and UniCredit's own guidance argue against. (analysis)
