NBB and TerraPay partner to connect traditional banking with the global digital payments ecosystem
Strategic collaboration combines NBB's banking expertise with TerraPay's extensive global payout network, expanding international payment reach and access to digital wallets to more than 12bn endpoints
 Dubai, UAE:  The National Bank of Bahrain (NBB), a leading bank in Bahrain has entered into a strategic partnership with TerraPay, a global digital money movement company, to expand its international payout capabilities and enhance cross-border payment experiences for its customers.  
The collaboration combines NBB's established banking infrastructure, customer relationships and regional expertise with TerraPay's extensive digital global payout network. Through this partnership, NBB will leverage TerraPay's digital infrastructure to extend its international reach, simplify cross-border payout operations and enable access to a broader range of payment destinations. Central to this partnership is TerraPay's ability to receive Swift ISO 20022 messaging and seamlessly deliver cross border transactions in real-time safely and securely. All with the benefit of complete transparency to the customer with no deductions from the principal amount.  
Through TerraPay's strategic Swift connectivity, banks can leverage existing Swift infrastructure and established RMA key exchange processes to access TerraPay's network of bank accounts, digital wallets and local payment systems worldwide. This approach significantly reduces the complexity of direct integrations while enabling faster access to new markets and payout corridors. Through this collaboration with Swift, TerraPay enables banking partners such as NBB to concentrate on their core strengths: customer relationships, banking services and delivering an increasingly digital customer experience. 
Commenting on the occasion, Vineet Munot, Group Head of Transaction Banking at NBB, said, “International connectivity is becoming increasingly important to how our customers operate, and our role is to ensure that NBB’s transaction banking expertise continues to serve those needs with speed and reliability. This collaboration is a strategic step forward in scaling our payments proposition through infrastructure that can improve the cross-border transaction experience. Through TerraPay, we see an opportunity to connect our customers in Bahrain and the region with a wider range of payment destinations, supporting their growth as their requirements become more global.”  
Ani Sane, Co-Founder and Chief Business Officer at TerraPay, said: “Banks want to extend their international payment reach without having to build and maintain separate connections into every market, wallet and local payment system. That is exactly the problem TerraPay solves. By combining TerraPay’s global payout network with Swift connectivity, NBB can use its existing banking infrastructure to access more than 12 billion bank accounts, digital wallets and local payment systems through a single global network. This partnership gives NBB a faster and more scalable way to expand into new corridors, broaden customer choice and improve the cross-border payment experience, while allowing the bank to retain control of the customer relationship and its core banking infrastructure. NBB is a strong example of how leading banks are choosing TerraPay to extend traditional correspondent banking into the rapidly growing world of instant payments and digital wallets.” 
By bringing together NBB's established banking capabilities and TerraPay's global digital payout infrastructure, the two organisations aim to bridge traditional banking and emerging payment ecosystems, creating new opportunities for more accessible, efficient and connected crossborder money movement.

# Context-enrichment: National Bank of Bahrain partners TerraPay for international payouts
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- On **13–14 October 2026**, formalised at **Sibos 2026 (Miami)**, TerraPay and NBB announced a "strategic partnership" under which NBB plugs into TerraPay's global payout network to **expand its cross-border/outbound payout reach**. Mechanically, NBB keeps its **existing Swift infrastructure and RMA key-exchange** and uses TerraPay's **Swift ISO 20022 connectivity** as a single on-ramp to reach "bank accounts, digital wallets and local payment systems worldwide" — instead of building bilateral integrations per market (per NBB/TerraPay release via Zawya, fintechfutures.com, finextra.com, 14 Oct 2026).
- **This is an announcement, not a live launch.** Future-tense throughout ("NBB *will* leverage… to *extend* its international reach"). **No go-live date, no named corridors, no volumes/GMV, no FX-margin or fee disclosure, no customer-segment scope** (retail vs corporate vs transaction-banking). The NBB spokesman is **Vineet Munot, Group Head of Transaction Banking** — signalling the intended beneficiary is corporate/transaction-banking flow, not consumer remittance, though the PR never says so explicitly (analysis).
- **Watch the headline number.** The release claims reach to **"more than 12 billion endpoints."** TerraPay's own boilerplate in the SAME release states **7.5bn+ bank accounts in 156+ countries** plus **3.7bn wallets** via Xend. "12 billion" is a loose roll-up (bank accounts + wallets, and elsewhere TerraPay cites 12bn+ *cards*) — a marketing aggregate, not a crisp count of reachable destinations. Treat all "endpoint" figures as vendor-stated, unaudited.
- **Why framed this way:** the value-prop is sold to the *bank*, not the end-user — "retain control of the customer relationship and your core banking infrastructure" while renting TerraPay's last-mile. The PR anchors to TerraPay's network scale and the "no deductions from principal / full transparency" line because the real pitch is **replacing slow, opaque correspondent-banking chains with one API-plus-Swift hop**. That is the genuine substance; everything else is scale boilerplate.

## [1] Competitors / peers
NBB had a wide menu of near-identical "bank rents a cross-border rail" options; TerraPay is one of several:
- **Thunes** — the closest direct comp; in the same month signed **[[Aljazira Bank adopts Thunes for cross-border transfers]]** (KSA, Sep 2026) and across the Gulf **Banque Saudi Fransi** and **First Abu Dhabi Bank**. Same "plug into our Direct Global Network" motion, same scale boilerplate.
- **Visa Direct / Mastercard Move** — card-network payout rails chasing the same bank-channel flows; notably TerraPay itself rides both (**[[Mastercard collaborates with TerraPay on wallet acceptance]]**, Dec 2025; Visa is a TerraPay strategic investor).
- **Nium** — 190+ countries / 100+ real-time corridors; regional rival (Emirates NBD).
- **Swift itself** — moving up the stack with **[[Swift launches pay-by-alias cross-border initiative]]** (Oct 2026); TerraPay's whole pitch piggybacks on Swift ISO 20022 + RMA, so Swift is both enabler and, longer-term, a potential disintermediator of the "value-add rail."
- **Position: parity / follower, not lead.** NBB is a late, incremental bank logo in a motion that infra players have been repeating across the GCC all year. No disclosed differentiator for choosing TerraPay over Thunes/Nium/Visa Direct.
- **+ Why the landscape is this way:** every GCC bank faces the same structural choice — rebuild ageing correspondent coverage (slow, capital-heavy) or rent a network and stay the customer-facing brand. Second-order: value accrues to whoever owns the **destination endpoints + FX spread** (TerraPay), while banks commoditise into distribution front-ends. Signing more banks makes TerraPay's corridors denser/cheaper — a flywheel that quietly turns the rail into regional infrastructure with pricing power over the very banks feeding it volume.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **NBB (est. 1957):** Bahrain's first locally owned bank; branches in Abu Dhabi and Riyadh; owns **Bahrain Islamic Bank (BisB)**. This continues a clear **digital-modernisation streak**: in Oct 2025 NBB adopted **[[National Bank of Bahrain adopts JPMorgan's Kinexys]]** (blockchain/programmable payments). Note the contrast: Kinexys was a **blockchain-native** bet; TerraPay is **pragmatic Swift-plus-API** — NBB is hedging across both modern rails rather than committing to one, which is sensible for a mid-sized bank that cannot build either itself (analysis).
- **Fit:** logical and low-risk. For a small bank, renting TerraPay's network to deepen GCC outbound and transaction-banking connectivity defends/grows a fee line without balance-sheet or build cost. Aligns with the Central Bank of Bahrain's "fintech hub" positioning.
- **+ Why NBB acts this way:** Bahrain is a small, open, expat-heavy economy competing with Dubai/Riyadh for financial-hub relevance. NBB cannot out-spend regional giants on bilateral market integrations, so buying reach is the only viable path to keep corporate clients whose needs are "becoming more global" (Munot). The structural pressure is **relevance-defence**, not expansion ambition.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** The capability (Swift ISO 20022 → TerraPay network → wallets/accounts) is not new — TerraPay has been signing banks on exactly this all year (e.g. **[[National Bank of Bahrain and TerraPay]]** has direct analogues in TerraPay–Deutsche Bank correspondent/FX, Aug 2026; Reap; TPBank; Banco Ganadero). This is a **new customer, not a new product**.
- **Traction: unproven.** Zero adoption metrics — no go-live, no live corridors, no transaction counts, no pricing. "Will leverage" ≠ live.
- **+ Who captures the margin:** TerraPay owns the endpoints, the FX conversion and the compliance/settlement layer (and leans on Deutsche Bank for USD correspondent settlement); NBB owns the customer relationship and the local funding leg. The durable per-transaction + FX-spread economics sit with **TerraPay**; NBB's gain is **defensive fee retention and client stickiness**, not a new high-margin line. The "no deductions from principal" transparency claim, if real, actually **compresses the bank's traditional lift-fee economics** — the bank trades margin for retention.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **The real signal to watch:** a go-live announcement with **named corridors** (India/Philippines/Bangladesh/Egypt/Levant for consumer; GCC/UK/US for corporate) and whether it covers **transaction-banking/B2B** flow (the Munot framing) vs retail remittance.
- **Market backdrop (sobering for a Bahrain deal):** GCC outbound remittances hit a record **~$161bn in 2025 (+13.6% YoY)** — but **Bahrain was the ONLY GCC country to decline, ~$2.5bn, −4.3%** (Arab News / Gulf News / AGBI, Oct 2026). So the *addressable domestic remittance pool is small and shrinking*; the upside must come from **corporate/transaction-banking and regional (NBB's UAE/KSA branches)** flow, not Bahrain consumer remittance — reinforcing the read that this is a transaction-banking play dressed as "international payouts."
- **Risks / silence:** non-exclusive (NBB keeps Swift/correspondents and already uses JPMorgan Kinexys); no FX-margin, fee-split or fraud/AML-liability disclosure; stablecoin rails (Circle/USDC networks) are the medium-term disintermediation threat to fiat payout networks.
- **+ Second-order:** the more GCC banks route through TerraPay, the more it becomes the **de-facto regional outbound utility** — efficient now, but concentrating pricing power and a single point of failure. For Bahrain specifically, with a declining remittance base, the deal is more about **keeping NBB's corporate clients from defecting to better-connected regional banks** than about capturing growth.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Cross-border payouts / remittance infrastructure, specifically GCC outbound corridors. Market size: GCC outward worker remittances hit a record ~$161bn in 2025, +13.6% YoY, the highest of any bloc globally — UAE $62.1bn, Saudi ~$58bn, Oman $9.5bn (per Gulf News / Economy Middle East, 2025). The GCC *cross-border payments* market (the fee pool, not the flow) is a smaller ~$9.1bn in 2025 growing to ~$15.7bn by 2032 at ~8.1% CAGR; Bahrain specifically is only ~$547m of that (per Ken Research, 2025 — secondary analyst figure, treat as indicative). Structure: fragmented at the "last-mile" (every country has its own wallets/rails) but consolidating at the infrastructure layer, where a handful of global payout networks (TerraPay, Thunes, Nium, Visa Direct, Mastercard Move) aggregate endpoints and resell connectivity. The barrier is regulatory licensing across many markets (TerraPay claims licenses in 29 countries) + the network itself. Why now: Swift completed its MT→ISO 20022 cutover on 2025-11-22 (per Swift / PaymentExpert), so banks can now attach structured ISO 20022 flows to a payout network via existing Swift + RMA keys rather than building bilateral integrations — exactly the wedge TerraPay is selling NBB.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: corridors/countries covered, endpoints reached (wallets + accounts), real-time settlement share, and take rate per transaction (none disclosed here — `[UNSOURCED]`). Key players and basis of competition = reach + distribution, not price: TerraPay (156+ countries, 7.5bn+ bank accounts, 3.7bn wallets via its Xend interop network), Thunes (130+ countries, 550+ direct integrations, 3bn+ wallets/accounts), Nium, plus card-scheme rails Visa Direct and Mastercard Move. Recent dated moves: Aljazira Bank adopted Thunes for Saudi cross-border transfers (2026-10-08) and Banque Saudi Fransi partnered Thunes (2025-10) — i.e. the GCC bank-distribution land-grab is active and TerraPay's direct competitor is winning adjacent Saudi bank deals in the same weeks. TerraPay's own GCC footprint is deep (botim, UAE remittance flows). Protagonist position: TerraPay is a reach-leader but this specific deal is a distribution win, not a scale event — NBB is a single mid-size Bahraini bank (est. 1957, branches in Abu Dhabi/Riyadh, owns Bahrain Islamic Bank). Moat `(analysis)`: network effects + multi-market licensing + switching costs once a bank wires ISO 20022 flows through one aggregator; weak pricing power because banks can and do multi-home (Thunes/Visa/Mastercard are one RFP away).

**Comps & multiples.** Private names, no listed equity — multiples not computable from free sources, so EV/Revenue etc. = "no data". Funding/valuation reference points instead:
- TerraPay — ~$195.6m raised total; last round ~$95m debt+equity (Jul 2024, IFC-led), prior ~$100m Series B (Apr 2023); valuation last pegged ~$250–500m (2023, Tracxn/Dealroom — stale, treat as floor).
- Thunes — ~$362m raised; $150m Series D (Apr 2025, Apis + Vitruvian), reportedly crossed ~$1bn valuation (vs ~$900m Jul 2023, per PYMNTS/Finovate/Fintech Magazine). → Thunes carries roughly 2–4x TerraPay's last valuation marker despite narrower country count — a reasonable `(analysis)` read is TerraPay is either under-marked on stale 2023 data or priced for its thinner balance sheet; not a clean comp without current revenue.
- Nium — FY2024 revenue S$167.2m (~US$124m), +13.3%, net loss ~S$88m flat YoY (per Kapronasia / fintechnews.sg) → the read-across is that scaling a global payout network is "ruinously expensive" and structurally loss-making at this stage; growth does not yet convert to profit.
Internal comps (base): [[Aljazira Bank adopts Thunes for cross-border transfers]], [[Thunes and Banque Saudi Fransi partner on cross-border payments]], [[botim money expands remittances via TerraPay partnership]], [[TerraPay launches Xend to power borderless payments for billions of wallet users]], [[Nium, Singapore-based fintech, narrowed annual loss to S$88M ($69M) with 13.3% revenue]]. Distribution not computed (no ≥3 comparable multiples) → qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **PR-metric inflation / de-PR.** The note's headline "12 billion endpoints" exceeds TerraPay's own stated 7.5bn accounts + 3.7bn wallets (~11.2bn) and conflates distinct endpoint types; reach claims are marketing, not audited — the economics (take rate, fraud liability, FX margin, who keeps the spread) are entirely undisclosed by both parties. Why it matters: a bank-distribution partnership with no disclosed volume commitment can be announced-not-live for a long time.
2. **Rails dependence / disintermediation.** NBB is handing the cross-border execution layer to a third-party aggregator and keeping only the customer relationship. Why: if the payout network captures the margin and the bank is reduced to front-end, the bank's cross-border fee pool erodes; conversely TerraPay sits one layer above and is itself disintermediable by Visa Direct/Mastercard Move/stablecoin rails.
3. **Competitive concentration in GCC.** Thunes is signing Saudi bank deals (Aljazira 2026-10, BSF 2025-10) in the same window; TerraPay's win at one Bahraini bank (Bahrain = ~$547m, the smallest GCC cross-border pool) is a modest foothold. Why: the real prize (UAE/Saudi bank balance sheets) is being contested and TerraPay is not visibly winning the largest corridors.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a distribution/consolidation data-point, not a re-rating: the ISO 20022 cutover is converting bank correspondent flows into addressable demand for payout-network aggregators, and GCC banks are the current battleground. Falsifiable thesis: the aggregator layer (TerraPay/Thunes) consolidates GCC bank cross-border payouts over the next 12–18 months. Trigger to watch: a disclosed *volume/corridor-live* milestone (not just a signing) from NBB, or TerraPay landing a UAE/Saudi tier-1 bank. What breaks the thesis: card schemes (Visa Direct, Mastercard Move) or stablecoin rails winning the same bank deals directly, collapsing the independent-aggregator margin.
