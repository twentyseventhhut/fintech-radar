Owlting Group (NASDAQ: OWLS) and Decaf Open U.S. Dollar Accounts Beyond the United States on OwlPay Harbor
Decaf, a global payments platform reporting US$48 million in its own monthly payment volume, has moved Decaf Business into production on OwlPay Harbor, opening dollar accounts to eligible businesses and organizations outside the United States

ARLINGTON, Va., Oct. 14, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- Owlting Group (NASDAQ: OWLS) ("Owlting" or the "Company"), the operating brand of OBOOK Holdings Inc., a global fintech company building regulated payment infrastructure, today announced that Decaf, a global financial platform that makes stablecoins usable for everyday business payments, has embedded OwlPay Harbor 1 as regulated payment infrastructure behind Decaf Business, its global business account platform, and is now opening U.S. dollar account access to eligible companies and organizations incorporated outside the United States.
Those customers can hold a U.S. dollar account in their own name, fund it, and pay suppliers, employees and contractors in the United States, without first setting up a U.S. entity.
Decaf, a payments platform that reported US$48 million in monthly payment volume, has built OwlPay Harbor into a commercial product of its own. 2 Harbor carries live payments across that platform: it moved scholarship and per diem funds for delegates at the International AIDS Conference in Rio de Janeiro in July 2026, and Decaf Business, Decaf’s company account product, has been in production on Harbor since August 2026.
Decaf owns the customer, the onboarding and the interface. OwlPay Harbor runs underneath as the licensed payment infrastructure, the layer that takes years of licensing and bank integration to assemble, and Owlting earns fees on the payment volume that moves through it. Revenue from this channel therefore grows with Decaf's commercial execution and with the payment volumes of every business and organization on its platform, without a separate sales cycle for each customer.
“An exporter in Colombia and an aid organization working in a conflict zone have the same problem: getting dollars where they need to go, on time, through systems that were never designed to work together,” said Rick Martin, CEO, Decaf . “Stablecoins solved the movement of money years ago. What was still missing is licensed access to the dollar itself, and that is what we can now put in our customers' hands.”
“Infrastructure proves itself when other people build their products on it,” said Darren Wang, Founder and CEO, Owlting Group . “The economics are different from selling accounts one at a time: every corridor we add reaches a partner’s whole customer base at once. We expect more platforms to follow.”
 What is live today 
OwlPay Harbor powers U.S. dollar payments within Decaf Business. Eligible customers, now including companies and organizations incorporated outside the United States 3 , hold a U.S. dollar account in their own name, fund it by domestic wire and ACH, and hold balances as USDC stablecoin on the Stellar network. On the way out, Harbor supports U.S. domestic payouts by wire, ACH and RTP, and card payouts to eligible U.S. debit cards through its Visa Direct integration. 4 
Customers manage the whole flow from a single dashboard, with verification applied on a risk-based model according to transaction profile, size and applicable regulatory requirements. Businesses using the service today include U.S.-registered companies in the renewable energy equipment trade whose founders operate between Latin America and the United States, and whose finance teams move funds across the United States, Latin America and Asia.
 A dollar economy that runs on dollar accounts 
Latin America and the Caribbean traded US$1.18 trillion in goods with the United States in 2024, 5 and roughly 96% of trade across the Americas has been invoiced in U.S. dollars, 6 making regional commerce a dollar economy conducted by businesses that generally find dollars difficult to hold. A Colombian exporter or a Mexican importer usually sends payment through a chain of intermediary banks, losing days to settlement and paying a fee at every step, even though the money was in dollars from the start. A dollar account they can use directly removes both, which is one reason the region posted 63% year-over-year growth in on-chain value received, second only to Asia-Pacific. 7 
Decaf operates across the region's most active stablecoin payment markets, including Colombia and Mexico, combining U.S. dollar accounts and local-currency rails with a cash network that serves businesses and individuals underserved by traditional banking. That reach gives OwlPay Harbor access to the region's trading companies, exporters, business operators and international organizations through a single connection.
 Where conventional banking does not reach 
Not all of the customers on these rails are trading companies. MedGlobal, a Chicago-based humanitarian health organization that delivers medical care in crisis-affected regions, and the International AIDS Society (IAS), the world’s largest association of HIV professionals with members in more than 170 countries, both use OwlPay Harbor through Decaf to fund and convert money for their programs, paying local staff and contractors, covering operating costs, and getting funds to the people those programs are meant to reach.
Those payments often go to destinations where conventional banking coverage is limited, including parts of the Middle East and North Africa, sub-Saharan Africa and Latin America. Decaf pairs OwlPay Harbor with its own network of local bank, card and cash payout partners, so an organization can use whichever method actually reaches a given destination. 8 
A donation program in Wyoming applies the same approach, with program funds held as stablecoins and each movement traceable on a public blockchain from arrival to payout. 9 
 What comes next 
Owlting expanded its U.S. payment coverage in September 2026, securing a money transmitter license in Connecticut and extending its payment operations to 43 U.S. states. 10 Decaf Business customers will be able to send a payout from an overseas-held dollar account straight to a recipient’s eligible non-U.S. debit card and the bank account linked to it, a capability Owlting expects to enable by the end of 2026. The same customers intend to begin paying suppliers in China and Singapore using OwlPay Harbor’s payout coverage in Asia.
The Company continues to track toward its previously stated target of surpassing US$1 billion in cumulative processed payment volume across the OwlPay platform, covering both fiat and stablecoin payments, by the end of 2026. 11 Owlting expects additional platforms to integrate OwlPay Harbor in the same way, expanding the corridors served and the volume moving across them.

# Context-enrichment: Owlting and Decaf open US dollar accounts on OwlPay Harbor
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-14 Owlting Group (NASDAQ: OWLS, operating brand of OBOOK Holdings) announced that Decaf — a stablecoin-based payments platform — has embedded **OwlPay Harbor** as the licensed payment rail behind **Decaf Business**, and is now extending **USD-account access to companies incorporated OUTSIDE the United States** (previously the product was limited to US-registered entities).

De-PR'd, the actual delta is narrow:
- Decaf Business has **already been in production on OwlPay Harbor since August 2026** (per the PR itself and OBOOK's 6-K). Harbor also carried live payments earlier (scholarship/per-diem for the International AIDS Conference, Rio, July 2026). So this is **not a new launch — it is an eligibility expansion** of an existing live product to non-US-incorporated entities. The new "news" is one onboarding-eligibility change plus restated marketing.
- The headline "US$48 million in monthly payment volume" is **Decaf's OWN volume, not Harbor's / not Owlting's**, and it is **recycled from a 2025 Stellar Community Fund Impact Report** (published 2026-03-30), i.e. a ~1-year-old third-party figure for the 2025 reporting period — not a current Harbor metric. The PR's own "Third-Party Information" disclaimer explicitly says such figures "do not represent the Company's transaction volume or revenue." So the one number anchoring the release is not Owlting's and not fresh.
- Mechanics that ARE live today: eligible non-US firms hold a USD account in their own name, fund by domestic US wire/ACH, hold balances as **USDC on Stellar**, and pay out via US wire/ACH/RTP and card via **Visa Direct** (Visa Direct tie disclosed 2026-04-09, also not new). US money-transmission is provided by OwlTing USA (NMLS 2324336), now across **43 US states** after a Connecticut MTL in Sept 2026.
- Forward items ("end-of-2026": non-US debit-card payouts; China/Singapore supplier payouts; the "$1B cumulative processed volume by end-2026" target) are all "expects / intends" — announced, not live.

**Why framed this way:** Owlting is a sub-$10M-revenue micro-cap (FY2025 revenue ~US$7.86M; H1-2026 ~US$3.87M) that direct-listed on Nasdaq in Oct 2025. With almost no own traction to show, the PR borrows a partner's year-old volume figure, big TAM statistics (LatAm–US trade of US$1.18T; 96% dollar invoicing), and NGO logos (MedGlobal, IAS) to dress an incremental eligibility change as a market event. The real model: Owlting is a wholesale **infrastructure/BaaS fee-taker** — Decaf owns the customer, Owlting earns a cut of volume, so Owlting's revenue depends entirely on Decaf's execution.

Prior corpus: [[OwlTing secures $10m with access to $50m total funding]] (2026-04) — the same channel (Connecting the Dots in Fintech) covered Owlting raising $10M (up to $50M) for OwlPay/licensing expansion; this note is the product-side follow-through of that licensing build-out.

## [1] Competitors / peers
The capability — "a dollar account for a business that has no US entity, settled over stablecoin rails" — is already a **crowded, well-funded category**, and Owlting is a late, small entrant:
- **Stripe / Bridge — Stablecoin Financial Accounts, live since May 2025 in 101 countries** (USDC + Bridge's USDB, 8 chains, ACH/wire/SEPA). This is the *same* product thesis (dollar account + stablecoin balance for non-US businesses) at global scale, from a company that paid US$1.1B for Bridge. This is the dominant reference point and it predates Owlting's version by ~17 months.
- **Brex** — native USDC payments (via Column) since Sept 2025; **Ramp** opened stablecoin accounts to US customers 21 Jul 2026 (USDC/USDT, 7 networks). (US-focused, but same rail direction.)
- **Slash / Meow** — fiat+stablecoin business banking; Slash reported ~US$1B annualized stablecoin volume early 2026 (uses Bridge-issued token).
- **Conduit** — cross-border payment infra, 100+ countries, 15+ fiat; $36M Series A (May 2025).
- **Rain** — stablecoin card issuance infra; $250M Series C (Jan 2026) at ~$1.95B valuation, 200+ partners incl. Western Union.
- LatAm-specific rails: **MoneyGram Ramps** (Stellar/USDC on/off-ramp, a Decaf partner), plus **Meta** rolling stablecoin payouts in Colombia (Apr 2026).

**Position: catching up / sub-scale.** Owlting's differentiator is not the account — it is the *regulated US money-transmission + Visa Direct + Stellar anchor* stack packaged as embeddable infra behind someone else's brand. That is a real but **commoditizing** asset: Stripe/Bridge, Conduit and Rain sell the same "we did the licensing so you don't have to" story. **Why the premium (if any) isn't name-specific:** nothing in the stack is proprietary — USDC (Circle), Stellar, Visa Direct and state MTLs are all available to competitors; the only moat is the specific corridor/partner relationships (Decaf in LatAm).

## [2] Company history / fit
Owlting began as a Taiwan hospitality/e-commerce group (OwlNest, OwlTing Market) and pivoted into cross-border payments via **OwlPay**, then stablecoins via **OwlPay Harbor** (launched May 2025 as an AI-framed USDC on/off-ramp / Stellar Anchor). It direct-listed on Nasdaq Oct 2025 (pitched as "Asia's first fintech direct listing"), raised $10M (up to $50M) in Apr 2026 for licensing/expansion ([[OwlTing secures $10m with access to $50m total funding]]), expanded Visa Direct (Apr 2026) and US state coverage to 43 states (Sept 2026).

**Why it acts this way (structural logic):** revenue is tiny (~US$7.86M FY2025) relative to the licensing/compliance cost base it is assembling. A one-customer-at-a-time account business can't service that cost structure, so Owlting is deliberately repositioning as **wholesale infrastructure** — "every corridor we add reaches a partner's whole customer base at once" (Wang quote). Economically this is the right move (software/volume multiple vs. retail-account grind), but it makes Owlting a **dependent fee-taker on partners' GMV** rather than an owner of demand — the classic BaaS tradeoff. This fits logically with the Apr-2026 capital raise earmarked for licensing: Harbor-as-a-product is what that money was supposed to build.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: low.** Nothing here is a first. Dollar accounts for non-US firms over stablecoin rails is Stripe/Bridge's 2025 product; USDC-on-Stellar is Circle/Stellar; Visa Direct payouts were already disclosed in April. The only genuinely new fact is the **eligibility extension to non-US-incorporated entities** on an already-live product.

**Traction: thin and borrowed.** The only hard number is Decaf's **US$48M/month — Decaf's own, from a 2025 report, not Harbor's and not current.** Owlting discloses **no** Harbor-specific volume, revenue, account count, or customer count. Named customers are mostly NGOs (MedGlobal, IAS) and "a renewable-energy trading company," "a Wyoming donation program" — illustrative anecdotes, not scale metrics. The "$1B cumulative processed volume by end-2026" is a *target*, not achieved.

**Who captures the margin:** Owlting takes a thin fee on volume it does not control; Decaf owns the customer, onboarding and interface; Circle earns the USDC float; Stellar/Visa earn rail fees. In this stack Owlting is the most disintermediable layer — if Decaf (or any partner) later secures its own MTLs or switches to Bridge/Conduit, Owlting's fee evaporates. **Value-add is real but structurally weak:** it saves a partner the multi-year licensing build, but that advantage decays as licensed-infra competitors multiply and as the capability commoditizes.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Stated next steps (all "expects/intends", end-2026): non-US debit-card payouts; supplier payouts into China/Singapore; crossing **US$1B cumulative processed volume** (fiat+stablecoin) across OwlPay; "more platforms to integrate Harbor."

**Why the market goes this way:** the stablecoin-dollar-account category is genuinely growing (LatAm +63% YoY on-chain value received per Chainalysis; dollar demand in inflation-prone economies is real), and GENIUS-Act-era US regulatory clarity is pulling banks/fintechs onto compliant stablecoin rails. So the *tailwind* is real. **Counterintuitive second order:** that same tailwind is what draws Stripe/Bridge, Ramp, Conduit and Rain — the bigger the category, the harder it is for a <$10M-revenue micro-cap to hold a wholesale-infra position against balance-sheet-rich incumbents who can underprice volume fees. Capital/scale concentration here makes the small embedded-infra provider *more* fragile, not safer. Key risks: partner concentration (Decaf), fee disintermediation, regulatory exposure across many state MTLs, USDC/Stellar dependency, and OWLS micro-cap volatility. Sentiment: the market/press coverage is largely PR-wire reprints; no independent analyst endorsement of traction.

Entry barrier is regulatory: US money-transmitter licensing across states is the moat OwlTing is buying (43 states / MTLs as of Sep 30 2026, per note). **Why now:** regional dollar demand — LatAm/Caribbean traded US$1.18tn in goods with the US in 2024 (ECLAC, note fn5) and ~96% of Americas trade is dollar-invoiced (Fed, 1999–2019 avg, note fn6); LatAm on-chain value +63% YoY (Chainalysis 2025, note fn7) — meets maturing USDC rails and MiCA/US licensing regimes.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: processed payment volume (TPV), take rate on volume, number of corridors/partners, licensing coverage (states/countries). The note frames OwlTing's economics explicitly as fee-on-volume via a partner's whole customer base ("every corridor we add reaches a partner's whole customer base at once") — a B2B2X infrastructure model, not direct account sales. **Key players / basis of competition:** BVNK (embedded stablecoin wallets, from zero to ~US$4bn volume in its first year; enterprise SLA/virtual-account positioning), Rain (embedded non-custodial wallets + card issuing + global payouts API), Conduit (USD/EUR/GBP virtual accounts + IBANs via partner banks), Brex (USDC payments but acquired by Capital One Apr 2026, and explicitly NOT open to non-US-registered entities), Mercury (no native stablecoin balances). **Risk flags.**
1. **Valuation vs fundamentals:** ~60x P/S on flat revenue (1H26 +0.8% YoY) — the price embeds the US$1bn-volume target and future platform integrations, so any slip in Decaf's execution or the 2026 volume target is a direct de-rating risk.
2. **Single-partner concentration / disintermediation:** channel revenue "grows with Decaf's commercial execution." Harbor is the infrastructure layer underneath — Decaf "owns the customer, the onboarding and the interface," so OwlTing is one renegotiation or partner-switch away from losing the volume; and the issuer/chain (Circle/Stellar) capture their own layer of economics.
3. **Regulatory / compliance surface:** opening US-dollar accounts to non-US-incorporated entities, routing to MENA, sub-Saharan Africa and LatAm, with "risk-based" verification — high AML/sanctions exposure; Harbor's value rests entirely on maintained MTLs (43 states) that can be revoked or fail to extend.

Falsifiable: watch 2H26 revenue and the cumulative-US$1bn-volume print by end-2026, plus whether a SECOND platform (beyond Decaf) actually embeds Harbor as management claims to expect. Thesis breaks if revenue stays flat into 2027 despite "live" volume — i.e. volume grows but OwlTing's take doesn't convert, confirming it captures too little of the stack.
