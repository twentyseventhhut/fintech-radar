zerohash Launches AUTH to Simplify Onchain Movements
On the page 
Get in touch
AUTH by zerohash turns onchain transfers into simple account-to-account experiences through 600+ connections.
 Money 20/20 USA, LAS VEGAS — Oct. 19, 2026 — zerohash , the leading onchain infrastructure provider, today announced the launch of AUTH by zerohash* , a paradigm-shifting onchain authentication and account-linking product. AUTH solves for customer usability of onchain movements including for crypto, stablecoin and tokenized securities. Customers do not have to copy alphanumeric addresses or make chain decisions. 
As onchain infrastructure scales across every asset class and moves into every mainstream financial product, usability and risk remain the biggest barriers to adoption. AUTH by zerohash solves for:
 Usability: While onchain rails are incredibly powerful, transfers rely on wallet addresses, chain selection, and manual workflows that create user friction. By structuring onchain asset movement around accounts instead of wallet addresses, AUTH lets users link self-custody and custodial accounts directly, transferring onchain assets without needing to use a raw wallet address or making a chain selection.
 Trust: AUTH replaces raw addresses with authenticated, user-permissioned accounts.
 Control: Every platform has their own risk appetite. AUTH provides the flexibility for partners to configure transfer types, onchain assets and enabled accounts.  Configurations put AUTH clients in control of the experience. 
AUTH offers 600+ connections across custodial and self-custodial accounts, representing approximately 250 million users across 190+ countries, including platforms such as Robinhood, Gemini, Coinbase (Exchange and Wallet), MetaMask , Phantom, and more. 
To lead this next phase of growth, zerohash has also named Ryan Beaudry as General Manager of AUTH. Most recently, Ryan was Executive Vice President of Global Product & Operations for Open Finance at Mastercard, where he scaled open finance across nearly 100 million connected accounts and 10,000+ applications, powering 15 billion API calls serviced globally annually. 
“Onchain rails scale when users stop thinking about the underlying technology. Usability, trust and control is the inflection point for mass adoption,” said Ryan Beaudry, GM of AUTH. “AUTH removes the sharp edges from onchain transfers and replaces them with experiences people already trust.”
 Deep Dive: How AUTH Works 
Once an account is linked, AUTH handles authentication and settlement automatically, with no wallet addresses or blockchain selection. Transfers follow a user-friendly, secure flow:
 Select connection: User selects a custodial or self-custodial account to link.
 Authenticate: Login with account credentials and multi-factor authentication.
 Transfer: Assets move directly onchain.
 Near-Instant movement: Onchain transfer completes.

# Context-enrichment: Zerohash launches AUTH to simplify onchain account linking
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 19 Oct 2026 at Money 20/20 USA (Las Vegas), Zerohash launched **AUTH by Zerohash** — an account-linking + authentication layer for onchain transfers. Instead of copying a raw wallet address and picking a chain, a user logs into a linked custodial/self-custodial account (credentials + MFA) and assets move onchain account-to-account. Zerohash calls it "paradigm-shifting"; de-PR'd, it is an **abstraction/UX layer** over existing rails — not a new chain, settlement protocol, or asset.
- **The headline number is "connections," not usage.** "600+ connections … ~250M users across 190+ countries, incl. Robinhood, Gemini, Coinbase (Exchange & Wallet), MetaMask, Phantom." The 250M is the *addressable user base of the connected platforms*, not AUTH users or AUTH transfer volume. Classic reach-vs-adoption inflation.
- **Live vs announced is mixed inside the release itself.** tastytrade's quote says "Once we connect, AUTH … will deliver…" — i.e. tastytrade is NOT live yet. So "600+ connections" blends live integrations with signed/pipeline partners. No transfer volume, no linked-account count, no conversion metric is disclosed.
- **Why structured this way:** AUTH is secured by **Connect Ltd. (a Bermuda affiliate of Zerohash)**, and users must accept Connect's T&Cs. Routing the account-linking/auth entity through Bermuda (not the US regulated entities) signals a deliberate regulatory-perimeter choice: keep the sensitive "hold credentials / broker the link" function offshore, outside the 51-jurisdiction US money-transmitter stack. Worth flagging — login-credential custody is the risk-dense part.
- **Governance tell:** new GM Ryan Beaudry came from **Mastercard, EVP Global Product & Ops for Open Finance** (~100M connected accounts, 10k+ apps, 15B API calls/yr). AUTH is explicitly modeled as **"open banking / Plaid-style account linking, but for onchain."** That framing is the real product thesis.

## [1] Competitors / peers
The problem AUTH attacks (address-copy friction, chain selection) has many partial solvers:
- **Circle CCTP** — abstracts cross-chain USDC (burn/mint) so users don't bridge manually; live on major chains, deep wallet support (MetaMask, OKX). Solves *chain selection* for USDC, not *account linking*.
- **ENS / human-readable addresses** — removes alphanumeric copy, but still address-centric and self-custody only.
- **Coinbase Wallet link-sends / username sends, PayPal-style crypto links** — remove address copy within a walled garden; not cross-platform/cross-custodian.
- **Account abstraction (EIP-7702, MetaMask Smart Accounts), intent routers (Eco/ERC-7683)** — improve self-custody UX but don't bridge custodial exchange accounts.
- **BVNK (now Mastercard)** — stablecoin payment infra, the direct strategic rival now sitting inside Mastercard.
**Position:** AUTH's differentiator is spanning **custodial AND self-custodial** accounts cross-platform via authenticated login — closest analogue is **Plaid for onchain**, which nobody owns at scale yet. That is genuinely white-space. **But** the margin/lock-in question (analysis): account-linking aggregators are a thin layer; value accrues to whoever owns the *rails + settlement*, and incumbents (Visa/Mastercard, Circle) control those. Second-order risk: AUTH could become a commoditized directory while the economics sit elsewhere.

## [2] Company history / fit
Zerohash is a "leading onchain infrastructure provider" (crypto/stablecoin/tokenized assets via API + MCP suite; regulated in EU, LatAm, Bermuda, 51 US jurisdictions). Trajectory: raised **$104M Series D-2 at ~$1B (Sep 2025, led by Interactive Brokers)**; **walked away from a Mastercard takeover** (~$2B) in early 2026; Mastercard then weighed a minority stake, then **dropped it after acquiring BVNK (~$1.8B)**; Zerohash since pursuing a new round at **>$1.5B** valuation. Clients incl. Morgan Stanley (E*Trade), Interactive Brokers, Stripe, BlackRock BUIDL, Franklin Templeton, DraftKings. 2026 product cadence: Monad/USDC (Feb), Staking-as-a-Service (Jun), Tempo validator seat (Oct), Selini liquidity (Oct), now AUTH (Oct). See [[Zerohash in talks to raise $250M after dropping Mastercard deal]], [[Zerohash pursues funding above $1.5 billion valuation]], [[zerohash named a Tempo validator for stablecoin rails]], [[Zerohash launches Staking-as-a-Service via single API]].
**Why this move:** having rejected acquisition and chasing a software-multiple raise, Zerohash needs to show it's a **platform/network, not a pipe**. Pure API take-rate is commodity; an account-linking network with 600+ connections is a **moat/multiplier story** to justify >$1.5B. AUTH is as much a fundraising narrative as a product.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Really new:** a cross-platform, cross-custodial account-linking standard for onchain transfers — "Plaid for onchain." The mechanism delta vs incumbents (one sentence): competitors abstract *chains or addresses within one domain*; AUTH abstracts the *account relationship across custodial and self-custodial domains* via authenticated login. That is a real conceptual step.
**Traction, de-PR'd:** essentially **unproven**. Disclosed = connections and partner logos; NOT disclosed = AUTH linked-account count, transfer volume/GMV, conversion lift, or go-live dates per partner (tastytrade explicitly "once we connect"). So this is a **launch/announcement, not demonstrated adoption**.
**Why value-add may be real or not (deeper):** real value = removing the #1 retail friction (wrong-address loss) and enabling exchange↔wallet flows that exchanges historically gate. **But who captures margin?** AUTH sits above rails it doesn't own; if it only authenticates, it's a directory with weak pricing power. Value is durable only if AUTH also captures settlement/FX/compliance economics on each linked transfer (where Zerohash's regulated-entity + stablecoin-rail stack could plug in). The unanswered question is whether AUTH monetizes the *transfer* or just the *link*.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect Zerohash to (a) convert "connections" to live go-lives and publish usage, (b) tie AUTH into its Tempo validator + stablecoin payout rails and Airtm destination layer for end-to-end payouts, and (c) use AUTH as the headline for the **>$1.5B raise**. Backdrop: stablecoin/onchain infra is hot (GENIUS Act era, Visa Direct/Marqeta/Worldpay + Tempo), and "usability" is the consensus next battleground — favorable sentiment. **Risks / counterintuitive second-order:** (1) credential-linking + an offshore (Bermuda/Connect) auth entity is a **fraud/account-takeover and regulatory-scrutiny magnet** — a single high-profile linked-account drain would hit harder than an address typo; (2) Mastercard (now owning BVNK) and Circle could replicate the linking layer and bundle it with rails they already control, squeezing an independent aggregator; (3) exchanges may resist letting a third party broker outbound transfers that cannibalize their own withdrawal UX/retention. Net: strong thesis, early proof.

## Top challenge / red-team questions (second-order)
_Importance: 3/5._

1. **"600+ connections / 250M users" — is that AUTH usage or partner reach?** Reach. It's the addressable base of connected platforms, not AUTH linked accounts or transfer volume. Inflation flag. **Open** on actual AUTH adoption.
2. **Live or announced?** Mixed — tastytrade's own quote says "once we connect … will deliver," so some "connections" are signed/pipeline, not live. No per-partner go-live dates disclosed. Launch ≠ adoption.
3. **What transfer volume / GMV / linked-account count has AUTH processed?** Not disclosed. The one metric that would prove value is absent. **Open.**
4. **Why is AUTH secured by Connect Ltd. (Bermuda), not Zerohash's US entities?** Deliberate regulatory-perimeter choice — the credential-holding/linking function sits offshore, outside the 51-jurisdiction US MTL stack. Raises the "who's liable for a linked-account drain" question. **Open** on fraud-liability allocation.
5. **Is this actually new, or Plaid/open-banking account linking rebadged for crypto?** Conceptually new (cross-custodial + self-custodial onchain), but the architecture is explicitly open-banking-style (GM is ex-Mastercard Open Finance). Novelty is the *application to onchain*, not the primitive.
6. **What already did the same?** Circle CCTP (chain abstraction for USDC), ENS (readable addresses), Coinbase link-sends, account abstraction (EIP-7702) — all partial, single-domain. None span custodial↔self-custodial cross-platform. AUTH's whitespace is real.
7. **Who captures the margin — does AUTH monetize the transfer or just the link?** Unknown and decisive. A pure directory/auth layer has weak pricing power; durable value requires capturing settlement/FX/compliance economics per transfer. **Open.**
8. **Can Mastercard (now owning BVNK) or Circle just replicate the linking layer and bundle it with rails they control?** Plausibly yes — this is the disintermediation risk. An independent aggregator above owned rails is squeezable.
9. **Will exchanges cooperate long-term?** Letting a third party broker outbound transfers can cannibalize withdrawal UX / retention. Current partner logos may reflect launch goodwill, not durable commitment. **Open.**
10. **Is the fraud surface bigger, not smaller?** Yes (analysis): shifting from address-copy to credential-login + MFA moves the attack vector from fat-finger to account-takeover; a single publicized linked-account drain is reputationally worse than a typo.
11. **Is AUTH primarily a fundraising narrative?** Partly — Zerohash is raising at >$1.5B and needs a network/platform story over commodity API take-rate. AUTH is the moat headline as much as a product.
12. **What's the real moat vs a standards body / open protocol?** Network effects of 600+ connections — but if an open standard (an ERC) emerges, the aggregator advantage erodes. **Open.**
13. **Does the Bermuda T&C requirement add user friction / consent fatigue that offsets the UX win?** Each use requires agreeing to Connect's terms — a UX and trust wrinkle the PR glosses. **Open.**
14. **Freshness — is this a reprint of a prior Zerohash note?** No. Distinct new product (AUTH, 19 Oct 2026) vs Tempo validator, Selini liquidity, Staking-aaS, funding notes. **Fresh.**
15. **Why does it matter at second order?** If AUTH wins, Zerohash shifts from "crypto API vendor" to owning the *account-linking graph* for onchain money — the Plaid position. If it stays a directory, it's a feature incumbents absorb. The central question is graph-ownership + transfer monetization, not "did they ship a UX tool."
