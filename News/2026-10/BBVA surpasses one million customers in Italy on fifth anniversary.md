# BBVA surpasses one million customers in Italy on its fifth anniversary

BBVA has surpassed one million customers in Italy as it marks five years since entering the country's retail banking market. This milestone confirms the strong reception of its 100% digital banking model, where the key goal is to become the primary bank for Italian customers, as BBVA Chair Carlos Torres Vila highlighted during his visit to celebrate the anniversary. BBVA is reaffirming its long-term commitment to the Italian market, where it also has a significant Corporate and Investment Banking (CIB) business, supporting Italian companies in their growth and international expansion plans.

###### In the photo: Walter Rizzi, Head of Digital Banking at BBVA Italy; Carlos Torres, Chair of BBVA; Alberto Maroni, Head of BBVA CIB Italy; and Murat Kalkan, Global Head of Digital Banks at BBVA, yesterday during the BBVA Chair’s visit to Italy.

#### BBVA launches its partnership with Inter and unveils new offers in Italy

"We have reached one million customers in Italy, reflecting the success of our 100% digital banking model. We are now entering a new phase in Italy, focused on becoming our customers' primary bank and deepening our relationships with them. Our commitment to Italy is long-term, and we want to continue growing in the country," said **Carlos Torres Vila**, Chair of BBVA.

Since its launch in October 2021, **BBVA has established a banking proposition in Italy that combines the simplicity** and agility of a fully digital experience with the strength and broad range of financial products and services offered by an international banking group.

BBVA Italy's value proposition combines attractive deposit remuneration and cashback promotions on card purchases with a comprehensive range of financing products, credit cards, including personal loans and mortgages, as well as savings and investment solutions, such as mutual funds and exchange-traded funds (ETFs). This comprehensive offering is driving greater customer engagement with BBVA, with an increasing number of customers choosing the bank as their primary financial institution to manage their day-to-day finances.

"Our real success lies in the quality of the relationships we are building with our customers in Italy. Customers who join BBVA stay with us and increasingly trust us to manage their everyday finances, their savings and their major life projects through mortgages and loans," said **Walter Rizzi,**Head of Digital Banking at BBVA Italy.

Beyond digital banking, BBVA has been present in Italy for more than four decades through its **Corporate and Investment Banking (CIB)** business, which it aims to continue strengthening in the coming years. Through financing solutions, capital markets services and advisory expertise, BBVA supports Italian companies in their growth and international expansion plans, helping them connect with strategic markets such as the United States and Latin America.

This activity is underpinned by a specialized business model that combines deep knowledge of the local market with the Group's global presence. BBVA's priorities also include supporting Italian companies in their energy transition projects and the development of sustainable infrastructure, as well as their investments in artificial intelligence and digital transformation.

## Digital Banks

The success of BBVA's retail banking business in Italy is demonstrating the strength of the Group's strategy of growing through digital banks in Europe, leveraging its **technological capabilities** and expertise in digital transformation to enter new markets efficiently and at scale.

The Italian experience laid the foundation for **BBVA's expansion into Germany,** where the Group launched its digital banking model in June 2025, surpassing its first 100,000 customers before completing its first year of operations.

“Our digital banks are seeing a very positive reception in Italy and Germany, where BBVA combines the innovation and convenience of neobanks with the financial strength, security and broad range of products of a bank with nearly**170 years of history and 80 million customers worldwide**. Our efforts are now focused on strengthening our business in these two markets, with the aim of achieving sustainable and profitable growth,” said **Murat Kalkan,** Global Head of Digital Banks at BBVA.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 23 Oct 2026 BBVA announced it has surpassed **one million retail customers in Italy**, five years after launching its fully digital bank there in October 2021. BBVA Chair Carlos Torres Vila visited Milan to mark the anniversary. The bank framed this as entering "a new phase" focused on becoming customers' *primary* bank. BBVA's value proposition pairs attractive deposit remuneration (currently **4%, raised to 4.25% on new liquidity from 1 Oct 2026**, with a rate commitment extended to 2030) and card cashback with a now-broad product set: personal loans, mortgages, credit cards, mutual funds and ETFs.

**[1] Why it matters.** The 1M milestone validates BBVA's thesis that an incumbent (nearly 170 years old, ~80M customers worldwide) can grow organically in new European markets via a greenfield 100%-digital bank, combining neobank UX with a universal-bank balance sheet and product range. Italy was the pilot and template for the Group's broader European digital-expansion strategy; its success "laid the foundation" for the **Germany** launch (June 2025). The move up the value chain into mortgages, loans and investments (vs. a deposits-and-cards hook) is the key story: deposit-led neobanks are cheap to acquire but hard to monetize, so the real test is engagement and primary-bank status, which drive lending and fee income.

**[2] Trajectory & context.** BBVA Italy targeted 1M customers by 2026 back in Dec 2025 (see [[BBVA Italy expands lending and investments to become digital bank]]) — this hits that goal on schedule. Reported momentum: +35% customer base since ~Nov 2025, financing granted multiplied ~8x, ~1,800 mortgages originated. BBVA also launched a **multi-year sponsorship with Inter Milan** (official sleeve partner) in late Sep/Oct 2026 to lift brand/engagement. Leadership: Walter Rizzi heads Digital Banking Italy (appointed 2025, see [[BBVA appoints Walter Rizzi head of digital banking in Italy]]); Murat Kalkan is Global Head of Digital Banks; Alberto Maroni heads BBVA CIB Italy (BBVA has had a CIB presence in Italy for 40+ years).

**[3] Competitive landscape.** BBVA Italy's 1M is modest beside **Revolut**, which passed **6M Italian customers** (9 Oct 2026) — Italy is Revolut's 4th-largest market globally. **N26** operates across ~23 EU markets with ~4M+ active users. Incumbent-backed digital play **Isybank** (Intesa Sanpaolo) is also scaling. BBVA's differentiation is the full universal-bank product set and balance-sheet strength rather than raw user count; its stated aim is "sustainable and profitable growth," not land-grab. Germany comparison: BBVA passed 100k customers before completing its first year there (see [[BBVA launches fully digital bank in Germany]] and [[BBVA appoints Jurgen von der Lehr to head German digital bank]]), with ~90% activated/funded and ~60% monthly-active — unusually high engagement for a neobank.

**[4] Watch next.** (a) Any disclosed P&L / breakeven timeline for the Italian unit — BBVA has not published Italy-specific profitability, only Group record profit (~€10bn FY2024). (b) Whether primary-bank conversion (payroll, direct deposit, mortgage attach) actually rises, not just headline accounts. (c) Deposit-rate cost: 4–4.25% teaser rates compress net interest margin as ECB rates fall; sustainability of the offer. (d) Germany pacing toward its own 1M-by-end-2026 target. (e) Whether BBVA extends the Italy/Germany digital-bank template to a third European market.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. "One million customers" — is this *opened accounts* or *active/funded* customers? BBVA's Germany disclosures carefully separate the two (~90% activated there); the Italy release gives no active-rate, so the headline may overstate real engagement.
2. Is the 1M an all-time cumulative tally or net of churn? Deposit-teaser (4–4.25%) acquisition is notoriously rate-shoppy; how many accounts stay once the promo rate normalizes?
3. No Italy-specific P&L is disclosed. Is the unit profitable, or still loss-making and subsidized by Group? "Sustainable and profitable growth" is stated as an *aim*, implying it isn't there yet.
4. How much of the growth is bought via 4%+ deposit rates and the Inter Milan sponsorship vs. organic/product-led? High-cost deposits + marketing can inflate counts cheaply.
5. Context/size check: 1M in Italy vs. Revolut's 6M — is BBVA a marginal #4–5 player rather than a market mover? Does the milestone matter competitively?
6. "Primary bank" claim: what share of the 1M actually use BBVA for payroll/day-to-day vs. parking savings for yield? The release gives no primary-relationship metric.
7. Mortgage/lending figures (~1,800 mortgages, 8x financing growth) are off a tiny base — is this material, or a vanity denominator effect?
8. The 4.25% new-liquidity rate committed to 2030 could squeeze NIM sharply as ECB cuts; is this a sustainable offer or a margin trap?
9. Timing/framing: milestone announced during the Inter Milan partnership launch and a Chair visit — is "1M" a genuine threshold hit now, or rounded up for a PR moment (release says "approaching" in some coverage)?
10. The "Italy laid the foundation for Germany" narrative — is the template actually transferable, or is Italy a one-off? Germany is earlier-stage (100k).
11. Any regulatory/licensing nuance — does BBVA Italy operate as a branch of the Spanish bank or a separately capitalized entity? Affects deposit guarantees and standalone economics.
12. Is the 1M figure independently verifiable, or sole-sourced to BBVA's own press release? (It is BBVA-sourced; no regulator/third-party confirmation.)
13. Does the aggregated note text (deposits + cashback + loans/mortgages/investments) match the source? Yes — consistent; no cross-wiring with another entity.
14. Could this be a duplicate of the Dec 2025 "reach 1M by 2026" note? No — that was a *target*; this is the *achievement*. Distinct event, fresh.

**Importance: 3/5** — A solid, on-strategy milestone that validates BBVA's European greenfield digital-bank playbook and the Italy→Germany template, with a credible move up the product value chain. But it is a company-sourced headline with no profitability or active-user disclosure, and 1M is small next to Revolut's 6M in the same market. Strategically meaningful for watching incumbent-led neobank expansion in Europe; not a market-moving or standalone-financial event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Italian retail banking is a large, consolidated incumbent market (Intesa Sanpaolo, UniCredit) into which EU neobanks have pushed aggressively since ~2020. Secondary estimates put neobanks at roughly a quarter of Italian banking relationships in 2026 (per aggregator citations, as of Oct 2026 — treat as order-of-magnitude, not an audited share). BBVA Italy launched Oct 2021 as a 100% digital bank and crossed 1M customers at its fifth anniversary (per BBVA, 23 Oct 2026) — a ~200k net adds/year run-rate (analysis). Drivers / "why now": (1) BBVA is running a deliberate "greenfield digital bank" playbook across Europe — Italy was the template, Germany followed in Jun 2025 and passed 100k customers inside its first year (per BBVA); (2) the stated pivot from pure acquisition to *primary-bank* status (deposits + mortgages + investments) signals the land-grab phase is giving way to an engagement/monetisation phase. Second-order effect: primary-bank status is where deposit funding and cross-sell margin actually accrue — customer count alone is a vanity metric until primacy is proven.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: total vs *active/primary* customers, deposit balances, and cross-sell (products per customer); customer count is the headline but primacy is the real economics. Players in Italy: incumbents Intesa Sanpaolo, UniCredit, Fineco, Mediolanum; disruptors Revolut, N26, Hype, Illimity. Basis of competition: price (deposit remuneration + card cashback — exactly BBVA Italy's wedge) and product breadth (mortgages, ETFs). Recent dated moves: Revolut reached 4M Italian customers and claimed "fifth-largest bank in Italy" (Sep 2025) and 5M (May 2026); N26 ~5M active customers group-wide (2026). BBVA's position vs these: **behind on raw scale** — at 1M it is roughly a fifth of Revolut's Italian base — but differentiated by a full-group balance sheet (loans/mortgages/CIB, ~170yr history, 80M customers worldwide per BBVA) rather than a thin app. Moat (analysis): switching costs via primacy + balance-sheet lending capacity a thin-capital neobank can't easily match; not network effects.

**Comps & multiples.** BBVA is a listed universal bank, so the group P/E — not a neobank revenue multiple — is the meaningful figure; the Italy digital bank is a small, non-separately-disclosed sliver, so a standalone Italy multiple = no data.
- **BBVA (group):** market cap ~$138bn (companiesmarketcap, Oct 2026); H1-2026 net attributable profit €6.05bn (per BBVA), ~€12bn annualised → trailing P/E reported ~13x (macrotrends/Yahoo, Sep 2026). In-line with large European banks.
- **Revolut (peer, private):** $75bn secondary valuation (Aug 2025, per Revolut) and a reported $115bn secondary (22 Jul 2026, per press); FY2025 revenue $6.0bn, net profit $1.7bn (per Revolut annual report). P/S at $115bn = $115bn / $6.0bn = **~19x**; P/E = $115bn / $1.7bn = **~68x**. These are *private round/secondary* valuations, not market caps, and price a global growth story — not comparable to BBVA Italy.
- The gap (BBVA ~13x P/E as a profitable incumbent vs Revolut ~68x on secondary marks) is the valuation chasm between "bank that happens to run a digital arm" and "fintech priced on growth." BBVA's 1M-in-Italy milestone does not move its group multiple.
- Internal comps: [[Revolut reaches 5 million customers in Italy]], [[Revolut claims fifth-largest bank status in Italy with 4M users]], [[N26 passes 275,000 customers in Belgium, eyes under-18s]], [[Connecting the Dots Europe's neobanks move into mortgages]].

**Risk flags.**
1. **Scale laggard in a crowded market** — at 1M, BBVA is ~5x behind Revolut (5M) in the same country; late-mover subscale digital banks struggle to reach the cost-income breakeven that primacy requires. Why: fixed-cost tech/compliance base amortises poorly over a small book.
2. **Deposit-remuneration + cashback as the acquisition wedge** — the customers bought with promotional yield/cashback are the least sticky and most margin-dilutive; the Oct-2026 "become primary bank" language is a tell that primacy (and therefore profitability) is *not yet* proven. Why: rate-sensitive deposits reprice away the moment BBVA trims the offer or rates fall.
3. **Profitability opacity** — BBVA discloses group profit but not Italy digital-bank P&L or whether the Italian unit is loss-making after five years; a 1M headline with no unit economics is PR, not evidence of a working model. [UNSOURCED] Italy-level cost-income / breakeven.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a "phase-change" milestone, not a re-rating: BBVA is signalling its greenfield-digital-bank model (Italy → Germany) is repeatable, which matters more for its European growth narrative than the 1M number itself. Falsifiable thesis (analysis): if BBVA Italy is a durable model, watch for it to disclose *active/primary* customer and deposit figures — and ideally Italy-unit profitability — within 12 months; continued reliance on total-customer vanity counts and promotional cashback would suggest the unit is still buying growth rather than earning it. Catalyst: next BBVA investor day / Germany's second-year customer update.
