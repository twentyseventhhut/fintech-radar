## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened (2026-10-22).** London-based B2B payments fintech Sokin launched **Balance Boosts**, letting *business* customers earn yield on idle balances held in its multi-currency account. Two modes: an **Easy Access Boost** (variable daily rate, withdraw anytime, no lock-up) and a **Fixed term Boost** (rate locked upfront for terms of **60 / 90 / 180 / 360 days**, up to **4.15% APY**). No fees, no maximum deposit, minimum ~$1,000 per Boost. Rates quoted as correct "as of 22 Sept 2026" and move with central-bank rates. Eligibility is restricted to Sokin Business customers with a USD/GBP/EUR multi-currency account (source: sokin.com/balance-boosts).

**What Sokin is.** Cross-border B2B payments/treasury platform founded 2019 by CEO Vroon Modgill; sends/exchanges 70+ currencies, holds balances in 26. It is **not a bank** — it operates as an **EMI / payment institution** (UK via Plata Capital UK Ltd, FCA-authorised EMI, and historically as a Modulr distributor; EEA via Plata Capital Europe AS, registered with Norway's FSA). Funding history (priors): [[Sokin raises $50M Series B for global payments]] (Dec 2025, Prysm Capital-led, ~$300M valuation, ex-PayPal execs Marino/Britto investing), [[Sokin secures $100m debt facility from Oxford Finance]] (Jan/Feb 2026), [[Sokin launches stablecoin capabilities for hybrid finance platform]] (Mar 2026), [[Sokin and Adyen partner on US payments and treasury]] (Apr 2026), [[Sokin launches Checkout and Payment Links for UK businesses]] (Jul 2026), plus CFO hire [[UK paytech Sokin names Tom Steer as CFO]] and sponsorships (Man United, PTO). Total raised ~$96M.

**How the yield / safeguarding actually works.** Because an EMI cannot pay interest from a deposit-taking balance sheet, Sokin states Boosts are "provided in partnership with established, global financial institutions." Funds are segregated and safeguarded under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011 — placed with reputable banking partners — but, crucially, **e-money products are NOT covered by FSCS** (the UK £85k deposit guarantee). This is the central risk/marketing gap: "your funds are protected" refers to safeguarding/segregation, not to deposit insurance. Where the yield is generated (money-market fund vs. partner-bank deposit vs. interest-rate spread captured by Sokin) is not disclosed on the product page.

**Competitive positioning.** This puts Sokin into the fast-growing "idle-cash / treasury yield for business accounts" race. Closest comparable priors/peers: **Airwallex Yield** (up to ~3.70% on idle USD via a J.P. Morgan government money-market fund, tiered 3.18–3.45% APY, no lock-up), **Wise Business** (Wise Interest, variable, limited currencies), while **Revolut Business** notably offers no comparable yield. Adjacent stablecoin-earn wave the DB is already tracking: [[Sentora onchain earn balances tend to stay put]] (sentUSD vault ~7.63% 30-day APY) and [[Fintech Brainfoo: stablecoin earn becomes the killer app]] (treasury balances earning ~5% via stablecoins). Sokin's 4.15% top rate is competitive vs. Airwallex but it is a *fixed-term* (360-day) headline rate, not instant-access, so the comparison is not apples-to-apples.

**Dated timeline.**

- Dec 2025 — $50M Series B, ~$300M valuation.
- Jan–Feb 2026 — $100M debt facility (Oxford Finance).
- Mar 2026 — stablecoin capabilities launched.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. The "up to 4.15% APY" is a **360-day fixed-term, single-currency** headline — what are the actual rates for shorter terms (60/90/180) and for GBP vs EUR vs USD? The marketing rate flatters a product most SMEs won't fully use.
2. Is this even "news"? Sokin's own H1-2026 blog describes Boosts as already live with Easy Access "in preparation." Was 22 Oct a true launch, a GA of Easy Access, or a newsletter re-surfacing (single channel, single mention)?
3. **FSCS gap:** funds are safeguarded (EMR 2011), NOT FSCS-insured. Does Sokin's page make that distinction clearly enough, or does "funds are protected / banking partners you can rely on" risk misleading SMEs into assuming deposit-guarantee cover?
4. Where does the yield come from — a money-market fund (like Airwallex's JPM MMF), a partner-bank deposit, or Sokin capturing a spread? Undisclosed = unquantifiable credit/liquidity risk for the customer.
5. Who are the "established global financial institutions" providing the Boosts? Unnamed partners mean the customer cannot assess counterparty risk or where their cash actually sits.
6. For a **fixed-term** Boost, what happens if the customer needs the cash early — is there any break option, penalty, or is it fully locked to maturity? (Page implies fully locked.)
7. Variable "easy access" daily rate — is it a teaser that can be cut unilaterally ("subject to change depending on Central Bank rate change")? No floor is stated.
8. $1,000 minimum per Boost with "no maximum" — but is there a per-customer or per-partner concentration cap for safeguarding, and what if a single partner bank fails while holding uninsured EMI funds?
9. How is interest taxed/reported for the business, and is "APY" computed on a 360 vs 365 day basis — does the quoted figure survive compounding assumptions?
10. Competitive reality: Airwallex Yield (~3.70% instant-access MMF) and stablecoin-earn (~5–7%) already exist. Is a 4.15% *locked* rate actually differentiated, or is Sokin late to a crowded idle-cash race?
11. Regulatory: can an FCA-authorised EMI legally market "earn interest/APY" on e-money balances, or is this structurally a referral into a third-party product (which changes who is liable if returns don't materialise)?
12. Geographic availability is "subject to region" and Sokin "reserves the right to withdraw" — how many markets can actually access this today vs. the "global" framing?
13. Single source = Sokin's own product page + one trade newsletter. No independent reporting, no customer uptake data, no AUM/balance figures. Promotional, not substantiated.

**Importance: 2/5** — A credible, logical treasury extension for a mid-size ($300M-valued) B2B payments EMI, broadening it from payments into idle-cash yield where Airwallex already leads. But it is a product-feature launch from a non-systemic player, single-channel/single-mention, with a material (and under-explained) FSCS/safeguarding distinction and undisclosed yield mechanics. Noteworthy as a data point in the "business account yield" trend, not market-moving on its own.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a B2B treasury-yield product on an e-money/multi-currency account, NOT consumer savings — only for Sokin Business customers holding a multi-currency account (per note / sokin.com/balance-boosts). The driver is a 2024–26 "yield-on-idle-cash" land-grab among payment EMIs: with policy rates still elevated, cross-border payment fintechs are bolting interest/treasury features onto balances that previously sat earning nothing, to lift balance retention and net revenue. Peer timeline: Airwallex launched Yield in Singapore (2025-07, post-MAS) and in the US (2026-03); Wise rolled out a multi-currency interest feature (Canada, 2026-05) and now pays ~3.4% variable on USD/GBP/EUR (per Wise, via wise.com); Airwallex Yield quotes up to ~3.69% on USD as a 7-day annualised money-market-fund rate net of fees, embedding J.P. Morgan Asset Management MMFs (per Airwallex). "Why now": the UK's CASS 15 supplementary safeguarding regime took effect 2026-05-07 (per FCA), tightening how EMIs hold/segregate client funds — so yield features are increasingly a competitive differentiator precisely as the balances behind them come under stricter rules. No reliable TAM for "EMI balance-yield" specifically — no data.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for payment EMIs: balances held / deposit retention, net interest or MMF spread captured, take rate on FX/payments, TPV. Key players offering balance yield to businesses: Airwallex (Yield, MMF-backed, ~3.69% USD), Wise (variable interest, ~3.4%), Venn (high-yield account for Canadian businesses — see internal comp), Tide (dedicated SME investment account, 2026-07). Revolut Business historically did not offer a yield feature, and as of 2026-03 Revolut holds a full UK banking licence (FSCS-covered deposits) — a structurally different proposition. Basis of competition: headline rate, lock-up flexibility, currency coverage, and crucially fund protection. Sokin's position: catching up / fast-follower. Its headline "up to 4.15% APY" is a fixed-term (60–360 day lock) rate and sits at/above peers' variable rates — but it is NOT like-for-like: peers' ~3.4–3.7% figures are variable/instant-access, so the 4.15% is partly compensation for the lock-up, not a clean rate win (analysis). Moat: thin — the yield is sourced from "established, global financial institutions" / banking partners (per sokin.com), i.e. Sokin is a distribution layer over someone else's rails, so no durable rate advantage. Sokin context: founded 2019, London-based, ~$8bn payments processed annually, 70+ currencies, claimed 100% YoY revenue growth (per Sokin/Finovate); regulated UK/EU/US/Canada/Australia/India.

**Comps & multiples.** Internal comps (balance-yield / treasury for businesses):

Valuation anchor: Sokin raised a $50M Series B (Dec 2025, led by Prysm Capital) at a ~$300m post-money, plus a $100m Oxford Finance debt facility (per PYMNTS/Finovate/Finextra). Trading multiples not computable: Sokin is private with no disclosed revenue in absolute terms (only "100% YoY" and "$8bn processed"), so EV/Revenue, P/S etc. = no data / [UNSOURCED]. Rate comparison (not a valuation multiple): Sokin fixed-term up to 4.15% vs Airwallex ~3.69% USD variable vs Wise ~3.4% variable — Sokin headline-rich but term-locked, so in-line-to-slightly-above on a liquidity-adjusted basis (analysis).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Where the yield comes from / sustainability.** Sokin does not disclose the underlying asset (MMF vs partner-bank deposit vs term placement). The rate is "subject to change depending on the Central Bank rate change" and rates are "correct as of 22 Sep 2026" (per sokin.com) — so the 4.15% is explicitly rate-cycle dependent; a cutting cycle compresses or removes the product and the spread Sokin earns. Second-order: if Sokin guarantees a fixed term rate but its partner reprices, Sokin bears the gap — margin/liability risk it is silent on.
2. **Deposit protection gap (de-PR'd).** Sokin leans hard on "funds are safeguarded / segregated / protected" language but as an EMI this is safeguarding under FCA CASS 15, NOT FSCS/FDIC deposit insurance. Business customers chasing 4.15% are taking EMI + banking-partner credit risk on potentially large, uninsured, locked balances (no max deposit; ~$1,000 min). This is the material unstated risk — peers Wise/Airwallex carry the identical caveat, but the fixed-term lock makes Sokin's version worse on liquidity.
3. **Dependence on someone else's rails + regulatory load.** The economics depend on third-party "global financial institutions"; Sokin captures only a spread it doesn't control, and CASS 15 (live since 2026-05-07) raises the compliance/operational cost of holding these balances. Concentration/counterparty exposure to those unnamed partners is undisclosed.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Not a re-rating event — it is table-stakes catch-up: Sokin is matching Airwallex/Wise so idle-balance flight doesn't erode its payments franchise, and converting float into retention + spread revenue ahead of a possible rate-cut cycle. Falsifiable thesis: balance-yield becomes a commodity feature across payment EMIs within ~12 months, competed to the rate floor, so the differentiator shifts to protection/transparency, not headline APY. Watch triggers: (a) first central-bank cut and whether Sokin's 4.15% survives it; (b) disclosure of the yield source / partner (would de-risk flag 1); (c) whether Sokin ever obtains a banking licence like Revolut (2026-03) to offer FSCS-covered deposits instead of safeguarded balances.
