Fintech argentina Cocos lanza Cocos Crypto, una app independiente para operar y pagar con activos digitales
La Fintech argentina Cocos lanzó Cocos Crypto , el servicio de criptomonedas que ofrece desde febrero de 2024, como una aplicación independiente para comprar, vender, custodiar, enviar y pagar con cripto. En el corto plazo, el módulo cripto dejará de estar disponible en la app principal de Cocos. Así, la compañía separa su negocio de activos virtuales mientras avanza en su conversión en banco, tras la compra de Banco Voii , que pasará a llamarse Cocos Bank.
Los clientes ingresan a la nueva app con el mismo usuario y contraseña de Cocos y no tienen que transferir sus criptoactivos: las tenencias siguen apareciendo en el portafolio general. La app, publicada en la App Store el 25 de septiembre, permite operar las 24 horas en pesos o en dólares con Bitcoin, Ethereum, USDT, USDC y Solana, entre otras criptomonedas. También permite hacer transferencias sin costo entre cuentas de Cocos Crypto, enviar y recibir cripto desde y hacia billeteras externas, y pagar con la tarjeta Cocos con el saldo en cripto.
Según comunicó a sus clientes, Cocos busca sumar nuevos productos bancarios. El Banco Central restringe la operatoria de los bancos con criptoactivos. Con una app y una estructura separadas, Cocos puede incorporar depósitos, cuentas y crédito sin mezclar la normativa bancaria con la de los proveedores de servicios de activos virtuales (PSAV).
 Movimientos para convertirse en Banco 
 Cocos cerró el 30 de junio la compra del 100% de Banco Voii por unos US$20 millones . La asamblea de accionistas aprobó un aumento de capital que quintuplica la base patrimonial del banco y el cambio de nombre a Cocos Bank S.A. , ya publicado en el Boletín Oficial y pendiente de aprobación del Banco Central. Cocos designó como gerente general a Martín Diez, ex CFO de Banco Hipotecario con más de 11 años en Deutsche Bank; su nombramiento también está sujeto a esa aprobación.
Cocos fue fundada por Ariel Sbdar , su CEO, y Nicolás Mindlin . Sbdar trabajó antes en fusiones y adquisiciones en Banco Macro, en estrategia en BIND y en la mesa de dinero de Allaria Ledesma. Cocos Crypto ya funcionaba como una sociedad separada de la sociedad de bolsa, aunque dentro de la app principal. Según la compañía, Cocos reúne cerca de dos millones de usuarios y administra más de US$2.000 millones en activos. En Brasil, la compañía compró la plataforma Warren .

_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Cocos (Argentine investment/brokerage super-app, ~2M users, >US$2bn AUM) carved its existing crypto service — live since **Feb 2024** — out of the main app into a **standalone "Cocos Crypto" app**, published on the App Store **25 Sep 2026**; the crypto module will soon disappear from the main Cocos app. Features: 24/7 buy/sell/custody/send/pay in ARS or USD across BTC, ETH, USDT, USDC, SOL; free transfers between Cocos Crypto accounts; send/receive to external wallets; spend crypto balance via the Cocos card. **De-PR'd: this is not a new product, it is a legal/regulatory unbundling of an existing one.** Same login, no asset migration, holdings still show in the general portfolio — so for the user almost nothing changes; the change is structural.
→ **Why structured this way:** Cocos closed the **100% acquisition of Banco Voii for ~US$20M on 30 Jun 2026** (renaming to Cocos Bank S.A., capital raise 5x, GM Martín Diez — all pending BCRA approval). Argentina's BCRA bars banks and PSPs from touching crypto (Comms "A" 7506 / 7759; reaffirmed 13 Oct 2026, see [[Argentina's central bank rejects near-term crypto banking]]). To become a bank Cocos must **ring-fence crypto into a separate PSAV entity/app** so bank regulation and virtual-asset-provider (PSAV, registered at CNV under Law 27,739 / Res. 1058-2025) regulation don't contaminate each other. The split is the price of the banking license, not a growth initiative.
→ **Second order:** this reveals the regulatory arbitrage line in Argentina — you can be a bank OR offer crypto, not both under one roof. Cocos chose to keep both by splitting the stack.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Two competitive fronts, because Cocos is splitting into two businesses:
- **Crypto app front:** [[Argentina crypto fintech Lemon raises $20M Series B round]] (Lemon, >2M users, Visa crypto card, the reference retail crypto wallet), Belo ([[Squads, Belo and Segura raise capital for stablecoin platforms]]), Buenbit, Ripio (oldest, since 2014), Bitso, plus stablecoin-native wallets like [[Takenos raises $5 million seed for stablecoin wallet]]. Cocos Crypto is a **late, me-too entrant** into a crowded, mature segment — it is catching up on standalone-app UX that Lemon/Belo have had for years. Its edge is the **2M-user installed base** it can cross-sell, not product novelty.
- **Banking/super-app front:** Mercado Pago, Ualá ([[Uala lays off 135 staff, mostly in Argentina]], [[Uala to launch fractional US shares in Mexico]]), Naranja X, Brubank ([[Brubank launches dollar-backed credit card in Argentina]]), Revolut. Here Cocos is the one **adding a bank to a brokerage**, the inverse of wallets adding investments.
→ **Why the landscape is this way:** every Argentine fintech is converging on the same super-app (invest + pay + bank + crypto); the regulatory wall forces them to express it as **separate entities/apps**, so UI unbundling (like this) will recur across the sector. The real contest is distribution and cross-sell, not features — all offer near-identical USDT rails.

## [2] Company history / fit
Founded by CEO **Ariel Sbdar** (ex-M&A Banco Macro, strategy at BIND, Allaria Ledesma trading desk) and **Nicolás Mindlin**. Trajectory: brokerage (sociedad de bolsa) → crypto service (Feb 2024, already a separate company inside the app) → Brazil expansion via **Warren acquisition (~US$60M, signed Jul 2026)** → **Banco Voii purchase (US$20M, closed Jun 2026)** → banking license (pending) → crypto app spin-out (Sep 2026). Projected ~US$5bn AUM and >US$100M revenue across AR+BR if Warren closes.
→ **Why the company acts this way:** a brokerage take-rate is thin and cyclical; to earn a durable deposit/credit franchise (and a software/banking multiple) Cocos needs a bank. But a bank can't hold crypto in Argentina → so the already-separate crypto sociedad gets its own app. The spin-out is the **logical consequence of the M&A**, executed on the regulator's timetable.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: low.** The crypto capability existed since Feb 2024; this is repackaging, not new function. Standalone crypto apps with cards, external-wallet transfers and stablecoin rails are commodity in Argentina (Lemon, Belo, Ripio). **Traction: unmeasured for the crypto app specifically** — the 2M users / US$2bn AUM are Cocos-wide (brokerage-led), with no disclosed figure for how many actually use crypto or will migrate. Announced ≠ adopted; no crypto-only DAU/volume given.
→ **Why the value-add is (modestly) real:** the structural value is not the app but the **license optionality** — by cleanly separating PSAV from bank, Cocos preserves the ability to offer deposits/credit AND crypto to the same 2M base, something a pure wallet (no bank) or a pure bank (no crypto) can't. **Who captures margin:** the bank side (NIM on deposits/credit) is the prize; crypto is a low-margin, commoditized spread/fee business kept mainly for retention and dollar-access demand. The app spin-out protects the high-margin license, not the low-margin crypto.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch: (1) BCRA approval of Cocos Bank name change, capital raise and Martín Diez appointment — all currently pending; (2) whether the crypto module is actually forced out of the main app and user migration holds; (3) Warren/Brazil close and the US$100M revenue target; (4) BCRA policy — on **13 Oct 2026 the central bank reaffirmed its crypto-banking ban for ~2 years**, explicitly expecting banks to run crypto via **separate entities** (exactly Cocos's structure), see [[Argentina's central bank rejects near-term crypto banking]].
→ **Why the market goes this way:** Argentine stablecoin demand is structural, not cyclical — ~94% of peso crypto volume is in stablecoins and usage kept rising even as monthly inflation fell from ~25% to ~2%; it's become a dollar-access habit (BCRA still projects ~30% 2026 inflation). So crypto/stablecoin access is a must-have retention feature for any super-app, which is precisely why Cocos keeps it despite the regulatory friction.
→ **Counterintuitive second order:** the BCRA ban doesn't shrink crypto — it **entrenches the separate-entity super-app pattern**, rewarding incumbents (Cocos, Ualá, Mercado Pago) that can afford two regulated structures and squeezing out single-license players. Regulation here concentrates, not fragments.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Argentina is the single clearest case of crypto product-market fit: ~20% adult adoption (~8.6m users) and #1 globally for everyday stablecoin use, with stablecoins at ~61.8% of all crypto volume and ~94% of peso-denominated crypto trading volume dollar-backed (per Artemis/Chainalysis, via KuCoin & crypto.news, Aug 2026). The driver is dollarization-as-survival, not speculation: USDT/USDC as a savings and payments rail under 30%+ inflation and capital controls. Argentina retail crypto revenue est. ~US$0.94bn (2025) → ~US$1.1bn (2026), +16% (per copytradeinsider.com, as of 2026) `[figures from secondary aggregators — treat as directional]`. Structure: fragmented retail layer (Lemon, Belo, Buenbit, Ripio, Bitso + exchange entrants like Bitget) sitting below a banking layer that is legally walled off from crypto. Barriers are now regulatory, not technical — Law 27,739 (effective Mar 2024) + CNV Res. 1058/2025 require PSAV registration, min. net worth US$150k and annual audits. "Why now": the structural separation is forced by the BCRA — its 2022 ban keeps banks out of crypto, and the companion note [[Argentina's central bank rejects near-term crypto banking]] (2026-10-13) confirms no change for ~2 years. Cocos is standing up Cocos Crypto as a separate PSAV app precisely so its pending Cocos Bank license is not contaminated by crypto activity.

**Competitive landscape.** Retail-crypto KPIs: funded accounts/MAU, stablecoin balances & flows, trading take rate/spread, card spend. Spreads are the pricing battleground — Lemon and Belo run the tightest at ~0.5–0.6% (per copytradeinsider.com, 2026), which caps the spread Cocos can charge. Players & scale (web, 2026, company/aggregator-sourced — not audited): Lemon ~3m+ Argentine accounts (raised a [[Argentina crypto fintech Lemon raises $20M Series B round]]; now shipping a BTC-backed card [[Lemon launches Bitcoin-backed credit card in Argentina]] and LatAm expansion [[Lemon opens Colombia waitlist for crypto-enabled Visa card]]); Belo ~4m users; Buenbit ~1m; Ripio/Bitso regional. Basis of competition: spread + catalog + card/payments utility + fiat on-ramp. Cocos's position: catching-up niche in pure crypto, but with a distinct cross-sell moat none of the crypto-natives have — it already runs a ~US$2bn-AUM brokerage and is acquiring a bank ([[Cocos wins central bank nod to acquire Banco Voii for $20 million]], ~US$20m, Jun 2026). Moat = distribution + switching costs: same login, crypto holdings stay in the unified portfolio, and crypto balance is spendable via the Cocos card. Recent move: standalone app live on the App Store 25 Sep 2026; crypto module to be pulled from the main app shortly after `(de-PR: announced, migration not yet complete)`.

**Comps & multiples.** No public market cap or disclosed round valuation for Cocos → EV/Revenue, P/E = no data. Internal comps (brokerage/crypto, LatAm): [[Argentina crypto fintech Lemon raises $20M Series B round]] (2025-10) and [[Cocos wins central bank nod to acquire Banco Voii for $20 million]] (bank acquired for ~US$20m). Price-per-user sanity check on the bank deal: ~US$20m / the note's claimed ~2m users = ~US$10/user — but that US$20m buys Banco Voii (a banking licence + balance sheet), NOT the user base, so the ratio is not a valuation signal, only context. Scale figures conflict and must be flagged: the note (company-sourced) claims "~2m users / >US$2bn AUM" for the Cocos *group*; third-party profiles (Tracxn/Crunchbase, via web) put Cocos Capital at ~300k accounts and ~US$70m 2025 revenue. The ~2m almost certainly counts registered app users across the whole platform, not funded crypto accounts — crypto users are a subset. Multiples: distribution not computed, qualitative comparison only. `[UNSOURCED]`: Cocos crypto AUM split, crypto take rate, CAC/LTV.

**Risk flags.**
1. Regulatory wall is the whole premise and it can move either way. The BCRA bars banks from crypto (confirmed through ~2028, companion note), so the split protects the Cocos Bank licence — but a new PSAV/crypto-tax regime or a reversal of capital-control liberalisation could re-fragment the model and strand the standalone app. Second-order: Cocos is betting on a wall it does not control.
2. The adoption thesis is dollarization-driven, and that motive is eroding. With the official/blue/MEP/crypto-dollar spread compressed toward ~0% in 2026 (web), the arbitrage reason to hold crypto is gone and only the store-of-value motive remains — if inflation keeps cooling, retail stablecoin demand (Cocos's core volume) softens structurally.
3. Spread compression + crowded field. Lemon/Belo at ~0.5–0.6% spreads and newly-registered global entrants (Bitget) squeeze take rate; Cocos is a late, sub-scale entrant in pure crypto and must subsidise via the broader platform. Monetization is undisclosed — "who's silent about what" on crypto unit economics.
4. Execution/approval dependency. Cocos Bank name change and GM appointment are "pending BCRA approval"; the bank thesis (deposits/accounts/credit) that justifies splitting out crypto is not yet live — if the licence conversion stalls, the standalone-app rationale weakens to a cost, not a strategic edge.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a brokerage-to-super-app play mirroring Nubank/Revolut/Ualá, but inverted by regulation: Cocos is *de-bundling* crypto into its own regulated entity so it can *re-bundle* a bank around it. Watch whether the Banco Voii → Cocos Bank conversion closes and whether crypto balances become the default funding source for the Cocos card — that would turn the standalone app from a compliance artifact into a genuine stablecoin-to-spend rail. Falsifiable: if BCRA approval slips past H1-2027 or crypto MAU doesn't migrate to the new app, the thesis (crypto as the on-ramp to a dollar-denominated neobank) is wrong and the split is just regulatory housekeeping.
