## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened.** On 2026-10-07, French fintech **Shares** announced it had **completed** the acquisition of 100% of **Treezor**, the Paris-based Banking-as-a-Service (BaaS) platform, from **Société Générale**, after all closing conditions (definitive agreements, social procedures, regulatory approvals) were satisfied. This is the CLOSING of a deal first flagged in January 2026 — not a fresh announcement.

**Dated timeline.**
- **2026-01-29/30** — Shares enters *exclusive negotiations* with Société Générale to acquire 100% of Treezor (announcement stage). See [[Shares in exclusive talks to acquire Treezor from Societe Generale]].
- **2026-02-16** — Deal reported more widely; terms undisclosed; context on Treezor's EMI license and SocGen's fintech divestments. See [[Shares in talks to acquire SocGen BaaS subsidiary Treezor]].
- **2026-10-07** — Transaction **completed** (this note; the digest surfaced it 2026-10-11).

**Deal terms.** Financial terms **not disclosed** in either the announcement or the completion. 100% of Treezor's share capital transferred from Société Générale to Shares.

**On Treezor.** French BaaS platform founded 2016, acquired by Société Générale in 2019. Operates as an electronic money institution (EMI) licensed across ~25 European countries; self-styled "Unicorn Farmer" for powering early-stage fintechs including Qonto, Lydia, Swile and Shine. Has processed >EUR 130bn in transaction flows and issued >8m cards; serves 150+ clients. Faced an ACPR fine of ~EUR 1m in April 2024 for AML control deficiencies (regulator noted subsequent remediation).

**On Shares.** French investech founded 2021; raised ~$90m across three rounds (Valar Ventures, Singular, Global Founders Capital, Red Sea Ventures); ~110 employees across France, UK, Poland; CEO/co-founder Benjamin Chemla. Holds ACPR investment-services license + AMF digital-asset registration (June 2023). Started as a retail social-investing app, pivoted to B2B (Shares Pro for wealth managers; white-label investment API; AXA employee-savings partnership in 2024). Previously relied on **Modulr** for payment accounts — owning Treezor flips it from a BaaS *customer* into a BaaS *owner* with its own EMI/card-issuing rails.

**Strategic rationale / positioning.** The combined group aims for an **end-to-end European financial infrastructure** spanning accounts, cards, payments, savings and investment — extending the historically payments-centric BaaS model into savings/investment use cases. Post-close, Cédric Cassini (ex-Treezor CRO) was named head of the BaaS platform, with Julien Mortuaire as CTO.

**SocGen angle.** Continues a pattern of fintech divestments — SocGen also sold freelancer neobank Shine (which also ran on Treezor rails), per [[French neobank Shine expands across Europe after SocGen sale]]. Fits SocGen's digital-priorities streamlining toward BoursoBank.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** European BaaS / embedded finance — infrastructure that lets non-banks (and now other fintechs) embed accounts, cards, payments and savings via API under a licensed provider. Structure: capital- and licence-intensive, fragmented across a dozen regional players, and now visibly *consolidating/shaking out* rather than growing — the defining dynamic here. "Why now": the 2021-era BaaS boom unwound hard — Solaris' valuation collapsed from ~€1.6bn (peak) to ~€90m at its Feb-2025 €140m Series G led by SBI (per fintech.global / techfundingnews, Feb 2025); Railsr, once ~$1bn, was sold in a 2023 pre-pack for ~$0.5m (per Sifted / Yahoo Finance). Against that backdrop an incumbent bank (SocGen) is exiting a BaaS unit it no longer wants to fund. No reliable free TAM figure for EU BaaS — no data (secondary market-size reports here are paywalled/promotional, not citable).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: processed payment volume/transaction flow, cards issued, programme/client count, and — post-shakeout — the rails licence + path to profitability. Key EU players: Solaris and Swan (regulated, Germany/France), Treezor (SocGen until now), Intergiro; Railsr the UK comparator; core-banking adjacents Mambu / Thought Machine sit upstream. Basis of competition has shifted from growth/land-grab to *survival, compliance and unit economics*. Recent moves: Swan appointed Camille Tyan CEO (Nov 2025, [[Swan appoints Camille Tyan as CEO]]); Solaris cut ~20% of staff to go "AI-native" (Mar 2026, [[Solaris cuts 20% of staff to become AI-native bank]]); bunq opened its own BaaS platform (Jun 2026, [[bunq opens banking-as-a-service platform to industry]]). Treezor scale per Treezor PR (7 Oct 2026): >€145bn transaction flows and >8.5m cards issued since inception. Shares' position: a French investing/savings app (Benjamin Chemla) buying *downstream* into payment rails — not a BaaS incumbent catching up, but a cross-vertical entrant stitching investing + payments into an "end-to-end" stack. Moat `(analysis)`: Treezor's EU licence + installed client base (switching costs) is the real asset; Shares brings investing/savings product, not BaaS distribution.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal price undisclosed, so no acquisition multiple can be computed — no data. The tell is the structure: SocGen recapitalised Treezor via an €8m capital increase at €1 nominal, *no premium* — "prix plancher", a first in Treezor's history (per mind.eu.com, re Mar-2026 operation). That floor-price recap just before a sale signals a distressed/low-value exit, not a premium franchise sale.
- Combined Shares+Treezor target: ~€100m revenue + profitability by 2027 (per Maddyness, 7 Oct 2026) — a *forward target*, not actuals; `[UNSOURCED]` for current revenue → EV/Revenue not computable.
- Internal comps (valuation trajectory, not clean multiples): [[Shares in exclusive talks to acquire Treezor from Societe Generale]] and [[Shares in talks to acquire SocGen BaaS subsidiary Treezor]] (deal origination, Jan–Feb 2026); Solaris ~€1.6bn→~€90m and Railsr ~$1bn→~$0.5m as the sector's re-rating anchors.
Qualitative read: distribution not computed (no disclosed figures); directionally the whole EU-BaaS cohort has re-rated *cheap* vs its 2021 marks — the Treezor exit is consistent with that, not an outlier.

**Risk flags.**
1. *Integration / execution* — Shares is an investing app absorbing a regulated payments-rails business (different licence regime, risk/compliance, ops); the €100m-by-2027 + profitability target is ambitious for a loss-making unit SocGen chose to offload. Second-order: if integration slips, the "end-to-end" thesis is just two sub-scale businesses under one roof.
2. *Regulatory / fraud liability* — BaaS is exactly where BaFin-style enforcement hit Solaris; a smaller independent owner (vs SocGen's balance sheet) has less capital buffer for AML/compliance remediation. Second-order: any supervisory action could force another recap.
3. *Quality of the asset* — the €1-nominal floor-price recap and undisclosed price suggest SocGen valued Treezor low; the headline scale stats (flows/cards *since inception*) can mask flat or shrinking current volumes — "who's silent": no current revenue, churn, or active-client figures disclosed.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Confirms EU BaaS is in its consolidation/shakeout phase: incumbents divesting, survivors (Solaris, Swan, Treezor-under-Shares) regrouping around profitability and licences rather than growth. Falsifiable thesis: a *cross-vertical* owner (investing + payments) can make BaaS economics work where a bank subsidiary couldn't — trigger/catalyst to watch: whether Shares discloses Treezor's *current* revenue and a credible path to the 2027 €100m/profitability mark; what would break it: a further recap, licence/AML action, or client attrition post-close.

**IR grounding.** Shares is IR-covered but the latest filed material is the AXA France employee-savings partnership (press release, 27 Mar 2025, https://www.shares.io/resources/axa-france-and-shares-join-forces), plus a 2024 Inter Invest PER acquisition and a 2024 brand campaign. These predate and are tangential to the Treezor BaaS deal — they confirm Shares' investing/savings DNA (the complementarity Chemla cites) but add nothing on BaaS economics; treated as background only.
