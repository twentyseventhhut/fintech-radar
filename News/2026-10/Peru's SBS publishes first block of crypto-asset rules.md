# Peru's SBS publishes first block of crypto-asset rules

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[This Week in Fintech] 🇵🇪 Peru’s Superintendency of Banking, Insurance and Private Pension Funds (Superintendencia de Banca, Seguros y AFP, SBS) published the first block of rules for the country’s Open Finance System (Sistema de Finanzas Abiertas, SFA) for public comment until October 23. The draft covers the system’s scope, data, participant roles, registration, and how users give and revoke consent.

# Context-enrichment: Peru's SBS publishes first block of Open Finance (SFA) rules
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

> **Title is a mislabel.** The note title says "crypto-asset rules," but the aggregated body, the primary source (sbs.gob.pe idnoticia/4015), and all external reporting agree: this is the **first normative block of Peru's Open Finance System (Sistema de Finanzas Abiertas, SFA)** — NOT crypto. Peru's crypto/VASP regime is a separate, AML-only track (see [4]). Treat this item as an **open-finance regulation** item; the "crypto" framing is wrong and should not drive the digest.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On ~Oct 5, 2026 Peru's SBS (Superintendencia de Banca, Seguros y AFP — the banking, insurance and pension supervisor, NOT a securities/crypto regulator) published for public consultation the **draft "General Regulation of the Open Finance System," Titles I–IV** — the first of several planned regulatory blocks. Comment window: ~18 working days from publication in El Peruano, **deadline Oct 23, 2026**.
- The first block covers: **scope/objectives/principles; the data in-scope; participant roles and responsibilities; registration and technical enablement; and user-experience + consent (grant/revoke) management.**
- This is **a draft for comment**, not a binding rule. Nothing is live. The SBS has said a **second block** (further system aspects) follows "in coming weeks."
- **Why structured this way:** the SBS is doing a staged, block-by-block consultation rather than one big decree — a deliberate contrast to Colombia's single mandatory decree. It front-loads the governance scaffolding (who participates, consent, registration) before the hard interoperability/API specs, which land in later blocks. This reveals a cautious, infrastructure-first posture (the SBS's own four principles: security, interoperability, innovation, user protection) and buys the four main banks adaptation time.

## [1] Competitors / peers (LatAm open-finance regulators)
- **Brazil (BCB):** the regional benchmark — mandatory Open Finance live since 2021, now iterating (credit portability, new data rules in 2026). Years ahead. [[Brazil's central bank proposes new Open Finance data rules]]
- **Colombia (SFC):** moved to a **mandatory** SFA framework via decree in 2026 — among the few obligatory LatAm models. [[Colombia establishes mandatory Open Finance framework]], [[Colombia makes open finance mandatory for banks]]
- **Chile (CMF):** mandatory rollout **delayed to July 2027** after industry pushback; now running the **ATENA sandbox** with the IDB. [[Chile's CMF delays open finance mandatory rollout to July 2027]], [[Chile's CMF launches ATENA open-finance sandbox with IDB]]
- **Mexico (CNBV):** still litigating/stalled — "Open Finance takes the CNBV to court." [[Open Finance takes Mexico's CNBV to court]]
- **Position:** Peru is a **late mover** — drafting its first block in late 2026 while Brazil is live and Colombia/Chile already have mandatory frameworks (even if phased). **Why:** Peru's SBS is sequencing Open Banking first, expanding to insurance/pensions later, and explicitly giving its concentrated banking sector time. Second-order: being late lets Peru copy Brazil/Colombia design lessons (consent UX, phased mandates) and avoid Mexico's litigation trap — a "fast follower" bet, not leadership.

## [2] SBS trajectory / fit
The SBS has been steadily building a digital-finance rulebook: 24-hour outage/cyber-incident notification proposal (Jan 2026, [[Peru's SBS proposes 24-hour outage notification rule]]); raising e-money / basic-savings limits (Mar 2026); approving Revolut's bank and Prex's first fully-digital credit license (2026). The SFA draft fits logically as the next layer — data-sharing rails on top of an expanding regulated-entity base. **Why:** the SBS published an SFA Roadmap in 2025 with milestones through 2029; this Oct-2026 draft is the roadmap executing on schedule (BaaS reg mid-2026 → general Open Finance norm early 2027 → first data exchanges ~2028).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty:** genuinely new for Peru — first time the SFA scope/consent/registration rules are on paper. But it is **a draft, not adoption.** No APIs live, no data flowing, no participants registered.
- **Traction = zero, by design.** External roadmap: mandatory initially for the **four main banks**; first data group enabled ~late 2027, second ~mid-2028; general norm expected **January 2027** to give a year of adaptation, first exchanges ~Jan 2028. So real-world impact is **12–18+ months out.**
- **Who captures value:** aggregators/fintechs (Prometeo, Belvo, Fiskil are already active in LatAm open finance) stand to gain API access; incumbent banks bear the build cost. The consent layer is where trust (and friction) will concentrate — a known LatAm pain point ("Most Colombians unaware of open finance").

## [4] What's next / sentiment + the crypto clarification
- **Next:** comment deadline Oct 23, 2026 → second regulatory block (APIs/interoperability detail) in coming weeks → general SFA norm ~early 2027 → phased data exchanges 2027–2028.
- **Regulatory backdrop:** part of a broad LatAm open-finance wave; Peru deliberately gradualist.
- **On crypto (why the title is wrong):** Peru has **no comprehensive crypto law**; the framework bill stalled in committee in 2025. The SBS/UIF supervises **VASPs only for AML/CFT** (Resolution N° 02648-2024: compliance officer + AML measures), and the Travel Rule (Supreme Decree 006-2023-JUS, Ch. VIII) took effect **Aug 1, 2026**. The SBS has stated it is **not competent to regulate crypto-assets broadly.** None of this is what the Oct-2026 draft is about — the draft is Open Finance. Any "first block of crypto-asset rules" reading is a mis-tag.

**Sector & drivers.** This is an **open-finance regulation** event, not a crypto one — the note's title ("crypto-asset rules") is mislabelled: SBS Resolution N° 02433-2026 (published 2026-10-05 in El Peruano, comment window to 2026-10-23) covers Titles I–IV of the General Regulation of the **Sistema de Finanzas Abiertas (SFA)** — scope, data, participant roles, registration/technical enablement and user consent (per SBS/Gestión/Andina, as of 2026-10). Sizing: Peru's payments ecosystem hosts ~120 solutions (16 launched in 2025), with open finance + the sandbox expected to drive 18+ more entrants in 2026 (per The Fintech Times, 2025-11); the broader national fintech count is ~193 local / ~346 incl. foreign startups, est. ecosystem value ~US$255M (per Finnosummit, undated — treat as indicative, not a hard TAM). **Why now:** regulatory sequencing — SBS published an Open Finance Roadmap in Feb-2026 and is now releasing the binding rules in phased "blocks," mirroring the LatAm wave (Brazil live, Colombia mandatory in 2026). Structure: Peru's financial system is bank-concentrated; a consent-based data-sharing mandate is the lever that shifts data from an incumbent moat to a portable user asset. Second-order effect: lowers switching costs → opens the credit/aggregation layer to the 120+ paytechs.

**Competitive landscape.** This is a *regulator* action, not a company; the relevant "KPIs" for an OF regime are: (i) phasing model (voluntary vs mandatory), (ii) implementation timeline, (iii) data/participant scope. SBS's draft is phased and currently at **public-consultation** stage — i.e. not yet binding, earliest/least-advanced of the Pacific-Alliance peers. Peer regimes (basis of competition = speed + breadth of mandate): **Brazil** — most mature, 4 OF stages live since 2021, now refining data-use rules (2026-03); **Colombia** — moved from voluntary to **mandatory** via Decree 0368/2026 (2026-04) after the SFC extended its timeline 18→30 months (2026-02); **Mexico** — 2018 fintech law, narrow (account-information only), slow. Peru's position: **laggard/catching up** — last Pacific-Alliance mover to codify binding OF rules, now consulting on Titles I–IV only. No company moat applies; the "moat" here is the regulator's ability to enforce API standardization against concentrated incumbents.
