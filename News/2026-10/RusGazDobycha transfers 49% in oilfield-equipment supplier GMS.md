 «РусГазДобыча» передала 49% в поставщике нефтегазового оборудования ГМС 
ООО «Гидромаш МСК» Артема Молчанова 14 октября получила 49% ООО «Гидроиндустрия», головной компании группы ГМС, выпускающей оборудование для нефтегазовой отрасли, следует из ЕГРЮЛ. Ранее 100% компании принадлежали «РусГазДобыче» (РГД; входит в «Национальную газовую группу»), сейчас в собственности РГД остался 51% «Гидроиндустрии». Господин Молчанов — гендиректор ГМС, в СМИ его также называют одним из основателей группы. «Гидромаш МСК» топ-менеджер учредил в апреле 2026 года, пишет " Коммерсант ".
По последней раскрытой отчетности по МСФО, в 2024 году выручка группы ГМС увеличилась на 54%, до 95,5 млрд руб., EBITDA — на 47%, до 12,6 млрд руб., в том числе за счет реализации СПГ-проектов. Ключевой производственный актив ГМС — «Гидромашсервис», производитель насосного, компрессорного оборудования для нефтегазовой отрасли, тепловой и атомной энергетики, ЖКХ, металлургии, химической промышленности и судостроения. В 2021 году ГМС провела IPO на Лондонской фондовой бирже, в ходе которого привлекла $360 млн, капитализация составила $967 млн. В 2022 году ГМС сообщила о выкупе российского бизнеса мажоритарным акционером. В отчетности структур ГМС за 2021 год бенефициаром назывался бизнесмен Владимир Лукьяненко.
В конце 2024 года президент РФ Владимир Путин своим указом ввел в компаниях ГМС временное управление, а годом позже разрешил РГД приобрести активы группы, включая «Гидромашсервис». По данным СПАРК, «Гидромашсервис», УК «Группа ГМС», «ГМС Процессинг Текнолоджис» и ЦПСИК сегодня контролируются «Гидроиндустрией». Агентство «Эксперт РА» отмечало, что под контроль «Гидроиндустрии» перешли все ключевые операционные активы ГМС.
Руководитель отдела финансово-экономической экспертизы «Аверта Групп» Александр Тихов считает, что передача 49% в «Гидроиндустрии» Артему Молчанову завершает процедуру по выведению актива из иностранного контура в российский. По его словам, для машиностроительного актива с узкой инженерной компетенцией сохранение основателя в капитале удерживает команду и профильную экспертизу при смене собственника. Партнер Pen & Paper Екатерина Токарева полагает, что передача доли могла быть связана с ролью Артема Молчанова в управлении группой, налаживании бизнеса, а также в развитии компании в будущем.
В октябре «Эксперт РА» при понижении кредитного рейтинга «Гидромашсервиса» сообщал, что давление на финансовые метрики компании оказывает снижение операционной прибыли группы из-за постепенного завершения выполнения работ по портфелю действующих контрактов и снижения объема новых договоров по причине инвестиционной паузы крупнейших заказчиков в условиях геополитической нестабильности. При этом по группе наблюдается рост долгового портфеля, направленный на финансирование капитальных затрат и пополнение оборотного капитала, указывало агентство.
Агентство НКР в сентябрьском обзоре «Гидромашсервиса» обращало внимание, что на апрель 2026 года объем подписанных контрактов компании составлял 72 млрд руб., что на 27% меньше значения на апрель 2025 года. Такое снижение в первую очередь объясняется завершением реализации крупнотоннажных проектов по сжижению природного газа с поставками в 2025–2026 годах, поясняли в НКР. Объем вновь подписанных к апрелю 2026 года проектов остался на уровне прошлого года, который, в свою очередь, был ниже суммы новых контрактов на апрель 2024 года. Из-за сдвигов в сроках реализации крупных проектов восстановление выручки компании до значений 2024 года ожидается не ранее 2027 года, прогнозируют в НКР.

# Context-enrichment: RusGazDobycha transfers 49% in oilfield-equipment supplier GMS
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 14 Oct 2026, OOO "Gidromash MSK" — a vehicle founded in April 2026 by Artem Molchanov, CEO of GMS — acquired 49% of OOO "Gidroindustriya," the holding company of Group GMS. RusGazDobycha (RGD), part of the National Gas Group, kept 51%. Gidroindustriya controls the operating assets: "Gidromashservis" (HMS / Gidromashservice — pumps, compressors for oil & gas, thermal/nuclear power, utilities, metallurgy, chemicals, shipbuilding), UK "Group GMS," "GMS Processing Technologies," and TsPSIK (per SPARK; confirmed by Expert RA that "all key operating assets moved under Gidroindustriya").

This is NOT a cash sale with a disclosed price. It is a registration change in the EGRUL (company register) — the article infers "transfer" from a filing, not an announced M&A deal with a valuation. **Why structured this way (analysis):** the deal is the final leg of de-offshoring an asset seized from owners the state linked to Ukraine. GMS was placed under temporary (interim) state management by Putin's decree in late 2024; in Aug 2025 Putin lifted that management and authorised RGD to buy the assets; the April 2026 RGD acquisition closed the purchase; the Oct 2026 49% hand-off to the CEO "completes the procedure of pulling the asset out of the foreign contour into the Russian one" (Averta Group's Aleksandr Tikhov). Keeping the founder/CEO in the cap table is the retention mechanism — a machine-building asset with "narrow engineering competence" needs its team to stay (Tikhov; Pen & Paper's Ekaterina Tokareva echoes Molchanov's role in running and developing the group). So the 49% looks like management-equity-for-retention, not a true third-party buyer — but neither price nor whether Molchanov paid anything is disclosed (open).

**Entity check (confirmed):** GMS = HMS Group (HMS Hydraulic Machines & Systems Group Plc), the Cyprus/London-listed entity. HMS IPO'd GDRs (ticker HMSG) on the LSE in 2021, raising $360M at a ~$967M cap. 2022: it bought back the Russian business via its majority shareholder; delisting from the LSE followed. The 2021 beneficiary named in filings was businessman Vladimir Lukyanenko — the party Russian authorities later linked to Ukraine, triggering the 2024 seizure. (Separately, Ukraine's justice ministry has sought confiscation of HMS pump-building assets.) RGD itself is controlled via the National Gas Group (Arkady Rotenberg 51% / Artem Obolensky 49%) through Cyprus offshore Olpon Investments — i.e. a Rotenberg-orbit state-adjacent buyer.

## [1] Competitors / peers
This sits in two peer sets:
- **Russian forced-redistribution / de-offshoring deals of seized Western-linked assets (2024–2026):** Rosatom's 77bn-rub buyout of Delo Group ([[Rosatom completes buyout of Delo Group for 77bn rubles]]), the UniCredit Russia situation ([[Putin allows sale of UniCredit's Russian assets]], [[Финтехно: UniCredit Russia sale requires asset-split first]]), PSB's repeated failed auctions for nationalised Vektor Rail ([[PSB fails third auction for nationalized Vektor Rail]]), and the broader frozen-assets backdrop ([[US and EU float rival control plans for frozen Russian assets]]). GMS is a cleaner case than most: the asset has been successfully re-homed to a strategic owner (RGD) and the management locked in, versus PSB's failed auctions showing how illiquid some nationalised assets are.
- **Oil & gas / LNG equipment makers:** Kazankompressormash (also redistributed to the Rotenberg orbit per press), plus the import-substitution peer group feeding Russian LNG (Novatek's Arctic LNG, etc.). GMS's edge is incumbency in pumps/compressors and a captive position in domestic LNG localisation after Western majors exited.
**Why the landscape is this way (analysis):** sanctions + the 2022 exit of Western equipment vendors turned domestic pump/compressor makers into protected near-monopolies for LNG and energy projects — but the SAME inward turn concentrates revenue in a handful of state megaprojects, so demand is lumpy and policy-driven, not a broad market.

## [2] Company history / fit
HMS/GMS trajectory: 2021 LSE IPO ($360M, $967M cap) → 2022 Russian-business buyback + delisting → late-2024 interim state management (beneficiary Lukyanenko linked to Ukraine) → Aug 2025 Putin lifts management, authorises RGD purchase → Apr 2026 RGD acquires Gidromashservis/GMS Neftemash → Oct 2026 49% of holdco Gidroindustriya to CEO Molchanov. Financials (last disclosed IFRS, FY2024): revenue +54% to RUB 95.5bn, EBITDA +47% to RUB 12.6bn, boosted by LNG project deliveries.
**Why RGD acts this way (analysis):** RGD is a Gazprom-adjacent gas-monetisation/LNG player; owning its key pump/compressor supplier is vertical integration that secures equipment for its own LNG build-out amid sanctions — classic captive-supplier logic. Handing the CEO 49% rather than consolidating 100% signals RGD wants operational continuity, not just the asset title.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
What is genuinely new: the 49% CEO stake is a fresh, dated event (14 Oct 2026) that closes the de-offshoring arc — distinct from the Aug 2025 decree and the Apr 2026 purchase. It is NOT a reprint of those.
Real value-add is modest for an outside reader: no price, no new capital, no operational change — it is a governance/retention move. **Why the "value" is thin, one level deeper (analysis):** the thing that actually matters is the deteriorating order book, which the ownership shuffle does nothing to fix. Signed contracts were RUB 72bn as of Apr 2026, down 27% y/y (NKR), driven by the wind-down of large-tonnage LNG contracts delivering in 2025–2026 and an investment pause by big customers amid geopolitical instability. Debt is rising (capex + working capital). Expert RA assigned ruA; NKR cut the rating A+.ru → A.ru with a negative outlook. NKR expects revenue to recover to 2024 levels no earlier than 2027. So the "who captures the margin" question is beside the point here — the binding constraint is order intake, and the equity move is retention insurance against that downturn, not a growth catalyst.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect: continued de-offshoring formalities complete; GMS remains a captive LNG/energy equipment supplier to Russian megaprojects; order-book recovery pinned to 2027+ once new LNG/energy capex restarts. Ratings on negative/developing outlook — financial pressure near-term. **Why the market goes this way + second-order (analysis):** concentration in a few state-linked customers (RGD itself, Gazprom-orbit LNG) makes GMS strategically safe but cyclically fragile — its fate tracks a handful of sanctioned megaprojects, not a diversified market. The retention of the founder is a tell that the engineering talent is the real scarce asset; if Molchanov's equity is a one-off grant rather than a purchase, it hints the business is harder to run than own. Counterintuitively, being "protected" from foreign competition does not insulate GMS from the demand hole left by the LNG-project pause.

7. **What is GMS's customer concentration?** Heavily tied to a few LNG/energy megaprojects (incl. RGD's own) — strategically safe, cyclically fragile. Exact concentration % **open**.
8. **What drove the FY2024 +54% revenue?** LNG project deliveries (SPG) — a pull-forward now unwinding, hence the 2025–2026 order-book decline. Recovery to 2024 levels not before 2027 (NKR).
9. **Why ratings diverge (Expert RA ruA vs NKR A.ru negative)?** Different scales/timing; both flag rising debt + falling operating profit from the investment pause. Net direction: negative pressure.

12. **Does this fit the broader forced-redistribution wave?** Yes — peers: Rosatom/Delo, UniCredit Russia, PSB/Vektor Rail, Kazankompressormash. GMS is a relatively "clean" completion vs failed auctions elsewhere.
13. **Any fintech/capital-markets angle for the digest?** Thin — tagged capital-markets but it's an M&A/ownership-register event, not a financing or markets product. Weight comes from the de-offshoring theme, not a deal innovation.
14. **What would make this a 4–5?** A disclosed valuation, fresh capital, or a signed order recovery. None present → caps importance.
15. **Downside trigger to watch?** Further LNG-project delays + continued debt growth → another rating cut; or confiscation/legal action abroad impairing IP/supply.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russian oilfield/industrial equipment — pumps, compressors, block-modular units for oil & gas, nuclear/thermal power, LNG. No reliable public TAM — sector sizing for Russian pump/compressor OEMs is "no data" (post-2022 the segment lost disclosure; LSE delisting removed the one free public window). Structure: capital-intensive, long-cycle project manufacturing (multi-year EPC-style supply contracts), with high entry barriers from engineering IP, certification for nuclear/LNG duty, and incumbency on reference projects — concentrated, not fragmented. Driver + "why now": import-substitution demand after Western OEMs (Sulzer, Baker Hughes, Siemens Energy compressors, GE) exited Russia in 2022 made GMS the default domestic supplier for LNG trains, nuclear and pipeline pumps — a structural tailwind. But the "why now" cuts both ways: GMS's 2024 surge was LNG-project-driven (per note, revenue +54% to RUB 95.5bn, EBITDA +47% to RUB 12.6bn, "за счёт реализации СПГ-проектов"), and that cohort is now rolling off — contract book RUB 72bn at Apr-2026, down 27% YoY (per NKR, via mergers.ru), with revenue recovery to 2024 levels not expected before 2027.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for project OEMs: contract backlog (book-to-bill), EBITDA margin, debt/EBITDA, new-order intake. GMS's lead asset Gidromashservice makes pumping/compressor gear across oil & gas, nuclear, thermal power, water/utilities, metallurgy, chemicals and shipbuilding — a broad domestic franchise. Basis of competition in Russia today is less price than capability + sanctions-proof domestic sourcing: after the 2022 foreign-OEM exit, few local players can supply certified LNG/nuclear-duty rotating equipment, so GMS competes largely on availability and reference base rather than against live Western bids. Protagonist position: domestic incumbent / near-monopoly in several niches `(analysis)` — moat is intangibles (engineering competence, certifications) plus switching costs on installed base; the deal's stated logic (per Averta Group's A. Tikhov, via mergers.ru) is that keeping founder-CEO Molchanov in the cap table "retains the team and core expertise" — i.e. the moat is partly human capital, a fragile moat. Recent peer/own moves: HMS opened a RUB 10.7bn compressor plant in Tatarstan (Kazancompressormash) in 2026 (per tadviser) — capacity build-out consistent with import-substitution, but also the source of the rising debt NKR/Expert RA flag.

**Comps & multiples.** No clean public trading comp: GMS delisted and the 49% transfer is a related-party/EGRUL re-registration, not a priced market transaction, so no deal EV or multiple is disclosable — valuation = **no data**. Historical anchor: 2021 LSE event raised $360M at ~$967M "capitalisation" (per note text); treat as a round/market reference, not current worth — external record shows HMS listed on the LSE far earlier and the 2021 figure is not independently corroborated here `[UNSOURCED]`; GDRs later moved to MOEX and the Cyprus parent transferred 100% of JSC HMS Group to JSC HMS Holding (reported at $2.7bn, per marketscreener — treat as a reported related-party transfer price, not a market cap). On fundamentals only (no valuation to divide against): EBITDA margin = RUB 12.6bn / RUB 95.5bn = **13.2%** (2024, per MSFO via mergers.ru) — mid-single-to-low-double-digit margin typical of heavy project OEMs, in-line, not rich. Internal comps (Russian capital-markets / state-asset ownership change, `[[wikilink]]`): [[Russian state sells 67% of zinc and lead processor Dalpolimetall]] and [[Russia weighs selling up to 5% of NSPK to single investor]] — both show the same template of state/quasi-state control redistribution rather than open-market M&A; [[Mergers.ru Incab Holding raises 2 billion rubles in IPO]] as a Russian industrial capital-markets datapoint. Distribution not computed (no comparable valuation figures) — qualitative only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Backlog roll-off / revenue air-pocket** — book down 27% YoY to RUB 72bn (Apr-2026) as LNG trains complete and major customers pause capex amid "geopolitical instability" (NKR/Expert RA via mergers.ru); 2024's +54% was a peak, recovery not before 2027 — the headline growth overstates the forward run-rate.
