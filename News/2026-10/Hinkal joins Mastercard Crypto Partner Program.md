Hinkal joins the Mastercard Crypto Partner Program
 Hinkal joins the Mastercard Crypto Partner Program 
Hinkal has joined the Mastercard Crypto Partner Program , a network of crypto and blockchain teams working with Mastercard on how digital assets fit into everyday commerce.
We're bringing privacy that already runs inside other teams' products. Wallets, payment platforms and chains plug Hinkal in when stablecoin activity needs to stay confidential.
 What the program is about 
The Crypto Partner Program connects crypto-native companies with Mastercard's teams, its global payments network, and each other. Members span stablecoin issuers and infrastructure providers, on and off ramps, crypto card enablers, blockchains, and blockchain service providers.
Mastercard describes it as a way to turn technical capabilities into compliant use cases that work in global commerce, and to move past pilots through partner discovery and go-to-market collaboration. Select members also get access to financial institutions across Mastercard's issuing and acquiring network, plus regulatory and compliance support aligned with global standards.
 What it brings to stablecoins 
Stablecoins already move value fast and around the clock. What they've lacked is an easy way into the systems businesses and consumers use every day.
The program is built directly for that. Among the use cases Mastercard lists are cross-border payments and remittances, B2B transactions, treasury and settlement flows, stablecoin-based payments, and card programs that let people spend digital assets at regular merchants. Stablecoin builders get a shared framework with one of the largest payment networks in the world, and a room full of teams working on the same problem each in their own way.
When stablecoins start carrying payroll, supplier payments and treasury moves, privacy stops being a niche request. Businesses don't publish their payments on a bank statement for anyone to read, and they'll expect the same when they settle onchain.
 Hinkal's role 
Beyond the Hinkal applications, we run inside wallets and platforms that build us into their own products.
We let stablecoins settle in private balances and move with zero-knowledge proofs, so the chain verifies each transfer without revealing the amount, the sender or the recipient. We're live on Ethereum, Polygon, Solana, TRON, and other major EVM chains, and teams plug us in through our SDKs and API.
That model is already live across the ecosystem such as:
 Polygon Wallet that offers Private Send powered by Hinkal.
 Tether's Wallet Development Kit which includes Hinkal private send for wallet builders.
 Turnkey wallets that can add privacy through the Hinkal SDK.
 Avvio that runs Hinkal's private payments inside its own interface.
Each of those teams owns its product and its users while Hinkal adds confidentiality, so privacy becomes a feature they can ship instead of a system they have to build.
 Privacy that fits the framework 
Mastercard's program puts compliance at the center, and so do we. Funds are screened before they enter Hinkal, and viewing keys let users and businesses share their transaction history with an auditor or regulator whenever they need to. Activity stays private to the public and visible to the people entitled to see it.
It's privacy built for regulated payments, which is where this program is headed.
 Looking ahead 
Mastercard’s program exists to help digital assets earn a place in everyday commerce, built on trust and compliance. Hinkal’s mission is to make privacy a normal part of onchain money, inside the products people already use and within the rules they already follow. We’ve always believed onchain money should be as private as the cash in your pocket. Joining Mastercard’s program is one more way we’re bringing that to the payments people use every day.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-10-09, privacy-infrastructure protocol **Hinkal** announced it had joined the **Mastercard Crypto Partner Program** — the ~85-member network Mastercard launched in March 2026 to wire crypto-native firms into its payments rails (see [[Mastercard launches Crypto Partner Program]] and [[Mastercard crypto program adds PayPal, Ripple, Circle, Binance]]). Hinkal positions itself as the program's privacy layer for stablecoins: wallets, payment platforms and chains "plug Hinkal in" to keep stablecoin activity confidential. The announcement is carried on Hinkal's own blog and echoed by crypto outlets (KuCoin, ChainCatcher, TokenPost, Blockonomi); Mastercard did not issue a dedicated press release and Hinkal does not appear on Mastercard's published program roster.

**[1] What Hinkal is.** Hinkal is an on-chain privacy / confidential-transactions protocol. It lets stablecoins settle in private balances and move via zero-knowledge proofs (ZK-SNARKs) plus stealth addresses, so a chain verifies a transfer without revealing amount, sender or recipient. It is **non-custodial** (funds never sit with Hinkal), and live on Ethereum, Polygon, Solana, TRON and other EVM chains; teams integrate via SDK/API. Existing deployments already in production: Polygon Wallet "Private Send" (powered by Hinkal — see [[Mastercard taps Polygon for Crypto Credential initiative]] for Mastercard's separate Polygon tie-up), Tether's Wallet Development Kit, Turnkey wallets, and Avvio. So the Mastercard tie is an incremental distribution/credibility step on top of an already-shipping product, not a new build.

**[2] The program & what membership confers.** The Crypto Partner Program (launched 2026-03-11, 85+ members incl. Binance, PayPal, Ripple, Circle, Gemini, Paxos, plus compliance infra like Chainalysis/Fireblocks/TRM) connects crypto-native companies to Mastercard teams, its global network (200+ countries), and each other. Stated benefits: moving past pilots via partner discovery and go-to-market collaboration; **select** members get access to issuing/acquiring financial institutions and regulatory/compliance support aligned with global standards. Target use cases: cross-border payments/remittances, B2B, treasury & settlement, stablecoin payments, and crypto cards. Crucially, membership is a collaboration framework — it is **not** a commercial contract, card program, or confirmed Mastercard product integration.

**[3] Compliance angle.** Both Mastercard's program and Hinkal foreground compliance. Hinkal screens funds before entry (ZK proof checks against the Chainalysis blacklist, enforced in-contract) and uses **viewing keys** so users/businesses can disclose transaction history to an auditor or regulator on demand — "private to the public, visible to the entitled." This is the pitch that separates Hinkal from mixer-style privacy tools (e.g. Tornado Cash) and is what makes "confidential stablecoin payments" plausible inside a regulated-payments program. The thesis: as stablecoins carry payroll, supplier payments and treasury, business-grade confidentiality becomes table-stakes rather than a niche request.

**[4] Internal context & trajectory.** Fits Mastercard's steady 2026 crypto push: program launch + expansion ([[Mastercard launches Crypto Partner Program]], [[Mastercard crypto program adds PayPal, Ripple, Circle, Binance]]), crypto-linked cards ([[SoFi, Orbi and Mastercard launch crypto-linked card in Mexico]]), Crypto Credential/Polygon identity work ([[Mastercard taps Polygon for Crypto Credential initiative]]), and stablecoin acquisitions (Mastercard–BVNK). Hinkal itself previously surfaced only tangentially in [[Stablecoin volumes grew 91% to $10.9 trillion last year]]. Net: a small privacy vendor earning a logo-level association with a large network — directionally interesting for the stablecoin-privacy theme, but low on confirmed commercial substance.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Two overlapping markets: (a) stablecoins as payment rails and (b) on-chain privacy / confidential-transaction infrastructure, converging under institutional-crypto-compliance. Stablecoin supply reached ~$314-322bn in mid-2026, with Citi and US Treasury Sec. Bessent projecting ~$420bn by year-end (per spark.money / stablecoininsider, as of Jun-2026); USDT+USDC hold >85% share. Monthly transaction volume hit a record ~$1.79tn in Jun-2026, though "economic" (non-bot) volume is far lower (~$4.2tn annualized per blockchain-data adjustments). Why now: 2025-26 was an inflection for on-chain privacy — the SEC closed its three-year Zcash review in Jan-2026 with no enforcement, and "pragmatic privacy" (confidentiality + compliance hooks / viewing keys for auditors) became the institutional-acceptable model, while FATF travel-rule enforcement tightened (per insights4vc, bex.co, 21shares, as of 2026). Structure: payment rails are consolidated (Visa/Mastercard duopoly + a handful of stablecoin issuers); privacy infra is fragmented and early — barriers are cryptographic R&D, chain integrations and, increasingly, a compliance story regulators accept.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for this layer: integrated chains/wallets (distribution), TVL / shielded-pool value, transaction throughput, and compliance features (selective disclosure / viewing keys). Four privacy architectures compete (per bex.co, Feb-2026): shielded pools (Zcash), sub-transaction privacy (Canton), encrypted extensions (Solana confidential transfers), private smart contracts (Aztec); Railgun is a direct shielded-pool peer. Hinkal's basis of competition is embeddability — it ships as an SDK/API inside third-party wallets and platforms (Polygon "Private Send", Tether Wallet Development Kit, Turnkey, Avvio), live on Ethereum, Polygon, Solana, TRON and EVM chains, with zk-proofs + pre-entry screening + viewing keys. Recent moves: Mastercard launched the Crypto Partner Program on 2026-03-11 with 85+ members (Circle, Binance, Ripple, Gemini, Paxos, PayPal; later Alchemy Pay), and both Visa and Mastercard are backing Open Standard's "Open USD" (per Bloomberg / cryptobriefing, 2026). Position: Hinkal is a niche-but-ahead specialist in the confidential-stablecoin slot — it is the privacy layer for others' products rather than a chain or issuer. Moat is intangibles (zk engineering + the compliance-aligned viewing-key design) plus modest embedding switching-costs `(analysis)`; it is not scale or network effects.

**Comps & multiples.** Internal comps (same program / stablecoin-rails theme): [[SoFi, Orbi and Mastercard launch crypto-linked card in Mexico]], [[Citi details stablecoin checkout with Coinbase]], [[zerohash named a Tempo validator for stablecoin rails]], [[Polygon Labs adds TRON support to Open Money Stack]]. Hinkal valuation: raised $4.1M (Nov-2023) and $1.4M (Apr-2024) at a ~$70M round valuation (per crypto-fundraising.info / search) — a seed-stage round mark, NOT a market cap. Revenue/TPV undisclosed → EV/Revenue, P/S, price-per-tx = **no data**; no trading multiple computable `[UNSOURCED]`. Macro anchor (the counterparty, not Hinkal): Mastercard Q1-2026 (quarter ended 2026-03-31) net revenue $8.4bn, +16% reported / +12% currency-neutral; value-added services & solutions net revenue $3.5bn, +22% reported — the fastest-growing segment, which houses digital-asset/new-flows and compliance services (per Mastercard Q1 2026 Earnings Release, 2026-04-30 — https://s25.q4cdn.com/479285134/files/doc_financials/2026/q1/1Q26-Mastercard-Earnings-Release.pdf; latest filed IR doc: 8-K 2026-06-17 — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1141391/000114139126000041/ma-20260616.htm). Comparison: a ~$70M seed micro-cap plugged into a ~$9tn-network, VAS-led incumbent — distribution asymmetry is the whole story; distribution not computed, qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Rails dependence / disintermediation.** Hinkal rides Mastercard's and partner wallets' distribution and owns neither the user nor the chain; program membership is non-exclusive (85+ members) and Mastercard could favor another privacy approach (Solana confidential transfers, Aztec) — second-order: Hinkal captures a thin feature-layer margin, not the payment economics.
2. **Regulatory tail-risk on privacy.** The model lives or dies on the "privacy-with-viewing-keys" framing staying acceptable; a FATF/sanctions crackdown on shielded transfers (the Tornado-Cash shadow) could force chains/wallets to drop embedded privacy — second-order: a single adverse ruling hits all integrations at once.
3. **"Announced vs live" + silent economics.** Program membership is a partner designation, not revenue; neither Hinkal nor Mastercard disclosed fees, volume routed, or fraud/compliance liability split — de-PR: this is a GTM and credibility stamp, not yet monetized scale.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Signals that privacy is becoming a compliance *feature* inside regulated stablecoin rails rather than a regulatory red flag — a small re-rating of "pragmatic privacy" infra legitimized by a card network. Falsifiable thesis: if confidential stablecoin payments gain institutional traction, embeddable compliant-privacy SDKs (Hinkal, Railgun) see rising integration counts; watch for (trigger) a named issuer/bank routing live stablecoin volume with privacy on, or Mastercard publishing a privacy standard. Breaks if regulators force selective-disclosure privacy off public chains, or if Mastercard/Visa build the capability in-house.
