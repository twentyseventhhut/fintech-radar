dLocal and Western Union expand their partnership to cover remittance payouts in Africa
 
Cameroon is the first market launching under this expansion, building on the collections partnership the two companies already run together.
3 min. read
 Montevideo, Uruguay, October 15, 2026 – dLocal (NASDAQ: DLO) , the leading payments platform connecting global merchants to emerging markets, and Western Union , a global leader in cross-border money movement, today announced an expansion of their partnership in Africa to include payouts. This adds to the collections work they already run together, with dLocal providing the technology and connectivity layer, while regulated local partners execute the relevant payment and payout services. The expansion will start with a live payout corridor in Cameroon.
 A more reliable journey, end to end 
This new chapter of the partnership gives remittance recipients in African markets a faster, more reliable way to receive money, in the local payment methods they already use day to day. In Cameroon, one of the region's more fragmented, wallet-based markets, recipients will be able to receive remittances directly into supported local wallets through local payout partners operating under applicable local regulatory frameworks (MNOs or financial institutions), closing a gap that has historically left them with few reliable options.
The reliability remittance senders already count on now extends all the way to the recipient's end too, creating one dependable path from send to receipt. The expansion also strengthens the foundation for Western Union to rely on a single partner across both collections and payouts, supporting continued growth of digital remittance corridors across emerging markets.
“At Western Union, our priority is always to ensure that our customers get their money reliably, seamlessly, wherever they may be,” said Mohamed Touhami El Ouazzani, Regional Vice President, Head of Africa at Western Union. "Extending our work with dLocal into payouts means we can offer that same reliability on the disbursement side too, starting in markets like Cameroon."
"Collections and payouts have always been treated as two separate problems," said Agustin Botta, Head of EMEA at dLocal. "Half a remittance solved is still half a problem. With Western Union, we're working together to close that gap, building a more consistent path from send to receipt."
 Beyond Cameroon 
Looking ahead, the two companies are exploring extending the payout expansion into other African markets, including Nigeria, with bank transfers and wallets, as part of a broader push to cover the full remittance journey across more emerging markets.
Emerging markets are at a turning point for remittances, as recipients increasingly expect the same speed and reliability on the receiving end that senders have come to rely on. The World Bank estimates that remittance flows into Sub-Saharan Africa reached roughly US$ 57B in 2024 , a reminder of how central this money is to the households waiting on it. dLocal and Western Union see this as one more step in building that reliability, corridor by corridor.

# Context-enrichment: dLocal and Western Union expand remittance payouts into Cameroon
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
dLocal (NASDAQ: DLO) and Western Union (NYSE: WU) extended an **existing collections partnership** to add the **payout (disbursement) leg**, with **one live corridor: Cameroon**. dLocal supplies the "technology and connectivity layer"; the actual regulated payment/payout is executed by **local partners (MNOs or financial institutions)** — i.e. dLocal is the orchestration/API, not the licensed payer in most legs. Recipients get funds into **local mobile wallets** (Cameroon is ~54% mobile-money-penetrated, dominated by MTN MoMo ~60% and Orange Money ~35%), replacing WU's historical **cash-agent** payout. Nigeria (bank transfer + wallets) is named only as **"exploring"** — forward-looking, not live.

**+ Why framed this way / what it reveals:** The PR leads on "reliability" and "closing the gap from send to receipt," which is soft language masking a narrow fact: this is **one country, wallet-only, via third-party licensees**. The real strategic hook, understated in the PR, is WU consolidating onto **"a single partner across both collections and payouts"** — vendor consolidation, not a new product. Note the corpus connection the PR omits: dLocal's June 2026 scaled-back AZA Finance asset deal (see [[dLocal closes scaled-back $23.7 million AZA Finance asset deal]]) explicitly left it with **AZA's Cameroonian payments license, IP and African client book** — so Cameroon being "first" is almost certainly **license-driven**, not demand-driven. That reframes the news from "chose Cameroon for the opportunity" to "launched where it already had the rails."

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Onafriq (ex-MFS Africa):** largest African mobile-wallet interoperability network (~500M wallets, 35+ countries) — the deepest incumbent payout rail dLocal competes against for exactly this job.
- **Thunes:** cross-border payout network already live in Africa (see [[Thunes and Ecobank to power instant payments in Africa]]) — direct peer for "last-mile to wallet/bank."
- **Flutterwave:** collections-depth leader across 30+ African markets; also remittance payout.
- WU's own African payout history is **multi-partner, not dLocal-exclusive**: cf. [[Western Union, Zoona and Chipper Cash launch transfers in Zambia]] and [[Western Union and Sasai Fintech launch Africa money transfer app]] — WU hedges across local partners per corridor.

**+ Why the landscape is this way:** Last-mile emerging-market payout is a **commodity-with-a-moat** business: the moat is local licenses + wallet integrations, which Onafriq/Thunes already hold at scale. dLocal's edge is **bundling collections + payout on one API/contract for the merchant (here, WU)** — convenience, not unique reach. Second-order: if WU standardizes on dLocal, the value accrues to whoever controls the wallet rail (MTN/Orange), not necessarily to dLocal, which stays the **low-margin orchestration layer**.

## [2] Company history / fit
dLocal's 2025–26 arc is a push from LatAm collections into **global payouts + Africa**: AZA Finance acquisition announced Jun-2025 ([[dLocal to acquire African remittance firm AZA Finance]]), closed scaled-back Jun-2026 ([[dLocal closes scaled-back $23.7 million AZA Finance asset deal]], Cameroon license acquired), National Exchange payout deal across Africa/APAC/LatAm Apr-2026 ([[dLocal and National Exchange partner on remittance payouts]]), and stablecoin-funded remittances with Félix ([[dLocal and Félix launch WhatsApp remittances to Central America]]). Q2 2026: TPV ~$17.7B (+92% YoY), Africa+Asia revenue ~$73M (+36% YoY); management flags **payout mix growing but lower-margin**.

**+ Why dLocal acts this way:** Collections take-rate is compressing and the business is mixing toward lower-margin local-to-local payouts — so dLocal must **add corridors/volume to defend gross profit**, and reuse acquired licenses (AZA/Cameroon) to monetize sunk M&A cost. This deal fits: it turns a license it already paid for into billable WU flow.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Genuinely **new vs the corpus**: prior WU-Africa notes are about *sending* apps (Sasai, Zambia/Chipper); prior dLocal-WU items were **collections only**. This is the **first live WU payout corridor through dLocal** → a real "collections → payouts" follow-up, not a reprint. BUT traction is minimal today: **one country, wallet-only, zero disclosed volume/fees/pricing**, and the delivery is through unnamed local licensees.

**+ Why value-add is thin (1–2 levels deeper):** The margin in remittance payout sits with (a) the wallet (MTN/Orange cash-out fees ~1.5–2%) and (b) WU's FX/send spread. dLocal earns a **thin orchestration fee** and bears integration cost. The durable value is only if WU **consolidates many corridors** onto dLocal (the stated ambition) — a volume game, not a pricing-power game. Disintermediation risk: WU could integrate wallets directly or use Onafriq/Thunes per corridor.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Stated plan: extend payouts to **Nigeria (bank + wallets)** and more African markets "corridor by corridor"; anchor claim is World Bank's ~$57B 2024 Sub-Saharan Africa remittance inflow (TAM framing, not addressable revenue). Sentiment on dLocal is constructive on TPV growth but wary on **margin dilution from payouts**.

**+ Why / second-order:** The counterintuitive read: landing more WU payout volume can **grow TPV while diluting gross margin** — exactly the mix-shift management already flagged. So this partnership is good for the "emerging-markets infrastructure" narrative and bad for near-term margin optics. Regulatory: executing via licensed MNOs/FIs keeps dLocal out of direct licensing liability but makes delivery dependent on partners it doesn't control.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) remittance inflows reached ~US$57bn in 2024 (per World Bank, via the dLocal PR, as of 2024) — the number anchoring this corridor play. SSA is the world's most expensive remittance region and one of the most fragmented: payouts fan out across mobile-money wallets (MNO-dominated in Cameroon), banks and cash agents, with few interoperable rails — which is exactly the gap dLocal is monetizing. Structure: the collections side (sender) has long been well served; the payout/disbursement side is the fragmented, under-built half, so value accrues to whoever can aggregate local wallet/MNO endpoints under one API and absorb local licensing. "Why now": (1) regulated-partner payout models are maturing, letting a tech layer like dLocal sit on top of licensed MNOs/FIs rather than hold every license itself; (2) incumbents (Western Union) are pivoting from retail cash to digital — branded digital was 43% of WU CMT transactions in Q2 2026 (per WU, 2026-07-30) — and need reliable last-mile payout partners to defend share.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for dLocal — TPV, net take rate (GP/TPV), gross profit growth; for a money-transfer operator — cross-border principal, transaction volume, digital mix. Key players / basis of competition: dLocal competes on breadth of emerging-market payout endpoints and single-API/single-contract distribution ("One dLocal"); money-transfer peers (WU, which here is a *customer* not a rival, plus Remitly, Thunes, Chipper, Nala, Félix) compete on price + corridor coverage; MNOs own the local wallet rail. Recent moves: WU acquired Intermex and leaned into digital through 2026 (per fxcintel, 2026); dLocal keeps extending corridors — e.g. [[dLocal and Félix launch stablecoin WhatsApp remittances in LatAm]] (2025-11) and wallet rails like [[Thunes launches instant transfers to China via UnionPay]] (2026-01) show the same aggregator-of-payouts pattern across the sector. Protagonist's position: dLocal is *ahead* as an infrastructure layer in emerging-market payouts — moat is intangible/network (accumulated local licenses, MNO/FI connections, single contract = switching costs for enterprise clients like WU) `(analysis)`. Not disclosed: corridor-level economics, who bears FX/fraud liability — `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Comps & multiples.** dLocal (NASDAQ: DLO): price ~$15.04 (2026-10-15), market cap ~$4.0–4.4bn, EV ~$3.5bn (per stockanalysis/investing, Oct 2026). Against dLocal's own Q2 2026 scale — revenue $399.7m in the quarter, i.e. annualizing ~$1.6bn run-rate — P/S ~3.1x and EV/Sales ~2.6x (reported directly by sources; EV/Sales = $3.5bn / ~$1.35bn LTM = ~2.6x, reported). EV/EBITDA ~12.6x (per stockanalysis, Oct 2026). These are *modest* multiples for a name growing TPV +92% YoY — i.e. in-line-to-cheap vs growth, consistent with the market pricing in take-rate compression (see risks). EPS guide $1.75–1.85 (per WU, 2026-07-30) — trades on a single-digit P/E, the opposite profile; included only to frame the two sides of this deal (dLocal = growth infra, WU = legacy distribution). Internal comps: [[dLocal reports Q1 2026 results with payment volume up 73%]], [[dLocal and Félix launch stablecoin WhatsApp remittances in LatAm]], [[Noah and Nala launch instant stablecoin settlement network]]. Distribution not computed (only one directly comparable listed payout-infra name); qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Take-rate compression is structural, not transient.** Net take rate fell to 0.72% in Q2 2026 from 1.07% YoY (per earnings coverage, 2026-08) as volume skews to large enterprise/low-margin geos. A remittance-payout corridor like Cameroon is high-volume, low-value-per-transaction — it likely *adds TPV while diluting take rate further*. Second-order: revenue/GP growth decouples from headline TPV growth, so the "+92% TPV" narrative can mask flat gross-profit dollars.
2. **Customer concentration / rails dependence.** This expansion deepens reliance on a single large client (Western Union) across both collections *and* payouts. dLocal gains volume but cedes negotiating leverage; if WU in-sources or multi-sources payout rails, the corridor economics evaporate. Mirror risk: dLocal itself depends on local MNOs/FIs executing the payout, so it captures only the thin connectivity-layer margin, not the full remittance spread.
3. **Regulatory/FX fragility in single-country corridors.** Cameroon (CEMAC/BEAC FX controls, MNO-dominated) and the flagged next step Nigeria (recurrent FX/repatriation friction) are exactly the markets where a licensing change or FX regime shift can shut a corridor overnight — execution is "live in one corridor," everything beyond Cameroon is forward-looking ("exploring"), not committed.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is consolidation of the *payout* half of the remittance stack toward infrastructure aggregators: WU is effectively outsourcing emerging-market last-mile, validating dLocal as the "payout rail of record" for incumbents rather than just a merchant-collections play. Falsifiable thesis: if dLocal converts WU payouts beyond Cameroon (Nigeria, bank + wallet) into *disclosed, growing gross-profit dollars*, it re-rates from a take-rate-compression story to a volume-scale story. Trigger to watch: next quarter's net take rate and GP growth vs TPV growth, and whether a second major MTO signs for payouts. What breaks it: take rate keeps sliding below ~0.70% while GP growth stalls — then TPV wins are vanity, and the corridor expansion is subsidizing WU's margins, not dLocal's.
