 Генпрокуратура отказалась от иска об обращении в доход РФ имущества президента FIDE Тимура Турлова 
Заместитель генпрокурора РФ Игорь Ткачев отказался от иска о конфискации имущества президента Международной шахматной федерации (FIDE) Тимура Турлова и связанных с ним структур.
"14 октября через экспедицию было получено заявление об отказе (от исковых требований), подписанное заместителем генерального прокурора Ткачевым", - сказала судья в Тверском суде Москвы, где решается вопрос о назначении даты начала заседания по иску.
В начале октября Генеральная прокуратура России подала судебный иск о конфискации имущества Турлова и связанных с ним структур. Иск заявлен на обращение в доход государства денежных средств и иных активов, а также движимого и недвижимого имущества, на общую сумму в сотни миллионов долларов, сообщили " Интерфаксу " в правоохранительных органах.
Помимо Турлова ответчиками по иску заявлены связанные с ним компании Freedom Holding Corp., Freedom Finance Europe Ltd и W-Empirical Holding Corp., а также деловой партнер Турлова предприниматель Аркадий Щукин.
Ожидалось, что беседа по иску пройдет в суде 15 октября.
Турлов в 2022 году отказался от российского гражданства в пользу казахстанского. Он основатель, мажоритарный акционер и глава финансовой группы Freedom Holding Corp., которая зарегистрирована в США, и имеет листинг на бирже Nasdaq. В РФ группа Турлова была активным игроком на рынке ценных бумаг, но в июне 2022 года объявила о выходе с российского рынка, а также о предстоящей продаже активов локальному менеджменту. В начале 2023 года сделка была закрыта, а бывшие активы Freedom в России были переименованы. В конце сентября Турлов был избран главой FIDE.
Freedom Holding Corp. зарегистрирована в США и имеет подразделения более чем в 20 странах, среди которых Казахстан, Кипр, Польша, Испания, Узбекистан, Азербайджан. Корпорация работает в финансовой сфере, включая розничное брокерское обслуживание, инвестиционное консультирование, торговлю ценными бумагами, маркет-мейкинг, корпоративный инвестиционный банкинг, андеррайтинг, страхование.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] TL;DR** — On 14 Oct 2026, Russia's Deputy Prosecutor General Igor Tkachev formally withdrew the Prosecutor General's lawsuit seeking to confiscate "hundreds of millions of dollars" in assets from Timur Turlov — founder/majority owner of Nasdaq-listed Freedom Holding Corp. (FRHC) and newly elected FIDE (world chess) president — and related entities. A Tverskoy/Tver District Court in Moscow terminated the case; the preliminary hearing had been set for 15 Oct. The suit had named Turlov, Freedom Holding Corp., Freedom Finance Europe Ltd, W-Empirical Holding Corp. and business partner Arkady Shchukin.

**[1] What happened / timeline**
- ~24 Sep 2026: Prosecutor General's Office files the confiscation suit in Moscow's Tverskoy District Court (filed two days before the FIDE election).
- 26 Sep 2026: Turlov elected president of FIDE (International Chess Federation).
- ~1–2 Oct 2026: Suit becomes public (Meduza, Interfax, Novaya Gazeta Europe). Claim: transfer to the Russian state of cash and movable/immovable property worth hundreds of millions of USD. Russian media (Kommersant) report the likely legal basis is seeking to declare Turlov and co-defendants "extremists" over alleged financial support to Ukraine.
- 2 Oct 2026: Freedom Holding publicly responds it is "bewildered," stresses it has NO assets or operations in Russia and sees no material risk to the holding.
- 14 Oct 2026: Deputy Prosecutor General Tkachev files a statement withdrawing the claim; court registers it. Hearing that was scheduled for 15 Oct is mooted; case terminated. (Note: in Russian procedure, withdrawal of a claim is resolved by the court and generally bars re-filing the same claim.)

**[2] Why it matters**
- The target is not a fringe actor: Freedom Holding Corp. is a US-registered, Nasdaq-listed (FRHC) financial group with brokerage/banking units across 20+ countries (Kazakhstan, Cyprus, Poland, Spain, Uzbekistan, Azerbaijan, Türkiye). A confiscation/extremist designation in Russia would have been reputational/sanctions-adjacent noise even though the group exited Russia in 2022–23.
- Rapid reversal (suit → public outcry → withdrawal in ~2 weeks) is unusual for Russian asset-seizure drives, which typically proceed to judgment. The withdrawal removes an overhang but the motive/backstory remains opaque.
- Governance optics: Turlov renounced Russian citizenship for Kazakh in 2022; Freedom sold its Russian brokerage to local management (closed early 2023, assets rebranded). The case tested how Russian legal pressure could reach a founder of a US-listed firm now leading an international sports body.

**[3] Prior coverage (internal) & relations**
- [[Turkish regulator approves Freedom Holding acquisition of Turkish Bank]] (2026-07) — FRHC expansion into Türkiye via Freedom Finansal Hizmetler AŞ.
- [[Freedom Holding enters Turkey's banking market]] (2026-03) — TurkishBank acquisition (Ozyol Holding + National Bank of Kuwait transferring 99.31%).
- Mentioned in passing: [[Ozon to launch brokerage arm Ozon Investments in autumn 2026]], [[Russia's central bank rejects Sber, Alfa, T-Bank payment system project]] (Freedom Finance Russia legacy references).
- This story is the regulation/legal thread; the others are the M&A/expansion thread for the same group.

**[4] Open questions / what to watch**
- Does the Russian state re-file under a different theory, or pursue the alleged "extremist/aid-to-Ukraine" angle administratively? (An administrative lawsuit was also reportedly filed — iz.ru.)
- Any FRHC (Nasdaq) share-price or 8-K disclosure reaction — no confirmed stock move found.
- Fate of co-defendant Arkady Shchukin and W-Empirical Holding Corp.
- Interaction with Ukraine's Dec 2022 sanctions on Freedom Finance/Turlov (opposite-side pressure).

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a legal/political-risk event, not a deal — the market layer is "Russia's post-2022 asset-nationalization / conversion-to-state wave and its selective reversals," plus Freedom Holding's (Nasdaq: FRHC) position as a Kazakhstan-domiciled brokerage group with a public US listing and residual Russia-adjacency. Facts: on ~2026-09-24/10-01 Russia's Prosecutor General filed an administrative suit at Moscow's Tverskoy court seeking to convert to state revenue "hundreds of millions of dollars" of assets of Turlov and linked entities (Freedom Holding Corp., Freedom Finance Europe Ltd, W-Empirical Holding Corp., partner Arkady Shchukin), on a proposed "extremist" designation tied to his ~$2m donation to Ukraine's embassy in Kazakhstan and FRHC's €2m to UNITED24 (per Meduza/worldchess, 2026-10-01). On 2026-10-14/15 the deputy prosecutor general withdrew the claim; the court terminated proceedings — which bars re-filing the same demands on the same grounds (per TASS/Moscow Times/Novaya Gazeta Europe, 2026-10-15). Structure/"why now": the timing (filed days after Turlov's 2026-09-26 election as FIDE president, the first non-Russian in 30 years, who kept sanctions on Russian chess; withdrawn two weeks later) reads as politically instrumented pressure rather than an asset grab with economic logic — FRHC states it has no assets in Russia and does not operate there (exited 2022, sold local arm to management early 2023, per kz.kursiv.media). The barrier being tested here is jurisdictional reach, not capital or regulation.

**Competitive landscape.** The relevant "KPIs" for FRHC as a broker-bank group: retail brokerage customer base, fee & commission income, interest income, segment net income. Scale (Q1 FY2027, qtr ended 2026-06-30): revenue $732.5m (+40% YoY from $524.0m); net income $31.7m ($0.52/dil); fee & commission income $156.7m (+46%); interest income $295.1m (+49%); segment net income — Brokerage $133.6m, Banking $3.7m, with Insurance (-$19.2m) and Other (-$86.5m) dragging (per Nasdaq/Yahoo, 2026-08-10). FY2026 (ended 2026-03): revenue $2.19bn, net income $153.3m. The "peers" for this specific event are other ex-Russia financiers / foreign owners caught in the nationalization wave, not product competitors. Recent in-base moves showing the wave is live and mostly NOT reversing: [[LUKOIL-Carlyle foreign asset sale agreement expired in July]], [[Germany begins privatization of nationalized Gazprom unit]], [[Rosatom completes buyout of Delo Group for 77bn rubles]], [[PSB auctions Agropromkredit bank for 2.85bn rubles]], [[PSB fails third auction for nationalized Vektor Rail]], [[RZD's fourth Riga Station auction fails without bids]]. Turlov's position: an outlier where the state *reversed course fast* — because the economic prize was near-zero (no Russian assets) and the cost (pressure on a sitting FIDE president, a Nasdaq issuer) outweighed it. `(analysis)` The "moat" protecting Turlov was precisely his 2022 exit — having no Russian footprint left little to seize.

**Comps & multiples.** Protagonist FRHC (public): price ~$176 (2026-10-15), market cap ~$11.2bn, ~63.8m shares out (per CNN/benzinga/wallstreetzen, 2026-10-15).
- **P/S (TTM)** ≈ $11.2bn / ~$2.65bn TTM revenue ≈ **~4.2x** (TTM revenue approximated from FY2026 $2.19bn + Q1 FY2027 +40% run-rate; treat as `(analysis)`, not a clean filing figure). Vs the July note's ~4.0x P/S on a ~$8.8bn cap — the multiple held while the cap re-rated up ~27% (to ~$11.2bn, near a 52-wk high of $178), i.e. growth-driven, not multiple expansion.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Russia political/legal risk is live and arbitrary, not retired.** The claim was dropped for political convenience (FIDE optics), not on a finding of no wrongdoing; the state demonstrated willingness to weaponize an "extremist"/Ukraine-aid theory against a Nasdaq issuer. Second-order: any future deterioration in the Turlov↔Kremlin relationship (chess politics, sanctions posture) could resurface pressure on *other* grounds, even if these specific demands are barred — a recurring headline/sentiment overhang rather than a balance-sheet hit.
2. **Governance / disclosure overhang compounds the optics.** FRHC still carries the unresolved 2023 Hindenburg short-report history (alleged sanctions evasion, AML/KYC, FST Belize revenue) and ~69% founder control (per prior note / Simply Wall St). Second-order: a Russia "extremist"/sanctions-flavored suit — even withdrawn — feeds the exact narrative short-sellers and US regulators (DOJ Mass./SEC Boston probes reported Oct 2023) already probe; concentrated control weakens minority protection.
3. **Key-man + Kazakhstan concentration.** The entire episode is a personalized attack on Turlov; FRHC's franchise, control and strategy are inseparable from him, and the earnings engine remains Kazakhstan-centric (KZT) with a loss-making "Other" segment. Second-order: idiosyncratic founder/jurisdiction shocks transmit directly to the equity.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` For the ex-Russia-financier cohort, this is a rare *reversal* of the nationalization wave — but a non-durable, non-precedential one: it worked only because there were no Russian assets to take and the target held an inconvenient international office. Falsifiable thesis: the withdrawal is cosmetic and FRHC's equity is indifferent to Russia legal noise as long as the Kazakhstan/Turkey growth story compounds (Q2 FY2027 due ~2026-11-06). Trigger to watch: the Q2 FY2027 print and whether the "Other" segment loss narrows; what breaks the thesis — a *re-filed* Russia action on new grounds, a US (SEC/DOJ) escalation, or a Turkey/SPK or AML setback that lets the Hindenburg narrative re-attach to a concrete regulator action.
