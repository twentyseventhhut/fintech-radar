The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)  plans to use wholesale central bank digital currency (CBDC) to settle tokenised MAS Bills.
MAS Managing Director Chia Der Jiun outlined the plans at the INSEAD Digital Finance & Agentic AI Summit on 9 October.
The initiative aims to bring tokenised assets and digital central bank money onto common infrastructure to support settlement finality.
MAS made wholesale Singapore dollar CBDC available through its Singapore dollar Test Network in 2025 .
Its first successful trial involved the settlement of interbank overnight lending transactions between DBS, OCBC and UOB.
 “As usage of private digital money scales, CBDCs will ultimately be needed to anchor monetary stability,” 
Chia said.
Through its BLOOM initiative , MAS is working with the industry on domestic and cross-border settlement using tokenised bank deposits and regulated stablecoins.
The regulator has also consulted on legislative amendments to implement its Single-Currency Stablecoin framework , covering reserve backing, capital requirements, redemption and disclosure.
MAS Pushes for Wider Adoption of Tokenised Assets
The regulator is working to expand commercial tokenisation through Project Guardian , with adoption still largely confined to smaller ecosystems.
Its Global Layer One (GL1) initiative is also developing common standards to improve interoperability between digital financial networks.
MAS recently published Version 1.1 of its Safeguards for Agentic Finance at Runtime (SAFR) white paper .
It also released an open-source reference implementation to support controls over AI agents executing financial transactions.

# Context-enrichment: MAS plans to settle tokenised MAS Bills with wholesale CBDC
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**Freshness: FRESH.** This is a new policy signal from Chia Der Jiun's keynote "Building Trusted Foundations for Digital Finance" at the INSEAD Digital Finance & Agentic AI Summit on 9 Oct 2026 (MAS speech published same date). It is distinct from — and a sequel to — [[MAS launches BLOOM for tokenised liabilities and stablecoins]] (Oct 2025, which covered tokenised *bank liabilities/stablecoins*, not central-bank-money settlement of *government bills*). Not a duplicate of any prior note.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
De-PR'd: MAS *stated an intention* ("plans to", "next step", "will develop the capability") to settle **tokenised MAS Bills** (short-dated MAS-issued money-market instruments used for SGD liquidity management) using **wholesale SGD CBDC** on common infrastructure, targeting atomic DvP settlement with finality. This is a **speech/roadmap signal, not a live product** — no issuance size, no launch date beyond "2026" in secondary coverage, no named participant roster for *this specific* bills pilot in the primary MAS text. Press framing ("MAS launches tokenised government bills pilot") over-states it; the primary source is forward-looking.
- **Why framed this way:** the keynote's thesis is a hierarchy-of-money argument — Chia explicitly cautioned that **stablecoins "may not be fit for large wholesale settlement"** and "cannot be assumed to hold par value," so "CBDCs will ultimately be needed to anchor monetary stability." Announcing a concrete wholesale-CBDC use case (settling the central bank's *own* bills) is the proof-point that gives that thesis teeth. It reveals MAS positioning **central-bank money, not private stablecoins, as the terminal settlement asset** for wholesale tokenised markets.
- **Second order:** settling MAS's own paper is the lowest-risk, highest-control first instrument — MAS controls both the asset (the bill) and the cash leg (wholesale CBDC), so it can demonstrate end-to-end atomic settlement without depending on commercial-bank balance-sheet risk. It is a sandbox for the *infrastructure*, sequenced ahead of tokenised SGS/corporate bonds.

## [1] Competitors / peers (other central banks)
- **BIS Project Agorá** (launched Apr 2024; 7 central banks incl. Fed, ECB, BoE, BoJ, BoK + 40+ private firms via IIF): atomic settlement of cross-border tokenised deposits + wholesale CBDC — but **experimental prototype, lessons-learned report due H1 2026**, not production.
- **BIS Project Helvetia (SNB)**: already settled tokenised bonds in *live* wholesale CBDC on SIX Digital Exchange — arguably further along on the *exact* use case (tokenised securities settled in real wholesale CBDC).
- **Bank of England**: [[Bank of England will not rush digital pound, eyes stablecoins]] (Oct 2026) — explicitly *deprioritising* retail CBDC, leaning on stablecoins + RTGS 24/7 upgrade; a deliberately *different* bet than MAS's wholesale-CBDC-as-anchor stance.
- **ECB**: pursuing wholesale DLT settlement (Pontes/Appia) rather than issuing a tokenised sovereign-bill equivalent.
- **Position:** MAS is **in the front rank but not uniquely first** — SNB/Helvetia reached live tokenised-bond settlement in wholesale CBDC earlier. MAS's differentiation is tokenising *its own central-bank bills* and the breadth of its surrounding stack (Guardian + BLOOM + GL1).
- **Why the landscape splits:** jurisdictions diverge on the terminal asset — Singapore/BIS-Agorá camp backs wholesale CBDC; UK/US lean toward stablecoins + upgraded RTGS. The second-order dynamic: whoever's standard wins determines whether banks or central banks sit at the base of tokenised settlement.

## [2] Company (MAS) history / fit
MAS has a multi-year, deliberately sequenced tokenisation programme: **Project Guardian** (2022–, asset tokenisation pilots, "still largely confined to smaller ecosystems" per the source), **Global Layer One / GL1** (shared-ledger interoperability standards), wholesale SGD CBDC made available on the **SGD Test Network in 2025** (first trial: interbank overnight lending between DBS, OCBC, UOB), and **BLOOM** (Oct 2025, tokenised bank liabilities + regulated stablecoins across G10/major-Asian currencies; see [[MAS launches BLOOM for tokenised liabilities and stablecoins]]). It also runs the Single-Currency Stablecoin framework and delayed cryptoasset capital rules to 2027 ([[MAS delays cryptoasset capital rules to 2027]]).
- **Why MAS acts this way:** as a small open economy and financial hub with no monetary-sovereignty-at-home panic, MAS competes on *infrastructure leadership* — being the reference regulator/rails for tokenised finance is itself the strategic asset. Settling its own bills in wholesale CBDC is the logical capstone of an infrastructure it has been assembling layer by layer (standards → test network → bank-liability settlement → now central-bank-asset settlement).
- Fits logically: it converts the 2025 test-network capability from a lab demo into a use case against a real, recurring instrument.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new vs prior MAS work:** yes — prior pilots settled interbank lending and (via BLOOM) bank liabilities/stablecoins; this applies wholesale CBDC to **MAS's own tradable bills**, i.e. a tokenised sovereign money-market asset + central-bank cash leg on one ledger.
- **Globally novel:** partially — Helvetia already did live tokenised-bond-vs-wCBDC; the specific twist (central bank tokenising its *own* bills) is less common.
- **Traction (the honest read):** **none yet confirmed.** This is "plans to / will develop the capability." The only live traction MAS can cite is the 2025 DBS/OCBC/UOB overnight-lending trial and Guardian pilots that MAS itself admits remain in "smaller ecosystems." Treat as roadmap, not adoption.
- **Who captures value / why it matters:** if wholesale CBDC becomes the settlement anchor, **central-bank money stays at the base of the tokenised stack** and stablecoins are relegated to a transactional/retail layer — structurally limiting how far private stablecoin issuers (StraitsX, Circle, etc.) can move "up" into wholesale settlement. That is the real stake, and it is why Chia's stablecoin caveat and the bills plan are two halves of one message.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Plans:** develop capability to settle tokenised MAS Bills with SGD wholesale CBDC on common infrastructure; continue Guardian (expand commercial tokenisation), GL1 (interoperability standards), BLOOM (domestic + cross-border settlement via tokenised deposits/regulated stablecoins); legislative amendments for the Single-Currency Stablecoin framework; SAFR v1.1 white paper + open-source reference implementation for agentic-finance controls.
- **Sentiment:** regional tokenisation momentum is real and corroborated — [[Sygnum brings Fidelity tokenized liquidity fund to Singapore]] and [[OCBC and Ant International launch tokenised deposit solution]] (both Oct 2026) show the private side filling in; Singapore crypto economy grew 55% to ~$284bn in H1 2026.
- **Risks:** (a) perennial gap between MAS pilots and scaled adoption ("confined to smaller ecosystems"); (b) wholesale CBDC addresses only the interbank layer — it does not by itself solve end-user/retail tokenised money; (c) policy risk that the "CBDC-as-anchor" bet loses to the stablecoin+RTGS model BoE is pursuing.
- **Counterintuitive second-order:** by publicly doubting stablecoins for *large wholesale* settlement while embracing them in BLOOM for smaller flows, MAS is *bifurcating* money by use case — which could legitimise stablecoins at retail/mid-market even as it fences them out of the wholesale core.

### Red-team / challenge questions

1. **Announced or launched?** Launched — no. The primary MAS text is forward-looking ("plans to", "next step", "will develop the capability"). Secondary coverage saying "launches pilot" over-states it. *Verdict: roadmap signal, not a live product.*
2. **Is this a duplicate of BLOOM (Oct 2025)?** No. BLOOM = settlement of tokenised *bank liabilities + regulated stablecoins*. This = wholesale *CBDC* settling *MAS's own bills*. Sequential, distinct. Fresh.
3. **Has any central bank already done the exact thing (live)?** Largely yes for the generic case — BIS Project Helvetia (SNB/SDX) settled tokenised bonds in *live* wholesale CBDC. The narrower novelty is a central bank tokenising *its own bills*. Open on whether that specific wrinkle matters operationally.
4. **What's the issuance size / launch date / named participants for THIS bills pilot?** Open — not disclosed in the primary source. Secondary sources say "2026" and reuse the DBS/OCBC/UOB trio, but that trio is from the *2025 overnight-lending* trial; attributing them to the bills pilot is unverified.
5. **Is there any real traction/adoption?** No confirmed adoption for this item. Only prior traction: the 2025 DBS/OCBC/UOB overnight-lending trial and Guardian pilots MAS itself calls "confined to smaller ecosystems."
6. **Precise mechanism delta vs incumbents?** Atomic DvP of a tokenised sovereign money-market instrument against tokenised central-bank money on one ledger with settlement finality — same principle as Helvetia/Agorá, applied to MAS's own paper.
7. **Why tokenise MAS Bills first (not SGS or corporate bonds)?** (analysis) MAS controls both legs (asset + cash), minimising counterparty/credit risk and isolating the infrastructure test — the cleanest possible first instrument.
8. **What is MAS silent about?** No volumes, no go-live date, no settlement-window/UX detail, no cost/efficiency metrics vs today's MEPS+ RTGS, no interoperability commitment to GL1 for this specific flow.
9. **Who loses if wholesale CBDC becomes the settlement anchor?** Private stablecoin issuers (StraitsX/XSGD, Circle) — fenced out of wholesale core even as BLOOM admits them for smaller flows. The stablecoin caveat and the bills plan are one coordinated message.
10. **Is the stablecoin caveat new policy or restatement?** (analysis) Consistent with MAS's existing Single-Currency Stablecoin framework; the *framing* ("not fit for large wholesale settlement") is a sharper public line that elevates CBDC as terminal asset.
11. **What-if the CBDC-as-anchor bet is wrong?** BoE is betting the other way (stablecoins + 24/7 RTGS, no retail CBDC). If markets standardise on stablecoin+RTGS, MAS's wholesale-CBDC rail risks being a well-engineered island.
12. **Does wholesale CBDC solve the actual bottleneck?** Only the interbank layer. It does nothing for retail/end-user tokenised money; the "scaling" problem MAS cites is mostly downstream of this.
13. **Is "common infrastructure + settlement finality" new vs MEPS+ today?** (open) RTGS already gives finality; the delta is *atomic DvP with tokenised assets on shared ledger* — benefit is conditional on tokenised asset issuance actually scaling, which Guardian has not yet achieved.
14. **How much is hub-positioning vs economic necessity?** (analysis) Mostly positioning — Singapore faces no monetary-sovereignty threat; the payoff is being the reference rail/regulator for tokenised finance, an intangible strategic asset.
15. **Second-order risk of bifurcating money by use case?** By doubting stablecoins for wholesale while using them in BLOOM for smaller flows, MAS may legitimise stablecoins at retail/mid-market precisely as it walls them off from the wholesale core — a double-edged signal.

Importance: 3/5 — Credible, well-sequenced step from a top-tier regulator that sharpens the global "CBDC vs stablecoin as settlement anchor" debate, and the stablecoin-caveat framing has signalling weight. But it is a speech/roadmap with no live product, no figures, no confirmed participants, no new adoption, and the core capability (tokenised-asset-vs-wholesale-CBDC settlement) already exists live elsewhere (Helvetia). Directionally important, operationally not yet material.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Subvertical: wholesale CBDC / tokenised government securities / DLT settlement — central-bank-money-on-chain, not retail CBDC. Market structure is still pre-commercial: a cluster of national/regional pilots (MAS, SNB, ECB, UK, BIS) racing to put sovereign/safe assets and settlement money on common infrastructure; no TAM figure is publicly verifiable here (no data — don't invent). Structure is concentrated by design: central banks are the sole issuers of settlement money, so the "value chain" splits between the CB (the risk-free cash leg) and private market-infra/custody/tokenisation platforms (the asset leg + rails). Entry barrier = regulatory monopoly on CB money + market-infrastructure licensing, not capital. Drivers / "why now": (1) settlement finality — delivery-vs-payment for tokenised assets needs an on-ledger risk-free cash leg, which only CB money provides (private stablecoins/deposits carry issuer credit risk); Chia framed it exactly this way ("CBDCs will ultimately be needed to anchor monetary stability"). (2) Policy competition — Singapore is sequencing wholesale CBDC → BLOOM (tokenised deposits/stablecoins) → Project Guardian/GL1 (asset standards) to keep its tokenisation hub status as the EU (Pontes live 21 Sep 2026) and UK (DIGIT gilt pilot) move. Second-order: whichever jurisdiction lands a liquid on-chain safe-asset + CB-cash leg first sets the de-facto interoperability standard others must bridge to.

**Competitive landscape.** This is not a company with KPIs — MAS is a regulator; the relevant "KPIs" for the sector are adoption metrics: number of live settlements vs pilots, value settled, and breadth of participating banks. De-PR'd: MAS's wholesale SGD CBDC is a *test-network* capability (available 2025), with one disclosed live trial (interbank overnight lending among DBS, OCBC, UOB); settling tokenised MAS Bills is *announced/planned*, not live. Peer central banks and recent dated moves: SNB **Project Helvetia III** — in production since Dec 2023 on SIX Digital Exchange (SDX); seven tokenised bond issuances + one secondary trade settled in real wholesale CHF CBDC (per BIS) — the only program with repeated *live* sovereign/quasi-sovereign settlement. ECB — Governing Council committed 1 Jul 2025 to DLT settlement in CB money via dual track: **Pontes** (bridge to TARGET, went live 21 Sep 2026 with ~13 banks + 4 DLT operators incl. Clearstream, SWIAT) and **Appia** (long-term native). UK — **DIGIT** digital gilt, six JLMs named (Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, Morgan Stanley, NatWest, RBC) on HSBC Orion, issuance targeted Q1 2027. MAS's position: *catching up on live settlement* (SNB is ahead with production bond settlement) but *ahead on breadth* — Guardian (40+ institutions, 15+ trials, 6 currencies) + GL1 cross-border standards give it the widest ecosystem play. Moat for Singapore = network effects + intangibles (standard-setting via GL1/Guardian), not scale `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/deal metrics in this item (regulator initiative) — EV/Revenue, P/E etc. = **not applicable, no data**; this is a policy/infrastructure comp, not a trading comp. Qualitative peer comparison instead (distribution not computed):
- SNB Helvetia III — live wholesale CBDC bond settlement on SDX since Dec 2023 (most mature).
- ECB Pontes/Appia — live bridge since Sep 2026; EUR 1.5bn+ processed in 2024 exploratory trials (per ECB).
- UK DIGIT — gilt issuance pilot, Q1 2027 target (earliest-stage).
Internal comps from base: [[MAS launches BLOOM for tokenised liabilities and stablecoins]] (the private-money layer MAS is pairing with wholesale CBDC), [[ECB outlines three models for central bank money on-chain]] (direct comp — same design question, published 2026-10-05), [[UK names six banks for first digital gilt issuance]] (sovereign tokenised debt peer), [[BIS advances multicurrency unified ledger project]] and [[Hong Kong pilots tokenized deposit and digital asset transactions]] (regional CB-money-on-chain peers). Relative read: MAS sits *in-line with* the leading-edge CB cohort on ambition, *behind SNB* on live sovereign settlement — no over/under-valuation call possible (no market prices).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Announcement vs adoption gap.** MAS states tokenisation adoption "still largely confined to smaller ecosystems" and MAS Bills settlement is *planned*, not live — execution risk that wholesale CBDC stays a test-network showcase (as retail CBDCs have elsewhere); second-order: standard-setting leverage decays if volume never materializes.
2. **Fragmentation / interoperability risk.** Each jurisdiction building its own stack (SDX, TARGET/Pontes, Orion, SGD Test Network) risks siloed liquidity; MAS's GL1 is a bet on being the bridge, but if ECB/UK standards dominate, Singapore bridges to them rather than vice-versa — loss of rail control.
3. **Disintermediation of private money.** Positioning CB money as the settlement anchor implicitly competes with the tokenised-deposit/stablecoin layer MAS itself is building under BLOOM and its stablecoin framework — tension over which money form captures settlement economics `(analysis)`.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Signals the wholesale-CBDC race is shifting from *interbank cash* (overnight lending) to *tokenised safe assets* (MAS Bills) — the higher-value use case that makes an on-chain DvP market real. Falsifiable thesis: Singapore converts breadth (Guardian/GL1) into live MAS Bill settlement within ~12 months and sets a cross-border interoperability reference. Trigger/catalyst: a disclosed *live* MAS Bill settlement with named banks (vs today's planning language). What breaks it: MAS Bill settlement stalls at trial stage while SNB/ECB scale production volume — then Singapore is a standards convener, not a settlement hub.
