 AI-Native Product Factory reimagines product development for the agentic era, turning ideas into products at unprecedented speed and scale with built-in security, governance and human oversight 
 NEW YORK and SUNNYVALE, Calif. —  Oct. 8, 2026  — Zip Co Limited (ASX: ZIP), a digital financial services company, today announced it has selected Google Cloud to build an AI-Native Product Factory designed to transform how Zip creates, develops, tests, launches, and continuously improves new products and experiences in the U.S. 
 
In financial services, new products still move like a relay race. Research hands off to design, design to engineering, and engineering to risk, legal, and compliance. Every handoff adds weeks, and every launch starts largely from scratch. Zip's AI-Native Product Factory replaces that relay with a shared, governed agentic environment where teams work in parallel.
The initiative represents a shift from using AI to augment day-to day-work to reimagining the entire product-development lifecycle with Gemini Enterprise. Zip’s Product Factory will connect customer research, design, engineering, risk, legal, compliance, and customer experience through a shared, governed agentic environment. The strategic value of the Product Factory is designed to compound over time, enabling Zip to pursue multiple concepts simultaneously, shorten the time taken between insights and launch, and progressively lower the incremental cost and complexity of bringing new experiences to market.
“With Google Cloud and Gemini Enterprise, we’re creating a new operating model for what AI-native consumer financial services looks like in practice,” said Rory Herriman, U.S. chief technology and operating officer of Zip US. “The real power comes from combining AI with what is uniquely ours: our data, our understanding of our customers, our product and risk expertise, and the institutional knowledge we have built over time. The Product Factory turns that intelligence into a compounding advantage. Every product we build creates reusable intelligence and capabilities that make the next product faster and better. That is how we accelerate innovation at a scale that simply wasn’t possible before.”
Zip is working with Google Cloud to establish the agentic foundation that makes this possible. Google Cloud’s Gemini Enterprise will underpin the AI-Native Product Factory and Zip’s broader AI industrialization environment, where agents work alongside Zip employees across internal workflows. With access to frontier and open-weight AI models, Zip can apply the right intelligence to each opportunity while building on a common enterprise foundation.
“This isn’t just about changing how we build products. It’s about building an intelligence-driven enterprise, where our data, knowledge, and expertise continuously compound and can be applied across our products, decisions, and operations.” said Herriman.
That foundation is being designed for the realities of financial services from the outset. Security, governance, traceability, and human oversight are built into how agents access data, interact with systems, and execute work, allowing Zip to accelerate innovation while maintaining clear accountability. For Zip, speed and control are not competing objectives; building AI safely is what enables it to scale.
“Agentic commerce is changing the way consumers discover and purchase products, which requires a rewiring of how financial services are delivered so AI agents can interact at scale," said Georgina Bulkeley,  Managing Director, Global Financial Services Industries Lead, Google Cloud. "The financial companies that will win in this era will be the ones that are already using  agents themselves. Zip recognises this and is building a company that will be ready for it, by embedding agentic intelligence from Gemini Enterprise right into its core operating model.”
 Bringing agentic intelligence into the customer experience 
Zip’s AI strategy extends beyond how products are built and how work gets done internally. Zip is also developing agentic experiences natively within its products, bringing the same growing body of data, knowledge, and intelligence directly into how the company serves its customers.
Zia, Zip’s customer-facing AI assistant, will be enabled by Gemini Enterprise, creating a common agentic foundation across Zip’s Product Factory, internal AI environment, and customer-facing experiences. As Zia evolves, specialized agents will be able to work together using a single, intuitive customer experience, drawing on Zip’s growing intelligence and capabilities to understand needs, take appropriate action, and resolve increasingly complex customer requests.
“What customers experience should become simpler as the intelligence behind it becomes more sophisticated,” said Herriman. “Zia is evolving from an AI support assistant toward an agentic experience embedded directly into our products. Customers won’t need to understand which agent, system, or team can solve their problem—they should simply be able to tell Zia what they need. Behind that experience, specialized agents, our people, and the intelligence we continue to build can work together to deliver the right outcome.”
 Building from an established AI foundation 
“The next competitive advantage in consumer finance will come from the ability to turn insights into trusted products faster than conventional operating models allow,” said Joe Heck, CEO of Zip U.S. “We believe product velocity will become one of the most important moats in our industry and we intend to make it one of ours.”  “This is growth infrastructure,” Heck continued. “It gives our people the capacity to pursue more opportunities and serve more customers without adding complexity at the same rate. We are combining human judgement and ingenuity with the speed, scale, and intelligence of AI.”
Zip is also helping develop the external infrastructure required for an agentic economy. The company is a co-creator of the Unified Trust Layer, an open-source industry framework focused on making trust measurable and portable in AI-initiated commerce.
“Together, these initiatives address both sides of the agentic opportunity. 'This is the progression of our strategy from AI-Powered teams to AI-Native operations,' Heck said. 'We are building a company that can learn faster, create faster, and compound innovation with every product it launches.”

# Context-enrichment: Zip US & Google Cloud build an "AI-Native Product Factory" (Gemini Enterprise)
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 8 Oct 2026 Zip Co (ASX: ZIP) announced it has **selected** Google Cloud to build an "AI-Native Product Factory" on **Gemini Enterprise** for its **US** business. De-PR'd: this is an **internal product-development operating model**, not a consumer product and not a revenue deal. The "factory" is a shared, "governed agentic environment" meant to connect research, design, engineering, risk, legal and compliance so teams work in parallel instead of via sequential hand-offs. Gemini Enterprise will also power **Zia**, Zip's customer-facing assistant, and sit under a broader "AI industrialization" environment.
- **Live vs announced:** Announced/framed only. No launch date, no rollout timeline, no spend, no number of agents/workflows, no committed KPI. Language is future tense ("will underpin", "is being designed", "as Zia evolves"). This is a **selection + vision statement**, not a shipped capability.
- **Why structured this way (analysis):** The PR anchors to a flattering abstraction ("product velocity as a moat", "compounding intelligence") precisely because there is no shippable deliverable yet to point at. Framing it as a new *operating model* rather than a tool deployment lets Zip claim strategic transformation without committing to a measurable milestone. Note the timing: it dropped around Google's **Gemini at Work 2026** event — a co-marketing moment for Google Cloud as much as a Zip initiative.
- **Who it really serves:** For Google Cloud, Zip is another named **Gemini Enterprise for Financial Services** logo (that product GA'd 25 Aug 2026 with Deutsche Bank, CME, BNY, Citi Wealth, Lloyds, Macquarie). For Zip, it reinforces a US "AI-native" narrative to the US investor base it is courting ahead of a potential Nasdaq listing.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Klarna** — deepest AI posture: Shopping Search in ChatGPT, 100M products via an MCP server, plus Gemini/AI-Mode buy-buttons (May 2026). AI-visibility leader (~69.8% appearance rate across AI engines).
- **Affirm** — Gemini/UCP/Stripe orbit; buy-buttons in Google Gemini & AI Mode (May 2026), BNPL shipping into Perplexity/ChatGPT. ~68.4% AI-visibility.
- **Afterpay (Block)** — materially behind on agentic/AI-commerce visibility.
- **Position:** On *consumer-facing* agentic commerce Zip is **behind** Klarna/Affirm (its own analog is the Stripe Shared Payment Tokens tie-up, [[Zip US expands Stripe partnership for agentic commerce payments]]). But this announcement is a **different axis** — internal product-ops tooling, not checkout distribution — where there is no obvious BNPL peer publicizing the same. That makes it hard to call "ahead" or "behind": it is largely incomparable, which is itself a yellow flag (no competitor feels the need to announce their internal dev platform).
- **Why the landscape looks this way (analysis):** Klarna/Affirm compete for *presence inside AI shopping surfaces* (distribution = where the margin is). An internal "product factory" does not win distribution; at best it lowers Zip's own cost/time-to-launch. Second order: the value only shows up if faster launches translate into TTV/revenue — otherwise it is opex dressed as strategy.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Zip's center of gravity has shifted decisively to the **US**: FY26 US TTV ~**$8.6B (+42.5%)**, US revenue **$613M (+44%)**, US now **~76% of group TTV** and the large majority of cash earnings; group cash EBTDA **$268.9M (+57.9%)**, statutory NPAT **$116.4M (+45.7%)**, record ~20% operating margin. FY27 guide: group cash EBITDA ~$340M, US TTV growth ≥30%. US is the growth engine and the reason for the Nasdaq-listing push ([[Zip targets Nasdaq listing to fuel US BNPL growth]], [[Zip shares jump 12% on strong update and buyback]]).
- Clear, dated agentic-commerce arc: [[IXOPAY and Zip US launch Unified Trust Layer for agentic commerce]] (Mar 2026, co-created "Unified Trust Layer") → [[Zip US expands Stripe partnership for agentic commerce payments]] (Jun 2026, Shared Payment Tokens) → Gemini "Product Factory" (Oct 2026). The new PR explicitly cites the UTL as Zip's external-infrastructure play, so this is a **continuation**, not a stray initiative.
- **Why Zip acts this way (analysis):** Zip is a US BNPL with strong but commodity-ish unit economics competing against better-capitalized, more AI-visible Klarna/Affirm. It needs an "AI-native" equity story for US investors and a credible reason product velocity will keep US TTV compounding at 30%+. "Product Factory" is that narrative packaging. Structural pressure: a dual-listing candidate must look like a tech platform, not just a lender.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new?** The *concept* (cross-functional agentic dev environment) is not novel — it is a branded application of Gemini Enterprise, the same platform Deutsche Bank/CME/Commerzbank etc. already use. Novelty is in the *framing* ("Product Factory" as an operating model), not the technology.
- **Traction:** **None disclosed.** Zero live products attributed to the factory, zero cycle-time reduction figures, no agent/workflow counts, no cost delta. Compare: Commerzbank publicly cited cutting a document-QA task from 20 hours to 1 — Zip offered no equivalent proof point.
- **Where the margin sits (analysis):** The platform margin accrues to **Google Cloud** (recurring Gemini Enterprise + compute spend). Zip captures value only if the factory measurably shortens time-to-launch AND those launches lift US TTV/revenue — an unproven two-step. If not, this is an AI opex line and a Google ARR win dressed as a Zip moat. The honest read: value-add is **asserted, not demonstrated**.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Watch for: a named product actually shipped via the factory with a cycle-time number; Zia moving from "support assistant" to agentic; any US TTV acceleration Zip attributes to velocity. Shares reportedly ticked ~3% on the news — a mild, narrative-driven move, not a re-rating.
- **Regulatory backdrop:** US BNPL/credit scrutiny (CFPB posture) and the governance burden of agents touching risk/compliance workflows are the real constraints; Zip leans hard on "governance, traceability, human oversight" language precisely because regulators will ask.
- **Second-order (analysis):** The counterintuitive risk is that "AI-native" becomes table stakes — if every BNPL runs Gemini/OpenAI internally, an internal factory confers **no durable edge**; the durable edge stays in *distribution* (being inside ChatGPT/Gemini checkout) and *credit/data advantage*, where Klarna/Affirm currently lead. So the central question is not "does Zip have an AI story" (it does) but "can internal product velocity convert into distribution and TTV faster than Google commoditizes the tooling for everyone."

**Sector & drivers.** US BNPL is large and still compounding: per a US BNPL market report (GlobeNewswire, as of 2026-02), the US sector was ~$107.4bn in 2025 heading to ~$258.4bn by 2031 — a double-digit CAGR (another tracker cites ~16.4% CAGR 2026–31, per Mordor Intelligence); still only ~1.1% of US card spend in 2025 (Richmond Fed, 2026), so runway is structural, not saturation. Structure is consolidating around a handful of scaled players (PayPal, Affirm, Klarna, Afterpay/Block, Zip) plus checkout rails (Stripe, Worldpay) — barriers are capital (balance-sheet funding of receivables), underwriting data, and merchant/distribution network effects. "Why now" is two-layered: (1) the CFPB withdrew its 2025 BNPL interpretive rule (per sector press), loosening near-term US regulatory pressure; (2) the agentic-commerce shift — Google Cloud launched *Gemini Enterprise for Financial Services* on 2026-08-25 (googlecloudpresscorner), and this Zip deal is a downstream adoption, not a standalone model. Second-order: if purchase discovery moves to AI agents, BNPL providers must be machine-callable or risk disintermediation at checkout — which is exactly what this "Product Factory"/Zia play targets.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TTV/GMV, revenue take rate (revenue/TTV), active customers, spend-per-customer, and cash EBTDA margin. Players & basis of competition: Affirm (US-premium, high take rate, merchant-exclusive deals), Klarna (global scale, debit/wallet pivot), Block/Afterpay (Cash App ecosystem), PayPal (distribution), Zip (US-focused ANZ-listed challenger). Basis = distribution + underwriting + product velocity, not price. Recent AI-cluster moves: Klarna–Google Cloud strategic AI partnership (2025-10-13, [[Klarna and Google Cloud enter strategic AI partnership]]); Affirm to support Google's Agent Payments Protocol ([[Affirm to support Google's Agent Payments Protocol]]); Google adds Affirm & Klarna BNPL to AI shopping ([[Google adds Affirm and Klarna BNPL to AI shopping]]); Zip itself co-created the Unified Trust Layer ([[IXOPAY and Zip US launch Unified Trust Layer for agentic commerce]]) and deepened Stripe agentic rails ([[Zip US expands Stripe partnership for agentic commerce payments]]). Zip's position: catching up in absolute scale but strongly US-levered and growing fast — US was 76% of group TTV and US TTV +42.5% USD in FY26 (Zip FY26 results, via Investing.com/Rask, 2026-08). Moat (analysis): switching costs/network effects are thin for Zip vs Affirm; its differentiator here is operating-model velocity + proprietary risk data — a process moat, which is harder to verify than a balance-sheet or network moat.

**Risk flags.**
1. **AI spend without disclosed economics (de-PR).** The release is all "announced", zero dollars, timelines, or committed products — product-velocity as a "moat" (Heck's words) is a claim, not a metric; watch whether Zia/Product Factory show up in shipped-product cadence or US unit economics, else it's a narrative layer on an un-moated process.

If agentic checkout concentrates demand through Google's Agent Payments Protocol (where Affirm/Klarna are already plugged in), Zip risks margin/positioning being set by the platform layer it doesn't control.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is an adoption datapoint in the "every BNPL names Google Cloud for AI" cluster — not a re-rating on its own, but it reinforces that agentic commerce is now table-stakes, pushing differentiation from price to product/operating velocity. Falsifiable thesis: if product-velocity is a real moat, Zip's US revenue take rate and new-product contribution should visibly expand through FY27 (guidance already >30% US TTV growth); trigger to watch = FY27 results / the planned Nasdaq listing re-rating the ~1.9x P/S toward Affirm's band. What breaks it: AI opex rises with no attributable revenue lift, or agent-checkout commoditizes BNPL into a platform-selected utility.
