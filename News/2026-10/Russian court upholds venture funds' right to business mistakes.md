# Context-enrichment: Russian court upholds venture funds' right to business mistakes
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 4/5._

**Case identified:** Moscow Arbitration Court case **A40-350334-2025**, decision published **2026-10-09** (per the kad.arbitr document). Plaintiff: **LLC "Venture Investments"** (ООО «Венчурные инвестиции»); defendant: **Sk Capital** (the rebranded **Skolkovo Ventures** / «Сколково — венчурные инвестиции»). Claim: **~320.6 mln rubles** in damages. The court **fully dismissed the suit**, and — unusually — the full reasoning is sealed: "the judicial act was adopted in a closed court session." (Confirmed via vc.ru / The Edinorog reporting on the same case; the Mergers.ru aggregated snippet matches.)

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- The dispute is **not** fund-vs-startup (Skolkovo's usual posture — see [1]); it is **LP-vs-fund-manager**. The plaintiff, an LP in a Russian **investment partnership** (договор инвестиционного товарищества), alleged the fund breached the agreement by **investing in only 5 of the 8 companies** it was contractually meant to back, and sought ~320.6 mln rubles for the shortfall/losses.
- The court **dismissed the claim in full**. Reasoning is **not public**: the hearing was closed because (a) the plaintiff's ultimate owners/management are **concealed by government decision to avoid sanctions**, and (b) the investment-partnership contract is a **trade secret**. So we have the **outcome** (no liability) but not the court's stated legal basis.
- **Why structured this way / what it reveals:** The headline frame "right to business mistakes" is **interpretive, not a quoted holding** — it comes from VC-industry commentary (a Yandex legal executive warned that a plaintiff win would be "catastrophic for Russian venture capital," stripping funds of the right to err), not from a published ratio. The de-PR'd reality: a dismissal that the market *reads* as protecting manager discretion, but whose doctrine is sealed. The real precedential weight is therefore **weaker than the headline** — a closed, unpublished reasoning cannot be cited as binding guidance, only as a data point.
- The de-PR'd legal backdrop (analysis): Russian law has **no codified business-judgment rule**; manager liability runs through **SAC Plenum Resolution No. 62** (good faith + reasonableness) and the Civil Code's recognition of entrepreneurial risk. Courts have historically read "business" narrowly (profit = the only legitimate aim), which fits awkwardly with a high-failure-rate VC model.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Skolkovo Ventures itself as plaintiff, historically:** the fund is best known for **suing its own portfolio startups** — e.g. the **Brandquad** case (A40-35919-2023), ~164.88 mln rubles over a €1.5M convertible loan it refused to convert "for geopolitical reasons" (founder's account). So the fund that is now *defending* its right to make mistakes has a track record of *pursuing* startups for theirs. (Irony worth flagging, analysis.)
- **Avito v. Gigant** (cited in Forbes' «Право на риск») — a corporate (>33% holder) suing a startup director for losses — is the mirror-image fact pattern feeding the same unsettled doctrine on "reasonable entrepreneurial risk."
- **Regional VC peers, same month:** [[Moscow Venture Fund commits 250M rubles across four deals]], [[Indie GO launches 5bn-ruble venture fund for gaming IPOs]], [[NTI Fund takes 3% in AFK Sistema's space unit Sputniks]] — all signal that **state-adjacent Russian VC is still deploying**, so the legal question of manager liability is live money, not academic.
- **Position:** This is the clearest **pro-manager** data point so far, but it is not a doctrine-setting appellate ruling — parity-to-slightly-ahead of prior practice, not a breakthrough.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Skolkovo Ventures → Sk Capital:** an investment platform spun up in 2017 to run state-anchored venture funds (LPs reportedly include/included **RZD, United Aircraft Corporation, Helicopters Russia** — named as third parties in this case). The rebrand to **Sk Capital** and the **sanction-driven concealment of the plaintiff's beneficiaries** show how deeply this corner of Russian VC is now wired into state and sanctions-avoidance structures.
- **Fit (analysis):** A fund with state/defence-SOE LPs facing an LP clawback suit over *undeployed* capital is a logical late-cycle event: in a frozen, sanctions-constrained market, a fund that couldn't find/close 8 deals is an easy target for an LP seeking to recover committed capital. The dismissal protects the **manager's discretion not to deploy** as much as its "right to a bad bet."

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **What's genuinely new:** a Russian court **refusing to hold a fund manager liable** for not fully/successfully deploying an investment-partnership mandate — a rare pro-fund outcome in a jurisdiction where startups/managers usually lose.
- **Why the value-add is limited (1–2 levels deeper):** the reasoning is **sealed and first-instance**. No published ratio ⇒ no citable precedent ⇒ funds cannot yet rely on it to price manager-liability risk. Traction = **one dismissal**, not a line of cases. The "right to mistakes" is the *market's narrative*, not the court's words. If appealed and reversed, the signal flips entirely.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Likely appeal** to the 9th Arbitration Appeal Court / Moscow District cassation — worth tracking; an appellate affirmation (ideally with open reasoning) is what would turn this into real precedent.
- **Sentiment:** VC-side relief (the Yandex-lawyer "catastrophic" framing); the industry wants this to crystallize a de-facto business-judgment rule.
- **Second-order (analysis):** The *closed* nature cuts both ways — it protects sanctioned LP identities but also **denies the VC industry the public doctrine it is celebrating**. The counterintuitive effect: a ruling hailed as a landmark for "right to risk" may, precisely because it is sealed, leave the law as uncertain as before.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Importance: 4/5** — A genuinely novel, pro-manager outcome in a jurisdiction where funds/startups usually lose, touching a live doctrine (no codified business-judgment rule) with real money behind it (320.6 mln rub; state-SOE LPs). Docked from 5 because the reasoning is **sealed and first-instance** — it is a data point, not a citable precedent, and the "right to mistakes" framing is market commentary, not a quoted holding.

1. Is "right to business mistakes" the court's actual holding, or industry interpretation of a dismissal? — **Interpretation.** The reasoning is sealed; the frame comes from a Yandex-lawyer quote, not a published ratio. (load-bearing caveat)
2. What was the plaintiff's actual legal theory — breach of the investment-partnership contract (5 of 8 deals) or fiduciary/loss claim? — **Breach of mandate (undeployed capital)**; "losses" is the LP's framing.
3. On what basis did the court dismiss — no breach, no causation, no proven loss, or discretion protected? — **Open** (sealed reasoning). Cannot be stated as fact.
4. Is this first instance or appellate? — **First instance** (Moscow Arbitration Court). Precedential weight is low until affirmed on appeal.
5. Has it been / will it be appealed? — **Open.** An appeal and its openness is the key thing to track.
6. Who is the plaintiff LLC "Venture Investments" really? — **Concealed by government decision** (sanctions avoidance); beneficiaries not public.
7. Why does that concealment matter to the "landmark" narrative? — It forced the closed hearing, which **denies the industry the public doctrine** it is celebrating.
8. Does the dismissal protect "right to a failed bet" or "right not to deploy"? — Primarily **discretion not to deploy** (5 of 8); the "bad investment" framing is looser (analysis).
9. Is there a codified business-judgment rule in Russian law? — **No**; liability runs through SAC Plenum No. 62 (good faith + reasonableness) + Civil Code risk recognition.
10. Does Skolkovo/Sk Capital have clean hands on "right to mistakes"? — **No** — it previously sued its own startup Brandquad (A40-35919-2023, 164.88 mln rub). Irony, not a legal defect.
11. Who are the third parties and why do they matter? — **RZD, UAC, Helicopters Russia** (former fund LPs/shareholders) — shows state/defence-SOE exposure.
12. Could the dismissal be purely procedural (standing, limitation) rather than substantive? — **Open**; sealed reasoning prevents ruling it in or out. Would gut the "doctrine" reading if so.
13. How much does one sealed first-instance dismissal move the law? — **Little**; it's a signal, not binding guidance. Hence importance 4 not 5.
14. Is this a duplicate / re-run of a prior note? — **No.** No prior note covers A40-350334-2025, Sk Capital, or this 320.6M suit. Related only thematically ([[Moscow Venture Fund commits 250M rubles across four deals]], [[Indie GO launches 5bn-ruble venture fund for gaming IPOs]]). **FRESH.**
15. What would flip the verdict? — An appeal that **reverses** the dismissal, or published reasoning that is purely procedural — either would collapse the "right to mistakes" significance.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** The item sits in Russian state-backed venture/PE under the "right to business mistake" (право на риск / право на ошибку) doctrine — a regime that evaluates state fund managers on PORTFOLIO performance rather than individual deal losses, shielding them from subsidiary/criminal liability for inevitable venture write-offs. The doctrine was formalized via amendments to the Law "On Science and State Scientific-Technical Policy" and recommendations to the Prosecutor General and Audit Chamber, applying to state venture companies VEB, RVC (RVK) and Rosnano; "success" is defined as overall portfolio value growth even if individual projects fail (per Kommersant/Izvestia/economy.gov.ru). The macro backdrop is a collapsing market: Russian venture investment fell ~48% YoY in H1 2026 to RUB 4.6bn across 54 deals (−45% deal count), and the 2025 market totaled ~$159m / RUB 13.6bn, −~10% YoY (per Kama Flow study, 5 Oct 2026, via KuCoin). Corporate VC fell ~4x to RUB 1.2bn in 2025 (per TAdviser). Why now: with sanctions having gutted private LP capital and exits, the state is the residual funder — but managers were steering money only to late-stage, low-risk projects to avoid prosecution, defeating the point of venture. This court dismissal (closed hearing, case A40-350334-2025, ruling 2026-10-09) is a JUDICIAL application of the doctrine: a claim against a fund (Skolkovo Ventures orbit per tags) over a failed/loss-making investment was fully rejected, signalling that courts will not retroactively punish good-faith venture losses. `(analysis)`

**Competitive landscape.** KPIs in state VC are not revenue multiples but governance metrics: portfolio IRR/DPI, write-off ratio, share of early vs late-stage tickets, and — distinctively here — prosecution/clawback exposure per RUB deployed. Key actors: Rosnano, RVC/RVK, VEB Ventures, IIDF/FRII, RDIF, FASIE, and regional vehicles (Moscow Venture Fund was the main active state investor, ~10 deals / $3.2m over 9 months per Kama Flow). Basis of "competition" is less pricing than regulatory shelter and budget allocation. Recent contrast: Rosnano remains the cautionary pole — criminal cases against 10+ former top managers (Vakhterov jailed Apr 2026, Gonchar Jun 2026; Podolsky/Galstyan arrested Jan 2025 over 2017 distorted financials), and Rosnano sued ex-chief Chubais in Dec 2025 over a failed memory-chip project (per Moscow Times/Izvestia). Position of the doctrine: a prospective firewall AGAINST a repeat of the Rosnano prosecution playbook. This ruling is the first notable precedent testing it in court (ahead of a settled body of case law — `(analysis)`). Moat for state funds is purely institutional (budget access + now legal protection), not a competitive one.

**Risk flags.**
1. Precedent opacity — the ruling was issued in a CLOSED hearing with reasons withheld ("судебный акт принят в закрытом судебном заседании"), so it cannot be relied on as transparent, citable case law; a dismissal on procedural/standing grounds would NOT actually establish a "right to mistake" doctrine. Second-order: headline overstates the legal weight. [UNSOURCED — ruling text not extracted]
2. Scope narrowness — the statutory shelter applies only to designated state companies (VEB/RVC/Rosnano) and budget-funded venture; it does not protect private GPs or mixed vehicles, so most of the shrinking market gets no relief. Second-order: entrenches state crowding-out as the only "safe" capital.
3. Reversibility/selectivity — in a regime where Rosnano managers are still being prosecuted in parallel (2025–2026), a protective doctrine coexisting with active criminal cases signals selective, politically contingent enforcement; managers may not trust the shelter enough to actually raise risk appetite. Second-order: the chilling effect persists de facto even if removed de jure.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` If upheld and cited, this shifts state VC governance from deal-by-deal blame toward portfolio accountability — the precondition for state funds to re-enter early-stage after the ~48% H1 2026 collapse. Falsifiable thesis: state-fund early-stage ticket share rises over the next 4 quarters. Trigger to watch: whether RVC/VEB publicly cite the doctrine when greenlighting loss-tolerant seed deals, and whether any new Rosnano-style prosecution is dropped on "right to risk" grounds. What breaks the thesis: a higher court reversing, or a fresh criminal case against a fund manager for a failed bet despite the doctrine.
