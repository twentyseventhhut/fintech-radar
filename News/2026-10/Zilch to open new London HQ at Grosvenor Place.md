Zilch HQ is getting a big upgrade
 We’re more than doubling our London office capacity with a new 33,160 sq ft headquarters, supporting our next phase of growth in the UK and Europe. 
Zilch will move to a new London headquarters as we continue to scale the business and invest in our future growth.
Our new home will occupy the top two floors of 40 Grosvenor Place (just around the corner from our current HQ in Victoria). The 33,160 sq ft space will more than double our current London office capacity and can accommodate more than 400 employees.
We expect to move in spring 2027, making a £77 million commitment over the next 10 years as we expand our operations and plan to create more jobs in the UK.
What it means
The move from our current base at 111 Buckingham Palace Road gives us the space to grow our team, attract exceptional talent, and build the infrastructure to support our growth ambitions in the UK and Europe. 
 City Minister Rt Hon Lucy Rigby KC MP said: 
 “This is a major milestone for Zilch and it’s brilliant to see their continued success. The UK is a fintech powerhouse and I’m committed to backing the sector to thrive and grow.” 
 Philip Belamant, Co-Founder and CEO of Zilch, said: 
 “It has been a transformational year for the business with a number of significant milestones, and our customers moving beyond £8 billion of spend across every region in the UK. As we continue to expand rapidly, our new headquarters reflect the scale of our ambition and the momentum behind our growth. This is an important investment in Zilch’s future, giving our team an incredible base to attract exceptional talent at home and beyond. We are proud to be investing further in the UK as we enter the next phase of growth.”

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Zilch (UK consumer BNPL "ad-subsidised" lender) has signed a **10-year lease on the top two floors of 40 Grosvenor Place, Victoria** — 33,160 sq ft, capacity for 400+ staff — committing **£77m over 10 years** (≈£7.7m/yr gross). Move-in **spring 2027**, relocating from its current 111 Buckingham Palace Road base ~round the corner. Landlord is PATRIZIA (per Property Week/Estates Gazette). This is a **property/real-estate commitment, not a financing or product event**.
- **De-PR'd:** "more than doubling London office capacity" is the headline, but today's headcount is only **~291 employees** (Pitchbook/IPO coverage). The 400+ figure is forward capacity, i.e. the lease pre-provisions for ~40% headcount growth over several years — an aspiration, not current scale. £77m over 10 yrs is ~7% of one year's revenue (£110m) spread annually, so it is affordable rather than bold.
- **Why structured this way → what it reveals:** The timing is not incidental. The HQ was unveiled **12 days after** Zilch invited 15+ banks to pitch for a **2027/early-2028 London IPO** ([[Zilch invites banks to pitch ahead of IPO]]). A long-dated, London-anchored, government-ministerial-blessed (City Minister Lucy Rigby quoted) lease is **IPO stagecraft**: it signals permanence, a London listing venue, scale ambition and UK commitment to the LSE at a moment when UK listings are thin ([[42sec UK fintech funding falls to lowest level in a decade]]). CEO Belamant's quote explicitly ties it to "customers moving beyond £8bn of spend" and "the next phase of growth" — the press release is written for public-markets investors, not property press.

## [1] Competitors / peers
BNPL/consumer-credit peers operating in the UK:
- **Klarna** (NYSE-listed Sept 2025; see [[Klarna goes live on M&S.com and the M&S app]], [[Klarna deepens Wayfair partnership with 0% APR financing]]) — far larger, global, the IPO benchmark Zilch will be priced against.
- **Clearpay/Afterpay** (Block-owned), **PayPal Pay in 3**, **Monzo Flex**, **Revolut** — all UK-active.
- The entire UK BNPL sector came **under full FCA regulation from 15 July 2026** (affordability checks, FOS access, Section-75-style protection £100–£30k) — Zilch already secured its FCA payments licence ([[Zilch secures FCA payments license]]).
- **Position:** Zilch is a UK mid-cap unicorn (last priced ~$2bn in 2021, held through the Nov-2025 $177m KKCG round — [[Zilch raises over $175M ahead of potential IPO]]), **behind Klarna in scale** but one of the few UK BNPL names with a credible near-term listing story.
- **Why the lay of the land is this way (analysis):** A physical London HQ is table-stakes signalling that Klarna (Stockholm-rooted, listed in NY) did NOT lean on. Zilch's differentiator is being the **"home" UK champion for the LSE** — the £77m lease + ministerial quote converts "UK fintech powerhouse" rhetoric into a tangible London anchor, strengthening the case for a London (not New York) debut.

## [2] Company history / fit
Trajectory: 5m customers by mid-2025 ([[Zilch doubles revenue and reaches 5 million customers]]) → Arsenal FC partnership ([[Zilch becomes official payment partner of Arsenal FC]]) → $175m+ debt/equity (Nov 2025) → FCA licence (Dec 2025) → **Fjord Bank (Lithuania) acquisition for an EU banking licence** ([[Zilch to acquire Lithuania's Fjord Bank for EU expansion]], first flagged [[Zilch acquires Lithuania's Fjord Bank for EU license]]) → board upgrade with ex-Microsoft UK CEO Dame Clare Barclay ([[Zilch appoints Microsoft veteran Dame Clare Barclay to board]]) and a Chief Corporate Affairs Officer ([[Zilch appoints Florence Quirici as Chief Corporate Affairs Officer]]) → **bank-pitch for IPO** (Oct 2026) → **new HQ** (this note).
- **Fit:** logical and consistent. **Why the company acts this way:** Zilch is in a deliberate **IPO-readiness build-out** — EU licence (Fjord) for international TAM, a heavyweight independent board, a corporate-affairs hire for public-markets comms, and now a flagship HQ. Each move de-risks the listing narrative. The board/CCAO/HQ cluster is textbook pre-IPO institutionalisation.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty of THIS event: low.** A lease is not a product or financial innovation. The real value is the **traction it implicitly discloses and the signal it sends.**
- **The traction that actually matters (FY to Mar-2025, strongest numbers):** revenue **£110.3m (+93%)**, **GMV £1.9bn (+73%)**, take-rate up to **6.08%**, gross margin **49%**, net loss cut **79% to £10.5m** (from £50.2m), first month of operating profit reported July 2024. These are the figures the HQ PR is really flexing ("£8bn cumulative spend").
- **Why value-add is real/not (analysis):** The durable story is the **ad-subsidised model** (merchant/retail-media revenue — [[Zilch builds retail media network with Intelligent Commerce]]) lifting take-rate to 6%+ and margin to ~49%, materially above thin-margin pure-BNPL. **Who captures the margin:** Zilch's edge is owning the ad/commerce-media layer on top of credit, not just the transaction spread — that is what underpins a software-ish multiple rather than a lender multiple. **What breaks it:** FCA affordability rules raising cost-to-serve, and whether the "near-break-even" actually tips to profit before listing.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **IPO:** 15+ banks invited to pitch; listing targeted **H2 2027–early 2028**, London favoured over NY. Reported likely valuation **below the $2bn** 2021 mark (two people familiar, per IPO coverage) — i.e. BNPL public multiples have compressed. Management wants to be **profitable before listing**; next accounts will show if the £10.5m loss flipped.
- **HQ move completes spring 2027**, roughly aligned to the listing window — a deliberate "new HQ + IPO" narrative pairing.
- **Why the market goes this way (analysis):** UK fintech funding is at a decade low ([[42sec UK fintech funding falls to lowest level in a decade]]) and the LSE is starved of tech listings, so a profitable, London-HQ'd BNPL champion is politically and commercially courted (hence the City Minister quote). **Counterintuitive second-order:** a long-dated £77m fixed lease is a **cost commitment made BEFORE the IPO cash lands** — if the listing slips or prices below $2bn, Zilch carries a large fixed property liability against still-thin profits, turning a confidence signal into a modest downside risk.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** UK BNPL lending reached ~£13.8bn in 2024 with ~10.9–11 million adult users (per FCA / Business Expert, as of 2024–2026). The defining "why now" is regulation: BNPL moved inside the FCA perimeter on **15 July 2026**, imposing affordability checks, Consumer Duty and Financial Ombudsman access (per FCA, 2026). Analysts model a near-term **10–20% volume reduction** in regulated BNPL as thinner-file customers get declined (analysis, via sector press). Structure: consolidating around a few scaled players (Klarna, Clearpay/Afterpay, PayPal, Zilch) over a long tail; barriers are capital (securitisation/debt warehouses), regulatory authorisation and merchant distribution/network effects. This HQ move (£77m over 10 years, 33,160 sq ft, ~400 staff, in by spring 2027) is a capacity/talent bet timed into the pre-IPO window, not a revenue event.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: GMV/TPV, revenue take rate, gross margin, loss provisions/credit losses. Zilch's reported basis of competition is its **ad-subsidised / retail-media** model (take rate inflated by advertiser revenue, not just consumer/merchant fees). Reported FY2025 (year to Mar-2025, per IR annual report & press): revenue **£110.3m (+93%)**, GMV **£1.9bn (+73%)**, take rate **6.08%**, gross margin **49%**, net loss cut 79% to **£10.5m**, 5m+ registered customers; CEO cites **>£8bn cumulative customer spend** (per zilch.com HQ release, 2026-10-20). Recent peer moves: Klarna IPO'd (NYSE: KLAR) in 2026; Affirm trades publicly ~$24bn mkt cap (Jun-2026). Zilch's position: UK/EU-focused challenger, **ahead on growth rate** but far smaller than Klarna/Affirm in GMV; moat = ad-subsidised economics + UK brand/distribution (Arsenal, Visa card), FCA payments licence (2025-12), EU licence via Fjord Bank acquisition (2026) — `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Internal comps: [[Zilch invites banks to pitch ahead of IPO]] (H2-2027/early-2028 London listing, banks pitching), [[Zilch raises over $175M ahead of potential IPO]] (KKCG-led, 2025), [[Zilch doubles revenue and reaches 5 million customers]], [[Zilch secures FCA payments license]].
- **Klarna (NYSE: KLAR):** ~$6–7.4bn mkt cap (mid-2026), TTM revenue ~$3.8–4bn → EV/Revenue ≈ `$7.4bn / $3.8bn ≈ 1.9x` (per Multiples.vc / press). Trades ~16x earnings.
- **Affirm (AFRM):** ~$24bn mkt cap (Jun-2026) → **~5x EV/Revenue**, ~27x on growth ~26% (per press).

Verdict: **private mark looks stretched vs public BNPL comps** → re-rating risk at IPO. EV/EBITDA not computed (Zilch not yet profitable; EBITDA not publicly clean) → `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory drag (15-Jul-2026 FCA perimeter):** affordability checks + Consumer Duty threaten the 10–20% of volume from thinner-file users — directly compresses the GMV growth story Zilch is pricing its IPO on.
2. **Valuation gap:** $2bn private mark implies ~14x revenue vs 1.9x (Klarna) / 5x (Affirm); if public comps hold, Zilch likely lists below carry → down-round optics into the HQ spend commitment.
3. **Still loss-making + credit cycle:** losses cut to £10.5m but provisions rising with customer growth; a UK consumer-credit downturn hits loss rates just as regulation raises compliance cost.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** The HQ lease is a pre-IPO confidence signal, not a fundamentals shift — the real catalyst is whether the H2-2027/2028 London listing prices near the $2bn carry. Falsifiable thesis `(analysis)`: Zilch lists **below $2bn** because public BNPL multiples (1.9–5x revenue) re-rate it off its private mark; watch the IPO range vs reported FY2026 revenue/GMV and post-regulation volume trend. Thesis breaks if the ad-subsidised take rate keeps revenue compounding ~90%+ through regulation, justifying a growth premium.

## Earnings Review

> No fresh results event — this is an HQ-expansion announcement (£77m over 10 years, 40 Grosvenor Place). Grounded in Zilch's latest filed period, FY2025 (year ended 31 March 2025), reported Aug 2025, to pressure-test the growth narrative behind the £8bn-cumulative-spend / IPO framing.

**Verdict (headline read).** NO FRESH RESULTS (HQ-expansion story). Latest filed period FY2025 (y/e 31 Mar 2025) reads BEAT on the top line and loss reduction: revenue £110.3m (+93% YoY), net loss narrowed to £10.5m (−79% YoY), gross margin 49% (from 39%). The de-PR flag: credit-loss provisions +116% to £27.4m — losses are shrinking on the P&L while the credit book is being provisioned far harder.

**Key figures (FY2025, y/e 31 Mar 2025, vs FY2024).**
- Revenue £110.3m, +93% YoY (from £57.1m).
- Transaction + advertising revenue £31.5m, +81% YoY.
- Gross profit £54.5m, +143% YoY (from £22.4m); gross margin 49% vs 39%.
- Operating loss £5.1m, from £33.1m (−85% YoY).
- Net loss after tax £10.5m, −79% YoY (from £50.1m).
- GMV £1,893m (£1.9bn), +73% YoY.
- Take rate 6.08% vs 5.44% prior.
- Registered customers >5m (CAC spend £9.8m, +62%).
- Adjusted operating cash flow +£15m, from −£32.9m outflow.
- Credit-loss provisions £27.4m, +116% YoY (from £12.7m); credit losses/GMV 1.5% vs 1.2%.

**By segment / driver.** Growth is ad-subsidised BNPL working as designed: GMV +73% pulls transaction + advertising revenue +81%, and a rising take rate (6.08% vs 5.44%) means Zilch is monetising each £ of spend harder — the gross-margin jump to 49% is the clearest operating-leverage signal. The loss reduction is driven mostly by operating-leverage (op loss £5.1m vs £33.1m), not one-offs; swing to +£15m adjusted operating cash flow is the standout.

**vs expectations / prior period.** No public consensus (private company) → beat/miss is qualitative vs prior year: revenue nearly doubled, losses cut ~four-fifths, first positive adjusted operating cash flow — unambiguously an improving trajectory. CEO's "beyond £8 billion of [cumulative] customer spend" claim in the HQ note is a cumulative-spend milestone, not a period figure, and sits above the FY2025 annual GMV of £1.9bn — consistent, not contradictory. No prior Zilch earnings note exists in the base to wikilink.

**Guidance / forward.** No guidance (private; UK statutory accounts give none). Forward context from press: Zilch is in early banker talks for a London IPO targeted late-2027/early-2028, floated at ~$2bn — the same valuation as its 2021 round and its 2025 £134m raise, i.e. flat for ~4 years and FT sources say a listing is "unlikely to beat" the 2021 $2bn mark. The £77m/10yr HQ (400+ seats, spring 2027 move-in) is explicitly an IPO-adjacent scale-up signal, not a results event. (analysis) Plausible but valuation-flat: the HQ commitment reads as pre-IPO credibility-building.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. Provisions +116% to £27.4m while net loss falls −79% → the headline loss-reduction is real but the credit book is being provisioned more than twice as hard; credit losses/GMV ticking 1.2%→1.5%. Why it matters: if BNPL impairment keeps outpacing revenue as UK regulation tightens, the path to statutory profit is more fragile than the loss-reduction headline implies. Second-order: a key swing item an IPO book-build will stress-test.
2. Ad-subsidised model is compounding: take rate 6.08% (from 5.44%) + gross margin 49% (from 39%) → monetisation per GMV £ is improving, de-risking the unit-economics thesis. Second-order: supports a quality-of-revenue story for IPO vs pure-credit BNPL peers.
3. Flat ~$2bn valuation across 2021→2025→2027(E) while fundamentals ~doubled → the equity story is "grow into the price," and the £77m HQ is management putting capital behind the next-phase narrative ahead of a listing. Risk flag: IPO timing/valuation hinges on BNPL regulatory clarity (FCA regime), which the Forbes framing calls the key swing factor.
