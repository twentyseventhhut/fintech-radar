# Context-enrichment: FT — Pokemon cards near sports-card market as investment asset
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
This is FT commentary (not a fintech event, deal, or product launch): the paper argues that collectible Pokemon cards, now selling for millions, are catching up to the long-established sports-card market as a recognised investment/alt-asset class. The real, citable substance behind the framing:
- **Grading has flipped from sports to TCG.** Major authenticators graded a record ~26.6M cards in 2025; PSA alone graded 19.26M (+26% YoY, ~72% share). TCG/non-sports grading (16.8M) overtook sports (10M) in 2025 for the first time; Pokemon was the single most-graded category at ~16.1M and made up 97 of PSA's top-100 cards graded in H1 2025. TCGs (Pokemon/Magic/Yu-Gi-Oh!) are now roughly half of PSA's business — more than all sports cards combined.
- **Watershed prints.** A Pikachu Illustrator PSA 10 sold for $16.49M at Goldin (Feb 2026); other seven-figure Pokemon and Magic (Black Lotus ~$2.2M) sales followed.
- **Returns framing.** Various indices claim Pokemon cards up ~3,261–3,821% over ~20 years vs ~483% for the S&P 500; the CardTrack-100 rare-Pokemon index +150.7% since Sept 2021. These are index/survivorship-flavoured figures, not a liquid, investable benchmark — treat as marketing-grade, not audited returns.
- **→ Why framed this way:** the "near sports-card market" headline leans on the grading-volume crossover (a real, measurable fact) to imply investment parity. But grading volume ≠ market capitalisation: the sports-card market was still valued at ~$13B in 2024, and the broader TCG market is sized $7.8B (BCC) to $13.3B (Mordor) for 2025 — wide, non-comparable estimates. The story conflates "most cards graded" with "biggest investment market," which are different claims.

## [1] Competitors / peers
The relevant "peers" are the financial-infrastructure players turning cards into an asset class, not card brands:
- **Marketplaces/authentication:** eBay (see [[eBay invests in TrueLayer and adds Pay by Bank]], [[eBay Seller Capital integrates open banking for financing]]) runs an Authenticity Guarantee mandatory on cards >=$250, routing them through its authentication centre — eBay is the de-facto liquidity + trust layer for secondary card trading.
- **Grading:** PSA (~72% share) vs CGC (4.92M cards, +121% YoY, ~25% TCG share) — grading is the real chokepoint/gatekeeper that makes a card "investable."
- **Collectible-backed lending:** Qollateral, JM Bullion lend 40–60% of verified secondary-market value against PSA/BGS/SGC/CGC cards (no credit check) — the same playbook as [[India's organised gold loan market reaches ₹11.8 lakh crore]] and gold-backed finance, applied to cards.
- **Fractional platforms:** Rally (467 SEC-verified series, median 1.20x exit across 111 closed positions as of May 2026), Collectable — parallels [[WinZO and SafeGold launch ZO Gold digital gold investing]] and [[Bite Investments raises $25 million for alternative investment platform]] on democratising alt-assets.
- **→ Position:** Pokemon is "catching up" on grading throughput and headline prices but trails sports on total market value and on depth/age of financial infrastructure. The second-order point: cards become an "asset class" only to the extent the plumbing (grading, insured vaulting, lending, fractional SEC wrappers) exists — and that plumbing is largely sports-card-era infrastructure being retooled for TCG.

## [2] Company history / fit (eBay as the fintech-relevant anchor)
The only durable fintech thread is eBay. eBay has been building financial rails around its marketplace: open-banking Pay by Bank + a strategic investment in TrueLayer ([[eBay invests in TrueLayer and adds Pay by Bank]], Feb 2026) and seller financing via open banking ([[eBay Seller Capital integrates open banking for financing]], Aug 2025). GameStop also bid for eBay ([[GameStop CEO drops $35bn bonus to focus on eBay bid]], June 2026), underscoring eBay's strategic value as a collectibles marketplace.
- **→ Why this matters structurally:** a marketplace that owns authentication + payments + seller credit captures fee economics on every leg of a collectible's lifecycle (sale, authentication fee, and potentially financing). The card-as-asset narrative is, for eBay, a GMV-and-take-rate story, not a hobby story — which is why eBay invests in trust (authentication) and payment friction reduction.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new:** the grading-volume crossover (TCG > sports in 2025) and the $16.49M Pikachu print are new, datable facts. Collectible-backed lending and fractional SEC-qualified card shares are maturing (Rally's 111 closed positions = real traction, not announcements).
- **Not new / overstated:** "collectibles as an asset class" is a decade-old thesis (Rally founded 2016; sports cards, art, wine long financialised). The FT piece is a trend commentary, not a novel development — no new instrument, regulation, or deal.
- **Who captures the margin:** graders (PSA/CGC) and the marketplace/authentication layer (eBay) capture reliable, recurring fees regardless of card price direction; lenders (Qollateral) earn spread + collateral haircut; fractional platforms take a cut on primary + secondary. The collector/"investor" bears the volatility and fraud/saturation risk. So the durable economics sit with the picks-and-shovels (grading, authentication, lending), not with holding cards.
- **→ What breaks the multiple:** supply (The Pokemon Company printed ~10B cards in FY2024–25) and fraud. PSA's first public fraud report (May 2026) flagged >$200M projected market value of fakes caught in 2025; fake Pokemon cards +125%, altered +400%. A NY jury convicted Anthony Curcio (Jan 2026) for forged-PSA-grade sales (>$2M victim losses). Grading fraud directly undermines the "verified asset" premise the whole asset-class thesis rests on.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Sentiment:** strongly bullish in retail/collector media; adviser-world is cautiously engaging (Kevin O'Leary suggests 3–5% portfolio allocation to collectibles; hedge funds reportedly treating cards as a legit asset class). InvestmentNews framing ("the $16.5M Pikachu crashing the planning meeting") shows the theme reaching mainstream wealth channels — hence the industry/wealth tag.
- **Structural drivers:** low rates on nostalgia-driven demand + financialisation infrastructure (grading, lending, fractional) lowering friction. Tax ambiguity is an open risk (collectibles taxed as such in the US; 24/7 Wall St notes the code predates seven-figure cards).
- **→ Counterintuitive second-order effect:** the more cards are financialised and graded as "assets," the more the category attracts fraud and speculative supply — which are exactly the forces that historically deflated collectible bubbles (1990s junk-wax era). Mainstream legitimisation (O'Leary, hedge funds) can mark a late-cycle top rather than a durable new asset class. The real question is not "are Pokemon cards an asset class?" but "who in the stack (graders, marketplaces, lenders) earns a durable fee vs who is left holding a volatile, fraud-prone, oversupplied collectible?"

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Collectibles-as-investment (trading cards). Sizing (note gives none; from web): global sports trading cards market ~$13.5bn in 2025, ~$14.5bn in 2026, ~7.9% CAGR to ~$24.7bn by 2033 (per Grand View Research). Pokemon TCG is a ~$2.7bn annual ecosystem (as of Mar 2026); TCGCharts puts the graded Pokemon segment alone at ~$10.8bn. So the FT's "near sports-card market" framing is directionally right on GRADED/secondary value and on grading VOLUME, but the overall sports-card market is still materially larger on primary + secondary GMV. Structure: fragmented on the primary/retail side, but the resale/authentication value chain is highly concentrated — Collectors/PSA held ~80% grading share in 2025 (after SGC + Beckett acquisitions), capturing 72% of graded cards and ~76% of sports (per GemRate via cllct). Grading is the gatekeeper: a card's investability is largely set at the grading step, which is where margin and pricing power sit. Drivers / "why now": (1) grading volume inflected — ~26.6mn cards graded in 2025, +32% YoY, and for the first time TCG/non-sports (16.8mn) OVERTOOK sports (10mn), with Pokemon the single largest category at ~16.1mn graded; (2) asset-class narrative — Pokemon reportedly +28% YTD 2026 vs S&P +13% and BTC -29% (per Yahoo/BigGo), pulling in "investor" (not hobbyist) capital; (3) crypto/onchain tokenization now circling the category (CoinDesk, Aug 2026) — a liquidity/fractionalization push.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: GMV, graded-card volume & grade mix (PSA 10 premium), take rate, and population reports (supply cap per card). Key players & basis of competition: marketplaces (eBay, StockX, Whatnot — distribution/liquidity), authenticators (PSA/Collectors, BGS, SGC — trust/brand), auction houses (Goldin — high-value lots), and alt-asset/fractional platforms (Rally, Collectable — access/fractionalization). eBay's position (protagonist): a full-stack collectibles play, built by M&A not organically — acquired TCGplayer for $295mn and auction house Goldin (2024), and notably SOLD the eBay Vault to PSA/Collectors while taking Goldin in a swap (per ebayinc / TechCrunch, Apr 2024). Read: eBay chose marketplace + high-end auction (demand/liquidity layer) and ceded storage+grading (the Vault) to PSA — i.e. it does NOT own the concentrated grading chokepoint. Collectibles outpaced the rest of eBay's marketplace in GMV growth through 2025 (9 straight quarters of trading-card GMV growth through Q1 2025, per eBay filings); but context: eBay total GMV was ~$18.8bn and revenue ~$2.6bn in Q1 2025, +1-2% — collectibles is a growth bright-spot inside a low-growth whole. Moat: network effects / liquidity depth on the marketplace side (analysis); weak control of the authentication layer.

**Comps & multiples.** No deal/valuation/metric in this FT item → no company multiple to compute here (note carries 0 numbers). Market-level anchors instead: graded Pokemon ~$10.8bn vs sports-card market ~$13.5bn (2025) → graded-Pokemon is ~0.8x the whole sports-card market by that measure = the "catching up" claim. TCGplayer was bought at $295mn (2024); not enough public financials to derive an EV/Revenue → no data. Internal comps (alt-asset democratization / collectibles-platform precedents in the DB): [[German banks open private equity to everyday investors]], [[Bite Investments raises $25 million for alternative investment platform]], [[FCA backs tokenization of funds in asset management]] — same secular thread (retail access to illiquid/alternative assets + tokenization), useful as the "what fractionalization looks like when it works/regulates" reference set. No comparable graded-figure distribution computed (qualitative comparison).

**Risk flags.**
1. Momentum/bubble risk. The "+28% YTD, beats stocks and BTC" framing is exactly how collectibles cycles top out; trading cards have no yield and value is pure sentiment + scarcity. A grading-volume surge (+32%) can INCREASE supply of high grades and compress premiums — second-order: the narrative that draws investors can also break their returns.
2. Fraud & authentication dependence. Collectibles is a documented fraud surface — the 2025 "Mister ManCave" case uncovered ~$350mn of fakes on eBay (jerseys + cards). eBay sold the Vault/grading to PSA, so trust in the asset rests on a third party eBay doesn't control; a PSA grading-integrity scare would hit eBay GMV it can't directly defend.
3. Liquidity / fractional-platform fragility. Fractional-collectibles venues are thin: Rally's PPEX secondary "is not a liquid market in any meaningful sense," and Collectable halted secondary trading on an SEC-filing deficiency (per ArtTactic/alts.co). If tokenization/fractionalization is the "institutionalization" thesis, the plumbing has already shown it can freeze — liquidity is advertised, not guaranteed.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is a re-rating of a hobby into an asset class, not a new business model — the economically interesting move is onchain/fractional tokenization of cards, which would shift value toward whoever owns custody + authentication + the trading venue (i.e. PSA/Collectors and liquidity platforms), NOT the marketplace. Falsifiable thesis: if Pokemon/graded-card tokenization gains real secondary liquidity, expect the authentication-plus-custody stack (PSA) to capture the re-rating, while eBay stays a demand funnel. Watch next: crypto/onchain card-trading launches (CoinDesk flagged this Aug 2026) and whether any fractional venue sustains liquidity through a drawdown — a frozen secondary market during a price drop would break the "asset class" thesis.
