## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened (one-liner).** On 2026-10-07 the Bank of Russia published its first official registers of licensed crypto-market infrastructure: 4 digital-currency exchange operators and 5 digital custodians/depositaries, admitted under the new crypto law whose core provisions took effect 2026-09-01. (This Binance Square aggregation is dated 10-08.)

**[1] Who registered / the registry specifics.**
- **Custodians / digital depositaries (5):** Sberbank, VTB Bank, Atomyze, Voltari, Cloud Infrastructure.
- **Exchange operators / exchangers (4):** T-Invest Lab (Т-Технологии/Tinkoff group), Zefir («Зефир»), Sistema-Crypto, VTB Bank. (Note: VTB appears on both lists; source attributions vary slightly — some list Zefir, Sistema-Crypto, T-Invest Lab + VTB.)
- Entries published on the CBR registry (cbr.ru/registries/admissionfinmarket). The prior internal note flagged «Зефир» as the anomalous first-wave entrant: a shell-like LLC registered 11.08.2025, charter capital RUB 10,000, no public crypto product, GD Andrey Kokorev / owner Irina Egorova.

**[2] Legal basis / regime.**
- Umbrella law: "On Digital Currency and Digital Rights" (referred to in the Russian channel as № 282-ФЗ), signed by President Putin in August 2026 (reported 04.08.2026); core provisions in force **01.09.2026**.
- The CBR admission mechanism (the registers themselves) took effect **05.10.2026** — which is why registrations appear only now.
- Framework: CBR licenses ~5 participant types (exchanges, brokers, asset managers, depositories, exchangers), each needing a registry entry, minimum equity (reported RUB 15m floor, scaling to RUB 50m–250m depending on open-ledger work / post-trade settlement), and SRO membership.
- Retail guardrails: non-qualified investors capped at RUB 300,000/year per intermediary after a suitability test; qualified investors unlimited. Domestic crypto payments for goods/services remain **banned**; cross-border trade settlement is permitted under the experimental legal regime (EPR), though EPR settlement remains little-used per CBR first deputy governor.

**[4] Why it matters / forward look.** First concrete operationalization of Russia's comprehensive crypto regime: state banks (Sber, VTB) and Tinkoff-group infrastructure now hold the first legal licenses, signaling a bank-controlled, heavily-supervised onshore market rather than an open exchange market. Watch: Sber custody launch (BTC/ETH/USDT) targeted 1 Dec; VTB trading "as early as November"; whether independent/shell operators (e.g. Zefir) are fronts or real; retail uptake under the RUB 300k cap.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russia's new crypto regime (Law on Digital Currencies/Digital Rights, in force Sept 1 2026) just moved from statute to operational infrastructure: on ~Oct 6-7 2026 the Bank of Russia published its first registers — 4 exchange operators (T-Invest Lab, Zefir, Sistema-Crypto, VTB) and 5 custodians (Sberbank, Atomyze, Voltari, Cloud Infrastructure, VTB) (per Cointelegraph/crypto.news, Oct 2026). Market size: domestic holdings estimated at ~₽3.7tn / ~$40bn across ~20mn investors; the regulated market is projected at up to ₽4tn (~$46bn) in year one, rising to ₽4.75–5.25tn by 2028 and ₽7.5tn by 2029 (per KuCoin/Cryptometer citing local estimates — secondary, treat as directional, not official). Structure: this is a licence-gated, bank-centric market — the gatekeeper is the central bank, and barriers are regulatory (registration) plus balance-sheet/compliance scale, which structurally favours large incumbent banks over crypto-native startups. Why now: a deliberate state pivot to a supervised, onshore venue for digital assets — partly to capture tax/oversight and partly as a sanctions-era channel for cross-border settlement (bitcoin permitted for cross-border trade from Sept 1).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for a regulated venue/custodian: registered-operator status (binary gate), AUC (assets under custody), trading volume/turnover, number of onboarded investors, and investor-cap compliance (non-qualified investors capped at ₽300k/yr via one intermediary after a test; qualified investors uncapped). Basis of competition is distribution + trust, not price: the winners are banks that already own the retail/HNW channel. Recent moves: T-Technologies (T-Bank) confirmed subsidiaries Atomyze and T-Invest Lab are licensed; VTB plans to open digital-currency trading via "VTB My Investments" and its own exchange in Nov–Dec 2026 (Interfax/crypto.news, Oct 2026). Protagonist here is the Bank of Russia itself — a regulator, not a competitor; it is the market-maker of the rulebook. Position: Russia is early among G20 peers in standing up a formal licence register for both trading and custody (one Yahoo Finance framing calls it "outpacing" the US), but this is regulatory-first, not product adoption — nothing is de-PR'd as "live at scale" yet. (analysis)

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/multiple attaches to this news — it is a regulatory registration, so EV/Revenue, P/S etc. = no data; no arithmetic to show. Internal comps on the custody/exchange-regulation theme from the base: [[Russia's Sberbank plans crypto-backed loans for corporate clients]] (same jurisdiction, same incumbent now a licensed custodian); [[Deutsche Bank to launch crypto custody service in 2026]], [[Citi plans to offer crypto custody services in 2026]], [[Ripple brings crypto custody to Spain via BBVA]], [[Bybit and Sygnum partner on off-exchange institutional custody]], [[Deribit joins Komainu's off-exchange settlement solution]], [[Bakkt, Galaxy and FalconX select Fireblocks for custody]] — the global pattern is regulated, bank-grade custody becoming table stakes; Russia's version differs in that the same incumbent banks hold BOTH the exchange and custody licences (VTB in both registers), which is the opposite of the West's segregation-of-duties / qualified-custodian separation. Distribution not computed, qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
- Sanctions / unbankability (primary): several named operators are sanctioned or sanction-exposed — OFAC designated VTB (Sept 14 2026) over Iran-linkage, and Russia's ruble stablecoin A7A5 (>$100bn volume) has reportedly stalled after US/UK/EU sanctions (daily volume ~$1.5bn → ~$500mn). A licensed-at-home venue run by sanctioned banks has little path to Western liquidity/fiat rails; the "market" may stay walled-garden and illiquid vs the global crypto stack.
- Concentration / self-dealing structure: the same state-linked banks hold both exchange and custody licences (VTB in both), concentrating counterparty, operational and conflict-of-interest risk in a few entities — the exact segregation Western qualified-custody regimes are built to avoid.
- Source/figure reliability: the $40bn / $46bn / ₽7.5tn figures are secondary/estimate-driven (KuCoin, Cryptometer, local projections), not CBR-verified — treat as directional; the "cross-border trade" use case invites further sanctions-evasion scrutiny abroad.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This formalises an onshore, state-controlled crypto rail that is deliberately insulated from (and unreachable by) the Western digital-asset stack — a parallel system, not a new node in the global one. (analysis) Falsifiable thesis: adoption will track distribution, not regulation — the licensed incumbents (Sberbank, VTB, T-Bank) will dominate domestic volume within a year. What to watch / trigger: VTB's Nov–Dec 2026 retail trading launch going live (first real de-PR'd volume) and whether any further OFAC/EU action targets the newly-registered operators — the latter would break the "legitimisation" thesis. What would make it wrong: meaningful foreign-liquidity access appearing despite sanctions.
