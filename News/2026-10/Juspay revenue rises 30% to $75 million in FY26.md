## Indian fintech firm Juspay revenue rises 30% to $75m

Share

Add TIA as a preferred source

Juspay, a Bengaluru-based payments software firm that helps businesses manage digital payments, said on October 13 that its FY26 revenue rose 30% year on year on a constant-currency basis to US$75 million.

The company reported a US$10 million net loss and US$4 million in adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization, excluding non-cash employee stock-option costs, as it invested in expansion abroad and AI.

The SoftBank-backed company said its platform processed 400 million daily transactions.

Juspay said it serves more than 500 enterprises and banks globally and expanded across APAC, Europe, the US, Latin America, and the Middle East.

The loss followed FY25, when Juspay reported profitability alongside 61% year-on-year revenue growth.

FY26 investments included Xyne, an open-source enterprise AI platform, and Breeze Buddy, a no-code AI voice platform.

Payments orchestration is a distinct layer alongside full-stack payment providers such as Stripe and Adyen.

# Context-enrichment: Juspay revenue rises 30% to $75M in FY26
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Juspay (Bengaluru payment orchestration / checkout SDK) reported FY26 (ended 31 Mar 2026): revenue **US$75M, +30% YoY** on a constant-currency basis (from ~$61M in FY25). The honest, PR-buried line: **the company swung back to a net loss of ~$10M** after FY25 profitability — i.e. **growth decelerated sharply (30% vs 61% in FY25) AND it went from profit to loss** in one year. Management reframes this around "adjusted EBITDA positive at $4M (ex-ESOP)" and "India payments business was profitable" — both true but both carve-outs that hide the group-level reversal. Metrics cited: ~400M daily transactions, annualised TPV >$1T, 500+ enterprises/banks, expansion into APAC/Europe/US/LatAm/Middle East. FY26 cash went into international expansion + AI (Xyne, an open-source enterprise AI platform; Breeze Buddy, no-code AI voice).

**Why structured this way (the red-team core):** FY25 profit (₹62cr / ~$7M) was the headline a year ago ([[Juspay turns profitable with ₹62 crore net profit in FY2025]]). To avoid reporting "we went back into the red," the PR (a PR Newswire press release, 13 Oct 2026, picked up verbatim by Tech in Asia, CFOtech, ecommercenews, Manila Times) leads with top-line + adjusted-EBITDA + "India profitable." The deceleration (61%→30%) and the loss are the signal. Crucially, FY26 is the first full year AFTER Juspay's core Indian orchestration customers abandoned it — see [1]/[3]; the $75M is being earned from a structurally different (international + direct-PA) business than FY25's.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Indian payments peers, FY26-ish revenue (from corpus):
- **Razorpay** — ~₹3,783cr (~$430M) FY25, +65%; confidentially filed for ~$600M IPO at ~$5B ([[Razorpay revenue jumps 65% in FY25]], [[Razorpay confidentially files for roughly $600 million IPO]]). Several × Juspay's scale.
- **Cashfree** — ~₹1,000cr (~$100M) FY26, +toward ₹1,000cr; raising $80–100M now ([[Cashfree Payments revenue nears ₹1,000 crore in FY26]], [[Cashfree Payments in talks to raise $80M-$100M]]).
- **PhonePe** — IPO-bound, ~$10–15B ambitions ([[Walmart-backed PhonePe targets $10.5 billion valuation in India IPO]]).
- Global orchestration/full-stack: Stripe, Adyen, Gr4vy, Spreedly, CellPoint, Solidgate ([[Payment orchestrator Gr4vy partners with Mastercard on integration]], [[Spreedly launches standalone payment vault for merchants]]).

**Why the landscape is this way (second order):** Juspay's three biggest domestic distribution partners — **PhonePe (Dec 2024), then Razorpay, Cashfree, and Paytm (2025)** — all suspended integrations and moved to direct merchant integration. The trigger: Juspay got its RBI Payment Aggregator licence and became a **direct competitor**, so aggregators had no reason to route volume through a rival's orchestrator. This is the defining competitive fact of FY26: Juspay's domestic moat (being the neutral router) collapsed *because* it climbed the stack into PA. That is why the $75M increasingly rests on **international** (Dubai/Brazil/HSBC/Mastercard Click-to-Pay tie-ups: [[Juspay opens Dubai headquarters to expand across GCC]], [[Juspay accelerates Brazil expansion with $50M cash]], [[HSBC and Juspay launch full-stack acquiring platform]], [[Juspay joins Mastercard Engage network for Click to Pay]]) rather than domestic orchestration.

## [2] Company history / fit
Trajectory: founded 2012, Accel 2016, VEF-led Series B 2020, **SoftBank Vision Fund 2 Series C Dec 2021 (~$460M val)**, Kedaara-led ~$60M (SoftBank/Accel) Apr 2025 (~$900M), **WestBridge $50M Jan 2026 → $1.2B unicorn** ([[Juspay raises $50M, becomes India's first 2026 unicorn]]); ~$161M raised total. FY24 loss ₹97cr → FY25 profit ₹62cr → **FY26 back to ~$10M loss**. **Why it acts this way:** classic orchestration squeeze — a neutral router earns thin take and is one license away from being disintermediated by both sides (aggregators integrate direct; merchants open-source the router via Juspay's own Hyperswitch). To defend a software multiple, Juspay is forced to (a) become a PA itself and (b) go global + add AI — but (a) is exactly what cost it the domestic partners, and (b) is why it is now burning again. The $1.2B unicorn label (Jan 2026) sits uneasily against a decelerating, loss-making FY26.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Nothing new is "launched" here — it's a results print. Real traction: TPV >$1T annualised and 400M daily txns are large and credible given India UPI scale; international expansion is live (HSBC acquiring, Brazil, GCC, Mastercard Click-to-Pay). **Why the value-add is contestable (deeper):** payment orchestration captures margin only if it stays the neutral layer that *routes* — but Juspay **open-sourced its routing engine (Hyperswitch, Mar 2025)**, meaning the router becomes the merchant's in-house, non-third-party layer. Who captures the margin then? Not the orchestrator. So Juspay's value migrates to (i) PA/acquiring take-rate (commodity, where Razorpay/Cashfree are bigger and now direct) and (ii) international acquiring + AI tooling (unproven revenue). The "adjusted EBITDA positive / India profitable" framing is the tell that the *growth* parts (international + AI) are loss-making. Durable-vs-transactional revenue mix is **open** and decisive.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect continued international push, AI monetization attempts (Xyne/Breeze Buddy), and pressure to re-prove a path back to group profit to justify the $1.2B mark. Regulatory backdrop: NPCI/RBI tightening around UPI market caps and PA licensing reshapes who can orchestrate ([[Smaller UPI firms to meet NPCI seeking level playing field]], [[RBI constitutes six-member Payments Regulatory Board]]); Apple Pay/Revolut/global entrants raise India competition ([[Apple launches Apple Pay in India with Axis Bank]]). **Why / counterintuitive second order:** Juspay's pivot to PA may *look* like climbing the value chain but it detonated its neutral-router distribution; the more it competes with aggregators, the less volume it orchestrates. The central question is not "is Juspay growing" but **"after losing domestic orchestration partners, can international + AI replace that margin before the $1.2B valuation needs defending in a down round?"** (analysis).

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** India payments splits into a consumer UPI layer (PhonePe, Google Pay, Paytm) and a merchant-facing aggregator/orchestration layer where Juspay sits. UPI dominates rails volume (~62% of India's digital-payment transaction volume, per gateway-pricing aggregators, 2026), but it is structurally hard to monetise: RBI mandates 0% MDR on UPI P2M, and even the new NPCI step — 0.4% MDR on P2M above ₹2,000 (capped ₹300), effective 15 Oct 2026 — leaves sub-₹2,000 tickets (most retail volume) zero-MDR (per fintechspecs.com, appadvisor.in, 2026). The value chain therefore pays software/orchestration players a fee for *routing, auth, conversion and success-rate* rather than for MDR take. "Why now": aggregators are converging on profitability (Cashfree EBITDA-positive Mar 2026, PayU India first full-year operating profit FY26) while Juspay deliberately went the other way — $10m net loss in FY26 to fund AI + overseas expansion. Barriers are real: RBI Payment Aggregator licensing, bank/enterprise integrations, and success-rate know-how (switching costs on the orchestration layer).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/annualised payment volume, transactions/day, number of enterprise/bank clients, take rate, and payment success-rate uplift. Juspay's own disclosure: 400m transactions/day, annualised TPV >$1 trillion, 500+ enterprise/bank clients across APAC/EU/US/LatAm/MENA (per PR Newswire, 13 Oct 2026). Juspay's position is distinctive — it is an **orchestration/software layer** (routing, Breeze checkout, SDKs), *not* a balance-sheet acquirer like Stripe/Adyen, nor primarily a merchant-of-record aggregator like Razorpay/Cashfree/PayU. Recent peer/own moves: `[[HSBC and Juspay launch full-stack acquiring platform]]`, `[[Juspay joins Mastercard Engage network for Click to Pay]]`, `[[Juspay opens Dubai headquarters to expand across GCC]]`, `[[Breeze by Juspay and super.money announce checkout partnership]]`; on the aggregator side `[[Razorpay gets shareholder approval for Rs 2,700 crore IPO]]` and `[[Cashfree Payments in talks to raise $80M-$100M]]`. Moat (analysis): bank/enterprise integration depth + success-rate optimisation on high-frequency rails = switching costs; the thin monetisation of UPI is actually what makes a software/orchestration model (vs MDR skim) rational.

**Comps & multiples.** Revenue scale (constant-currency / reported), India peers:
- **Juspay** — FY26 $75m (+30% cc; FY25 ~$61m, FY24 ~$39m → ~2yr ~40% CAGR per PR Newswire); $10m net loss, +$4m adj. EBITDA ex-ESOP. Last round: $1.2bn valuation (unicorn, WestBridge Series D ext., Jan 2026) → `[[Juspay raises $50M, becomes India's first 2026 unicorn]]`. Implied round P/S on $75m rev ≈ $1.2bn / $75m ≈ **16x** (round valuation, not market cap; rich vs aggregator peers but on a far smaller/software-mix base).
- **Razorpay** — FY25 rev ₹3,783cr (~$450m) +65% YoY → `[[Razorpay revenue jumps 65% in FY25]]`; IPO-bound (Rs2,700cr fresh issue approved). ~6x Juspay's revenue, faster growth.
- **Cashfree** — FY26 rev ~₹1,000cr (~$120m), EBITDA-positive Mar 2026 → `[[Cashfree Payments revenue nears ₹1,000 crore in FY26]]`; raising $80–100m.
- **PayU India** — FY25 rev +23%, loss ₹248cr narrowing → `[[PayU Payments narrows FY25 loss to Rs 248 crore]]`; first full-year operating profit FY26.
EV/Revenue and EV/EBITDA = **no data** (all private, no clean EV; multiples beyond the round-implied P/S are `[UNSOURCED]`). Comparability caveat: Juspay's revenue is orchestration/software + partial acquiring, not gross aggregator take — direct revenue-multiple comparison overstates how "cheap/rich" it is vs Razorpay/Cashfree. Distribution not computed (<3 clean valuation figures); qualitative only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **UPI monetisation ceiling / NPCI dependence** — zero-MDR on sub-₹2,000 P2M caps rails economics; the 15 Oct 2026 0.4% MDR tier is narrow and policy-reversible, so revenue leans on software/success-rate fees that merchants can renegotiate. Second-order: pricing power is weaker than headline TPV suggests.
2. **Profitability reversal while peers converge** — swung from FY25 profit (₹62cr net / 61% growth) to an FY26 $10m loss just as Cashfree/PayU turned profitable; the AI/overseas bet must pay back before investors re-rate, or growth-funded losses compress the $1.2bn valuation.
3. **Client/revenue concentration + disintermediation** — 500+ clients but TPV skewed to a few large enterprises/banks; as an orchestration layer it sits between merchant and acquirer, exposed to banks/acquirers (incl. partners like HSBC) in-sourcing routing and capturing the margin.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** India's merchant-payments layer is bifurcating: aggregators (Razorpay/Cashfree/PayU) chasing IPO-grade profitability vs Juspay re-investing losses into AI + global orchestration — a bet that software/success-rate, not MDR, is where durable margin lives (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if FY27 shows TPV/revenue growth re-accelerating *with* a return toward adj-EBITDA/net profit, the $1.2bn unicorn mark re-rates; trigger to watch — overseas (GCC/LatAm/EU) revenue mix and whether bank partners keep routing *through* Juspay rather than around it. Thesis breaks if net loss widens in FY27 without visible international revenue traction.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (headline read).** MIXED — top-line IN-LINE, bottom-line MISS vs own prior-year profit. Operating revenue ₹664 crore (~$75M), +30% YoY (constant-currency), decelerating hard from +61% in FY25; swung from a ₹62.3 crore net profit (FY25) to a ₹90 crore net loss (FY26); adjusted EBITDA stayed positive at ₹33 crore (~$4M, ex-ESOP). India payments core was profitable for the year; the loss is a deliberate spend on international expansion and AI, not a demand problem. No forward guidance given (private company). **Key figures (with growth).**

**Thesis-flags.**
1. **Growth halved (+61%→+30%) while TPV flattened near $1T** → the domestic UPI-volume S-curve is maturing → future rupee growth must come from mix-shift (orchestration/tokenization value) and geography, which are lower-certainty than the FY23–25 volume ramp → second-order: valuation that priced the 60%+ trajectory is now exposed to a re-rate if 30% is the new run-rate.
2. **Profit → loss swing (₹62cr → -₹90cr) with "adjusted EBITDA positive" framing** → management is steering attention to the ex-ESOP EBITDA line and the profitable India core, staying quieter on the ₹90cr GAAP loss and the quantum/timeline of AI spend → de-PR: "adjusted EBITDA positive" ≠ cash-generative once ESOP/D&A and overseas burn are counted.
3. **AI pivot (Xyne, Breeze, Genius) is capital-intensive and off the payments-core P&L** → a SoftBank-backed infra firm diversifying into open-source enterprise AI / agentic shopping is a bet far from switching/orchestration economics → if these don't monetise, they are a structural drag on the ex-India-core consolidated result.
4. **Orchestration is the durable moat (>50% of FY25 revenue, Stripe/Adyen-adjacent layer), but concentration risk persists** → ~80% of orchestration flows via payment aggregators and reliance on a few large anchors (HDFC, HSBC, Google Pay, Super.money) → churn or in-sourcing by any large anchor hits revenue disproportionately.
