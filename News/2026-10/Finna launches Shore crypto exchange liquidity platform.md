## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
### [0] What happened
On 2026-10-22, Finna (Finna Protocol), a Nigerian stablecoin-native fintech, launched **Shore** — an "exchange and liquidity" platform that lets P2P/OTC merchants, independent exchange operators, creators/influencers and trading businesses run **branded crypto exchanges under their own name** without building the underlying matching, wallet, fiat/crypto-rail or liquidity infrastructure. Shore provides branded exchange front-ends, fiat and crypto APIs, and on-demand liquidity to fulfil customer orders. ([[Finna launches Shore crypto exchange liquidity platform]])

### [1] Hard numbers (as reported)
- **>$500,000** processed by merchants on Shore during private beta.
- **>$100M** in total transactions processed by Finna since its own launch, of which **~80%** came from merchants running their own trading desks — the usage pattern Finna says Shore was built to productise.
- Model is white-label / liquidity-as-a-service: revenue presumably via spread/fee on routed liquidity + platform fees (not disclosed in coverage).

### [2] Who/why
- Founders (Nigerian crypto veterans): **Agbona Igwemoh** (co-founder/CEO), **Kparobo Abala** (co-founder/CTO), and **Adebayo Juwon** (co-founder, ex-FTX). Finna's core product is a stablecoin-to-fiat lending/liquidation protocol for emerging markets; it also runs a consumer app ("Finna: Loan, Buy & Sell Crypto").
- Stated rationale (Igwemoh): merchants already drove most activity on Finna's existing rails, so Shore formalises the "merchants-as-exchange-operators" pattern.
- Compliance posture: Finna says it has obtained documentation to be licensed as a finance company in Nigeria and holds AML certification — relevant given Nigeria/SEC scrutiny of P2P crypto.

### [3] Context / comparables
- **White-label-exchange-as-a-service** is an established global category — AlphaPoint, Shift Markets, B2BROKER, ChainUp, HollaEx all sell turnkey exchange stacks + bundled liquidity. Shore's differentiation is its focus on Africa's large informal **P2P/OTC merchant** layer rather than institutional/enterprise operators.
- Adjacent institutional-liquidity moves in the news DB: [[Bitget partners Fibonacci Capital on institutional liquidity]], [[FalconX and Copper extend ClearLoop off-exchange settlement]], [[Ripple extends $250M lifeline to crypto exchange Gemini]] — but those target institutional counterparties, not the retail-merchant long tail Shore chases.
- African peers (Yellow Card, Busha, Bundle, Mara) mostly run their *own* branded exchanges; Shore inverts that by selling the pipes to thousands of small operators.

### [4] So what / watch
- If the >$100M / 80%-merchant figure is durable, Shore is a credible wedge into the fragmented African P2P/OTC market that CEXs (Binance P2P) and incumbents don't serve well.
- Key risks to verify: these are **self-reported, unaudited** numbers from a launch press release; "licensed-as-finance-company" ≠ a securities/VASP licence for running exchanges; and enabling thousands of unvetted merchant-run exchanges concentrates AML/KYC and consumer-protection risk on Finna.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
### Red-team questions
1. Are the **>$100M processed** and **80%-merchant** figures audited, or just self-reported in the launch PR? (All coverage traces to Finna's own announcement.)
2. Is **>$500K in private beta** cumulative gross volume or net revenue? Over what period, and how many merchants?
3. What exactly is Finna "licensed" for in Nigeria — a generic finance-company registration vs. an SEC/VASP/exchange licence? Does Shore itself need separate authorisation?
4. Who provides the actual **liquidity** behind Shore — Finna's own book, third-party market makers, or aggregated CEX order flow? Counterparty/credit risk if Finna fronts it?
5. If thousands of merchants run branded exchanges on Shore, who holds AML/KYC and consumer-protection liability when one facilitates fraud/money-laundering?
6. Does the ex-FTX co-founder pedigree (Adebayo Juwon) raise diligence/perception issues given FTX's collapse?
7. Is this genuinely novel, or just a repackaged white-label stack vs. AlphaPoint/Shift/B2BROKER/HollaEx already selling exchange-as-a-service?
8. How does Shore defend against Binance P2P and large CEXs that already serve African merchants at scale?
9. What's the unit economics — does Finna earn spread, SaaS fees, or both, and is it positive at these volumes?
10. Regulatory tail risk: Nigeria's SEC and CBN have periodically restricted crypto/P2P; could a crackdown kill merchant-operator demand overnight?
11. Custody: do merchants/end-users hold their own keys, or does Finna custody funds (making it a de facto exchange with custody risk)?
12. Is there any independent confirmation (not Finna-sourced) of merchant adoption or volume?
13. Does "creators/influencers running exchanges" create pump-and-dump / affinity-fraud exposure?
14. What's the geographic scope — Nigeria-only or pan-African? Which fiat corridors are live?

Importance: 2/5 — A genuinely interesting go-to-market (productising Africa's P2P/OTC merchant layer as white-label exchange infra), but it's an early launch from a small, unlisted Nigerian startup with only self-reported, unaudited volume numbers, no disclosed funding, and in a crowded white-label-exchange category. Noteworthy as a signal on African crypto-infra models rather than a market-moving event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector context — crypto exchange liquidity infrastructure.** Shore is a white-label exchange-and-liquidity layer: it lets P2P/OTC merchants, independent exchange operators and creators run branded crypto exchanges without building the underlying tech, drawing on Finna's liquidity to fill customer orders (TechCabal, 2026-10-22). This sits in the "exchange-as-a-service" / liquidity-provisioning segment, where the operator owns the brand and KYC relationship while a vendor supplies the orderbook, matching and liquidity. The closest Western analogues are AlphaPoint, B2Broker, Shift Markets, HollaEx and PayBito, which sell modular white-label exchange stacks bundled with sourced liquidity and back-office/compliance tooling (Finance Magnates "Top White Label Crypto Exchange Providers of 2026"). Finna's angle is narrower and regionally specific: sub-Saharan Africa stablecoin rails, serving the large informal P2P/OTC merchant base that today runs on chat apps and spreadsheets.

**Scale / traction (claimed, unaudited).** Finna says merchants processed >$500K during Shore's private beta, and that Finna has processed >$100M in cumulative transaction volume since launch, with merchant-run trading desks accounting for ~80% of that volume (TechCabal; Business Tech Africa). Note the headline-source body ("launched Shore") carries no volume figures; all scale claims come from Finna's own statements to the press — treat as self-reported, not independently verified. Finna Protocol was a Circle USDC-grant cohort company (one of 4 African projects selected); no disclosed priced equity round was found. No IR coverage exists for company/finna (ir_coverage.json), so no filings to ground against.

**In-base comparables.** The news DB has several adjacent liquidity-infrastructure plays, mostly at a very different (institutional) altitude:
- [[News/2026-03/Crossover Markets raises $31m Series B at $200m valuation]] — institutional spot-crypto liquidity pools, $200M valuation, Tradeweb-led. Shows investor appetite for liquidity infra, but targets regulated institutions, not retail OTC merchants.
- [[News/2025-10/Binance introduces crypto-as-a-service for institutions]] — white-label crypto rails + liquidity for licensed banks/brokerages; the "big player enters your segment" risk for a niche provider like Shore.
- [[News/2026-01/Crypto.com expands CryptoStruct tie-up for high-frequency trading]] and [[News/2025-08/Bitget partners Fibonacci Capital on institutional liquidity]] — HFT/market-maker liquidity partnerships at tier-1 exchanges; set the depth/pricing bar incumbents can offer.
- [[News/2025-08/FalconX and Copper extend ClearLoop off-exchange settlement]] — off-exchange settlement, the institutional counterpart to the counterparty-risk problem Shore's model concentrates.
All in-base comps are institutional; there is no direct in-base comp for an Africa-focused, merchant-/creator-facing white-label exchange layer, so the opportunity (and the lack of validation) is distinctive.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Unverified metrics.** The $100M cumulative and ~80%-merchant-volume figures are self-reported to media with no audited source and no IR disclosure; beta volume of only ~$500K is modest and may not extrapolate.
2. **Liquidity-depth dependency.** The value proposition hinges on Finna sourcing enough liquidity to fill merchant orders; in thin/volatile African stablecoin markets, slippage and fill failures directly hit the operator's brand — an execution risk Finna bears but doesn't control end-to-end.
3. **Crowded, better-capitalised white-label field.** AlphaPoint (150+ platforms), B2Broker, Shift, HollaEx and PayBito already sell exchange-as-a-service with liquidity bundled; Binance's crypto-as-a-service can undercut on liquidity cost. Finna's moat is regional/stablecoin focus, not technology.
4. **Regulatory / enabling-infrastructure risk.** Letting creators and "independent operators" run branded exchanges can shift KYC/AML and licensing burden onto parties unequipped for it; African regulators are tightening (cf. P2P crackdowns elsewhere, e.g. [[News/2026-10/Bank of Russia to ban direct P2P crypto trading from July 2027]], and Nigerian P2P scrutiny), raising the chance that the merchant-exchange model itself gets restricted.
5. **Counterparty / custody concentration.** A B2B2C model concentrates settlement and custody risk at Finna; a single operational failure propagates to every white-label operator and their end-users at once.
