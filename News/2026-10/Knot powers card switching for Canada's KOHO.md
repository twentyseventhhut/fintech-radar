# Context-enrichment: Knot powers card switching for Canada's KOHO
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Knot (NYC merchant-connectivity startup) is now the engine behind an in-app "set my card as default at merchants" feature inside the app of [[KOHO raises CAD$130M as it pursues Canadian bank charter|KOHO]], a Canadian neobank with ~2.5M customers. KOHO users can set their KOHO **prepaid Mastercard** as the saved card-on-file at merchants they already use ("a few taps, without leaving the app"). This is Knot's **CardSwitcher** product embedded via SDK.

**Why Mastercard is the source (and why that matters):** the KOHO card is a Mastercard credential and Mastercard is a Knot partner/investor-adjacent party (Mastercard appears on Knot's client/partner wall). So the "source" is network brand-marketing amplifying a credential win — Mastercard is **not** operating the tech. De-PR'd: this is NOT Mastercard's Automatic Billing Updater (ABU). ABU/VAU auto-propagate a *changed* PAN/expiry to already-enrolled merchants; Knot does *user-driven net-new placement* — the user logs into each merchant via Knot's SDK (handling MFA/step-up), Knot authenticates and writes the KOHO card as default, typically <15s.

**The real tell — "expansion in Canada":** this is Knot's **second** Canadian client, not its market debut. Knot entered Canada ~2026-07-23 via RBC (its first expansion outside the US; no internal note exists on that yet). So KOHO is *incremental proof of a Canada motion*, not a new market or new product. (analysis)

**Live vs announced:** PR reads as live/rolling out ("is now powering"), but there is NO independent confirmation of GA, a KOHO-specific merchant list, or any user-uptake metric. Treat "live for KOHO users" as PR-asserted, unverified.

## [1] Competitors / peers
**For Knot (card-on-file switching):** the adjacent rails are network account-updaters — Mastercard **ABU** and Visa **Account Updater (VAU)** — but these *maintain* an existing card-on-file by pushing new credentials to enrolled merchants; they do NOT place a *new* card where no prior relationship exists, nor drive top-of-wallet. Different problem → complementary, not head-to-head. Direct scaled fintech peers are few; Plaid is a Knot referral *partner/investor*, not a rival here. Knot's moat = its pre-built merchant-automation library + SDK handling of MFA/error states. **Position: de-facto category leader**, but on a thin ~$13M funding base — notably, Amex and Plaid chose to *fund/partner* rather than build, which both validates the category and caps Knot's defensibility if a strategic decides to internalize it. (analysis)

**For KOHO (Canadian neobanks):** Wealthsimple (largest/broadest), Neo Financial (Calgary), EQ Bank (Equitable Bank's CDIC-member digital arm), plus incumbent arms Tangerine (Scotiabank) and Simplii (CIBC). KOHO was the 3rd fintech onto Interac e-Transfer directly ([[KOHO joins Interac e-Transfer directly as it nears banking licence]]).

## [2] Company history / fit
**Knot:** NYC; founders Rory (CEO) & Kieran (CTO) O'Reilly. Founding year reported inconsistently (2019/2020/2021 — open). Funding ~$13M total: $3M seed (2021) + $10M Series A (Aug 2023, led by Nava Ventures, with Amex Ventures, Plaid, angels). No verified Series B. Headcount ~40–52. Named clients span Amex, PayPal/Venmo, Current, OnePay, Bilt, Step, Fizz, Klarna, Sezzle, RBC. KOHO fits the pattern cleanly: a prepaid-Mastercard neobank's economics depend on its card being the *default spend card*, and CardSwitcher directly drives top-of-wallet, activation, and interchange-bearing spend.

**KOHO:** Toronto; ~2.5M customers (up from ~1M in late 2023 — figure consistent across Money.ca/Yahoo/BetaKit, verified). Prepaid Mastercard (switched from Visa in 2022), savings, credit-building, crypto trading (launched ~May 2026, see [[KOHO launches crypto offering powered by Ndax in Canada]]). Raised ~CAD$130M on 2026-06-11 at a CAD$1.33B valuation (unicorn; Mubadala, Tobi Lütke, et al.), explicitly to fund a Schedule 1 federal bank-licence bid ([[KOHO raises CAD$130M as it pursues Canadian bank charter]]). **Why KOHO wants this now:** retention/engagement feature that deepens card usage and supports the "we behave like a bank" narrative ahead of the OSFI process. (analysis)

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Moderately novel but **incremental**: a new *client win* on an already-announced Canada expansion — no new tech, no new market. Real traction signal: Knot has landed two Canadian names (RBC → KOHO) in ~3 months, a credible beachhead. BUT no hard metrics disclosed (merchants live for KOHO, switch volumes, conversion); the 2.5M figure is KOHO's reach, not Knot usage. **Who captures the margin:** value accrues first to KOHO (more top-of-wallet default-card spend → more interchange), and to Knot as B2B SDK licensing — but the switching mechanic is credential-automation (scraping/login flows), which is brittle and may sit uneasily against merchant ToS, a structural durability risk if merchants or networks formalize an alternative. (hypothesis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect Knot to keep stacking Canadian issuers/fintechs, leaning on network sponsors (Mastercard here) for distribution. For KOHO it's engagement plumbing supporting the bank-licence story. Sentiment: mildly positive, PR-grade. Watch: (a) whether KOHO's eventual migration from prepaid Mastercard to a licensed-bank debit/deposit product changes the product this feature rests on (CardSwitcher likely still applies); (b) any Knot Series B (thin $13M base vs. ambition); (c) credential-automation durability vs. merchant ToS. **Second-order:** the more issuers Knot signs, the more it becomes infrastructure a network (Visa/MC) or Plaid would rather own than rent — concentration of the category in one thinly-funded vendor makes it an acquisition target, not an independent moat. (analysis)

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Card-on-file (COF) switching / credential-management is a thin middleware layer sitting between an issuer's card and merchants' stored-card vaults; the job is to make an issuer's card the *default* at merchants a consumer already uses, driving "top-of-wallet" primacy. No clean TAM is publicly sourceable — "no data" (don't invent). Structure: fragmented at the switching-API layer (startups: Knot, Spreedly's vaulting adjacency, Atomic for payroll/deposit switching) but the economic value accrues to issuers/neobanks chasing primacy, and the plumbing increasingly overlaps with the card networks' own rails. Why now: (1) regulatory — from **October 2026 all Mastercard Credential-on-File transactions must use network tokens** (per Mastercard dev docs), making COF/token management a live operational requirement, not optional; (2) neobank economics — primacy/engagement is the single biggest lever on interchange + deposit revenue, so "switch your card in a few taps" is a direct ARPU play. Knot entered Canada earlier in 2026 via **RBC** (its first market outside the US), so KOHO is a *follow-on* Canadian win, not the beachhead (per TradeInformer/Yahoo Finance).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for the neobank — card primacy / % of spend captured, active-card ratio, interchange-per-active-user; for Knot (API vendor) — number of connected merchants, # of issuers live, switch conversion rate (none disclosed → `[UNSOURCED]`). Players & basis of competition: (a) **Knot** — flagship CardSwitcher + AccountUpdater/TransactionLink APIs; prior partners include **PayPal** (CardSwitcher live Apr-2025), **Plaid** (distribution partner, also investor), **Fiserv/Lyft Direct** (Feb-2026), **RBC** (Canada entry 2026); (b) **Mastercard/Visa network tokenization** — MDES / Visa Token Service with native Account Updater, the incumbents whose rails Knot's "switch" ultimately rides and who could internalize the use case; (c) adjacent switching players — **Atomic** (direct-deposit/payroll switching), **Spreedly** (card vaulting/orchestration). Basis of competition = merchant-coverage breadth + distribution deals, not price. Knot's position: *ahead* among pure-play COF-switching startups (named-logo issuer wins across US + now Canada); moat = merchant-connection coverage + switching costs once embedded in an issuer's app `(analysis)` — but it is a distribution/integration moat, not a network-tokenization moat.

**Comps & multiples.** **KOHO (the client, internal comp):** [[KOHO raises CAD$130M as it pursues Canadian bank charter]] — Series F CAD$130M (~USD$93M) at **CAD$1.33bn / ~USD$951M** valuation (Jun-2026); >$200M annual revenue, +50% YoY → P/S = `$951M / $200M ≈ 4.8x` on revenue (round valuation, not market cap; in-line for a ~50%-growth neobank). Other KOHO base context: [[KOHO joins Interac e-Transfer directly as it nears banking licence]], [[KOHO registers as payment service provider with Bank of Canada]], [[KOHO launches crypto offering powered by Ndax in Canada]]. **Knot (the protagonist):** total funding ~**$13M** (Seed $3M 2021 + Series A led by Nava Ventures w/ Amex Ventures & Plaid); last recorded valuation **~$25–50M (2023, stale)** per PitchBook/CB-type profiles — too old and too private to compute a credible multiple → revenue/EV multiples = **no data**. Distribution not computed — only one comparable revenue-multiple figure (KOHO), so qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Disintermediation by the networks.** Knot's switch rides on Mastercard/Visa COF + tokenization rails; with mandatory network tokens for Mastercard COF from Oct-2026, the schemes have both the mandate and the plumbing to internalize "move the card across merchants," compressing the independent switching layer's value capture.
2. **Thin economics / undisclosed terms.** The PR is silent on who pays Knot, per-switch economics, and conversion — classic de-PR gap; with only ~$13M raised and a stale ~$25–50M valuation, Knot is sub-scale vs the issuers it serves, raising counterparty/durability risk for a partner KOHO depends on.
3. **"Announced vs adopted" + concentration.** This is a launch announcement (prepaid Mastercard, "add in a few taps"), not disclosed adoption; Knot's revenue appears concentrated in a handful of large logo deals (PayPal, RBC, Fiserv), so any single churn is material.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` For KOHO this is a primacy/engagement tool timed to its bank-charter push ([[KOHO raises CAD$130M as it pursues Canadian bank charter]]) — making its prepaid card top-of-wallet before it can hold deposits. The falsifiable thesis: COF-switching stays a standalone layer only until the networks' mandatory-tokenization rails (live Oct-2026) absorb it; watch whether Knot's next wins are *net-new issuers* vs the networks shipping a native "switch-my-card" primitive — the latter would break the independent-vendor thesis.
