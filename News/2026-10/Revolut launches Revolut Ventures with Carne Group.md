## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened.** On 2026-10-26/27 Revolut announced the incorporation of **Revolut Ventures**, a new platform built in collaboration with **Carne Group** (described as Europe's largest third-party management company / ManCo) to give eligible Revolut customers in-app access to **unlisted, pre-IPO technology companies**. The feature is slated to go **live in 2027** — i.e. this is an announcement/incorporation, not a launched product. It is positioned as the natural evolution of Revolut's **Private Markets** feature, which rolled out in select EEA markets in **July 2026**.

**Structure and partner roles (key detail).** Revolut Ventures is being structured as a **European Long-Term Investment Fund (ELTIF)** — not a classic VC fund where Revolut deploys its own balance sheet. Per multiple reports: **Carne Group operates the fund and acts as the AIFM** (alternative investment fund manager); **Revolut is the delegated investment manager**; **Alter Domus** handles fund administration; and **BNY** acts as custodian/depositary. EEA investment services are provided via **Revolut Securities Europe UAB** (regulated by the Bank of Lithuania). So despite the "Ventures" name, this is closer to a **retail brokerage/distribution feature wrapped in a fund** than a true venture-capital vehicle — Revolut's edge is distribution (its app + ~60M+ user base) and manufacturing, with Carne providing the regulated fund machinery.

**The thesis Revolut sells.** Companies stay private longer — median age at IPO rose from **6.9 years (2014) to 11 years (2025)** — so most value creation now happens pre-listing. Separately, the universe of US listed equities roughly **halved, from ~7,000 (2000) to ~3,900 (2025)**. Conclusion: retail is structurally excluded from the best growth, and Revolut wants to "tear down one of the last barriers in wealth management." Quote from **Rolandas Juteika, Head of Wealth & Trading (EEA)**.

**What is NOT disclosed (important).** No **fund size**, no minimum investment, no eligibility definition ("eligible investors" undefined), no target countries, no fee schedule, no specific portfolio companies, no check sizes. All material commercial terms are TBD. Some German-language outlets (finews) headline that PE specialists (Apollo, Ares, Hamilton Lane, Partners Group) "invest from 2027" — treat this as unverified/likely conflated with Revolut's broader Private Markets fund partners rather than confirmed Ventures backers.

**Internal context — THIS is one of three distinct Revolut events this cycle.** Treat separately from [[Revolut in talks with Philippine central bank for banking license]] and [[Revolut launches personal loans in the UK]]. This note sits in Revolut's wealth/trading expansion arc:
- Valuation/IPO momentum: [[Revolut hits $121.2B valuation as IPO talk intensifies]] (importance 4) — a private-markets retail product is strategically on-brand while Revolut itself is a pre-IPO mega-cap; [[Revolut favors US primary listing for potential IPO]].
- Platform ambition: [[Revolut aims to become global tech company beyond finance]] / [[Revolut plans broader technology product suite beyond finance]].
- Wealth build-out precedent: [[Revolut gets UK approval to expand investment and wealth services]] (2026-05). Private Markets (July 2026) is the direct predecessor — there appears to be **no dedicated note for the July 2026 Private Markets launch** in the vault, so this is the first wealth/private-markets note of its kind here, not a duplicate.
- Comps for retail private-markets access: [[Gridline raises $18.5M Series A for private market investing]], [[Capital Engine and Regiment ally on private markets co-syndication]], [[iAltA Holdings acquires BridgeFT for wealth infrastructure]]; secondary-market/pre-IPO liquidity context via Forge Global pricing cited in the valuation note. ## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. "Launch" is misleading — this is an **incorporation/announcement with a 2027 go-live**. Is there any committed launch date, or could this slip the way Revolut's UK banking licence repeatedly did?
2. Who actually qualifies as an **"eligible investor"**? ELTIF 2.0 lets retail in, but with suitability tests and (in some regimes) concentration caps. Without disclosed eligibility, is this really "retail for everyone" or a thin top slice?
3. **No fund size, no minimum, no fees disclosed.** What are the all-in costs (ManCo + admin + custody + Revolut's margin) and how do they compare to just buying listed tech ETFs?
4. In an ELTIF wrapper **Carne is the AIFM and Revolut is only delegated investment manager** — how much real discretion/selection power does Revolut have, and who bears liability if a portfolio company blows up?
5. **Liquidity trap:** the source itself admits these holdings are far less liquid than listed equities. ELTIFs typically have gated/periodic redemptions. How do retail users exit, and is that risk clearly surfaced in-app?
6. **Valuation/mark problem:** private companies are marked infrequently and subjectively. How are NAVs struck, and could retail be buying at stale or promotional marks (adverse selection — why would prime allocations flow to a retail channel rather than top-tier VCs)?
7. **Adverse selection on supply:** the hottest pre-IPO names are oversubscribed by institutions. What quality of deal flow realistically reaches a retail ELTIF — is Revolut getting primary allocations or just secondary scraps at a premium?
8. The "companies stay private longer / listed universe halved" **narrative is accurate but self-serving** — it justifies the product but doesn't prove retail gets good risk-adjusted returns. Where's the evidence retail private-markets access outperforms net of fees?
9. **finews claim** that Apollo/Ares/Hamilton Lane/Partners Group "invest from 2027" — is that about Revolut Ventures specifically, or conflated with the separate July 2026 Private Markets fund lineup? Needs verification before repeating.
10. **Regulatory/conduct risk:** selling illiquid private equity to mass-market retail is exactly the area UK FCA/EU regulators are scrutinising. Given Revolut's existing conduct/fraud-complaints baggage ([[Revolut tops UK bank fraud complaints for third year]]), is mis-selling exposure material?
11. **Jurisdiction gap:** EEA services run through Revolut Securities Europe UAB (Lithuania). Does this exclude the UK (Revolut's home/largest market) at launch, and does that blunt the headline?
12. **Conflict of interest:** Revolut is itself a flagship pre-IPO tech company ([[Revolut hits $121.2B valuation as IPO talk intensifies]]). Could Revolut shares or its own cap-table relationships end up in a Revolut-managed fund?
13. Is this primarily an **engagement/AUM-gathering play** (keep users and deposits in-app ahead of its own IPO) rather than a genuine returns product?
14. **Scale reality:** with no fund size, is this a marketing banner that quietly serves a tiny cohort, or a product that can absorb meaningful retail inflows without distorting the underlying private markets?

**Importance: 3/5** — Strategically notable (Revolut extending its wealth/trading stack into private markets, reinforcing the "super-app before IPO" story) and well-sourced, but it is a 2027-dated announcement with no fund size, pricing, eligibility, or portfolio disclosed, and structurally a distribution feature rather than a capital commitment. Higher than routine product news, below the valuation/IPO and licensing stories.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Retail access to private markets / pre-IPO equity — a 2025–26 land-grab across fintech. Structural "why now": companies stay private far longer (median age to IPO 6.9y in 2014 → 11y in 2025, per Revolut/Carne via [fundssociety](https://www.fundssociety.com/es/noticias/alternativos/revolut-crea-revolut-ventures-en-colaboracion-con-carne-group)) and the listed universe has shrunk (US listed companies ~7,000 in 2000 → ~3,900 in 2025, same source), so value creation migrates pre-IPO. Ondo cites ~87% of US companies with >$100M revenue as privately held, with US PE returning 13.2% annualized over 20y vs 11.3% public (per [ondo.finance](https://ondo.finance/blog/introducing-ondo-private-markets), 2026-10-07). US private firms are now estimated >$10tn in aggregate (per [Bloomberg via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/robinhood-opens-private-market-retail-210432926.html), 2026-02). Structure: historically fragmented and gated behind accreditation / $75K+ minimums (Moonfare), with distribution — not sourcing — the real barrier; the entrant who owns the retail funnel (app + brokerage licence + brand) captures the wrapper economics. Drivers: regulation easing retail access (EU ELTIF 2.0), AI-era private unicorns (OpenAI, SpaceX, Databricks) as demand magnets, and incumbent brokers racing to add private exposure.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: AUM / inflows into the wrapper, management-fee take rate, minimum ticket, and number/quality of underlying names. Basis of competition = distribution reach + name access + fee/minimum friction, not investment skill. Key players and recent moves:
- **Robinhood** — filed to raise $1bn for its closed-end RVI fund, no accreditation, no minimum, $25/share; portfolio spans Airwallex, Databricks, Ramp, OpenAI-adjacent names — and notably Revolut itself (per [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-17/robinhood-s-1-billion-fund-pitches-pre-ipo-stock-as-next-craze), 2026-02). This is the closest head-to-head: a licensed retail broker wrapping private names for the mass market.
- **Ondo** — tokenized, 24/7-tradable private exposure, pitching on-chain liquidity as its edge ([[Ondo launches Ondo Private Markets for tokenized exposure]], 2026-10-07).
- **Moonfare** ($75K min, accredited only; $125M Series C led by Insight) and **Republic** — the accredited-investor incumbents Revolut/Robinhood aim to undercut on access.
- In-base peers building private-markets rails: [[Vennre raises $9.6M pre-Series A for MENA wealth]], [[Gridline raises $18.5M Series A for private market investing]], [[Capital Engine and Regiment ally on private markets co-syndication]].

Revolut's position: **catching up on product, ahead on distribution.** It is not first (Robinhood/Ondo launched live in early/mid-2026; Revolut Ventures is only *incorporated*, with the vehicle slated to launch in **2027** as an ELTIF run by Carne with Revolut as delegated investment manager — per [Business Review](https://business-review.eu/investments/revolut-targets-private-markets-with-new-investment-platform-for-retail-customers-302296) and [Yahoo Finance](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/revolut-launch-platform-investing-private-105046154.html), 2026-10-26). But Revolut's moat is scale distribution — 68.3M retail customers (FY2025) and a built-out in-app wealth stack (US/EEA stocks, ETFs, bonds, money-market and Private Market funds) — a funnel neither Moonfare nor most rivals possess *(analysis)*.

**Comps & multiples.** Revolut is private; no EV/Revenue is computable from public filings — valuation comes from secondary marks, not a market cap.
- **Revolut (protagonist):** FY2025 revenue $6.0bn (+46%), PBT $2.3bn (+57%, 38% margin), 68.3M retail customers, balances $67.5bn — per Revolut Annual Report 2025 (year to 31 Dec 2025), [IR PDF](https://assets.revolut.com/pdf/annualreport2025.pdf) and [Revolut newsroom](https://www.revolut.com/en-US/news/revolut_reports_record_profit_of_2_3bn_for_2025_as_revenue_surges_to_6bn/). Latest secondary mark $121.2bn (Forge, 15 Oct 2026) → implied P/S ≈ `$121.2bn / $6.0bn = 20.2x` *(secondary-market valuation, not public market cap)* ([[Revolut hits $121.2B valuation as IPO talk intensifies]]). That ~20x is at the top of the sanity range but defensible on ~46% revenue growth + genuine $2.3bn profit (growth-justified, not a flag on its own).
- **Carne Group (partner):** private; last-round / revenue multiple not publicly verifiable → `[UNSOURCED]`. Carne is described as Europe's largest third-party management-company / ManCo (per [fundssociety](https://www.fundssociety.com/es/noticias/alternativos/revolut-crea-revolut-ventures-en-colaboracion-con-carne-group)).
- **Moonfare:** private, Series C led by Insight Partners; post-money not disclosed here → `[UNSOURCED]`.
- No ≥3 comparable public figures → peer-multiple distribution not computed; qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Announcement vs live — 2027 gap.** Only the entity is incorporated; the ELTIF launches in 2027, so Revolut is shipping a press release while Robinhood/Ondo already transact. Execution and regulatory-approval risk between now and launch; first-mover framing is PR, not reality.
2. **Retail suitability / illiquidity & capital-loss risk.** Revolut's own text concedes these instruments are "significantly less liquid than listed equity" with "substantial risk of capital loss." Mass-marketing illiquid pre-IPO exposure to 68M retail users invites mis-selling / consumer-duty scrutiny (esp. FCA/EU) — the same conduct exposure that already dogs Revolut (fraud-complaint record). Second-order: a drawdown in a hyped AI name could trigger regulatory backlash across the whole retail-private-markets category.
3. **Delegated-manager / ManCo dependency + silence on economics.** Revolut is only *delegated* investment manager; Carne runs the ELTIF, so the fee split, minimum ticket, and which names are accessible are all undisclosed — the economics most relevant to whether this is margin-accretive are exactly what the PR omits.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Confirms private-markets retail access is becoming table-stakes for every scaled neobank/broker, not a niche — the differentiator shifts from *access* to *distribution + brand + fee friction*, which structurally favors the app with the largest logged-in base *(analysis)*. Falsifiable thesis: Revolut's 68M-user funnel lets it out-gather AUM vs accredited-only incumbents (Moonfare) once live. Trigger to watch: the 2027 launch economics (fee take rate, minimum ticket, named underlying companies) and whether any EU/UK regulator constrains retail distribution of the ELTIF — either would make or break the thesis.

Revolut is IR-covered, so its latest filed results are folded in below as financial context for the company behind the move. Verdict on the company's standalone print: **BEAT / strong** (record FY2025).

**Verdict (headline read).** FY2025 (year ended 31 Dec 2025): revenue $6.0bn (+46% YoY) · profit before tax $2.3bn (+57% YoY) · net profit $1.7bn (up from $1.0bn) · drivers: customer growth +30% to 68.3m and a 66% jump in customer balances · fifth consecutive year of net profitability. No sell-side consensus (Revolut is private/non-listed) → beat/miss framed qualitatively vs prior year.

**Key figures (with growth).**
- Group revenue: $6.0bn (£4.5bn), +46% YoY, from $4.0bn (£3.1bn) in FY2024.
- Profit before tax: $2.3bn (£1.7bn), +57% YoY; PBT margin 38%, up from 35% in FY2024.
- Net profit: $1.7bn (£1.3bn), up from $1.0bn (£790m) in FY2024.
- Retail customers: 68.3m at year-end, +30% YoY (16m net new added); business customers 767K, +33% YoY.
- Customer balances: $67.5bn (£50.2bn), +66% YoY, from $38bn (£30bn).

**By segment / driver.** Diversification is broad: 11 product lines each cleared ~$135m (£100m) in revenue, and Revolut Business now contributes 16% of total income. Growth is powered by net customer adds plus a step-up in deposited balances (+66%), which feeds interest income — i.e. the mix is shifting from pure interchange/subscription toward a bank-style balance-and-NII engine. (analysis) The Ventures/Private Markets push sits in the Wealth & Trading line, Revolut extending its monetisation of retail balances into higher-margin, less-liquid private assets.

**vs expectations / prior period.** No public analyst consensus exists for a private company → [UNSOURCED] for any "expected" baseline; assessed vs prior year. Every top-line metric accelerated: revenue +46% (vs the slower prior-year base), PBT +57% with margin expanding 300bps to 38% — operating leverage, not just top-line. Net profitability now five years running.

**Guidance / forward.** No formal quarterly guidance (private company). Management's stated long-term targets: 100m retail customers by mid-2027 across 100+ countries, backed by a ~$13bn five-year investment commitment. Plausibility: reaching 100m from 68.3m by mid-2027 implies sustaining ~30% net adds — ambitious but in line with the current run-rate (analysis). The US push and continued banking-licence rollout are the key forward assumptions.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. Margin expansion (PBT margin 35%→38%) shows the model scales with operating leverage, not just growth-at-any-cost → supports a premium private valuation and funds the $13bn investment plan without diluting profitability.
2. Customer balances +66% to $67.5bn → NII becomes a larger, rate-sensitive revenue pillar; second-order risk is earnings sensitivity to falling rates, a line management does not foreground.
3. Product breadth (11 lines >$135m) de-risks reliance on any single stream; Revolut Ventures/Private Markets is the latest adjacency extending wallet share in Wealth → incremental take-rate on balances already on-platform rather than new-customer-dependent.
4. Scale (68.3m customers, $6bn revenue) gives distribution to launch private-markets access cheaply — the Carne Group partnership supplies fund-structuring/third-party management expertise Revolut lacks, de-risking the regulatory/operational side of alternatives.
