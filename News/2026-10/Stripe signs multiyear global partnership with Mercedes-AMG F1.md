# Stripe signs multiyear global partnership with Mercedes-AMG F1

### stripe.com

*384 слов · direct*

LONDON—Stripe, the programmable financial services company, today announced a multiyear global partnership with the Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS F1 Team. As the team’s Official Global Payments Partner and an Official Team Partner, Stripe will collaborate across the team’s global commercial operations, applying Stripe’s technology to support the continued evolution of its financial and payments capabilities worldwide. Stripe’s branding will debut on the team’s cars and across team assets from the first race of the 2027 season. The first of a series of product deployments will launch at key races during the remainder of 2026, beginning with the team’s retail experience.
Alongside the announcement, Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS F1 Team Principal and CEO Toto Wolff joins Stripe Cofounder John Collison’s podcast Cheeky Pint , released today.
John Collison, Cofounder and President at Stripe, said, “Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS F1 Team has built one of the most ambitious and successful organisations in sports. Behind that is an extraordinary standard of engineering and a relentless drive to improve. That combination resonates with how we build at Stripe. We’re looking forward to applying that same approach to the financial infrastructure supporting their next stage of growth.”
Toto Wolff, Team Principal and CEO, Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS F1 Team, said, “Stripe is one of the world’s leading fintech brands and a hugely influential technology company that helps businesses operate and grow on a global scale. When the opportunity arose to rethink our approach in this sector, we wanted to be ambitious and partner with the very best. Stripe was the only choice. Having a partner that has Stripe’s expertise and their huge reach will help us build the right foundations for the future as we continue to develop our business in this fast-moving and increasingly connected world.”
Richard Sanders, Chief Commercial Officer, Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS F1 Team, said, “As part of such a global sport, we operate across multiple markets, currencies, and customer touchpoints, from merchandise and experiences to partner relationships and international transactions. Stripe’s platform will help us simplify and strengthen the way we manage those interactions, creating a more seamless experience for fans, partners and stakeholders around the world. Beyond supporting our day-to-day operations, this partnership gives us the flexibility and infrastructure to continue evolving how we engage with a growing global audience.”
Watch the full episode of Cheeky Pint featuring Toto Wolff and John Collison.

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened (2026-10-21).** Stripe announced a multiyear global partnership with the Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS F1 Team, taking the title of **Official Global Payments Partner** and Official Team Partner. Beyond branding, Stripe will apply its technology across the team's global commercial operations — merchandise/retail, experiences, partner relationships, international multi-currency transactions. Branding debuts on the cars from the first race of the 2027 season; the first product deployments launch at key races in the remainder of 2026, starting with the team's retail experience. The deal was announced alongside Team Principal/CEO Toto Wolff appearing on Stripe cofounder John Collison's "Cheeky Pint" podcast. Financial terms were not disclosed.

**Why it matters / Stripe scale.** This is a brand/distribution play by one of the largest private fintechs. Stripe reported **~$1.9T total payment volume in CY2025** (up ~34%, ≈1.6% of global GDP) across 5M+ businesses, and was valued at **$159B** in a Feb 2026 employee tender offer (up ~74% from ~$91.5B a year earlier); net revenue was reported ~$6.9B for 2025. Against that scale, an F1 sponsorship is a global-visibility lever more than a commercially material contract — the operational payments mandate (currencies, merchandise, cross-border) is the substantive hook.

**Red-team questions**

1. Are financial terms disclosed? No — neither press release nor coverage states deal value or duration ("multiyear" only). Treat any dollar figure as unverified.
2. Is this primarily sponsorship or a real payments mandate? The release claims operational deployment (retail, cross-border, currencies) but the first concrete deliverable is just "retail experience" in late 2026 — scope of the actual payments mandate is thin on specifics.
3. Does "Official Global Payments Partner" conflict with Mercedes' existing Nubank/UBS finance partners? No — those are neobank/wealth-banking roles; Stripe is payments infrastructure. But the overlapping "finance super-team" framing risks reader confusion.
4. Is this a duplicate of the same-week Airwallex-McLaren item? No — different company (Stripe vs Airwallex), different team (Mercedes vs McLaren), different title ("Payments Partner" vs "AI Finance Partner"). Distinct event.
5. Is there precedent risk — did Stripe have a prior F1/Mercedes tie that makes this an extension, not a new deal? No prior found; appears to be a net-new relationship.
6. Could the $1.9T TPV / $159B valuation figures be stale or conflated? They are CY2025 / Feb-2026 tender numbers; directionally current as of Oct 2026 but pre-IPO and self-reported.
7. Does the car-branding timing (2027) vs product-launch timing (late 2026) create a headline that overstates immediacy? Yes — branding is 2027; readers may assume full go-live now.
8. Is there a revenue impact for Stripe? Immaterial relative to scale; this is brand/distribution, not a growth driver.
9. Any regulatory/IPO signaling angle? A high-visibility consumer-facing deal fits a pre-IPO brand-building narrative, but that's inference, not stated.
10. Source quality? Primary = stripe.com + mercedesamgf1.com newsrooms (both parties, promotional). Independent outlets (finews, Inside Sport) corroborate facts but add no financial detail.
11. Is "Stripe's first F1 deal" claim safe? No explicit claim made in note; avoid asserting "first ever" without confirmation.
12. Does the note risk implying Stripe displaces an incumbent payments provider for Mercedes? Not stated; Wolff framed it as "rethinking our approach in this sector" — implies a change but no named predecessor.
13. Geopolitical/currency exposure relevance? The cross-border/multi-currency rationale is real for a global sports entity but generic — don't overstate as a differentiator.
14. Could this be reversed/renegotiated like sponsorships often are? Multiyear deals in F1 are periodically restructured; "multiyear" is not a firm commitment signal.

**Importance: 3/5** — A marquee, globally visible partnership between a top-5 private fintech and a flagship F1 team, and a clean marker of the fintech land-grab in F1 (Revolut/Audi, Airwallex/McLaren, Nubank+UBS/Mercedes). But terms are undisclosed, it is brand/marketing rather than a product or financial-infrastructure shift, and commercially immaterial to Stripe's $1.9T/$159B scale. Signal value (sector trend) outweighs standalone deal impact.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Global payments infrastructure is a large, structurally two-sided market: Stripe alone processed $1.9T total payment volume in 2025 (+34% YoY), overtaking PayPal's ~$1.79T, with Adyen at ~€1.39T / ~$1.58T (per CNBC/Adyen FY25 update, as of 2026-02 / H2-25). At Stripe's single-digit-bps-to-low-percent blended take rate the processing layer is a scale game — fixed-cost infra amortised over volume, so TPV growth compounds margin. The market is oligopolistic at the top (Stripe / PayPal / Adyen / Visa+Mastercard rails underneath) but fragmented below, with high barriers (regulatory licensing, multi-market acquiring, fraud/risk models, network effects). "Why now" for THIS deal is not a payments-economics event but a brand/distribution one: fintechs are in an F1 sports-sponsorship land-grab — ~34 financial-company sponsors on the 2026 grid vs ~20 in 2025 (+70% in one season; finance = 74% of finance/crypto sponsors), chasing F1's ~1.6B global viewers, 65% under-35 (per fxcintel / fintechnews.ch, 2025–26). Stripe is a late entrant to a category where Visa (Cash App RB, renewed to 2030) and Revolut (Audi title sponsor, 2026) already sit.

**Competitive landscape.** Payments KPIs: TPV/GPV, blended take rate (bps), revenue growth vs volume growth (mix shift), attach of value-added products. Key players and basis of competition: Stripe (developer-first platform + rising "revenue suite" / treasury / issuing / stablecoins — product breadth), Adyen (single-stack unified commerce, enterprise, rising take rate 16.2→17.1bps H2 on Platforms/POS mix), PayPal (branded checkout + Braintree scale), Visa/Mastercard (rails + sponsorship muscle). Recent moves: Adyen FY25 net revenue ~€2.36bn +21% cc; Adyen acquiring Talon.One for €750M (2026-05); Stripe's 2025 annual letter ($1.9T, robustly profitable), $159B tender valuation (2026-02). Stripe's position on PAYMENTS: ahead on volume and growth; moat = network effects + developer switching costs + scale. Position on SPORTS MARKETING: catching up / niche — this is Mercedes-AMG's own treasury/merch/commercial-ops payments plus livery branding, i.e. a B2B flagship-logo deal, not consumer acquisition like Revolut/Cash App. (analysis)

**Comps & multiples.** Deal economics are undisclosed — sponsorship fee, term length, and any processing-volume commitment are all "no data" (de-PR: Stripe/Mercedes silent on $ and on whether Stripe handles ticket-price merch volume vs just branding). Valuation comp for Stripe itself: $159B tender valuation (2026-02) on ~$6.9B 2025 net revenue ⇒ P/Revenue = $159bn / $6.9bn ≈ 23x (round valuation, not public market cap — mark accordingly). Vs Adyen public: using ~€2.36bn FY25 net revenue, Adyen trades richly on net-revenue multiples typical of high-margin payments (exact live EV/Rev = [UNSOURCED], no free verified EV figure). 23x private revenue multiple is high but inside the "justified by growth" zone for a +34%-volume, profitable franchise (growth-multiple correlation) — flag as rich-but-not-unsupported, not an automatic red flag. Internal comps (base): [[Adyen tops revenue estimates despite US tariff exemption end]], [[Adyen agrees to acquire loyalty platform Talon.One for €750 million]], [[Stripe unveils 288 launches for AI economic infrastructure]], [[Stripe enables payments in ChatGPT with OpenAI]], [[Ramp in talks to raise $750 million at over $40 billion valuation]]. Distribution not computed (comparable sponsorship-fee figures unavailable) — qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
- Brand-spend without disclosed payback: a multiyear F1 deal is marketing/brand opex with no public cost or processing-volume commitment — if Stripe is only the team's internal/merch processor (small volume) the economics are negligible and the value is pure brand, which is hard to underwrite pre-IPO. Why: sponsorship ROI is unmeasurable; a late, crowded-grid entry risks diluted share-of-voice (34 finance sponsors) rather than the category ownership Visa/Revolut already bought.
- Me-too / strategic-gap buying: the deal's own framing ("absence from F1 feels like a strategic gap") suggests defensive, not accretive, spend. Why: category tipping-point dynamics push fees up while marginal brand lift falls — classic late-mover overpay risk.
- Commercial dependence on counterparty performance: value is tied to Mercedes-AMG's on-track/commercial trajectory, which Stripe doesn't control. Why: a team slump compresses the audience/association Stripe is paying for.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Reads as a pre-IPO brand play: Stripe buying premium, global, under-35 visibility ahead of a widely-expected 2026/27 listing, planting a flag in the one high-signal category where Visa and Revolut already sit. Falsifiable thesis: if Stripe later discloses it is Mercedes-AMG's acquiring/treasury/issuing provider (real TPV + product attach), this is infra land-grab, not just a logo; trigger = a follow-on product-deployment announcement at 2026 races (the PR pre-commits to "retail experience" first). What would make the thesis wrong: deal stays branding-only with no product footprint, confirming it's marketing spend with no payments-volume upside. (analysis)
