Revolut has backed the Competition and Markets Authority’s draft decision to force Apple to open its Near Field Communication (NFC) technology to third-party developers. For fintech firms, this move signals a potential end to the hardware monopoly on iOS devices, allowing non-Apple wallets to offer seamless contactless payments directly to UK consumers.

The Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) has issued a draft decision that would require Apple to grant third-party digital wallet providers access to the NFC hardware on iOS devices via Host Card Emulation (HCE) functionality. Currently, Apple maintains exclusive control over the NFC chip for mobile payments, effectively mandating the use of Apple Pay for contactless transactions on iPhones. This regulatory shift aims to dismantle the technical barriers that have historically prevented fintech companies from offering their own integrated tap-to-pay solutions on the iOS platform.

Revolut has publicly supported this direction, viewing it as a necessary step for fostering genuine competition within the UK’s mobile payments ecosystem. The company argues that opening this infrastructure is vital for "payments sovereignty" and will allow domestic fintechs to compete on an equal footing with global Big Tech entities. The CMA’s proposal follows similar regulatory pressures in other jurisdictions, most notably the European Union, where Apple recently agreed to open NFC access to settle antitrust investigations. The UK consultation process will determine the final framework, which Revolut intends to engage with to ensure the resulting rules effectively lower the barriers to entry for third-party payment services.

> "Revolut strongly welcomes the CMA’s decision to open NFC access via HCE functionality. Opening up this infrastructure is a critical step towards driving genuine competition and helping the UK’s payments sovereignty. The CMA’s direction creates a real opportunity for UK fintechs to compete on an equal footing with Big Tech, and we look forward to engaging with the CMA during the consultation to ensure the final framework delivers real competition for UK consumers."

David Tirado, Chief Commercial Officer at Revolut.

Revolut is one of the most prominent names in the global fintech sector, having grown from a digital-only banking alternative into a comprehensive financial "super-app." Headquartered in London, the company has expanded its footprint significantly, now boasting over 250 mentions in FF News coverage. Its product suite has evolved to include international transfers, stock trading, and cryptocurrency services, positioning it as a primary challenger to traditional retail banks. David Tirado serves as a Partner Chief Commercial Executive at the firm.

Apple, meanwhile, remains a dominant force in the global technology and payments landscape. Through its iOS ecosystem and the Apple Pay service, the company has established a massive user base for mobile transactions. While Apple has historically cited security and user experience as reasons for restricting NFC access to its own proprietary wallet, it has faced increasing scrutiny from regulators worldwide over anti-competitive practices. With over 100 stories in the FF News archive, Apple’s influence on the fintech sector is undeniable, particularly as it continues to expand its financial services offerings into new markets.

FF News has tracked Revolut’s aggressive expansion and diversification across multiple sectors. Recently, the company moved beyond pure finance with the launch of Revolut Disrupts German Telecom Market with Launch of Revolut Mobile, offering data plans to its German customer base. The firm has also focused on loyalty and social impact, as seen in its Revolut and Eurostar Partner to Turn RevPoints into European Rail Travel initiative and its work to Revolut Partners with International Rescue Committee to Enable Global Humanitarian Donations. Meanwhile, Apple continues to push its payment rails into new territories, recently making headlines when Apple Pay Launches in India: Axis Bank Customers Gain Secure Contactless Payments.

This move by the CMA represents a significant challenge to the "walled garden" strategy that has protected Apple’s payment margins for years. If the draft decision becomes final, the competitive pressure on Apple Pay will intensify overnight. Fintechs will no longer be mere participants in an Apple-controlled ecosystem; they will be direct competitors for the "default wallet" slot on millions of devices. This raises a critical question for the industry: can third-party wallets offer a user experience smooth enough to lure consumers away from the convenience of a native system? For the UK, this is a test of whether regulatory intervention can truly level the playing field against entrenched Big Tech dominance.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Revolut issued a supportive statement backing the CMA's **draft decision** (published 20 Oct 2026, for consultation to 17 Nov 2026) that would require Apple to open iPhone NFC hardware to third-party wallets via **Host Card Emulation (HCE)**. De-PR'd:
- **This is a PR reaction to a proposal, not an event in itself.** Nothing is mandated, filed or live. The ffnews framing ("Revolut has backed... signals a potential end to the hardware monopoly") inflates a consultation-stage comment into a milestone.
- **The underlying CMA ask is a FEE fight, not "access".** Apple already opened a *paid* third-party NFC path in the UK in 2024 — but the CMA notes **no rival wallet product launched** through it, i.e. it was commercially unworkable. The CMA wants fees "substantially lower" but "stopped short of proposing a fixed price" (the loophole Apple will litigate), plus support for both major NFC standards (SE + HCE).
- **Revolut's "payments sovereignty" framing is lobbying, not substance.** It signals intent to "engage with the CMA during the consultation" — i.e. Revolut has no live iOS tap-to-pay wallet to ship; access ≠ adoption.
- **Why structured this way:** fintechs amplify regulator-friendly PR during a consultation window precisely to shape the final FRAND fee. The real battleground is the unpriced fee, which the Revolut PR conveniently glosses.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Fellow beneficiaries welcoming the proposal:** PayPal, Monzo, Klarna, UK banks; industry bodies Innovate Finance (Adam Jackson) and UK Finance. Revolut is one voice in a uniform fintech chorus, not a distinct actor.
- **EU prior art (the template):** under the 2024 DMA commitments Apple opened NFC/HCE **free** and default-wallet choice — and real wallets launched: Vipps (Dec 2024, with Visa), Curve Pay on iOS across the EEA (May 2025), PayPal, Klarna, BBVA (per EC IP/24/3706; techcrunch 2024-07-11; appleinsider 2026-10-20). The UK CMA itself says the EU concessions "don't go far enough."
- **Android:** the open-NFC counterfactual the whole case leans on.
- **Why the lay of the land is this way:** Apple's Secure-Element control is a single global chokepoint; once the EU forced it open for free, every regulator inherited a ready template and Apple's "security" defence weakened. Revolut's leverage is regulatory tailwind, not a product edge.

## [2] Company history / fit
Revolut is a London-HQ'd ~$75bn-valuation super-app (banking, FX, trading, crypto) and a serial public backer of pro-competition payments regulation in its home market. Backing the CMA fits its standing playbook of positioning as the challenger-champion vs Big Tech. ## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** UK mobile-wallet access is being re-regulated: on 2026-10-20 the CMA opened a consultation (closing 2026-11-17) on a conduct requirement forcing Apple to grant third-party developers NFC access on iOS via Host Card Emulation (HCE) and secure-element (SE) technology, "on fair, reasonable, transparent and non-discriminatory terms" (per CMA/GOV.UK, as of 2026-10-20). This rests on Apple's Strategic Market Status (SMS) designation for its mobile platform under the UK digital-markets regime — a 5-year status with fines up to 10% of global turnover for non-compliance (per CMA, via Global Banking & Finance). Market structure is a classic incumbent-gated bottleneck: Apple Wallet is used by ~50% of UK adults and 62% of UK adults use a digital wallet overall (per CMA, as of 2026); contactless was 39% of all UK payments in 2025 vs ~3% a decade earlier (UK Finance via CMA). The value is captured at the hardware/OS gate — Apple takes 0.15% on credit-card transactions routed through Apple Pay, the fee UK lenders have specifically challenged (per CMA, via Innovation Village). Why now: the EU already forced Apple to open HCE NFC free-of-charge in July 2024 to settle an antitrust probe (per EU Commission, 2024-07-11); the CMA explicitly notes that in the UK, unlike the EU, "not a single firm has launched a wallet-type product to compete with Apple's" — regulatory copy-paste with a fee-reduction twist.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for wallet/contactless: wallet penetration/active users, tap-to-pay transaction share, per-transaction fee (the 0.15% toll), and for issuers attach/default-wallet share. Basis of competition today is distribution + OS control, not product — Apple's moat is the pre-installed default-wallet slot and SE monopoly, i.e. switching costs + platform network effects, not a better UX. Key players: incumbent Apple (Apple Pay/Wallet); challengers that would benefit = Revolut, banks and fintechs wanting own tap-to-pay (recent UK wallet moves: [[Klarna launches debit card and digital wallet in UK]] (2025-10); EU-side account-to-account rail [[Wero to launch in Luxembourg with Payconiq and EPI]]). Recent regulatory moves with dates: EU opening 2024-07-11; UK CMA SMS designation + 2026-10-20 consultation; parallel UK private litigation [[Apple faces UK legal battle over iPhone tap-to-pay rules]]. Revolut's position here: vocal policy backer, not a shipped product — it "welcomes" the decision and will "engage with the consultation" (David Tirado, CCO). It is a beneficiary/lobbyist, not yet a live NFC-wallet operator; its real moat is its 68.3m-customer super-app distribution that could default-load a Revolut wallet once the gate opens (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** Protagonist Revolut is private; the news carries no deal economics, so this is a positioning comp, not a transaction comp. Latest filed primary (Revolut Group Holdings 2025 annual report, FY ended 2025-12-31): group revenue £4.5bn (+46%), profit before tax £1.7bn (+57%), 5th straight profitable year; retail customers 68.3m (+30%), business 767k, customer balances up 66%. **Risk flags.**
1. **Terms-not-economics risk (de-PR):** the CMA decision is a *draft* consultation to 2026-11-17, not a live mandate; Apple can litigate/delay (it is already in UK court, [[Apple faces UK legal battle over iPhone tap-to-pay rules]]) and the "substantially lower" fee is undefined — whoever wins the fee-setting captures the margin. For Revolut this is upside optionality, not booked value.
2. **Adoption, not access, is the bottleneck:** EU access has existed since mid-2024 yet the CMA itself notes near-zero competing-wallet launches — opening the NFC chip does not dislodge the default-wallet habit; second-order effect is that Revolut could spend to win the slot and still see Apple Pay keep most taps.
3. **Dependence on someone else's rails:** any Revolut tap-to-pay still runs on Apple's HCE/SE and iOS gatekeeping; Apple retains the ability to re-price or technically throttle access, so Revolut's payments-sovereignty narrative sits on an incumbent's goodwill + regulatory enforcement.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a regulatory re-opening of a gated market, not a re-rating event for Revolut — it marginally de-risks Revolut's UK payments optionality and strengthens its policy narrative ahead of a mooted $150–200bn IPO (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if the CMA finalises the HCE/SE mandate with a materially sub-0.15% fee by H1-2027, expect ≥1 UK fintech (plausibly Revolut) to ship a native iOS tap-to-pay within ~12 months; what would make it wrong — the EU precedent, where open access produced no competing wallet, repeating in the UK. Watch: final CMA decision post-2026-11-17 and the actual fee number.
