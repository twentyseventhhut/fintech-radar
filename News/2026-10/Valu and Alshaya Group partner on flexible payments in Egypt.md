Valu and Alshaya Group partner to expand flexible payment solutions across leading retail brands in Egypt
Aims to expand access to flexible payment solutions across participating Alshaya retail brands in Egypt
 Cairo:  Valu, the leading universal financial technology powerhouse in the Middle East and North Africa (MENA), announced a strategic partnership with Alshaya Group, one of the world’s leading franchise operators, to expand access to flexible payment solutions across participating Alshaya retail brands in Egypt.
The partnership brings together Valu’s financial technology capabilities and growing merchant ecosystem with Alshaya Group’s extensive retail footprint, creating a more seamless and flexible shopping experience for customers across some of the world’s most recognized retail brands. Participating brands will include H&M, Bath&Body Works, American Eagle, and Victoria’s Secret.
Through the partnership, customers will be able to use Valu’s flexible payment solutions when shopping across participating Alshaya brands, giving them greater purchasing power and the ability to manage purchases through convenient, interest-free payment plans. The offering will be available through both online and in-store channels, further supporting a seamless omnichannel customer journey.
The collaboration comes at a time when consumer expectations are evolving toward greater flexibility, convenience, and digitally enabled payment experiences. By embedding flexible financial solutions within everyday retail journeys, the partnership supports the broader shift toward digital payments and greater accessibility to alternative payment solutions in Egypt.
 Habiba Naguib, Chief Market Expansion and Strategy Officer of Valu , said:   “We are pleased to partner with Alshaya Group, bringing together two platforms with a shared focus on delivering greater value and convenience to consumers. This collaboration represents an important step in expanding Valu’s presence across high-impact retail touchpoints and strengthening our merchant ecosystem. By integrating flexible financial solutions into the shopping journeys of leading brands, we are not only enhancing the customer experience but also contributing to the continued evolution of Egypt’s digital payments and retail landscape.”
 Francois Orhan, Vice President, Loyalty, Payments & Data at Alshaya Group , said: “At Alshaya, we are continually looking for ways to enhance the customer experience and make shopping across our brands more convenient, accessible and relevant to the evolving needs of our customers. Our partnership with Valu brings greater flexibility to the way customers in Egypt can shop and pay, both in-store and online. By combining our portfolio of leading international brands with Valu’s innovative payment solutions, we are creating a more seamless omnichannel experience and giving customers greater choice in how they manage their purchases.”
The collaboration reinforces Valu’s role in connecting consumers, merchants, and financial solutions within an increasingly integrated digital ecosystem, while enabling Alshaya Group’s brands to meet evolving consumer expectations by offering greater payment flexibility and convenience.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Valu (U Consumer Finance S.A.E., EGX: VALU.CA — Egypt's listed BNPL/consumer-finance leader, backed by EFG Holding with a ~3.95% Amazon stake) signed a **merchant partnership** with **Alshaya Group** (Kuwaiti family-owned franchise operator, est. 1890; 50+ brands, 3,500+ stores across MENA/Türkiye/Europe) to offer **interest-free installment ("flexible payment") plans** at participating Alshaya brands **in Egypt** — named: H&M, Bath & Body Works, American Eagle, Victoria's Secret. Online + in-store.

De-PR'd: this is a **merchant-acquisition / distribution deal**, not a capital event, JV, or new product. No deal value, no commercial terms (take rate, subsidy split, exclusivity), no number of stores, no go-live GMV target, no launch date beyond "announced" are disclosed. Starbucks — Alshaya's flagship brand — is conspicuously **not** on the list, so this is a fashion/beauty-skewed subset, not Alshaya's full Egypt estate. "Interest-free" is the standard BNPL frame: in Valu's model the 0% is set by merchant agreement (merchant subsidy), and merchants are paid 100% upfront while Valu carries the credit/collections risk. **Why framed this way:** classic BNPL land-grab PR — add a marquee logo wall (H&M/Victoria's Secret) to the merchant ecosystem for customer-acquisition optics; the economics live in the undisclosed merchant discount rate, not in the headline.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Egypt BNPL market**: ~USD 1.26–1.67bn (2025), ~23% CAGR to ~USD 4.7bn by 2030 (ResearchAndMarkets/Nexdigm). Led by **valU and Shahry**, with **MNT-Halan** (launched BNPL into its super-app), plus Sympl ("save now, pay later"), Khazna, Blnk, Fawry, Paymob as channel. Valu is the scale/listed leader (9,000+ stores, 955k active customers H1 2026).
- **Merchant land-grab is the live competitive game**: compare same-month [[MoneyHash and Tabby simplify flexible payments in UAE and KSA]] (Tabby expanding merchant reach via API) — the Gulf analog of exactly this move. Valu's own channel plays: [[valU launches digital onboarding on UAE marketplace noon]], [[Egypt's ValU launches Shop'IT marketplace with cash back]].
- **Position**: parity/incremental. Signing Alshaya is a quality logo win, but every Egyptian BNPL is racing to lock the same large multi-brand retailers; none disclosed exclusivity, so Alshaya can (and likely will) also plug Shahry/MNT-Halan at checkout. **Why it looks this way:** BNPL merchant deals are rarely exclusive → a logo on Valu's page is a distribution footnote, not a moat; the second-order question is whether Valu's balance sheet/funding cost (listed + Amazon + EFG + bank credit lines — see [[Valu secures $64m credit line from National Bank of Egypt]]) lets it out-subsidize 0% offers longer than venture-funded rivals.

## [2] Company history / fit
Trajectory: EGX listing June 2025 ([[Egypt BNPL firm ValU goes public on Egyptian Stock Exchange]]) with Amazon exercising its EFG-option for ~3.95% ([[Amazon exercises option to acquire stake in ValU]]); H1 2025 revenue +94% YoY to ~$53.8m ([[Egypt's Valu reports 94% revenue growth in H1 2025]]); H1 2026 (per EFG/Zawya): **GMV EGP 14.6bn (+38% YoY), transactions 5.2m (+45%), 955k active customers, gross revenue EGP 3.2bn (+29%), net income EGP 486m (+43%)**. Regional push: Jordan license + launch ([[Valu expands to Jordan with $7 million]], [[Central Bank of Jordan grants ValU consumer financing license]], [[Valu Jordan and HyperPay partner on BNPL in Jordan]]); Egypt licence expansion ([[Egypt's FRA approves Valu and five firms for fintech activities]], [[Egypt's BNPL provider Valu secures SME financing license]]); vertical plays ([[Valu and ElTawkeel.com launch digital car platform with auto financing]]). **Fit:** yes, textbook. A listed consumer lender's job is to keep GMV compounding → it does so by stapling itself to every high-frequency retail checkout. Alshaya (fashion/beauty, repeat-purchase, higher ticket) is a logical add to a merchant base historically weighted to electronics/appliances/furniture. **Why:** as a listed name Valu needs visible GMV/transaction growth to defend its multiple, and merchant breadth is the cheapest growth lever vs. launching new product lines.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Novelty: **low**. This is a BNPL operator adding a (large, reputable) merchant group — a routine distribution event, not a new capability, rail, or market. Nothing here that Valu, Tabby, Tamara, MNT-Halan haven't done dozens of times. Traction: **unproven / announcement-only** — zero disclosed metrics for this deal (no GMV, no store count, no attach rate, no launch confirmation beyond PR). **Why the value-add is thin:** interest-free is funded by the merchant discount rate, so the real margin sits in (a) the undisclosed MDR Alshaya concedes and (b) whatever interest Valu earns on longer tenors customers roll into — neither disclosed. Without exclusivity, Valu doesn't capture the merchant relationship; it's one of several checkout options → it captures a slice of transaction margin, not the customer. The durable asset Valu is building is the **credit/underwriting + funding stack** (listed balance sheet, bank lines, Amazon halo), not any single retail logo.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Egypt BNPL structurally tailwinded: thin card/credit penetration, young population, e-commerce growth, high inflation making installments attractive. But the **regulatory backdrop tightened sharply in 2026**: Egypt's FRA issued its first comprehensive consumer-finance rulebook, mandated real-time/event-by-event credit-data linkage for non-bank lenders, and has pursued enforcement (suspension + criminal referral over a data breach at one firm). **Why it matters (second order):** tighter disclosure + real-time credit reporting raises compliance cost and curbs the over-lending that inflates GMV → favors scaled, funded, listed players like Valu over thin-capital rivals (consolidation tailwind), but also caps the "growth at any cost" GMV optics that support the equity story. Watch for: whether the deal goes live and discloses any attach/GMV numbers; whether it's extended to Starbucks/food (higher-frequency, lower-ticket — different economics); EGX reaction (likely negligible — not a material event).

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Egypt BNPL/consumer-finance is a small-but-fast market: ~US$1.37bn GMV in 2025, grown at a 38.4% CAGR 2022–25, with forecasts of ~US$1.6–1.74bn in 2026 and ~US$4.16bn by 2031 (19.0% CAGR 2026–31) — per ResearchAndMarkets/GlobeNewswire, as of Feb 2026. Structure: moderately fragmented, named leaders are valU, Shahry, Fawry and PayMob; Valu itself claims a 21% market share as of May 2026 (per Valu/EFG H1 2026 release). Entry barriers are regulatory (FRA fintech/BNPL licensing) and capital/funding cost — lenders must fund the receivable book. **Why now:** two reinforcing drivers — (1) a structurally under-banked, young, e-commerce-adopting consumer base pulling toward installment credit; (2) merchant-side demand for omnichannel conversion tools, which is exactly what the Alshaya tie-up (H&M, Victoria's Secret, Bath&Body Works, American Eagle) represents. Second-order: embedding BNPL into blue-chip international-brand checkouts (not just electronics/appliances) moves Valu up the discretionary-fashion spend curve and broadens its merchant moat.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: GMV/transaction volume, take rate, active customers, transaction frequency, and — critically for a balance-sheet lender — NPL ratio and funding cost. Key players & basis of competition: in Egypt valU vs Shahry/Fawry/PayMob (distribution + merchant network); regionally the reference points are KSA/UAE BNPL giants Tabby and Tamara, which compete on scale and capital, not (yet) directly in Egypt. Recent peer moves: Tabby hit a US$4.5bn secondary-sale valuation (2025-10-29); Tamara secured UAE Central Bank approval (2025-10-21). Valu's own recent moves: IPO on EGX (Jun 2025), Amazon stake, Jordan launch under a CBJ specialized-finance licence (May 2026), EGP 3bn/$64m NBE credit line (Jan 2026), FRA SME-financing licence (May 2026). **Protagonist position:** domestic category leader scaling into adjacent geographies and a broader "financial powerhouse" product set (prepaid/co-branded Visa cards, Valu Invest, Valu Business), ahead of local peers on disclosed scale but a fraction of the Gulf leaders' size (analysis). Moat: merchant network (9,000+ stores) + distribution + EGX-listed access to capital; the Alshaya deal deepens the merchant/network leg.

**Comps & multiples.** Valu is EGX-listed, so a market multiple is computable. Market cap ~EGP 22.1bn (price ~EGP 10.51, per TradingView/stockanalysis, late-Sep/early-Oct 2026). H1 2026 gross revenue EGP 3.2bn → annualized run-rate ~EGP 6.4bn: **P/S ≈ 22.1bn / 6.4bn ≈ 3.5x** on annualized gross revenue (analysis; H1 annualization, not a reported FY figure — treat as indicative). P/E on annualized H1 net income (EGP 486mn × 2 = EGP 972mn): **≈ 22.1bn / 0.97bn ≈ 22.8x** (analysis). These sit mid-range for a profitable hyper-grower (GMV +38%, net income +43% YoY). Internal comps: [[Gulf Startup Tabby nabs $4.5 billion valuation in secondary sale]] (private, US$4.5bn round valuation — scale benchmark, not market cap); [[Tamara secures UAE Central Bank regulatory approval]]; [[Egypt BNPL firm ValU goes public on Egyptian Stock Exchange]] (IPO ref ~$0.015/share); [[Egypt's Valu reports 94% revenue growth in H1 2025]] (H1'25 rev $53.8m, +94% — note 2026 growth has decelerated to +29% on a larger base); [[Valu secures $64m credit line from National Bank of Egypt]]. EV/EBITDA, clean EV and forward consensus: no free source → [UNSOURCED]. In-line-to-slightly-rich vs the growth rate; not an outlier.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Funding-cost / rate squeeze (why):** Valu is a balance-sheet lender funding BNPL receivables; with CBE lending rate at 20% and inflation ~14.5% (Sep 2026), the spread between its cost of funds and "interest-free" consumer plans must be recovered via merchant discount/fees — compressing margins if rates stay high, and the revenue multiple assumes continued profitable growth.
2. **FX / macro translation (why):** EGP has weakened to ~52.3/US$; USD-reported growth flatters/distorts vs EGP. The reported H1 deceleration (gross revenue +29% vs +94% a year earlier) signals growth normalizing on a bigger base — second-order risk to the ~3.5x P/S if the market has priced hyper-growth.
3. **Deal economics undisclosed / "announced vs live" (why):** the Alshaya release is pure PR — no exclusivity, no ticket size, no take rate, no fraud/credit-liability split, no go-live metrics. One merchant partnership (even blue-chip) is not yet GMV; discretionary fashion also carries higher default and return-fraud risk than appliances/electronics.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Incrementally positive: Valu is pushing BNPL from big-ticket durables into everyday international-brand fashion, lengthening its lead on merchant breadth and omnichannel reach in Egypt (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if the Alshaya and similar brand tie-ups convert, Egypt fashion/discretionary BNPL re-rates Valu's GMV mix upward and supports the current multiple. **Watch/trigger:** next quarterly GMV split by category and active-customer adds; NPL ratio drift above the current 1.16% would break the thesis (fashion-mix credit quality), as would further growth deceleration without margin offset.
