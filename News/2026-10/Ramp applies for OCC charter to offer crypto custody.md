# Context-enrichment: Ramp applies for OCC charter to offer crypto custody
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On **2026-10-16** Ramp — the US corporate spend/card fintech, NOT the EU on/off-ramp "Ramp Network" (MiCAR, [[Ramp Network receives EU-wide MiCAR authorisation]]) — filed an application with the **OCC** to charter **Ramp National Trust Bank**, a **non-depository, limited-purpose national trust bank**. Publicly surfaced 2026-10-19. If approved, it would offer **digital-asset custody** and **fiduciary investment management**. Hard limits (OCC trust template): **no deposit-taking, no commercial lending, no FDIC insurance**.

Status is **application only** — no conditional/final approval, no disclosed timeline, no capital/condition disclosures. This is a filing, not a live capability.

**+ Why structured this way / what it reveals:** The custody-only, no-deposit, no-lend contour is the exact shape the OCC has been approving under Comptroller Gould's framework — it keeps Ramp clear of FDIC/Fed-master-account/full-prudential supervision while gaining a **federal** wrapper for holding client crypto + funds. The real "why": Ramp already holds **>$5bn in client funds** across checking/investment/treasury products for **~85,000 corporate customers**, but had to **rent** regulated custody/safekeeping from third parties. Owning the trust layer lets it keep those balances in-house, capture custody + float/reserve economics, and extend treasury management from software/credit into institutional asset custody. De-PR'd: this is a **treasury/custody land-grab anchored to an existing $5bn balance pool**, framed as "crypto custody" to ride the digital-asset narrative — the fiduciary-investment-management piece (managing client fiat) is arguably the bigger near-term prize than crypto.

## [1] Competitors / peers
This is now a **crowded, well-trodden OCC trust-charter path**, which materially lowers the novelty. Internal corpus confirms the cluster, nearly all custody-only / no-deposit:
- **Anchorage Digital** — original OCC-chartered crypto trust bank (2021); note it just [[Anchorage Digital cuts 17% of staff]] → charter ≠ profitability.
- **Circle** — final approval 2026-07-10 (First National Digital Currency Bank / Circle National Trust), the furthest-along comp ([[Circle applies for US national trust bank charter]], [[Circle wins final OCC approval for national trust bank]]).
- **Dec-2025 cohort** — Ripple/Circle/BitGo/Fidelity/Paxos conditional ([[OCC grants conditional trust bank approval to five crypto firms]]).
- **Coinbase** conditional 2026-04 ([[Coinbase wins conditional approval for national trust charter]]); **Crypto.com** ([[Crypto.com files for OCC National Trust Bank charter]]); **Kraken/Payward** ([[Kraken parent Payward applies for OCC national trust charter]]); **Zerohash** ([[Zerohash applies for US National Trust Bank charter]]); **Agora** conditional Sep-2026 ([[Agora secures conditional OCC approval for trust bank]]).
- **Closest profile peers** (payments/treasury software, not exchanges): [[Modern Treasury applies to establish national trust bank]] (2026-10-05) and [[Rain seeks US national trust bank charter]] (2026-10-05) — same custody-only template, filed ~10 days before Ramp.

**Position: late follower.** Ramp applies ~10–12 months after the first wave secured conditional/final approvals; Circle is already fully operational. Not a first-mover, not early.

**+ Why the landscape is this way / second-order:** GENIUS Act (2025) + a crypto-friendly OCC turned the trust charter into the **default institutional wrapper** for digital-asset custody. Once Circle/Coinbase got federal legitimacy, every player with client balances faced rent-or-own. Ramp's differentiator vs the exchange crowd: it is not crypto-native — it brings a **captive $5bn balance base and 85,000 enterprise relationships** that most chartered peers lack. The charter's signaling value is already decaying (10+ firms ahead); the game reverts to **who owns the distribution/client flow** — where Ramp is genuinely strong.

## [2] Company history / fit
Trajectory fits cleanly with an aggressive scale-up moving up the stack:
- Founded 2019; corporate cards → Bill Pay → Treasury (launched Jan 2025, $1.5bn+ AUM) → procurement → AI agents.
- Oct 2026: **$2bn annualized revenue** ([[Ramp annualized revenue crosses $2 billion]]) and a **$60bn pre-money** raise of ~$1.85bn ([[Ramp raises $1.85bn at $60bn pre-money valuation]]) — most valuable private corporate-spend fintech by far.
- Prior crypto/AI posture: self-custody wallet rollout via the EU arm ([[Ramp Network rolls out multichain self-custody wallet]]); AI-native pivot ([[Analysis Coinbase and Ramp restructure as AI-native finance firms]]).
- Now (2026-10): the trust-charter filing.

**+ Why Ramp acts this way — structural pressure:** Ramp's core card business rests on commodity-prone **interchange** economics; to justify a ~40x-ARR, $60bn mark it must keep layering higher-quality, licence-gated revenue. It already warehouses **>$5bn of client treasury/checking balances** on which it earns float — but that float sits behind partner banks/custodians, who take a cut and own the regulated relationship. A trust charter lets Ramp **capture the custody/float economics directly** and deepen lock-in (clients custodying assets are far stickier than those routing payments). It is the logical capstone of the "finance OS" expansion, de-PR'd: the charter protects and monetizes a balance pool Ramp already controls.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: low.** The charter *type* is routine (10+ firms ahead; Circle fully approved). What's differentiated is the **asset base**: unlike exchange applicants, Ramp brings an existing $5bn in corporate funds and 85,000 enterprise relationships — so if approved it could reach meaningful custody AUC faster than a cold-start crypto bank. The "crypto custody" headline overstates it; fiduciary management of **fiat** client balances may matter more.

**Traction: zero today.** This is an **application**, not approval, not a live product. No AUC, no revenue tied to it. Even approved peers show a 6–12+ month gap filing→operating (Circle: ~7 months conditional→final).

**+ Why value-add is real-or-not, deeper:** If approved, Ramp's margin shifts from thin interchange toward **custody + float/reserve economics** — higher-quality, licence-protected, and a stickiness moat. BUT the counter: with Circle/Coinbase/Paxos/BitGo/Anchorage et al. already chartered, **custody is commoditizing even as it federalizes** — the charter is becoming table stakes, not a moat. And in the agentic-payments/AI-finance stack Ramp is chasing, the rails (Visa/Mastercard) and now the custody licences are both getting crowded — so the question shifts from "does Ramp get a charter" to **"who captures the margin once everyone holds one"** — decided by distribution, where Ramp's 85k-customer base + $5bn balances are its one real edge.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Next: OCC review (conditional → final), typically multi-month, no disclosed timeline; a **public comment period** and the **ICBA v. OCC** lawsuit (filed 2026-10-02, D.D.C. No. 1:2026cv03441) hang over the whole pathway — ICBA seeks to vacate the March-2026 rule/Interpretive Letter 1176 that lets trust banks do non-fiduciary (crypto) activity. So Ramp's bid lands in **open legal uncertainty**: the charter route itself could be enjoined.

**+ Why the market goes this way / second-order:** The federal trust charter is consolidating US digital-asset custody into a handful of OCC entities — a regulatory moat favoring scaled incumbents. Counterintuitive effect: as the charter becomes ubiquitous its signaling value decays, and the differentiator reverts to **owning the client flow** (Ramp's strength). Ramp-specific risks: (a) approval not guaranteed, slow, and legally contested; (b) running a regulated bank adds compliance/capital drag to a software P&L at the exact moment it is priced for ~40x-ARR growth; (c) it is late — rivals may already hold the institutional custody mandates Ramp wants.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Digital-asset custody is a modest-but-fast-growing niche: market sizing is fragmented and methodology-dependent — ~$3.5bn (crypto-custody provider) to ~$6bn (digital-asset custody) in 2026, growing >20% CAGR (per secondary market-research firms: The Business Research Company, SNS Insider, via WebSearch, as of 2026) — treat the absolute figures as soft, the direction (institutionalization) as the signal. The "why now" is regulatory: the OCC has reopened the national trust bank charter as a federal on-ramp for digital-asset firms. Five conditional approvals landed 2025-12-12 (Circle, Ripple, BitGo, Fidelity Digital Assets, Paxos), three more in 2026-02 (Protego, Stripe/Bridge, Crypto.com), with Circle reaching full approval 2026-07-10 (per Banking Dive / OCC releases, via WebSearch). Ramp's filing (~2026-10-16, for "Ramp National Trust Bank" — custody/fiduciary only, NO deposits or lending) is a late entrant into a now well-trodden path. Structure: the charter trend is a fintech-to-bank convergence where custody becomes a federally-regulated, licensed utility; barriers are regulatory (charter) + compliance/capital, not technology.

**Competitive landscape.** Custody economics run on AUC (assets under custody), custody/staking fee take-rate on AUC, and the recurring/predictable share of revenue. Incumbents are specialist custodians — Coinbase Custody, BitGo, Anchorage Digital (federally chartered since 2021), Fidelity Digital Assets, Fireblocks, Gemini. BitGo discloses $104bn AUC with custody+staking ~80% of revenue at ~$160–170m/yr (per CoinDesk/Yahoo at its 2026-01 IPO). Basis of competition: regulatory status + institutional trust + integration into existing rails, not price. Ramp's position is a NEW ENTRANT with zero disclosed crypto-custody AUC or product — the charter application is an option, not a live business `(analysis)`. Its moat here is NOT custody expertise but distribution: 85,000+ corporate finance teams on its spend-management platform (per Ramp disclosures via WebSearch) it could cross-sell treasury/stablecoin custody into — a moat adjacent to, not inside, the custody vertical.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Charter framework under legal attack.** The ICBA sued the OCC (reported 2026-10-05, per Consumer Finance Monitor) challenging its authority to grant these crypto trust charters — a live, systemic risk that could stall or unwind approvals for the entire cohort, Ramp included. Approval odds are NOT the clean rubber-stamp the 8 prior approvals suggest.
2. **Late + crowded entry.** Ramp files AFTER 8 peers already won conditional approval and BitGo/Anchorage already operate at scale ($104bn AUC). Competing on custody-as-utility against entrenched specialists with no disclosed AUC is an uphill, low-differentiation fight — the economics (thin ~2% price-to-AUC) reward scale Ramp doesn't have.
3. **Compliance/capital burden vs. strategic rationale.** A trust bank imports bank-grade compliance, capital and supervisory overhead onto a company whose $60bn valuation rests on software margins; if custody stays small, it's a margin drag with reputational/regulatory downside and little revenue upside `(analysis)`.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a strategic option on Ramp becoming a corporate-treasury + stablecoin-custody hub for its 85k finance teams, not a bet on winning standalone custody. Falsifiable thesis: if the charter clears AND Ramp ties custody to stablecoin/treasury flows inside its platform, it re-rates as infrastructure, not just spend software; it fails if the charter is the headline and no integrated product/AUC follows within ~12 months. Watch: the ICBA litigation outcome (binary for the whole cohort) and whether Ramp discloses any custody AUC or stablecoin-treasury product post-filing.
