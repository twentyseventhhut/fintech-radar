Mastercard Bets Shoppers Will Trust AI Agents One Purchase at a Time 

 
 
 
 Highlights 
 

 Consumers may give AI agents more purchasing authority after repeated transactions show that the technology can follow their preferences. 

 AI could recommend which payment credential to use based on benefits, offers, available credit and the consumer’s finances. 

 Merchants will need to make inventory, pricing and product information available for agents to evaluate in real time. 
 Watch more: The Agentic Experience With Mastercard’s Pablo Fourez 

Consumers won’t hand artificial intelligence agents unrestricted purchasing authority on day one, but repeated successful transactions could expand what they’re willing to delegate.
It’s a topic that now dominates agentic AI discussions. The first time Mastercard Chief Digital Officer Pablo Fourez lets an AI agent buy an airline ticket, he told PYMNTS CEO Karen Webster , he wants to check its work. If the agent gets the fare, refund terms and seat right, and keeps getting them right, he sees less reason to keep checking. A lot depends on the transaction itself, or the use case.
“There’s going to be a gradual consumer adoption,” Fourez said. “Initially, you may ask [the agent] to confirm the purchase before it executes it. And you might try that a couple of times. And then you might say, ‘Oh, this is actually working.’”
The amount of authority consumers grant an agent will also depend on what they’re buying.
Fourez used his own purchases to draw the boundaries. He said he likes Paul Smith striped socks and knows the product well enough that he would be comfortable letting an agent buy them. His weekly grocery order also contains many of the same products, allowing an agent to learn his preferred brands. It could take recipes he wants to cook, assemble the ingredients and order them.
“I might not immediately get them to select the wine,” he said. “I might want to have a point of view on that.”
Webster said she sees a similar progression toward delegation.
“I think it’ll be up to the consumer,” she said.
What begins as authority over the purchase could eventually extend to other personalization capabilities, including asking an agent to evaluate the offers and benefits available across different retailers and payment methods, enabling consumers to make more informed decisions on where and how they shop.

 The Merchant Readiness Imperative 
As consumers gradually grow more comfortable delegating decisions to AI agents, merchants will face the parallel challenge of preparing their products, experiences and payment infrastructure to keep up.
Fourez described agentic commerce as a new channel for commerce, drawing a comparison with the move from desktop to mobile. That transition forced merchants to redesign how consumers reached them and required payment providers to change how transactions worked on a phone.
Merchants will need to consider how agents discover what they sell, Fourez said. Catalogs, prices and current inventory must be available in a form that lets an agent find the product, evaluate the offer and present it to the shopper.
The consumer, merchant and payment credential are three parts of the framework that Fourez and Webster discussed. The fourth is the ecosystem connecting them.
That requires establishing who is involved in an agent-led transaction, Fourez said. The payments system needs to be able to connect the identity of the consumer with the agent acting for that person and the merchant receiving the order. The consumer needs confidence that the agent is carrying out the instruction given to it, while the merchant and financial providers need confidence in the transaction they’re being asked to fulfill and process.
Agentic commerce creates another interface. This time, the customer reaching the merchant or payment provider may be software acting under authority granted by a consumer.
The parameters of that authority will ultimately be the consumer’s decision. The infrastructure surrounding it will determine how far that authority can safely travel.

# Context-enrichment: Mastercard bets shoppers will trust AI purchasing agents
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
This is **not a product announcement** — it is a PYMNTS commentary/interview piece (conversation held **early September 2026**, published Oct 2026) with Mastercard Chief Digital Officer **Pablo Fourez**. The substance: a strategy thesis, not a launch.
- **Thesis:** consumers will NOT hand AI agents unrestricted purchasing authority on day one; trust is earned **"one purchase at a time"** — start with confirm-before-execute on low-stakes, repeat SKUs (Fourez's examples: Paul Smith socks, weekly grocery/recipe orders), withhold judgement items (wine). Delegation scope expands only after repeated correct executions.
- **Second claim:** agents could pick the optimal payment credential based on benefits/offers/available credit — i.e. Mastercard repositioning the card-choice decision into the agent layer.
- **Merchant imperative:** merchants must expose catalog, price and real-time inventory in agent-readable form (framed as desktop→mobile-scale transition); and an "ecosystem" must bind consumer identity ↔ agent ↔ merchant.
- **Why framed this way (red-team):** Mastercard's actual agentic product, **Agent Pay**, was already announced **29 Apr 2025**. By Sept 2026 the network story has shifted from "we launched rails" to "will consumers trust the rails" — a demand-side narrative. The gradualist "trust ladder" framing is also conveniently de-risking: it lets Mastercard claim the category is inevitable while setting expectations that mass adoption is slow, insulating against the gap between live pilots and real volume.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Agentic-commerce rails are a **three-way network land-grab**, all moving in 2025–2026:
- **Visa — Intelligent Commerce / Trusted Agent Protocol**; "Agentic Ready" program expanded to Canada, APAC, LatAm (see [[Visa expands Agentic Ready program to Canada]]); Visa also extended support for Stripe/Tempo's Machine Payments Protocol. Rough parity with Mastercard.
- **Stripe + OpenAI — Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP)** with Shared Payment Tokens (launched late 2025); this is the merchant/checkout-side de-facto standard powering ChatGPT Instant Checkout. PayPal wallet also went into ChatGPT ([[PayPal launches Agentic Commerce Services for AI checkout]]).
- **PayPal** — Agent Pay integrated into PayPal wallet ([[Mastercard and PayPal partner on agentic commerce]], [[PayPal integrates Mastercard Agent Pay into wallet]]).
- **Google AP2** (Agent Payments Protocol) — 60+ firms incl. Mastercard, Amex, PayPal, Coinbase; **Mastercard is a co-signer, not the owner**.
- **Why this matters (analysis):** the networks win the **tokenisation/trust layer** (Agentic Tokens vs Visa tokens), but the **intent/protocol layer** is being set by OpenAI/Stripe (ACP) and Google (AP2). Mastercard risks owning the plumbing while Big Tech owns the consumer interface and the standard — exactly the disintermediation risk this commentary quietly talks around.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Apr 2025:** Mastercard launches **Agent Pay** (launch partners Microsoft, IBM, Braintree); **Agentic Tokens** = extension of Mastercard Digital Enablement Service (MDES) binding a tokenised credential to a specific agent + merchant scope + consent policy (card number never exposed).
- **2025–2026:** phased rollout; live firsts — [[Mastercard and Rabobank complete Netherlands' first AI agent payment]] (via Priceless.com, passkeys + consent), [[PhotonPay completes first live agentic payment with Mastercard]] (HK/APAC), Europe first with Santander/PayOS.
- **Jun 2026:** **Agent Pay for Machines** (M2M), 30+ participants (Adyen, Checkout.com, Cloudflare, Coinbase, Stripe, Polygon, Ripple, Solana Foundation, Turnkey, Nevermined…).
- **Sep 2026:** new "trust and intelligence services" (identity/intent/behavioral/fraud signals) + a **no-code acceptance** framework so any Mastercard merchant can recognise certified agents.
- **Fit (analysis):** Mastercard is a commodity take-rate on rails; agentic commerce is a **defensive moat move** — if agents pick the credential, Mastercard must be inside the decision or risk being disintermediated by wallets/LLMs. The gradualism narrative is consistent with a network that monetises on volume and needs trust (not speed) to protect fraud economics.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty of THIS item: low.** No new product, partner, number or date — it restates the Agent Pay thesis via an executive interview. The concrete substance (Agent Pay, Agentic Tokens, merchant-readiness) all predates this by 5–17 months.
- **Real traction elsewhere (not in this piece):** multiple "firsts" are **pilots/single live transactions** (Rabobank, PhotonPay, Santander) — proof-of-concept, **not GMV/volume**. Industry (Forrester) frames B2C agentic payments as still early. No conversion or volume figures disclosed by anyone — the loud silence.
- **Who captures the margin (analysis):** Mastercard's value-add is real on the **trust/token layer** (fraud liability, credential security, no-code acceptance lowers merchant CAC). But if ACP/AP2 commoditise the intent layer and LLMs own the shopper, the network could be relegated to a tokenisation utility — margin shifts to whoever owns the agent relationship.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Expect continued pilot→scale messaging; Mastercard's Sept 2026 trust/intelligence services + no-code acceptance are the real near-term levers to convert the thesis into merchant coverage.
- **Regulatory/liability backdrop:** unresolved — who is liable when an agent buys wrong? Consent/mandate frameworks (AP2) are the battleground.
- **Counter-intuitive second-order (analysis):** the gradualist "trust one purchase at a time" story is as much expectation-management as strategy. If the networks over-promise agentic volume and consumers stay on confirm-before-execute for years, the category's near-term P&L impact is small — and the current multiple/hype on "agentic commerce" names may be ahead of monetisable reality.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Agentic commerce — AI agents transacting on a consumer's behalf — is an emerging channel bolted onto the existing card-network rails, not a new rail. Sizing is early and dispersed: per Morgan Stanley (via morganstanley.com, as of 2026), agentic shoppers could represent $190bn–$385bn of US e-commerce spend by 2030 (10–20% of the market); Bain (via bain.com, 2026) puts the US figure at $300bn–$500bn (15–25% of e-commerce). These are channel-mix estimates, not Mastercard revenue — "no data" on how much accrues to networks vs. model/checkout layers. Structure: payments is a consolidated duopoly at the network layer (Visa + Mastercard) with deep moats (two-sided network effects, regulatory licensing, global acceptance). The agentic layer above is fragmented and contested — model providers (OpenAI), checkout stacks (Stripe, PayPal, Shopify) and protocols (Visa TAP, Google AP2, OpenAI/Stripe ACP) all competing to own the agent↔merchant↔credential handshake. Why now: 2025–26 protocol land-grab — every major payments and AI player shipped an agentic framework in a ~12-month window (see internal comps), and Mastercard's pitch here is trust/adoption gradient (delegate socks before wine), i.e. volume follows repeat success, not a big-bang switch.

**Competitive landscape.** Network KPIs: GDV, cross-border volume (the highest-yield slice), switched transactions, net revenue growth, take rate. Reported (IR, primary): Q2 2026 net revenue +14% to $9.3bn (+12% currency-neutral), GDV +8% LC to $2.9tn, cross-border +12% LC, switched transactions +9%, adj. EPS $5.04 (EDGAR 8-K ma06302026, 2026-07; s25.q4cdn.com/.../2Q26-Earnings-Release.pdf). Q1 2026: net revenue +16% to $8.4bn (+12% CN), GDV +7% LC to $2.7tn, cross-border +13% LC, VAS +22% (EDGAR 8-K ma03312026). FY2025: net revenue $32.8bn (+16%), payment-network revenue $19.5bn, VAS $13.3bn, GDV +15% LC, cross-border +15% (10-K/ARS, 2026-04-27). Key players / basis of competition: Visa (framework — Trusted Agent Protocol, launched 2025-10-17), Mastercard (Agent Pay), PayPal (Agentic Commerce Services; integrates Mastercard Agent Pay into its wallet), Stripe + OpenAI (payments in ChatGPT), Google (AP2, with Affirm/Klarna) — competition is on protocol adoption, identity/trust and merchant reach, not price. Protagonist position: the networks (Mastercard + Visa) are positioned to ride agentic volume regardless of which agent wins, since agents still settle over card credentials — this is a commentary/positioning piece (Agent Pay already shipped in 2025), so "catching up vs. OpenAI/Stripe at the agent interface, entrenched at the credential layer" (analysis). Moat: network effects + tokenized-credential/identity assets (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** Public network duopoly, directly comparable (same model/scale/geo):
- Mastercard (MA): mkt cap ~$502bn (companiesmarketcap/macrotrends, Sep 2026); trailing P/E ~31.6x; fwd P/E ~24.6–28.0x (gurufocus/finbox/yahoo, 2026). On FY2025 net revenue $32.8bn: P/S ≈ $502bn / $32.8bn ≈ 15.3x.
- Visa (V): mkt cap ~$612–679bn; EV/Revenue ~14.4x on ~$43bn revenue; trailing P/E ~31.9x; fwd P/E ~24.0–26.4x (multiples.vc/yahoo, 2026).
Both rich vs. the broad EV/Rev ~0.5–20x range but in-line with each other and justified by mid-teens revenue growth + ~55%+ operating margins — a classic high-growth/high-margin pair, not an outlier to flag. Internal comps (base): [[Mastercard and PayPal partner on agentic commerce]], [[PayPal integrates Mastercard Agent Pay into wallet]], [[Visa launches Trusted Agent Protocol for AI commerce]], [[Stripe enables payments in ChatGPT with OpenAI]], [[Affirm to support Google's Agent Payments Protocol]] — these are product/partnership moves, no round valuations attached ("no data" on standalone agentic-commerce comps). EV/EBITDA, clean forward consensus → [UNSOURCED] beyond the fwd P/E above.

**Risk flags.**
1. Disintermediation at the agent interface — if an AI layer (OpenAI/Stripe ACP, Google AP2) owns credential selection and routing, it can commoditize the network's VAS and steer to the cheapest rail; the network keeps settlement but loses the high-margin "which card, which offer" decision Mastercard is pitching here (second-order: margin migrates up-stack to whoever owns the agent).
2. Fraud-liability / authority ambiguity — the piece is silent on who eats losses when an agent transacts under delegated authority (consumer, merchant, network, agent vendor); unresolved liability slows the exact repeat-trust adoption curve the thesis depends on.
3. Protocol fragmentation / standards risk — competing frameworks (TAP vs. AP2 vs. ACP vs. Agent Pay) may not interoperate; backing the losing standard strands merchant-onboarding investment.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Agentic commerce is additive volume over existing rails, not (yet) a re-rating event for the networks — a new channel analogous to desktop→mobile, where incumbents kept the toll but had to re-plumb (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if agent-originated transactions become a disclosed, growing KPI in Mastercard/Visa earnings within ~4–6 quarters, the networks are capturing agentic volume; trigger to watch — first quarter either network breaks out agentic/Agent Pay volume. Thesis breaks if agent platforms route around card credentials (stablecoins/A2A, e.g. Coinbase x402, bank-rail agentic pay) at scale, shrinking the networks' share of agent spend.

**Verdict (headline read).** BEAT (vs public consensus) · Q1 2026 (quarter ended 31 Mar 2026, reported 30 Apr 2026) net revenue $8.4bn (+16% GAAP / +12% currency-neutral; vs ~$8.26bn expected, ≈+$0.14bn / +1.7%) · adjusted diluted EPS $4.60 (+23% YoY; vs ~$4.41 expected, ≈+$0.19 / +4.3%) · drivers: cross-border +13% and value-added services +22%; operating leverage lifting margins · guidance: none given in the release. The result is a healthy beat, and management used the CEO statement to foreground "agentic commerce" — i.e. the print is the balance-sheet backing for the bet in this story.

**Key figures (with growth).**
- Net revenue: $8.4bn, +16% GAAP / +12% currency-neutral.
- Net income (GAAP): $3.9bn, +18%; GAAP diluted EPS $4.35, +21%.
- Adjusted net income: $4.1bn, +20%; adjusted diluted EPS $4.60, +23%.
- Operating margin: 58.4% (+1.2 ppt); adjusted operating margin 60.8% (+1.5 ppt) — operating leverage, not a one-off.
- Gross dollar volume (GDV): $2.7tn, +7% (local currency).
- Cross-border volume: +13% (local currency) — the highest-margin growth line.
- Switched transactions: +9%.
- Value-added services & solutions: +22% (+18% currency-neutral) — outgrowing the core network and the structural home for agentic/AI monetization.

**By segment / driver.** Growth is led by the two lines that matter most for the agentic thesis: cross-border (+13%, premium economics) and value-added services (+22%). VAS — cybersecurity, fraud, identity, data/analytics — is exactly the toolkit an agent-led transaction needs (identity binding of consumer↔agent↔merchant, fraud liability, credential selection), so its 22% growth is both a revenue engine today and the product surface Mastercard is extending into agentic commerce ("Mastercard Agent Pay" is featured on the company homepage in the note). GDV +7% is the mature core; the delta between GDV (+7%) and VAS (+22%) is the whole mix-shift story.

Momentum is strong and broad-based (+16% headline revenue, +23% adjusted EPS). **Thesis-flags.**
1. VAS +22% >> GDV +7% → the mix is shifting toward software/services → agentic commerce is a VAS extension, not a new cost center → second-order: Mastercard can monetize the agent layer (identity, fraud, credential orchestration) without needing new transaction volume to validate the bet. This is the single strongest financial support for the story's thesis.
2. 60.8% adjusted operating margin (+1.5 ppt) and $4.0bn/quarter buyback capacity → the agentic bet is being placed from a position of structural profitability → second-order: Mastercard can afford a multi-year, "gradual consumer adoption" ramp (exactly the slow trust-building Fourez describes in the note) without P&L pressure.
3. Cross-border +13% is the premium growth engine → agentic commerce (travel/airline tickets, the first use case Fourez cites) is cross-border-heavy → if agents capture travel booking, it flows into Mastercard's highest-margin line → second-order: strategic incentive to own the agent-identity/credential standard is financial, not just defensive.
4. De-PR / who's silent: the story is commentary (CDO interview conducted early Sept 2026) with zero adoption metrics — no agent-transaction volume, no take-rate, no revenue attributable to Agent Pay. The financials prove Mastercard *can fund* the bet; they do **not** yet prove agentic commerce *contributes* revenue. Treat the bet as funded and credible, but unproven on traction.
