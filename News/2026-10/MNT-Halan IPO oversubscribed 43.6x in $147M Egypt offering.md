MNT-Halan retail IPO oversubscribed 43.6x times on final day
 Arab Finance: The retail tranche of MNT-Halan ’s initial public offering (IPO) attracted orders for approximately 2.093 billion shares on its sixth and final subscription day, with demand reaching 43.6 times the 48 million shares allocated to retail investors.
Subscription requests totaled nearly 2.093 billion shares, implying a proportional allocation ratio of approximately 2.29% based on the shares available. Demand substantially exceeded the retail tranche, leaving investors competing for a limited allocation.
Retail shares are priced at EGP 24.50 each, with a minimum subscription of 100 shares. The tranche accounts for 15% of the total offering and 3% of the company’s share capital.
The overall offering comprises 320 million shares, representing a 20% stake and valued at approximately EGP 7.84 billion. The transaction consists entirely of a secondary sale of existing shares.
Trading on the Egyptian Exchange (EGX) is expected to begin on October 20 under the ticker HALN, according to Mounir Nakhla, the company’s founder and CEO.
MNT-Halan provides financial technology services, including digital financing solutions. The offering forms part of its listing process ahead of making its shares available for trading on the EGX.
It is worth mentioning that MNT-Halan’s private placement closed 11.2 times oversubscribed at an offer price of EGP 24.50 per share.

# Context-enrichment: MNT-Halan IPO oversubscribed 43.6x in $147M Egypt offering
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**FRESHNESS: fresh.** This is the **subscription-RESULT / book-close** step of the SAME offering whose *launch & pricing* was already enriched at [[MNT-Halan plans $150M IPO on Egyptian Exchange]] (s2e6d60b6, 6-Oct-2026, importance 4). Same price (EGP 24.5), same 320m shares (~20%), same ~$147–150m, same 20-Oct-2026 trading start under **HALN**. What is genuinely NEW here: the books have **closed oversubscribed** — retail **43.6x on the final (6th) day**, private tranche **11.2x (19.4x ex-cornerstone)**, combined ~**16x** / ~**EGP 125.9bn** of demand. So it is NOT stale (not a reprint of the launch), but it is a **lower-weight follow-on** of an already-deeply-enriched event: the terms/valuation/financials are unchanged; only the demand outcome is new. All structural analysis (valuation, multiples, carve-out, funding stack) lives in the prior note and is not re-litigated here.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
The subscription window for MNT Tech Holding's EGX IPO **closed oversubscribed across both tranches**, confirming the deal clears ahead of **20-Oct-2026** trading under ticker **HALN** (listed on EGX 14-Sep-2026 as HALN.CA).
- **Retail tranche: 43.6x on the final day.** ~2.093bn shares bid for the **48m** retail shares (15% of the offer, 3% of capital) → ~43.6x, implying a ~2.29% proportional allocation. Note the trajectory: ~2.68x by day 3 → 43.6x by day 6 — i.e. **demand was heavily back-loaded into the final session** (per Arab Finance/Zawya), a classic FOMO/allocation-gaming pattern, not steady 6-day accumulation. The headline multiple flatters the real breadth.
- **Private (institutional) tranche: 11.2x, or 19.4x excluding cornerstones** (272m shares). The **ex-cornerstone 19.4x is the cleaner read** of genuine third-party institutional appetite; the reported 11.2x is diluted by the pre-committed anchors (CIB up to EGP 2bn/~$38m; Redwheel ~$20m) already in the book.
- **Combined demand ~16x / ~EGP 125.9bn (~$2.5bn of orders)** against a ~EGP 7.84bn ($147m) deal. **Why this is noisy, not clean:** several reports flag that domestic investors **liquidated existing EGX holdings to fund subscriptions**, estimated to have **pulled ~EGP 204bn ($3.9bn) of market value out of other Egyptian stocks** — so part of the "record demand" is **rotation within a thin market**, not fresh/foreign inflow. The oversubscription is real but partly a cannibalisation of the rest of the EGX.
- **Nothing changed on terms:** 100% secondary sale (no new capital to the company), fixed EGP 24.5, Egypt unit valued **EGP 39.2bn (~$748m)** — all as already de-PR'd in the launch note.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Unchanged from [[MNT-Halan plans $150M IPO on Egyptian Exchange]]: direct EGX read-across is **Valu (EGX: VALU)** (~$370m, public since May-2025 — [[Egypt BNPL firm ValU goes public on Egyptian Stock Exchange]], [[Egypt's Valu reports 94% revenue growth in H1 2025]], [[Amazon exercises option to acquire stake in ValU]]); **Fawry (EGX: FWRY)** as Egypt's first fintech IPO (2019). Gulf anchors **Tabby (~$3.3bn), Tamara (~$1bn)** are poor proxies (BNPL vs lending-led NBFI). On **subscription-demand** specifically: 43.6x retail is strong even by hot-EM-IPO standards, but the back-loaded curve and the holdings-liquidation funding mean it reads less emphatically than, say, a Gulf retail book that draws fresh deposits.

## [2] Company history / fit
Full trajectory in the prior note. Relevant delta to the *result*: scale figures are as-disclosed in the prospectus — **~$947m gross loan book (30-Jun-2026), ~$6.1bn cumulative disbursed, ~1.9m active borrowers, 1,200+ distribution points / 25 governorates**, 24.3% of non-bank microfinance by loan book / 14% of non-bank consumer-finance disbursements (end-2025). New financial nuance surfaced by IPO-week coverage: net profit **EGP 2.27bn ($42.6m) 2023 → EGP 1.37bn ($25.6m) 2024 → EGP 1.98bn ($37.8m) 2025** (a dip-and-recover, not smooth growth, as funding cost outran loan yields in 2024); and the revenue mix is **shifting to securitisation/offloading gains — 45.5% of revenue in H1-2026 vs 17.7% in 2024**, while interest income fell to ~32% (per Launch Base Africa, Oct-2026). That mix shift is the single most important caveat behind the "profitable" label.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **The new, hard fact = demand, as traction of the *offering* (not the product):** books closed well oversubscribed, so the deal is **funded and will trade** — the June "trial balloon" → Oct "priced" → now "subscribed/closing" conversion is complete. That de-risks execution.
- **But oversubscription ≠ value creation for buyers.** It is a **100% secondary sale** (PE/vintage-holder exit liquidity; no capital reaches the company), the float is thin (48m of 320m public), and part of the demand is **recycled EGX money**. High oversubscription on a tiny, cheap-ticket (EGP 24.5) retail slice in a cash-starved market is a **weak signal of fundamental conviction**; the real test is the aftermarket from 20-Oct.
- **Margin-capture (deeper):** the 2024 profit dip + the swing to offloading/securitisation gains (45.5% of revenue) shows earnings are **sensitive to the funding-cost cycle and to realising gains on selling the book**, not purely durable NIM — so the ~20x P/E on the prior note's $748m mark rests on a revenue base whose durability is the open question.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Immediate:** allocation/refunds, then **first trade 20-Oct-2026 (HALN)**. The decisive event is now the **aftermarket**, not the book: does it hold above EGP 24.5 with visible foreign participation, or fade once the allocation-driven retail flips?
- **Second-order:** the IPO is framed as Egypt's best chance in years to pull foreign flows back to the EGX, yet the **$3.9bn drained from other EGX names to fund it** is the counterintuitive risk — a single large float can **drain liquidity from the rest of a thin market** rather than deepen it. If HALN trades well, it templates bank-backed PE exits and re-opens the ~8-listing 2026 EGX pipeline; if it fades, it signals the private marks (incl. the $1.4bn group) are soft. Standing EGP-devaluation translation risk persists on an EGP-earning book.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Egypt non-bank credit, served here via microfinance + consumer finance. MNT-Halan held 24.3% of Egypt's non-bank microfinance market by loan book and 14% of non-bank consumer-finance annual disbursements at end-2025 (per [[MNT-Halan closes $82M in securitised bond issuances]]) — a structurally concentrated subvertical where scale + a funding franchise (securitisation, a cornerstone bank-linked investor) are the entry barriers, not product. Size: active loan portfolio EGP46.7bn (~$947M) at 30-Jun-2026; cumulative disbursements since inception ~EGP178bn (per Launch Base Africa / Dealroom, Oct 2026). Why now: this is Egypt's biggest EGX IPO since 2021 (per Ecofin/Arab News, Oct 2026) — a capital-markets reopening driven by macro stabilisation: inflation down to ~13-14% from a ~38% 2024 peak, CBE policy rate held at 19% (6th straight meeting, Sep 2026), EGP ~52.3/$ after the brutal 2022-24 devaluations (per Daily News Egypt / Trading Economics, 2026). High real deposit rates (~4-5pts) that anchor the carry are a double edge — they also raise the cost of funding a loan book (analysis).

**Competitive landscape.** Lending KPIs: active loan book, net interest margin, cost of funds, NPL/par-30, disbursement growth. Listed EGX fintech peers and basis of competition (distribution + licence + funding access, not price): e-finance (state-owned, largest listed) and Fawry (payments). BNPL/consumer-finance peers regionally: Tabby, Tamara (GCC), and Valu / Fawry's Erada tie-up in Egypt ([[Fawry partners Erada Finance for instant microfinance in Egypt]], [[Egypt's BNPL provider Valu secures SME financing license]], 2026). Recent peer moves: Tabby $233M Series F at $6.5bn (Sep-2026, per Enterprise/Finextra); Tabby/Tamara combined with 7 other MENA fintech unicorns (~$17.75bn, per Fintech News UAE, Sep-2026). Protagonist position: dominant and profitable in Egyptian mass-market lending (ahead domestically), niche regionally vs GCC BNPL. Moat: scale + the lowest-income borrower data set + non-bank funding access (A- MERIS rating on recent securitisations) — switching costs are low for borrowers, so the moat is funding cost + distribution, not stickiness (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** Note the structure: the IPO sells 20% of the **Egyptian subsidiary (MNT Tech Holding)** only, for EGP7.84bn ($147-150M), implying ~EGP39.2bn (~$745M) for that entity (per Ecofin/thndr, Oct-2026). This is NOT the ~$1.4bn whole-group private valuation from [[MNT-Halan hits $1.4bn valuation in round led by Al Ahly Capital]] (Jun-2026) — it is a carve-out of the Egypt business, so "IPO below last round" is apples-to-oranges, not a down-round (analysis).
- **P/E (listed-peer basis):** H1-2026 net profit EGP910M → annualised ~EGP1,820M; EGP39,200M / 1,820M ≈ **21.5x**. Treat as rough — H1 doubling not run-rate verified → `[UNSOURCED]` for true FY run-rate.
- **P/S:** EGP39,200M / FY2025 revenue EGP19,814M ≈ **2.0x** (revenue roughly doubled from EGP9,412M in 2023, per Ecofin).
- **[[Fawry partners Erada Finance for instant microfinance in Egypt|Fawry]] (EGX, listed comp):** market cap ~EGP67.72bn; FY2025 revenue EGP8,651.5M → P/S ≈ **7.8x**; net profit EGP2,889.2M → P/E ≈ **23.4x** (per Zawya/TechCabal, 2026).
- **Tabby (private):** $6.5bn post-money / ~$378M revenue (Saudi unit) ≈ **17.2x P/S** — a growth BNPL multiple, poor comparability to a profitable balance-sheet lender.
Read: on P/E MNT Tech (~21.5x) sits **in line** with Fawry (~23x). On P/S it looks ~4x cheaper than Fawry (2.0x vs 7.8x), but that gap is largely artefact: a lender books gross interest income as revenue (capital-intensive, spread-driven), whereas Fawry's is asset-light payments revenue — the multiples are not like-for-like, so the P/S discount is NOT a clean "cheap" signal. Distribution not computed (only 1 truly comparable listed peer).

**Risk flags.**
1. **FX / macro translation.** Loan book, earnings and valuation are EGP-denominated against a currency that lost >70% since 2022 and sits at ~52.3/$; any renewed devaluation (regional-conflict / import-cost driven, per Middle East Insider 2026) erodes USD value and can spike defaults in a mass-market book — second-order: it hits the funding cost of the securitisation stack simultaneously.
2. **Funding-dependence + rate sensitivity.** A balance-sheet lender nearing a $1bn book at a 19% policy rate lives on the spread; the model depends on continuous securitisation access (A-/MERIS). If rates stay high or securitisation demand cools, NIM compresses and growth stalls — the moat is funding, so funding stress is existential, not cyclical.
3. **Perimeter / minority & float risk.** Only 20% of the Egyptian sub floats (3% of group capital via retail); the parent keeps the Türkiye/Pakistan/UAE upside. Public holders own a ring-fenced slice with limited governance and thin float — valuation re-rating risk on the first disclosed NPL / par-30 print, which the IPO marketing is silent on (de-PR: oversubscription is demand, not fundamentals disclosure).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** A profitable EGX lending IPO at ~21x earnings reopens Egypt's equity market as an exit for MENA fintech and sets a listed benchmark the GCC BNPL names (still private, loss-adjacent) lack (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: HALN holds/re-rates above offer only if post-listing filings show the H1 profit is run-rate AND par-30 stays contained; trigger/what breaks it — first post-IPO quarterly with rising NPLs or an EGP step-devaluation would de-rate it fast. Watch the Oct-20 debut and the first disclosed asset-quality figures.
