# Context-enrichment: Aljazira Bank adopts Thunes for cross-border transfers
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- On **28 September 2026** Thunes announced that **Bank Aljazira** (Saudi, Riyadh-HQ'd, Sharia-compliant; see [[Thunes and Banque Saudi Fransi partner on cross-border payments]] for the near-identical template) **signed an agreement / "selected" Thunes** to power outbound consumer remittances via Thunes' **Direct Global Network** (DGN). Aljazira's **retail** customers get real-time payout to bank accounts + mobile wallets, with "optimised corridors" in APAC, Europe and the GCC.
- **This is an announcement, not a live launch.** The wording is "signed a new agreement" / "selects … to power next-generation remittances" — future tense. No go-live date, no volumes, no pricing, no FX-margin disclosure, no named destination corridors beyond regional hand-waving. The internal aggregated text even says "signed an agreement to use" — intent, not traction.
- **Why framed this way:** the PR anchors to Thunes' network scale (DGN self-description now claims 140+ countries / 90+ currencies / 12bn+ wallets & accounts / 15bn cards — up from the 130/80/7bn stated in the Oct-2025 BSF release, i.e. Thunes is restating its own marketing stats, not Aljazira-specific metrics) and to a market stat ("56% of Saudi residents sent money abroad in the past year", Thunes×Juniper index — see [[Thunes and Juniper Research index reveals global payments infrastructure gap]]). None of that is a delta for *Aljazira*; it's Thunes' standard Saudi sales deck.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Saudi outbound remittance is a crowded infra land-grab, and Thunes is not uniquely positioned:
- **Wise Platform — Saudi Awwal Bank (SAB):** the direct analogue — a tier-1 Saudi bank routing retail international transfers through an infra provider. Wise typically brings transparent FX, a competitive threat on the exact value-prop ("faster, cheaper") Thunes claims.
- **TerraPay — D360 Bank:** went *live* with **inbound** payouts to Saudi Arabia (P2P/C2B/B2C/B2B) — note "live" vs Aljazira's "signed", and inbound vs Aljazira's outbound focus.
- **Nium — Emirates NBD (expanding into KSA):** regional rival rail; see [[Nium and Emirates NBD expand real-time payments in UAE]].
- **Thunes' own Saudi/Gulf book:** [[Thunes and Banque Saudi Fransi partner on cross-border payments]] (BSF, Oct 2025), [[First Abu Dhabi Bank partners Thunes for cross-border payouts]] (FAB-UAE, Nov 2025), plus TeleMoney (Arab National Bank's remittance arm) joining DGN.
- **Position:** parity, not lead. Aljazira is a mid-tier Saudi bank (smaller branch/retail footprint than BSF, SNB, Al Rajhi); this is Thunes adding another logo to an already-contested Kingdom, while Wise/TerraPay/Nium cover the same demand.
- **+ Why the landscape is this way:** Saudi outbound remittance (huge expat workforce → S/SE Asia, Egypt, Levant) is high-volume and until recently bank-intermediated at poor FX. Vision-2030 digital-finance push + SAMA openness invite infra providers to bank-channel partnerships. The second-order point: value accrues to whoever owns the *destination endpoints* (wallets in PH/IN/BD/EG) and the FX spread — i.e. Thunes — while the bank stays the customer-facing brand. The banks are becoming distribution, not rail owners.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Bank Aljazira:** Riyadh-HQ'd Saudi joint-stock Islamic bank; historically known for its remittance product ("Fawri"/retail transfer services) and a sizeable money-transfer franchise relative to its balance-sheet size. Adopting an infra partner to refresh ageing correspondent-bank rails and defend/grow its remittance fee line against fintech MTOs (Wise, STC Pay/urpay, Barq, Western Union) is a logical, defensive move — not a strategic pivot.
- **Thunes:** aggressive Riyadh-based Gulf expansion; post-$150M Series D ([[Thunes opens New York office in US expansion after $150M Series D]]) it is signing banks across the GCC as a repeatable playbook. Aljazira is the 3rd–4th Gulf bank logo in ~12 months.
- **+ Why:** for Aljazira, remittance fees are a durable retail revenue stream under threat from cheaper digital MTOs; buying a ready DGN is faster/cheaper than rebuilding correspondent coverage. For Thunes, each bank signing deepens Saudi volume density that makes its corridors cheaper — a flywheel where logos compound network economics.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** This is a repeat of the BSF template from exactly a year earlier (same DGN, same "Vision 2030 / global hub" boilerplate, same Riyadh signing cadence around SIBOS season). Not a new capability — a new customer.
- **Traction: unproven.** Zero adoption metrics: no go-live, no transaction/GMV figures, no corridor-level pricing, no customer counts. "Signed/selects" ≠ live.
- **+ Who captures the margin:** Thunes owns the endpoints, FX conversion and the SmartX treasury/compliance layer; Aljazira owns the customer relationship and the SAR funding leg. The durable economics sit with Thunes (per-transaction + FX spread across its whole Saudi book), while Aljazira's benefit is defensive fee retention, not a new high-margin line. If Saudi mandates FX transparency (as UAE/EU trend), the bank's remittance margin compresses regardless of who the rail is.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Watch for a **go-live** announcement with actual corridors (PH, IN, PK, BD, EG likely) and whether it reaches business/SME flows (PR says *retail* only) — that would be the real signal.
- **Backdrop:** SAMA's open, pro-fintech stance + Vision 2030 keep attracting infra rails; Saudi is the single most contested outbound-remittance market in MEA right now.
- **Risks / silence:** no exclusivity (Aljazira can also use SWIFT/others), no FX-margin or fraud-liability disclosure, no volume commitment. Thunes faces stablecoin-rail disintermediation longer-term (its own [[Thunes partners with Circle Payments Network for stablecoin settlement]] hedge shows it knows this).
- **+ Second-order:** the more Saudi banks Thunes signs, the more it, not the banks, becomes the de-facto national outbound-remittance utility — a concentration that is efficient now but gives Thunes pricing power over the very banks that fed it volume.

## Top challenge / red-team questions (second-order)

1. **Signed or live?** The PR says "signed a new agreement" / "selects". Any go-live date or production flow? → **Open / likely just announced** (28 Sep 2026). Treat as announcement, not adoption.
2. **Any numbers at all?** Volume, GMV, customer count, corridor pricing, FX margin? → **None disclosed.** Pure capability PR.
3. **Is this a duplicate of the BSF deal?** → **No — different bank** (Bank Aljazira vs Banque Saudi Fransi). Same template/vendor, distinct customer and date → **fresh**, not stale.
4. **Which exact corridors?** PR only says "APAC, Europe, GCC". → **Open.** No named destination countries; expat-worker corridors (PH/IN/PK/BD/EG) are the obvious targets but unconfirmed.
5. **Retail only?** PR specifies *retail/consumer* remittances. Does it cover SME/B2B? → **No — retail only as stated.** Narrows the deal's weight.
6. **Exclusivity?** Is Thunes Aljazira's sole cross-border rail, or one of several (alongside SWIFT/correspondents)? → **Open; almost certainly non-exclusive.**
7. **Who captures the margin?** → Thunes (endpoints + FX spread + treasury/compliance layer). Aljazira gets defensive fee retention, not a new margin line.
8. **Why Thunes over Wise/TerraPay/Nium?** All are live in Saudi (SAB-Wise, D360-TerraPay, ENBD-Nium). → **Open** — no stated differentiator; likely corridor coverage/compliance/relationship, not a hard technical edge.
9. **Is the "56% of Saudis send money abroad" stat Aljazira-specific?** → **No** — it's Thunes×Juniper market research reused as sales framing.
10. **Are the DGN scale stats (140 countries / 12bn wallets) new?** → **No** — restated/updated Thunes marketing vs the 130/7bn in the Oct-2025 BSF release; not a deal metric.
11. **What's the real strategic driver for Aljazira?** → Defend remittance fee revenue against cheaper digital MTOs (STC Pay/urpay, Barq, Wise, WU) by modernising ageing correspondent rails. Defensive.
12. **Fraud/AML liability split?** → **Silent.** Thunes markets a "Fortress Compliance Platform" but liability allocation with Aljazira undisclosed.
13. **Stablecoin disintermediation risk?** → Real medium-term; Thunes hedges via Circle Payments Network ([[Thunes partners with Circle Payments Network for stablecoin settlement]]).
14. **Second-order concentration risk?** → Thunes becoming the de-facto Saudi outbound-remittance utility across multiple banks gives it pricing power over those same banks later.
15. **Does this change the central question?** → Yes: not "did Aljazira get a cross-border upgrade" but "is Thunes quietly becoming the national remittance rail while Saudi banks commoditise into distribution front-ends" — and whether that survives stablecoin rails + FX-transparency regulation.

**Importance: 2/5 — rationale:** Fresh (distinct bank, Sep-2026 deal), but low-novelty — a near-verbatim repeat of the Oct-2025 BSF template with no disclosed go-live, no numbers, retail-only scope, and a crowded competitive field (Wise/TerraPay/Nium all live in-Kingdom). Matters mainly as another data point in Thunes' Saudi land-grab, not as a standalone event. Would merit a 3 only with live volumes/corridor economics.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Cross-border remittances out of the Gulf are large and growing: GCC expat remittance outflows hit a record ~$161bn in 2025, +13.6% YoY (per Saudi Gazette/Nukta citing GCC data, as of Feb 2026); Saudi personal expatriate remittances alone reached SAR 165.5bn in 2025 (~$44bn), +15% YoY vs SAR 144.2bn in 2024 (per enterpriseam.com citing SAMA, Feb 2026). The Saudi *digital* remittance slice is far smaller — ~$655m market value cited by Research and Markets (undated, treat as indicative, not verified). Structure: historically bank/MTO + correspondent-banking rails (slow, opaque FX, layered fees); the value chain is shifting toward network/infrastructure players (Thunes, Nium, TerraPay, Visa Direct) that banks plug into rather than build. "Why now": Vision 2030 digital-finance push, SAMA open-banking Phase 2, and SAMA's participation in BIS Project mBridge (handed to partners 2024, flagged "not mature enough to operate" by BIS) all pressure legacy correspondent banking; e-payments reached 79% of Saudi retail transactions in 2024 (per vision2030/J.P. Morgan citations).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/payment volume, take rate per corridor, settlement speed, corridor/country reach, direct-integration count. Basis of competition is network reach + unit economics, not price alone. Thunes positions as a network ("Direct Global Network"): 130+ countries, 80+ currencies, 7bn+ wallets/accounts, 15bn cards via 320+ payment methods (per Thunes PR — vendor-stated, unaudited). Named peers/recent moves: TerraPay took Visa as strategic investor and connected Visa Direct to its wallet network, and partnered Deutsche Bank for correspondent reach + FX (Aug 2026); Nium spans 190+ countries / 100+ real-time corridors and joined Visa's stablecoin settlement pilot; Wise Platform gained Payments Canada membership (Jan 2026). Protagonist's position: in Saudi, catching up / building share — Thunes already has a Riyadh office and signed Banque Saudi Fransi (Oct 2025) and First Abu Dhabi Bank (Nov 2025); Aljazira is an incremental bank win, not a new-market entry. Moat (analysis): direct-integration breadth + in-house treasury/compliance (SmartX / Fortress) create switching costs for members, but banks typically multi-home across networks, capping lock-in.

**Comps & multiples.** Internal comps (same vendor, same Gulf motion): [[Thunes and Banque Saudi Fransi partner on cross-border payments]] (Oct 2025), [[First Abu Dhabi Bank partners Thunes for cross-border payouts]] (Nov 2025), [[Thunes opens New York office in US expansion after $150M Series D]] (Jun 2026), [[Nium and Emirates NBD expand real-time payments in UAE]] (Oct 2025), [[Standard Chartered partners with Dandelion for cross-border payouts]] (Oct 2025). Thunes valuation: $150M Series D (Apr 2025, led by Apis + Vitruvian), total funding ~$362m, third-party trackers put it past ~$1bn valuation for the first time (per EPI/FinTech Futures; valuation is a round/tracker figure, NOT audited market cap). Multiples: EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E = no data — Thunes is private, revenue/EBITDA undisclosed, so no multiple can be computed; corridor-level take rate and unit economics also undisclosed [UNSOURCED]. Qualitative only: comparison not computed on a numeric basis.

**Risk flags.**
1. Economics undisclosed (de-PR): the release is an "agreement"/adoption announcement with no ticket size, take rate, go-live date, or FX-spread split — classic "who's silent about what." Risk that this is MOU-stage, not live volume. Why: headline-win optics can outrun realized TPV.
2. Non-exclusive, multi-homing: banks commonly run several networks (Nium, TerraPay, Visa Direct all active in Gulf); Thunes winning Aljazira does not lock the flow, so share is contestable corridor-by-corridor. Why: caps pricing power and durability of the relationship.
3. Rails disintermediation / margin squeeze: stablecoin settlement (Nium–Visa pilot) and card-network direct rails (Visa Direct via TerraPay) could capture part of the cross-border stack and compress network take rates. Why: second-order margin risk to pure routing networks.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Incrementally, not structurally: this is the deepening of Thunes' Saudi land-grab (3rd+ named Gulf bank in ~12 months), consistent with banks renting cross-border infrastructure rather than building it. Falsifiable thesis: Thunes is becoming the default outbound-remittance rail for mid-tier Saudi banks. Trigger to confirm: a disclosed go-live + corridor volume, or a 4th Saudi bank signing; what breaks it: Aljazira also signing Nium/TerraPay, or the deal staying MOU-stage with no live flow.
