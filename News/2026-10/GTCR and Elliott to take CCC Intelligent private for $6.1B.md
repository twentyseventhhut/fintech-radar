CHICAGO, Oct. 15, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- CCC Intelligent Solutions Holdings Inc. (NASDAQ: CCC) (“CCC” or the “Company”), a leading SaaS and AI platform provider for the multi-trillion-dollar insurance economy, today announced that it has entered into a definitive agreement to be acquired by a consortium of private investment firms led by GTCR, and including Elliott Investment Management L.P. (together with its affiliates, “Elliott”), in an all-cash transaction with an enterprise value of approximately $6.1 billion.
Under the terms of the agreement, CCC stockholders will receive $7.75 in cash for each share of CCC common stock, representing a premium of approximately 44% to CCC’s unaffected closing stock price on July 9, 2026, the last trading day prior to media speculation regarding a potential sale of the Company, and a premium of approximately 59% to the 30-day average price as of July 9, 2026.
Based in Chicago, CCC connects more than 35,000 businesses across the insurance economy. Drawing on 40 years of industry data and over a decade of AI embedded into mission-critical workflows, CCC helps customers improve decision-making, efficiency and outcomes across the claims and repair lifecycle.
“Throughout CCC’s history, we have helped customers navigate growing complexity and achieve better outcomes through technology and innovation,” said Githesh Ramamurthy, Chairman & CEO of CCC. “With GTCR and Elliott’s experience and resources, we will continue to build on our momentum and extend our leadership. Together, we will accelerate the AI and agentic capabilities our customers want from us, expand the network at the center of our platform, and continue to develop and build our world class team. I am deeply grateful to our employees for their dedication and commitment to our customers, and I am excited for all that we will accomplish together in this next chapter.”
Ramamurthy added, “Today’s announcement follows a thorough review of strategic options with assistance from our financial and legal advisors. Our Board of Directors is confident this transaction recognizes the strength of the business our team has built while delivering immediate and premium cash value to our stockholders.”
“We have followed CCC for years and believe the Company is uniquely positioned to support their customers as they increasingly adopt AI solutions to improve decision making and efficiency across the claims and repair lifecycle,” said Mark Anderson, Managing Director, Head of Technology, Media & Telecommunications at GTCR. “We look forward to partnering with Githesh and the talented CCC team to support continued investment in product innovation and AI capabilities, expand the Company’s offerings and build long-term value. Our focus will be on helping CCC deliver better outcomes for its customers while supporting CCC’s next phase of growth.”
“We have strong belief in CCC’s position as the trusted third party at the center of the automotive insurance and repair industry,” said David Kerko, Head of Global Private Equity at Elliott. “CCC’s role within the large ecosystem of insurers, repair facilities, automakers and parts suppliers creates strong data advantages and network benefits for their more than 35,000 customers. We believe these advantages make CCC a highly durable franchise in vertical software – which AI will only make more valuable as CCC delivers the next generation of capabilities to its customers.”
 Transaction Approvals and Timing 
The transaction was approved by CCC’s Board of Directors and is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027, subject to customary closing conditions, including the receipt of required regulatory approvals and the approval of CCC stockholders. Until the transaction closes, CCC remains publicly traded and will continue operating as an independent company. The transaction is not subject to a financing condition. Upon completion of the transaction, CCC will become a privately held company, and its common stock will no longer be listed on the Nasdaq.

# Context-enrichment: GTCR and Elliott to take CCC Intelligent Solutions private for $6.1B
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- **Deal.** GTCR-led consortium (incl. Elliott Investment Management) to acquire CCC Intelligent Solutions Holdings (NASDAQ: CCC) in an **all-cash take-private at $7.75/share, EV ≈ $6.1B**. Signed Oct 15, 2026; expected close **Q1 2027**; subject to stockholder + regulatory (HSR) approval. **No financing condition** — committed debt from J.P. Morgan (lead arranger) + Citi. Advisors: Morgan Stanley (CCC); J.P. Morgan + Citi (buyers).
- **Premium framing (watch the base).** The "~44% premium" is measured to the **unaffected close on July 9, 2026 — the last day before media leaked a possible sale**. "~59%" is to the 30-day average to that date. These are the *flattering* bases: the stock had already run on deal speculation for 3+ months before signing, so the premium vs. the Oct pre-announcement price is far thinner. Standard take-private PR mechanics — anchor to the pre-leak price, not the pre-signing price.
- **The real "tell" is the valuation vs. 2021.** CCC went public via the **Dragoneer Growth Opportunities SPAC in 2021 at ~$7B EV**. This deal is **~$6.1B EV — a lower enterprise value ~5 years later**, even though revenue has grown from ~$700M (2021) to **$1.057B (2025)**. So the multiple compressed hard: the market de-rated SPAC-era vertical SaaS, and PE is buying the *cash flows*, not a growth story. (analysis)
- **What it is, de-PR'd.** CCC is the dominant **US auto physical-damage claims/estimating network** — not a generic "AI insurance platform." The $6.1B is a bet on an entrenched, cash-generative workflow monopoly, lightly repainted with an "AI/agentic" narrative.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Direct estimating/claims rivals:** **Mitchell International (Enlyte)** — distant #2 in US collision/claims software, deeply embedded with high switching costs; **Solera / Audatex (Qapter)** — global auto-damage estimating, strong international + parts data. CCC is the clear #1 in US auto physical damage, processing $100B+ of transaction value through its network (network-effect moat).
- **Broader P&C software:** **Guidewire** (core systems), **Verisk** (data/analytics) — adjacent, not head-to-head in collision estimating.
- **Deal/sponsor peers.** Elliott is a repeat tech-PE/activist actor in the corpus: [[Elliott Management builds stake in Global Payments]] (2025, activist payments), [[Klarna and Elliott deepen partnership with $2bn facility]] (2026, credit). GTCR is a recurring fintech buyer: [[GTCR to acquire FMG Suite from Aurora Capital Partners]] (2025, advisor martech). This CCC deal is the **largest** of that GTCR/Elliott fintech-adjacent cluster.
- **Why the lay of the land is this way (analysis):** vertical-SaaS take-privates are the 2025–26 PE theme — durable, high-margin (41% adj. EBITDA), low-growth assets that public markets won't pay a growth multiple for but PE will lever. CCC fits the template precisely: a toll-booth on a non-discretionary workflow (cars crash regardless of the economy).

## [2] Company history / fit
- Chicago-based, **40+ years of industry data**, 35,000+ connected businesses (insurers, repairers, automakers, parts suppliers). Owned by **Advent International (2017)** → **Dragoneer SPAC 2021 (~$7B EV, NYSE)** → now **back to PE at $6.1B**. A full public-market round-trip in ~5 years ending at a *lower* EV.
- **Financials (de-PR'd):** 2025 revenue **$1.057B (+12% YoY)**, adj. EBITDA **$436M (41% margin)**. 2026 guide **~$1.147–1.157B (~9% growth)**, adj. EBITDA **$477–485M (~42% margin)**. Q2'26: +10% rev, AI solutions ~11% of revenue growing ~45% YoY. **Deceleration** (12% → 9%) is visible — a key reason the public multiple fell.
- **Implied deal multiple (analysis):** ~$6.1B EV / ~$481M 2026E adj. EBITDA ≈ **12.6x EBITDA**, ≈ **5.3x revenue** — a sober, cash-flow multiple, not a growth-SaaS multiple.
- **Why act now (analysis):** growth has slowed to high-single-digits, the SPAC premium is long gone, and AI capex/narrative pressure is rising. Going private lets CCC invest in AI/agentic without quarterly scrutiny — and lets PE harvest 41–42% margins and lever the committed debt.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Not a new event type** — this is a textbook sponsor take-private of mature vertical SaaS. The value to the asset owner is the **network moat**: CCC sits in the middle of insurer↔repairer↔automaker↔parts flows, which is hard to dislodge and compounds data (Elliott's own thesis: "trusted third party at the center").
- **AI angle is real but small/early:** AI solutions ~11% of revenue (Q2'26), growing ~45% — genuine traction but a minority line; the "agentic" language is forward-looking, not the thesis. Buyers are paying for the legacy toll-booth, with AI as optionality.
- **Who captures the margin (analysis):** CCC captures it today — 41–42% EBITDA margins confirm pricing power from the network. The downside risk: if generative-AI photo-estimating (where Solera/Qapter and insurer in-house tools also play) commoditizes the estimate itself, CCC's moat shifts from "estimating engine" to "network/distribution." The deal bets the network survives even if the estimating layer commoditizes.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Timeline:** Q3'26 results Oct 29, 2026 (no call, given pending deal). Proxy → stockholder vote → HSR → **close Q1 2027**. No financing condition lowers close risk; main risk is a competing bid (the agreement explicitly contemplates it) or regulatory delay — antitrust risk is modest given no horizontal buyer overlap.
- **Market read (analysis):** stock trading near $7.75 post-announcement = market views a topping bid as unlikely but not impossible (Elliott + no-finance-condition signal a firm deal). 
- **Second-order:** confirms the de-rating of 2020–21 SPAC vertical-SaaS — several are being re-privatized below their SPAC EVs. For the insurance-tech sector it's a **liquidity event / validation of durable cash flows**, not a sign of frothy M&A. Under PE, expect bolt-on M&A, pricing optimization, and an AI product push away from public disclosure.
- **Risk:** leverage + ~9% growth means limited margin for a demand shock or faster AI commoditization of core estimating.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** CCC sits in vertical SaaS for P&C auto-claims — the digital rails connecting insurers, collision repairers, automakers and parts suppliers across the claims-and-repair lifecycle. The subvertical is structurally oligopolistic and network-driven: CCC claims 35,000+ businesses, 900,000+ active users and 250+ ecosystem partners on a 40-year data moat (per CCC PR, 2026-10-15; per Q2'26 call, via Fool). Demand is counter-cyclical to frequency: over the past decade US personal-auto claim *counts* fell <1%/yr while average $/claim rose ~6%/yr, so claims-cost inflation (parts, labor, vehicle complexity) drives CCC's addressable spend even as fewer accidents occur (per Q2'26 call, via Investing.com). Why now: (1) AI penetration is still early — AI-based solutions were ~10-11% of revenue (~$100m) growing ~45% YoY, with AI touching only low-single-to-double-digit % of total claims (per Q2'26 call, via Fool) — a long runway the sponsors are underwriting; (2) a wave of PE take-privates in profitable vertical SaaS (Hg/OneStream $6.4bn Jan'26; Thoma Bravo/Dayforce $12.3bn Feb'26; Thoma Bravo/Olo $2.0bn ~6.2x revenue) (per saasrise Vertical SaaS M&A 2026). CCC itself was Advent-owned (2017), SPAC-listed 2021, Advent exited 2025 — this is its second take-private (per Insurance Journal, 2026-10-15).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: ARR/revenue growth, adj. EBITDA margin, net retention, and AI-revenue mix. CCC runs at ~9-12% revenue growth with a ~41-42% adj. EBITDA margin — a classic Rule-of-40-plus profile (~12%+41%=53% in FY25). Key players: Guidewire and Duck Creek (core policy/claims admin for carriers), Verisk and Solera/Mitchell (data, analytics, auto estimatics) are the nearest public/peer references; the basis of competition is data network effects and embedded workflow switching costs rather than price. Recent peer moves: Verisk bought SuranceBay (~$163m, ~7.0x rev, Jul'25); Guidewire and Duck Creek flagged as active insurance-vertical buyers in 2026 (per multiples.vc / saasrise). CCC's position: category leader in auto physical-damage claims with a durable franchise (Elliott's thesis: "trusted third party at the center" — treat as sponsor PR, not fact), but a *narrower* auto-claims footprint vs Guidewire's broader carrier-core suite. Moat = network effects + 40yr proprietary dataset feeding AI; `(analysis)` the AI edge is real but still <15% of revenue, so most of the base is mature, slower-growth estimatics. NRR/CAC not disclosed → `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal: EV ≈ $6.1bn, $7.75/sh cash, ~44% premium to unaffected 2026-07-09 close (per CCC PR).

- EV/Revenue (FY26E ~$1.16bn midpoint): $6.1bn / $1.16bn = **5.3x**
- EV/EBITDA (FY25 adj. $436m): $6.1bn / $0.436bn = **14.0x**
- EV/EBITDA (FY26E adj. ~$488m midpoint): $6.1bn / $0.488bn = **12.5x**
(revenue/EBITDA per CCC FY25 release + Q2'26 guidance, via Investing.com/coverager)
Peer context: Guidewire ~30.0x EV/EBITDA on ~$1bn revenue (Jun'26, multiples.vc); mature-vertical-SaaS LBO range cited at ~15-25x EV/EBITDA and ~4-8x EV/Rev (saasrise/axiagrowth); Olo take-private ~6.2x revenue. On revenue (5.8x) and EBITDA (14.0x) CCC prices **in-line-to-cheap** vs the vertical-SaaS LBO range and well below high-growth Guidewire — consistent with CCC's lower growth (~9-12%) and the standard take-private discount; the ~44% premium is to a depressed pre-leak price, not a rich outright multiple. Internal comps (insurance-AI/claims, all private VC rounds — scale-incomparable, directional only): [[Reserv raises $125 million Series C for AI claims technology]], [[Novella raises $21 million to expand AI wholesale insurance brokerage]], [[Artificial Labs raises $45 million Series B for digital underwriting]], [[Liberate raises $50 million Series B for insurance AI]]. Distribution not computed — only one at-scale comp (Guidewire); qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Growth deceleration into a leveraged structure.** Revenue growth slowed to 9.8% in Q2'26 from 12.0% a year earlier, and management flagged a legacy first-party casualty customer roll-off cutting ~1pt from 2H'26 growth (per Q2'26 call, via Investing.com/Fool). An LBO loads debt onto a decelerating top line — if growth drifts toward mid-single-digits, debt-service coverage tightens and the AI-upsell thesis has to carry the model.
2. **Deal-break / regulatory & timing risk.** Close targeted Q1'27, subject to HSR antitrust clearance and a stockholder vote; the PR explicitly admits competing-offer and litigation risk (per CCC PR). No financing condition reduces funding risk but not regulatory/vote risk; the spread to $7.75 reflects this.
3. **Concentration on auto physical-damage + insurer-pricing cycle.** CCC is levered to US auto-claims volume/severity and a concentrated base of large carriers; a soft auto-insurance pricing cycle or a single large-carrier roll-off (as just seen) directly dents revenue — second-order, a buyer with CCC's switching costs can hold price, but can't manufacture claims volume.
