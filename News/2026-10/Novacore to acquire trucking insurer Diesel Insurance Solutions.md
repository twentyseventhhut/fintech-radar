Acquisition adds deep cross-border trucking, physical damage and cargo expertise to Novacore's Specialty Transportation segment. 
 CONSHOHOCKEN, Pa. , Oct. 13, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Novacore , an independent, next-generation specialty insurance provider, announced today it has entered into a definitive agreement to acquire Diesel Insurance Solutions Inc . , a California-based managing general underwriter specializing in cross-border trucking, physical damage and cargo coverage.
The acquisition of Diesel expands Novacore's Specialty Transportation segment with a dedicated cross-border trucking and cargo platform, adding underwriting expertise tailored to carriers and logistics operators moving freight across the U.S.-Mexico border. As part of Novacore, Diesel is expected to broaden its offering beyond physical damage and cargo into additional specialty transportation lines, deepening Novacore's ability to serve trucking and logistics clients across the full spectrum of their risk.
"The acquisition of Diesel Insurance Solutions represents a strategic expansion of Novacore's Specialty Transportation segment into cross-border trucking, physical damage and cargo coverage," said Aaron Miller, CEO of Novacore. "Diesel brings deep underwriting expertise and a strong niche in an underserved corner of the transportation market. Together, we are continuing our approach of profitable growth across the Novacore platform, and we're excited to support Jason and his team as they broaden Diesel's offering into new lines."
Founded and led by Jason Wexler, Diesel is known for its specialized underwriting in cross-border trucking, physical damage and cargo coverage, serving carriers and logistics operators navigating the complexities of freight movement throughout US and Cross-Border. The full Diesel team will join Novacore.
"Joining Novacore marks an exciting next chapter for Diesel," said Jason Wexler, Founder of Diesel Insurance Solutions. "Becoming part of the Novacore platform gives our team access to a deep agent network, new technology and the resources to grow. It positions us to build on the foundation we've established in cross-border trucking and expand into new lines with the full support of a broader specialty insurance organization."
The transaction underscores Novacore's continued commitment to building a diversified, specialty-focused platform that combines underwriting expertise, technology and services to meet the evolving needs of complex insurance markets.
 About Novacore 
Novacore is an independent, next-generation specialty insurance provider with a bold mission: transforming insurance — for agents, carrier partners, clients and the future. Building on the 35-year track record of NSM Insurance Group, Novacore delivers industry-specific insurance programs through specialized underwriting, advanced technology and trusted carrier partnerships. The company partners with more than 20,000 agents nationwide and offers 20+ specialty programs across a diverse set of markets. For more information, visit novacore.com.

## [0] What happened (one-paragraph fact base)

On **2026-10-13**, **Novacore** — an independent, New Mountain Capital-backed specialty insurance platform in Conshohocken, PA (built on the ~35-year track record of NSM Insurance Group's former US commercial business) — announced a **definitive agreement to acquire Diesel Insurance Solutions Inc.**, a California-based managing general underwriter (MGU) specializing in **cross-border trucking, physical damage and cargo coverage** for carriers and logistics operators moving freight across the U.S.–Mexico border. Diesel is folded into Novacore's **Specialty Transportation** segment; founder **Jason Wexler** and the **full Diesel team** join Novacore. **Financial terms were not disclosed.** (Source: PRNewswire release; confirmed by The Insurer, Insurance Business, Coverager, FinTech Global, Truck News.)

## [1] Who the players are

- **Novacore** — relaunched **April 2025** as the rebrand of NSM Insurance Group's US commercial division after **New Mountain Capital** acquired it (NSM's Carlyle-backed D2C brands were separately rebranded **Ignyte Insurance**). ~**$1.3B premium**, **20,000+ agents**, **20+ specialty programs / 15+ segments**, 11 offices, 350+ staff. CEO **Aaron Miller** (ex-NSM chief commercial lines officer); COO/CUO Chase Clark; Phil Dwyer leads corporate development. Running an aggressive **buy-and-build**: prior bolt-ons include **CP Insurance Associates** (TX lender-placed / FI) and **Minglewood Risk** (real estate), plus a new transactional-risk unit **Zion Underwriting** and a reinsurance sidecar **NovaRe** (via New Mountain's VictoryRe) launched to feed program capacity.
- **Diesel Insurance Solutions** — CA MGU **founded 2024** by **Jason Wexler**, a repeat insurtech operator (founder/COO/CUO of **Cover Whale**; former head of insurance underwriting at **Procore**). Niche: underserved **US–Mexico cross-border** trucking physical damage + cargo. Expected to extend beyond PD/cargo into additional specialty transportation lines post-close. (dieselinsure.com)

## [2] Why it matters / market context

Cross-border US–Mexico freight is a structural growth corridor: the market is estimated around **$95.6B in 2026** growing ~**4.5% CAGR** through 2031, driven by **nearshoring** (400+ new manufacturing facilities in northern Mexico), USMCA rules and e-commerce — against the **tightest cross-border capacity year in recent memory**. That makes specialized MGU underwriting of cross-border trucking/cargo risk a scarce, high-barrier capability. For Novacore this is a classic **MGA buy-and-build** move: acquire a founder-led niche underwriter, plug it into a $1.3B platform with agent distribution, tech and reinsurance capacity (NovaRe), and expand its line breadth.

## [3] Prior art in our DB (internal)

- [[Indemni raises $4M for freight risk and insurance]] — 2026-10, freight risk-management + insurance insurtech funded by Field/Diagram Ventures; same freight-insurance vertical, venture-funded challenger vs. Novacore's roll-up approach.
- [[DB Insurance acquires US specialty insurer Fortegra Group]] / [[DB Insurance in talks to acquire Fortegra for $1.5B]] — 2025, strategic/PE-adjacent consolidation of US specialty insurers; comparable M&A thesis (buy specialty US underwriting capability).

## [4] Open questions / what to watch

- Deal size and structure (undisclosed) — Diesel is a ~2024 startup MGU, so premium volume and headcount are likely small; strategic rather than scale.
- Whether the bolt-on cadence (CP, Minglewood, Diesel, Zion, NovaRe) signals Novacore gearing toward a larger exit/recap under New Mountain.
- Execution risk on retaining founder-led underwriting talent (Wexler) and expanding Diesel into new lines without diluting its niche edge.

9. **Is the cross-border/nearshoring tailwind real or hype?** Multiple independent market reports confirm ~$95B 2026 market and nearshoring-driven freight growth, though exact figures vary by vendor. Directionally solid.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Subvertical: US specialty commercial P&C / MGA-MGU distribution, specifically commercial trucking (physical damage + cargo) in the US-Mexico cross-border lane. Size/growth: total US MGA premium climbed 12% in 2025 to $102.6bn (per Gallagher Re 2026 MGA Market Report, via insurancebusinessmag); commercial truck insurance is a large sub-line (third-party market-report estimates vary widely, ~$130-167bn global for 2026 — treat as low-confidence, [UNSOURCED] for US-only). The relevant demand driver is cross-border freight: the US-Mexico cross-border freight market is projected ~$91bn (2025) → ~$119bn (2031), ~4.5% CAGR, with Laredo loads reportedly up ~10x since 2022 on nearshoring (per freightwaves/trade reports — directional, not audited). Structure: fragmented, distribution-led. Value is created at the MGA/underwriting layer (pricing niche risk the carriers won't touch) while capacity sits with carrier/reinsurance partners; barriers are underwriting data + agent distribution, not balance-sheet capital. **Why now:** commercial auto is in a hard-market 14th straight year of underwriting losses — industry combined ratio ~107.2 in 2024, S&P projects ~104.4 for 2026 and >100% through 2029; CIAB logged the 59th consecutive quarter of commercial-auto rate increases (+5.8% Q1 2026, the largest of any major line). Social inflation / nuclear verdicts (>$10m verdicts +52% 2023→2024) keep severity rising. A hard, chronically-unprofitable line is exactly where disciplined niche MGAs can earn — that is the strategic logic of bolting a cross-border specialist onto Novacore's Specialty Transportation segment.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for an MGA: gross written premium (GWP) under management, program loss ratio / underwriting margin, carrier-capacity retention, and agent count (distribution). Novacore itself: ~$1.3bn premium, 20,000+ agents, 20+ specialty programs, built on NSM's 35-yr track record; it is the former NSM US commercial business, carved out and sold to New Mountain Capital, relaunched as Novacore in April 2025 (per theinsurer / coverager). Diesel = California MGU founded by Jason Wexler, niche in US-Mexico cross-border physical damage + cargo; financial terms NOT disclosed (deal size, Diesel GWP, loss ratio all undisclosed — `[UNSOURCED]`). Basis of competition here is underwriting expertise + distribution, not price. Recent peer moves: HDVI (trucking insurtech MGA) was itself consolidated in 2026 (per insurancebusinessmag); [[Roadzen acquires majority stake in EliteCover Insurance Solutions]] (Dec 2025, commercial-auto MGA roll-up); [[DB Insurance acquires US specialty insurer Fortegra Group]] (specialty-insurer M&A, ~$1.5bn per [[DB Insurance in talks to acquire Fortegra for $1.5B]]); [[Porch Group to acquire Allied Trust insurance entities for $200M]] (Oct 2026, carrier+MGA+captive). Protagonist's position: PE-backed consolidator buying into a niche it did not previously serve — a tuck-in, not a market-share land-grab (analysis). Moat is intangible/underwriting-data + the 20k-agent distribution it can now cross-sell Diesel's product through.

**Comps & multiples.** The deal is undisclosed — no price, no Diesel financials — so NO deal multiple can be computed (EV/Rev, EV/EBITDA = no data). External sector benchmark only, as a GUIDE: specialty MGA/program platforms with underwriting authority have been transacting roughly 12-18x EBITDA in 2024-26 (richest end 15-22x at scale, per ctacquisitions / mccombiegroup sell-side guides — secondary, directional). Whether Diesel fits that band is unknowable here → `[UNSOURCED]`. Internal comps from the base (qualitative, distribution not computed):
- [[Roadzen acquires majority stake in EliteCover Insurance Solutions]] — closest analog: commercial-auto MGA roll-up, also undisclosed terms.

Flag: no over/under-valuation call possible — undisclosed terms.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Loss-ratio / line-cycle risk.** Commercial auto has lost money for 14 straight years (CR ~107 in 2024, projected >100% through 2029). Buying a niche in a chronically-unprofitable line only pays if Diesel's book is genuinely better-than-market — undisclosed loss ratio means that is unverifiable. If Diesel's underwriting isn't disciplined, Novacore inherits the line's structural unprofitability.
2. **Concentration on a single corridor + policy risk.** Diesel's niche is specifically the US-Mexico cross-border lane. The nearshoring thesis is real but cyclical and politically exposed — USMCA renegotiation, tariffs, or border disruption would hit this one book disproportionately (second-order: a corridor-specific shock has no diversification offset in a dedicated platform).
3. **PE-buy-and-build execution / integration risk.** Novacore is a <2-yr-old New Mountain Capital carve-out doing tuck-in M&A; retention of founder Jason Wexler and the full team is the stated value, so key-person dependence and culture-fit are live risks (analysis). Undisclosed price also means no public check on whether a PE sponsor overpaid in a 12-18x MGA market.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is consolidation, not re-rating: a PE-backed MGA platform is assembling a diversified specialty-transportation book by buying niche underwriting teams rather than building them — expect more trucking/cargo MGU tuck-ins into platforms like Novacore, Ryan Specialty and Amwins as the hard commercial-auto market makes niche underwriting scarce and valuable (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if nearshoring freight volumes keep compounding and commercial-auto stays hard, cross-border trucking MGAs re-rate as scarce profitable-niche assets; it breaks if the commercial-auto loss cycle finally softens (CR back below 100) or USMCA/tariff disruption stalls the Laredo corridor — then the niche premium evaporates. Trigger to watch: disclosed Diesel GWP/loss ratio at close, and the next trucking-MGA deal's multiple.
