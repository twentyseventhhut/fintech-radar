# Stripe's Privy partners Ripple to bring embedded wallets to XRP Ledger

> [!info] 2026-10-13 · 1 упоминаний · 1 источника(ов) с текстом
> Каналы: Connecting the Dots in Fintech

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Connecting the Dots in Fintech] 🇺🇸 Stripe's Privy partners with Ripple to bring embedded wallets to the XRP Ledger. Developers can create XRPL wallets and sign transactions without building the infrastructure, with support for XRP and RLUSD. Server side wallets will let AI agents hold funds and pay for online services through the x402 protocol.

Stripe-owned Privy said on October 13, 2026, that it is partnering with Ripple to bring its wallets to the XRP Ledger (XRPL). Developers will be able to create XRPL wallets and sign transactions without building that infrastructure themselves.
 The Brief 
Privy will support XRP, Ripple’s RLUSD stablecoin and other XRPL assets for wallet creation and transaction signing in developer apps.
Server-side wallets will let AI agents hold funds and pay for online services through the x402 payment protocol.
The XRPL has more than 8 million funded accounts and more than $1 trillion in value moved, according to Privy.
Users will be able to export their private keys and seed phrases if they want to move a wallet elsewhere.
Privy builds embedded wallets that sit inside an app, so users don’t need to connect a separate crypto wallet. In its announcement , the company aimed that model at payments firms and fintechs. Those firms can offer stablecoin payments without asking customers to handle keys. Ramp’s stablecoin accounts, for one, already run on Privy’s infrastructure.
 1/ We’re partnering with @Ripple to bring Privy wallets to the XRP Ledger (XRPL).

This makes it easier for developers to build stablecoin payments, fintech applications, and agentic commerce on XRPL. https://t.co/vUhKJdfxBT — Privy (@privy_io) October 13, 2026 
 Stripe’s wallet arm plugs into Ripple’s network 
 Payments giant Stripe agreed to buy Privy in June 2025. Privy has kept operating as a separate product since then. Henri Stern , Privy’s co-founder and CEO, also serves as head of crypto and stablecoins at Stripe.
Stern’s dual role puts a Stripe executive at the center of a Ripple integration. Stripe already runs its own stablecoin business through Bridge , the infrastructure firm it bought for $1.1 billion. Its wallet unit will now carry RLUSD , Ripple’s dollar stablecoin, as well. Neither company said whether Bridge-issued tokens will also reach the XRPL through Privy wallets.
Stern framed the move around payments. Stablecoins “ are changing how money moves, ” he said, adding that the XRPL has focused on payments since its early days. Markus Infanger , SVP of RippleX at Ripple, made a similar case. He said Privy’s tooling makes it easier for fintechs to put the ledger into shipping products, from stablecoin payments to agent-driven commerce.
 AI agents get a payment rail on XRPL 
The second half of the pitch targets software that spends money on its own. Privy said server-side wallets can give agents a way to hold and move funds, including through x402.
 Coinbase developed x402 as an open protocol that revives the HTTP 402 “ Payment Required ” status code. Clients, whether people or machines, can use it to pay for APIs and online content programmatically. No accounts or sessions are required. A server answers an unpaid request with a 402 response that carries the payment instructions.
The resulting stack pulls pieces from three separate companies: Stripe-owned wallets, Ripple’s ledger and Coinbase’s payment standard. That gives the XRPL a direct line into a standard built outside Ripple’s own product line. 
Developers can start from the XRPL guide that Privy linked in its post. People holding funds in apps built this way keep the key-export option described above. 
 Open Questions 
The post explains what developers will be able to do, but it doesn’t say how many apps are already building. It also leaves practical questions open:
 Which payment firms or fintechs will ship XRPL wallets first? 
 Will agent payments over x402 settle in RLUSD, XRP or both? 
 How will Privy price XRPL wallets against its other supported networks? 
 Will Ripple route any of its own payment products through Privy wallets? 
Privy’s stated next step is adding policy controls and gas sponsorship, which would let developers set transaction rules and cover users’ network fees. The company gave no date for either feature. Until those arrive, the integration centers on wallet creation, signing and key export. The announcement came three weeks after a September 23 Privy post on new ways to build with TRON.

### [0] What happened (one line)
On **2026-10-13** Stripe-owned embedded-wallet provider **Privy** announced a partnership with **Ripple** to bring Privy's embedded/server-side wallets to the **XRP Ledger (XRPL)** — letting developers create XRPL wallets, sign transactions for **XRP and RLUSD**, and give AI agents a payment rail via Coinbase's **x402** protocol. ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-10-13-stripe-privy-ripple-xrp-ledger-wallets-418463), [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/10/14/ripple-adds-stripe-owned-wallets-to-xrp-ledger-as-ai-payments-pass-14-million), [Privy blog](https://privy.io/blog/building-payments-and-agentic-commerce-with-the-xrp-ledger))

### [1] Who are the players
- **Privy** — embedded-wallet infra (wallets live inside an app; no separate crypto wallet to connect). Founded 2021 (orig. Horkos Inc.); powers 75M+ accounts across 1,000+ developer teams. Acquired by Stripe June 2025. Co-founder/CEO **Henri Stern** also serves as **Stripe's head of crypto and stablecoins** — so a Stripe exec sits at the center of this Ripple integration. ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/06/11/stripe-acquires-crypto-wallet-infrastructure-provider-privy/), [CoinDesk acq.](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/06/11/stripe-to-acquire-crypto-wallet-startup-privy-in-bid-to-expand-web3-capabilities))
- **Ripple / RippleX** — spokesman **Markus Infanger** (SVP RippleX). Owns **RLUSD** stablecoin; XRPL is a payments-focused L1 with (per Privy) 8M+ funded accounts and >$1T value moved over 14+ years.
- **Stripe** — owns Privy AND runs its own stablecoin stack via **Bridge** (bought for $1.1B). Neither company said whether Bridge-issued tokens reach XRPL via Privy. The partnership notably carries *Ripple's* RLUSD on Stripe's wallet unit.
- **Coinbase** — authored **x402** (reuses HTTP 402 "Payment Required" for machine-to-machine stablecoin payments; no accounts/API keys). The resulting stack pulls from three competitors: Stripe wallets + Ripple ledger + Coinbase standard.

### [2] Why it matters
- **Third-party distribution for XRPL.** XRPL gets a developer on-ramp from one of the most widely adopted embedded-wallet SDKs — lowers the build cost for fintechs/payments firms to ship XRPL stablecoin payments without users handling keys (as Ramp already does on Privy).
- **Agentic commerce rail.** Server-side wallets + x402 let AI agents hold/spend funds autonomously. Context: AI agents made **>138M (passing 14M/… growing) x402 transactions** per CoinDesk's 2026-10-14 report — this is a land-grab for the agentic-payments standard. ([CoinDesk x402](https://coindesk.com/business/2026/10/14/ai-agents-make-138-million-crypto-payments-as-u-s-dominates-spending-on-ai-models), [XRPL docs](https://xrpl.org/docs/agents/agentic-payments-x402))
- **RLUSD momentum.** RLUSD crossed **$2B+ market cap** (up ~86% in 2026), with ~$1B native on XRPL; third-largest US-regulated stablecoin positioned for GENIUS Act. Distribution via Privy/Stripe widens its reach. ([bitcoin.com](https://news.bitcoin.com/crypto-news/ripples-rlusd-market-cap-crosses-2b-nearly-1b-on-xrp-ledger/))

**Red-team questions**

2. What is actually live today vs promised? (Live: wallet creation, signing, key export. Promised/undated: policy controls, gas sponsorship.)
3. Does Privy carrying *Ripple's* RLUSD conflict with Stripe's own stablecoin via Bridge? Is this a strategic contradiction or deliberate chain-neutrality?
4. Is XRPL's "8M funded accounts / $1T moved" stat self-reported by Privy/Ripple? (Yes — attributed to Privy, not independently verified.)
5. How material is XRPL as a destination vs Privy's existing Solana/Ethereum/base of 75M accounts? Is XRPL a small add-on chain (like the Sept TRON add)?
6. Is the x402/agentic angle real volume or hype? (CoinDesk: 138M agent payments — but how much on XRPL specifically? Likely near-zero at launch.)
7. Does RLUSD even need XRPL distribution, given ~half of its supply is already on Ethereum?
8. Competitive moat: Coinbase CDP, Circle, Dynamic/Fireblocks, Turnkey all offer embedded/agent wallets — does XRPL support differentiate Privy, or is it table-stakes multichain parity?
9. Who benefits most — Ripple (gets a dev on-ramp + validates XRPL) or Privy/Stripe (marginal new chain)? Reads as a bigger win for Ripple's developer story.
10. Any regulatory exposure from Stripe (US bank-charter aspirant) powering agentic/crypto payments on a Ripple-controlled ledger?
11. Key-export claim ("users can export private keys/seed phrases") — does that hold for *server-side* agent wallets too, or only user-facing ones?
12. Three-party stack (Stripe + Ripple + Coinbase's x402) — is the cross-competitor dependency durable, or a fragile alliance?
13. Pricing undisclosed — could XRPL wallets be loss-leader positioning to seed the ledger?

**Importance: 3/5** — Credible, well-sourced, strategically interesting (Stripe exec driving a Ripple deal; ties XRPL into the x402 agentic-payments race and extends RLUSD distribution). But it is an integration/announcement with no named live apps, key features still undated, and XRPL is one more chain in a multichain embedded-wallet field where competitors offer parity. Incremental infrastructure news, not a market-moving event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Embedded / wallet-as-a-service (WaaS) infrastructure sitting on stablecoin rails. No clean TAM for WaaS specifically — "no data"; proxy is the stablecoin market it feeds: stablecoins crossed ~$312bn total market cap in 2026, with transaction volume ~$33tn in 2025 (+72% YoY) and B2B stablecoin payments ~$226bn in 2025 (+733% YoY) (per spark.money / stablecoininsider, as of 2026). For the specific chain: XRPL cites ~8M funded accounts and >$1tn value moved over its ~14-year life; it added ~490k new accounts in H1 2026 to ~8.4M, though "funded accounts ≠ active users" (per primexbt / cryptoninjas, 2026). Structure: the WaaS layer is fragmented and consolidating fast — Turnkey, Privy, Dynamic, Portal, Dfns collectively raised >$150m in 2024–25 (per coingape, 2026). "Why now": the x402 agentic-payments narrative (HTTP 402 revival by Coinbase) plus multi-chain stablecoin issuance are pulling wallet infra toward being the default onboarding primitive; XRPL wants in on a standard built *outside* Ripple's own stack.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: monthly active wallets (MAW), signatures/tx volume, networks supported, per-MAU or per-signature price. Pricing basis diverges — Privy is per-MAU (free ≤499 MAU, then ~$299/mo), Turnkey is per-signature (~$0.10/sig after free tier) (per openfort, 2026). Key players and consolidation: Stripe acquired **Privy** (Jun 2025); **Fireblocks acquired Dynamic** (Oct 2025); **Turnkey** raised a $30m Series B (Bain Capital Crypto), powering Polymarket, Magic Eden, Worldcoin. Basis of competition = distribution + chain coverage + developer UX, not price. Privy's position: **ahead on distribution** via the Stripe/Bridge umbrella (Ramp stablecoin accounts, Klarna [[Klarna signs partnership with Privy to develop crypto wallets]] already on Privy); moat is distribution + switching costs once wallets/keys are embedded in a shipped app `(analysis)`. For Ripple, adding a top wallet-infra provider is a distribution play for XRPL/RLUSD rather than a revenue event — neither side disclosed economics `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Comps & multiples.** This is a partnership, not a round/deal — no transaction value, so no deal multiple to compute ("no data"). Reference valuations for context (round valuations, NOT market caps):
- **Privy** — acquired by Stripe Jun 2025 [[Stripe to acquire crypto wallet provider Privy]]; price not disclosed `[UNSOURCED]`.
- **Ripple** — $500m strategic investment Nov 2025 valued the company at **$40bn** (Fortress/Citadel Securities, per Ripple IR, ripple.com/ripple-press, 2025-11-05); a subsequent **share buyback was struck at ~$50bn** [[Ripple launches share buyback at $50 billion valuation]] (2026-03); SBI pledged up to $1.25bn toward a future Ripple IPO [[SBI CEO pledges up to $1.25 billion for a future Ripple IPO]].
- **Turnkey** — ~$30m Series B; **Bridge** bought by Stripe for **$1.1bn**; **BVNK** — Mastercard agreed to buy for up to **$1.8bn** (Mar 2026) (per stablecoininsider, 2026).
Revenue/EBITDA for these private names is undisclosed → EV/Revenue, P/E = "no data"; distribution not computed (fewer than 3 comparable revenue figures). RLUSD scale for context: ~$2bn market cap, with ~$963m on XRPL vs ~$1.05bn on Ethereum; XRPL-native RLUSD supply +257% QoQ to ~$677m at Q2 2026 close (per yahoo/cryptonomist, 2026).

**Risk flags.**
1. **XRPL adoption / activity gap** — the headline "8M accounts / $1tn moved" is lifetime and account-based, not active-user or current-flow; XRPL has *fewer active accounts* even as value per trade rises (per coindesk, 2026-09). Wallet creation ≠ usage, so the partnership's real traction is unmeasured until apps ship — the note itself admits no count of apps building.
2. **Disintermediation / RLUSD vs the Stripe-Bridge stack** — Privy's owner Stripe runs its own stablecoin issuance via Bridge (USDC/USDT/PYUSD across Base, Solana, Ethereum, Stellar); carrying Ripple's RLUSD puts a Stripe exec (Henri Stern, Privy CEO + Stripe head of crypto) at the center of a *competing* stablecoin's distribution. Which token gets default placement/economics is unstated — Ripple risks being a feature in someone else's funnel, not the beneficiary.
3. **L1 competition — XRPL is the weakest card** — the agentic/stablecoin payments battle is being set on Base, Solana, Ethereum and Stripe's own Tempo L1; x402 is a Coinbase standard. XRPL gaining "a direct line" into x402 is a catch-up move; if developers default to higher-liquidity chains, XRPL wallet volume stays thin regardless of the integration.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Read this as a distribution land-grab, not a re-rating: Stripe/Privy is becoming the neutral embedded-wallet layer across *every* chain (TRON three weeks prior, now XRPL), which commoditizes the L1 and shifts bargaining power to the wallet/rails layer — the value accrues to whoever owns onboarding, not to the ledger. Falsifiable thesis: XRPL's share of stablecoin/agentic payment flow stays a minority vs Base/Solana even with Privy live. Trigger to watch: whether x402 agent payments settle in **RLUSD vs USDC** and whether any named fintech ships an XRPL wallet in production within ~2 quarters — a wave of RLUSD-settled x402 volume would break the thesis.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**No full earnings report in the news** — this is a distribution/infrastructure partnership (Privy embedded wallets on XRPL), not a results release. Neither Privy (private, Stripe-owned) nor Ripple (private) publishes audited quarterly financials, and Ripple's IR "quarterly" cadence is the XRP Markets Report, which was discontinued after Q1 2025 (`q1-2025-xrp-markets-report`, 2025-05-05). Financial-context sizing only below.

**Sizing the two sides (financial context, not earnings).**
- **Ripple** — private, last priced at $40bn in a $500M round led by Fortress + Citadel Securities (2025-11-05 IR: "Ripple Announces $500 Million Strategic Investment... Valuing the Company at $40 Billion"), stepped up to a reported ~$50bn via a $750M buyback (Mar 2026, press). Reported revenue ~$1.2bn (as of the Nov 2025 round), with management guiding a ~$1bn run-rate ex-XRP into end-2026 (public comps/press, label "public, unaudited"). Ripple Payments has cleared >$95bn cumulative volume (press). So this is a well-capitalized counterparty, not a distressed one — the deal is a distribution grab, not a lifeline.
- **RLUSD** (the asset Privy wallets now carry) — circulating market cap ~$2.49bn as of 2026-09-28, +86% YTD from $1.336bn (KuCoin, citing CoinMarketCap). Split ~56% Ethereum / ~44% XRPL (~$1.125bn on XRPL). RLUSD is still an order of magnitude below USDC/USDT and ~3rd-4th-tier (closing on PYUSD per KuCoin). The XRPL carry is incremental supply, not a step-change.
- **Privy** — Stripe-owned (acquired ~$1.1bn, Jun 2025). Scale per Privy: 160M+ accounts across 180+ countries, $15B+ monthly transaction volume, 1,000+ dev teams (privy.io; Goldsky case study cites a 100M+ milestone earlier). Privy is the demand side here — XRPL is one more network added to a multi-chain wallet footprint (Ramp stablecoin accounts, TRON added Sep 2026).

**XRPL scale cited in the deal (vendor-reported, de-PR'd).** Privy/Ripple claim XRPL has 8M+ funded accounts and >$1tn cumulative value moved; Ripple says AI-agent payments have hit 13M and now ~25% of network activity (24/7 Wall St, 2026-10-13). These are cumulative/lifetime and self-reported — not run-rate revenue; treat as marketing scale, not a financial metric. No per-wallet pricing, no volume commitment, and no revenue-share terms were disclosed by either side.

**Verdict / thesis-flags (financial-context read, no beat/miss).**
- *No numbers that move either company's P&L.* Zero deal economics disclosed (pricing, minimums, rev-share) — materiality to Ripple's ~$1.2bn revenue or Privy/Stripe is immaterial on day one. Fact → no committed volume → can't size revenue → this is optionality, not booked growth.
- *RLUSD distribution, not RLUSD demand.* Adding RLUSD to Privy's 160M-account surface widens where RLUSD *can* be held; it does not create issuance. With only ~44% of a $2.49bn float on XRPL, the swing factor is developer adoption Privy itself flagged as unproven ("doesn't say how many apps are already building").
- *Watch:* whether Ripple routes its own ~$95bn-volume payment products through Privy wallets (an open question in the note) — that would be the first path from this partnership to actual volume/revenue.
