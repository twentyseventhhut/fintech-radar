Coinbase has joined a four-day operation with U.S. and UK law enforcement to disrupt West African scam networks by tracing illicit funds, supporting asset freezes and sharing intelligence.
The Oct. 6–9 London operation targeted West African scam networks and the services supporting them.
Coinbase’s investigators traced financial activity and shared intelligence with law enforcement and industry partners.
Targeted schemes included fake crypto investments, police impersonation, and staged arrests conducted over video calls.
The operation involved the DOJ’s Scam Center Strike Force and the UK’s National Crime Agency.
Coinbase said in an Oct. 13 post that its Global Intelligence team participated in Joint Disruption Week in London alongside Tech Against Scams, the UK’s National Crime Agency, the U.S. Department of Justice’s Scam Center Strike Force and international law enforcement partners.
According to the exchange, participating teams targeted the accounts, digital infrastructure and payment systems used by criminal networks operating in West Africa. In its accompanying statement, the company also identified African law enforcement partners among the participants and said investigative work remained ongoing.
Coinbase traces funds behind West African scam networks
During the London operation, Coinbase said its investigators followed illicit financial activity, supported efforts to freeze assets connected to scam networks and passed information to law enforcement in real time.
The company described an investigative process that connected blockchain transfers with evidence from fake profiles, messaging accounts and websites. By combining financial records with information held by other services, the exchange said participating teams could identify connections between activities that might otherwise appear unrelated.
Among the schemes targeted, Coinbase identified fraudulent cryptocurrency investments, criminals pretending to be law enforcement officers, and fake video-call arrests used to pressure victims into sending money.
In the exchange’s account, scammers use social media to find potential victims, messaging services to develop trust, and financial services to move stolen funds. Technology and telecommunications companies joined financial firms and police during the operation, which the NCA hosted in partnership with the DOJ’s Strike Force, according to the post.
Coinbase Vice President Leah Bressack pointed to the company’s earlier work against Southeast Asian scam compounds:

 “When we disrupted scam compounds in Southeast Asia earlier this year, we proved that coordinated action across industry and law enforcement works.” 

Bressack described the effort as “sustained pressure,” while Coinbase said criminal networks were expanding into West Africa as authorities pursued their operations in Southeast Asia.
U.S. and UK agencies agree to pursue shared scam cases
Ahead of the London event, the DOJ announced on Sep. 3 that American and British authorities had signed an agreement covering joint investigations into cryptocurrency investment fraud and other online scams.
As crypto.news previously reported, the joint scam-center agreement involved the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the District of Columbia, the Crown Prosecution Service of England and Wales, and the NCA.
Under the arrangement, the DOJ said investigators would exchange information about organized crime groups, pursue common targets and decide which jurisdiction should prosecute cases when investigations overlap. The department had identified several cases of shared interest and announced plans for an October disruption event with private companies in London.
For American victims, the department’s figures showed reported losses from online investment fraud reaching $8.65 billion in 2025, up 89% from $4.57 billion in 2023. The DOJ attributed the figures to the FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center and cautioned that unreported cases could leave actual losses higher.
According to the department, the Strike Force’s remit covers cryptocurrency investment fraud, human trafficking and money laundering connected to scam centers.
Earlier operations targeted crypto wallets and online accounts
In an earlier operation covered on June 4, Coinbase said it had frozen over $3 million in cryptocurrency connected to Southeast Asian scam networks. The exchange reported sharing intelligence with the DOJ, Meta, Microsoft, Starlink and international police agencies.
During that effort, Meta said more than 1.4 million Facebook and Instagram accounts, pages and groups were disabled. Microsoft suspended about 20,000 fraudulent accounts, while Starlink terminated connections for thousands of kits linked to unlawful use, according to the companies.
By September, U.S. investigators were also pursuing businesses accused of supplying services to scam operators. In a Sep. 9 announcement, the DOJ said an operation against Xinbi Guarantee and its vendors had restrained over $52 million in cryptocurrency.
The department reported seizing two wallets containing approximately $12 million and seeking restraints against another 47 wallets. According to prosecutors, vendors advertised money laundering, fraudulent investment websites and recruitment services for Southeast Asian scam compounds.
On Sep. 7, a federal court in Washington authorized the seizure of Telegram channels hosting the marketplace, the DOJ said. Investigators reported tracing funds belonging to U.S. victims to vendors advertising laundering services.
FinCEN identifies $12.7 billion in suspicious scam activity
In separate U.S. coverage published Sep. 4, the Treasury Department’s Financial Crimes Enforcement Network flagged $12.7 billion in suspicious activity associated with digital asset investment scams.
FinCEN’s analysis covered 33,904 Bank Secrecy Act reports submitted by approximately 1,300 financial institutions between Sep. 8, 2023, and Dec. 31, 2025. According to the agency, the reported activity involved victims across all 50 states and several U.S. territories.
The agency cautioned that suspicious activity totals do not directly measure victim losses because filings can include attempted transfers, reporting errors, and transactions reported by multiple institutions.
Within the dataset, FinCEN said money services businesses, mostly digital asset firms, submitted 55% of reports and identified $5.5 billion in suspicious activity. Banks reported another $6.4 billion.
Although at least 22 digital assets appeared in the filings, blockchain analysis cited by FinCEN found that scam proceeds were typically converted into stablecoins, almost exclusively USDT, before moving through decentralized finance services or exchanges outside the United States.
FinCEN also reported cases in which victims financed transfers through retirement savings, home equity credit, second mortgages, and personal loans.

# Context-enrichment: Coinbase joins US and UK police to target West African scam networks
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**FRESHNESS: fresh.** Genuinely new event: the Oct 6–9, 2026 "Joint Disruption Week" at the NCA's London HQ, announced via Coinbase's Oct 13 blog ("Consumer Protection Tuesday: Following the money. Stopping the scammers."). It is the West-African sequel to the June 2026 Southeast-Asia action ([[Coinbase Freezes $3M | web]]; see also [[US Justice Department charges scam-center operators]]), not a reprint of it. NOT a duplicate of any prior corpus note. NB: the note's own crypto.news source is thin — it omits every operational outcome figure (65 victims protected, $415K saved, 28 arrests, $11M flagged, 117 trafficking victims, 3.7M accounts, Binance's parallel participation). Those are added below from Coinbase's blog + DOJ/Meta/press coverage.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- **The event:** A four-day "Joint Disruption Week" (Oct 6–9, 2026) hosted at the UK National Crime Agency's London HQ, convening the NCA, the DOJ's Scam Center Strike Force / FBI, African law enforcement (Ghana, Nigeria), and private firms under the **Tech Against Scams** coalition — Meta, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI, plus exchanges **Coinbase AND Binance**. Announced Oct 13.
- **Coinbase's actual contribution (de-PR'd):** on-chain tracing of illicit flows, supporting asset freezes, and real-time intelligence sharing. Coinbase-specific claimed outcome: **>65 active scam victims proactively identified and >$415K in losses stopped before they occurred** (Coinbase blog). These are modest, Coinbase-attributable numbers — honest, not inflated.
- **The broader operation's outcomes (NOT Coinbase-attributable — note the gap):** **28 arrests** (19 in Sasaabi near Accra, Ghana; 303 devices seized), **117 human-trafficking victims identified/rescued** awaiting repatriation to Nigeria, **~3.7M fraudulent accounts/pages removed** (Meta-led), and **>$11M in crypto flagged as potentially freezable** (DOJ). The FBI cited an **$17B** scam-center tally and the DOJ's IC3 figure of **$8.65B** reported US online-investment-fraud losses in 2025 (up 89% vs 2023's $4.57B).
- **Why structured this way / what it reveals:** the PR frame ("Coinbase joins police") blends Coinbase's small direct contribution ($415K) with the whole coalition's large numbers ($11M flagged, 3.7M accounts, 28 arrests). Most of the headline impact is Meta's (accounts) and law enforcement's (arrests/trafficking); Coinbase's measurable share is on-chain tracing of a few hundred $K. This is a **reputation/compliance play** dressed as an enforcement win — valuable, but the de-PR'd delta is small.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Binance** was in the SAME London operation — Coinbase has no exclusive here; both exchanges contribute blockchain intelligence. Binance runs its own law-enforcement-training / FIU-cooperation programs at larger global scale.
- **Tether** is the structurally pivotal peer: FinCEN found scam proceeds are "almost exclusively" converted to **USDT** before exiting via DeFi/offshore exchanges. Tether says it has frozen **$4.2B** over crime links ([[Tether says it has frozen $4.2 billion over crime links]]) — orders of magnitude above Coinbase's $415K / $3M figures. The real choke-point is the stablecoin issuer, not the fiat on-ramp.
- **Chainalysis / TRM** are the actual tracing-tech incumbents ([[The 2026 Crypto Crime Report from Chainalysisestimates that crypto scams and fraud]], [[Chainalysis maps crypto flows to suspected human trafficking]]); Coinbase's "Global Intelligence team" is partly doing in-house what these vendors sell.
- **Prior DOJ Strike Force actions dwarf this one:** [[DOJ Scam Center Strike Force seizes over $578 million in crypto]] (Mar 2026, $578M+); [[DOJ seizes $15B bitcoin from Cambodia pig-butchering scam]] (Oct 2025). Against those, the West-Africa week's $11M flagged is early-stage.
- **Position:** Coinbase is a willing, repeat private partner (parity with Binance), not a differentiator. **Why:** on-chain tracing is increasingly table-stakes; the competitive edge is regulatory goodwill, not capability.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Coinbase has built a deliberate "good-citizen / compliance-first" brand arc: OCC national trust charter application ([[Coinbase applies for OCC national trust charter]]), the Microsoft Tycoon-2FA phishing takedown ([[Coinbase and Microsoft disrupt Tycoon 2FA phishing platform]]), the Ripple/North-Korea-hacker cooperation ([[Ripple partners with Coinbase to counter North Korean hackers]]), and the June-2026 SE-Asia freeze ($3M). VP Leah Bressack explicitly frames West Africa as "sustained pressure" following SE Asia.
- **Why the company acts this way (structural logic):** Coinbase is a US-listed exchange whose core business depends on regulatory trust and banking access. After a bruising 2026 (steep Q1 loss, ~700 layoffs — [[Coinbase posts steep Q1 2026 loss as revenue falls]], [[Coinbase cuts about 700 jobs in AI-driven restructuring]]) and a UK ad banned for being misleading ([[Coinbase 'everything's fine' crypto ad banned by watchdog]]), visible law-enforcement cooperation is cheap insurance for its charter ambitions and its UK/EU regulatory standing. Fighting scams also directly protects its own users (the $415K saved are Coinbase customers), so the incentive is genuine, not purely PR.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **What's actually new:** geographic expansion of the public-private model from SE Asia to **West Africa (Ghana/Nigeria)** — tracking criminal networks as they relocate under SE-Asia pressure. The **mechanism delta** vs incumbents in one sentence: a live, co-located week where blockchain tracers sit beside platform/telecom data and police to fuse off-chain identity with on-chain flows in real time (vs the usual asynchronous subpoena loop).
- **Traction (real numbers, not announcements):** >65 victims protected, >$415K saved (Coinbase); 28 arrests, 117 trafficking victims, 3.7M accounts, $11M flagged (coalition). These are concrete and dated — better than a "will explore" partnership. But Coinbase's own slice is small.
- **Why the value-add is only partly real (deeper):** the margin/choke-point in the stack sits with (a) the **stablecoin issuer (Tether/USDT)** that can freeze at scale, and (b) **Meta** which removes the top-of-funnel accounts. Coinbase sits mid-stack as a tracer/on-ramp; it can flag and freeze what touches its own rails but cannot stop USDT moving through DeFi/offshore venues. So the durable value-add is **brand/compliance capital**, not a defensible enforcement moat.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Plans:** DOJ–UK signed a Sep 3, 2026 agreement for joint crypto-fraud investigations (US Attorney DC, CPS, NCA) with jurisdiction-sharing; investigative work "remains ongoing," so more arrests/seizures likely. Expect continued quarterly "Disruption Week" cadence and new regions.
- **Regulatory backdrop:** FinCEN flagged **$12.7B** in suspicious digital-asset-scam activity (Sep 2023–Dec 2025, 33,904 BSA reports); losses rising ~89% since 2023. This fuels pressure for stablecoin/exchange AML obligations — a double-edged sword for Coinbase (goodwill vs compliance cost).
- **Counterintuitive second-order effect:** aggressive, well-publicized cooperation raises the baseline expectation for ALL exchanges; it commoditizes "being a good partner" and shifts regulatory scrutiny toward whoever cooperates *least* (and toward stablecoin issuers). Coinbase's best outcome is defensive — charter/approvals goodwill — rather than any revenue upside. **Importance 3/5:** real, dated, cross-verified event with genuine (if modest) Coinbase-specific traction and strong brand/compliance signaling; capped because Coinbase's direct impact is small and the capability is becoming table-stakes.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** This sits in crypto-exchange compliance/trust positioning, not a product line — "regulatory goodwill as a moat" rather than revenue. The scam problem it addresses is large and growing: DOJ cited reported online investment-fraud losses of $8.65bn in 2025, up 89% from $4.57bn in 2023 (per FBI IC3, via crypto.news, as of 2026-10); FinCEN separately flagged $12.7bn of suspicious digital-asset scam activity across 33,904 BSA reports Sep-2023–Dec-2025 (per crypto.news). Structure: enforcement is fragmented across agencies (DOJ Strike Force, NCA, FinCEN) and private firms (exchanges, blockchain-analytics vendors, Meta/Microsoft/Starlink) — Coinbase inserts itself as the on-chain tracing node. Why now: (1) scam proceeds now convert "almost exclusively" to USDT before hitting DeFi/offshore venues (per FinCEN, via crypto.news) — stablecoin rails make tracing both necessary and tractable; (2) Coinbase holds an OCC national trust charter application and is courting institutional/government trust (see [[Coinbase applies for OCC national trust charter]]), so visible law-enforcement cooperation is cheap regulatory goodwill ahead of that. Second-order: fighting scams also protects Coinbase's own subscription/stablecoin revenue base, where USDC/USDT flows are the laundering vector.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for the compliance/analytics layer: funds traced/frozen, # of law-enforcement partnerships, and (for vendors) ARR and government revenue share — Coinbase discloses none of these for this program beyond anecdotes ("over $3M frozen" in the June SE-Asia op, per crypto.news). Two distinct competitor sets: (a) other exchanges on trust-positioning — Binance, which spent 2025 rehabilitating after a $283M settlement and a CZ pardon (see [[Binance pays $283 million, denies causing crypto crash]], [[Trump pardons Binance founder Changpeng Zhao]]); (b) blockchain-analytics vendors Chainalysis and TRM Labs, who sell the tracing capability Coinbase is building in-house. Basis of competition here is distribution and credibility with agencies, not price. Recent moves: TRM Labs reported North Korea hackers took 76% of 2026 crypto-hack value (see [[TRM Labs North Korea hackers took 76% of 2026 crypto hack value]]); Chainalysis mapped flows to human trafficking and extended a compliance partnership with BVNK (see [[Chainalysis maps crypto flows to suspected human trafficking]], [[Chainalysis and BVNK extend self-hosted payments compliance partnership]]). Coinbase's position: niche-but-leading among exchanges on public enforcement cooperation (it cites a prior SE-Asia compound disruption as precedent, per crypto.news); moat is intangible — reputation/regulatory trust and proprietary on-chain + KYC data linking blockchain transfers to real identities (analysis).
