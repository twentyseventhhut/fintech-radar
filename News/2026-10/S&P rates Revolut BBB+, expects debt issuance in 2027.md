#### Overview

*   Revolut’s strong capital position and profitability, predominantly low-risk liquid asset base, wide product and geographical breadth, strong brand, and scalable technology platform support its credit profile. Despite the bank's success in building a profitable cross-border business, we note risks and operational complexity associated with its rapid growth, competition from well-entrenched incumbents, and the challenge of growing primary customer relationships.
*   We view U.K.-based operating company Revolut Ltd. as a core part of the Revolut group, reflecting its role as a central service provider to other operating entities and its function centralizing the group's liquidity management.
*   We assigned our 'BBB+' long-term issuer credit rating to Revolut Ltd.
*   The positive outlook reflects our expectations that the group will likely build an additional loss-absorption capacity (ALAC) buffer over the next two to three years to meet its minimum requirement for own funds and eligible liabilities (MREL), which could provide additional credit support to the group's operating entities, including Revolut Ltd. 

#### Rating Action

On Oct. 22, 2026, S&P Global Ratings assigned its 'BBB+' long-term issuer credit rating to Revolut Ltd. The outlook is positive.

#### Rationale

**In our view, Revolut's strong consumer brand, scalable technology platform, and increasingly diversified product offering supports its business position.**As of October 2026, Revolut served about 80 million customers across 41 countries, having more than tripled its customer base since 2022. To expand its business, Revolut has leveraged its advanced technology platform, meaningfully expanded its product breadth, and prioritized user experience. Over the past 12 months growth remained robust, underpinned by the group's strategy execution to increase its market share in the EU and the U.K., enter new markets, scale lending and business banking, and increase customer engagement. We think that the group's technology platform, supported by in-house development of most critical systems and a growing AI adoption, will remain an important enabler of rapid product development and geographical expansion in the next two to three years.

Nevertheless, Revolut's share of primary banking relationship, customer monetization, and market share remain below those of established banks in many of the markets it operates in, while its lending franchise remains small. Although we see an improvement in customer retention and growing revenue per active user, we think Revolut's challenge is to become the primary bank for more of its users, especially in jurisdictions with strong customer loyalty to incumbents.

**Revolut's regulatory infrastructure, including full banking licenses in the U.K., the EU, Australia, and Mexico, supports its scale and competitive advantage compared with other neobanks.**In March 2026, the Prudential Regulation Authority granted Revolut a full U.K. banking license. We view this as a significant milestone that could strengthen customer trust and enable Revolut to widen its product offering for 14 million customers in the U.K., including through on-balance sheet Financial Services Compensation Scheme-protected deposits and selected lending products. We think that Revolut's efforts to receive a banking license in the U.S., New Zealand, and a number of countries in Latin America may increase its client base and support growth, although we note intense competition from well-established incumbent banks and other neobanks in these markets. At the same time, Revolut's fast expansion across multiple jurisdictions and regulatory regimes may heighten its operational complexity and compliance risks and require sustainably high investments to mitigate them.

**Revolut's business model is predominantly fee-driven, while plans to grow its lending business will create an additional source of revenue growth.**In 2025, the group's operating revenue (S&P Global Ratings-adjusted metric) increased by 42.4% and reached £3.67 billion, with fees and commissions representing 73%. Subscription revenue showed the fastest growth last year at 67% and represented 21% of total fees, while the diversity of the fee income mix improved, with no income stream generating more than 30% of total fees. Despite substantial loan portfolio growth in 2025 (+119.6% year on year), the bank's lending franchise remains small, with interest income from customer loans generating just 16% of the total interest income received last year (the rest came from treasury investments and balances with financial institutions). In our view, further expansion of the lending business could support revenue growth and increase monetization of Revolut's existing customer base in the coming years. But successfully penetrating segments such as mortgage lending will likely prove more difficult than its current transactional-driven services.

**We view Revolut's very strong capital position--with our forecast risk-adjusted capital ratio exceeding 30% over the next 24 months--as a key rating strength.**The bank's large capital buffer, low-risk asset base, and robust profitability will continue to support capital adequacy over the rating horizon, with a common equity Tier 1 ratio of 47.8% as of June 30, 2026--one of the highest among rated peers. We expect substantial expected growth in fees and commission (25%-30% per year) and economies of scale should support Revolut's strong profitability and internal capital generation, with forecast return on average equity of close to 30% over the next two years. We expect Revolut's assets will remain primarily low risk, predominantly made up of cash held in central banks, investments in sovereign securities, and balances with highly-rated financial institutions. Despite plans to grow its loan portfolio, we expect Revolut's customer loans will remain below 20% of total assets in the next three years.

**We assess Revolut's track record of managing nonfinancial risks as adequate, although its exposure to these risks will remain high, in our view.**The group has developed advanced systems to effectively control and mitigate financial and nonfinancial risks. Nevertheless, Revolut's business model brings material operational, regulatory, and compliance risks that we reflect in our risk position assessment. In 2025, the European Central Bank imposed restrictions on releasing new products in the European Economic Area, requiring a more rigorous assessment of the risks that these new products may have on the group's capital, funding, and liquidity. We also note a recent data breach in Europe, which affected around 680 Revolut customers. Although these episodes have not materially slowed Revolut's growth or damaged its franchise in Europe, in our view, they highlight the group's increased exposure to regulatory risks.

**Revolut's credit loss performance has remained broadly stable over the past two years, but its underwriting standards remain untested through a credit downturn.**In 2025, Revolut's cost of risk reduced to 3.8%, from 4.0% in 2024, reflecting the increasing maturity of the group's lending experience and credit risk assessment. We anticipate Revolut’s loan portfolio will more than double in 2026 and again in 2027 from a low base since management plans to selectively expand its unsecured retail lending and develop mortgage lending in certain jurisdictions. We see a risk that this high lending growth across multiple jurisdictions may increase credit losses over the next two to three years. That said, considering the small size of the loan portfolio and strong earnings generation from other sources, Revolut has significant capacity to absorb higher credit losses if they arise.

**In our view, Revolut's high share of liquid assets mitigates the predominance of on-demand deposits in its liabilities.**The group's funding profile mainly consists of personal and business current accounts, which represented 69% of the group's funding sources as of year-end 2025, down from 79% in 2024. Positively, Revolut's current accounts are granular and substantially operational in nature, with a high insured element. We expect Revolut will continue to build its deposit franchise with an increasing proportion of interest-bearing savings accounts. We note that Revolut holds a substantial portion of liquid assets, which represented close to 90% of its total assets. This explains the bank's very high regulatory net stable funding ratio of 333%, and liquidity coverage ratio of 176% as of mid-year 2026. We view Revolut's management of funding and liquidity risks as prudent and conservative and expect this will remain the case.

**Revolut Ltd. is a core operating subsidiary for the group.**The entity acts as a central service provider to 32 operating entities of the Revolut group, developing and commercializing all major banking and money management products and services for the group, consolidating major technological developments and holding the intellectual property for the entire group. Revolut Ltd. also consolidates the group's liquidity management, including managing surplus liquidity for other operating entities, facilitating customers' cross-border payments and settlements with card schemes, and acting as the face for the group on the foreign currency market. In our view, the entity will remain closely integrated with the rest of the group, including strategy, governance, and risk management after the migration of U.K. clients from Revolut Ltd. to Revolut Bank UK Ltd. is complete by the end of 2026.

**We expect Revolut Ltd. will play a key role in any recovery and resolution scenario for the group.**The entity is included within the group's recovery and resolution planning and will support operational continuity in resolution, continuing to provide critical services and managing intragroup liquidity flows. We believe Revolut Ltd.'s creditors will benefit from the group's future bail-inable liabilities. We therefore equalize our rating on Revolut Ltd. with our 'bbb+' group credit profile on Revolut.

**Revolut's requirement to build an MREL buffer over the next two to three years should support Revolut Ltd.'s senior creditors.**Revolut's balance sheet exceeds the Bank of England's £40 billion threshold for a bail-in-led resolution strategy, with nonoperating holding company Revolut Group Holding Ltd. (RGHL; not rated) likely to act as the single point of entry. Accordingly, we expect RGHL will start issuing senior unsecured debt and build an ALAC-eligible debt buffer in 2027. Based on the likely MREL requirement, we expect the buffer will likely exceed 4% of the group's expected S&P Global Ratings' risk-weighted assets (RWAs), which should support a one-notch rating uplift for Revolut Ltd. We raise this threshold above the 3% baseline in our methodology due to likely maturity concentrations in the ALAC buffer in the early stages of RGHL's MREL issuance.

#### Outlook

The positive outlook on Revolut Ltd. reflects our expectations that the bank's senior creditors should benefit from the planned buildup of ALAC buffers over the next two to three years to meet the group's MREL requirement.

##### Downside scenario

We could revise the outlook to stable if we think Revolut is unlikely to reach our adjusted ALAC threshold of 4% of S&P Global Ratings' RWAs. A deterioration of Revolut's group credit profile may prompt a similar rating action or lower the rating on Revolut Ltd.

##### Upside scenario

We could raise the rating on Revolut Ltd. over the next 12-24 months if RGHL starts issuing bail-in debt and we are certain confident the group's ALAC buffer will exceed our adjusted 4% threshold of S&P Global Ratings' RWAs.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 22 Oct 2026 S&P Global Ratings assigned **Revolut Ltd.** a **'BBB+' long-term issuer credit rating with a positive outlook** — Revolut's first-ever rating from a major agency. S&P equalizes the rating with its 'bbb+' group credit profile and expects the non-operating holding company **Revolut Group Holdings Ltd. (RGHL, unrated)** to begin issuing **senior unsecured debt in 2027** to build an ALAC/MREL buffer. ([S&P source](https://www.spglobal.com/ratings/en/regulatory/article/-/view/type/HTML/id/3647875); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-22/uk-fintech-revolut-expected-to-issue-debt-in-2027-s-p-says))

**[1] Why it matters.** BBB+ is **investment grade** (3 notches above junk). For a neobank this is a landmark: it validates Revolut's transition from a VC-funded fintech to a rated bank that can tap the public debt markets, lowering its future funding cost and institutionalizing its balance sheet. It is a direct dividend of the [[Revolut's full UK banking licence held up by risk concerns|full UK banking licence]] finally granted by the PRA in March 2026, and it reinforces the credibility of the ~$75bn→$100-120bn equity story behind [[Revolut raises $3B at $75 billion valuation|the $75bn round]] and the [[Revolut plans secondary share sale at $100-120 billion valuation|$100-120bn secondary]]/[[Revolut explores dual listing in London and New York|dual-listing]] plans.

**[2] The mechanics (SACP build-up).** S&P's stand-alone credit profile (SACP) = 'bbb+', anchored at 'bbb+':
- **Capital & earnings: Very Strong (+2)** — CET1 ratio **47.8%** (Jun-2026), one of the highest among rated peers; forecast RAC >30% over 24 months; forecast ROE ~30%.
- **Business position: Moderate (-1)** — 80m customers / 41 countries, 2025 adj. operating revenue **£3.67bn (+42.4%)**, fees 73% of revenue; but low primary-banking share and a small lending franchise.
- **Risk position: Moderate (-1)** — rapid multi-jurisdiction growth, untested underwriting (cost of risk 3.8% in 2025), ECB new-product restrictions in the EEA, a 680-customer EU data breach.
- **Funding & liquidity: Adequate/Adequate (0)** — ~90% liquid assets, NSFR **333%**, LCR **176%**; deposits 69% of funding (down from 79%).

**[3] The 2027 debt story.** Revolut's balance sheet exceeds the **Bank of England's £40bn bail-in-led resolution threshold**, with RGHL as the likely single point of entry. S&P therefore expects RGHL to start issuing senior unsecured, **ALAC-eligible** debt in **2027** to meet its **MREL** requirement. S&P set a **4% of RWA** ALAC threshold (above its 3% baseline, due to early maturity concentration); clearing it would deliver a **one-notch uplift** — the basis for the positive outlook. UK clients are migrating from Revolut Ltd. to Revolut Bank UK Ltd. by end-2026. ([BoE MREL update](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2025/july/boe-announces-measures-to-promote-banking-resilience))

**[4] Peer / sector frame.** Most neobank peers (Nubank, Monzo, Starling, N26, Chime) are not publicly rated by S&P, making Revolut an early mover into rated public debt. S&P published "How We Rate Fintech Companies" (7 Oct 2026) and "Future Of Banking: Neobanks Come Of Age" (1 Jul 2026) alongside this action, signalling a broader push to rate the sector. Outlook could revert to stable if the 4% ALAC buffer looks unreachable; upside if RGHL issues bail-in debt and the buffer is secured within 12-24 months.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team / challenge**

1. **Is BBB+ actually investment grade?** Answered: Yes — BBB+ is 3 notches above the BB+ junk boundary; lowest-but-one tier of investment grade. Confirmed by Bloomberg/panews framing "first investment-grade rating."
2. **Who exactly is rated?** Answered: **Revolut Ltd.** (the core UK operating company), NOT the group holdco. RGHL (the issuer of future debt) is explicitly *unrated*. Easy to conflate "Revolut rated BBB+" with the whole group.
3. **Is the debt issuance confirmed or just S&P's expectation?** Open/caution: It is S&P's *forecast* ("we expect RGHL will start issuing… in 2027"), not a committed Revolut plan. No mandate, size, or coupon disclosed. Headline risk of overstating certainty.
4. **Is the positive outlook contingent?** Answered: Yes — the one-notch uplift depends on RGHL hitting a 4% RWA ALAC buffer within 2-3 years. Downside scenario = outlook to stable if that looks unlikely.
5. **How solid is the 47.8% CET1 and ~30% ROE?** Answered (per S&P): Jun-2026 CET1 47.8%; these are S&P-adjusted/forecast figures, not audited year-end disclosures — reasonable but agency projections.
6. **Does the small lending book undercut the rating?** Answered: S&P flags it as a weakness (Moderate business position, -1); lending is <20% of assets, interest from customer loans only 16% of interest income. Rating rests on capital, not credit intermediation.
7. **Untested underwriting risk?** Open: S&P notes underwriting "untested through a credit downturn" with loan book set to >double in 2026 and 2027; a genuine open tail risk.
8. **Regulatory/operational overhang?** Answered: ECB EEA new-product restrictions (2025) + 680-customer EU data breach cited in Risk position; S&P judges them not franchise-damaging but flags elevated compliance risk.
9. **Does this change Revolut's valuation/IPO math?** Partly open: Supports the funding/cost-of-debt narrative behind the $100-120bn secondary and dual-listing talk, but S&P does not rate equity; no direct valuation read-through.
10. **Are peers rated for comparison?** Answered: No major S&P public ratings found for Nubank/Monzo/Starling/N26/Chime — so "peer neobank ratings" context is thin; Revolut is an outlier/first-mover.
11. **Is £40bn balance sheet / BoE threshold accurate?** Answered: Corroborated by Bloomberg and BoE MREL framework (indicative thresholds raised toward £25-40bn total assets); Revolut sits at/above the bail-in resolution threshold.
12. **Duplicate check?** Answered: No prior note in the vault reports an S&P rating for Revolut; prior notes cover licence, valuation, listing, expansion — this is a new, distinct event. FRESH.
13. **Could the rating be withdrawn/revised quickly?** Open: New rating with positive outlook; stable if ALAC path falters. Monitor 2027 issuance execution.
14. **Source quality?** Answered: Primary = S&P's own rating action article (highest quality); secondary = Bloomberg + crypto/aggregator flashes (lower quality, used only for framing).

**Importance: 4/5** — A genuine milestone: first investment-grade rating for a major neobank, opening public-debt access and institutionalizing Revolut's balance sheet ahead of its IPO. Docked from 5 because the headline-grabbing debt issuance is S&P's forecast rather than a committed, sized transaction, and the uplift is still contingent on a 2027 ALAC buffer.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Neobank credit ratings are a new-ish frontier: until recently most challengers were unrated because they either didn't issue debt or lacked a full banking licence. The structural shift is that the largest neobanks are now transitioning from fee-driven pre-paid/e-money models into deposit-taking, lending, debt-issuing banks — which forces them into the rated-bank universe. Revolut's BBB+ (assigned by S&P on Oct 22, 2026, positive outlook) is a direct product of its March 2026 full UK banking licence (per note) plus its balance sheet crossing the Bank of England's £40bn bail-in-resolution threshold (per S&P), which triggers an MREL requirement and hence expected senior-unsecured/ALAC debt issuance by holdco RGHL in 2027. "Why now": once a neobank is large enough for resolution planning, it must build loss-absorbing debt — i.e. a rating and a bond curve become mandatory, not optional. Barriers to this tier are high: capital, a full licence, and a resolution-grade balance sheet. Investment-grade threshold = BBB-/Baa3; Revolut at BBB+ sits three notches into IG.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for a scaling neobank-bank: operating revenue growth, fee mix/diversification, CET1 ratio, cost of risk, loan/asset mix, and primary-banking-relationship penetration. Key players by rating/scale:
- **Revolut** — BBB+/Positive (S&P). FY2025 S&P-adjusted operating revenue £3.67bn (+42.4% YoY), fees/commissions 73% of revenue, CET1 47.8% (Jun-30-2026), cost of risk 3.8%, ~80m customers/41 countries (per S&P in note). This is an unusually conservative balance sheet for a fintech: ~90% liquid assets, NSFR 333%, LCR 176%.
- **Nubank (Nu Holdings)** — S&P global scale 'BB' (upgraded May 2024), 'brAAA' local; ~$72bn market cap, Q2'26 gross revenue ~$5.9bn (+39% YoY). Its global rating is effectively capped by Brazil's sovereign — a structural ceiling Revolut (UK/EU domicile) does not face.
- **Chime** — public (NASDAQ: CHYM), ~$11.4bn market cap (Oct 2026), ~10.4m members; unrated by major agencies, US-only, still on a bank-partner (Stride) model not a full charter.
- **N26** — private, ~$3bn (May 2026, down from $9bn peak 2021); first FY net profit 2025 (€1.6m on €501.6m revenue); unrated.
- **Monzo / Starling** — no public S&P/Moody's/Fitch rating located; UK challengers that have not yet entered the rated-debt universe.
Basis of competition: product breadth + distribution + brand; Revolut's moat is its in-house tech platform, multi-jurisdiction licence stack (UK/EU/Australia/Mexico) and cross-border FX franchise (analysis). Positioning: **ahead** of the neobank cohort on both rating (BBB+ vs Nubank BB, rest unrated) and capital strength, but **behind incumbents** on primary-relationship penetration and lending maturity (per S&P).

**Comps & multiples.** Revolut's rating is the headline comp: at **BBB+** it sits **two notches above Nubank's BB** global rating despite Nubank being larger by revenue — reflecting Revolut's non-EM domicile (no sovereign cap) and very strong capital, vs Nubank's sovereign-ceiling drag. Valuation cross-check (private vs public, not a like-for-like multiple):
- Revolut: ~**$75bn** private valuation (secondary share sale finalised Nov 2025, up from $45bn Aug 2024). On FY2025 revenue ≈ $4bn-$6bn (note's other S&P figure £3.67bn adj.; a separate base note cites $6bn revenue / $2.3bn profit FY2025): price/sales ≈ $75bn / ~$5bn ≈ **~15x** (rough, mixing private valuation and non-standardised revenue — treat as indicative only, not a clean multiple).
- Nubank: ~**$72bn** public market cap on ~$5.9bn quarterly (≈$20-24bn annualised) gross revenue → P/S ≈ **~3x** on a public, EM-discounted, IFRS-revenue basis.
The two headline numbers ($75bn vs $72bn) are near-identical but NOT comparable: Revolut's is an illiquid private secondary mark; Nubank's is a liquid public cap. P/E / EV/EBITDA not computed — Revolut is private with no standardised public filings and the revenue bases differ by definition. Internal comps: [[Revolut valuation rises to $75 billion in secondary share sale]], [[Revolut reports record $2.3bn profit and $6bn revenue for 2025]], [[Revolut secures full UK banking licence]], [[Nubank surpasses 112 million customers in Brazil]].

**Risk flags.**
1. **Rating is contingent, not banked.** The positive outlook and any one-notch uplift depend on RGHL actually issuing ALAC-eligible senior debt in 2027 and building a buffer >4% of RWAs. S&P explicitly says it could revise to **stable** if that 4% threshold looks unreachable — so the "positive" is an IOU on a 2027 funding event that hasn't happened. Second-order: a weaker-than-expected debut bond market reception could stall the uplift.
2. **Untested lending through a cycle + rapid book growth.** Cost of risk 3.8% and S&P flags underwriting "untested through a credit downturn," with the loan book set to more than double in 2026 and again 2027 across multiple jurisdictions (incl. new mortgage lending). Rapid multi-geo credit expansion is the classic point where fast-growing lenders discover mispriced risk.
3. **Operational/regulatory/compliance load.** S&P pins Revolut's risk position at "Moderate (-1)": 2025 ECB product-launch restrictions in the EEA and a European data breach (~680 customers). Fast expansion across ~41 countries and multiple regulators structurally raises compliance/operational risk that requires sustained high spend — a margin and headline-risk drag.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is the moment a neobank crosses into the rated-bank-debt universe: Revolut is now investment-grade and will build a bond curve in 2027, which re-rates it from "fintech" toward "bank" in how debt/credit investors price it (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if RGHL issues senior unsecured debt in 2027 and the ALAC buffer clears S&P's 4% RWA threshold, expect a one-notch upgrade to A-; trigger to watch = the first RGHL debt issuance and MREL buildup. Thesis breaks if the 2027 issuance slips or the buffer undershoots — S&P then moves outlook to stable, and the "path to A-" narrative stalls. Watch also whether peers (Monzo, Starling, N26) seek ratings as they scale, signalling the whole top tier entering capital markets.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (headline read).** No earnings print here — this is an S&P **new rating** (BBB+/Positive, assigned Oct. 22, 2026), not a results release. But the rating rationale is built on Revolut's **own filed FY2025 accounts** (Revolut Group Holdings Ltd, Annual Report for the year ended 31 Dec 2025), which read as a **BEAT**: operating revenue **£4.5bn (+46% YoY** vs £3.1bn FY2024; vs ~£4.2bn Bloomberg-compiled consensus — a beat), pre-tax profit **£1.7bn (+57%** vs £1.09bn), net profit **£1.3bn** (vs £0.8bn) — fifth straight profitable year. BBB+ with positive outlook; one-notch uplift possible once RGHL starts senior-unsecured/MREL issuance in 2027.

**Key figures (with growth) — Revolut Group Holdings FY2025 (filed accounts).**
- Revenue: **£4.5bn, +46% YoY** (from £3.1bn FY2024); ahead of the ~£4.2bn Bloomberg analyst average.
- Pre-tax profit: **£1.7bn, +57% YoY** (from £1.09bn); ~38% PBT margin.
- Net profit: **£1.3bn** (from £0.8bn FY2024, ~+63%).
- Retail customers: **68.3m, +30% YoY** (from 52.5m; +15.8m added in 2025); business customers **767k, +33%**. (S&P cites ~**80m across 41 countries as of Oct 2026**, >3x since 2022 — a run-rate figure, not the FY2025 year-end count.)
- Customer balances/deposits: total balances **£50.2bn, +66% YoY**.
- Revenue mix (per press/own report): subscriptions £708M (+67%), card payments ~£1.0bn (+45%), wealth £663M (+31%), FX £606M (+43%).

**By segment / driver.** Growth is **fee-led and diversified** — on S&P's adjusted basis, operating revenue +42.4% to £3.67bn with fees & commissions 73% of revenue, no single income stream >30% of fees (vs concentration risk a year ago). Fastest line: **subscriptions +67%** (21% of fees). Lending is the emerging-but-small driver: **loan portfolio +119.6% YoY**, yet customer-loan interest income was only **16% of total interest income** (the rest is treasury/central-bank balances) — i.e. revenue is still overwhelmingly transactional, not net-interest. S&P expects the loan book to more than double again in both 2026 and 2027.

**vs expectations / prior period.** Beat on the top line: £4.5bn revenue **vs ~£4.2bn Bloomberg consensus (as of Mar 2026 reporting), ~+7%**; +46% YoY. PBT +57% YoY and net profit ~+63% YoY both accelerated off a smaller FY2024 base (£1.09bn PBT / £0.8bn net). No public per-quarter consensus for a private company — variance is vs the Bloomberg-compiled full-year estimate and vs prior year. No prior Revolut earnings note in the base to wikilink.

**Guidance / forward.** No company guidance in the S&P action. The **forward-looking content is S&P's**: operating revenue and fee/commission growth of **25%–30% per year**, forecast **RoAE ~30% over the next two years**, **risk-adjusted capital (RAC) ratio >30% over 24 months**, **CET1 47.8% as of 30 Jun 2026** (among the highest of rated peers), **NSFR 333% / LCR 176%** mid-2026. Management's own long-term target (per reporting): **100m retail customers by mid-2027**. The debt event: nonoperating holdco **RGHL expected to start issuing senior unsecured / ALAC-eligible debt in 2027** to build an MREL buffer (>4% of RWAs), which S&P says could add a one-notch uplift for Revolut Ltd.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. **Capital is the rating's load-bearing pillar, not lending.** CET1 47.8% + RAC >30% + ~90% low-risk liquid assets → "Very Strong (+2)" capital & earnings score, which offsets the "Moderate (−1)" business and risk-position scores to land BBB+. Second-order: the rating is resilient to a lending stumble but hostage to any capital-return or balance-sheet-leverage shift as Revolut scales the loan book.
2. **The BBB+ is already "full" on standalone basis — the upside is purely structural debt.** S&P equalizes the rating with the bbb+ group credit profile and scores ALAC/group support at 0; the **only path to A− is RGHL actually issuing the 2027 MREL debt** and sustaining the buffer >4% of RWAs. If the 2027 issuance slips or falls short, outlook reverts to stable — the positive outlook is an IOU on a bond deal, not on earnings.
3. **Lending is the growth option but the untested risk.** +119.6% book growth with **cost of risk only improving to 3.8% (from 4.0%)** — but S&P flags underwriting is "untested through a credit downturn" and plans into mortgages. Second-order: credit losses are the single most likely source of a future downgrade in the "Risk position (−1)" bucket, even though today the book is too small (interest income 16% of total) to dent the P&L.
4. **Operational/regulatory risk is the de-PR'd soft spot.** S&P explicitly cites the **ECB restriction on new EEA product launches (2025)** and a **data breach affecting ~680 EU customers** — the kind of items the headline numbers paper over. UK full banking license (Mar 2026) and client migration to Revolut Bank UK Ltd by end-2026 are the structural enablers but also raise complexity/compliance cost.
