## Контекст

### [0] What happened (one-paragraph fact base)
On 2026-10-14 UK consumer group and ratings provider **Fairer Finance** and behavioural-design consultancy **Sideways** launched a "Michelin-star-style" accreditation scheme for retail financial-services firms (banks, insurers, lenders). Unlike Fairer Finance's existing outside-in Customer Experience and Product star ratings, the accreditation is run *with* the firm's cooperation: a deep dive into governance, MI dashboards, fair-value assessments and complaints-trend analysis. It is positioned as external reassurance of UK Consumer Duty compliance and a consumer-facing proof point. The pair also launched an AI-powered tool that checks customer-facing communications/touchpoints against Sideways's "Trust Signals" framework and flags trust-eroding patterns in real time. (Source: [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/111226/fairer-finance-and-sideways-launch-michelin-star-system-for-banks); [The Intermediary](https://theintermediary.co.uk/2026/10/fairer-finance-and-sideways-partner-to-launch-consumer-duty-accreditation/); [Mortgage Soup](https://mortgagesoup.co.uk/fairer-finance-and-sideways-develop-consumer-duty-accreditation-scheme/))

### [1] Who the players are
- **Fairer Finance** — British independent consumer group / ratings provider led by managing director **James Daley**; rates 30+ product types (banking, insurance, credit) and runs the twice-yearly **Trust in Banking Index** (tracking ~10,000 consumers since 2015). Spring 2026 index shows sector-average trust rising from ~38% (2015) to ~60%, with challenger banks (Starling, Monzo, First Direct) and Nationwide ranking most trusted. ([Fairer Finance](https://www.fairerfinance.com/); [Trust in Banking Index](https://www.fairerfinance.com/insights/trust-in-banking-index))
- **Sideways** — behavioural-design consultancy ("makes trust measurable and designable") co-founded by **Daljit Singh** (ex-ANNA Money, 100k+ customers), **Nicolas Roope** and **Andrew Beckley**; focuses on UK regulated firms, churn/risk reduction and Consumer Duty. Its Trust Signals framework (variously reported as six or seven: Clarity, Honesty, Reliability, Fairness, Care, Friction). ([Sideways](https://trustsideways.com/))

### [2] Why it matters / regulatory backdrop
The FCA's **Consumer Duty** has been in force since **31 July 2023** (new products) and **31 July 2024** (existing products), shifting from "treat customers fairly" to an outcomes-based standard (fair value, clear communications, effective support). The FCA's 2025–26 focus moved from implementation to *embedding*, with multi-firm reviews on product design, fair value and customer-journey design. The accreditation is an attempt to productise Consumer-Duty assurance — turning a compliance obligation into a marketable trust badge. It parallels how Consumer Duty now anchors other UK retail-finance obligations, e.g. [[FCA confirms BNPL protections under Consumer Duty from July 2026]] and the FCA's pressure on basic-account access in [[FCA tells UK banks to improve basic account access]]. ([Eversheds-Sutherland](https://www.eversheds-sutherland.com/en/united-kingdom/insights/fca-reports-on-the-consumer-duty-price-and-value-outcome))

### [3] Open questions / what to watch
- Adoption: which banks/insurers actually sign up and pay for accreditation; no launch customers were named.
- Independence/conflict: Fairer Finance both *rates* firms (outside-in) and now *accredits* them (paid, cooperative) — is there a conflict if an accredited firm scores poorly on the public index?
- Pricing/tiering of the "Michelin stars" and how stars map to measurable outcomes.
- Regulatory standing: the accreditation is private, not FCA-endorsed; its weight with the regulator is unproven.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector context (UK bank ratings / Consumer Duty / regtech).** The UK's Consumer Duty (in force 31 Jul 2023) requires retail financial firms to deliver good outcomes across products/price, understanding, support and avoidance of foreseeable harm — and, critically, to *evidence* it with governance, MI dashboards, fair-value assessments and complaints-trend analysis. This evidencing burden is exactly the gap Fairer Finance + Sideways target: a paid, firm-cooperative "Michelin-star-style" accreditation plus an AI diagnostic that scores customer-facing material against Fairer Finance's "Seven Trust Signals" framework. The FCA's 2026 posture is to apply *existing* frameworks (Consumer Duty, SMCR, operational resilience) to AI rather than issue standalone AI rules, and it expects AI outputs to be tested for fairness/foreseeable harm and to be explainable — a tailwind for regtech that helps firms demonstrate compliance, but also a reminder that the FCA itself, not a third party, is the authority (see sources).

**Positioning / what this actually is.** This is an extension of Fairer Finance's existing ratings consultancy (it already rates 30+ product types incl. banking, savings, insurance), not a new regulated license or an FCA-endorsed mark. The accreditation *sits alongside* its existing Customer Experience and Product star ratings. It is pay-and-participate (the firm cooperates and shares data), so it is a commercial consultancy/badge model — closer to a trust-mark + diagnostic SaaS than to independent supervision.

**In-base comps (grep fallback; sem semsearch returned HTTP 402 / insufficient credits, no retry).** No direct UK bank-accreditation comp exists in-base; adjacent Consumer-Duty / AI-compliance signals: `News/2026-01/The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) set out how its rules will apply (2).md` (FCA extending Consumer Duty scope to crypto — regulator owns the standard, Jan 2026); `News/2026-01/This Week in Fintech Webinar on AI for risk and compliance (2).md` (fintechs using AI to streamline risk/compliance — demand-side signal). Broader regtech/compliance tooling in-base (e.g. `News/2025-10/ComplyAdvantage and Google Cloud address India data rules.md`, `News/2025-10/Zania raises $18M Series A for agentic AI compliance platform.md`) shows AI-compliance is a crowded, well-funded category — raising the "why this badge, why now" question for a small UK consumer group.

**Risk flags (skeptical).**
1. **Conflict of interest / independence.** The accreditation is paid for and conducted *with the cooperation of the assessed firm*, yet Fairer Finance also publishes "independent" consumer ratings on the same firms. A pay-to-be-accredited badge sitting next to independent star ratings risks muddying both and inviting "marking your own homework" criticism.
2. **No regulatory standing.** The badge is not an FCA mark and confers no safe harbour; the FCA is explicit that *firms* must evidence Consumer Duty outcomes. An accreditation could create false reassurance without reducing supervisory exposure.
3. **AI tool substance unverified.** The "Seven Trust Signals" AI diagnostic is a proprietary, undisclosed framework; no accuracy/validation, false-positive rate, or model-governance detail was found — ironic for a tool sold on Consumer-Duty "understanding"/fairness, where the FCA expects AI to be tested and explainable.
4. **Scale / adoption unknown.** No launch customer count, pricing, or committed banks were disclosed; Fairer Finance is a small consumer group entering a crowded, well-capitalised regtech/AI-compliance market, so commercial traction is unproven.
5. **Single-source / early-stage.** Coverage traces to one Finextra press article + trade-press pickups (Intermediary, Mortgage Soup, Cover) — essentially a company announcement, not independent evaluation; treat metrics as vendor claims.

**IR grounding:** Fairer Finance and Sideways are private, non-IR-covered; IR skipped.
