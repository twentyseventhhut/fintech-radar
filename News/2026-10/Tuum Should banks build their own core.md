# Tuum: Should banks build their own core?

> [!info] 2026-10-08 · 1 упоминаний · 0 источника(ов) с текстом

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Connecting the Dots in Fintech] 💡Tuum puts a familiar question back on the table: “Surely we can build it ourselves.” But when banks compare building from scratch with using a modern core, the conversation isn't only about TCO. Time-to-market matters too - years spent building and maintaining infrastructure can mean years before a new product reaches customers. A modern core can shorten that cycle, giving banks more room to launch, experiment and adapt. Tuum explores why time-to-market deserves a place alongside TCO. Should You Build, Extend or Buy Your Core?

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What this is.** A Tuum thought-leadership piece ("Should You Build, Extend or Buy Your Core?") reopening the perennial "surely we can build it ourselves" question for banks. Tuum's angle: the build-vs-buy decision is usually framed around total cost of ownership (TCO), but time-to-market deserves equal weight — years spent building and maintaining infrastructure are years before a new product reaches customers; a modern core shortens that cycle. This is vendor commentary (self-interested — Tuum sells a core), not news of a deal or launch. (Source: https://tuum.com ; Tuum blog "Build vs Buy debate in banking technology" https://tuum.com/blog/build-or-buy-debate-in-banking-technology/)

**[1] Who Tuum is.** Estonian (Tallinn) cloud-native, API-first, modular core-banking vendor, founded 2019 (originally Modularbank) by Rivo Uibo, Jüri Kirme, Jan Lakspere and Ove Kreison. ~$49.8M raised across 5 rounds; most recent was a €25M Series B led by CommerzVentures (with Speedinvest). Customers across ~10 countries (strong UK/Nordics presence): Banking Circle, LHV UK, Nets, Veriff, Multitude Bank, maib, plus Middle East/Africa expansion (Uptex Bank/OBD Holding, Oman). Leadership refresh in 2026: ex-Vocalink CEO Gregor Dobbie named CEO ([[Tuum names Vocalink veteran Gregor Dobbie as CEO]]); James Bushby as CRO ([[Tuum names James Bushby as Chief Revenue Officer]]); Renato Cassinelli head of marketing ([[Tuum appoints Renato Cassinelli as head of marketing]]). Positions its "Xtensibility" story as a third option between build and buy. (Sources: https://tracxn.com/d/companies/tuum ; https://commerzventures.com/news/tuum-series-b ; https://www.fintechfutures.com/core-banking-technology/core-banking-tech-challenger-tuum-secures-25m-series-b-to-tap-new-territories ; https://tuum.com/blog/setting-banking-free-introducing-the-xtensible-core/)

**[2] The build-vs-buy facts underneath the debate.** Independent 2026 sources broadly agree with (and quantify) Tuum's framing:
- Building in-house: slowest/most expensive path — ~3–5 years and $50M+ with a 50+ engineer team; full core-replacement programs at tier-1 US banks run $300M–$1B+ over the lifecycle. Owning a core means owning ledger design, transaction rules, audit trails, security, product config, reporting, integrations, upgrades and perpetual maintenance.
- Buying/integrating a vendor core: ~18–36 months, roughly $5M–$100M depending on tier (one source cites $20M–$150M for a mid-tier bank's full modernization).
- Execution risk is real: cited figure that ~94% of core modernization projects exceed timelines; recurring failure modes = big-bang migration, underestimating legacy-data mess, never decommissioning the old core.
- Tuum's own marketing claims clients go live on average ~7 months vs 1–3 yrs for legacy — treat as vendor-supplied, not independently verified.
(Sources: https://zoolatech.com/blog/core-banking-modernization/ ; https://pitechsol.com/blog/pillar-core-banking-modernization/ ; https://antongrid.com/insights/core-banking-modernization-the-2026-guide-for-us-banks ; https://tuum.com/resources/on-the-radar-...)

**[3] Competitive landscape.** The "buy" side Tuum competes in is crowded and commercially maturing:
- Thought Machine (Vault Core): tier-1 deployments (Lloyds, Standard Chartered); FY2025 revenue topped $100M (+57% YoY), ARR crossed $100M in Q2 2026; raised £30M/$41M from a tier-1 bank in 2026 ([[Thought Machine secures GBP 30m funding from new investor]]).
- Mambu: API-first, cloud-native (AWS/Azure/GCP), $5.5B valuation (2021), ~$446M raised; broad BaaS/composable footprint (see [[Wio Bank leverages Mambu for composable banking]], [[Mambu selected as core banking provider by Nyla]]).
- Temenos Transact: incumbent scale (~950 banks, 150 countries) but expensive/complex; rollouts 18–36 months, $50M–$300M.
- FintechOS: active in 2026 (launching "Blue Sky AI" at Elevate '26 — [[FintechOS to launch Blue Sky AI platform at Elevate '26]]).
- Adjacent build-vs-buy signals in the DB: Starling's Engine SaaS deals ([[Starling signs 10-year Engine deal with New Zealand's SBS Bank]]), 10x Banking, Lerian/Pismo, Fimple, Vilja.
(Sources: https://finovate.com/thought-machine-secures-41-million-in-funding-tops-100-million-revenue-milestone/ ; https://multiples.vc/private-comps/mambu ; https://labhub.hopto.org/blog/culture/2026-05-25-core-banking-...)

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Core-banking software is a mid-size, fast-growing infra market: ~$19–20bn in 2025 and projected ~$22.3bn (2026)→~$80.7bn (2034) at ~17.4% CAGR (per Fortune Business Insights, via web, as of 2025; other firms cite 9–15% CAGRs — figure dispersion is real, treat the band not the point). Structure: a two-tier market — incumbent monoliths (Temenos, FIS, Finastra, Oracle FSS) sitting on decades-old installs, versus cloud-native challengers (Thought Machine, Mambu, 10x, Tuum, FNZ) selling composable/API-first cores. Barriers are high: regulatory certification, data-migration risk, and multi-year switching costs lock banks in — which is exactly why the "build it ourselves" reflex persists. Why now: the build-vs-buy math has shifted — a full in-house core transformation runs ~$20–100m over 3–5 years, while a modern vendor core reaches production in ~18–36 months (per web, as of 2026). Tuum's pitch reframes the debate from pure TCO to *time-to-market* — i.e. the opportunity cost of years not shipping product `(analysis)`.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: ARR / ARR-growth, net revenue retention, gross margin, and deployment time-to-live (a core-specific KPI the vendors lean on). Key players & basis of competition — incumbents compete on installed base + breadth; challengers on cloud-native architecture, deployment speed and API extensibility. Recent peer moves (via corpus): Mambu signed Nyla as core provider (2026-03) and partnered Mastercard on cross-border (2026-09); Thought Machine signed Shawbrook live on Vault Core (2025-08) and raised £45m as losses widened (2025-10); 10x shipped v10.0 / 100th release (2026-03); Temenos pushed embedded AI agents across core (2026-05). Tuum's own trajectory: Multitude Bank partnership extension (2025-11), maib (Moldova/Europe), new CEO Gregor Dobbie and CRO James Bushby (2026-06) — a leadership build-out consistent with a scale-up pushing into DACH/Southern Europe/MEA. Position: niche challenger — smaller than Thought Machine/Mambu by revenue and raise, competing on composability and speed rather than scale; moat is early switching-cost/reference-customer accumulation, thin vs incumbents `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.**
- **Tuum (protagonist):** Series B €25m led by CommerzVentures + Citi Ventures follow-on, ~$49m total raised; revenue €3.68m (FY2023), >250% 3-yr CAGR (self-reported, de-PR: off a tiny base). No post-money disclosed → valuation multiple = no data.
- **Thought Machine:** last headline valuation ~$2.7bn (2022); revenue £74.8m FY2025 (+57% YoY), ARR >$100m (Q2 2026). Round-valuation / FY25 revenue ≈ $2.7bn / ~$100m ≈ **27x** — rich, but a stale 2022 mark against 2025 revenue; `[UNSOURCED]` for any current mark (co. raised £45m in 2025 *as losses widened*, suggesting a likely down/flat re-rate, not up) `(analysis)`.
- **Mambu:** Series E €235m at €4.9bn / $5.5bn post-money (Dec 2021); no newer round surfaced — mark is ~4 yrs stale, treat as ceiling not current.
- **Temenos (public benchmark):** market cap ~$5.41bn (Oct 2026). EV/Revenue not computed — current revenue not verified from a free source → no data.
- Internal comps: [[Thought Machine lands GBP 45M funding as losses widen]] · [[Mambu selected as core banking provider by Nyla]] · [[Temenos launches embedded AI agents across core and digital banking]] · [[Tuum names Vocalink veteran Gregor Dobbie as CEO]].
- Distribution not computed (mixed private round-marks vs one public cap, different vintages) → qualitative only. Flag: private challenger marks (Mambu ~$5.5bn, TM ~$2.7bn) sit near/above public Temenos' ~$5.4bn cap despite a fraction of the revenue — classic 2021-vintage private/public re-rating gap, stale and likely optimistic.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Stale private marks / re-rating risk** — challenger valuations are 2021–22 vintage; Thought Machine raising "as losses widened" signals the sector may be marking down, so any implied Tuum valuation anchored to peers is optimistic.
2. **Thesis is vendor marketing, not evidence** — the "time-to-market beats TCO" framing is Tuum's own blog (de-PR: it's a sell-side argument). The bank's counter-case (control, differentiation, no vendor lock-in / rail dependence) is exactly what Tuum is silent on; build-vs-buy is genuinely bank-specific, not universally "buy".
3. **Sub-scale execution risk** — €3.68m revenue (FY23) off a tiny base vs peers at $100m+ ARR; margin for error in simultaneous DACH/S-Europe/MEA expansion is thin, and deployment/migration slippage is the sector's chronic failure mode.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Reinforces a secular shift from in-house cores to composable vendors — the TCO+time-to-market argument structurally favors buy/hybrid for most banks, consolidating spend toward a handful of challengers `(analysis)`. Watch for: a fresh Tuum round (would reveal a real mark) or a marquee tier-1 bank win as the catalyst; the thesis breaks if a high-profile vendor-core migration fails publicly or if incumbents' AI-retrofits (Temenos' embedded agents) blunt the challengers' speed advantage.
