## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened (2026-10-21/23).** Nomba, a Lagos-based business-banking and payments-infrastructure fintech, extended its existing DRC payments corridor into Zambia via licensed local partners. The new rail lets businesses in the DRC pay Zambian suppliers instantly in US dollars or Congolese francs across bank accounts and mobile wallets, targeting agriculture, retail and wholesale trade — sectors that previously moved money on cash and informal channels amid FX shortages and settlement delays.

**The $1.6B figure.** The headline number is Zambia's goods exports to the DRC, which reached ~$1.6B in 2025 (up roughly a third year-on-year) per the Bank of Zambia. Note the sourcing is not uniform: several outlets frame the DRC–Zambia corridor as ~$1.2B, so the exact size of the addressable flow is reported inconsistently — the $1.6B is best read as total bilateral trade along the route rather than a payments-volume Nomba will capture.

**Who Nomba is.** Founded 2017 as Kudi (a financial-transactions chatbot), rebranded to Nomba in 2022. Core business is merchant/SME payments in Nigeria — POS terminals, QR, transfers, invoicing, a payment gateway — serving ~200k+ small businesses. It has been pushing beyond Nigeria into a cross-border/B2B infrastructure play.

**Recent trajectory feeding this move.**
- Sep 2026: Nomba secured a ~$3M debt facility from CardinalStone Finance to scale cross-border infrastructure, with a stated goal of growing cross-border volume from ~$480M to ~$1B monthly (and press framing an Africa–Asia payment-corridor ambition).
- Feb 2026: acquired a licensed Canadian payments firm to power Africa–Canada trade ([[Nomba acquires licensed Canadian payments firm]]).
- Mar 2026: partnered with Volume to enable GBP collection for Nigerian businesses ([[Nomba and Volume enable GBP collection for Nigerian businesses]]).
- Nov 2025: first expanded into Congo/DRC with remittances, recruiting physical agents for inflows from corridors like China and Dubai ([[Nomba expands into Congo with remittances]]). This Zambia launch is a B2B extension of that same DRC base, not a fresh market entry.

**Competitive context (intra-Africa cross-border payments).** The corridor sits in a crowded field: [[NALA strengthens Nigeria cross-border payments with new license]], [[Thunes and MoMo PSB enable cross-border payments in Nigeria]] / [[MTN's MoMo PSB partners Thunes for Nigeria cross-border payments]], stablecoin rails ([[Flutterwave partners with Tempo for stablecoin settlement]]), and PAPSS-based initiatives ([[Onafriq and PAPSS to launch cross-border payments from Ghana]]). On the DRC inbound side specifically, [[DigiPay and Belmoney launch DigiTransfer remittances to Congo and DRC]] targets Europe→Congo retail flows, whereas Nomba is B2B intra-Africa (DRC→Zambia supplier payments). The differentiator is licensed local-partner settlement in both legs rather than pure stablecoin or remittance rails.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is "$1.6B trade route" a payments opportunity Nomba captures, or just the size of bilateral trade? It's the latter — Zambia's 2025 goods exports to the DRC (Bank of Zambia). The headline risks implying Nomba's addressable volume is $1.6B when it is not.
2. Why do some outlets say $1.2B and others $1.6B? The figure is reported inconsistently (TechBuild/Techeconomy ~$1.2B; others ~$1.6B). Which base year and which direction of trade is each using? Unresolved — treat the number as soft.
3. Is this a NEW corridor launch or an extension? Extension: Nomba already ran a DRC corridor (since Nov 2025); this adds Zambia as a destination leg. The title's "opens ... corridor" slightly overstates novelty.
4. Does Nomba hold its own licenses in DRC/Zambia, or rely on third-party licensed partners? Reporting says "licensed local partners" — so settlement/compliance risk and unit economics depend on partners Nomba doesn't control.
5. What volume is actually flowing day one? No live-volume figure disclosed for the Zambia leg — only the aspirational $480M→$1B/month cross-border target for the whole business.
6. Does the directionality match the trade? The corridor lets DRC businesses pay Zambian suppliers, i.e. money flows DRC→Zambia. But the $1.6B figure is Zambia's exports TO the DRC — so the payment direction and the headline trade stat are aligned, but worth stating explicitly.
7. Is FX a real edge? Paying in USD or Congolese francs across FX-short markets is the core value prop; but settlement FX spreads and liquidity in CDF/ZMW are exactly where margins and reliability can break.
8. Competitive moat vs Thunes/MoMo PSB, NALA, PAPSS, stablecoin rails? Nomba's angle is B2B supplier payments with local-partner settlement; unclear how defensible versus PAPSS (central-bank backed) or stablecoin players on cost.
9. How material is $3M debt? A $3M facility is small for building multi-market cross-border infrastructure; is it enough to fund the stated $1B/month ambition, or a bridge?
10. Credibility of the $480M→$1B monthly target — is $480M current run-rate verified, or self-reported? Self-reported; no audited figure.
11. Regulatory/political risk: DRC and Zambia both have FX-control and AML regimes; corridor viability hinges on sustained partner licenses and correspondent access.
12. Is this the same event as the 2025-11 Congo note? No — distinct dated event (Oct 2026, Zambia leg). Not a duplicate, but should cross-link so the DRC base isn't double-counted as a separate "expansion."
13. Why DRC→Zambia specifically and not Nigeria-linked? This is intra-SADC/Central-Africa trade with no Nigeria leg — a strategic step away from Nomba's home market; execution risk in unfamiliar markets.
14. Durability signal: is agriculture/retail/wholesale trade sticky, or seasonal/commodity-driven (copper, agri inputs)? Commodity cyclicality could make volumes lumpy.

**Importance: 2/5 — rationale.** A genuine, dated intra-Africa B2B corridor extension by a notable Nigerian fintech, with a clear funding runway (Sep 2026 CardinalStone facility) and a credible value prop (FX-scarce supplier payments). But it is incremental (extends an existing DRC base, not a new market), the splashy "$1.6B trade route" is bilateral-trade size rather than captured volume, no live Zambia-leg volumes are disclosed, and the build rests on third-party licensed partners and a small debt facility. Noteworthy as a data point in Nomba's cross-border build-out and the broader intra-Africa payments race, but not market-moving.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Intra-Africa cross-border payments were ~$329bn in 2025, projected to ~$1trn by 2035 (~12% CAGR) (per secondary analyst citations, via microsave/duplo trackers, as of 2026 — single-source, treat as indicative). The corridor logic here is concrete, not TAM hand-waving: Zambia's goods exports to the DRC hit $1.6bn in 2025, up ~a third YoY (per Bank of Zambia, via Disrupt Africa, 2026-10-23) — that is the "$1.6B trade route" in the title, i.e. a copperbelt-adjacent B2B flow still clearing on "cash and hope." Structure: deeply fragmented — correspondent banking, mobile-money silos (Airtel/MTN/MoMo), FX bottlenecks between CDF/ZMW, and a thin bank rail. Barriers are regulation (per-country licensing) and liquidity/FX settlement, not tech. Why now: AfCFTA + PAPSS are compressing settlement cost (per-transaction savings cited at 92–95%, ~$5bn/yr fees saved continent-wide; PAPSS now ~19 countries, 160+ banks — per PAPSS/techcabal, 2026) (analysis), opening room for a merchant-first challenger to undercut incumbents on a specific lane.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: monthly cross-border volume (TPV), take rate per transaction, corridor liquidity/FX spread, licensed-country count. Players: Flutterwave (continent's largest cross-border API, stablecoin B2B settlement), Onafriq (switch, PAPSS-linked, partnered with Flutterwave Sep-2025), Chipper Cash (mobile-to-mobile consumer), M-PESA/MTN MoMo, Western Union/Zoona on the Zambia lane. Basis of competition: corridor depth + FX liquidity + merchant distribution, not just price. Nomba's position: niche/early — it processes >$480m/mo cross-border (DRC ops + Canadian MSB) and targets $1bn/mo; the DRC→Zambia lane lets Congolese businesses pay Zambian suppliers in USD or CDF via bank accounts and wallets (per CNBC Africa / techmoran, 2026-10-21). Moat (analysis): a de-PR'd read is that the moat is distribution into one real trade flow + local licensing, not proprietary rails — thin and replicable. Recent moves: Nomba $3m debt facility via CardinalStone (Sep-2026) to fund DRC→Zambia→Uganda rollout and scale $480m→$1bn/mo; plans a further $20–50m debt in stages.

**Comps & multiples.** Nomba last priced at >$150m post-money on its $30m pre-Series B (Base10, Shopify, Khosla; ~$42.7m total raised) — that is a 2023 round valuation, NOT a current mark; no revenue disclosed, so EV/Revenue, P/S = no data. Price-per-business (analysis, round-dated): $150m / ~300,000 businesses = $500/merchant — indicative only, pre-corridor. Peer multiples: Flutterwave/Onafriq are private; last marks not freshly verifiable here → [UNSOURCED]. Distribution not computed (fewer than 3 comparable public figures) → qualitative comparison only. Internal comps from base: [[Nomba expands into Congo with remittances]] (2025-11, the DRC entry this corridor extends), [[Western Union, Zoona and Chipper Cash launch transfers in Zambia]] (2025-08, same lane, incumbents), [[Onafriq and PAPSS to launch cross-border payments from Ghana]] (2025-06, PAPSS merchant/SME rail), [[Flutterwave partners iPaylinks on Africa-Asia cross-border payments]] (2025-08, B2B corridor peer).

**Risk flags.**
- Corridor/market concentration: the entire thesis rests on one $1.6bn CDF↔ZMW trade lane; a single-corridor FX or liquidity shock (copper cycle, kwacha/franc swings) hits volume directly (second-order: debt-funded scaling amplifies the downside).
- Debt-funded growth, not equity: $3m facility + $20–50m planned debt against an undisclosed-revenue, 2023-vintage-$150m-valued business — if the $480m→$1bn/mo ramp stalls, interest obligations bite before the corridor turns profitable.
- Disintermediation by the rails: PAPSS + bank/switch players (Onafriq) and Flutterwave can serve the same lane; Nomba depends on licensed local partners and third-party wallets/banks for settlement, so margin could be captured by the rail layer it rides.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a new-entry, corridor-by-corridor land-grab, not a re-rating (analysis): challengers now pick a single real B2B trade flow and digitize it ahead of the incumbent banks. Falsifiable thesis: if Nomba's monthly cross-border TPV moves materially toward $1bn within ~12 months AND it names a second live corridor (Uganda), the merchant-corridor model is working; trigger to watch — next disclosed TPV / a follow-on equity (not debt) round. What breaks it: TPV flat near $480m or the DRC→Zambia lane stays "pilot" with no named volume.
