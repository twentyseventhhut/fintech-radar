## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Lyon-based fintech **Wavo** (founded 2024; CEO Julien Decherf, co-founders Adrien Plat, Tanguy Clerget; team ~16) raised **€10M** in an early-stage round, announced **2026-10-13**. Lead: **Eurazeo**; with **Axeleo Capital** and **HUB612**. This is Wavo's **second** raise — it follows a **€3.5M** round (Clint Capital lead, Kima Ventures, HUB612, angels incl. Tony Parker). Combined external capital raised ≈ **€13.5M**. Valuation **not disclosed**. The source note in the vault says "details on use of funds remain to be verified" — the de-PR'd facts (lead, size, model, traction) come from Dealroom / Maddyness / mind Fintech, not from the thin aggregated blurb.

**Model (de-PR'd):** Wavo is **not a lender** — it **buys** a company's inventory outright to free cash, then **re-sells it back** to the company as it sells through. Revenue = a **commission/markup on repurchase** (goods bought at 100, repurchased at ~102–105). It funds this by **issuing bonds to private investors** and connects to client ERP/inventory systems via an **API with AI stock-rotation scoring**. The pitch is "**debt-free** working capital" — structurally true (it is an off-balance-sheet sale-and-repurchase, not a loan), **but** economically it is a short-term financing cost by another name; the "no debt" framing is a balance-sheet/accounting selling point, not free money.

**→ Why structured this way:** the sale-and-repurchase wrapper lets SMEs (who can't get cheap inventory lines reserved for large corporates) access stock financing without adding bank debt or covenants, and lets Wavo match it to private-investor bond demand. **→ What it reveals:** the real question is not "is inventory financing new" (it isn't) but **who bears the inventory/obsolescence risk and at what effective APR** — the PR is silent on this.

## [1] Competitors / peers
SME/B2B working-capital & inventory financing in Europe is **crowded**:
- **Defacto** (France, 2021) — infrastructure-as-a-service B2B lending incl. **stock financing**; >€300M–€1.8B/yr deployed, ~15k SMBs, 8 countries. The clear French incumbent and most direct comp.
- **Hokodo** (UK) — B2B BNPL across 6 markets.
- **Billie, Mondu** (Germany), **Kriya** (UK) — B2B BNPL / invoice finance.
- **Finmid** (Germany) — embedded lending infra; note the same-day vault item [[Finmid raises €17M extension, strikes Bolt financing deal]] shows how well-capitalized this niche's infra players are.
Wavo's differentiator vs these: it **takes title to the inventory** (true purchase) rather than lending against receivables/payables — a narrower, more operationally heavy model.

**→ Why the landscape is this way / second order:** most peers chose **receivables/payables BNPL** because it's capital-light and scales via API. Wavo's **buy-and-resell** is capital-intensive and carries **goods-obsolescence and resale risk** — differentiated, but it is **harder to scale** and the margin (2–5 pts) must cover that risk. Against Defacto's scale (15k SMBs) Wavo (~100 clients) is **years behind**; the €10M mostly buys survival/expansion runway, not a lead.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **2024:** founded, Lyon.
- **2025 (approx):** €3.5M seed (Clint Capital, Kima, HUB612, angels).
- **~first 12 months live:** €35M inventory financed, ~€100M client sales enabled, ~100 clients, **€1M revenue** in <1 year.
- **2026-10:** €10M, Eurazeo lead.
**Fit:** logical — the raise scales a model with early revenue traction. **→ Why it acts this way:** a capital-intensive buy-and-resell model **consumes balance sheet per client**, so Wavo must raise equity to fund both opex and the inventory book (plus bond-investor matching) — hence a second round only ~1 year after seed.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Not novel as a concept** — inventory/stock financing is decades old and Defacto already does stock financing for French SMEs. The **real novelty is the delivery**: API-connected, AI stock-rotation underwriting, sale-and-repurchase (vs a loan) targeted at **SMEs** who were previously excluded from corporate-grade inventory lines.
**Traction is genuine but small:** €1M revenue, €35M financed, ~100 clients in year one — real, not vaporware, but a **seed-stage footprint**.
**→ Who captures the margin / what breaks it:** margin = the 2–5pt repurchase spread **minus** cost of bond capital, obsolescence/resale losses, and ERP-integration opex. The model works only if **AI stock scoring keeps default/obsolescence low**; a demand shock leaving Wavo holding unsellable stock is the structural failure mode. This is **principal risk**, not fee risk — a materially different profile from receivables-BNPL peers.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Use of funds: platform build, hiring (16 → ~50), **European expansion starting Northern Europe**; target **~500 clients by end-2027**. **Backdrop:** French fintech funding in 2026 is **active but selective** — ~$1.4B YTD across 37 deals through July, early-stage more selective, a known **late-stage gap** in France. A €10M early-stage round with a brand-name lead (Eurazeo) is a **solid but not standout** outcome in this climate.
**→ Why / second order:** Eurazeo's lead is a credibility signal, but the **5x client growth to 2027** depends on scaling a **capital-hungry** book — each new client needs inventory funding, so Wavo will likely need **debt/securitization or a much bigger equity round** before 2027, and France's late-stage gap makes that non-trivial. The counterintuitive risk: **faster growth increases balance-sheet and obsolescence exposure**, so scale makes the model *more* fragile, not safer, unless the bond-funding and AI underwriting hold.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Wavo is not generic "fintech-infrastructure" — it is SME inventory financing (de-PR'd from the note's thin tag): it buys a company's stock, pays within 48h "no debt / no collateral," and the client repurchases as it sells (per Dealroom / Maddyness, 13 Oct 2026). This sits in the European non-dilutive working-capital / revenue-based-financing (RBF) niche. Size: Europe's RBF/working-capital-for-ecom segment is cited at ~$2.5bn+ with 50%+ CAGR projections (per Settle/secondary 2026 round-ups, via WebSearch) — treat as an order-of-magnitude indication, not an audited TAM; a hard European inventory-finance TAM is "no data." Structure: fragmented and capital-intensive — the binding constraint is funding cost and balance-sheet/warehouse capacity, not software; barriers are access to cheap debt + underwriting data (ERP/stock-rotation signals), not network effects. Why now: SMEs face dear bank credit and want non-dilutive cash against stock; two same-month European peers raising into the exact adjacency — [[Finmid raises €17M extension, strikes Bolt financing deal]] (embedded SME lending, 6 Oct 2026) and [[Cleavr raises €8M seed led by Varsity]] (French AR/invoice automation, Oct 2026) — show investor appetite for SME cash-flow infrastructure concentrated right now.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: inventory/assets financed (vol), loss/default rate, cost of funds vs repurchase spread (gross take), and funding-line headroom. Wavo's disclosed economics: ~€35m inventory financed → nearly €100m client sales, €1m revenue in <1yr; repurchase at "102–105 when bought at 100," i.e. a ~2–5% gross take per cycle (per Dealroom, CEO quote). Players & basis of competition (price of capital + speed + underwriting data): Wayflyer (RBF, $1.6bn val, $676m+ raised, $250m ATLAS SP facility — per fintech.global/Tracxn 2026), Settle and Shopify Capital (US inventory/working-capital). Recent moves: Wayflyer crossed ~$100m revenue in 2026 and keeps stacking debt facilities. Wavo's position: niche and early — founded 2024, 16 people, ~100 clients, targeting ~500 by end-2027 and Northern-Europe expansion (per Dealroom). Moat `(analysis)`: thin so far — the only defensible edge is proprietary ERP/stock-rotation underwriting data; capital is a commodity and a buy/sell-back structure is replicable. **Risk flags.**
1. Balance-sheet / funding-model risk: the €10m is equity, but the buy-stock-then-resell model consumes capital per deal — scaling to 500 clients needs large debt/warehouse lines Wavo has not announced; growth is gated by cost and availability of that funding, and the note is silent on it.
2. Credit & inventory concentration risk: Wavo owns the stock and bears repurchase/liquidation risk if a client under-sells or fails; a ~2–5% gross spread leaves thin cushion against defaults or obsolete/perishable inventory, and loss rates are undisclosed `[UNSOURCED]`.
3. Execution / competitive-encroachment risk: a 2024-founded, 16-person firm expanding geographically against well-funded RBF incumbents (Wayflyer) and adjacent SME lenders (Finmid); the buy/sell-back structure is replicable, so the data moat must prove out before scale.

**What this changes (idea-lens) `(analysis)`.** Wavo signals the European SME non-dilutive-financing wedge is widening from receivables/invoices (Cleavr, Iridium, Xepelin) into inventory specifically — a less-crowded sub-niche. Falsifiable thesis: if Wavo (or peers) announces a sizeable debt/warehouse facility within ~12 months, the equity round is a prelude to balance-sheet scaling and the model works; if instead the next news is a down-round or stalled client growth (vs the 500-by-2027 target), the unit economics of owning SME inventory don't scale — watch for the funding-line announcement as the trigger.
