**London, October 15, 2026** — Klarna, the everyday finance network, is now available as a payment option at Marks & Spencer for Fashion, Home & Beauty purchases online, bringing shoppers at the UK’s most trusted retailer* access to the country’s largest flexible payments provider.

Shoppers on M&S.com and the M&S app can now pay with Klarna for Fashion, Home & Beauty purchases, splitting the cost into three equal, interest-free payments with transparent terms and no revolving balance.

"People deserve flexibility without paying interest for it, so our goal is simple: meet them wherever they shop," said Klarna Head of UK, Ireland & Western & Southern Europe, Raji Behal. "M&S is a beloved British icon that brings second-to-none quality and value to millions of shoppers every day. We’re proud to now offer them a transparent way to spread the cost with no interest and a clear end date."

Katherine Carlson, Director of Financial Services at M&S, said: “At M&S, we are always looking for ways to make it easier for our customers to shop with us. Customers shopping on M&S.com and through our app can now spread the cost in three interest-free payments with Klarna, offering more flexibility in how they pay for the things they love.”

M&S puts customers at the heart of everything it does, and Klarna’s delighted to be working with a retailer that shares that focus. M&S joins more than 1.2 million merchants globally that partner with Klarna, including all of the UK’s major department stores.

**About Klarna**  
Klarna is the everyday finance network. With over 120 million global active Klarna users and 3.8 million transactions per day, Klarna’s AI-powered payments and commerce network is empowering people to pay smarter with a mission to be available everywhere for everything. Consumers can pay with Klarna online, in-store and through Apple Pay & Google Pay. Over 1.2 million retailers trust Klarna’s innovative solutions to drive growth and loyalty, including Uber, H&M, Saks, Sephora, Macy’s, Ikea, Expedia Group, Nike and Airbnb. Klarna is listed on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE: KLAR). For more information, visit Klarna.com.

**About M&S**  
M&S is a leading British retailer bringing exceptional quality, great value food, clothing and homeware to millions of customers around the world.

_*GlobalData’s UK most trusted retailer ranking 2025_

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Klarna went live as a checkout payment option on **M&S.com and the M&S app** (online only — not in physical stores) on **15 Oct 2026**, covering **Fashion, Home & Beauty** purchases. The product is Klarna's **interest-free pay-in-3** (three equal instalments, fixed end date, no revolving balance) — i.e. the plain-vanilla "Pay in 3" DPC, *not* a financing/interest-bearing line. Third-party coverage adds the detail Klarna's own PR omitted: eligible baskets are roughly **£30–£2,000** after reductions (Retail Week / securities.io). M&S is described as "the UK's most trusted retailer" per GlobalData 2025 — a marketing anchor, not a deal metric.

**+ Why framed this way / what it reveals:** The PR leans entirely on brand prestige ("beloved British icon", "most trusted retailer") and on Klarna's scale boilerplate (120M users, 3.8M txns/day, 1.2M merchants) because **the deal itself is small and undifferentiated** — a single incremental online merchant adding a commodity pay-in-3 button. There is **no disclosed economics** (take-rate, exclusivity, GMV target, duration), no in-store rollout, and no food category (M&S's largest business). That omission is the story: this is a merchant-acquisition announcement dressed as a strategic partnership. Notably it lands *after* UK BNPL became FCA-regulated on 15 Jul 2026, so M&S is adding Klarna under the new consumer-credit regime (affordability checks per transaction, FOS access, S75-style protections) — lower reputational risk for a "trusted retailer" than pre-regulation.

## [1] Competitors / peers
UK BNPL is a three-horse race: **Klarna, Clearpay (Afterpay/Block), PayPal Pay in 3**, with homegrown **Zilch** and bank entrants (**Monzo, Revolut, Barclays**) expanding instalment offers (GlobalData UK BNPL 2026; market ~$48.6B GMV 2026). Clearpay is strongest in fashion/lifestyle; PayPal Pay in 3 is embedded in SME/marketplace checkout. For a fashion-heavy retailer like M&S, **Clearpay was the obvious incumbent-style pick**, so Klarna winning the slot is a mild competitive data-point — but pay-in-3 buttons are typically **non-exclusive**, so M&S can and likely will stack providers over time.

**+ Why / second-order:** The win is **name-specific, not structural** — M&S picked the brand with the largest merchant network and app engagement (Klarna "leads app engagement" per GlobalData), which matters for referral traffic from Klarna's own shopping app. The real competitive lever is no longer the instalment mechanic (fully commoditised, all three now FCA-regulated and near-identical on terms) but **distribution**: who sends shoppers *to* the merchant via their app. That shifts the question from "who has the better BNPL" to "who owns the shopping-discovery funnel" — where Klarna's 120M-user app is its moat, not the pay-in-3 itself.

## [2] Company history / fit
Klarna's 2025–26 arc: UK debit card + digital wallet launch ([[Klarna launches debit card and digital wallet in UK]], [[Klarna signs Qatar Airways and launches UK debit card]]), passing [[Klarna passes one million merchants milestone|1M merchants]], FCA authorisation in 2025, then a rocky **NYSE IPO (KLAR)** followed by a ~66% share-price drop and [[Klarna loses senior executives after 66% share-price drop|senior-exec departures]]. Financially it has turned: [[Klarna reports Q1 2026 revenue up 44% to $1 billion|Q1 2026 revenue $1.0B +44%]] and Q2 2026 revenue $1.042B (+27%), **net income $9M** (vs −$53M a year earlier — second consecutive profitable quarter), GMV $36.6B, credit losses down to 0.52% of GMV.

**+ Why Klarna acts this way:** As a newly public, just-profitable lender under IPO-drop scrutiny, Klarna needs **visible, low-risk GMV growth** to defend its multiple — and blue-chip UK merchant logos (M&S, after Qatar Airways, Uber, H&M, Nike) are cheap credibility that signals "regulated, trusted, mainstream". Adding a trophy British retailer post-FCA-regulation is exactly the de-risked growth a post-IPO narrative wants. It also feeds Klarna's app funnel (card + wallet + shopping app), where it monetises beyond merchant fees.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: near-zero.** Interest-free pay-in-3 at a fashion/home retailer is the most commoditised BNPL product in the UK; nothing in the mechanic is new. The only "new" is the *merchant* (M&S) and the *channel* (M&S.com + app). **Traction is unproven** — this is a go-live announcement with **no GMV, conversion-uplift, or adoption numbers**; "launched" ≠ "material". M&S's online Fashion/Home/Beauty is a meaningful but not huge basket for Klarna's $36.6B/quarter GMV.

**+ Where the margin sits:** Klarna captures a merchant discount rate (MDR) on each transaction plus app-referral economics; M&S captures conversion/AOV uplift and reaches younger shoppers. The value-add is real but **thin and shared** — because the product is commoditised and non-exclusive, pricing power is low and M&S can add Clearpay/PayPal to compete the MDR down. What underpins any durable Klarna premium is **the app/discovery funnel and the debit-card/wallet attach**, not this instalment button; the button alone is a race-to-parity line item.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect the usual BNPL playbook: category expansion (eventually in-store M&S?), and M&S likely adding a second provider. Sector backdrop: UK BNPL GMV ~$48.6B in 2026 heading to ~$106B by 2031 (GlobalData), now fully FCA-regulated since 15 Jul 2026 — which **raises compliance cost but legitimises the product** and favours scaled, authorised players (Klarna, Clearpay, PayPal) over small entrants. Market sentiment on Klarna itself is cautious post-IPO (−66% from listing, exec churn) despite the return to profit.

**+ Counterintuitive second-order:** FCA regulation is usually read as a headwind, but it is **net-positive for Klarna here** — per-transaction affordability checks and formal consumer protections are exactly what let a conservative, reputation-sensitive retailer like M&S sign on. Regulation is becoming a **distribution unlock** for incumbents and a barrier to new entrants, concentrating the market in the top three rather than fragmenting it.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** BNPL/deferred-payment-credit (DPC) at retail checkout. No single clean TAM — third-party estimates diverge wildly by methodology (annual transaction value ~$640bn+ vs. "market" figures of $27–37bn for 2026; CAGR cited anywhere 12–28%), so treat any single BNPL "market size" as unreliable — "no data" on a defensible TAM. The structural read matters more: BNPL is a thin-take-rate (~2–3% of GMV) volume game layered onto existing card/checkout rails; value accrues to whoever owns merchant distribution + consumer frequency, with credit-loss management as the gating competence. Why now, UK-specifically: the FCA began regulating DPC on 2026-07-15 — every agreement now needs a proportionate affordability/creditworthiness check and lenders must be FCA-authorised (or in the temporary permissions regime); consumers get Financial Ombudsman access (per FCA). That reframes an M&S launch landing just 3 months into a newly-regulated UK regime — compliance cost is now table-stakes, which favours scaled incumbents like Klarna over sub-scale players.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: GMV, take rate (revenue/GMV), provisions-for-credit-losses as % of GMV, active users/transaction frequency. Players: Klarna (reported ~65% global BNPL share in Q3, per 247WallSt/comps aggregators), Affirm (~19%, and the fastest MAU grower), Afterpay/Clearpay (Block), PayPal Pay-in-3, Zilch, plus card-network installments (Mastercard Flex Pay, Visa). Basis of competition: merchant distribution + checkout placement, not price (the three-interest-free-payments product is commoditised). Recent moves: Klarna has been aggressively expanding UK/EU retail distribution and product breadth (debit card, Premium/Max tiers — see in-base comps). Protagonist position: ahead on distribution — M&S adds to "all of the UK's major department stores" and >1.2m global merchants (per the PR); the moat is the two-sided network (merchant breadth × 120m+ users, 3.8m txns/day claimed) plus switching costs for integrated merchants `(analysis)`. Note the user base is only flat-to-shrinking globally (Klarna MAU reported -3.9% YoY in the comps data), so share leadership rests on GMV/merchant depth, not user growth.

**Comps & multiples.** Klarna reported actuals (Q2 2026, per earnings release): GMV $36.6bn (+18% YoY), revenue $1,042m (+27%), comparable take rate 2.84%, provisions $192m = 0.52% of GMV (down from 0.56% YoY), net income +$9m (vs -$53m), EPS $0.01. FY26 guidance was CUT: GMV to $149–151bn (from >$155bn) and revenue to $4.08–4.16bn (from >$4.34bn) — a demand/German-consumer softness signal, not a margin problem.

**Risk flags.**
1. **UK regulatory drag (new).** DPC regulation live since 2026-07-15 means mandatory affordability checks on every M&S transaction — adds checkout friction and compliance cost exactly as Klarna scales UK retail volume; could dent conversion, the whole merchant value-prop.
2. **Demand softness / guidance cut.** Klarna already trimmed FY26 GMV and revenue guidance on consumer weakness (notably Germany); UK retail (M&S) is incremental but into a cooling European consumer — volume, not margin, is the question mark.
3. **Thin-take-rate commoditisation + disintermediation.** At ~2.84% take rate the pay-in-3 product is undifferentiated; card networks (Mastercard Flex Pay, Visa) and PayPal are pushing installments on their own rails — if issuers/networks capture BNPL at checkout, a layer like Klarna risks margin compression from the stack above it.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** A single-retailer go-live doesn't move Klarna's numbers, but M&S — GlobalData's "UK's most trusted retailer" — is a brand-signalling land-grab that completes UK department-store coverage and pressures Clearpay/PayPal on the same shelves `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: Klarna's UK GMV share keeps rising through 2027 as it wins trusted-brand merchants; trigger to watch — the next 1–2 earnings prints for UK/Europe GMV re-acceleration (would challenge the ex-growth narrative driving the ~1.3x P/S) vs. a further guidance cut (would confirm it). Watch too whether post-FCA affordability checks visibly hit BNPL conversion sector-wide.
