London-based payments platform Checkout.com says it's launching a share buyback program for employees at a $12 billion valuation.
The internal valuation represents a significant decline from the $40 billion assigned to Checkout.com in a 2022 funding round.
Several other fintech firms have opted to allow employees to sell shares instead of going public, including Stripe and Revolut.
LONDON — Fintech unicorn Checkout.com is giving staff a way of cashing in their shares: buying them out.
The London-headquartered payments platform said Friday that it plans to launch a share buyback initiative for employees to "provide them with a path to liquidity."
The share buyback program is based on a new internal valuation of $12 billion, Checkout.com said. Although internal, the valuation marks a significant drop from its last fundraising round — Checkout.com was valued at $40 billion in a $1 billion funding round in 2022.
The company previously lowered its internal valuation to $11 billion in 2022, and then again to $9.35 billion in 2023. Checkout.com says it regularly monitors the value for its employees in its share incentive program.
The fintech competes with payment service providers such as Stripe, Adyen and PayPal . The company processes billions of dollars in transactions every year for the likes of eBay , IKEA and Sainsbury's.
Such share sales have proven an increasingly popular way for startups to offer longtime employees and other investors liquidity, particularly as tech companies stay private for longer amid a multi-year decline in initial public offerings.
Checkout.com says it is now on track to exceed a target of 30% core net revenue growth this year and is forecasting $300 billion in annual e-commerce payment volume.
"We are relentlessly focused on growth and innovation, particularly with the impact of AI and the expected rise of agentic commerce," said Guillaume Pousaz, the company's CEO and founder, in a press release.
Several other private fintechs have opted to allow employees to sell shares in recent months.
In February, Stripe announced a tender offer allowing early investors and employees to sell shares at a valuation of $91.5 billion. Revolut, meanwhile, earlier this month offered staff the chance to sell shares on the secondary market at a $75 billion valuation.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- **The de-PR'd fact:** On 26 Sep 2025 Checkout.com set a **new internal $12B valuation** to run an **employee share buyback** (liquidity for staff), down from the **$40B** reached in its **$1B Series D (Jan 2022)**. The $12B is a **409A valuation** — an assessment by an **independent third party** tied to the buyback, **not** a priced external round. No new outside investors set this mark.
- **Why the framing matters:** "$12B, down from $40B" is a 70% haircut headline, but the trajectory is actually a **recovery**, not a fresh collapse: the internal mark had already been cut to **$11B (end-2022)** and **$9.35B (2023)**; $12B is the first mark-UP in three years. The "down from $40B" anchor flatters the drama by comparing to the 2022 ZIRP peak, not to the 2023 trough. **→ The real story is a slow re-rate off the bottom, mislabeled as a down-round.**
- **Why a buyback, not an IPO/round:** CEO Pousaz has said there are "no plans to go public." A 409A + staff buyback lets the company (a) give long-tenured employees liquidity without an IPO, and (b) re-mark equity on its own timetable rather than exposing itself to public-market or late-stage-VC pricing. Peers did the same in 2025: **Stripe** tender at **$91.5B** (Feb 2025), **Revolut** secondary at **$75B** (Sep 2025).

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Stripe:** peaked ~$95B (2021) → cut to $50B (2023) → recovered to $91.5B (Feb 2025), with secondary chatter near ~$107B. Same down-then-up arc as Checkout, but Stripe's trough-to-peak recovery is far larger.
- **Adyen:** the public yardstick (listed, profitable, EBITDA-margin disciplined) — the model Checkout is implicitly valued against.
- **PayPal:** legacy incumbent, multiple compressed.
- **Position:** Checkout is a **distant #3/#4 by scale** among global enterprise PSPs. At $12B on ~$750M annualised net revenue (see [2]), it trades ~16x net revenue — rich vs Adyen's public multiple but a fraction of Stripe's absolute valuation. **→ The multiple gap vs Stripe is about scale and growth durability, not sector; being a smaller, UK-based, profitable-but-slower-growing processor caps the re-rate.**

## [2] Company history / fit
- Trajectory: $40B (Jan 2022, $1B Series D) → $11B (end-2022) → $9.35B (2023) → $12B (Sep 2025, 409A/buyback).
- Operating turnaround since: **returned to full-year EBITDA profitability in 2025** ([[Checkout.com returns to profitability, passes $300B volume]]), **$300B volume (+64% YoY)**, net revenue **+30%** for a 2nd year, adj. EBITDA margin >10%, 1,000+ enterprise merchants, 63 "$1B club" (up from 39).
- **Sept 2026 update (newer, corroborated):** **$750M annualised net revenue (+28% TTM)**, first profit in **2024**, targeting **~$150M Adjusted EBITDA for FY2026**, ~$480B 2026 volume. NOTE: these are **"selective group financial figures"** (revenue is an August run-rate extrapolated ×12), **not audited accounts** — and $150M is **Adjusted EBITDA, not net profit**. **→ The $150M "profit" in the same-day digest is an adjusted-EBITDA target, caveated and self-reported.**
- **Why act this way:** a commodity take-rate business needs profitability + a software/agentic-commerce narrative to defend any multiple above a pure processor; hence the heavy AI/agentic-commerce and stablecoin positioning ([[Checkout.com prioritizes agentic commerce and stablecoins]], [[Checkout.com acquires euro stablecoin issuer Blue EMI]]).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Zero novelty in this item.** It is a year-old valuation mark re-surfaced as a newsletter link. The only genuinely new, dated data point in the surrounding corpus is the **Sep 2026 $750M / $150M adj-EBITDA** release — which belongs to a *different, later* story, not to this 26-Sep-2025 mark.
- **Where value is / isn't:** traction is real on the P&L side (profitability, $300B→$480B volume, growth 30%→28%). But the 28% figure vs 2025's 30%+ shows **decelerating growth**, which is precisely what a 409A-based $12B (vs Stripe's $91.5B) is pricing. **→ Who captures the margin: Checkout holds the merchant acquiring/processing layer, but agentic-commerce rails (Visa Intelligent Commerce, Mastercard Agent Pay) are controlled by the networks — Checkout risks being an integration/interop layer rather than the margin owner, which caps the multiple re-rate.**

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Sentiment framed as "glass half-full" (TechCrunch): decacorn status regained vs steep decline from peak. No IPO planned.
- Forward drivers: agentic commerce + stablecoins as the multiple-expansion story; continued EBITDA discipline.
- **Risks / second-order:** (1) growth deceleration (30%→28%) undercuts any re-rate toward Stripe-like multiples; (2) a self-set 409A mark is soft — not validated by an external round, so "$12B" is aspirational relative to what a priced round would yield; (3) network control of agentic rails caps where Checkout sits in the value stack. **→ For a digest, this item is at best a one-line "valuation context" footnote; the live, publishable Checkout story is the Sep-2026 $750M/$150M figures, not this 2025 mark.**

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Global payments processing/acquiring is a large, structurally growing but now multiple-compressed market. The protagonist is a private pure-play online acquirer (card + alt-methods + emerging stablecoin/agentic rails). Sector drivers: (1) secular e-commerce + cross-border volume growth — Checkout.com itself guides to ~$480bn payment volume and 28% net-revenue growth for FY2026 (per Tech.eu, 2026-09-29); (2) the "stay-private-longer" regime — with the IPO window thin, private fintechs run employee buybacks/tenders at internal marks instead of listing (Stripe, Revolut, Checkout all did so in 2025–26). "Why now": the 2022 peak-to-2026 re-rating is now visible in both private marks and public tape. Private: Checkout.com's internal mark fell $40bn (2022 round) → $11bn (2022) → $9.35bn (2023) → $12bn (2025) (per CNBC, note source); Klarna was cut ~85% from $45.6bn to $6.7bn before a ~$15bn IPO (per valueaddvc, 2026). Public: Fiserv shares -~60% YoY, revenue -4% in Q2 2026 on a cut guide (per PYMNTS/Investing.com, 2026); PayPal ~1.4x sales, ~70% below its 10-yr median (per americanbanker/windsordrake). The through-line: payments multiples compressed to ~5-yr lows by mid-2026, and private marks are re-anchoring toward public comps + realized profitability rather than 2021 growth-at-any-price.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/payment volume, net take-rate (net revenue / TPV), net-revenue growth, and EBITDA margin (profitability now the gating KPI, not just growth). Checkout.com implied net take-rate ≈ $750M / $480bn ≈ 0.16% (analysis) — thin, consistent with a large-enterprise/online acquirer mix (eBay, IKEA, Sainsbury's, Uber, Trip.com). Key players & basis of competition: Adyen (single-platform enterprise, price+reliability, 53% EBITDA margin FY2025), Stripe (developer distribution + product breadth, ~$1.9T TPV 2025), Fiserv/Clover and PayPal (incumbent scale, SMB/omnichannel), plus Worldpay. Recent peer moves: Stripe tender at $159bn (2026-02); Adyen FY2025 net revenue €2.36bn +18%, EBITDA €1.2bn (2026); Checkout pushing agentic commerce/stablecoins (Coinbase tie-up 2026-06, Forrester "leader" 2026-03). Protagonist position: niche-to-catching-up — a credible top-tier online-acquiring specialist but an order of magnitude smaller than Stripe/Adyen by revenue, and later to full-year profit (first profit 2024 vs Adyen's long-run high margins). Moat (analysis): switching costs + enterprise integrations and a modern single-stack, but no obvious network effect advantage over Adyen/Stripe; moat is "good but not differentiated."

**Comps & multiples.** Private-mark vs public-tape, revenue multiples (EBITDA-light names on revenue):
- **Checkout.com** — $12bn internal mark / ~$750M annualised net revenue = **16.0x** net revenue, at ~28% growth; adj. EBITDA ~$150M (2026E) → ~80x EBITDA (internal mark, not a market price).
- **Stripe** — $159bn tender / ~$6.8bn 2025 net revenue (est., Sacra) = **~23.4x**, at ~33% growth. (private round valuation, not market cap)
- **Adyen** (public) — EV/Revenue **~5.1x** (gurufocus, 2026-10-03; alt P/S 5.7x), EV/EBITDA ~10.8x; mkt cap ~$31bn, ~18% FY2025 growth, 53% EBITDA margin.
- **Fiserv** (public) — revenue declining, shares -~60% YoY; trades at a deep-value multiple (public mark-down comp, not a growth peer).
Internal base comps (wikilinks): [[Checkout.com reaches $12B valuation via employee share buyback]], [[Stripe reaches $159 billion valuation in employee tender offer]], [[Revolut plans secondary share sale at $100-120 billion valuation]], [[Adyen tops revenue estimates despite US tariff exemption end]].
Read: at 16.0x net revenue on ~28% growth, Checkout's internal mark sits **between** public Adyen (~5x, slower, highly profitable) and private Stripe (~23x, faster, far larger). Growth doesn't fully explain the gap to Adyen — the premium rests on private-mark discretion + growth optionality, not realized margin (Adyen's 53% EBITDA vs Checkout's ~20% implied on $750M rev). Not cleanly "cheap" or "rich"; call it **in-line-to-rich for a private mark**, with the caveat that an internal valuation is management-set, not a cleared market price. **Risk flags.**
1. **Internal-mark credibility / mark-to-market gap.** $12bn is a self-set share-plan valuation, not a priced round; the public comp set (Adyen ~5x, Fiserv/PayPal deeply de-rated) implies real downside if Checkout ever prints an external round or lists — why it matters: employee liquidity and recruiting leverage ride on a number the market hasn't validated.
2. **Thin take-rate + scale disadvantage.** ~0.16% net take-rate on $480bn means revenue is volume-levered and price-exposed; against Stripe/Adyen's scale and distribution, margin and pricing power can compress — second-order: profitability ($150M EBITDA) is young (first profit 2024) and could reverse under a share war.
3. **Narrative dependence on agentic commerce / stablecoins.** The growth-premium thesis leans on still-nascent rails (agentic checkout, stablecoin acceptance); if adoption lags, the 16x mark loses its justification and re-rates toward slower-growth acquiring comps.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a private-market re-rating story, not a fresh raise: a $40bn→$12bn round-trip crystallizes the 2022→2026 payments reset, with private marks now converging on public multiples + realized profit (analysis). Watch next: whether Checkout prints an external primary round (the real test of the $12bn), and whether net-revenue growth holds ~28%+ as it laps the TPV base — a slip toward Adyen-like high-teens growth without Adyen-like margins would make 16x look rich. Falsifying trigger: a priced external round or IPO at/above $12bn would validate the mark; a down-round or prolonged no-raise signals the internal number is still above clearing.
