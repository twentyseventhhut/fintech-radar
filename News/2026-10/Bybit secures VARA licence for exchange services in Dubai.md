Bybit secures VARA licence for exchange services in Dubai
The licence marks an important milestone in Bybit’s presence in Dubai, where the company established its international headquarters in 2022
Bybit, the New Financial Platform trusted by more than 80 million users worldwide, today announced that Bybit Fintech FZE has secured a Virtual Asset Service Provider (VASP) licence from Dubai’s Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (VARA) for Exchange Services, covering spot trading. 
The licence marks an important milestone in Bybit’s presence in Dubai, where the company established its international headquarters in 2022. It also supports Bybit’s continued efforts to build its business within established regulatory frameworks and contribute to the development of Dubai’s digital asset industry. 
“Dubai has made significant progress in building a regulatory environment for virtual assets, and we are pleased to take this next step in our journey here,” said Ben Zhou, Co-founder and CEO at Bybit . “We look forward to continuing our work in Dubai, supporting the local digital asset community and building our business in line with the standards set by VARA.” 
 Building for the Long Term   
“Securing this licence is a significant step forward for Bybit. We have great respect for VARA’s work in establishing a clear and robust framework for virtual assets,” said Peter Loo, Chief Legal and Compliance Officer at Bybit . “A healthy digital asset market needs strong oversight, sound risk management and a commitment to protecting users. We look forward to continuing our work with VARA as we grow our business in Dubai. In time, we also hope to expand our licensed activities to include derivatives, subject to the necessary regulatory approvals. Derivatives are a major part of Bybit’s global trading business, and we want to bring more of our offering to Dubai through the appropriate regulatory process.” 
For Bybit, the licence is part of a broader effort to build its business in each market in line with local requirements. The company continues to invest in compliance, security and risk management as it develops products and services for users across different jurisdictions. 
The Dubai milestone comes amid Bybit’s broader regulatory progress in 2026. In his October keynote, Ben highlighted five new licences and regulatory approvals across the European Union, the Cayman Islands, Indonesia, South Africa and El Salvador in 2026. 
These developments reflect Bybit’s continued focus on working with regulators and securing the appropriate permissions as it expands internationally. The company believes that strong regulatory relationships and locally compliant services are important to building lasting trust with users and market participants. 
 Supporting Dubai’s Digital Asset Ecosystem 
Since establishing its international headquarters in Dubai in 2022, Bybit has participated in the emirate’s growing blockchain and Web3 ecosystem through industry engagement, partnerships and initiatives that support knowledge-sharing and collaboration. 
Dubai’s regulatory framework and international reach make it an key market for the continued development of digital assets. Bybit intends to build on its presence in the emirate, working with relevant stakeholders and developing its services in line with applicable regulatory requirements.

# Context-enrichment: Bybit secures VARA licence for exchange services in Dubai
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 22 Oct 2026 Bybit announced that its Dubai entity, **Bybit Fintech FZE**, obtained a VASP licence from Dubai's **Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (VARA)** for **Exchange Services, spot trading only**. Two facts the press release soft-pedals:
- **It is spot-only, and not yet operational.** Derivatives — which are the core of Bybit's business (world's #2–3 by derivatives volume, ~$321B in Aug 2026) — are explicitly carved out and need a *separate* approval. VARA's register also shows Bybit must still complete VARA's launch process and meet operational requirements before it can actually serve customers. So this is a *permission to prepare to offer spot*, not a live product.
- **The licence register date is 20 June 2026**, i.e. the "news" is the public activation/announcement of a licence recorded months earlier (crypto.news / Yahoo frame it as "two years after a provisional nod" — Bybit's provisional/MVP step dates to its 2022–2023 Dubai arrival). This is a milestone-announcement, not a fresh grant.
**Why framed this way:** Bybit anchors the PR to "building within regulatory frameworks" and lists five other 2026 approvals (EU, Cayman, Indonesia, South Africa, El Salvador). That framing is doing reputational work post-2025: after the Feb-2025 **$1.5B Lazarus hack** (largest crypto heist ever), Bybit's narrative is "compliant, trusted, institutional-grade" — regulatory wins are the counter-story to the hack.

## [1] Competitors / peers
VARA's October-2026 register shows ~59 active licensees. On the spectrum of Dubai VASP status:
- **Binance** — full VASP licence since April 2024 (exchange + broker-dealer + lending); the dominant incumbent.
- **OKX** — full VASP, *already live retail* in Dubai with direct AED deposits/withdrawals. Ahead of Bybit operationally.
- **Kraken (Payward FZCO)** — got **full market approval** in Oct 2026, launching spot + margin + staking + tokenized equities with AED pairs in "coming weeks" ([[Kraken plans UAE launch after VARA market approval]], [[Kraken secures VARA authorization to expand in UAE]]).
- **Crypto.com** — preparatory/licensing stage, building AED banking rails ([[Crypto.com gets UAE approval to settle Dubai gov payments in stablecoins]]).
- **BitGo** — VARA broker-dealer ([[BitGo secures VARA broker-dealer licence in Dubai]], [[BitGo MENA licensed for institutional trading in Dubai]]).
**Position: catching up, and narrowly.** Bybit got a *narrower* grant (spot-only, not yet live) than Kraken's same-month full approval and well behind Binance/OKX who are already licensed and serving retail. **Why:** VARA phases licences (provisional → preparatory → operational), and derivatives + leverage draw the most regulator scrutiny — exactly Bybit's strength, so its Dubai licence lags its global product mix by design.

## [2] Company history / fit
Bybit set up its international HQ in Dubai in 2022 (global HQ since ~April 2023), did ~$33.5B MENA volume in year one, and has ~80M users. 2026 regulatory trail: [[Bybit secures full UAE virtual asset license]] (SCA — the *federal* UAE regulator, Oct 2025, covering trading/brokerage/custody/fiat), plus EU MiCA friction ([[Bybit limits EEA access as MiCA deadline nears]]), UK relaunch ([[Bybit relaunches UK crypto platform after two-year gap]]), and a pivot to "The New Financial Platform" (stocks, payments, super-app — [[Bybit opens US stocks trading to global users]], [[Bybit eyes payments, stocks, crypto super app in Türkiye]]). **Note the two-regulator nuance:** the 2025 SCA licence is a *UAE-federal* authorisation; VARA is the *Dubai emirate* authority — overlapping but distinct regimes, so this is not a duplicate of the SCA note. **Why Bybit chases licences hard:** post-$1.5B-hack, a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction compliance footprint is both a trust-rebuild and a pre-condition for the institutional/super-app pivot that needs banking relationships it cannot get while unlicensed.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Low novelty. Bybit is late to a licence Binance/OKX have held for 1–2 years, and it is narrower (spot-only) than peers' full approvals landing the same month. Real value-add is structural, not product: a VARA licence gives Bybit a *locally regulated Dubai entity* that can plug into AED banking rails and serve regulated retail/institutional flow — the gating factor for the super-app ambition. **Traction = zero so far:** the licence is not operational; no AED pairs, no live spot product announced, no derivatives (its actual money-maker). The margin in Dubai crypto accrues to whoever has (a) AED on/off-ramps and (b) derivatives permission — Bybit has neither yet. **What breaks the thesis:** if derivatives approval stalls, the Dubai entity is a low-margin spot venue competing head-on with already-live OKX/Binance.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Next steps: complete VARA's operational/launch process, stand up AED rails, then pursue the separate derivatives permission (Loo: "a major part of Bybit's global trading business"). Sentiment is neutral-positive but routine — this is one of many 2026 licences and coverage frames it as incremental ("but derivatives must wait"). Regulatory backdrop: VARA is consolidating Dubai as a hub (59 licensees, enforcement actions against unlicensed firms — KuCoin halt orders, 19-firm penalty) ([[Dubai's VARA orders KuCoin to halt unlicensed operations]], [[Dubai's VARA penalises 19 unlicensed crypto firms]]). **Second-order:** as VARA licenses the whole top tier (Binance, OKX, Kraken, Bybit, Crypto.com), a Dubai licence becomes table-stakes, not differentiation — compliance moats commoditise and competition shifts back to fees, liquidity and product depth, where Bybit's locked-out derivatives business is its real lever.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Dubai/VARA has turned into the leading structured onshore hub for a global crypto-exchange regulatory land-grab. Per the VARA public register as read 28 Jul 2026, ~52 fully licensed VASPs operate in Dubai, but only ~14 hold the "Exchange Services" category Bybit just received (via cryptonite.ae tracker). Structure is a barriered club: VARA layers permissions (Exchange / Broker-Dealer / Custody / Advisory / Lending) and gates each activity separately — Bybit's own licence is spot-only, with derivatives still to be applied for. Why now: post-GENIUS/MiCAR, large offshore exchanges are racing to convert flexible-but-fragile operating footprints into locally compliant entities; Bybit itself cites five new 2026 licences (EU, Cayman, Indonesia, South Africa, El Salvador) alongside Dubai (per note / company keynote). The driver is regulatory arbitrage closing: access to institutional money and banking rails increasingly requires an onshore licence, so the land-grab is really a scramble for the legitimacy and fiat on-ramps that unlock that capital (analysis).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: spot/derivatives trading volume, market share, funded users, proof-of-reserves. Bybit is a top-tier CEX globally: ~80M users (IR, Bybit 2025 Recap, 2026-01-03) and consistently #2 by spot volume behind Binance — ~9.19% spot share in Q2 2026 vs Binance ~32% (per TokenInsight, via spotedcrypto). Its true strength is derivatives: ~$321bn Aug-2026 derivatives volume, #2-#3 venue industry-wide at ~8.1% share (per PR Newswire / Manila Times, Sep 2026). In the VARA Exchange cohort it is a heavyweight relative to the other ~13 holders. Recent peer moves in the same Dubai lane: BitGo secured a VARA Broker-Dealer licence for institutional spot (Oct 2025); Bybit itself won a full UAE SCA Virtual Asset Platform Operator licence (Oct 2025), so Dubai/VARA layers on top of the federal SCA permission. Position: ahead on global scale, catching up on local scope — the moat is scale/liquidity and a 2022 Dubai HQ footprint (switching costs for existing users; network effects on liquidity) (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** Bybit is private and discloses no valuation, revenue or EBITDA — EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E all **[UNSOURCED]**; no multiple computable. Only usable public benchmark is a listed/semi-disclosed peer: [[Kraken reports Q3 2025 financial highlights]] posted Q3-2025 adjusted revenue of $648.0m (+50% q/q), adjusted EBITDA $178.6m (27.6% margin) on $576.8bn platform volume and 5.2m funded accounts — i.e. roughly Bybit-scale economics imply a large, profitable business, but with no Bybit numerator the comparison is **qualitative, distribution not computed**. Internal regulatory/scope comps: [[Bybit secures full UAE virtual asset license]] (SCA, full scope incl. custody/brokerage), [[BitGo secures VARA broker-dealer licence in Dubai]] (institutional spot). This VARA licence is spot-only, a narrower scope than Bybit's own SCA permission — read as incremental de-risking, not a re-rating.

**Risk flags.** (1) **Scope gap / derivatives dependence** — the licence covers spot only, yet derivatives (~$321bn/mo, ~8% share) are the core of Bybit's economics; until VARA approves derivatives, the Dubai entity captures the lower-margin, more-commoditised leg while the profitable book stays offshore/unregulated here. (2) **Issue-vs-announce lag & regulatory conditionality** — VARA recorded the issue date as 20 Jun 2026 but Bybit announced 22 Oct 2026 (per crypto.news); derivatives expansion is explicitly "subject to the necessary regulatory approvals," so the headline is partly a compliance PR beat, not new live capability. (3) **Legacy security/trust overhang** — Bybit carries the 2025 hack in its public record (per coinlaw.io); onshore licensing raises scrutiny on reserves and risk management, and any lapse now carries licence-revocation risk, not just reputational cost.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector, Dubai is consolidating as the compliance checkpoint every tier-1 CEX must pass; the live question is not "will Bybit get licensed" but "will it move derivatives onshore" — the falsifiable thesis: if VARA grants Bybit a derivatives permission within ~12 months, Dubai becomes a genuine onshore venue for leveraged crypto (bullish for the hub); if it stalls, the licence stays a spot-only marketing token and real volume remains offshore (analysis). Watch: VARA derivatives approval and the next proof-of-reserves report.
