Hello Clever partners with Infinia to scale across Latin America
 Hello Clever has partnered with Infinia to expand its payment operations across Latin America. The partnership gives Hello Clever access to local payment rails in Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, Colombia, Peru, Bolivia, Uruguay and Paraguay.
Latin America’s e-commerce market is projected to exceed US$215 billion in 2026, growing 1.5 times faster than the global average. As online commerce expands, merchants need payment experiences that reflect how consumers actually pay in each market.
Through Infinia’s infrastructure, Hello Clever will access virtual accounts, local pay-ins and payouts, and on- and off-ramp services through a single partner. This removes the need to manage multiple financial providers and separate local payment integrations in each country.
Local payment systems are central to this approach. From Brazil’s Pix and Mexico’s SPEI to Argentina’s Transferencias 3.0, Colombia’s Bre-B or Peru’s interoperable Yape payments, each market has its own established payment methods and infrastructure. Connecting to these systems helps merchants offer familiar ways to pay while simplifying the complexity behind cross-border operations.
For merchants, one integration with Hello Clever is now enough to sell into all eight countries. Shoppers in those markets can pay using the local methods they already know and trust. Merchants can grow their sales in the region without adding new payment providers.
“Expanding across markets requires reliable local infrastructure. Infinia came highly recommended, and it has deep local payment capabilities across Latin America. By connecting Infinia’s rails into our platform, we’re giving our merchants access to eight new markets without the complexity of adding new providers,” said Caroline Tran, Co-Founder and CEO of Hello Clever.
“Businesses operating across borders need more than international transfers. They need the local infrastructure to collect, convert and disburse funds in each market. We’re helping Hello Clever connect its platform to the financial rails needed to operate across Latin America,” said Ianai Urwicz, CEO and Co-Founder of Infinia.
The partnership combines Hello Clever’s payments platform with Infinia’s multi-market infrastructure. It provides a foundation for Hello Clever’s continued growth in the region, and both companies expect it to drive new partnerships and revenue as Hello Clever adds more payment corridors around the world.
“Every corridor we open makes it easier for our merchants to sell globally. Latin America is a major step in that journey, and we’re excited to be building it with Infinia,” said Tran.
The partnership comes as Hello Clever opens its new global headquarters in New York, marking the company’s next chapter of growth and a stronger global focus for its operations. Founded in Sydney, Hello Clever now has teams across Australia, Singapore, Japan and the US.

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On ~2026-10-21 **Hello Clever** (Sydney-founded AI payments/loyalty fintech) announced a partnership with **Infinia** (Montevideo-based payments-infrastructure startup) to connect Infinia's local payment rails across **eight Latin American markets** — Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, Colombia, Peru, Bolivia, Uruguay and Paraguay. Through one Infinia integration, Hello Clever gains **virtual accounts, local pay-ins/payouts, and on/off-ramp services**, letting its merchants sell into all eight countries via a single integration rather than stitching together market-by-market providers. Announced alongside Hello Clever opening a **new global HQ in New York**. This is a commercial rails/distribution partnership — no financial terms, volumes or committed merchant counts disclosed.

**[1] Who the parties are.**
- **Hello Clever** — founded 2021 in Sydney by CEO Caroline Tran and Gavin (Vy) Nguyen; an instant-cashback "buy-to-earn" / real-time payments platform originally built on Australia's NPP/PayTo/PayID (was the first platform to run a live PayTo pre-authorised payment in Australia). Raised a ~A$4.5M seed (led by Vectr Fintech). It reports "tens of millions" in annualised revenue and 1,000+ merchants, and positions itself as a "Payments Intelligence" platform unifying global real-time rails + loyalty + AI; teams across Australia, Singapore, Japan and the US.
- **Infinia** — see [[Infinia secures $13.5M Series A to bridge banking and digital assets]] (July 2026, $13.5M led by Bain Capital + Variant, YC) and [[Infinia What happens when we tokenize everything]] (Oct 2026 vision essay). Uruguay-based, CEO Ianai Urwicz (ex-dLocal founding team). Sells a unified API bridging local instant rails (Pix, SPEI, Transferencias 3.0, Bre-B, Yape) with digital-asset/stablecoin infrastructure for emerging-market pay-ins/payouts/virtual accounts. This deal is the kind of merchant-platform integration Infinia's Series A was meant to fund.

**[2] Why it matters.** It converts Infinia's "we build the LatAm off-ramp layer" thesis into a named distribution win, and gives Hello Clever a fast on-ramp into LatAm's large e-commerce region (digest cites ~US$215bn LatAm e-commerce in 2026, ~1.5x global growth; broader trackers put the top markets even higher). Builds on Hello Clever's global-expansion playbook of partnering for rails rather than building them — same pattern as [[Hello Clever partners Checkout.com for global expansion]] (Apr 2026, card/processing globally; this deal adds LatAm A2A local rails). LatAm context: Pix now ~70%+ of Brazilian e-commerce value with 160M+ users; Mexico's SPEI/wallets >45% of e-com payments — so "support local methods via one integration" is a genuine merchant pain-point.

**[3] Prior art / competition.** Infinia's local-rails aggregation competes directly with **dLocal** (Urwicz's former employer; Apr-2026 "Stablecoin Full" across 44+ EM markets), plus EBANX, PayRetailers, Bitso Business, and Stripe/Bridge. For Hello Clever the choice is build-vs-buy; it chose a single aggregator. Related in-base LatAm payments/rails: [[ACI Worldwide and Prosa upgrade Latin America payments infrastructure]], [[Alchemy Pay launches fiat payment rails solution, debuts with Brazil's PIX payment system]], [[Peru's Ligo integrates Apple Pay, Google Pay and QR payments]], [[Klavi, Trio and WePayOut approved as Brazil ITPs]].

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** LatAm cross-border payments orchestration — a merchant connects one integration and reaches multiple countries' local rails (Pix/BR, SPEI/MX, Transferencias 3.0/AR, Bre-B/CO, Yape/PE). Market pull: LatAm e-commerce projected >$215bn in 2026, "growing 1.5x faster than global average" (per the companies, via australianfintech.com.au, as of 2026-10-21 — a PR-cited figure, treat as directional); a separate industry guide puts the region's six largest e-commerce markets at ~$870bn in 2026 (per dLocal blog, undated — scope differs, "no data" on reconciliation). Structure: fragmented at the local-rail layer (each country has its own instant-payment scheme + regulator), which is exactly why an aggregation/orchestration layer exists — value accrues to whoever abstracts the 8 integrations into 1. Barriers: regulatory licensing per market (money transmission, FX), local banking relationships, fraud/payee-validation. "Why now": instant-payment rails (Pix launched 2020; Bre-B, Transferencias 3.0 newer) are now ubiquitous enough that card-only checkout leaves conversion on the table, so global merchants need APM coverage to enter LatAm.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV, take rate (gross profit / TPV), net revenue retention, number of local payment methods/corridors. Key players by model: pure-play emerging-markets payment processors (dLocal, EBANX), regional acquirers (Kushki), consumer-wallet-led (Mercado Pago), developer-stack global (Stripe), and infra/orchestration enablers (Infinia, Bitso Business). Basis of competition: breadth of local coverage + single-integration simplicity + settlement/FX + fraud. Recent moves: dLocal × ACI Worldwide (Jul 2026) — one integration to Pix/PicPay/Mercado Pago/NuPay (BR) and Mercado Pago/OXXO/SPEI (MX), with AR/CL/CO/PE planned — the same "fewer integrations, more local methods" thesis; Infinia also partnered with iPiD (payee validation) and Open Standard (USD infra) in 2026. Protagonist's position: Hello Clever is a merchant-facing payments/loyalty platform (Sydney-founded, now HQ'd NY; teams in AU/SG/JP/US), NOT a LatAm rail owner — it is a *distributor* of Infinia's rails, catching up to incumbents via a partner rather than building local presence `(analysis)`. Moat is thin on the LatAm side (it rents Infinia's infrastructure); its differentiation sits in its AI "buy-to-earn"/rewards layer and merchant base, not in payment rails.

**Comps & multiples.** Public anchor — **dLocal (NASDAQ:DLO):** market cap ~$4.4bn (289.8M shares × ~$15.23, Oct 2026) ≈ EV (modest net cash, treat EV≈market cap `(analysis)`); TTM revenue $1.09bn → **EV/Revenue ≈ $4.4bn / $1.09bn = 4.0x**. Operating profit is positive (Q2'26 op profit $64M; NRR 153%, TPV +92% YoY to $17.7bn) — a profitable, high-growth comp, so 4.0x is cheap-to-in-line for the growth. Private comp — **EBANX:** ~$1bn disclosed valuation on 2025 revenue $360.9M → **~2.8x revenue** (round/last-disclosed valuation, not a market cap; via getlatka, stale) — same emerging-markets model, roughly in-line with dLocal on a revenue basis. Internal comps: [[Hello Clever partners Checkout.com for global expansion]] (Apr 2026 — same protagonist, same partner-the-rails playbook, processing via Checkout.com), [[Infinia secures $13.5M Series A to bridge banking and digital assets]] (Jul 2026 — Infinia's $13.5M Series A; a seed/Series-A-stage infra provider), [[Infinia What happens when we tokenize everything]] (Oct 2026 — Infinia's tokenization thesis). No deal economics disclosed for this partnership (no revenue share, TPV target, or exclusivity) → multiple for the deal itself = "no data".

**Risk flags.**
1. **Rail dependence / disintermediation.** Hello Clever does not own LatAm rails — it fully depends on Infinia (itself a thin ~$13.5M-Series-A infra startup) for virtual accounts, pay-ins/payouts and on/off-ramps. Partner outage, re-pricing, or Infinia being out-executed by dLocal/EBANX directly undercuts the 8-market promise; the margin on each corridor is shared with Infinia, capping take rate `(analysis)`.
2. **Announced vs live.** Press says merchants "can now" sell into 8 countries, but there is no stated go-live date, licensing status per market, or merchant count on the corridor — classic PR silence on economics and adoption. Eight regulated markets at once is heavy execution risk for a partner-dependent rollout.
3. **Incumbent competition + "one integration" is now table stakes.** dLocal (public, profitable, $17.7bn quarterly TPV) and EBANX (~$1bn valuation) already offer the identical single-integration LatAm value prop with owned rails and deeper coverage; Hello Clever enters as a late, indirect distributor with no disclosed cost or coverage edge.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a *distribution* play, not a re-rating event — Hello Clever is bolting LatAm onto a loyalty/AI payments platform via a partner, extending the same "corridor-by-corridor" global strategy it ran with Checkout.com. Falsifiable thesis `(analysis)`: if the partnership is real traction, within ~2-3 quarters expect disclosed metrics (merchants onboarded, TPV/corridors live, or a named large merchant). Trigger that breaks the thesis: no adoption numbers surface and the next Hello Clever headline is another greenfield "partners X for region Y" announcement — a sign it is press-release expansion, not booked volume. Watch whether Infinia can scale regulated coverage before a well-capitalized incumbent (dLocal/EBANX) simply out-prices the orchestration layer.
