By Echo Wang and Milana Vinn
NEW YORK, Oct 20 (Reuters) - Blackstone Inc and Warburg Pincus are exploring a sale of IntraFi that could value the financial technology company at more than $10 billion, according to people familiar with the matter.
The private equity firms have hired Morgan Stanley and Guggenheim Securities to advise IntraFi on the sale process, which is in its early stages, said the people, who requested anonymity speaking about matters that are not public.
No final decision has been made and Blackstone and Warburg could opt to hold onto the business, the people added.
Blackstone and IntraFi declined to comment. Guggenheim, Morgan Stanley and Warburg Pincus did not immediately respond to requests for comment. 
Arlington, Virginia-based IntraFi operates what it describes as the largest reciprocal deposit network in the United States, helping banks spread customers' money across multiple banks to provide federal deposit insurance on balances exceeding $250,000.
Advertisement
The company's network also allows banks to buy and sell deposits. Banks with excess deposits can sell them to banks that need funding, with IntraFi earning fees from the transactions.
The failure of Silicon Valley Bank in 2023 made customers more selective about where they kept their money, intensifying competition among banks for deposits.
Founded in 2002 by three former bank regulators, IntraFi was acquired by Blackstone in 2019 from a group of backers that included Bank of New York Mellon, when the company was known as Promontory Interfinancial Network. Warburg Pincus invested in the business in 2022. IntraFi generates $630 million in annual EBITDA, the sources said.
The sale process comes as private equity firms seek to capitalize on improving dealmaking conditions. Warburg Pincus recently agreed to sell McGill and Partners and WildFire Energy, while Blackstone this month agreed to sell Clarion Events to Informa for about 2.2 billion pounds.
The two firms previously explored a sale of IntraFi, Bloomberg News reported in 2024.
 (Reporting by Echo Wang and Milana Vinn in New York; Editing by Nick Zieminski)

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
### [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Per Reuters (Oct 20, 2026), Blackstone and Warburg Pincus are **exploring** a sale of IntraFi that *could* value it at **>$10B**. Status is "early stages," no decision made, and the owners may keep the asset — this is a sell-side **sounding**, not a signed deal. They retained Morgan Stanley and Guggenheim Securities to run the process. The one hard number is **$630M annual EBITDA** (sourced to anonymous people, not audited): a >$10B tag implies roughly a **16x EBITDA multiple** — rich for a business whose revenue is fundamentally a derivative of banks' deposit-competition intensity and interest-rate levels.
- **Why framed this way (→ why it matters):** PE sell-side leaks at a round, flattering number ($10B+, EBITDA not revenue) are a price-discovery / IPO-dual-track tactic — floating a valuation anchor before committing. The same two owners "previously explored a sale" in 2024 (Bloomberg) and instead did a **$2B dividend recap** in Aug 2025 ([[Banks complete $2 billion debt deal for IntraFi]]) — i.e. they monetized without selling. A second public exploration after a prior abandoned one signals the owners want liquidity but may again settle for a recap/partial sale if bids miss $10B.
- **De-PR'd mechanism:** IntraFi runs the largest US **reciprocal-deposit network** (CDARS/ICS brands, ~3,000+ banks). It slices a depositor's >$250k balance across many member banks so each slice stays under the FDIC cap, keeping the customer "fully insured" while the home bank retains the relationship. It also brokers one-way deposit buy/sell between funding-short and funding-long banks, earning fees per transaction. The business is a **two-sided network/toll booth on deposit insurance**, not a tech product.

### [1] Competitors / peers
- **R&T Deposit Solutions** (Demand Deposit Marketplace / reciprocal + sweep) — distant #2, est. ~$48M revenue.
- **StoneCastle Cash Management** — acquired by **Fiserv** (announced Sep 2025, [[Fiserv to acquire cash manager StoneCastle]]); now embedded in a core-banking + FIUSD-stablecoin platform rather than a standalone network.
- **ModernFi** — VC-backed newer entrant (deposit network as API/embedded).
- **Why the landscape matters (→ second order):** IntraFi's moat is the **network effect** — the largest bank roster is the hardest to replicate, which is what justifies a premium over R&T/StoneCastle. But Fiserv's StoneCastle buy and the broader **tokenized-deposit** builds by top banks ([[Top US banks plan tokenized deposit network for 2027]], [[Anchorage launches tokenized deposit platform for banks]]) point to the real threat: deposit allocation getting **bundled into core/stablecoin/tokenization rails** owned by incumbents, commoditizing a standalone toll. A strategic (Fiserv-style) buyer values the network; a financial buyer must underwrite that bundling risk.

### [2] Company history / fit
Founded 2002 as **Promontory Interfinancial Network** by former bank regulators (invented reciprocal deposits). Sold by backers incl. **BNY Mellon** to **Blackstone for ~$2.5B in 2019**; rebranded IntraFi; **Warburg Pincus** bought in (2022). Aug 2025: **$2B debt deal** (Morgan Stanley–led) to pay a PE dividend and refinance riskier debt.
- **Why the owners act this way (→):** Classic PE arc — ~$2.5B entry (2019) → dividend recap (2025) → now exit-sounding at ~4x entry. The business threw off enough cash to support a $2B debt raise, confirming high-margin, low-capex economics that PE prizes. The sell-side timing ("improving dealmaking conditions," Blackstone selling Clarion Events, Warburg selling McGill/WildFire) is a **portfolio-wide liquidity push**, not IntraFi-specific urgency — strengthening the read that they'll walk if the bid is light.

### [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Nothing new operationally — this is a **transaction rumor**, not a product. The durable value-add is real and structural: reciprocal deposits convert the **$250k FDIC cap into recurring fee revenue**, and demand spiked after **SVB's 2023 failure** made depositors insurance-conscious. Traction = the ~3,000-bank network + $630M EBITDA.
- **Where the margin and the risk sit (→ 1–2 levels deep):** The revenue is **cyclical and policy-dependent**. It rises when (a) rates are high (banks fight for deposits) and (b) depositors fear bank failure. Both are mean-reverting. Worse, the single largest tail risk is **legislative**: if Congress raised or removed the $250k FDIC cap, the core reason-to-exist shrinks — and reporting (American Prospect, 2023) shows IntraFi has **lobbied to keep caps in place**, i.e. its moat is partly a regulatory artifact it actively defends. A financial buyer paying 16x is underwriting the durability of a cap IntraFi itself must protect.

### [4] What's next / market sentiment
Likely paths: (a) full sale to a strategic (payments/core/stablecoin platform wanting deposit distribution — the Fiserv/StoneCastle logic), (b) another **dividend recap / minority sale** if bids miss $10B, or (c) an **IPO dual-track**. Given the 2024 abandoned attempt + 2025 recap, (b)/(c) are plausible fallbacks, not just (a).
- **Counterintuitive second-order:** A $10B+ price looks like a vote of confidence in deposit-network economics, but it may mark a **cycle top** — valuing peak post-SVB, high-rate fee flows just as (i) rates ease, (ii) tokenized/stablecoin deposit rails threaten to disintermediate the toll, and (iii) FDIC-reform chatter resurfaces. The strongest bid will come from a strategic that can bundle the network; a pure-financial sponsor flip at 16x would be buying late-cycle cash flows dressed as a secular growth asset.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
1. **Is this a deal or a rumor?** A rumor — Reuters says "exploring," "early stages," no decision; owners may keep it. Not an event beyond a price signal.
2. **Is the $10B+ real or an anchor?** It's an anonymous-sourced sell-side target, not a bid or agreed price. Implies ~16x the (also anonymous) $630M EBITDA. Treat as the owners' ask, not market clearing price.
3. **Is $630M EBITDA audited?** No — "the sources said." Private company; no public financials. Load-bearing number is unverified.
4. **Haven't they tried this before?** Yes — Bloomberg reported a 2024 exploration that did NOT result in a sale; they did a $2B dividend recap in Aug 2025 instead. Raises odds this round also ends in a recap/no-sale.
5. **Why sell now vs. 2024?** "Improving dealmaking conditions" + a Blackstone/Warburg portfolio-wide liquidity push (Clarion, McGill, WildFire) — macro/fund-cycle driven, not IntraFi fundamentals. Open: whether DPI pressure on the funds is forcing exits.
6. **How cyclical is the revenue?** Highly. Driven by high rates (deposit competition) and post-SVB insurance anxiety — both mean-reverting. A buyer at 16x is paying for arguably peak-cycle cash flows.
7. **What's the single biggest tail risk?** Legislative change to the $250k FDIC cap. If raised/abolished, the reciprocal-deposit rationale shrinks. IntraFi lobbies to preserve the cap (American Prospect 2023) — moat is partly a regulatory artifact.
8. **Who disintermediates it?** Tokenized/stablecoin deposit rails (top-bank network for 2027; Anchorage; Fiserv+StoneCastle+FIUSD) could bundle deposit allocation into core/payments stacks, commoditizing a standalone toll. Open: timeline and whether reciprocal insurance is still needed in a tokenized world.
9. **Does the network moat hold?** Largest ~3,000-bank roster is genuinely hard to replicate — real network effect and the main premium justification vs R&T/StoneCastle. This is the bull case.
10. **Strategic vs financial buyer?** A strategic (payments/core/stablecoin) can bundle and extract synergy; the Fiserv–StoneCastle precedent shows strategics want deposit distribution. A pure-PE flip at 16x is riskier (buying late-cycle). Open: who actually bids.
11. **Why EBITDA not revenue in the leak?** EBITDA flatters a high-margin toll business and frames it as a cash machine; revenue would expose the thin topline and cyclicality. PR-framing tell.
12. **Could it IPO instead?** Plausible dual-track given the abandoned 2024 sale. Open: no filing or banker signal of an IPO path yet.
13. **Antitrust / concentration angle?** IntraFi + R&T + StoneCastle(now Fiserv) is a concentrated space; a strategic deposit-network consolidation could draw scrutiny. Open — not flagged in current reporting.
14. **What breaks the multiple?** Rate cuts, FDIC-cap reform, tokenized-deposit disintermediation, or a soft auction forcing a recap. Any one compresses the 16x.
15. **Does this change the central question?** Yes — from "how big is IntraFi's exit" to "is a 16x multiple on post-SVB, high-rate, cap-dependent fee flows a secular asset or a late-cycle flip?"

Importance: 3/5 — a large, named-advisor sell-side process for a systemically relevant deposit-infrastructure asset at a >$10B tag is noteworthy and newsworthy, but it is an exploratory rumor (not a signed deal), the key figures are anonymous, and the same owners abandoned a prior attempt — so it's a market signal, not a closed event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Reciprocal / insured-deposit networks are a niche B2B banking-infrastructure layer: they split a large deposit across many FDIC-insured banks so each $250k slice stays insured, and let banks buy/sell excess deposits, with the network operator earning per-transaction/placement fees. Market size: reciprocal deposit balances reached ~$440bn in September 2025 across ~2,105 participating banks, up from ~$156bn in 2022 — roughly a tripling in three years (per Dallas Fed research, as of 2025, via dallasfed.org). At an average access cost of ~12–15bps on ~$422bn, the Dallas Fed implies a ~$500–600m annual industry fee pool — i.e. IntraFi's $630m EBITDA is of the same order as the whole sector's gross fee take, consistent with its near-monopoly position (analysis). Structure: highly consolidated around one operator; the barrier is a two-sided network effect (a placement bank is only useful if thousands of counterparties already participate) plus regulatory familiarity (founded by three ex-bank regulators). Why now: the March 2023 regional-bank failures (SVB) made large depositors far more insurance-conscious, structurally lifting demand for insured-deposit placement and competition for deposits among banks — a secular, not cyclical, tailwind. "Why now" for the *sale*: PE sellers (Blackstone 2019, Warburg 2022) timing an exit into improved 2026 dealmaking conditions.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: deposits placed/under management (balance), number of participating institutions, and fee/placement take (bps). Key players: IntraFi (incumbent, dominant) vs much smaller challengers — StoneCastle (now Fiserv), R&T Deposit Solutions (Reich & Tang), ModernFi, and fintech-adjacent cash sweep programs. Basis of competition is network reach, not price: IntraFi reports 91% of the top-100 US banks and ~64% of all US banks as participants (per IntraFi/Semafor, as of 2024, via semafor.com) — a dominant position with a network-effects + scale moat. Recent peer move: Fiserv signed (Sept 2025) and closed (Dec 17, 2025) the acquisition of StoneCastle's deposit network (1,000+ FDIC institutions) — consolidation signal that strategics now want this rail bundled with core banking (per Fiserv/BusinessWire, SEC 10-K FY2025). IntraFi's position: clear category leader; the watch item is disintermediation by bank-built tokenized-deposit networks rather than by a like-for-like reciprocal competitor.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal multiple on the pick: ~$10bn / $630m EBITDA = **~15.9x EV/EBITDA** (reported EBITDA; revenue, growth rate and net debt not disclosed → EV/Revenue and P/E = no data). Internal comps:
- [[Fiserv to acquire cash manager StoneCastle]] / [[Fiserv to acquire StoneCastle Cash Management for stablecoin]] — StoneCastle deposit network bought by Fiserv for **$415m** (Dec 2025; ~$165m intangibles, ~$247m goodwill, per Fiserv 10-K FY2025). Earnings/EBITDA for StoneCastle undisclosed → implied multiple = no data; useful only as a scale reference (StoneCastle is ~1/20th the headline value of IntraFi, consistent with "competitors are tiny").
- IntraFi's own history: Blackstone bought it (as Promontory Interfinancial) for **~$2.5bn** in 2019 (per Buyouts/press). $2.5bn → $10bn+ over ~7 years ≈ ~4x headline value, i.e. roughly a ~22% CAGR on valuation (analysis) — most of the re-rating consistent with the post-2023 balance surge.
- Sector-adjacent deposit-infra moves in base: [[JPMorgan, Citi plan tokenized deposit network via Clearing House in 2027]], [[Top US banks plan tokenized deposit network for 2027]], [[BNY explores tokenized deposits for payments network]] — adjacent (bank-owned rails), not direct trading comps.

~15.9x EV/EBITDA is **in-line to modestly rich** for a software-like, high-margin, near-monopoly network with secular growth — within the normal 8–25x EV/EBITDA band and arguably cheap for a dominant, asset-light toll-booth if the deposit tailwind persists; the risk is paying a cyclical-peak EBITDA multiple as if it were structural.

**Risk flags.**
1. Regulatory/political risk to the core economics: IntraFi's fees depend on the $250k insurance cap staying in place; any move to raise or restructure deposit-insurance limits would shrink the reason customers need reciprocal placement — a direct hit to the fee pool (why: the product monetizes a gap created by policy).
2. Disintermediation by tokenized / bank-owned deposit networks (JPMorgan/Citi/BNY efforts for 2027): if the largest banks build their own shared deposit-movement rails, the top end of IntraFi's network could migrate off-platform, eroding exactly the top-100-bank base that anchors the network effect.
3. Cyclical EBITDA at a structural multiple: the $630m EBITDA partly reflects the post-SVB deposit scramble and a high-rate environment; a buyer paying ~16x on possibly peak earnings bears re-rating risk if balances/rates normalize (why: multiple × earnings both compress together).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a late-stage consolidation/exit print, not a new entry: PE is marking the category at ~$10bn after Fiserv's $415m StoneCastle tuck-in showed strategic appetite for insured-deposit rails (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: the dominant reciprocal-deposit toll-booth is durable and a strategic (large bank-tech vendor) or another PE buyer clears ~$10bn+. Trigger/catalyst: a signed deal and buyer identity (strategic vs PE). What would make it wrong: the process is held (the sellers explicitly may keep it), OR deposit-insurance reform / bank tokenized-deposit networks visibly start pulling balances off the network — either would cap the multiple below the $10bn ask.
