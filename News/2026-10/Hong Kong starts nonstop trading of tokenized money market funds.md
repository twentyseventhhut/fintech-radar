## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened.** On 26–27 October 2026 Hong Kong's Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) approved the world's first secondary-market trading of tokenised SFC-authorised public funds on a licensed virtual-asset trading platform (VATP). Two already-authorised tokenised money-market funds — the **ChinaAMC (HK) HKD Digital Money Market Fund** and the **ChinaAMC USD Digital Money Market Fund** — went live for around-the-clock (24/7) retail secondary trading. Until now, investors in such tokenised funds could only subscribe/redeem in the primary market during dealing hours; they can now buy and sell the tokens on an exchange at any hour.

**Who operates it.** This is not an HKMA/HKEX central venue. Secondary trading runs on **PantherTrade**, an SFC-licensed VATP incubated by **Futu Holdings** (accessed via Futu's Futubull app), jointly launched with **ChinaAMC (Hong Kong)**, the local arm of one of China's largest asset managers. PantherTrade reportedly began offering the two funds (tickers reported as CUMFU-USD / CAMFF-HKD) on 15 October 2026 at 17:00 HKT. The SFC supplies the regulatory authorisation; the private VATP supplies the matching/trading venue.

**Regulatory mechanics.** The launch sits on a **new SFC pilot framework published April 2026** for secondary trading of tokenised SFC-authorised funds on licensed VATPs. The SFC extends its existing ETF/VATP rulebook — fair-pricing duties, orderly-trading and liquidity-provider requirements, and disclosure standards — to on-chain open-ended funds. Separately, the SFC and **HKMA plan to use Project Ensemble** (the HKMA's wholesale-CBDC / tokenisation sandbox-turned-pilot) to connect tokenised deposits with asset trading and streamline trading-to-settlement, enabling real-time transfer of sale proceeds.

**Why it matters.** MMFs normally have early-afternoon dealing cut-offs and same-day-at-best settlement, so cash that might be needed overnight sits idle in a bank account. Continuous trading plus tokenised-deposit settlement lets investors keep surplus cash earning fund yield until the moment it is needed — the same "always-on idle-cash sweep" logic seen in [[Ant International sweeps idle cash into HSBC tokenized fund]].

**Prior art / internal links.** This is the trading-venue capstone of a multi-year HK programme: [[Hong Kong launches tokenisation pilot to boost fintech]] and [[Hong Kong pilots tokenized deposit and digital asset transactions]] (Project Ensemble moving from sandbox to live, flagged as focused on tokenised MMF trades + real-time treasury) and [[Hong Kong eases virtual asset licensing rules to boost liquidity]]. Globally, tokenised MMFs are led by **BlackRock BUIDL** (~US$2.5–2.9B AUM in 2026, via Securitize) and **Franklin Templeton FOBXX/BENJI** (roughly US$0.8–2B); see [[Franklin Templeton and DigiFT expand tokenized securities access in Asia]], [[Payward and Franklin Templeton collaborate on tokenized assets]], and [[Bybit and DigiFT enable UBS tokenized fund as trading collateral]]. The HK novelty is not tokenising a fund (common) but allowing **regulated retail secondary trading of authorised funds on a crypto exchange, 24/7**.

**Scale.** The two ChinaAMC funds are small: the USD fund ~US$564M and the HKD fund ~US$98M (≈US$663M combined), and that is total fund AUM, not the tokenised share classes alone. Hong Kong's broader retail tokenised-product universe is reported at ~17 products / ~US$1.3B AUM (up ~78% YoY). The story is a regulatory first rather than a large-dollar event.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is "world's first" defensible? It is specifically first **regulated secondary trading of SFC-authorised (public/retail) tokenised funds on a licensed VATP** — not first tokenised MMF, not first 24/7 crypto trading. Does the note risk overclaiming by dropping those qualifiers?
2. Who actually runs the venue? The aggregated text implies "Hong Kong starts" (state-run flavour), but trading is on **PantherTrade (Futu-backed private VATP)**; the SFC only authorises. Does the headline conflate regulator with operator?
3. Only one VATP so far (PantherTrade)? If a single exchange lists both funds, is this really an open 24/7 market or a single-venue pilot with concentration/liquidity-provider dependency?
4. What is genuine liquidity off-hours? 24/7 quoting ≠ 24/7 depth. Who are the liquidity providers overnight, and what spreads/premium-discount to NAV appear at 03:00 HKT on a US-dollar MMF?
5. How is NAV/fair-value enforced continuously? MMFs have a stable ~$1 NAV during US market hours; what pricing reference governs HKT overnight trades, and can the token trade meaningfully away from NAV?
6. Settlement reality: Project Ensemble linkage to tokenised deposits is stated as a **plan** ("will use"), not live. Is settlement actually real-time today, or still conventional behind a 24/7 trading front-end?
7. Redemption/subscription vs secondary trading: does primary creation/redemption remain gated to dealing hours, so true 24/7 only exists on the secondary book (and thus depends on LP inventory)?
8. AUM is tiny (~US$663M total fund, likely far less tokenised). Is this materially a markets event or a regulatory-signal event?
9. Investor-protection gap: retail buying MMF tokens on a crypto exchange — are there wrapper/custody risks (who holds the underlying fund shares, Futu/VATP insolvency remoteness)?
10. Cross-border reach: can non-HK investors access PantherTrade, or is this ring-fenced to HK-licensed users, limiting the "global first" to a local-access market?
11. Competitive response: Singapore (MAS Project Guardian/[[MAS launches BLOOM for tokenised liabilities and stablecoins]]), and US/EU — could they fast-follow and erode HK's first-mover edge within quarters?
12. Dependence on China/ChinaAMC: both inaugural funds are ChinaAMC products on a Futu venue — is this ecosystem genuinely open, or a mainland-linked captive pilot?
13. Source quality: original item is a bloomingbit aggregation; have the specifics (ChinaAMC, PantherTrade, Oct-26 date, 24/7) been corroborated by SFC's own release and SCMP/Caixin? (Yes — corroborated.)
14. What breaks if a tokenised MMF "breaks the buck" while trading overnight with no US-hours backstop and thin liquidity?
15. Yield vs crypto confusion: does 24/7 exchange listing invite retail to treat a low-yield MMF token as a trading instrument, and does the SFC's ETF-style disclosure regime adequately address that?

**Importance: 3/5** — A genuine regulatory global-first that strengthens Hong Kong's tokenisation leadership and the "always-on cash" thesis, but near-term scale is tiny (~US$663M total fund AUM across two ChinaAMC funds on a single Futu-backed VATP), settlement real-time linkage is still a plan, and it is more signal than market-moving event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Tokenized real-world assets (RWA) sit at ~$22bn onchain AUM as of May 2026, up from ~$8bn at start of 2024 (per bex.co, citing rwa.xyz data; YoY growth ~75%). Tokenized US Treasuries + money-market funds are the leading RWA category at ~$10bn, led by BlackRock BUIDL (~$2.3–2.5bn, the largest tokenized treasury fund) and Franklin BENJI (~$828m) (per altrady/bex, as of May 2026 — `[UNSOURCED]` for exact live figures). Structure: still an early, fragmented, issuer-by-issuer market where value has so far been captured by asset managers (BlackRock, Franklin, ChinaAMC) and tokenization rails (Securitize, Ondo); the *secondary-trading venue* layer was essentially absent until now. Barriers are overwhelmingly regulatory (fund authorization + VATP licensing), not technical. Why now: Hong Kong's SFC set out a new framework in April 2026 to pilot secondary trading of SFC-authorised tokenized funds on licensed VATPs; on 2026-10-26 it approved — "for the first time globally" (SFC PR 26PR174) — secondary-market trading of tokenized public funds, with two ChinaAMC (HK) MMFs going live. This moves HK from primary issuance (subscribe/redeem only) to 24/7 secondary liquidity, the structurally harder half of a real market.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: tokenized AUM, spread/slippage vs NAV, settlement finality time, and venue depth/liquidity. Key players by role: (a) issuers/managers — ChinaAMC (HK), BlackRock, Franklin Templeton, UBS; (b) VATP venues — PantherTrade (the licensed platform here, backed by Futu Holdings, trading via Futubull), plus HK rivals HashKey/OSL; (c) settlement rails — HKMA/SFC Project Ensemble (links tokenized deposits to asset trading for real-time proceeds). The two live products are the ChinaAMC HKD Digital MMF (CAMFF-HKD, ~$98.4m) and USD Digital MMF (CUMFU-USD, ~$564.3m), ~$662.7m combined fund AUM — though that is the whole-fund figure, not the tokenized share class or the PantherTrade float specifically (per Caixin/cryptopolitan, 2026-10). HK's retail tokenized segment is small: ~17 retail tokenized products, ~$1.3bn AUM, +78% YoY (per search, 2026-10). Protagonist's position: Futu/PantherTrade is first-mover on *exclusive* retail secondary distribution in HK — a distribution/venue moat (regulatory license + captive Futubull user base) rather than a product moat; the fund product itself is ChinaAMC's. (analysis)

**Comps & multiples.** This is a product/market-structure launch, not a capital event — no deal valuation attaches to the news. Protagonist proxy: **Futu Holdings (FUTU)** — mkt cap ~$15.44bn, LTM revenue ~HK$24.06bn (~US$3.1bn), Q2 2026 revenue US$918.2m (+35.6% YoY), net income US$464.4m (+41.6% YoY), P/E ~9.8x (per companiesmarketcap/stockanalysis/247wallst, as of 2026-10). P/E ~10x against ~35–50% revenue growth reads cheap/in-line for a profitable broker — the tokenized-MMF venue is a small call-option on top, not a mover of FUTU's valuation (analysis). ChinaAMC (HK) is a subsidiary of China AMC — no standalone public multiple, "no data". In-base comps (grep, semsearch DOWN): [[CME to launch 24 7 crypto futures trading next year]], [[Citi Token Services integrates with 24 7 USD clearing]], [[JPMorgan tokenizes private-equity fund on its blockchain]], [[BNY explores tokenized deposits for payments network]], [[Bybit and DigiFT enable UBS tokenized fund as trading collateral]], [[FCA backs tokenization of funds in asset management]], [[MAS launches BLOOM for tokenised liabilities and stablecoins]] — the cluster shows every major jurisdiction/venue racing toward 24/7 tokenized settlement; HK's edge is being first to *retail secondary* trading, not first to tokenization.

**Risk flags.** (1) Liquidity/depth risk — two funds, ~$662m whole-fund AUM, tiny tokenized float; "24/7 trading" without market-makers can mean wide spreads/gaps vs NAV, especially in off-hours — the headline promises continuous liquidity the order book may not deliver. (2) Settlement dependency — continuous trading only clears if Project Ensemble's tokenized-deposit leg is live; until deposit↔asset linkage is production-grade, "trading" and "settlement" run on different clocks (counterparty/timing risk). (3) Concentration/single-venue — distribution is exclusive to PantherTrade/Futubull, so the venue (not the fund) captures the flow and the regulatory single-point-of-failure; a VATP compliance issue would freeze an entire product line. (4) Regulatory reversal — a global-first pilot can be tightened or paused; the April 2026 framework is new and untested through a stress event.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a market-structure first, not a re-rating: HK is positioning as the RWA secondary-trading hub while US tokenized MMFs (BUIDL/BENJI) remain mostly subscribe/redeem. Falsifiable thesis — if continuous secondary venues become the norm, tokenized MMFs start competing with stablecoins as on-chain cash (yield-bearing + instant exit). Trigger to watch: tokenized-share-class AUM and realized spread-vs-NAV on PantherTrade over the next 1–2 quarters, plus whether a second VATP (HashKey/OSL) gets approved — a single-venue pilot that never broadens is the tell the thesis is wrong. (analysis)
