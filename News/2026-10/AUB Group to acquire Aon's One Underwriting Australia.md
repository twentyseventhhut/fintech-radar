AUB to acquire Aon underwriting agency
 21 October 2026 
 
 
 
 
AUB Group has struck an agreement to buy Aon’s One Underwriting Australia, strengthening its agencies offering.
Agencies CEO Denis Morrissey says the transaction brings an expanded suite of products, capacity partnerships and specialist offerings to AUB.
“One Underwriting’s business is highly complementary to the commercial offering that brokers enjoy today through the 360 Underwriting and Sura brands,” he told insuranceNEWS.com.au.
Aon says it regularly reviews its business portfolio to ensure alignment with strategic priorities and long-term growth objectives.
“Following that review, we believe One Underwriting will be best positioned for its next phase of growth under an owner focused on owning and growing underwriting agency businesses,” it said.
“One Underwriting has built a highly respected specialist underwriting business, and we are proud of the expertise, relationships and market reputation established by the team over many years.”
AUB and Aon say they will work together to support a smooth transition for customers, employees and other stakeholders. The deal is subject to regulatory approval and other completion conditions.
One Underwriting’s website says it services more than 250 brokers, has operated in Australia for more than 30 years and accesses capacity from global carriers.
Its products include niche motor such as classic vehicles, complex property and liability, hotels and pubs, mobile plant and equipment, brand and reputation insurance, commercial drone cover, fine arts and high-value assets, and personal accident and sickness.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
### [0] What happened
On 21 October 2026, ASX-listed **AUB Group** agreed to acquire **One Underwriting Australia**, the Australian underwriting-agency business of global broker **Aon**. Deal value was **not disclosed** by either party, and completion is **subject to regulatory approval and other conditions**. One Underwriting has operated in Australia for 30+ years, services 250+ brokers, and accesses capacity from global carriers across niche lines (classic/complex motor, property & liability, hotels/pubs, mobile plant, brand & reputation, commercial drone cover, fine arts, high-value assets, personal accident & sickness). AUB will fold it alongside its existing 360 Underwriting, Sura and Pacific Indemnity agency brands.
Source: [insuranceNEWS.com.au](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/corporate/aub-to-acquire-aon-underwriting-agency) · [Business Insurance](https://www.businessinsurance.com/aub-group-to-acquire-aons-australian-underwriting-agency/) · [Insurance Asia News](https://insuranceasianews.com/aub-group-to-acquire-aons-one-underwriting-australia/)

### [1] Why it matters
This consolidates distribution power in Australia's underwriting-agency market, where AUB and the Steadfast network together already write roughly a third of premium across ~300 agencies. AUB's agencies division writes ~A$1.3bn in premium across ~39 specialist agencies; adding One Underwriting deepens its specialty/niche capacity and broker reach. It also marks **Aon exiting owned underwriting-agency distribution in Australia** — Aon framed it as a portfolio review, preferring One Underwriting to sit with "an owner focused on owning and growing underwriting agency businesses." For brokers, it means fewer independent capacity sources and a larger AUB-controlled MGA footprint.

### [2] Key people / entities
- **AUB Group** (acquirer) — ASX-listed insurance broking & agency group; now the sole ASX-listed major broker after Steadfast's pending exit.
- **Denis Morrissey** — AUB Agencies CEO and founder of 360 Underwriting Solutions; said One Underwriting is "highly complementary" to 360 Underwriting and Sura.
- **Aon plc** (seller) — global broker divesting its Australian underwriting agency.
- **One Underwriting Australia** — the target MGA (250+ brokers, 30+ years, global carrier capacity).
- Sister AUB brands: **360 Underwriting**, **Sura**, **Pacific Indemnity**.

### [3] Market context / landscape
Australian insurance distribution is consolidating rapidly. The sector peers and comparable 2026-10 deals in the vault: [[Novacore to acquire trucking insurer Diesel Insurance Solutions]], [[Falvey Insurance to acquire Everspan's Providence Washington shell]], and new broker-side entrants such as [[Delegance Brokerage raises $3.1M pre-seed for AI insurance broking]]. Related insurer M&A: [[Inter&Co buys remaining 39.75% of Inter Seguros for $82M]]. The dominant backdrop is the ~A$7.7bn take-private of rival **Steadfast Group** (by Amwins/Dragoneer/KKR, targeted December), which will remove Steadfast from the ASX and leave AUB as the last listed major broker — the "$7.7 billion buyout" the source article alludes to. AUB itself fielded (and declined) a ~A$5.25bn/A$45-per-share bid from EQT (with CVC Asia Pacific) in late 2025 that the parties later scrapped.

### [4] What to watch next
- **Price & multiple** — currently undisclosed; watch for disclosure at AUB results or completion.
- **Regulatory sign-off** — ACCC competition scrutiny given AUB + Steadfast ~1/3 agency-premium concentration.
- **Integration** — whether One Underwriting keeps its brand/capacity panels or merges into 360/Sura; carrier/binder renewals.
- **Broker reaction** — retention of the 250+ broker relationships and any channel conflict concerns.
- **Steadfast–Amwins completion** (targeted December) reshaping the competitive field; AUB's post-deal agency premium and FY27 guidance.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Deal.** AUB Group (ASX:AUB) agreed on 2026-10-21 to acquire One Underwriting Australia, the Australian underwriting-agency arm of global broker Aon. Terms were not disclosed and the deal is subject to regulatory approval and completion conditions. One Underwriting services 250+ brokers, has operated in Australia for 30+ years, and writes niche/specialty lines (classic & complex motor, hotels/pubs, commercial drones, fine arts, high-value assets, brand/reputation, personal accident). AUB positions it as complementary to its existing 360 Underwriting and Sura agency brands, run under its Agencies division (CEO Denis Morrissey).

**Sector context — Australian underwriting-agency M&A supercycle.** The deal lands inside a once-in-a-generation reshuffle of Australian intermediary ownership. The market is highly concentrated: roughly nine broker groups account for ~100% of GWP, with Steadfast ~34%, Marsh ~20% and Aon ~13%. Australia's ~300 underwriting agencies write ~A$10.8bn in premium; Steadfast's agencies plus AUB already command roughly a third of that. The pivotal event is the ~A$7.7bn (US$5.5bn) take-private of Steadfast Group by a consortium of Amwins, Dragoneer and KKR (A$6.00/share scheme; scheme booklet ~2026-10-26, vote ~2026-11-27, expected completion ~2026-12-22, then ASX delisting). Critically, under that structure Amwins Australasia takes Steadfast's underwriting-agency segment while the consortium keeps broking. So the two largest agency platforms (Steadfast's and now Aon's One Underwriting via AUB) are both changing hands in the same quarter — AUB is consolidating agency capacity precisely as its largest rival goes private and its agency book moves to a US-backed owner.

**Strategic read on Aon's side.** For Aon this is a portfolio-trimming divestment, not a retreat from Australia: Aon keeps its dominant broking franchise and sheds a sub-scale agency unit it says will "grow faster" under an owner focused on agencies. It fits Aon's broader "3x3 Plan" / capital-discipline posture at the group (NYSE:AON) of concentrating on core broking, data and advisory rather than carrying balance-sheet-adjacent agency operations. Expect the consideration to be modest relative to Aon's scale — this is a tidy-up, not a headline divestiture.

**In-base comps (grep over News/2026-10, semsearch 402/down).** The vault's October flow confirms an unusually active insurance-intermediary / specialty-underwriting M&A tape:
- *UK P&I Club and TT Club complete acquisition of Thomas Miller* — managing-agent / mutual manager consolidation (marine/specialty).
- *Novacore to acquire trucking insurer Diesel Insurance Solutions* — specialty MGA/program buyer rollup (US).
- *Falvey Insurance to acquire Everspan's Providence Washington shell* — program/fronting carrier shell purchase.
- *Inter&Co buys remaining 39.75% of Inter Seguros for $82M* — bolt-on insurance-distribution consolidation.
- *Vinci Compass to acquire Brazilian reinsurer AustralRe for R$336M* — distinct (reinsurer), but same risk-transfer consolidation theme.
These corroborate that specialty underwriting agencies / MGAs and distribution assets are a hot M&A category in this cycle — AUB's move is squarely on-trend, not idiosyncratic.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Undisclosed price / unverifiable multiple.** No consideration was disclosed; One Underwriting's GWP, revenue and margin aren't public, so synergy and return economics can't be assessed. Do not infer value.
2. **Regulatory & completion risk.** Deal is explicitly conditional on regulatory approval and "other completion conditions." Low ACCC risk at this size, but not a done deal.
3. **Capacity-partner & carrier retention.** One Underwriting relies on binders/capacity from global carriers (some historically tied to Aon relationships). Capacity partners can re-paper or walk at change of control, which is the core value of any agency book.
4. **Broker/distribution attrition.** 250+ brokers and key underwriting staff are the asset; agency acquisitions routinely leak talent and panel relationships during transition.
5. **Rival-consolidation overhang.** Simultaneous Steadfast/Amwins take-private means AUB is buying into a market where its largest competitor is being recapitalised by deeper-pocketed US/PE owners — potential for intensified capacity competition and future bidding wars for agency assets.
6. **Integration/brand sprawl.** Adds a third agency brand (One Underwriting) alongside 360 Underwriting and Sura; execution risk in rationalising overlapping lines and systems.
7. **Soft-market timing.** If Australian commercial rates are softening into 2026-27, agency GWP and commission income could face top-line pressure post-close.
