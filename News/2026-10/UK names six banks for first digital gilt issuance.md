# UK names six banks for first digital gilt issuance

The UK government has appointed six banks to lead its first digital gilt issuance, with the pilot expected by the first quarter of 2027.
Six banks will handle underwriting, investor engagement and distribution for the DIGIT pilot.
HSBC’s Orion platform will host the bond within the UK’s Digital Securities Sandbox.
Industry executives say digital bonds need connections to cash, custody and existing settlement systems.
U.S. and UK authorities are examining common approaches to tokenized securities and settlement.
HM Treasury announced on Oct. 6 that Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, Morgan Stanley, NatWest and RBC Capital Markets will serve as joint lead managers for the Digital Gilt Instrument, known as DIGIT.
Following a competitive procurement process, the appointments allow investor engagement to begin, the department said. Economic Secretary to the Treasury Lucy Rigby disclosed the selection during her keynote address at UK Digital Assets Week.
UK digital gilt banks will handle the sale and investor engagement
Under the Treasury’s mandate, the six institutions will provide underwriting services, support discussions with investors and distribute DIGIT on issuance day. The department said its selection process assessed suppliers against transparent and objective criteria.
For the pilot itself, the Treasury has specified a short-dated bond issued directly on digital infrastructure, with onchain settlement and a platform operating inside the Digital Securities Sandbox. According to its design, the transaction will remain independent of the government’s main debt management programme.
In her speech , Rigby said the government intends to list DIGIT as the first digital asset on LSEG’s main market. Preparations for possible further issuances are also underway, subject to the success of the first transaction, she said.
The minister described the project on X as “a practical test of new financial market infrastructure,” while pointing to issuance early next year.
HSBC’s platform will connect with LSEG infrastructure
As crypto.news reported on July 15, the planned first digital sovereign bond will be denominated in sterling and issued through HSBC’s Orion platform. The report traced the pilot to its announcement in 2024 and HSBC’s appointment as platform supplier in February 2026.
At the time, the Treasury said the project would examine whether distributed ledger technology could shorten settlement times, reduce reconciliation work and lower operating costs. Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey also said the central bank would work toward accepting DIGIT as collateral in its market operations.
According to the Treasury’s latest announcement, HSBC and London Stock Exchange Group signed a memorandum of understanding in July to develop a bilateral Digital Securities Depository link. Rigby said the connection would allow investors to access DIGIT through either infrastructure.
During Tuesday’s address, the minister also said HSBC became the first firm approved to operate a live digital securities depository in the sandbox in July. ClearToken has since become the second firm to receive that approval, she said.
The government intends to introduce secondary legislation over the coming months to support digital services and issuances within the sandbox, according to Rigby.
Digital settlement needs cash and custody connections
For Richard Baker, Tokenovate’s CEO and founder, the pilot’s infrastructure test includes the connections between digital securities and established financial systems. Baker, who sits on HM Treasury’s Wholesale Digital Markets Industry Taskforce, told Cointelegraph:
“On-chain settlement will need to connect with cash, custody and existing settlement infrastructure, with common standards and legal certainty keeping lifecycle events consistent across systems.”
Building those connections from the outset could help establish whether tokenization improves liquidity and market efficiency without creating separate digital systems, he said.
A July 22 report examined the DIGIT cash settlement challenge , citing the Bank of England and Financial Conduct Authority’s work on payment options. The regulators were considering tokenized deposits, regulated stablecoins and central bank money as settlement assets.
According to the central bank’s May consultation, a planned synchronization service would link digital asset ledgers with sterling held in its real-time gross settlement system. The Bank targeted 2028 for the service, which would allow the securities and payment sides of a transaction to settle together.
In separate banking tests covered on Sep. 24, UK Finance said Barclays, Lloyds and NatWest completed two tokenized deposit mortgage transactions . Another group of three banks, including HSBC, tested a person-to-person payment tied to a simulated online marketplace purchase.
For the mortgage tests, funds remained locked during the property process and were released automatically upon completion, according to UK Finance. In the marketplace test, payment depended on confirmation that goods had been received, although no physical goods changed hands.
Participating banks plan to issue three digital bonds in the first quarter of 2027 that can be traded and settled using tokenized deposits, UK Finance’s payments and innovation managing director Jana Mackintosh told Reuters. The association also plans to establish a company, rulebook and governance framework for the deposit project.
U.S.-UK work covers cross-border securities settlement
For U.S. market participants, the related policy work includes cooperation between American and British financial regulators. An Aug. 12 report on the U.S.-UK tokenized markets recommendations covered proposals for cross-border experiments and common regulatory approaches, while distinguishing the recommendations from enforceable rules.
Under the task force’s July 14 recommendations , the SEC, CFTC, FCA and Bank of England will seek common approaches to the regulatory treatment of tokenized assets. Their work includes settlement finality and the potential use of stablecoins or tokenized money market funds as margin collateral at central counterparties.
The two governments also intend to engage a private-sector-led group for one year to test cross-border uses of tokenized assets and share practices with officials, according to the policy paper.
In comments to Cointelegraph, Axiology CEO and former central banker Marius Jurgilas said connecting securities issuance, distribution, trading and settlement through regulated infrastructure could “broaden their investor base and create more funding options.” He added that government support could help establish a market where capital moves more easily between countries and reaches more issuers.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
### [0] TL;DR
On 2026-10-06, HM Treasury appointed six banks — Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, Morgan Stanley, NatWest and RBC Capital Markets — as Joint Lead Managers for DIGIT, the UK's first digitally native gilt (Digital Gilt Instrument). The JLM mandate (underwriting, investor engagement, distribution) was awarded via competitive procurement and announced by Economic Secretary Lucy Rigby at UK Digital Assets Week. The short-dated bond will be issued on HSBC's Orion DLT platform inside the Digital Securities Sandbox (DSS), settle on-chain, and target issuance in Q1 2027. DIGIT is to be the first digital asset listed on LSEG's main market and is deliberately kept independent of the DMO's main debt-management programme.

### [1] Why it matters
This is a milestone, not a new idea: it converts a multi-year UK roadmap (announced Nov 2024, HSBC Orion selected Feb 2026) into a dated, funded syndicate with issuance imminent. It positions the UK alongside/ahead of G7 peers on sovereign DLT debt, and tests whether tokenisation can shorten settlement, cut reconciliation and lower operating costs on genuinely sovereign (vs supranational EIB) paper. Bank of England has signalled it would work toward accepting DIGIT as collateral — a key signal of institutional seriousness.

### [2] Internal context (prior coverage)
The UK tokenisation story is well-documented in the vault; this note is the capital-markets/sovereign-debt branch:
- [[Lloyds completes UK's first tokenized-deposit gilt purchase]] (2026-01) — Lloyds bought a tokenised gilt from Archax using tokenised sterling deposits on Canton; the *cash leg / deposit-token* precursor to DIGIT's settlement question.
- [[Bank of England and FCA launch tokenized wholesale markets consultation]] (2026-05) — the regulatory umbrella (synchronisation service targeted 2028, linking DLT ledgers to RTGS sterling) that DIGIT's cash-settlement design depends on.
- [[Six UK banks pilot tokenized sterling deposits]] (2025-09) and [[UK banks prep live pilot of tokenized sterling deposits]] (2025-10) — UK Finance's tokenised-deposit pilot; the same bank cohort (HSBC, Barclays, Lloyds, NatWest) plans three tradeable digital bonds settled with tokenised deposits in Q1 2027, overlapping DIGIT's window.
- Comparable sovereign/quasi-sovereign DLT issuance: [[Emirates NBD issues AED-denominated digital bond]] (2026-01, Euroclear D-FMI) and [[Hong Kong uses tokenized central bank money in green bond sale]] (2025-11, HK$10bn) show the global pattern DIGIT joins.

### [3] External timeline / prior art
- Nov 2024: DIGIT pilot announced (Autumn Budget-era roadmap).
- 2026-02-12: HSBC Orion selected as DIGIT DLT platform.
- Jul 2026: HSBC–LSEG MoU for a bilateral Digital Securities Depository link; HSBC becomes first firm approved to run a live DSD in the sandbox (ClearToken later the second).
- 2026-07-14: US-UK Wholesale Digital Markets taskforce recommendations (SEC/CFTC/FCA/BoE common approaches, cross-border tokenised-asset experiments).
- 2026-10-06: six JLMs appointed; investor engagement can begin; Q1 2027 issuance confirmed.
- Prior art (supranational, not sovereign): EIB has issued ~6 digital bonds since 2021 across 5 DLT platforms, incl. a 2023 GBP digital bond and a Nov 2024 EUR100m bond settled in wholesale CBDC via HSBC Orion / Banque de France DL3S, under the EU DLT Pilot Regime. DIGIT differs by being *sovereign* paper settling in the UK's own DSS.

### [4] Open questions / what to watch
- Size, maturity and coupon of DIGIT (undisclosed; "short-dated", independent of DMO programme).
- Cash-settlement asset: tokenised deposits vs regulated stablecoin vs (synchronised) central bank money — BoE synchronisation service only targeted for 2028, after planned Q1 2027 issuance.
- Whether BoE actually accepts DIGIT as repo/collateral, and whether secondary legislation (promised "over coming months") lands before issuance.
- Secondary-market liquidity and investor base vs a conventional gilt; whether further issuances follow the pilot.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
### Red-team / challenge

1. **Is this genuinely new vs the Feb 2026 HSBC Orion selection?** Yes — distinct milestone: Feb 2026 named the *platform*; Oct 6 2026 named the *six JLM banks* and confirmed Q1 2027. No prior vault note covers the JLM appointment. Fresh.

2. **Is DIGIT actually "the first digital gilt"?** As a digitally native *sovereign* gilt, yes. But it is NOT the UK's first tokenised-gilt transaction — [[Lloyds completes UK's first tokenized-deposit gilt purchase]] (Jan 2026) involved a tokenised gilt via Archax. The novelty is sovereign *issuance*, not tokenisation of gilts per se. Caveat noted.

3. **Is it "live" or just announced?** Announced only. Issuance targeted Q1 2027; nothing has settled. Open.

4. **Any hard numbers (size/coupon/maturity)?** None disclosed. Only "short-dated" and "independent of the main debt programme." Open — a real limitation on importance.

5. **Who leads vs who follows globally?** EIB (supranational) has issued ~6 digital bonds since 2021; HK, Emirates NBD, Slovenia, Germany (KfW) etc. have issued digital bonds. UK claim is narrower: first digitally native *sovereign gilt*. "G7 pole position" framing is promotional, not uncontested.

6. **What's the settlement-asset risk?** On-chain settlement needs a cash leg. BoE synchronisation service targeted 2028 — after Q1 2027. So DIGIT may settle via tokenised deposits or an interim mechanism, not central bank money. Genuine unresolved design gap. Open.

7. **Is the bank list reliable?** Confirmed consistently across crypto.news, Bloomberg, Cointelegraph, LeapRate, GOV.UK: Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, Morgan Stanley, NatWest, RBC Capital Markets. High confidence.

8. **Could the pilot slip past Q1 2027?** Plausible — depends on secondary legislation "over coming months," DSD approvals, and investor demand. Historical DIGIT timeline already stretched (announced 2024). Open risk.

9. **Conflict / concentration concern?** HSBC is both DLT platform supplier (Orion) AND a JLM AND first approved DSD operator — significant concentration in one firm. Worth flagging; no evidence of governance issue, but single-point dependence. Open.

10. **Does BoE collateral acceptance exist yet?** No — Governor Bailey said BoE "would work toward" accepting DIGIT as collateral. Aspirational, not committed. Open.

11. **Is this independent of the DMO's funding programme?** Yes, explicitly — a "practical test of infrastructure," not a funding exercise. Limits market impact; it's a sandbox pilot, not a mainstream gilt.

12. **Investor-base / liquidity proof?** Unproven. Axiology's Jurgilas argues DLT could broaden investor base, but that's a hypothesis the pilot is meant to test. Open.

13. **US-UK cross-border angle — material?** Real but preliminary: Jul 14 2026 taskforce recommendations are non-binding; a private-sector group to test cross-border tokenised assets for one year. Context, not a near-term driver.

14. **Duplicate risk within the vault?** Nearest neighbours (tokenised deposits, BoE/FCA consultation, Lloyds gilt) are adjacent but none report the JLM appointment. Not a duplicate.

15. **Headline vs substance gap?** The headline event (naming banks) is procedural; substance (size, settlement asset, BoE collateral, actual issuance) remains open. Importance rests on strategic signal, not executed value.

**Importance: 4/5 — a concrete, well-sourced milestone in a flagship G7 sovereign-DLT programme with a named syndicate, confirmed Q1 2027 timeline, HSBC Orion platform and LSEG listing. Marked down from 5 because nothing has issued, no size/economic terms disclosed, cash-settlement and BoE-collateral questions are unresolved, and the pilot is deliberately ring-fenced from the real funding programme.**

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Sub-vertical: tokenised capital markets / digital sovereign debt (DLT-based bond issuance + settlement). Market still nascent: >60 tokenised bonds issued for ~$8bn cumulative per ICMA's DLT bond tracker (via assettokenization.com, as of 2025); McKinsey's conservative projection is ~$300bn tokenised-bond issuance by 2030 (via ixs.finance) — i.e. a rounding error against a ~$143tn global bond market (via researchandmarkets, 2026). So this is an infrastructure/plumbing bet, not a near-term revenue line. Structure: issuance is consolidated around a handful of bank-run DLT platforms (HSBC Orion, Euroclear D-FMI, SIX, Canton Network) + regulated sandboxes; entry barriers are regulatory (DSS licence, DSD approval) and network-effect (connecting cash/custody/settlement rails), not capital. "Why now": the UK pilot runs inside the Digital Securities Sandbox, with the BoE's synchronisation service (RTGS ↔ DLT, targeted 2028, per FCA/BoE May-2026 consultation [[Bank of England and FCA launch tokenized wholesale markets consultation]]) and the July-2026 US-UK task-force recommendations on tokenised-asset settlement providing the legal scaffolding. DIGIT is explicitly ring-fenced from the main debt-management programme (per crypto.news), i.e. a test, not a funding tool.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for DLT bond platforms: settlement-cycle compression (T+n → atomic/intraday), on-chain outstanding notional, number of live DSD/DSS-licensed platforms, breadth of cash-leg options (tokenised deposits / stablecoins / wholesale CBDC). Players & basis of competition — platform-level (not price): HSBC Orion (UK gilt + Ant tokenised deposits [[HSBC launches tokenized deposit service with Ant International]]), LSEG's B3 DSD, Euroclear D-FMI (Emirates NBD AED bond [[Emirates NBD issues AED-denominated digital bond]]), Canton Network (Lloyds' tokenised-deposit gilt purchase via Archax [[Lloyds completes UK's first tokenized-deposit gilt purchase]]), plus sovereign precedents in Asia/MEA (Hong Kong HK$20bn digital green bonds 2026; Thailand G-Token [[KuCoin lists Thailand's G-Token, first tokenized sovereign bond]]; Shenzhen Futian on Ethereum [[Shenzhen's Futian Investment issues first tokenised bond on Ethereum]]). Recent moves: HSBC first firm approved for a live DSS digital securities depository (Jul 2026), ClearToken second (per crypto.news); HSBC-LSEG DSD MoU (Jul 2026); UK Finance tokenised-deposit mortgage tests with Barclays/Lloyds/NatWest (Sep 24, 2026). Protagonist position: HSBC is ahead on UK digital-bond infrastructure (platform + first DSD approval); Barclays and the other five banks are JLMs — i.e. distribution/underwriting roles, a conventional syndicate function on novel rails, not a platform moat. Moat for HSBC = intangibles/first-mover regulatory approval + rail integration (analysis); for the JLM banks, the "moat" is incumbency in gilt syndication, which tokenisation partly threatens (see risks).

**Comps & multiples.** No traded pure-play maps to DIGIT, and the six banks are diversified universal banks where this pilot is immaterial to group revenue — EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E on the news = **no data / not meaningful** (a short-dated pilot gilt moves no group multiple). DIGIT's size, coupon and tenor are **not disclosed** (per crypto.news / GOV.UK) → issuance-economics = **no data**. Internal comps (precedent sovereign/quasi-sovereign digital bonds, as scale reference): Hong Kong HK$10bn (~$1.29bn) digital green bond with tokenised central-bank money [[Hong Kong uses tokenized central bank money in green bond sale]]; Emirates NBD AED digital bond on Euroclear D-FMI [[Emirates NBD issues AED-denominated digital bond]]; Shenzhen Futian ~$70m Ethereum bond [[Shenzhen's Futian Investment issues first tokenised bond on Ethereum]]; Lloyds UK-first tokenised-deposit gilt purchase on Canton [[Lloyds completes UK's first tokenized-deposit gilt purchase]]. Distribution not computed (sizes span $70m → $1.29bn, non-comparable currencies/structures) → qualitative only: UK is late to live sovereign digital bonds vs HK/Asia but would be first among the G7 (per serrarigroup/Bloomberg) — a regulatory-signalling lead, not a volume lead.

**Risk flags.**
1. Pilot ≠ market. Announced, not live; issuance is Q1-2027, deliberately walled off from the main DMO programme and of undisclosed (likely token) size — de-PR'd, this is a sandbox experiment whose success is conditional on the BoE synchronisation service (not due until 2028), so the cash-settlement leg is the critical unsolved dependency, not the token itself.
2. Cash-leg / settlement-asset ambiguity. BoE/FCA are still choosing between tokenised deposits, regulated stablecoins and central-bank money (per the note); until atomic DvP against central-bank money is live, DLT's headline benefit (settlement compression, reconciliation cut) is unproven in production — second-order: the operating-cost savings the Treasury cites stay hypothetical.
3. Disintermediation of the syndicate. The longer-term thesis of on-chain issuance is fewer intermediaries; the six JLM banks' incumbent gilt-syndication economics are the very margin that tokenisation could compress — being inside the pilot is defensive positioning, and who captures the settlement-layer value (platform vs syndicate) is unresolved.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) DIGIT is a regulatory-infrastructure proof point — a G7 sovereign validating bank-run DLT rails — more than an investable catalyst; the re-rating, if any, accrues to platform owners (HSBC Orion, LSEG DSD) rather than the JLM syndicate. Falsifiable thesis: if Q1-2027 issuance settles on-chain with a working central-bank-money or tokenised-deposit cash leg AND the BoE accepts DIGIT as collateral, it unlocks repeat sovereign issuance and a UK DSD standard; what breaks it — a slipped issuance date, a sandbox-only (non-main-market) settlement asset, or the synchronisation service slipping past 2028, which would leave DIGIT a one-off demo. Watch: DIGIT size/tenor disclosure, the chosen cash-settlement asset, and secondary legislation promised "over coming months."
