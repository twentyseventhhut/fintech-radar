Octane Closes $1.4 Billion Forward-Flow Deal with New York Life, MetLife Investment Management, Equitable, Pacific Life, and Victory Park Capital

 
 
 
 
 October 6, 2026 
 
 

 
 
 
 9:05 am 
 
 Expanded Partnership with Leading Institutional Investors Marks Octane’s Largest Forward-Flow Transaction to Date 
 Octane executed a $1.4 billion forward-flow facility backed by fixed-rate installment, full-spectrum powersports and outdoor power equipment loans 
 Largest forward-flow transaction since Octane’s inception and third forward-flow deal announced in 2026 
 Builds on Octane’s existing relationships with New York Life, MetLife Investment Management, and Equitable and adds Pacific Life and Victory Park Capital as new forward-flow partners 
 NEW YORK, October 6, 2026 — Octane ® (Octane Lending, Inc.), the fintech unlocking the power of financial products for retailers and consumers, announced today that it has executed a $1.4 billion forward-flow facility, marking the Company’s largest forward-flow transaction to date and further deepening its relationships with leading institutional investors. 
 New York Life Investment Management, the asset management business of New York Life, was the Loan Arranger and Lead Investor along with MetLife Investment Management , the institutional asset management business of MetLife, Inc. (NYSE: MET), Equitable , a leading financial services organization and principal franchise of Equitable Holdings, Inc. (NYSE: EQH), Pacific Life , and Victory Park Capital , a leading global alternative investment firm specializing in private credit . AB CarVal , an established global alternative investment manager and part of AllianceBernstein’s Private Alternatives business, served as Investment Advisor to Equitable in connection with the transaction. Under the terms of the agreement, the parties have jointly agreed to purchase up to $1.4 billion backed by fixed-rate installment, full-spectrum powersports and outdoor power equipment loans originated by Octane’s in-house lender, Roadrunner Financial , LLC. Octane’s in-house loan servicer, Roadrunner Account Services, LLC, will service the loans.  
 This transaction marks Octane’s third forward-flow partnership with funds managed by life insurance providers and builds on its existing relationships with New York Life, MetLife, and Equitable, each of which participated in the Company’s $700 million forward-flow facility announced in April 2025. 
 “Our largest-ever forward-flow facility marks a significant milestone for Octane and our capital markets program,” said Nicholas Makarov, SVP and Head of Capital Markets at Octane. “We’re grateful to each of our partners for their continued confidence in our platform. The scale of this transaction and the quality of partners participating reflect the strength and consistency of our credit performance, the continued demand from leading institutional investors for Octane-originated assets, and our ability to build and deepen relationships with best-in-class capital partners.” 
 “Building on our inaugural facility, we are proud to close a second forward-flow commitment with Octane alongside an expanded group of institutional partners,” said Brendan Feeney, Managing Director and Head of Consumer Asset Based Finance, Private Fixed Income. Private Fixed Income is part of New York Life Investment Management (NYLIM), a global investment platform with approximately $837 billion assets under management, delivering tailored solutions across public and private markets. “This transaction reinforces NYLIM’s commitment to sourcing high-quality, differentiated assets for our portfolio while supporting Octane’s continued growth and market leadership.” 
 With this transaction, Octane continues to expand its base of high-quality institutional capital partners. To date, the Company has sold or secured commitments to sell more than $6.3 billion of consumer loans through its forward-flow and whole-loan programs, including approximately $3.4 billion in commitments in 2026. The transaction further strengthens Octane’s diversified capital markets platform, alongside an asset-backed securitization program that has issued more than $5 billion of notes to date, including its $337 million RV and Marine securitization , the Company’s largest in the asset class to date. 
 The forward-flow announcement follows a period of continued growth and momentum for Octane. In the first half of 2026, Octane grew originations by 37% year-over-year and surpassed $9 billion in all-time originations . Octane also announced significant Captive-as-a-Service partnerships in the powersports and RV markets, entered the auto industry , continued to expand across the powersports, outdoor power equipment , RV, and Marine industries, and was named ABS Issuer of the Year at the 2026 GlobalCapital US Securitization Awards.

# Context-enrichment: Octane closes $1.4B forward-flow deal with institutional investors
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Octane (Octane Lending, Inc.) signed a **$1.4B forward-flow facility** — a committed, ongoing purchase agreement under which five institutional investors agree to buy, on a rolling basis, **fixed-rate installment powersports + outdoor-power-equipment loans** originated by Octane's in-house lender Roadrunner Financial and serviced by Roadrunner Account Services. This is NOT a cash equity raise and NOT a securitization; it is Octane selling the credit risk/assets forward to balance-sheet buyers (insurers) who want yield from consumer ABF (asset-based finance). Octane keeps origination + servicing fees and recycles capital to originate more.
- Buyers: **New York Life Investment Management (NYLIM)** as Loan Arranger + Lead Investor; **MetLife Investment Management, Equitable, Pacific Life, Victory Park Capital**. **AB CarVal** (AllianceBernstein Private Alternatives) acted as Investment Advisor to Equitable — not a direct buyer here.
- "Up to $1.4B" = a commitment ceiling, not deployed capital. The flow is drawn as loans are originated; actual funded amount depends on origination pace.
- De-PR'd on the superlatives: "**largest forward-flow to date**" is true but has moved three times in ~18 months — $700M (Apr 2025) → $750M AB CarVal (Jul 2026, upsizable to $1.125B) → $1.4B (Oct 2026). The record is a function of Octane's growth cadence, not a one-off milestone.
- **Why structured this way:** Octane is a thin-equity, high-velocity originator ($242M total equity pre-Series F vs >$9B all-time originations). It cannot hold loans on balance sheet, so its business model REQUIRES a constant forward-flow + ABS + whole-loan machine to offload assets and free capital. Insurers are the natural counterparty: they want long-dated, yield-y, diversified consumer credit to match life/annuity liabilities, and NYLIM/MetLife/Equitable/Pacific Life all run private-ABF books hunting exactly this. The deal is "built via insurers" because that capital is the cheapest and stickiest available for this asset class.
- **What the PR anchors to (flattering base):** "37% H1-2026 origination growth," ">$9B all-time originations," ">$6.3B sold/committed," ">$5B ABS issued," "ABS Issuer of the Year 2026." These frame a commodity funding transaction as a franchise-quality signal. The real tell is repeat participation: the same three insurers (NY Life/MetLife/Equitable) are back for a second, larger round — that is the substantive datapoint, not the headline number.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Two distinct competitive frames:
- **Origination (who lends to powersports/RV buyers):** Octane/Roadrunner vs **Sheffield Financial** (Truist-owned, dominant in golf carts + OEM 0% promos, partners Club Car/EZGO), **Synchrony** (nearly-20-year Polaris relationship, renewed 2026), **FreedomRoad**, and OEM captives. Octane's differentiation is a full-stack fintech play (prequal, dealer portal, in-house underwriting + servicing + capital markets) rather than a bank balance sheet. Octane originations ~$2.1B in 2025 (+29% YoY per trade press); it is a scaled challenger but still smaller than the bank incumbents in absolute book.
- **Funding / forward-flow peers (who else sells consumer credit forward):** the corpus shows this is a crowded 2025–26 pattern — [[Klarna secures $26 billion forward flow deal with Nelnet]], [[YouLend secures $225 million forward-flow facility from Värde Partners]], [[Cross River extends $250M forward-flow for Figure crypto loans]], [[Affirm and CPP Investments renew and expand capital partnership]], [[Affirm gets $750 million loan deal from New York Life]], [[Kroo Bank enters forward-flow funding partnership with Glenhawk]]. Octane is **parity/ahead** on structure sophistication among non-bank originators, but the $1.4B is small next to Klarna's $26B — reflecting ticket size (powersports loans $15k–$50k, niche market) not weakness.
- **Why the lay of the land is this way (2nd order):** Insurers (NY Life also funds Affirm — see corpus) are systematically moving into private consumer ABF for yield pickup over corporates. The repeat NYLIM→Octane and NYLIM→Affirm pattern shows insurers are picking a short list of "best-in-class originators" and scaling with them. The competitive moat is therefore shifting from "who can underwrite" to "who can keep attracting committed institutional flow" — a capital-markets relationship game. Octane's "ABS Issuer of the Year" + repeat insurer participation is its wedge here.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Founded 2014; in-house lender Roadrunner Financial. Pure powersports → expanded to RV, marine, outdoor power equipment, and in 2026 entered **auto** and launched **Captive-as-a-Service** (white-label lending for OEMs).
- Equity: [[Octane raises $100M Series F at $1.3B valuation]] (Dec 2025, led by Valar Ventures; GAAP net-income profitable; $242M total equity pre-F).
- Capital-markets trajectory (dates/numbers): ABS program since Dec 2019, >$5B notes issued, $337M RV+Marine ABS (largest in class, Aug 2026). Forward-flow: $700M (Apr 2025, NY Life/MetLife/Equitable — first life-insurer facility) → $350M Nuveen [[Octane closes $350 million forward-flow agreement with Nuveen]] (May 2026, 5th forward-flow, total commitments $2.2B) → $750M AB CarVal (Jul 2026) → $1.4B (Oct 2026, "third announced in 2026"). All-time sold/committed now >$6.3B (~$3.4B of it in 2026).
- **Why the company acts this way:** Octane is structurally a capital-light origination+servicing platform that monetizes a take-rate, not a spread held to maturity. That model only compounds if funding scales faster than originations. Hence the relentless cadence of ever-larger forward-flows and the diversification across insurers (NY Life, MetLife, Equitable, Pacific Life, Nuveen/TIAA, AB CarVal) + ABS + whole-loan — it is deliberately avoiding single-funder dependence. The Captive-as-a-Service + auto expansion is the same logic: push the platform/software multiple, not just the loan book.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new here:** a new, larger commitment ceiling ($1.4B, ~2x the Apr-2025 $700M), and **two net-new institutional partners — Pacific Life and Victory Park Capital** — on top of the returning NY Life/MetLife/Equitable. That is real incremental traction, not a reprint.
- **What is NOT new / de-hyped:** the asset (same Roadrunner powersports/OPE paper), the mechanism (forward-flow), and three of five buyers. This is the *expansion of an existing* April-2025 relationship, framed as a milestone. The "largest to date" superlative has been used on at least three consecutive deals.
- **Traction that is real (numbers, not announcements):** repeat + upsized commitments from the same insurers is the strongest signal — institutions only re-up and enlarge if the credit has performed. >$6.3B cumulative sold/committed and >$5B ABS placed = a funding platform with demonstrated repeat demand. "ABS Issuer of the Year 2026" is a third-party validation.
- **Who captures the margin (2nd order):** In forward-flow, the INSURER captures the credit spread/yield; Octane captures origination + servicing fees and the capital-recycling velocity. So Octane's economics are fee/velocity-driven, not spread-driven — durable only as long as (a) origination volume grows and (b) insurers keep buying at attractive advance rates. The risk: in a consumer-credit downturn, forward-flow buyers can tighten terms or pull back, and powersports/RV is a discretionary, cyclical, affordability-sensitive category (trade press already flags "consumer affordability concerns"). The multiple rests on the platform/software + Captive-as-a-Service story, not on this commodity funding line.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Plans:** continue scaling originations (+37% H1-2026), push into auto, grow Captive-as-a-Service, keep diversifying funding (more insurer forward-flows + ABS). Expect further "largest-to-date" facilities as origination grows.
- **Sentiment:** positive — insurer appetite for private consumer ABF is structurally strong (yield search, liability matching); Octane is on insurers' short list (NY Life funds both Octane and Affirm). Awards + repeat buyers support an IPO-track narrative for the ~$1.3B-valued unicorn.
- **Risks / counterintuitive 2nd-order effects:** (1) **Concentration via insurers looks safe but is cyclical** — the same buyers who scale fast in good times can retrench together if powersports delinquencies rise; heavy reliance on a small insurer club is a correlated funding risk, not diversification. (2) **Discretionary collateral** — powersports/RV/marine are among the first consumer purchases cut in a downturn; a credit-quality wobble would hit advance rates and the whole flywheel. (3) **Fee-model fragility** — Octane earns on velocity; if originations slow, the capital-light model loses its compounding. (4) Macro-rate regime: forward-flow pricing is sensitive to insurer alternative yields. Watch the next ABS tranche's spreads and any change in advance rates as the real health signal — not the headline commitment size.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Octane sits at the intersection of specialty consumer lending (powersports / OPE / RV / marine installment) and the forward-flow funding of fintech lenders by private-credit and insurance capital. US private credit grew from ~$0.5tn to ~$1.3tn over the past five years and is projected to exceed ~$2tn AUM in 2026 (per Creative Planning, citing industry data, via creativeplanning.com, as of 2026). Insurers are a core buyer — ~94% of institutional investors now allocate to private credit (same source) — because long-dated, fixed-rate installment paper matches annuity/life liabilities. Structure: the originate-to-distribute model splits the chain — Octane keeps origination + servicing (Roadrunner Financial / Roadrunner Account Services) while insurers hold the credit. Barriers are dealer-network distribution and a demonstrated credit track record, not balance sheet. Why now: a maturing private-credit cycle has insurers (NY Life, MetLife, Equitable, Pacific Life) reaching for differentiated consumer assets, letting non-bank lenders scale originations without deposits. Second-order effect: funding capacity, not demand, becomes the binding constraint — and Octane is deliberately pre-funding ($3.4bn of 2026 commitments vs $9bn all-time originations).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: originations volume (flow), forward-flow/whole-loan commitments (funded capacity), credit performance (loss/delinquency), and cost/diversity of capital. Octane metrics (from PR, de-PR'd as "announced commitments," not drawn): >$6.3bn of loans sold or committed across forward-flow + whole-loan to date, ~$3.4bn committed in 2026, >$5bn ABS notes issued, H1-2026 originations +37% YoY, >$9bn all-time originations. Key players: Synchrony (bank, co-supports OEM incentives), FreedomRoad Financial, Horsepower Financial (leasing), Forest River Finance (OEM captive) — per Auto Finance News, 2026. Basis of competition: dealer relationships, OEM/captive partnerships (Octane's Camping World, Adventure Lifestyle, Forest River programs) and breadth of funding. Octane's position: a scaled originator ahead on capital-markets depth (ABS + multiple forward-flow partners), expanding beyond powersports into RV/marine/auto and white-label "Captive-as-a-Service" (analysis). Moat: distribution/network across the dealer base + a repeatable securitization/forward-flow funnel; switching costs for OEM captive partners (analysis). **Risk flags.**
1. Funding concentration in a single buyer class — three of Octane's forward-flow deals are with life-insurance-managed funds; a private-credit/insurance-allocation pullback (a theme flagged in 2026 private-credit vulnerability reviews, e.g. FSB) would hit Octane's capacity before its demand. Second-order: originations could outrun fundable volume if insurer appetite cools.
2. Credit-cycle / collateral risk — powersports/RV/OPE are discretionary, higher-beta consumer purchases; affordability pressure and RV trade-in softness are already noted for 2026 (Auto Finance News). Rising losses would re-price forward-flow terms and compress Octane's retained economics. The PR is silent on loss rates, advance rates, and pricing — the economics that actually matter.
3. Disintermediation of economics — in originate-to-distribute, the credit spread accrues to the insurer; Octane captures origination + servicing fees. If institutional buyers in-source origination or demand tighter terms as the asset class commoditizes, margin migrates up-stack away from the fintech.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is capacity-building, not a re-rating: the signal is that insurer demand for non-bank consumer paper is deep enough to pre-fund ~$3.4bn/yr, validating Octane's "capital-light originator" thesis (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: Octane keeps scaling originations >30% YoY while adding new institutional partners through 2027. What breaks it: a visible deterioration in credit performance or a forward-flow partner declining to renew (e.g. the one-year Nuveen facility lapsing) — the trigger to watch is renewal terms and advance-rate cuts on the next ABS/forward-flow print.
