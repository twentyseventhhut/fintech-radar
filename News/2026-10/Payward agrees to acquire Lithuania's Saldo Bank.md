# Payward agrees to acquire Lithuania's Saldo Bank

[Connecting the Dots in Fintech] 🇪🇺 Payward agrees to acquire Saldo Bank, expanding its European banking ambitions. The Kraken parent is buying the Lithuanian-licensed digital bank to add regulated banking capabilities, including deposits, lending, card issuance and SEPA payments, bringing traditional banking services closer to its crypto platform. ## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd).** On 14 Oct 2026, Payward (Kraken's parent) signed a **definitive agreement to acquire Saldo Finance, the holding company of Saldo Bank UAB**, a Lithuania-licensed digital-first specialised bank. Deal value is **undisclosed** (no price, no cash/stock split given — the first red flag of PR vagueness). Expected to **close in 2027, subject to regulatory approval** (Bank of Lithuania / ECB change-of-control). What Payward actually buys is a **live EU banking licence** (ECB-granted specialised-bank licence, Oct 2021) with a cross-border passport: Saldo serves **Lithuania, Finland, Sweden and Germany, plus a passport to operate in the Netherlands**, and offers interest-bearing EUR deposits (covered to EUR 100k by the Lithuanian deposit-guarantee scheme), automated consumer + SME lending, cards and SEPA payments. **Why structured this way:** this is the *acquire-an-existing-licence* route — exactly the option CEO Arjun Sethi flagged at Money20/20 Europe ("acquire existing licensed entities OR apply directly"). Buying beats applying de novo because a full/specialised Lithuanian bank authorisation is ECB-involved and takes 12+ months; buying a chartered bank with EUR 100k-guaranteed deposits and a passport compresses that to a change-of-control approval. Note the scale: Saldo is **tiny — EUR 162.9m total assets at end-2025 (~$182m)**, L/D ratio ~83.7% — this is a licence-and-rails acquisition, not a book-of-business acquisition.

**[1] Competitors / peers.** The clear peer frame is **crypto exchange EU-licensing strategy**, and Payward is genuinely differentiated: rivals stopped at **MiCA CASP licences, not bank charters**. [[Coinbase acquires onchain capital platform Echo for $375M|Coinbase]] took a MiCA licence via Luxembourg CSSF (20 Jun 2025, first US exchange); Gemini via Malta MFSA (Aug 2025); OKX/Crypto.com/Gate also Malta; Bitpanda via Austria FMA. None owns an EU *bank*. The direct behavioural analogue is a **neobank, not a crypto firm**: Revolut got a specialised banking licence from the Bank of Lithuania in 2018 (same regulator, same licence class) — the exact template. So Payward's position: **ahead / first-of-kind** among crypto-native firms to own an EU credit institution. **Why the landscape is this way (2nd-order):** MiCA is satisfied by a CASP licence (which Kraken already holds — [[Kraken secures MiCA license in Ireland|MiCA via Central Bank of Ireland, Jun 2025]], covering all 30 EEA states). So a bank charter adds **nothing to crypto-trading reach** — it only unlocks deposit-taking / net-interest income / lending. Peers haven't followed because the prudential capital (CRD/CRR, LCR/NSFR) + deposit-guarantee burden deters them; being first may signal a real lead, or that the economics don't clear for most. The moat shifts from matching-engine to **charter + balance sheet**.

**[2] Company history / fit.** This fits a now-unmistakable **charter-stacking pattern** ahead of the planned IPO: [[Kraken becomes first crypto firm with Fed master account|Fed master account via Wyoming SPDI (Mar 2026)]], [[Kraken parent Payward applies for OCC national trust charter|OCC national-trust-charter application (May 2026)]], [[Kraken secures VARA authorization to expand in UAE|UAE VARA]], [[Kraken parent Payward secures British Virgin Islands license|BVI licence]], plus the capability M&A string — [[Payward acquires Bitnomial for CFTC-licensed derivatives stack|Bitnomial (CFTC stack)]], NinjaTrader, Backed, Magna and [[Kraken parent Payward to acquire Reap for up to $600 million|Reap (stablecoin cards/payments, $600m)]]. The EU banking leg was explicitly pre-announced in [[Kraken to pursue European banking licence via Lithuania|July 2026 (CoinDesk/The Paypers)]] — which named Lithuania and listed **Saldo Bank** among the specialised banks there. **Why the company acts this way (structural logic):** Payward's core is pro-cyclical trading-fee revenue (~0.11% take rate); Q1 2026 adj. EBITDA collapsed to $18m (~3.6% margin) vs ~24% FY2025 as crypto volume fell ~38%. A bank charter is the single most credible lever to add **net-interest income** that is far less correlated to crypto price — de-cyclicalising the P&L and underpinning a "regulated financial institution" multiple for the [[Kraken files confidentially for US IPO at $20B|~$20bn IPO]] (which may [[Kraken cuts 150 jobs after AI deployment; IPO may slip to 2027|slip to 2027]]).

**[3] Novelty / value-add / traction.** NEW vs July: this is the **"reported intent → signed definitive deal with a named target"** follow-up — a concrete, confirmed M&A (not the unconfirmed CoinDesk trial balloon). The genuine novelty: **first crypto-native firm to own an EU bank**, enabling Payward to offer Europeans one account to *hold cash + crypto + tokenized stocks, move via SEPA, and borrow against assets without selling* (Sethi). **Why the value-add is real but bounded (2nd-order):** real — it unlocks deposit-taking/NII and "borrow-against-crypto" lending that a CASP licence legally cannot; the licence + deposit-guarantee + passport are hard to replicate. Bounded — (a) **Saldo is sub-scale** (EUR 163m assets): the acquired book is immaterial; value is the *licence and the ability to pour Kraken's 6m+ funded accounts into it*, which is execution-dependent and years out; (b) the **capital cost cuts the other way** — a crypto-heavy balance sheet under CRD/CRR + deposit-guarantee may draw heavier prudential treatment than Revolut faced in 2018, diluting the very NII margin it's meant to add; (c) **who captures the margin** depends on Payward actually migrating European users onto regulated deposits/lending — announced, not live. Traction so far: zero crypto deposits on the Saldo book; this is Day-0.

**[4] What's next / market sentiment. FRESHNESS VERDICT: FRESH.** This is a genuine new event — a *signed* acquisition of a *named* bank — materially advancing the [[Kraken to pursue European banking licence via Lithuania|July 2026 reported-intent note]]. It is NOT a reprint of that note; it is the confirmed follow-through (and it resolves the July open question "apply vs acquire" → **acquire**). Watch-items: (a) **regulatory approval** — Bank of Lithuania/ECB change-of-control review of a crypto-native owner of a deposit-taking bank is non-trivial; close only expected 2027; (b) **price never disclosed** — scrutinise when/if revealed; (c) whether Payward migrates Kraken EU users and crypto-collateralised lending onto the Saldo licence, and how the ECB treats crypto on a credit-institution balance sheet; (d) **IPO interplay** — bank-holding-company-like consolidated supervision could complicate a US listing even as it strengthens the equity narrative; (e) **peer response** — if it works, expect Coinbase/others to pursue EU bank charters, eroding the first-mover edge. Sentiment: coverage broadly frames it as a landmark "crypto-meets-tradfi" milestone; the skeptical read is that the hard part (regulatory close + actual integration) is entirely ahead and the acquired bank is tiny.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. **Price undisclosed** — why no deal value, cash/stock split, or multiple on a ~EUR 163m-asset bank? Is the figure immaterial (licence buy) or being withheld because it is unflatteringly small vs the $20bn-valuation story? (open)
2. **Freshness vs the July note** — is this just a re-report of the [[Kraken to pursue European banking licence via Lithuania|July 2026 reported intent]]? No: July was an unconfirmed "apply vs acquire" trial balloon; this is a signed definitive agreement with a named target (Saldo) — a genuine talks→deal follow-up. **Fresh.**
3. **Signed vs closed** — "definitive agreement subject to regulatory approval, expected to close 2027" means nothing is done. Will Bank of Lithuania/ECB approve a crypto-native firm owning a deposit-taking bank? (open — the binding risk)
4. **What is actually acquired?** A EUR 162.9m-asset bank is a *licence + passport*, not a franchise. Is the value the charter and the ability to migrate Kraken's 6m+ users, or the (immaterial) existing loan/deposit book? (licence, per analysis)
5. **Does it add addressable market or just optics?** Kraken already has MiCA (Ireland, all 30 EEA) + MiFID (Cyprus) + EMI. The bank licence adds deposit-taking/NII/lending that a CASP legally can't — so substantive, not just optics. But how much incremental revenue, realistically? (open)
6. **Capital/prudential burden** — does CRD/CRR + LCR/NSFR + deposit-guarantee on a crypto-heavy balance sheet draw heavier treatment than Revolut faced in 2018, diluting the NII margin the deal is meant to add? (open — key downside)
7. **Specialised vs universal bank** — Saldo holds an ECB specialised-bank licence (Oct 2021). Confirm scope limits (no full investment-services?) and whether that constrains Kraken's intended products. (specialised; scope limits apply)
8. **Deposit-crypto ring-fencing** — how is a EUR 100k-guaranteed deposit base insulated from volatile crypto on/around the balance sheet, and will the ECB force a hard separation? (open)
9. **Revolut analogy validity** — Revolut is a neobank; Kraken is a crypto exchange. Same regulator/licence class, but does a crypto balance sheet face far stiffer ECB scrutiny on change-of-control? (likely yes — analysis)
10. **First-mover: lead or warning?** No crypto exchange owns an EU bank. Is being first a real moat, or a signal the capital/regulatory economics deter peers (Coinbase/Gemini stayed at MiCA CASP) for good reason? (open)
11. **IPO interplay** — does owning an EU credit institution add consolidated/bank-holding-style supervision that complicates the US IPO, even as it strengthens the "regulated FI" narrative? (open — cuts both ways)
12. **Integration/traction** — zero crypto deposits sit on Saldo today. What proof exists Payward can migrate EU users onto regulated deposits + crypto-collateralised lending, and over what timeline (2027+ close)? (none yet — Day-0)
13. **Geographic reach** — Saldo serves LT/FI/SE/DE + NL passport. Can Payward passport the bank across all 30 EEA states, or is activation market-by-market? (open)
14. **Why Lithuania specifically** — capital-efficient specialised-bank regime + English-language fast process + Revolut precedent. But Lithuania's fintech hub has a mixed AML-scrutiny history — reputational risk to the approval? (analysis)
15. **Does this de-risk the cyclical P&L enough?** Q1 2026 adj. EBITDA was $18m (~3.6% margin) on a ~0.11% trading take-rate. NII from a EUR 163m-asset bank is trivial near-term — so is the "de-cyclicalisation" real for years, or narrative for the IPO? (narrative near-term — analysis)

**Importance: 4/5** — A concrete, well-corroborated, signed M&A that makes Payward the first crypto-native firm to own an EU bank and materially advances the July 2026 reported intent from "apply-vs-acquire" to a named, signed deal. Novel and strategically significant (charter + balance-sheet moat; NII de-cyclicalisation; IPO legitimacy). Held at 4 (not 5) because the target is sub-scale (~EUR 163m assets), the price is undisclosed, nothing closes until 2027 pending ECB/Bank of Lithuania approval, and all integration/traction is entirely ahead — a landmark signal, not yet a consequential operating fact.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Crypto exchanges are racing to bolt regulated banking/EMI rails onto trading platforms for the MiCA era. Payward (Kraken parent) agreed 2026-10-14 to buy Saldo Finance Oy (owner of Lithuania-licensed Saldo Bank UAB); terms undisclosed, close expected 2027 subject to regulatory approval (per Bloomberg/CoinDesk, 2026-10-14). Saldo brings ~€162.9m assets and €962k profit — a modest balance sheet; the real asset is an EU banking licence that passports deposits, lending, card issuance and SEPA across the bloc (CoinDesk). CoinDesk frames it as the first time a crypto firm buys a European *bank* (vs merely an EMI/CASP). Structure: EU crypto-banking access is fragmented and licence-gated — the barrier is regulatory capital + a bank/EMI charter, not technology. Why now: MiCA (live since 2024) plus national bank-licence routes let exchanges verticalise into fiat deposits/credit as pure trading take-rates compress; Lithuania is the preferred hub (fast English-language regulator, home to Revolut's and Robinhood's EU entities).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: trading take-rate / transaction revenue, AUC (assets under custody), and — post-deal — net interest income + deposit base. Routes to the same goal diverge: Coinbase consolidated national licences into a single Luxembourg MiCA CASP licence (2026-06-26) covering all 27 EU states but did **not** buy a bank; Robinhood EU and Gemini (MFSA, Malta, 2025-08) are MiCA-CASP only; Revolut already runs a full EU bank licence (Lithuania) — the model Payward is buying its way into. Payward's own ladder is the most aggressive: Lithuanian EMI → this bank acquisition in the EU, plus a Wyoming SPDI charter, a Fed master account (2026-03) and an OCC national-trust-charter application (2026-05) in the US — see [[Kraken parent Payward applies for OCC national trust charter]]. Recent adjacent M&A: Reap ($600m, stablecoin payments, 2026-05) [[Kraken parent Payward to acquire Reap for up to $600 million]] and Bitnomial (derivatives, 2026-05) [[Payward completes acquisition of derivatives platform Bitnomial]]. Position: **ahead** of pure-CASP peers on the regulated-banking axis; moat = regulatory licences + scale (intangible/regulatory, hard to replicate) `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Private-parent benchmark: Payward last raised at **$20bn** (2026-01/05) [[Kraken parent Payward raises capital at $20 billion valuation]]; a Nasdaq Ventures $100m investment implied **~$21bn** (2026-09), against ~$2.2bn 2025 revenue (+33% YoY) and ~$531m EBITDA → **round P/S ≈ $21bn / $2.2bn = ~9.5x** (round valuation, not a public market cap; per quantlogix/roic citations) `[UNSOURCED on audited figures]`. Public peer Coinbase (COIN): market cap ~$38.6bn at 2026-06-30 with **EV/Revenue ≈ 5.5x** (down from ~7.0x in May), Q2'26 net revenue $1.2bn, Adj. EBITDA $208m, net loss $359m (Coinbase 10-Q, 2026-06-30). So Kraken's ~9.5x round multiple sits **rich vs Coinbase's ~5.5x** — partly explained by higher growth and private-round optics, but the gap is wide enough to watch. The Saldo deal multiple itself = **no data** (price undisclosed); vs €162.9m assets and €962k profit the price is almost entirely licence/strategic premium, not book value. Internal licence/EU comps: [[Kraken parent Payward secures British Virgin Islands license]], [[Dubai's VARA grants Kraken's Payward preliminary approval]], [[Kraken parent Payward applies for OCC national trust charter]].

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory/closing risk** — deal needs Bank of Lithuania (and likely ECB-adjacent) approval; close slipped to 2027. A crypto parent buying a licensed deposit-taker invites heightened fit-and-proper and AML scrutiny; US bank lobbies already contested Kraken's Fed master account [[Bank groups voice concern over Kraken Fed master account]]. Second-order: a blocked/conditioned approval strands the EU banking thesis.
2. **Integration/capital drain** — a deposit bank consumes regulatory capital and demands credit-risk and compliance muscle Payward hasn't run at scale; this is the 3rd+ acquisition in ~5 months (Bitnomial, Reap, Saldo) while an IPO was already frozen once (2026-03). Second-order: acquisition sprawl + capital tie-up could push the IPO further out (it had risked slipping to 2027).
3. **Valuation vs comps** — ~9.5x round P/S vs Coinbase ~5.5x EV/Rev; if growth normalises toward peer rates, the private mark re-rates down at/near IPO. Second-order: a down-round optics risk for a pre-IPO name.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is the leading edge of crypto-exchange → full-bank verticalisation in the EU: the question shifts from "who has a MiCA CASP licence" to "who holds a deposit-taking bank charter." Falsifiable thesis: if the deal closes in 2027 and Saldo's licence lets Payward passport deposits/lending EU-wide, expect Coinbase/Gemini/others to pursue their own bank-licence M&A rather than stay CASP-only. Trigger to watch: Bank of Lithuania approval timeline; what would break the thesis — regulators ring-fencing the bank from the crypto parent (forced structural separation), making the licence far less synergistic.
