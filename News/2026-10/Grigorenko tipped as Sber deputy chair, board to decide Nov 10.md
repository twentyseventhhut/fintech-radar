## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened (dated).** On 2026-10-22 Sberbank announced that its Supervisory Board (набсовет) will on **2026-11-10** consider appointing Dmitry Grigorenko — until this week Russia's Deputy Prime Minister and Chief of the Government Staff — to the bank's management board. Russian wires (Interfax, RIA, Kommersant, Vedomosti) specify the post as **first deputy chairman of the board (первый зампред правления)**, a notch above the "deputy chair" wording in the source digest. His mandate: Sber's strategic projects and the group's interaction with federal/regional government, including digitalization and AI rollout in the public sector. Gref framed the hire as strengthening the bank's "systemic dialogue with the state." Sources: [Interfax](https://www.interfax.ru/russia/1124200), [RIA](https://ria.ru/20261022/grigorenko--2126216544.html), [Kommersant](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/9045196), [Vedomosti](https://www.vedomosti.ru/finance/news/2026/10/22/1239158-sber-rassmotrit-grigorenko).

**[1] Who Grigorenko is.** Deputy PM + Chief of Government Staff since Jan 2020 under Mishustin; career tax official (Federal Tax Service through 2013, incl. FNS digital modernization). As VP he curated the "Data Economy and Digital Transformation of the State" national project, state information systems, and government AI adoption; chaired the government's AI-development commission. He is regarded as a close Mishustin ally and one of the cabinet's most effective digital-transformation administrators. Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Grigorenko), [government.ru](http://government.ru/en/persons/621/), [CNews](https://www.cnews.ru/news/top/2026-10-07_dmitrij_grigorenko_zayavil).

**[2] Why now — the reshuffle.** His Sber move follows a government reshuffle announced Mon 2026-10-20: Grigorenko exits the cabinet; **Irina Okladnikova** was named Chief of Government Staff, but not all his portfolios transfer automatically — the digital block may go to VP Dmitry Chernyshenko. So this is an outbound senior-official move into a systemically important state-controlled bank, not a greenfield hire. Sources: [The Moscow Times (ru)](https://ru.themoscowtimes.com/2026/10/22/gref-priyutil-v-sbere-blizkogo-k-mishustinu-chinovnika-otstavlennogo-iz-pravitelstva-a209310), [ru-bezh](https://ru-bezh.ru/gossektor/news/26/10/20/dmitriy-grigorenko-pokinet-post-vitse-premera-kto-budet-kurirova).

**[3] Sber–state context.** Sber already pushes its tech into government: it automated bank-guarantee issuance for the Federal Customs Service down to ~7 minutes ([[Sber fully automates bank guarantees for Federal Customs Service]]), its GigaChat/AI agents classify budget spending at the Finance Ministry, and Gref publicly advises state companies on IPO discipline ([[Mergers.ru Gref advises state companies on successful IPOs]]). Notably, just two weeks before the announcement, on **2026-10-07** Gref and Grigorenko shared the stage at the government "Цифровые решения" IT forum and publicly disagreed over whether business or the state better understands the end user — underlining both the depth of the relationship and the role Grigorenko is being hired to bridge. Sources: [Kommersant 2026-10-07](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/9008524), [CNews 2026-10-07](https://www.cnews.ru/news/top/2026-10-07_dmitrij_grigorenko_zayavil).

**[4] What to watch.** (a) Whether the набсовет confirms on 2026-11-10 and at what rank (first-deputy vs deputy); (b) which exact functions he gets — GR/государственные продажи vs a formal AI/strategy mandate; (c) governance-independence optics of placing a just-departed senior federal official into a state bank with majority government/CBR-linked ownership; (d) whether this foreshadows deeper Sber penetration of GovTech procurement. No prior note covers this appointment — this is the first in the vault. Freshness: **fresh**.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russian state-adjacent banking + GovTech/AI-for-government. Sber is the structurally dominant incumbent: FY2025 IFRS net profit RUB 1.7tn (+7.9% y/y), ROE 22.7%, net interest income RUB 3,556bn (+18.5%), cost-to-income 30.3%, CET-style capital adequacy 13.7%, total equity +16.4% to RUB 8.3tn; FY2025 dividend recommended at 50% payout = RUB 850bn (per Sber FY2025 IFRS results, via TASS/Moscow Times/Investing.com, as of 2026-02-26). Market structure is highly consolidated around a handful of state/quasi-state banks (Sber, VTB, Gazprombank) plus private disruptors (T-Bank, Alfa); entry barriers = regulation, licence, capital, and — increasingly — control of domestic AI/cloud rails under sanctions. "Why now": (1) Grigorenko's nomination (board vote 2026-11-10) follows Putin's decree relieving him as Deputy PM / Chief of Government Staff the same day (per Moscow Times, 2026-10-22) — it imports a senior state-digitalisation operator directly into Sber's management board to run "strategic projects with government," i.e. productising GovTech; (2) sanctions push Sber to localise AI hardware (CEO Gref sought Chinese Ascend-class chips for GigaChat, per Reuters/Investing.com, 2026-05-20), raising the strategic value of locked-in state demand.

**Competitive landscape.** Bank KPIs: ROE, NIM/NII growth, cost-to-income, CAR; for the GovTech angle: share of public-sector digital spend captured and AI model adoption. Key players: Sber (dominant, own LLM GigaChat, cloud, ~half the sector's profit), VTB/Gazprombank (state, weaker tech), T-Bank/Alfa (private, digital-native, consumer-led), and Yandex (YandexGPT) as the rival domestic LLM. Basis of competition in GovTech is distribution + political access + a sovereign AI stack, not price. Recent moves: Sber already embeds AI in government — e.g. Minfin uses a Sber AI agent to classify budget spend, and Sber automated bank guarantees for the Federal Customs Service ([[Sber fully automates bank guarantees for Federal Customs Service]]). Position: clear leader in Russian GovTech; the Grigorenko hire deepens the moat via regulatory/relationship capital and insider knowledge of the "Data Economy" national project he previously ran — a switching-cost/intangibles moat (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** Russian listed banks trade on deep-discount earnings multiples (closed, sanctioned market — not comparable to global banks). Sber: market cap ~RUB 2.99tn vs FY2025 net profit RUB 1.7tn → trailing P/E ~ RUB 2.99tn / RUB 1.7tn = ~1.8x; forward P/E ~3.6x; EPS RUB 77.8 (+8.0%). VTB: trailing P/E ~1.2x, forward ~0.8x, market cap ~RUB 248bn (per stockanalysis.com / Yahoo Finance / MarketScreener, as of Oct 2026). So Sber is RICHER than VTB on P/E — justified by far higher ROE (22.7% vs VTB's thinner returns) and the tech/GovTech optionality. Internal comps: [[Sberbank H1 2026 net profit rises 20.4% to RUB 995bn]] (momentum into the hire), [[Russia's central bank rejects Sber, Alfa, T-Bank payment system project]] (state can also constrain Sber), [[Mergers.ru Gref advises state companies on successful IPOs]] (Sber's deepening state-advisory role). No clean EV/EBITDA for banks; price-per-GovTech-contract not disclosed → [UNSOURCED].

**Risk flags.**
- Concentration on a single counterparty — the Russian state: a management board seat explicitly for "strategic projects with government" ties revenue growth to political favour; the same state body (CBR) has already blocked a Sber-led payment project ([[Russia's central bank rejects Sber, Alfa, T-Bank payment system project]]), so dependence cuts both ways.
- Governance/independence optics: inserting a just-departed Deputy PM into the management board blurs the line between a public company and the state, raising the risk that commercial capital allocation bends to policy goals (second-order: harder for minority holders to price).
- Hardware/sanctions risk to the GovTech thesis: the AI-for-government pitch rests on GigaChat, whose scaling depends on contested access to Chinese chips (per Reuters, 2026-05-20) — a supply shock would cap the very "strategic projects" Grigorenko is hired to grow.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a re-positioning, not a re-rating: Sber is institutionalising GovTech as a board-level pillar and buying the relationships to win sovereign-AI mandates (analysis). Falsifiable thesis — within ~2-3 quarters of a confirmed 2026-11-10 vote, expect new/expanded federal AI and digital-government contracts flowing to Sber (GigaChat/GigaPlatform in Minfin, FNS, regions). Trigger to watch: the board decision on 10 Nov and the first post-appointment state AI deal. What breaks it: the nomination stalls/fails, or chip-supply constraints throttle GigaChat rollout.

## Earnings Review

**No full earnings report in the news** — this is a governance/leadership item (Dmitry Grigorenko tipped as Sber deputy chairman; supervisory board to decide Nov 10), not a results print. Below is Sber's OWN latest reported financials as scale/trajectory context, from its most recent IFRS filing.

**Latest reported period (context, not a fresh print).** FY2025 (12M 2025, IFRS), released 2026-02-26.
- Net profit: RUB 1,705.9bn (+7.9% YoY) — a record, but the slowest profit growth in years amid Russia's rate-driven slowdown. EPS RUB 77.8 (+8.0%).
- ROE: 22.7% — still elite, though below the ~24% the group ran at in prior years.
- Net interest income: RUB 3,556bn (+18.5% YoY); NIM expanded to 6.5% in Q4 2025 from 6.0% in Q4 2024 — high rates lifting the core spread.
- Net fee & commission income: RUB 834bn (-1.1% YoY) — the soft spot; fee income went backwards, so profit growth leaned almost entirely on NII.
- Cost-to-income: 30.3% (lean); cost of risk: 1.3% for the year — but reserving rose sharply through 2025 (9M 2025 provisions/revaluation RUB ~504bn, a 1.8x jump YoY), the clearest sign of asset-quality pressure in a high-rate economy.
- Balance sheet: corporate loans RUB 31,164bn (+12.4% YoY), retail loans RUB 19,207bn, total equity RUB 8.3tn (+16.4% YTD). CET1 13.0% (+0.9pp), total capital adequacy 14.1%.
- Capital return: dividend ~RUB 787bn declared; management reiterated a 50% net-profit payout policy.

**Trajectory.** Through 2025 the deceleration was visible quarter-on-quarter: 9M 2025 net profit RUB 1,307bn (+6.5% YoY), Q3 2025 RUB 448.3bn (+9.0% YoY), full-year +7.9%. So the growth engine is almost entirely NII/margin, with fees flat-to-down and cost of risk climbing. (analysis) The bank is earning record roubles off a wide rate spread while provisioning more against a slowing borrower base — a quality-of-earnings watch item, not yet a thesis-breaker given 22.7% ROE and a 14.1% capital ratio.

**Why this governance story matters to the financials (why-ladder).** Grigorenko would own "strategic projects with the state" — scaling Sber's AI/GigaChat and gov-tech (Minfin AI agent, regional automation) into the public sector. Fact: he ran the government's "Data Economy / digital transformation of the state" national project and the FNS modernisation. Why it matters: a credible public-sector distribution channel is exactly what could re-accelerate the stalling fee/non-interest line (-1.1% YoY) that NII growth is currently masking. Second-order: it also tightens Sber's already-deep entanglement with the Russian state — a concentration/governance risk as much as a growth lever. This is an org/channel bet, not a near-term earnings event; no figures attach to it yet [UNSOURCED].
