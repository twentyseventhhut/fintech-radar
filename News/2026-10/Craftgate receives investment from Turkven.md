Perakendeden turizme, e-ticaretten sigortacılığa farklı sektörlerde faaliyet gösteren işletmelere hizmet veren Craftgate, Turkven’in katılımıyla Türkiye ve global pazarlardaki büyümesini hızlandırmayı amaçlıyor. Şirket, bu alandaki yaklaşımını üye işyerlerinin ihtiyaçları ve somut fayda sağlayabilecek kullanım alanları doğrultusunda şekillendirmeyi hedefliyor.

_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Turkven, Turkey's largest PE firm (>$1.5B AUM, 26 portfolio companies as of Sep 2026), took a **minority** stake in Craftgate, a Istanbul-based payment-orchestration platform. The founding partners (Hakan Erdoğan et al.) **kept the majority**. **Deal size, stake % and valuation were all undisclosed** — the single most important fact the PR withholds, and it de-rates the item: a minority growth investment with no number attached is hard to weigh.

**Why structured this way:** founders retaining control + undisclosed amount signals a **growth-equity / secondary-tinted minority deal**, not a control buyout. Turkven is not re-platforming Craftgate; it is buying optionality on a cash-generative payments-infra asset. The PR leans on two self-reported traction figures — **$10B transaction volume processed in 2026** and **99.9992% uptime** — because without a deal number, volume/uptime are the only hard metrics to anchor investor confidence. (Independent corroboration of scale trajectory: ~€1B volume in 2023, €1.189B in H1 2024 per third-party profiles — i.e. the $10B/2026 figure represents a genuine order-of-magnitude step-up, though it is company-reported.)

**The AI language** ("evaluating opportunities AI could create") is explicitly aspirational ("değerlendirilmesi" = evaluation) — a roadmap gesture, not a shipped capability. Treat as PR filler.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Turkey's acquiring/gateway layer is crowded: **iyzico** (market leader, PayU+Paynet merged under it Jan 2026, now open-banking authorized), **Param** (1M+ wallet users, full stack: wallet+POS+issuing+gateway), **PayTR**, **Stripe** (local presence), **Troy** (domestic scheme), plus **Birleşik Ödeme**, **DgPays**, **Finartz**, **Vepara**.
Craftgate occupies a **different layer**: it is orchestration *on top of* acquirers, not an acquirer itself — it routes across all Turkish bank virtual-POS and PSPs (Klarna, Stripe, PayPal, Braintree, Alipay etc.) via one integration. **Position: category leader domestically for pure orchestration**, but the category itself is thin — most of the named "competitors" are gateways/acquirers, i.e. partly Craftgate's *suppliers*, not rivals.
**Why the landscape is this way (analysis):** Turkey's fragmented bank-POS + heavy installment culture + a domestic scheme (Troy) makes single-integration orchestration genuinely valuable locally — more so than in markets with 1–2 dominant acquirers. Globally the orchestration category is hot ($3.3B in 2026 → projected $11.4B by 2034, ~18% CAGR), which is likely what drew Turkven. But the second-order risk: large acquirers (iyzico/Param) can **absorb orchestration features**, compressing the independent orchestrator's moat.

## [2] Company history / fit
Founded **2020** by Hakan Erdoğan (ex-iyzico, ex-GittiGidiyor), Lemi Orhan Ergin, Murathan Özcan, Sabri Onur Tüzün. Early $1M from Hepsiburada + D4 Ventures. Scaled from ~€1B (2023) to a reported $10B (2026) processed volume; 500+ merchants (Beymen, Boyner, MediaMarkt, ebebek). Established **Craftgate Europe (Spain)** for international expansion.
**Why Turkven now (analysis):** orchestration is a software/take-rate hybrid with recurring, sticky merchant integrations — exactly the SaaS-flavored, cash-generative profile a PE fund wants. Craftgate is past product-risk and into scale-up/geo-expansion, which needs growth capital and a credible local partner for enterprise sales — Turkven supplies both. The deal fits Turkven's stated fintech/SaaS focus and its new **Fund V** (approved Jul 2026, targeting ~$400M).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Not a new product or new capability** — the orchestration platform has existed since 2020. The *event* is purely a capital/ownership change. Real traction is decent and above announcement-ware: $10B company-reported volume, named blue-chip merchants, live Spain subsidiary. **Where the margin sits (analysis):** orchestrators earn a thin software/per-transaction fee layered above acquirer economics; the durable value is **switching cost from the single-integration lock-in**, not a take-rate. The threat to the multiple is **disintermediation** — if acquirers bundle routing/retry for free, the standalone orchestrator's value-add commoditizes. Turkven's bet is that cross-border + AI-driven optimization keeps Craftgate a layer merchants pay for.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Stated plan: more Turkish merchants, faster product dev, AI features, continued EU/LatAm expansion (Spain live). Sentiment: a quiet, positive local-ecosystem story (Turkish fintech press coverage), no global resonance. **Why this matters little at scale (analysis):** undisclosed, minority, no number, no new capability — this is a routine growth-PE round in a mid-size private company, not a market-moving event. The counterintuitive read: the *most* interesting datapoint is Turkven (a classic buyout shop) validating pure payment-orchestration as an asset class in Turkey — a signal about the category, more than about Craftgate specifically.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Payment orchestration (a routing/abstraction layer that lets a merchant reach many PSPs, acquirers and alt-payment methods through one integration, with smart routing + retries to lift auth rates and cut cost). Market is small but fast-growing and estimates diverge widely: per Coherent Market Insights, ~$2.85bn in 2026 at ~12% CAGR to 2033; per Vantage Market Research, a higher ~$9.7bn-by-2035 trajectory at 13.2% CAGR; a third firm pegs ~$4.2bn in 2026 → $18bn by 2034 at 20% (via marketresearch.com / vantagemarketresearch.com / intelmarketresearch.com, as of Oct 2026 — treat as illustrative, the spread itself signals an immature, poorly-defined category). Structure: the orchestration layer is an independent middle tier sitting *above* gateways/acquirers (Stripe, Adyen, Checkout.com, Worldpay) and *below* the merchant — fragmented, with many sub-scale specialists (Spreedly, Primer, Gr4vy, IXOPAY, APEXX, BR-DGE, BlueSnap) plus incumbents moving in (ACI, see [[ACI Worldwide on payments orchestration]]). Entry barrier is integration breadth + reliability, not capital. Why now: proliferation of payment methods, multi-PSP setups and rising auth-rate/cost pressure push merchants toward an orchestration tier; Turkey adds a local "why now" — the country is pursuing SEPA membership (see [[Turkey pursues membership in Single Euro Payments Area]]) and opened open banking to iyzico/Odeal (see [[Turkey authorizes iyzico and Odeal for open banking]]), expanding the rails Craftgate would need to orchestrate.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/payment volume, auth-rate uplift, service uptime, merchant count, take rate. Craftgate's disclosed metrics: ~$10bn payment volume processed in 2026 and 99.9992% service-continuity, serving 600+ merchants across retail, travel, e-commerce and insurance (via egirisim.com / fintechistanbul.org, Oct 2026). Basis of competition is product/integration breadth and reliability rather than price. Recent peer moves: Gr4vy–Mastercard integration partnership, Oct-2025 (see [[Payment orchestrator Gr4vy partners with Mastercard on integration]]); Primer raised a $100m Series C in May-2026 pivoting to "AI payment agents" (per thenextweb.com/finance.yahoo.com) — the same AI-orchestration narrative Craftgate's CEO cites. Position: Craftgate is a regional leader/niche specialist — dominant in Turkey, sub-scale globally vs the named Western peers; moat is switching costs (merchants embed it as their single payment integration) + local acquirer/regulatory depth `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal terms (amount, valuation) NOT disclosed — Turkven joined the cap table with founders retaining majority (via egirisim.com). So no entry multiple can be computed — "no data". Reference points:
- iyzico (Turkey, closest domestic payments comp) — acquired by PayU for **$165m in 2019** (per webrazzi.com/dailysabah.com; the "$560m" figure in some coverage is cumulative PayU fintech spend, not the iyzico price). No revenue disclosed → EV/Rev "no data".
- Primer — last disclosed valuation **$425m (2021)**; revenue ~£2.9m FY2023 vs ~£15.6m operating loss; $170m raised total (per multiples.vc / tracxn). Implied ~100x+ historical P/S on stale numbers → `[UNSOURCED]` as a current multiple.
- Spreedly — $82.8m total funding, valuation undisclosed (per dealroom); EV/Rev "no data".
Internal comps: [[ACI Worldwide on payments orchestration]], [[Payment orchestrator Gr4vy partners with Mastercard on integration]]. Distribution not computed (no ≥3 comparable priced figures) — qualitative only. Note the gap: Craftgate's $10bn TPV dwarfs Primer's/Spreedly's disclosed scale, yet Western peers command far larger funding — a signal that revenue (take rate × TPV), not TPV, drives orchestration value and that Craftgate's take rate is undisclosed `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Risk flags.**
1. Single-market concentration — Craftgate's disclosed scale is Turkey-centric; the TRY's structural inflation/FX volatility means USD-stated TPV and any valuation are exposed to currency swings, and "global expansion" is stated intent, not demonstrated (de-PR: the PR says "aims to", not "operates abroad").
2. Disintermediation / margin squeeze from above and below — gateways (Adyen, Stripe, Checkout.com) increasingly bundle their own routing, and incumbents like ACI are entering orchestration; an independent thin middle layer risks being compressed out of the stack, capturing little of the economics it routes.
3. Undisclosed economics — no deal size, valuation, take rate or revenue disclosed, so growth-vs-price cannot be sanity-checked; the heavy "AI" framing (CEO) is roadmap, not shipped, and a classic PR tell when hard numbers are absent.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** A PE sponsor (Turkven, ~$5bn deployed / 26-co portfolio, track record of Mavi/Domino's/Pronet exits) taking a growth minority in a profitable-enough, cash-generative orchestrator reads as a consolidation/scale-up play, not a VC moonshot — expect a buy-and-build toward a regional (Turkey + MENA/CEE) payments champion and an eventual trade sale, echoing the iyzico/PayU template `(analysis)`. Watch for: (a) a follow-on bolt-on acquisition or geographic launch within ~12–18mo as the Turkven catalyst; the thesis is wrong if Craftgate stays single-market and the named Western gateways' native routing erodes its merchant base.
