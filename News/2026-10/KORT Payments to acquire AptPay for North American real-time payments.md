Combination adds AptPay's multi-rail money movement platform to KORT's omnichannel payments business 
 TORONTO , Oct. 19, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- KORT Payments ("KORT"), a specialized omnichannel payments provider, today announced that it has entered into a definitive agreement to acquire 100% of AptPay Inc. ("AptPay"), a Toronto-based payments technology company specializing in real-time payments and payment orchestration. Terms of the transaction were not disclosed.
AptPay's platform provides businesses with a single integration for moving money across multiple payment rails, including Mastercard TM  Send, VISA ®  Original Credit Transaction (OCT), Interac e-Transfer ® , Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT), Automated Clearing House (ACH), Real Time Payments (RTP), FedNow ®  and cross-border. It has built-in compliance tools for Know Your Customer (KYC), Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and sanctions screening. AptPay serves merchants and platforms across Canada and the United States, with a strong presence in online gaming and gambling , digital assets, financial services and other high-growth sectors.
The acquisition expands KORT's capabilities beyond card acceptance to include account-to-account payments and disbursements, enabling customers to collect and send funds through a single provider and consolidated platform. The transaction also supports KORT's growth strategy by broadening its product offering and accelerating its expansion in the United States.
"AptPay's real-time payout and orchestration capabilities are highly complementary to our platform, and together we can offer customers a complete money movement solution across North America," said Danny Chazonoff, President and Chief Operating Officer of KORT Payments. "This acquisition strengthens our position in the payments ecosystem by combining our team's deep industry expertise and established relationships with AptPay's strong market presence and robust, scalable technology platform."
"Joining KORT strengthens our business by bringing together two teams with a shared vision and deep experience in building and scaling payments businesses," said Shams Syed, Chief Executive Officer of AptPay. "That proven experience, combined with KORT's platform and resources, puts us in a stronger position to accelerate AptPay's growth. We'll be able to scale faster, expand our reach across North America and beyond, and continue delivering the speed, reliability, and innovation our partners have come to expect from us."
Following the closing, AptPay will operate as a division of KORT under the AptPay brand, and Mr. Syed will continue to lead that business unit. AptPay customers and partners will continue to be supported without interruption.
The transaction is expected to close in Q4 2026, subject to customary closing conditions.
 About KORT Payments 
KORT Payments is a specialized omnichannel payments provider that enables businesses to connect and transact seamlessly through capabilities in compliance, risk management and payment processing. KORT is powered by a full-stack, enterprise-grade platform and led by a seasoned management team. For more information, visit www.kortpayments.com .
 About AptPay 
Founded in 2019 and headquartered in Toronto, AptPay is a payments technology company that specializes in account-to-account payments, which combines payment processing and compliance in a single platform. AptPay enables businesses to collect and disburse funds in real time across multiple payment rails, from domestic payments to instant disbursements and cross-border transfers, with integrated KYC, AML and sanctions screening. For more information, visit www.aptpay.com .

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**The deal.** On 2026-10-19 KORT Payments (Toronto), a specialized omnichannel payments provider, signed a definitive agreement to acquire 100% of AptPay Inc. (Toronto). **Terms were not disclosed.** The transaction is expected to close in **Q4 2026**, subject to customary conditions. Post-close, AptPay runs as a division of KORT under the AptPay brand, with CEO Shams Syed continuing to lead the unit.

**What AptPay does.** Founded 2019 (Toronto; reported ~17 employees as of Apr 2026, no disclosed venture funding — per Crunchbase/Tracxn). It is an account-to-account (A2A) "money movement" / payment-orchestration layer: a single integration to push and pull funds across many rails — Mastercard Send, Visa OCT (Original Credit Transaction), Interac e-Transfer, EFT, ACH/Same-Day ACH, RTP, FedNow, Wires, Push-to-Card and cross-border — with built-in KYC/AML/sanctions screening. Its "Apt-Send" product targets instant disbursements/payouts. Customer base skews to high-velocity-payout verticals: online gaming/gambling, digital assets/crypto, and financial services across Canada and the US.

**Why KORT wants it.** KORT's existing business is card acceptance (omnichannel merchant processing). AptPay extends it into the opposite flow — A2A collections and disbursements/payouts — so KORT can offer "collect and send through a single provider." It also accelerates KORT's US expansion, since AptPay already carries US bank-rail connectivity (ACH/RTP/FedNow/Push-to-Card).

**KORT's backstory / roll-up pattern.** KORT is led by Joel Leonoff (former Paysafe founder/CEO) as CEO, with Danny Chazonoff (ex-Paysafe COO) as President & COO. KORT rebranded from **Merrco Payments** in July 2026 and has been running an aggressive M&A strategy — it also agreed to acquire **Paysafe Direct LLC** (Paysafe's direct-marketing payment-processing business). So AptPay is one of multiple bolt-ons building a full-stack North American payments platform under ex-Paysafe leadership.

**Market timing (the real hook).** Canada is the only G7 country without a live instant-payment rail. Payments Canada's long-delayed **Real-Time Rail (RTR)** — originally promised for 2019, repeatedly pushed (2022 → 2023 → 2026) — had its legal framework/bylaws come into force Aug 24, 2026, with a **phased Q4 2026 launch** (big banks/all participants not until ~summer 2027). So KORT is buying multi-rail orchestration exactly as Canada's domestic instant rail finally goes live, while the US already runs two instant systems at scale (FedNow ~1,725 FIs / ~19.7% of US FIs in Q1'26; RTP ~1,280 participants, ~142M txns / $576B in a recent quarter). AptPay's value is abstracting across all of these.

**Internal links.** Same AptPay surfaced a year earlier in [[Binance.US partners with AptPay to expand US fiat access]] (Oct 2025), where AptPay was Binance.US's orchestration layer for US fiat pay-ins/payouts across ACH/Same-Day ACH/Wires/RTP/FedNow/Push-to-Card — corroborating AptPay's crypto/high-risk vertical focus and the "single integration across rails" pitch. Adjacent Canadian real-time context: [[KOHO joins Interac e-Transfer directly as it nears banking licence]] (direct Interac access as a competitive moat) and [[Nuvion partners with Visa Direct for real-time global payouts]] (Visa Direct/OCT as a disbursement rail). See also [[Binance.US seeks up to $100M to expand US business]] for the counterparty's trajectory.

**Competitive frame.** As orchestration/disbursement middleware, AptPay/KORT compete with Visa Direct and Mastercard Move/Send (which are also rails AptPay rides), US payout orchestrators (Dwolla, Modern Treasury, Astra), and Canadian Interac-adjacent players. Edge claimed: unified multi-rail reach + embedded compliance for regulated, payout-heavy verticals.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. **Price is undisclosed** — no deal value, no multiple, no cash/stock mix. For a ~17-person, reportedly unfunded 2019 startup this signals a small/tuck-in deal; treat any "major acquisition" framing skeptically.
2. **Signed, not closed.** This is a definitive agreement expected to close Q4 2026 "subject to customary closing conditions." Regulatory/antitrust or integration risk could slip it; it is not done.
3. **Counterparty-risk exposure.** AptPay's book skews to online gambling, crypto/digital assets and other "high-risk" verticals — high-margin but high-churn, high-chargeback, and regulatorily fragile (e.g., Binance.US itself is capital-constrained per [[Binance.US seeks up to $100M to expand US business]]). How concentrated is AptPay's revenue in a few such clients?
4. **AptPay is a thin orchestration layer over other people's rails** (Visa OCT, Mastercard Send, RTP, FedNow, Interac). Those rail owners can disintermediate, reprice, or build competing orchestration. What is the durable moat beyond integration convenience + compliance tooling?
5. **Does KORT actually have RTR access?** Canada's Real-Time Rail launches phased in Q4 2026 and may exclude many fintechs until ~2027. "Real-time payments across North America" could be mostly US rails + Interac e-Transfer, not RTR itself. Verify what "real-time" concretely means here.
6. **Serial-acquirer integration risk.** KORT (ex-Merrco, rebranded Jul 2026) is simultaneously digesting Paysafe Direct and now AptPay under a new ex-Paysafe management team. Multiple concurrent bolt-ons raise integration/execution risk.
7. **Is this a Paysafe playbook replay?** Leonoff/Chazonoff ran Paysafe's own high-risk-vertical roll-up. Pattern-match the risks (reliance on gambling/crypto flow, leverage, exit-oriented M&A) rather than taking the press-release narrative at face value.
8. **Revenue/scale of AptPay is unknown** — no disclosed TPV, transaction volume, or revenue. Impossible to size the strategic contribution to KORT from the release alone.
9. **Retention risk.** Founders Vishnu and Barnes aren't named as staying; only CEO Shams Syed is said to lead the unit. Who actually stays, and are key integrations/clients tied to specific people?
10. **"Across North America" overstated?** Both firms are Toronto-based with Canadian roots; US expansion is aspirational ("accelerating expansion in the United States"). Current US footprint/licensing depth is unverified.
11. **Funding/ownership of KORT itself** is opaque — private, no disclosed backer or balance sheet. Can it fund an aggressive multi-deal roll-up without external capital or leverage?
12. **Compliance claims unaudited.** Built-in KYC/AML/sanctions screening for gambling/crypto flows is exactly where enforcement risk lives; the release asserts but does not evidence robustness.
13. **Novelty check.** Multi-rail payout orchestration with embedded compliance is a crowded category (Visa Direct, Mastercard Move, Dwolla, Modern Treasury). What is genuinely differentiated vs. marketing?

**Importance: 3/5** — A real, named, strategically coherent M&A (card acceptance + A2A disbursement roll-up by ex-Paysafe leadership) timed to Canada's RTR launch and US instant-payments momentum, making it a useful signal for the North American real-time-payments consolidation theme. Capped below 4 because terms are undisclosed, the target is tiny (~17 FTEs, unfunded), the deal is only signed (not closed), and the underlying rails/moat are not proprietary — modest standalone market impact.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** North American real-time payments is a fast-growing but still-early layer: the NA real-time payment market was ~$14.7bn in 2025, projected at ~28% CAGR to ~$135bn by 2035 (per Expert Market Research, via marketresearch.com, as of 2026). Rails are maturing fast: US RTP carried ~98% of bank-to-bank instant payments at ~$6.7bn/day and 1,322+ institutions (as of Jul 2026), while FedNow surpassed 1,700 institutions by May 2026 and more than doubled volume into Q2 2026 (per pymnts/paymentsdive, 2026). "Why now": the rail buildout (RTP + FedNow in the US; Canada's Real-Time Rail entering phased rollout late 2026) has shifted the bottleneck from the rails themselves to the orchestration layer — businesses need one integration to reach many rails rather than wiring each separately. Structurally the disbursement/orchestration layer is fragmented (many single-rail or regional players) and sits on top of rails it does not own; value accrues to whoever aggregates rails + compliance (KYC/AML/sanctions) into a single API. Barriers are regulatory (money-movement licensing, screening) and integration depth, not capital-intensity.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/payment volume, take rate per transaction, number of rails/integrations, and attach (payout added to existing acceptance). Basis of competition is product breadth (rail coverage + embedded compliance) and distribution into high-velocity verticals (gaming/gambling, digital assets, marketplaces) rather than price. Key players span incumbents moving into payouts (Visa Direct, Mastercard Send/Move as the underlying disbursement networks AptPay itself resells), acquirer-processors consolidating pay-in + pay-out (Nuvei, Paysafe, Repay), BaaS/rail enablers ([[Unit launches Real-Time Payments on its platform]], [[Corpay joins UK Faster Payments service]]), and cross-border payout specialists ([[dLocal and Western Union expand remittance payouts into Cameroon]]). Recent peer moves: Nuvei agreed to acquire Payoneer for ~$2.75bn to add cross-border disbursement to acceptance (2026); Repay acquired Kubra for $372M to deepen bill-pay/consumer disbursements (2026). Protagonist's position: KORT is a niche omnichannel acquirer buying its way beyond card acceptance into A2A/disbursements — the same pay-in + pay-out convergence the larger players are executing, one tier down. Moat is thin pre-deal (small scale); the acquired asset's moat is AptPay's single multi-rail integration + built-in compliance and its entrenchment in high-risk verticals where screening is a real switching cost `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal terms undisclosed, so no KORT/AptPay multiple can be computed — "no data" on price. AptPay (founded 2019, Toronto) appears to have raised no disclosed external funding, so no prior round valuation exists to anchor to (per Crunchbase/Tracxn, 2026) — `[UNSOURCED]` on any implied EV. External comps on the pay-in/pay-out convergence theme:
- Nuvei–Payoneer: ~$2.75bn ($7.40/share), combined ~$3bn revenue → EV/Revenue ≈ `$2.75bn / ~$3bn (combined) ≈ 0.9x` on the merged entity (per leaprate/thepaypers, 2026) — low, reflecting mature/listed cross-border disbursement at scale.
- Repay–Kubra: $372M cash for consumer bill-pay/disbursement (2026); revenue not disclosed → multiple "no data".
Distribution not computed (fewer than 3 comparable priced disbursement deals with revenue). Qualitative read: strategic pay-in+pay-out M&A in payments is clearing at modest revenue multiples (~1x for mature assets); an undisclosed-terms tuck-in like AptPay is most likely a small strategic/talent-and-tech deal, not a premium round — flag `(analysis)`, not a valuation signal.

**Risk flags.**
1. Rail dependence / disintermediation — AptPay does not own its rails; it resells Visa OCT, Mastercard Send/Move, Interac, RTP, FedNow. If the networks push direct access or compress reseller economics, the orchestration margin is captured upstream.
2. Vertical concentration in high-risk sectors — strong presence in online gaming/gambling and digital assets means revenue is correlated with regulatory/licensing swings in those verticals and heavier AML/sanctions liability; a crackdown in one geography hits the book.
3. Execution / integration risk — KORT is a small acquirer absorbing a multi-rail platform while simultaneously pushing US expansion; keeping AptPay as a standalone division (CEO retained) mitigates attrition but leaves the pay-in/pay-out cross-sell thesis unproven until integrated.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is small-cap consolidation of the orchestration layer, echoing Nuvei–Payoneer one tier down: acquirers that only do acceptance are buying payout/A2A to become "collect-and-send" single providers before Canada's Real-Time Rail goes live `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: if KORT converts its existing acceptance base onto AptPay payouts post-close (Q4 2026), attach/TPV should visibly rise; trigger to watch — whether terms/scale are ever disclosed and whether the networks (Visa/Mastercard) keep reseller access open. What breaks it: direct network access or rail owners undercutting orchestrators on price.
