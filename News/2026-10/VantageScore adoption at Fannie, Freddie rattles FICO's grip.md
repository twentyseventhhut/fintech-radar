## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What's genuinely new (Oct 2026).** The headline ("rattles FICO's grip") captures a late-Q3/early-Q4 2026 escalation that is materially *new* vs. the 2025 approval and the April 2026 acceptance announcement. Three fresh developments: (a) **Sept 9, 2026** — Fannie/Freddie opened VantageScore 4.0 to **all approved lenders**, dropping the prior-written-approval requirement; (b) **late Sept 2026** — FHFA Director Bill Pulte pushed the GSEs to a **single loan-level pricing (LLPA) grid** that treats Classic FICO and VantageScore 4.0 the same, **removing the previous 20-point downward adjustment** that had penalized VantageScore; (c) hard **adoption data** now exists — VantageScore was the sole score on ~9% of GSE-securitized mortgages since May 1 2026, ~3% of the ~$400B securitized in the six months through Sept was VantageScore-tied (up from ~0), and 37% of conventional applications in Fannie's Desktop Underwriter in September carried a VantageScore 4.0.

**[1] Why it matters.** This flips VantageScore from "technically permitted" to "economically rational to use." The unified grid erases the pricing disincentive, so lenders can now dual-pull and route borrowers to whichever score yields the better pricing bucket. Rocket Mortgage announced VantageScore 4.0 will become its **preferred** model for eligible mortgages in Q4 after a four-month pilot ("helped more clients qualify… reducing credit scoring costs"). Score-cost economics are lopsided: FICO raised its mortgage score price to ~$10 in 2026 (from <$1 in 2018); TransUnion is charging **$0.99** for VantageScore mortgage scores through Dec 2028; Equifax offers VantageScore 4.0 at ~$4.50 (>50% off FICO) through 2027.

**[2] Market reaction.** FICO (NYSE: FICO) fell ~17% in early Sept on the all-lender opening, then **~26-27% on Sept 29** (52-wk low ~$595) on the single-grid news; roughly -25% over 30 days (≈$932 → ≈$695 by early Oct) and down ~60% from peak. BofA cut its target to $700 and downgraded to Neutral. This is a repricing of FICO's long-prized mortgage moat, not just a headline.

**[3] Skeptic's counterweight.** Adoption is still single-digit percent of GSE volume; FICO remains the incumbent default with ~20 years of entrenchment and FICO 10T is slated for FHA underwriting in Jan 2027. A Milliman pilot analysis found borrowers were charged **up to 0.2pp higher rates** when loans used VantageScore vs. FICO, and VantageScore's thinner file requirement (~some months of history vs. FICO's longer bar) raises genuine risk/consistency questions that could slow institutional adoption. The "$1B savings" figure originates from VantageScore/FHFA advocacy and is not independently audited.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team / challenge questions**

1. Is this actually new, or a re-run of the April 2026 / July 2025 approval stories? (Verdict: new — the Sept 9 all-lender opening + the Sept-29 single pricing grid removing the 20-point VantageScore penalty are the fresh catalysts, not the original approval.)
2. The headline says "rattles FICO's grip" — but VantageScore was only ~3% of GSE securitization volume through Sept and ~9% sole-score since May. Is "grip rattled" hyperbole relative to actual share?
3. The "$1B first-year savings" claim comes from VantageScore/FHFA, not an independent auditor. Who benefits from pushing that number and is it verifiable?
4. Milliman found borrowers charged up to 0.2pp MORE under VantageScore in the pilot — does the "cheaper for consumers" narrative survive if the loans themselves price higher?
5. VantageScore needs a shorter credit history (~months) than Classic FICO — does loosening the thin-file bar import credit risk into GSE books, and who eats losses if VantageScore cohorts underperform in a downturn?
6. FICO 10T is slated for FHA in Jan 2027 — does the incumbent retain a strong counter-position, making the "monopoly over" framing premature?
7. How durable is the stock reaction? FICO already bounced (MoneyMorning "Wall Street loves this bounce", Oct 13) — is the ~26% drop an overreaction or correctly pricing permanent moat erosion?
8. Lender behavior: will lenders actually switch, or just dual-pull and arbitrage the better score? If dual-pull, FICO still gets paid on most originations — does revenue fall as much as the share narrative implies?
9. Pricing: FICO at ~$10 vs TransUnion $0.99 / Equifax $4.50 for VantageScore — but score cost is a tiny fraction of total mortgage cost. Is price really the swing factor, or is it regulatory push (Pulte) driving adoption?
10. Is the FHFA move politically contingent (Pulte / current administration)? Could a leadership change reverse or stall the single-grid policy, and is that tail risk priced?
11. Conflict check: is Bill Pulte's aggressive anti-FICO stance evidence-based or ideological? Does the policy rest on contested cost claims?
12. Data provenance: the ~37% Desktop Underwriter figure and ~3%/$400B figures — are they Fannie/FHFA self-reported, and do "application created with a VantageScore present" metrics overstate actual usage vs. loans closed on VantageScore?
13. Does ending the 20-point adjustment create a one-time gaming window where borrowers route to whichever score is momentarily higher, inflating early VantageScore volume unsustainably?
14. Counterparty risk for FICO's non-mortgage moat: mortgage is a minority of FICO's score volume (auto/card/B2B software are larger). Is the market overweighting the mortgage loss relative to total enterprise value?
15. Source WSJ is behind the aggregated "This Week in Fintech" one-liner — is the note's underlying claim fully supported by primary FHFA/GSE docs, not just secondary commentary?

**Importance: 4/5** — Genuine, well-documented escalation of a structural moat break (FHFA single grid + all-lender access + first real adoption data + ~26% one-day FICO drop) with clear fintech/credit-market relevance; capped below 5 because actual GSE share is still single-digit and the full displacement remains a multi-year, politically contingent thesis.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US consumer-credit scoring is a textbook duopoly: FICO (Fair Isaac, NYSE: FICO) and VantageScore (a JV of the three bureaus — Equifax, Experian, TransUnion). Mortgage scoring was effectively a FICO *monopoly* until the 2018 Credit Score Competition Act forced the GSEs to open the mandate; FHFA approved VantageScore 4.0 for Fannie/Freddie in July 2025, implementation went live April 2026, and FHFA is now moving Classic FICO and VantageScore 4.0 onto a *single* loan-level price-adjustment (LLPA) grid (per FHFA Dir. Bill Pulte). The economic prize: VantageScore claims adoption could cut lender/consumer costs by up to ~$1bn in year one (vantagescore.com PR — this is VantageScore's own number, treat as advocacy). Structure is highly consolidated with deep moats: both models sit on top of the same three bureaus, regulatory embedment (GSE/FHA acceptance) is the real barrier to entry, and switching costs for lenders/capital-markets plumbing are high. Why now: FICO raised the mortgage *wholesale royalty* from ~$4.95 to $10.00 per score for 2026 (via Quality Equities), drawing a Sen. Hawley/FTC pricing probe — the price hike is precisely what made a cheaper rival politically and economically attractive.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: price-per-score (royalty), scores pulled per funded loan (typically a tri-merge = 3 pulls), mortgage-origination volume share, and Scores-segment operating margin. Players: FICO (incumbent, ~monopoly rents in mortgage) vs VantageScore (challenger, bureau-backed, undercutting on price). Basis of competition is shifting from pure embedment toward *price + predictiveness* claims. Recent moves: VantageScore-scored GSE-backed originations jumped to ~$4bn in Aug 2026 from ~$8m in April (per Milliman) — real adoption, not just announced. United Wholesale Mortgage, the largest US lender, publicly reaffirmed FICO (adopting FICO 10T alongside Classic FICO) — a notable counter-signal that incumbency is sticky at the top of the funnel. FICO's position: still dominant and, paradoxically, financially *stronger* near-term — the $10 royalty drove Scores-segment revenue to $475m in Q2 FY26 (+60% y/y), mortgage originations revenue +127% y/y, at a ~91% segment operating margin. VantageScore's position: catching up fast on volume but off a tiny base; its moat is bureau ownership, not standalone distribution.

**Comps & multiples.** FICO (public): close $740.00 (12 Oct 2026), market cap ~$15.98bn, trailing P/E ~17.7x, P/S ~6.0x (stockanalysis.com / heygotrade). Sanity: P/E ~17.7x and P/S ~6.0x are *modest* for a ~91%-margin, +60% revenue-growth segment — the market is clearly pricing in erosion of the royalty, not current growth (stock -58% over trailing year). VantageScore: private JV, no public cap-table/valuation disclosed → market-cap/multiple = **no data**. Internal comps (same FICO/VantageScore mortgage-mandate thread): [[FICO shares plunge as US agencies accept alternative credit scores]] (Apr 2026, -13% on the day, ~-40% YTD), [[FICO stock falls as VantageScore approved for mortgages]] (Aug 2025, stock from >$2,400 Dec-2024 to <$1,400), [[This Week in Fintech VantageScore cleared to challenge FICO]] (Aug 2025), [[FHFA leaves FICO 10T out of mortgage overhaul for now]] (Jul 2025). EV/EBITDA not computed — clean EV/net-debt not sourced from a free filing → `[UNSOURCED]`. Qualitative read: FICO trades *cheap vs its own growth/margins* because the terminal royalty is in doubt; this is a disputed-moat discount, not a cheap operator.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Risk-pricing equivalence (systemic).** FHFA treating two different models as equivalent on one LLPA grid means a borrower can get the same price on a different risk read. TD Cowen's Jaret Seiberg: if the old grids measured risk correctly, the GSEs are taking *uncompensated* extra risk per VantageScore loan — a credit-quality/MBS-investor concern that could force a policy reversal.
2. **FICO royalty/price-power reversal (concentration).** FICO's recent earnings surge is *driven by* the very $10 price hike now under Hawley/FTC scrutiny; ~$1.13bn annualized at ~95% incremental margin (Quality Equities) sits on a regulated, politically exposed royalty. A capped or rolled-back price = direct revenue hit, which is why Wells Fargo cut its target to $950 from $1,350.
3. **Adoption durability (execution/second-order).** $4bn VantageScore volume looks large but is a sliver of GSE issuance and off an $8m base; UWM reaffirming FICO shows lenders may not switch the tri-merge default. Risk cuts both ways — VantageScore share could stall, undercutting the "FICO is losing mortgage" thesis.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a slow-motion de-rating of a regulated monopoly rent, not a clean disruption (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if VantageScore GSE-origination share keeps compounding (watch the monthly Milliman volume) *and* FHFA/Hawley cap the FICO royalty, FICO's terminal Scores economics re-rate down toward a regulated-utility multiple; conversely, if VantageScore volume plateaus and the single-grid risk-equivalence draws a reversal, FICO's current ~17.7x P/E is the cheap-moat entry it looks like. Trigger to watch: the next FHFA LLPA-grid ruling and any FTC action on per-score pricing.
