Reimagined app and website bring digital money movement, stablecoin -powered functionality and MoneyGram's global cash network together in one place 
 DALLAS , Oct. 19, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- MoneyGram today launched a new global consumer experience that brings together digital money movement, cash access and stablecoin -powered capabilities in one connected experience. Available through MoneyGram's updated app and website, the refreshed MoneyGram Online experience gives consumers more ways to send, receive, hold and use money across borders.

 The reimagined experience goes beyond sending money from one person to another. Consumers can receive and hold funds digitally, send money internationally, pay bills and top up mobile phones, and move between digital money and cash through MoneyGram's global retail network. In select markets, consumers can also maintain a stable, dollar-denominated balance, giving them more choice in how they hold and use their money.
Behind the experience is modern financial infrastructure, including stablecoin technology, but consumers do not need to understand or manage the technology themselves to benefit from it. MoneyGram handles the complexity of moving money in the most efficient way behind the scenes, giving consumers a simple, familiar way to move and manage money globally with greater speed and flexibility.
"Global money movement has become increasingly fragmented, forcing consumers to navigate different systems depending on how they want to pay, receive or use their money," said Anthony Soohoo, Chairman and CEO, MoneyGram. "We're changing that. MoneyGram is bringing digital money movement, cash access and stablecoin -powered services together in one connected experience. Consumers get more choice and greater control over how they send, receive, hold and use their money, wherever they are."
 One Global Experience, Built for Everyday Consumers 
MoneyGram's updated app and website are built around a straightforward promise: moving and managing money across borders should feel simple. Through the new experience, consumers can:
Send money globally to bank accounts, debit cards, digital wallets, retail locations and MoneyGram app users.*
Take advantage of MoneyGram's great exchange rates.
Pay bills and top up mobile phones.
Utilize MoneyGram balance to hold and use a stable, dollar-denominated balance in select markets.
Access cash through MoneyGram's global retail network.
Earn rewards by referring friends and family.
Use an experience tailored to their market and preferences, with support in 40 languages.
The experience is designed to bridge digital money and physical cash rather than treating them as separate ecosystems. MoneyGram's network is uniquely positioned to combine digital cross-border payments and local cash access to meet the varying preferences of the world's communities. Consumers can choose how they send, receive, hold or access their money based on what works for them.
MoneyGram is also increasingly connecting senders and recipients within the same digital network. Consumers in the United States can now send directly to MoneyGram app users in markets including Colombia and El Salvador. Recipients can receive and manage funds digitally while retaining the ability to access cash through MoneyGram's retail network where available.
This moves MoneyGram beyond the traditional remittance model of completing a single transaction. Senders and recipients can participate in the same network, creating more ways for people to manage money across borders after it has been received.
 Global Scale with the Speed of a Fintech 
The launch marks a definitive milestone in the transformation of MoneyGram's global payments network, combining digital product development with a physical network spanning more than 200 countries and territories.
MoneyGram has built a common global foundation across its app, website and MoneyGram balance. This allows the company to develop capabilities once and adapt them for local languages, payment methods and consumer needs, making it easier to introduce and scale new services across markets.
 Stablecoin infrastructure is part of that foundation, while the consumer experience remains designed for a mainstream audience. Consumers can access the utility of stablecoins and modern blockchain networks without needing to navigate the complexity typically associated with them.
Today, MoneyGram connects consumers to more than five billion digital endpoints, including wallets, bank accounts and cards, alongside access to nearly 500,000 retail locations worldwide. Together, that network enables consumers to move between different forms of money and different ways of accessing it, from digital value held in an app to cash collected locally.
"Our product strategy starts with the consumer, not the technology," said Luke Tuttle, Chief Product and Technology Officer, MoneyGram. "People shouldn't have to think about which product to use or how the technology works behind the scenes. They should simply be able to move, receive and use their money in the way that works for them. We've built one global platform that makes that experience simpler today and gives us the foundation to keep making it better."
The updated MoneyGram app and website are live across supported markets globally. Learn more: https://www.moneygram.com/us/en/send-and-receive/money-transfers-in-app 
*Available receive methods vary by corridor and appear during transaction flows online and in-app. More information is available at moneygram.com.

### [0] What happened
On 2026-10-19 MoneyGram launched "One Global Experience" — a reimagined app and website unifying digital money movement, cash access across its retail network, and stablecoin-powered capabilities into one connected product (the refreshed "MoneyGram Online"). Consumers can send/receive/hold/use money across borders: send to bank accounts, debit cards, digital wallets, retail locations and other app users; pay bills and top up mobile; and, in select markets, hold a stable USD-denominated "MoneyGram balance." Support spans 40 languages across a network of 500K retail locations in 200+ countries and 60M+ active customers. The hook: stablecoin rails power settlement behind the scenes, but the consumer never has to touch crypto. Live across supported markets globally as of launch day.

### [1] Why it matters
This is the consumer-facing capstone of MoneyGram's "refounding" from a legacy remittance teller into a network-led fintech. It converts the single-transaction remittance model into a persistent account relationship: senders and recipients can both sit inside the same digital network and manage money after receipt (first live corridors: US → app users in Colombia and El Salvador). Strategically it monetizes MoneyGram's 2026 stablecoin stack (MGUSD + USDC on Stellar) as invisible plumbing rather than a crypto product — the differentiated bet vs. pure-digital rivals is the hybrid digital+cash bridge over its physical network.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
### Red-team questions

1. "Live across supported markets globally" — how many markets actually have the full experience vs. just a UI refresh? The differentiated features (balance, app-to-app) are named only in Colombia and El Salvador. Is this a global launch or two corridors plus a reskin?
2. The press release is MoneyGram's own (PRNewswire). As a private company with no public P&L, every growth stat (>70% digital, ~$2B stablecoin run-rate, 60M customers) is self-reported and unauditable — how much is marketing?
3. Does "hold a stable USD-denominated balance" create a stored-value / e-money regulatory obligation (licensing, custody, reserves) in each market? Which markets permit a US-pegged balance for consumers?
4. Is the "MoneyGram balance" USDC, MGUSD, or both? Consumer disclosure of which stablecoin backs their funds — and redemption/reserve risk — is unclear.
5. Hiding the crypto ("consumers don't need to understand the technology") — is this consumer-friendly abstraction or an opacity that masks FX spread and stablecoin de-peg/custody risk?
6. Competitive moat: Wise is cheaper and Remitly is faster on mobile-wallet payouts. MoneyGram's edge is cash, but digital-first growth means competing where it is weakest on price/speed. Can it win mainstream digital users?
7. Post-take-private, Madison Dearborn needs an exit. Is this launch substantively about product, or about dressing up a "fintech" narrative ahead of a return-to-public-markets / sale?
8. App-to-app network effects require both sender and recipient on MoneyGram. Against WhatsApp-scale and incumbent wallets in LatAm, what is the realistic attach rate?
9. Cannibalization: shifting consumers from high-margin cash-pickup fees to a thin-margin digital balance — does unit economics improve or erode?
10. Execution risk: this is at least the second "reinvented app" in ~13 months (2025-09 next-gen app). Is this a relaunch of something that underperformed?
11. Stablecoin regulatory exposure (US GENIUS-era rules, EU MiCA) — does a consumer-held MGUSD/USDC balance pull MoneyGram into stablecoin-issuer/distributor obligations it is not framing?
12. "5 billion digital endpoints / nearly 500K retail" — endpoint reach is not usage. What share of 60M customers actually transact digitally vs. still walk into an agent?
13. FX transparency: Wise sells the mid-market rate; MoneyGram touts "great exchange rates." Is the spread competitive or is the stablecoin rail just internal cost savings not passed to consumers?
14. Dependency concentration: the balance rides Stellar + Bridge (issuer). Chain outage, issuer failure, or regulatory action against USDC/MGUSD would directly hit live consumer balances.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** MoneyGram sits in cross-border consumer remittances / digital-wallet money movement. The global digital-remittance segment was ~$28.9bn in 2025, rising to ~$31.7–33.4bn in 2026 at a ~15–19% CAGR (per Research and Markets / Fortune Business Insights / Grand View, via secondary analyst citations, as of 2026) — well above the low-single-digit growth of the broader cash-heavy remittance channel, so the value is migrating digital-first. Structure: fragmented at the top (Western Union, MoneyGram, Wise, Remitly, Zepz, plus hundreds of corridor-specialist MTOs) with high entry barriers from money-transmitter licensing in each jurisdiction and compliance/AML scale. Why now: two secular drivers converge — (1) cash-to-digital conversion of legacy remittance customers (MoneyGram itself reports >70% of transactions now run through digital channels, per paymentsdive, 2026), and (2) stablecoin rails compressing the cost/speed of the cross-border leg. Second-order effect: once the back-end is stablecoin and the front-end is a held balance, the product stops being a one-shot "send" and becomes a store-of-value + spend relationship — the same shift Zepz (Sendwave Wallet), Remitly (stablecoin wallet) and Western Union (USDPT on Solana) are all chasing.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: send/transfer volume (TPV), take rate (revenue / volume), active customers, and digital mix %. Key players and basis of competition: Wise competes on transparent FX + price and increasingly multi-currency banking; Remitly on corridor UX and mobile-first remittance; Western Union and MoneyGram on the hybrid cash-plus-digital network and physical reach. Recent peer moves: Remitly launched a stablecoin-powered cross-border wallet (Aug 2025) and keeps raising 2026 guidance; Western Union is building USDPT stablecoin on Solana (2026); Zepz launched the stablecoin-powered Sendwave Wallet (Oct 2025); Wise created a dedicated stablecoin product-leadership role (2025). MoneyGram's position: catching up on the pure-digital frontier but with a genuinely differentiated moat on the cash↔digital bridge — nearly 500,000 retail locations across 200+ countries and 60M+ active customers (company figures), reaching ~5bn digital endpoints. The moat is network/scale on cash-out, a hard-to-replicate intangible for digital-only rivals; the vulnerability is that cash is the shrinking side. CAC payback, LTV/CAC and MoneyGram's current revenue are not disclosed (private since 2023) → [UNSOURCED].

**Comps & multiples.** Peer trading comps (all private-vs-public, so this is a sector sanity check, not a valuation of MoneyGram, which is private):
- Wise plc: mkt cap ~£8.2bn (~$11bn); FY26 net revenue ~$2.5bn (ended Mar 2026) → P/S ≈ $11bn / $2.5bn ≈ 4.4x; profitable (~26% pre-tax margin). (via investegate / investingintheweb, 2026)
- Remitly (RELY): mkt cap ~$5.22bn (mid-Oct 2026); TTM revenue ~$1.81bn (to Jun 30 2026) → P/S ≈ $5.22bn / $1.81bn ≈ 2.9x; FY26 revenue guided $1.96–1.98bn, +20–21% YoY. (via Yahoo Finance / Remitly IR, 2026)
- MoneyGram: private; Madison Dearborn take-private valued at ~$1.8bn (Jun 2023); no current public revenue → EV/Rev, P/E = no data / [UNSOURCED].
Read: Wise trades richer than Remitly (4.4x vs 2.9x P/S), consistent with Wise's higher margin and ~$243bn FY26 cross-border volume vs Remitly's ~$23.5bn Q2 send volume — the multiple spread is explained by profitability + scale, so neither looks like an unjustified outlier within the ~0.5–20x EV/Rev sanity band. Internal comps from the corpus: [[Remitly launches stablecoin-powered cross-border wallet]], [[Zepz launches stablecoin-powered Sendwave Wallet for digital dollars]], [[Western Union plans USDPT stablecoin on Solana]], [[Remitly reports Q4 and full year 2025 results above outlook]].

**Risk flags.**
1. Disintermediation / stablecoin commoditization — if stablecoins make the cross-border transfer near-free and instant, the value could migrate to the front-end wallet and the retail cash network (MoneyGram's moat) becomes the shrinking leg; MoneyGram must win the held-balance relationship, not just the send.
2. Private-owner leverage & opacity — take-private at ~$1.8bn (2023) means an LBO capital structure and no public financials; execution on the "fintech-speed" transformation is unverifiable from outside, and a single-transaction cyberattack (2023) showed concentration/operational fragility (analysis).
3. Regulatory surface on the stablecoin/held-balance pivot — holding a "stable, dollar-denominated balance" in consumer apps across 200+ jurisdictions raises money-transmitter, stablecoin-issuance and consumer-protection exposure that pure remittance never carried; a corridor-by-corridor compliance drag on the "build once, adapt locally" thesis (analysis).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** The launch reframes MoneyGram from a transaction business to a multi-product money-management relationship — a bid to re-rate the network's hybrid cash+digital reach into recurring, higher-LTV wallet engagement rather than one-off sends (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if MoneyGram can convert senders AND recipients into same-network held-balance users (it now routes US→Colombia/El Salvador app-to-app), digital mix and revenue-per-customer should climb; watch for a disclosed held-balance/active-wallet metric or a refinancing/IPO-prep signal from Madison Dearborn as the trigger. What breaks it: if adoption stays sender-only and recipients keep cashing out immediately, it's a cosmetic app refresh, not a model shift.
