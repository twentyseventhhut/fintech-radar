# Mogul raises $15.5M Series A led by Draper Associates

The Draper Associates-led round included the largest participation from the founders of BlueChew, Stephen Sullivan & Dr. Alex Jovanovich, fueling Mogul's plans to scale its AI capabilities, while expanding its team and property portfolio 
 WASHINGTON , Oct. 13, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- M ogul , the AI-powered real estate investing platform, today announced it closed a $15.5 million Series A round led by Draper Associates, 55% above its original $10 million target. To date, Mogul's total funding exceeds $23.6 million. The Series A values the company at $126 million.
Already cash-flow positive, Mogul had no immediate plans to raise capital, but strong inbound interest from leading venture capitalists prompted the company to pursue the round. Mogul will use the new capital to scale its AI infrastructure and organization, expand its property portfolio and create new revenue streams. A significant portion of the funding will go toward hiring across engineering, data science and supply roles at the company.
While Mogul's investment team continues to lead due diligence processes, the company has leveraged AI to scale the supply side of its business, powering supply operations, targeting, performance analysis and ongoing asset monitoring. This has enabled Mogul to scale to more than 150 assets with just four supply-focused team members. Across its supply workflow, Mogul has reduced manual effort by 89% and cut the time required per property from 18 hours to just two.
The technology-driven approach reflects the company's founding mission to rethink an industry still dominated by manual processes. After seeing firsthand how antiquated real estate investing remained, former Goldman Sachs Real Estate Investment Group investors Alex Blackwood  and Joey Gumataotao  left Wall Street to rebuild the model from the ground up.
Founded in 2022, Mogul combines institutional-grade underwriting, technology-enabled operations and AI to give everyday investors access to carefully vetted residential real estate opportunities that have historically been reserved for institutions. Through the platform, users can invest in individual properties and receive monthly distributions based on their ownership.
Since launch, Mogul has achieved significant scale and investor traction, including:
$110M+ in real estate investments facilitated
150+ properties acquired nationwide
50,000+ investor signups
80% repeat investment rate
12-20% target IRR
The raise comes amid a period of significant growth for Mogul. The company reported 14.5x 2024- 2025 revenue growth, 5.6x year-over-year revenue growth, and 4x year-over-year customer growth, all delivered by a team of just 11 people. Mogul is now on pace to reach a revenue run rate of $9 million by year's end and plans to scale to $60 million within 18 months of this raise. The company also recently acquired the Mogul.com domain for $1.3 million, reflecting its ambitions to build a defining platform for real estate investing.
"The Series A raise validates our thesis that real estate is ready for an AI-powered overhaul. The industry is ripe for innovation and presents a trillion-dollar market cap opportunity," said Alex Blackwood, CEO and Co-Founder of Mogul. "The platform started as a way for Joey and me to invest in high-quality real estate without the headache, and I am proud to say I am still a power user of the platform today. We are typically the first and second checks into each property; however, with a majority of assets over the last three months selling out in under two hours, I have missed out on some of them because I didn't act fast enough. As a platform, we are already one of one, and the capital provides us with the capability to redefine the broader real estate industry. Looking ahead, our vision is to build a vertically integrated real estate ecosystem that supports the full lifecycle of an asset. By unifying these layers, Mogul can become the defining infrastructure for the industry. In 10 years' time, Mogul will be the first word that comes to your mind when you think of real estate." 
"Real estate has long been one of America's greatest wealth-building assets, but access to these investment strategies has largely been reserved to institutions, " said Joey Gumataotao, Co-Founder of Mogul. "Having worked on building Goldman Sachs' single-family rental platform from zero to $1 billion in under a year, I've seen firsthand what this asset class can deliver when the right technology and systems are applied. The Series A supercharges our supply infrastructure, allowing us to continue improving as we scale. Our thesis, process, and vetting have only strengthened as we've grown. This capital will help us expand our platform and portfolio while maintaining the precision and discipline our investors expect."
Key investors in the round include Tim Draper, founder of Draper Associates and a prominent Silicon Valley venture capitalist. An early seed investor and an active backer of Mogul, Draper has a track record of investing in companies such as Tesla, SpaceX, Coinbase , and Robinhood in their earliest stages. Stephen Sullivan, Co-Founder and CEO of BlueChew wrote the round's largest check and brings firsthand experience building and scaling a direct-to-consumer business. Other investors include Draper Dragon, Avalanche Blizzard Fund, Jason Calacanis' LAUNCH, Venture Togethers, and Gaingels, among other strategic angel investors across top tech and insurtech firms.
"We invested in Mogul at the pre-seed stage because Alex and Joey had elite institutional pedigree and a bold vision for a fundamentally different model for real estate investing," said Tim Draper, Founder of Draper Associates. "This past May, Alex came to our summit as one of our top-performing portfolio companies to share a business update. Seeing the continued strength of the business and the scale of what the team was building, our conviction only grew and by the end of the event, I knew we had to lead the Series A. Mogul is a true 'rhino,' charging ahead with investor retention, relentless growth and real execution. The company has the potential to become the defining model for the next era of real estate investing."
"Mogul is our biggest outside start-up investment so far because it seems clear that Alex and Joey have a truly disruptive business model that can become the #1 brand in the category very quickly. After a few months of chatting with Alex, it felt like a no-brainer," said Stephen Sullivan, Co-Founder and CEO of BlueChew/dgp.com. "When I first met Alex and Joey, I knew their big vision for the future of real estate investing was exciting, but when they showed me early traction with paid marketing on Google Search, I knew it was only a matter of time before this business hit hyper growth from word of mouth. Super excited for these guys!"
 About Mogul Mogul is an AI-driven real estate investing platform built by real estate investors, for real estate investors. Founded in 2022 by former Goldman Sachs Real Estate Investment Group alums Alex Blackwood and Joey Gumataotao, Mogul enables individuals to invest in individual properties and build diversified portfolios of single-family rentals, making residential real estate more accessible to a broader range of investors. By combining institutional-grade underwriting with AI-powered supply infrastructure, asset management, and investor operations, Mogul delivers an efficient and transparent investment experience. Headquartered in Washington, D.C., with offices in New York City, Mogul is building the ecosystem to power the future of real estate investing. Learn more and start building a portfolio at Mogul.com .

[0] **TL;DR** — Mogul, an AI- and blockchain-powered fractional real-estate investing platform (Washington, D.C. / NYC), closed an oversubscribed **$15.5M Series A led by Draper Associates** on 2026-10-13, **55% above** its original $10M target, at a **$126M valuation**. Total funding to date **>$23.6M**. The company is already cash-flow positive and raised opportunistically on strong inbound VC interest.

[1] **What Mogul does** — Founded 2022 by ex-Goldman Sachs Real Estate Investment Group investors **Alex Blackwood** (CEO) and **Joey Gumataotao**. Users buy fractional ownership in individual single-family rental homes and receive monthly distributions. Each property is **tokenized on the Avalanche blockchain** — ownership is a proprietary on-chain property NFT representing a fractional membership interest in the LLC that owns the real estate; investors fund via USDT or wire. Target IRR 12–20%. Open to non-accredited investors, not just accredited.

[2] **The round / investors** — Led by **Draper Associates** (Tim Draper, a pre-seed backer; track record: Tesla, SpaceX, Coinbase, Robinhood). Largest check came from **BlueChew founders Stephen Sullivan & Dr. Alex Jovanovich** (Sullivan called it BlueChew's biggest outside startup investment). Other participants: **Draper Dragon, Avalanche/Ava Labs' Blizzard Fund, Jason Calacanis' LAUNCH, Venture Togethers, Gaingels**, plus angels across tech/insurtech. Use of funds: scale AI supply infrastructure, hire across engineering/data-science/supply, expand property portfolio, new revenue streams.

[3] **Traction & the AI story** — **$110M+** real estate facilitated, **150+** properties nationwide, **50,000+** investor signups, **80% repeat-investment rate**. Revenue: 14.5x growth 2024→2025, 5.6x YoY, 4x YoY customer growth — all from an **11-person team**. On pace for **$9M revenue run-rate** by year-end, targeting **$60M within 18 months**. AI cut manual supply effort **89%** and per-property work from **18h → 2h**, letting 4 supply staff manage 150+ assets. Also bought the **Mogul.com** domain for **$1.3M**. Properties "selling out in under two hours."

[4] **Market, competitors & internal links** — Fractional/retail real-estate investing is a crowded space: **Fundrise** (>$3B AUM, pooled eREIT model), **Arrived Homes** (Bezos/Benioff-backed, ~$418–429M invested, 565+ properties, ~971K investors, $100 min, Q1-2026 LTR yields ~3.6%), plus **Roofstock, Lofty, Ark7**. Mogul's differentiators: on-chain tokenization (Avalanche), AI-driven supply underwriting, individual-property (not pooled-fund) model, and cash-flow positivity. Note yields comparison: Mogul advertises 12–20% *target IRR* vs. Arrived's realized ~3.6% dividend yield — IRR (incl. appreciation) is not comparable to dividend yield, treat the headline number skeptically. Tim Draper is a recurring presence in the DB: see [[Ryder raises $3.2 million seed from Tim Draper]]. ## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. The **12–20% "target IRR"** is a forward-looking target, not realized returns. What have actual historical distributions/IRRs been? Competitors like Arrived report realized dividend yields of ~3.6% — is Mogul's headline number apples-to-oranges marketing?
2. **$126M valuation on ~$9M run-rate** (≈14x forward revenue) for a company that was "not planning to raise" — is this a genuine mark or an inbound-driven story? Was the valuation set by Draper (an existing seed investor) rather than a fresh external lead, risking an inside round?
3. "**$110M+ real estate facilitated**" — is this cumulative GMV or AUM? How much is live vs. already exited? Revenue is only ~$9M run-rate against $110M facilitated — what's the actual take rate / fee model?
4. **Tokenization on Avalanche**: how liquid is the secondary market really? "Liquid fractional ownership" is claimed, but is there an active resale venue, or are investors locked in? NFT-as-LLC-membership-interest raises securities-law questions.
5. **Securities/regulatory exposure**: opening single-property real-estate deals to non-accredited investors via on-chain NFTs — under what exemption (Reg A+, Reg CF, Reg D)? Any SEC scrutiny of the tokenized-RE model?
6. **Only 4 supply staff for 150+ assets via AI** — does "89% manual effort reduction" compromise underwriting diligence quality? Who bears the risk if AI-sourced properties underperform?
7. **80% repeat-investment rate** and "sold out in under two hours" — are these driven by genuine demand or by artificially scarce/small property offerings? How large is the average deal?
8. **Concentration of the round**: largest check from BlueChew (an ED-pills DTC brand) founders, not an institutional RE/fintech investor. Does the cap table lack strategic real-estate or regulatory expertise?
9. **Macro risk**: single-family rental economics depend on rates, home prices, and rental yields. How exposed is Mogul if US housing softens or rates stay high? Model untested through a full down-cycle (founded 2022).
10. **Avalanche dependency**: ownership records on Avalanche + Blizzard Fund as investor = ecosystem lock-in. What happens to investor records if the chain or Ava Labs relationship changes?
11. **$1.3M for the Mogul.com domain** while raising $15.5M — reasonable brand investment or questionable capital allocation for a cash-flow-positive startup?
12. **Path to $60M revenue in 18 months** (≈6.5x from $9M run-rate) — what concrete drivers support this, beyond "AI scale"? Supply constraints ("I missed deals") suggest sourcing is the bottleneck, not demand.
13. **Crowded, well-funded competition**: Fundrise (>$3B AUM), Arrived (Bezos/Benioff). Is tokenization a real moat or a marketing wrapper over the same fractional-RE model?

**Importance: 3/5** — A mid-size ($15.5M) US Series A in a well-trodden category (fractional/tokenized real-estate investing), but notable for the Tim Draper lead, the explicit AI-for-underwriting narrative, Avalanche tokenization, and credible ex-Goldman founders with real traction and cash-flow positivity. Thematically relevant to tokenized-RWA and retail-investing-access trends, but not category-defining or systemically important; the lofty valuation and target-IRR marketing warrant skepticism.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Fractional/retail real-estate investing platforms — a niche inside residential proptech where retail investors buy shares in single-family rentals (SFR) for monthly distributions. Market size is thinly sourced: one secondary report pegs the "fractional real-estate platform" market at ~$4.2bn (2025), ~$4.8bn (2026e), to ~$14.8bn by 2034 (per Dataintelo, via WebSearch, as of 2026) — treat as directional, not audited. Structure: fragmented, with a few scaled incumbents (Fundrise ~$3.4bn AUM, ~402k active accounts, Mar-2026; Arrived ~$300M+ invested, 550+ properties, 885k+ registered investors) and a long tail of smaller/tokenized entrants. Entry barriers are modest on tech but real on (a) securities regulation (Reg A/Reg D offerings, SEC qualification) and (b) supply sourcing/underwriting at scale. **Why now:** US 30-yr mortgage spiked to ~7.4% (8-Oct-2026, 3-yr high) and affordability is stretched (median household needs ~42% of income to buy, Dec-2025), keeping would-be buyers renting → SFR renter-demand index +21.6% YoY (Sep-2026, per Zillow via Arbor). The structural twist: the 2026 ROAD to Housing Act bars for-profit entities controlling 350+ SFR homes from buying more, and the largest institutional landlords turned net sellers (~3,180 more homes sold than bought YTD-2026) — vacating acquisition lanes that smaller/retail-pooled buyers like Mogul can fill (analysis).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: AUM / total invested, registered-investor count, repeat-investment rate, target IRR, revenue run-rate. Key players & basis of competition: **Fundrise** (scale, multi-asset eREITs/credit/VC — breadth), **Arrived Homes** (Bezos-backed, SFR fractional, closest model-match), **Lofty/Ark7** (tokenized/liquid secondary — distribution). Competition is on supply quality + fee/return track record + brand, not price. Recent peer moves: Arrived closed a ~$27M round Nov-2025 (total funding ~$61.7bn→$61.7M, Bezos Expeditions among backers); Fundrise drew a $100M credit line Feb-2026 amid soft 2025 returns (6.24%). Mogul's position: **sub-scale challenger** on absolute size ($110M+ facilitated, 150+ properties, 50k+ signups) vs Fundrise/Arrived, but ahead on claimed efficiency (AI cut per-property effort 18h→2h, -89%; 11-person team) and growth (5.6x YoY revenue, 4x YoY customers). Moat is thin today — claimed edge is operational (AI supply ops) + a strong 80% repeat-investment rate (switching cost / trust); no network-effect or regulatory moat yet `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Mogul: $126M Series A valuation on a ~$9M year-end revenue run-rate → **$126M / $9M ≈ 14x** run-rate revenue. Note the base is a projected EoY run-rate, not trailing realized revenue, so the "real" trailing multiple is materially higher — rich, but partly defensible on 5.6x YoY growth (growth-adjusted, high-growth SaaS/platforms routinely clear 10-15x). External peer: **Arrived Homes** ~$137M valuation (Apr-2024) on $300M+ cumulative invested — different denominator (platform GMV, not revenue), so no clean cross-multiple; shown as a scale/valuation anchor (a far-larger-GMV peer at a comparable headline valuation ⇒ Mogul's $126M is full for its size). Fundrise: no disclosed recent equity valuation ("no data"). Internal comps (tokenized/fractional retail real estate, non-US, early-stage): [[SmartCrowd's Nawy Shares wins VARA approval for tokenized real estate]], [[India real-estate tokenisation startup Alt DRX raises ₹6cr]], [[Saudi proptech Jozo raises $2.21 million seed]] — adjacent model, but seed-stage/overseas, not valuation-comparable. **Flag: in-line-to-rich** — the 14x run-rate multiple is only justified if the 5.6x growth and $60M run-rate target (18 months) land.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Model concentration in SFR at a cyclical top (housing/rate).** Revenue and distributions ride single-family rental economics; rates at a 3-yr high (7.4%) and cooling home-price appreciation compress acquisition spreads and exit values → distribution/IRR risk that is invisible in a growth-stage revenue chart (second-order: a rent/price drawdown hits investor returns → repeat-rate and signups, the growth engine).
2. **Valuation leans on a projected run-rate, not realized revenue.** The 14x is computed on a year-end run-rate and a 6.5x-further $60M/18mo target; any slip in supply velocity ("sold out in under two hours" implies supply-constrained, not demand-constrained) re-rates the round downward.
3. **Securities/disclosure & liability silence.** The release is loud on growth metrics but silent on fee structure, offering economics, redemption/liquidity terms, and SEC offering status — the exact items that determine retail-investor downside and regulatory exposure (de-PR'd gap).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The institutional-landlord retreat (ROAD Act + net selling) opens a structural lane for retail-pooled SFR buyers — Mogul is positioning as the AI-native entrant into a space where incumbents (Fundrise/Arrived) are larger but older-architecture. Falsifiable thesis: if Mogul hits the ~$60M run-rate inside 18 months while holding the 80% repeat rate, it validates the AI-supply-ops moat and the $126M mark; **trigger to watch** — next-raise valuation step-up (or a down-round/flat extension), property-level IRR realizations vs the 12-20% target, and whether supply (not demand) remains the binding constraint. Thesis breaks if rate-driven rent/price softness drops realized IRRs below target and repeat investment rolls over.
