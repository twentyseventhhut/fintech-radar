# Context-enrichment: Revolut plans digital bank & e-money licences in Philippines
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On **2 Oct 2026** Philippine DTI Secretary Cristina Roque met Revolut's Global Head of Government Affairs **Adam Gagen** at Revolut's London HQ (reported 6 Oct 2026 by fintechnews.ph / BusinessWorld; the Finextra headline frames it as "eyes Philippines bank licence"). Revolut said it is **evaluating** pursuing both a **digital bank licence AND an e-money issuer (EMI) licence** from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), via a wholly owned local entity.

De-PR'd: this is **intention at the evaluation stage, NOT a filed application** — Revolut is "reviewing the regulatory and corporate structures it would need before applying." No customer target, no timeline, no capital commitment disclosed. The note's own phrasing ("plans to apply") already overstates a diplomatic-meeting readout.

**+ Why this framing and what it reveals:** The announcement is a government-affairs meeting, not a regulatory event — the counterpart was the *trade* secretary (DTI), not the central bank (BSP) that actually issues the licences. That ordering is the tell: Revolut is lining up political goodwill *before* a regulatory door is even open. Critically, **the BSP digital-bank window is currently CLOSED**: BSP lifted its 3-year moratorium on 1 Jan 2025 and raised the cap from 6 to 10 banks (4 new slots), but only **three** applicants filed before the **Nov/Dec 2025** deadline and the Monetary Board then imposed a **fresh moratorium**. Revolut was not among the three. So a near-term digital-bank *filing* is not actually possible — the realistic short-term path is the uncapped **EMI (wallet)** licence, not a full bank charter. The PR bundles both to sound bigger than the achievable step.

## [1] Competitors / peers
The Philippine market is already crowded and entrenched:
- **GCash (Mynt)** — ~94m registered users; dominant wallet.
- **Maya (Voyager)** — 50m+ users; holds both a wallet and one of the 6 digital-bank licences (Maya Bank).
- **Tonik** — first PH neobank with a digital-bank licence (2021).
- Other incumbents: UNO Digital, UnionDigital, GoTyme, Overseas Filifino Bank — the 6 existing digital banks.
GCash + Maya together hold ~95%+ of PH mobile wallets. PH digital-wallet market ~$4.1bn (2025).

**+ Why the lay of the land matters:** Revolut would arrive 5+ years after the incumbents, into a wallet duopoly with enormous installed bases, and *without* the one asset that let Maya/Tonik scale — a digital-bank licence it currently cannot apply for. An EMI-only entry puts Revolut head-to-head with GCash/Maya on their core turf (wallet + remittances) with no structural edge. This is the opposite of Revolut's winning playbook elsewhere (early mover, regulatory arbitrage) — here it is the late follower.

## [2] Company history / fit
Revolut's 2024–2026 expansion is a licence-accumulation machine driving toward its **100m-user** target (~80m as of 2026): full **UK** bank licence (restricted Jul 2024 → restrictions lifted ~Mar 2026, see [[Revolut secures full UK banking licence]]); **Mexico** full banking live Jan 2026 (see [[Revolut launches full banking operations in Mexico]]); **Colombia** operating licence Sep 2026; **UAE** payment licences Jun 2026; **India** payments/UPI live beta 2025–26 ([[India launch central to Revolut's 100 million user target]]); plus Brazil, Argentina, South Africa, Peru, Switzerland, Turkey, Israel bids. In the Philippines specifically it opened a **Manila tech hub** in Jul–Aug 2025 ([[Revolut launches second Asian tech hub in Manila]]) and in Apr 2026 was reported in talks to **acquire a major Asian bank** ([[Revolut in talks to acquire a major Asian bank]]).

**+ Why Revolut acts this way:** It needs headcount of licensed markets to justify a ~$115bn valuation (Jul 2026 secondary) on a user-growth narrative. The Manila hub gives it local staff and a political relationship; the PH licence talk is the logical next PR beat on that base. But the Philippines fits the *geographic-coverage* logic more than a *unit-economics* logic — it is a flag-plant, not obviously a profit centre.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: low.** Nothing has launched; nothing has even been filed. This is a statement of intent out of a bilateral meeting. Prior art: the Manila hub (2025) already established PH presence; the "evaluating a PH licence" idea is a continuation, not a new capability. There is **zero traction** to point to — no PH users, no product, no filing, no slot.

**+ Why the value-add is unconfirmed:** The binding constraint is regulatory supply (the slot moratorium), which Revolut does not control. Even if it wins an EMI licence, the margin in PH payments is captured by the entrenched wallet networks and their merchant/biller rails — Revolut would be a late entrant buying distribution, not capturing the stack. The real question is not "can Revolut enter the Philippines" but "is there any durable economic edge in being the 3rd wallet behind GCash and Maya in a market where it cannot (yet) take deposits" — and on current facts the answer is no.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Short term: possible EMI application; digital-bank filing only if/when BSP reopens the window (uncertain, possibly post-2026). Revolut will likely keep using the Manila hub + government relationship to position for the next slot round. Regulatory backdrop: BSP has been deliberately restrictive on digital-bank supply, which both protects incumbents and slows Revolut.

**+ Second-order:** The item's real signal is *strategic breadth*, not *Philippine substance* — it is one more pin on Revolut's global map in service of the 100m/IPO narrative. Counterintuitively, the proliferation of "evaluating/intends to" licence headlines dilutes their individual news value: each new "Revolut eyes country X" is worth less because the market now expects it everywhere. This is a low-weight, directional item.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** The Philippines digital-banking market was ~$540.6m in 2024, growing ~14.1%/yr through 2030 (per Nexdigm/secondary market reports, via web search, as of 2026); the broader digital-payments market reached ~$616.3m in 2025 (per IMARC Group, CAGR ~13.1% to 2034). Structure is a tiered, licence-gated oligopoly: the BSP lifted its digital-bank moratorium on 1 Jan 2025 but capped the market at 10 digital banks, i.e. only 4 new/converting licences on offer — a hard regulatory entry barrier, and a scarce slot Revolut would be competing for (per BSP/fintechnews.ph, as of 2025). "Why now": the moratorium lift + Revolut's July-2025 Manila Global Technology Hub + a stated no-local-bank-partner strategy mean a de-novo licence is the only path in. Note the de-PR point: as of 2 Oct 2026 (DTI–Revolut London meeting) this is at the evaluation stage — Revolut is still reviewing the regulatory/corporate structure needed before even applying; no application filed, no BSP approval, no launch date (per DTI/BusinessWorld, as of 2026-10-06).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for digital banks: deposit base, loan book / net interest income, active users, path-to-profit. Incumbents already hold the slots and scale: GCash (operator Mynt) has ~90m registered / ~81m active users and is the dominant e-wallet; Maya Bank leads digital banks by assets (>PHP 49.9bn as of Mar-2025), with ~5.4m users; Tonik became the first profitable standalone digital bank (May-2026, lending-led); GoTyme (>7m users, Aug-2025) took $13.9m fresh capital (Jul-2026) while still loss-narrowing; SeaBank, UNObank and OFBank round out the six incumbents. Basis of competition = distribution (telco/e-commerce ecosystems: Globe/Ant behind GCash, PLDT behind Maya, Sea behind SeaBank) and deposit-rate + lending reach into the underbanked. Revolut's position: new entrant, catching up from zero locally, no licence yet. Moat is global product breadth + brand + the Manila hub, but it is explicitly distribution-disadvantaged vs telco/e-commerce-backed incumbents and must win a scarce BSP slot `(analysis)`. Prior Revolut PH steps: [[Revolut opens early access waitlist in the Philippines]] (Jun-2026).

**Comps & multiples.** Private/pre-IPO peers; no public PH digital-bank equity, so trading multiples mostly "no data":
- GCash/Mynt — targeting >$8bn IPO valuation, raising ~$1bn (per Reuters/BusinessWorld, May-2026): [[GCash operator Mynt eyes $8 billion valuation in Philippine IPO]], [[Ant-backed GCash operator Mynt prepares Philippine IPO filing]]. Revenue not disclosed in-base → EV/Rev "no data".
- Maya Bank — reportedly may be valued >$2bn (per web, 2025); no public revenue → multiple "no data".
- Tonik — raised $12m pre-Series C (Dec-2025), $280m total raised context; profitable but private, valuation undisclosed → "no data".
- GoTyme — $13.9m capital injection (Jul-2026): [[GoTyme receives fresh $13.9 million capital injection]]; still loss-making.
Protagonist Revolut (group, not PH-specific): FY2025 revenue $6.0bn (+46%), PBT $2.3bn (+57%, 38% margin), 68.3m retail customers, $67.5bn balances (per Revolut FY2025 results). Distribution not computed — fewer than 3 comparable public revenue figures; qualitative only. Revolut is the largest and only profitable-at-scale name here, but its PH entity would start sub-scale; group multiples are not meaningful for the PH slot.

**Risk flags.**
1. Regulatory gating/execution: only ~4 BSP slots and applications have been "under review" with no firm timeline (as of Jul-2026); Revolut hasn't even applied and has ruled out a local-bank partner — a slow/failed licence is the central risk. Second-order: a prolonged evaluation lets incumbents deepen deposit moats before Revolut can transact.
2. Distribution disadvantage: incumbents ride telco/e-commerce ecosystems (Globe, PLDT, Sea, Ant) for cheap user acquisition; Revolut must buy growth. Second-order: higher CAC [UNSOURCED] pressures unit economics in a thin-margin, low-ARPU market.
3. Thin-margin / profitability race: most PH digital banks are still loss-making (only Tonik profitable), so even with a licence, path-to-profit is multi-year and capital-intensive. Second-order: group-level profits don't de-risk a loss-leading PH subsidiary.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Signals the SE-Asia neobank land-grab reaching the Philippines' scarce-slot phase — a potential new-entry story, not a re-rating `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: Revolut converts talk into a filed BSP application within ~12 months; trigger = a formal licence application or BSP shortlist. What breaks it: the "evaluation stage" stalls (no filing through 2027) or BSP awards the remaining slots to other applicants first.

**Verdict (headline read).** BEAT (vs prior year; no analyst consensus for a private company) · FY2025 (year ended 31 Dec 2025) revenue £4.5bn / $6.0bn (+46% YoY) · profit before tax £1.7bn / $2.3bn (+57% YoY) · net profit ~£1.3bn / $1.7bn · retail customers 68.3m (+30% YoY). Record 5th consecutive profitable year, revenue mix diversifying away from interest income. No formal numeric guidance; long-term target 100m retail customers by mid-2027 with a $13bn five-year investment commitment. Primary source: Revolut Group Holdings Ltd Annual Report 2025 / FY2025 results release (filing published 2026-04-29).

**Key figures (with growth).**
- Revenue: £4.5bn / $6.0bn, +46% YoY (FY2024: £3.1bn / ~$4.0bn). €-reported: €5.2bn, +46%.
- Profit before tax: £1.7bn / $2.3bn, +57% YoY; PBT margin ~38%.
- Net profit: ~£1.3bn / $1.7bn.
- Retail customers: 68.3m, +30% YoY (FY2024: 52.5m). Business customers: 767k, +33% YoY.
- Customer balances (incl. funds at partner institutions): £50.2bn / $67.5bn, +66% YoY.
- Interest income: £974m / ~$1.3bn, +23% YoY. Lending portfolio: £2.2bn / $2.9bn, +120% YoY.

**By segment / driver.** Fee-based revenue = 76% of turnover; growth is broadening off the interest-income base (interest = 21.6% of revenue vs a much larger share in prior high-rate years). FY2025 revenue mix: card payments £1.0bn / $1.3bn (+45%, ~22.2% of revenue), interest income (~21.6%), subscriptions £708m / $936m (+67%, ~15.7%), wealth £663m / $876m (+31%, ~14.7%), FX £606m / $800m (+43%, ~13.4%). 11 product lines each exceeded ~£100m in annual revenue — the clearest signal that growth is no longer dependent on any single line or on rates. Subscriptions (+67%) and lending (+120%) are the fastest movers.

**vs expectations / prior period.** Revolut is private — no sell-side consensus/whisper, so beat/miss is qualitative vs prior year [consensus: no data]. Acceleration on every headline line: revenue +46% YoY and PBT +57% YoY both outpace the customer-count growth of +30%, i.e. ARPU is rising (monetisation per user improving, not just user growth). Trajectory vs base: FY2023 revenue $2.2bn / PBT $545m → FY2024 $4.0bn / $1.4bn PBT → FY2025 $6.0bn / $2.3bn PBT. See internal corpus: [[Revolut raises $3B at $75 billion valuation]] (secondary at $75bn, Oct-2025), [[Revolut explores dual listing in London and New York]].

**Guidance / forward.** No formal numeric revenue/profit guidance (private company; annual cadence). Management forward markers: target of 100m retail customers by mid-2027, expansion across 100+ countries, and a stated ~$13bn five-year investment commitment to fund geographic and product build-out. Tone: confident/expansionary; the US push and global licensing are the emphasised growth vectors. What they leave out: no explicit margin guidance for FY2026 despite a heavy investment ramp — the $13bn spend commitment could compress the ~38% PBT margin if revenue growth normalises off the +46% pace.

**Thesis-flags (Philippines expansion relevant).**
1. The Philippines digital-bank / e-money licence plan (this note) fits the explicit 100-countries / 100m-customer growth engine and the $13bn investment budget — it is funded strategy, not an isolated bet. Second-order: APAC/emerging-market retail onboarding is cheap-ARPU at first, so near-term it dilutes group ARPU even as it feeds the 2027 customer target.
2. Monetisation is de-risking off interest rates: fee-based revenue now 76% of turnover and interest income only ~21.6% (vs rate-era dependence). Why it matters: a Philippines e-money/deposit licence adds low-cost deposit funding and local card/FX/subscription monetisation — the same diversified levers that drove FY2025, replicated in a new market. Resilient to the rate cycle.
3. Licensing-execution risk is the live constraint on the thesis, not demand. Revolut's own track record is mixed: UK full banking licence has repeatedly stalled on risk-control concerns ([[Revolut's UK banking licence bid hits risk-control setback]], [[Revolut's full UK banking licence held up by risk concerns]]), while Colombia approval landed ([[Revolut wins approval to establish bank in Colombia]]). So Philippines BSP approval timing/conditionality is the swing factor; the financials give ample capital, but regulators gate the upside.
4. Lending +120% YoY (to £2.2bn) is the fastest-growing and highest-risk line — credit/reserve quality is not detailed in the headline release (de-PR: they tout the growth, stay quiet on loss rates). If a Philippines consumer-lending push follows the licences, group credit risk rises on a book that is still young and unseasoned.
