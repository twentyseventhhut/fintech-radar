# Rain seeks US national trust bank charter

If the OCC approves, the New York based bank could custody digital assets and issue USD backed stablecoins. The filing came three days after the Independent Community Bankers of America sued the OCC over its trust charter framework.

Rain has filed an application to create a U.S. national trust bank that could custody digital assets, manage stablecoin reserves and issue dollar-backed stablecoins if the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency approves the plan.
Rain has applied for a national trust bank charter to custody assets and issue stablecoins.
Rain National Trust Bank would not accept deposits, offer consumer accounts, or make commercial loans.
The proposed bank could manage reserves for permitted stablecoin issuers under the federal GENIUS Act.
Community bankers sued the OCC days earlier, challenging rules supporting crypto national trust bank charters.
The OCC says its April charter rule clarified existing authority and did not expand powers.
According to Rain’s Oct. 5 announcement , the proposed Rain National Trust Bank would be headquartered in New York and operate as a separately capitalized subsidiary under OCC supervision. Rain itself would remain a stablecoin payments platform and would not become the bank.
The application arrived three days after the Independent Community Bankers of America filed a federal lawsuit challenging the legal framework the OCC uses for national trust bank charters, placing Rain’s bid into an expanding dispute over how crypto and payments firms can enter the federal banking system.
 Rain trust bank could hold reserves and issue stablecoins 
If approved, Rain National Trust Bank would have three main business lines serving institutional clients, according to the company.
The bank would provide fiduciary custody for approved digital assets and U.S. dollars, with client property separated from its own assets. It could manage reserves for permitted stablecoin issuers and act as the issuer of record for U.S. dollar-backed stablecoins under the GENIUS Act.
Rain CEO and co-founder Farooq Malik said customers using the company’s infrastructure want assets supporting their programs held by a fiduciary overseen by a federal regulator. He described the proposed bank as a separate operation that would hold client assets under OCC examination.
Rain currently provides infrastructure for stablecoin cards, wallets and money transfers. The company said partners serving millions of end users currently rely on a combination of state licenses, outside custodians and third-party stablecoin issuers. Its charter application seeks permission to bring some of those functions into the proposed federal trust bank.
The company has named Brandon Soto as proposed president and CEO, subject to OCC review. Soto previously served as chief financial officer of Square Financial Services, Block’s Utah-chartered industrial bank, and later held the CFO role at Coastal Financial Corporation.
 OCC charter would not turn Rain into a regular bank 
Despite the proposed bank name, Rain National Trust Bank would not operate like a traditional consumer bank.
Rain said the institution would not accept deposits, provide checking or savings accounts, offer consumer accounts or make commercial loans. It would operate as an uninsured national trust bank, meaning it would not carry FDIC deposit insurance.
Assets placed in custody would remain identified as client assets instead of becoming liabilities of the bank. Rain said reserves supporting any stablecoin issued by the proposed institution would not be pledged, lent or reused.
The structure resembles other crypto-focused national trust bank applications moving through the OCC. As previously reported by crypto.news, the regulator has processed applications from digital asset and payments companies seeking custody, settlement and stablecoin-related powers without taking conventional deposits.
Circle has already moved further through that process. In July, the stablecoin issuer received final OCC approval for First National Digital Currency Bank , which operates as Circle National Trust. Crypto.news reported at the time that the bank would begin with fiduciary digital asset custody and could later serve selected institutional clients.
Other applicants remain at different stages. Agora received preliminary conditional approval in September after filing its application in April, while Zerohash submitted a revised application in August after the OCC returned an earlier filing.
 Rain application comes as crypto charters face a court fight 
Rain’s filing comes as community banks are challenging the OCC’s authority to approve national trust banks whose activities extend beyond traditional fiduciary services.
The Independent Community Bankers of America sued the OCC and Comptroller Jonathan Gould on Oct. 2 in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia. The case, Independent Community Bankers of America v. Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, was assigned case number 1:2026cv03441 to Judge Carl J. Nichols.
The complaint challenges a March 2026 OCC rule, Interpretive Letter 1176 and the conditional approval granted to Protego Holdings. ICBA argues that the agency has “far exceeded” its authority by allowing national trust banks to conduct non-fiduciary activities under a limited-purpose charter. The allegations have not been decided by the court.
ICBA wants the court to find the March rule and Interpretive Letter 1176 unlawful. Its complaint seeks to stop the OCC from relying on the disputed framework for additional approvals and asks the court to vacate Protego’s conditional charter approval.
The OCC takes a different view of its powers. In its February bulletin announcing the rule, the regulator said the change merely clarifies its longstanding authority to let national trust banks conduct non-fiduciary activities alongside fiduciary work. The agency expressly said the rule neither expands nor contracts its chartering authority. It took effect April 1.
An OCC spokesperson said Monday that the agency does not comment on litigation, according to Banking Dive. Comptroller Gould has previously defended the agency’s approach to trust bank applications, saying the OCC assesses whether applicants have a reasonable chance of success instead of applying a zero-risk standard.
The legal fight follows months of objections from banking groups. Crypto.news reported in July that banking industry representatives were considering legal action after a series of crypto and fintech companies sought or received national trust bank charters.
 Rain now faces OCC review and a public comment period 
Rain’s application still requires OCC approval before the proposed bank can begin business.
The company said the review will include a public comment period, with the public portion of its application expected to appear on the OCC website. Rain has not provided a date for an OCC decision, and the regulator follows its own review timetable.
Rain worked with law firm Paul Hastings to prepare the application. Soto’s appointment as president and CEO remains subject to OCC review, while the proposed bank must receive all required regulatory approvals before starting operations.
The federal court challenge remains at an early stage. Public docket records show ICBA filed its complaint on Oct. 2, followed by attorney appearances and electronically issued summonses to the defendants and federal officials.
Rain said its national trust bank project is a multi-year initiative. Its existing card, wallet and money-movement programs will remain in place while the OCC reviews the charter application, and the company said the proposed bank would begin operating only after receiving all required approvals.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened.** On 2026-10-05, stablecoin card-infrastructure company **Rain** (rain.xyz — issuer of stablecoin cards/wallets/money-movement, Visa & Mastercard principal member; **not** the payroll "Rain" EWA app, **not** Zerodha's Rainmatter, **not** payments startup Rainforest) filed an OCC application to establish **Rain National Trust Bank**, a separately capitalized New York-headquartered subsidiary. The parent stays a payments platform; the bank would (1) custody digital assets and USD as a fiduciary, (2) manage reserves for permitted stablecoin issuers, and (3) be issuer-of-record for USD-backed stablecoins under the GENIUS Act. It would **not** take deposits, offer consumer/checking accounts, make loans, or carry FDIC insurance. Proposed CEO: **Brandon Soto** (ex-CFO of Square Financial Services and Coastal Financial). Counsel: Paul Hastings. Rain frames it as a multi-year effort; existing programs continue with current partners during OCC review + public-comment period.

**Why now / why it matters.** This is a vertical-integration move: Rain's partners today stitch together state licenses, third-party custodians, and outside stablecoin issuers. A federal trust charter would let Rain bring custody, reserve management, and issuance in-house under a single OCC-supervised entity, usable across all 50 states without state-by-state licensing — removing counterparty dependence and a margin layer. It rides the **GENIUS Act** stablecoin regime (signed 2025) that created the demand for federally supervised reserve/issuance vehicles.

**Internal comps (prior notes).** Rain is now in a crowded queue — by CoinDesk's count the **12th** crypto firm seeking an OCC trust charter:
- [[Circle applies for US national trust bank charter]] → later got final approval (First National Digital Currency Bank / Circle National Trust), the furthest-along comp.
- [[OCC grants conditional trust bank approval to five crypto firms]] (Ripple, Circle, BitGo, Fidelity, Paxos, Dec 2025) — the batch that set the template.
- [[Paxos pursues national trust bank charter from OCC]] and [[Crypto.com files for OCC National Trust Bank charter]] — direct precedent filings.
- [[Coinbase faces banker pushback on trust bank charter bid]] / [[Stripe faces pushback over national trust bank charter bid]] / [[Walmart, crypto firms and fintechs chase US bank charters]] — the bank-lobby opposition arc that now culminates in the ICBA suit.

Rain's own trajectory: [[Rain raises $250M Series C at $1.95B valuation]], [[Rain builds full stack for enterprise stablecoin cards]], [[Rain reaches $1.95 billion valuation and to launch Mastercard cards]], [[Naver makes first US crypto investment in Rain ahead of merger]] — a company aggressively building full-stack stablecoin rails, for which an in-house charter is the logical capstone.

**The twist — legal overhang.** Rain filed **three days after** the **Independent Community Bankers of America (ICBA)** sued the OCC (filed Oct 2, 2026; *ICBA v. OCC*, D.D.C., No. 1:2026cv03441, Judge Carl Nichols). ICBA challenges the OCC's March 2026 rule + Interpretive Letter 1176 + Protego's conditional approval, arguing the agency exceeded its authority by letting national trust banks do non-fiduciary (crypto) activities under a lightly-regulated limited-purpose charter, and skipped notice-and-comment. Relief sought: vacate the rule, the letter, and Protego's approval, and bar further approvals under the framework. The OCC says the rule merely clarified longstanding authority (effective Apr 1, 2026). So Rain's bid lands in open legal uncertainty: approval timeline is OCC-discretionary and the whole charter pathway could be enjoined.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Rain sits at the intersection of two subverticals: (a) stablecoin card-issuing infrastructure and (b) the OCC national-trust-bank charter wave for crypto/payments firms. The GENIUS Act (enacted 18 Jul 2025) created a federal pathway for "permitted payment stablecoin issuers," one route being an OCC-chartered uninsured national (trust) bank approved to issue/manage stablecoins (per Davis Polk / Troutman analyses of OCC's 2 Mar 2026 proposed GENIUS implementing rule; reserves must be 1:1 in cash + ≤93-day Treasuries / repo). The OCC's 26 Feb 2026 final rule (Interpretive Letter 1176, effective 1 Apr 2026) clarified that national trust banks may conduct non-fiduciary activities — the legal hinge Rain's filing relies on. Structure: the charter space is a land-grab — the OCC has approved/conditionally approved ~21 trust charters, ≥13 to crypto firms (per Consumer Finance Monitor, 5 Oct 2026). Why now: GENIUS made federal issuer-of-record status a competitive asset (institutional clients want reserves held by a federally examined fiduciary, per CEO Malik), and the charter is a multi-year moat play, not a near-term P&L event. Card-issuing TAM: F-Prime frames stablecoin cards as "the next $100bn" race (analysis, not a hard figure — treat as directional).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for the card-infra layer: annualized payment volume (TPV), active card base, partner count, principal-membership status (direct issuance vs sponsor-bank dependence), take rate `[UNSOURCED]`. Rain's disclosed metrics: >$3bn annualized TPV, >200 partners across 150+ jurisdictions, card base +30x and volume +38x YoY, Visa AND Mastercard principal member (per PYMNTS/The Block Jan-2026 Series C coverage; openbankingtracker 2026). Key players, two fronts:
- Card-issuing infra: Rain, Bridge (Stripe's "Open Issuance"), Reap (Kraken/Payward, Visa principal), Immersve (Mastercard principal), Baanx, Nium; incumbents Marqeta ($383bn processed 2025) and Galileo as non-stablecoin benchmarks. Basis of competition: principal membership (removes sponsor-bank dependence), developer experience, network reach.
- Charter race (direct comps to this news): Circle (First National Digital Currency Bank / Circle National Trust — final OCC approval Jul 2026, furthest along), Agora ([[Agora secures conditional OCC approval for trust bank]], conditional Sep 2026), Modern Treasury ([[Modern Treasury applies to establish national trust bank]], applied Oct 2026), Zerohash (revised filing Aug 2026), plus the Dec-2025 cohort Ripple/Circle/BitGo/Fidelity/Paxos and Feb-2026 Bridge/Protego/Crypto.com.
Position: Rain is ahead on the card-infra layer (dual principal membership, real TPV) but behind on the charter layer — it has only just filed (5 Oct 2026) while Circle is already approved and Agora conditionally approved. Moat (analysis): dual network principal membership + an institutional, non-deposit fiduciary charter would be a regulatory + distribution moat; today it is still an application, so the moat is prospective.

**Risk flags.**
1. Regulatory / legal-framework risk (primary). Rain's charter leans on the exact OCC rule (March 2026 / Interpretive Letter 1176) that ICBA is suing to vacate (filed 2 Oct 2026, D.D.C., case 1:2026cv03441). Second-order: an adverse ruling or vacatur could stall or void the whole crypto-trust-charter pathway, stranding Rain's (and peers') applications regardless of merit.
2. Execution / timing vs. already-approved peers. Rain just filed while Circle is approved and Agora conditional; a multi-year OCC review means Rain could be beaten to institutional issuer-of-record mandates. Second-order: first-movers may lock in the institutional custody/reserve relationships Rain is targeting.
3. Charter ≠ economics. The bank is uninsured, takes no deposits, makes no loans, cannot rehypothecate reserves — a thin fiduciary/custody model. Peer signal: Anchorage (first federal crypto trust bank) just cut 17% of staff, and ICBA alleges Protego has "insufficient capital and liquidity." Second-order: the charter is a credential/moat, not a profit engine; monetization still rides on the card-infra business.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a consolidation/legitimization move, not a re-rating: infra players are racing to internalize the stablecoin stack (issuer-of-record + custody + reserves) they currently rent from third parties, cutting counterparty dependence and capturing reserve economics (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if the OCC crypto-trust-charter framework survives the ICBA suit and Rain's charter is granted, Rain re-rates from "card rails vendor" to "regulated stablecoin bank + rails" and widens its moat vs sponsor-bank-dependent rivals. What breaks it: ICBA wins (framework vacated) OR the OCC review stalls past the point where Circle/Agora capture the institutional base. Trigger to watch: the D.D.C. ruling on ICBA v. OCC and Rain's appearance on the OCC public-comment docket.
