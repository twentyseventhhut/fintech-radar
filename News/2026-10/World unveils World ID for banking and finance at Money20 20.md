On stage at Money20/20 in Las Vegas, World co-founder Alex Blania unveiled World ID for banking and finance. The suite of technologies is now available to help enable AI-safe experiences for banking and financial institutions across internal and customer-facing systems, including interactions involving AI agents.
With World ID for banking and finance, organizations can have greater assurance that transactions, trades, customer requests, and even important video communications are AI-safe. The offering is backed by verifiable cryptographic Proofs such as Proof of Human , Proof of Presence, and Proof of Attributes. Bain Capital, OpenExchange, Outtake, Pantera Capital, and VanEck Funds are initial design partners utilizing the technology.
Proof of Human as essential infrastructure for the AI era
The internet, and many of the services it powers, was built on the assumption that humans would interact with other humans. Abundant and autonomous AI agents that can shop, transact, communicate, create, and ultimately outnumber humans online are now challenging this assumption. Today, trust is becoming the internet’s scarcest resource and most valuable commodity.
How AI is changing fraud in banking and finance
Enabling AI-safe experiences is particularly essential in the banking and finance industries where AI is being used to produce synthetic media and deepfakes convincing enough to trick the most advanced systems into approving transactions. More recently, the onslaught of personal agents from frontier AI organizations is opening up a new array of questions, challenges, and opportunities for consumers and financial institutions alike.
These risks are particularly significant in banking, asset management, and other financial services, where digital interactions can involve sensitive information and consequential financial decisions.
In early 2024, an employee at engineering firm Arup wired approximately $25.6 million after a video conference in which every other participant was a deepfake . Later that year, the U.S. Treasury’s Financial Crimes Enforcement Network warned that criminals were using deepfake photos and videos to get past checks on new-account applications.
Deloitte predicts that AI-enabled fraud losses in the U.S. could reach $40 billion by 2027, up from an estimated $12.3 billion in 2023.
Financial and banking institutions face growing pressure to confirm who is behind sensitive interactions while limiting the personal data they collect and store. World ID can help them address both needs.
World ID for banking and finance
 World ID for banking and finance aims to provide organizations with quantum-secure ways to establish important facts about the people behind digital interactions, both internally and externally, without collecting more personal information to store and secure. Depending on the use case, that can include whether someone is a unique human, whether they are present in the moment, or specific attributes such as age or nationality.
World ID for banking and finance brings Proof of Human to banking and financial institutions as well as their clients. Bain Capital , OpenExchange , Outtake , Pantera Capital , and VanEck Funds are design partners for the offering, which includes: World ID app, World ID for Agents , and World ID for Zoom.
World ID app: three Proofs for banking and financial services
 World ID app , now available to download in the App Store and Google Play across 150+ countries, offers three types of cryptographic Proofs:
 Proof of Human establishes that someone is a real and unique human with a one-time visit to an Orb . Orbs are also currently available at the NYSE , allowing individuals to easily claim their Proof of Human.
 Proof of Presence provides a quick liveness check on a phone to prove that a person is present.
 Proof of Attributes confirms specific information from an official government credential, such as nationality or being over a certain age. It reveals only the requested information.

Institutions can use these Proofs to confirm that a client or partner is a real human or to establish facts such as nationality or age without collecting documents or storing other personally identifiable information. That can happen when a client walks into a branch, transfers a significant sum of money, or opens a new account. Organizations can deploy the technology to offer clients high-assurance banking. That includes human-binding approvals for ACH and wire transfers, and human-binding requirements for account recovery that help prevent account takeover.
How World ID works with AI agents
World ID also extends to agents through AgentKit, a suite of developer tools, currently in beta, that allows autonomous agents to use World ID Proofs. With AgentKit, a person can delegate certain Proofs to an agent. The agent can then transact on their behalf and prove it is acting at the behest of a real human without revealing private information about that person. AgentKit makes it easier for builders to implement Proofs delegated by humans to their agents.
As agents take on more financial tasks, delegated Proofs could become a best practice. That is especially true for financial services use cases that must meet consumer protection and other compliance requirements.
World ID for Zoom: verified humans in high-stakes meetings
World ID for Zoom is designed to protect sensitive, high-stakes conversations by proving that a real and unique human is present. The integration uses cryptography to confirm that the person behind the screen is a verified human. It then grants them a Human Badge that everyone in the meeting can see. Here's how it works:
 Verify once. A participant completes a one-time World ID verification at an Orb and connects World ID app to their Zoom account.
 Confirm in the meeting. When they join a call, World ID for Zoom uses World ID app and a live selfie to confirm that the person behind the screen matches the Orb-verified person connected to the account. Once authenticated, a Human Badge appears on their Zoom tile. Zoom does not receive any personal information during the process.
 Set the rules. Meeting hosts can soon require participants to authenticate their Proof of Presence in a Zoom waiting room before entering a meeting. Alternatively, any participant can request a verification during a call.

# Context-enrichment: World unveils World ID for banking and finance at Money20/20
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
At Money20/20 Las Vegas (18 Oct 2026), World (ex-Worldcoin; Tools for Humanity, co-founded by Sam Altman and Alex Blania) packaged its existing identity primitives into a vertical go-to-market called "World ID for banking and finance." It is a bundle, not a new technology: (1) the **World ID app** with three cryptographic proofs — Proof of Human (one-time Orb iris scan, now also claimable at the NYSE), Proof of Presence (phone liveness), Proof of Attributes (nationality/age from a gov credential); (2) **World ID for Agents** via AgentKit — still in **beta** — lets a human delegate proofs to an AI agent; (3) **World ID for Zoom** — a "Human Badge" on the Zoom tile, now **generally available** to Zoom enterprise customers via the Zoom Marketplace.
- **Why structured this way:** the only genuinely new deliverable is Zoom GA and the banking framing/partner list; the proofs and AgentKit predate this (AgentKit launched March 2026 on Coinbase's x402 — [[World launches AgentKit identity toolkit on Coinbase's x402]]). World is converting a horizontal "proof of personhood" network into a sector sales motion where it can charge enterprises, rather than relying on the crypto/WLD-token flywheel.
- **The anti-PR tell (sourced):** the "design partners" are Bain Capital, OpenExchange, Outtake, Pantera Capital, VanEck Funds — i.e. **asset managers / VCs (several are World investors), not banks**. Cryptonomist (19 Oct 2026) flags explicitly that the suite "names no bank users." So this is an announcement with design partners, **not** a live bank deployment. "AgentKit remains in beta."

## [1] Competitors / peers
Deepfake/identity verification for financial services is a crowded field dominated by established biometric/IDV vendors, none of which require a proprietary eyeball-scanning hardware network:
- **iProov** — NIST 800-63-4 certified, surpassed **1M daily verifications in 2025** (biometricupdate, Mar 2026); the incumbent for bank liveness/deepfake defense.
- **Socure, Jumio, Incode, Mitek, Persona, Prove** — all publishing deepfake-detection updates in 2026; embedded in bank KYC/onboarding today.
- World's angle is distinct: "proof of unique human" (anti-sybil / anti-bot), **privacy-preserving, no PII**, ZK proofs. By its own framing (per [[Verifying humans without sharing data to ensure privacy]]) it is a **complement to KYC, not a replacement** — "not a competitor to KYC services." That caveat sharply limits where it displaces incumbents.
- **Why the lay of the land matters (analysis):** banks already have NIST-certified, regulator-friendly liveness from iProov et al. World's differentiator (one-human-one-identity for the AI-agent era) addresses a problem banks don't yet have at scale (agentic money movement), while its hardware-Orb dependency and regulatory baggage are frictions incumbents don't carry. Second-order: World is likely to win agentic-identity edge cases faster than core-KYC displacement.

## [2] Company history / fit
World's trajectory is a steady pivot from crypto token toward identity/finance infrastructure: launched 2023 as Worldcoin; US Orb rollout 2025; virtual bank accounts pilot (payroll→USDC) Nov 2025 ([[World App pilots virtual bank accounts converting payroll to USDC]]); full World App relaunch with chat, Earn (15-18% APY), 100+ assets, World Card Dec 2025 ([[World relaunches World App with verification and digital assets]]); AgentKit on x402 Mar 2026 ([[World launches AgentKit identity toolkit on Coinbase's x402]]); Tinder/Zoom/Docusign partnerships Apr 2026 (Rest of World). The banking suite is a logical next step along the same "real human network → monetize as infrastructure" path.
- **Why World acts this way (analysis):** the WLD-token/airdrop acquisition model is capped and regulation-exposed; selling verification to regulated enterprises is a durable, higher-quality revenue line that leverages the one asset World has that peers lack — ~18M Orb-verified unique humans. The Altman/OpenAI association gives it the "AI era needs proof of human" narrative credibility.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new here:** Zoom for Zoom GA (previously a partnership/announcement, now live to enterprise) and the banking-vertical packaging. Everything else (the three proofs, AgentKit beta) is repackaged.
- **Traction (de-PR'd):** >18M Proof-of-Human holders across 150-160+ countries; World App was most-used self-custody wallet by MAU (SensorTower, Sep 2025). BUT on the banking suite specifically: **zero named bank customers, design partners are investors, AgentKit in beta.** Adoption of the *banking* use case = announced, not live.
- **Who captures the margin / what breaks it (analysis):** World wants to be the identity toll layer under agentic finance. The risk is disintermediation — Visa/Mastercard, Coinbase (x402 is Coinbase/Cloudflare's), and bank-grade IDV vendors can all offer "is this a real human/authorized agent" without the Orb's regulatory liability. If card networks or OpenAI/Anthropic agent frameworks bundle identity natively, World becomes one optional proof among many (its own framing: "designed to be used alongside other identity systems").

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- World cites Deloitte: US AI-enabled fraud losses could hit **$40bn by 2027** (from ~$12.3bn in 2023), and the Arup ~$25.6M deepfake-video wire (early 2024) as the demand driver. FinCEN warned (2024) on deepfake account-opening fraud.
- **Market reaction:** WLD token rose on the announcement (cryptoticker, Oct 2026) — sentiment-driven, not revenue-backed.
- **Regulatory backdrop (the real risk):** Orb iris collection has been **banned/suspended/fined in Spain, Portugal, Germany, Brazil, Kenya, South Korea, Indonesia** (Rest of World, Oct 2026); Colombia ordered closure ([[Colombia orders immediate, definitive closure of Worldcoin operations]]); Thai SEC filed complaints ([[Thai SEC files complaints over unlicensed Worldcoin trading]]). Selling biometric identity to *regulated banks* while facing biometric-privacy bans is the central tension.
- **Why the market may still go this way (analysis):** agentic commerce ($3-5tn by 2030 per World's own cite) genuinely needs machine-readable "human behind the agent" primitives; even if World doesn't win, the category is real. Counterintuitive second-order: World's biggest asset (18M eyeballs) is also its biggest liability (a single, regulator-targeted biometric honeypot) — concentration makes it fragile, not safe.

## Top challenge / red-team questions (second-order)

1. **Are any actual banks using this, or only investors?** Answered: no named bank customers; "design partners" (Bain Capital, OpenExchange, Outtake, Pantera, VanEck) are asset managers/VCs, several of them World investors (Cryptonomist, 19 Oct 2026). This is an announcement, not a deployment.
2. **Is any of it actually live vs announced?** World ID for Zoom is GA (live via Zoom Marketplace). World ID app proofs exist. **AgentKit remains in beta.** Banking deployments: not live.
3. **Is this new or a repackage of AgentKit (Mar 2026)?** Mostly repackage + Zoom GA + banking framing. The core proofs and agent delegation predate this. Fresh as a *distinct event* (new vertical, new GA milestone), but low novelty.
4. **Does it replace bank KYC?** No — World itself says it is a complement to KYC, not a substitute (TFH interview, [[Verifying humans without sharing data to ensure privacy]]). This caps its revenue wedge in banking.
5. **How does it beat iProov/Socure/Jumio, who are already in banks and NIST-certified?** Open. World's edge is anti-sybil "unique human" + no PII; incumbents own regulator-friendly liveness/IDV with 1M+ daily verifications (iProov). No benchmark data shared.
6. **What's the business model / who pays?** Open — no pricing, no take-rate, no contract terms disclosed. Presumably enterprise SaaS licensing; unproven in banking.
7. **Does the Orb hardware dependency limit enterprise adoption?** Likely yes — Proof of Human needs a one-time physical Orb visit (now at NYSE), a friction and supply-chain constraint peers don't have. (analysis)
8. **Regulatory exposure for selling biometrics to regulated banks?** High and open: Orb iris collection banned/suspended/fined in Spain, Portugal, Germany, Brazil, Kenya, South Korea, Indonesia, Colombia; Thai SEC complaints. Banks are risk-averse on biometric-privacy liability.
9. **Is the $40bn fraud figure World's or independent?** Independent — Deloitte estimate (US AI-enabled fraud by 2027, from $12.3bn in 2023). Legitimate demand signal, but cited by vendor as marketing.
10. **Does WLD token price action reflect fundamentals?** No — WLD rose on sentiment/announcement (cryptoticker), no revenue tie.
11. **Who controls the agentic-payments rails this plugs into?** Not World — x402 is Coinbase/Cloudflare's; card networks (Visa/Mastercard) are building their own agent identity. World risks being one optional proof layer, by its own admission ("alongside other identity systems"). Disintermediation risk is real.
12. **What's the privacy architecture — is "no PII" credible?** Partly — ZK proofs + anonymized multi-party computation / sharded iris codes (TFH). But iris data collection itself is what regulators object to, regardless of downstream ZK. Open whether banks' DPOs accept it.
13. **Is there fraud-liability clarity if a Proof is spoofed/delegated maliciously?** Silent — no liability framework disclosed for a delegated-agent transaction gone wrong. Open.
14. **Could this cannibalize or conflict with World's own consumer crypto brand?** (analysis) Enterprise/bank trust vs a controversial crypto-airdrop brand is a positioning tension; selling to compliance teams may require distancing from WLD.
15. **What would make the banking thesis wrong?** A card network or agent-framework (OpenAI/Anthropic/Visa) bundling native "human-behind-agent" identity, or a major jurisdiction banning the Orb in a core banking market — either collapses World to a niche optional proof.

Importance: 3/5 — a real company in a real, growing category (deepfake/agentic-identity for finance) with a genuine differentiator (18M unique-human network) and one live milestone (Zoom GA). But the banking suite itself is a repackage with zero named bank customers, AgentKit in beta, heavy regulatory baggage, and strong incumbent IDV competition. Announcement-grade, not deployment-grade.
