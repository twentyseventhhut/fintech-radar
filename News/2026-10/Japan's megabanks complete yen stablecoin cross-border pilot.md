## How the Yen Stablecoin Pilot Worked

Three major Japanese megabanks and two partners took part in the pilot project. The banks issued one shared yen token, which the group tested end to end.

> BREAKING: 🇯🇵 Japan’s three largest banks completed their first cross-border stablecoin payment test.  
>   
> MASSIVE! 🚀
> 
> — Crypto Rover (@cryptorover) October 16, 2026

**The partners split the work across issuance, custody, and technology, and each firm was responsible for its own part of the process.**

*   The three participating megabanks conducted the pilot from July 17 to 23, 2026.
*   Mitsubishi UFJ Trust and Banking acted as trustee, while technology firm TISI Inc. joined as the technology provider.
*   The tokens were issued under Japan’s Payment Services Act, and the three banks acted as joint settlors.
*   Circulation was limited to KYC-verified users at participating banks and had no cap, while bank deposits backed the tokens.

A trustee holds assets for others and must follow the trust’s terms. Trustees must act in the interest of beneficiaries. Beneficiaries are the people who hold rights under the trust.

Settlors create a trust and place assets into it. KYC, or know-your-customer, means banks verify each user’s identity before granting access.

## What Did Japan’s Pilot Actually Prove?

The pilot’s main use case was cross-border settlement between Japanese headquarters and the overseas offices of Mitsubishi Corporation.

Cross-border settlement means finalizing payments between parties in different countries. Redemption means returning tokens for cash. The pilot verified the full cycle: issuance, transfer, and redemption.

Results confirmed the safety, reliability, and operational viability of joint issuance. Participants gained practical insight into systems and operations. Compliance work also covered anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing measures.

Those findings matter because the banks had not yet run any live transactions. For the wider industry, the result is a workable template for banks exploring digital money. Live transactions remain the next milestone for the project.

The Financial Services Agency-backed project was selected in November 2025 under its FinTech PoC Hub and Payment Innovation Project. SMBC provided the blockchain platform, which TISI built.

**In June, the banks announced a joint council to study governance and operations. Fiscal 2026, the target year for real transactions, runs from April 2026 to March 2027.**

# Context-enrichment: Japan's megabanks complete yen stablecoin cross-border pilot
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**FRESHNESS: fresh.** This is the first RESULT milestone (a completed PoC with findings), not a reprint of the plan. Prior notes covered only the *intention* to launch — [[Three Japanese megabanks launch yen-pegged stablecoin]] (2025-10, the "will launch by March 2027" announcement) and [[Japan's three megabanks plan joint stablecoin launch by 2027]] (2026-06, the joint-council plan). This note reports that the July 17–23, 2026 cross-border PoC actually ran and verified issuance→transfer→redemption. New reporting content ("talks/plan → executed trial with results"), so not a duplicate. The headline verb "complete" is accurate; the PR overreach is that "complete" still means *no live transactions yet*.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- On **Oct 16, 2026**, Mizuho, MUFG and SMBC announced they **completed a PoC** (run **July 17–23, 2026**) for a single **shared yen stablecoin** issued jointly by the three banks acting as **joint settlors** under Japan's **Payment Services Act**, backed by **bank deposits**, KYC-gated to participating banks' users, no issuance cap.
- Structure: **Mitsubishi UFJ Trust & Banking = trustee**; **TISI Inc. = technology provider**; **SMBC provided the blockchain platform** (TISI-built). FSA-backed, selected Nov 2025 under the **FinTech PoC Hub / Payment Innovation Project (PIP)**.
- Use case tested: **cross-border settlement between Mitsubishi Corporation's Japan HQ and its overseas offices** — i.e. a captive intra-group corporate treasury flow, not open-market payments.
- **What it proved (de-PR'd):** the *full lifecycle* (issue → transfer → redeem) technically works across a **multi-bank joint-issuer model**, plus AML/CFT controls. **What it did NOT do:** any live/commercial transaction, any external counterparties, any FX leg at scale, any public circulation.
- **Why structured this way → what it reveals:** the joint-settlor + single-trust design is the whole point. Under Japan's PSA, a bank-issued stablecoin needs a trust/deposit backing; having three rival megabanks issue *one* token (not three competing tokens) is the hard governance problem they are de-risking. The pilot is really a **governance/interoperability proof**, not a tech proof — the tech (Progmat/Avalanche rails) was already demonstrated. Anchoring the pilot to a single friendly corporate (Mitsubishi Corp, a MUFG-affiliated trading house) lets them claim "cross-border" while avoiding real counterparty, FX and liquidity risk.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **JPYC Inc.** — launched Japan's first *regulated, live* yen stablecoin on **Oct 27, 2025** (Type II funds-transfer licence; deposit + JGB backed; Ethereum/Polygon/Avalanche; ¥10tn ambition). JPYC is **already live and retail-facing**; the megabanks are still at PoC. So on "first to market" the banks are **behind a startup** — but they are targeting a different segment (B2B corporate cross-border, not retail minting).
- **Project Pax** (the banks' own cross-border platform, launched Sept 2024 with **Progmat + Datachain + TOKI**) — targets **¥1tn B2B stablecoin volume by 2028** on a "rails abstraction" model (clients send normal SWIFT instructions; settlement happens on-chain invisibly). This PoC is effectively a Pax building block.
- **Global peers:** Citi/Coinbase stablecoin checkout ([[Citi details stablecoin checkout with Coinbase]]), bank consortium stablecoins in the US/EU, SBI/Mesh/Money Forward Japan crypto JV ([[SBI, Mesh and Money Forward plan Japan crypto payments JV]]). Position: **catching up / parity on tech, behind on live adoption**; distinctive on the *three-rival-banks-one-token* governance model.
- **Why the landscape is this way:** Japan's PSA gives banks/trusts a regulatory moat (100%+ deposit/JGB reserve rule) that excludes most crypto-native issuers except licensed ones like JPYC. The megabanks' edge is **corporate balance-sheet trust + treasury relationships**, not technology — hence they compete on institutional B2B settlement, not retail.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Timeline: **2024** — banks begin exploring stablecoins together (Project Pax; "3 Mega SC" with Nomura/Daiwa for securities settlement). **Late 2024/Nov 2025** — FSA classifies it a Payment Innovation Project. **Oct 2025** — public "will launch by March 2027" announcement. **June 2026** — joint governance council announced. **July 2026** — this PoC run. **Oct 2026** — results announced. Target: live transactions in **FY2026 (Apr 2026–Mar 2027)**.
- Fit: logical and consistent — this is the next checkbox on a multi-year roadmap, not a pivot.
- **Why the banks act this way:** domestic lending margins are thin and SWIFT cross-border is slow/expensive; tokenized deposit-backed settlement lets them defend the **corporate transaction-banking franchise** (cf. [[MUFG launches transaction banking to enter $1.3tn market]]) against both fintech disruptors and foreign stablecoins. Moving jointly (not three competing coins) is a defensive move to avoid fragmenting their own corporate client base.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new:** a *joint multi-issuer* bank stablecoin with three megabanks as co-settlors under one trust — an unusual governance construct. The tech lifecycle itself is not new (Progmat has demonstrated this).
- **Traction = essentially zero so far.** This is a **6-day closed PoC with one captive corporate** and **no live transactions**. "Successful pilot" ≠ adoption. The real traction test is FY2026 live B2B volume and whether counterparties beyond Mitsubishi Corp sign on.
- **Who captures the margin?** If it becomes real, the banks keep the **float/deposit economics + FX spread**, while Progmat/Datachain/TISI are infrastructure vendors earning platform fees. The risk: if "rails abstraction" (Pax) makes the stablecoin invisible to clients, the banks capture the value but the *stablecoin itself* is just plumbing — the moat is the corporate relationship, not the token. Conversely, JPYC's open retail model could commoditize yen-on-chain before the banks go live.
- **Why value-add is real-but-unproven:** cross-border corporate settlement in Japan is slow and the addressable market is huge (Pax cites the $182tn cross-border market; ¥1tn B2B target by 2028). But everything hinges on live counterparty adoption, which hasn't started.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Next milestone: **live/commercial transactions in FY2026** (by March 2027); formal governance council operationalized; likely expansion of the issuer/participant set (Pax prototype already has 10+ domestic/international banks).
- Sentiment: positive crypto-press framing ("MASSIVE", "landmark"), but this is **announcement-grade enthusiasm** — read it skeptically. Regulatory backdrop is favorable (FSA actively sponsoring via PIP; Japan also opening to foreign stablecoins in 2026).
- **Risks / second-order:** (a) JPYC and foreign USD stablecoins may win yen-on-chain mindshare before the banks launch; (b) three-bank governance is slow — joint decision-making could delay live rollout; (c) if clients never "see" the stablecoin (rails abstraction), there is no network/brand effect, so the banks are building infrastructure that competitors could later undercut; (d) FX and liquidity at real scale are untested. **Counterintuitive:** moving jointly looks safe but concentrates execution risk — a single slow consortium rather than three competing fast movers.

4. **Was any FX/liquidity actually tested at scale?** — Open/unlikely. "Cross-border" here = yen token moving between group entities; no evidence of a live multi-currency FX leg or external liquidity provision.
5. **How does this compare to JPYC?** — JPYC went **live Oct 27 2025** as Japan's first regulated yen stablecoin (retail-facing). The banks remain at PoC. Banks are **behind on live**, differentiated on B2B segment.
6. **What's the real novelty?** — The **three-rival-megabanks-one-token joint-settlor/single-trust** governance model. The underlying tech lifecycle (Progmat rails) is not new.
7. **Who captures the margin if it goes live?** — Banks keep float/deposit + FX spread; Progmat/Datachain/TISI earn vendor platform fees. Risk: "rails abstraction" makes the token invisible → banks own the client relationship but the stablecoin is commoditized plumbing.
8. **Is the ¥1tn-by-2028 Pax target credible?** — Open. It's an aspiration anchored to a $182tn cross-border market; zero live volume exists yet, so treat as marketing until FY2026 live numbers appear.
9. **Why move jointly rather than three competing coins?** — Defensive: avoid fragmenting their shared corporate client base and concentrate liquidity in one token. Trade-off: slow consortium governance.
10. **What's the second-order risk of the joint model?** — Execution-risk concentration: one slow three-party decision process instead of three fast movers; could be out-paced by JPYC / foreign USD stablecoins.
11. **What's the regulatory status?** — Favorable: FSA-sponsored via the Payment Innovation Project / FinTech PoC Hub (selected Nov 2025); issued under the Payment Services Act with deposit backing and a trust. No new regulatory hurdle created by the pilot.
12. **What would make this fail?** — No external counterparties adopt in FY2026; JPYC/USD stablecoins win yen-on-chain mindshare first; consortium governance stalls; FX/liquidity proves unworkable at scale.
13. **Who is silent about what?** — No disclosure of transaction volumes, fee economics, FX spread, settlement latency vs SWIFT, or which blockchain/Progmat chain was used in this specific PoC. (open)
14. **Did the prior version "fly"?** — There was no prior live version; the 2025/2026 announcements were plans. First actual execution is this PoC. Adoption = 0 to date.
15. **What is the single next proof that matters?** — A **live commercial cross-border transaction with an external counterparty** in FY2026 (by March 2027). Until then this is infrastructure, not a market.

Importance: 3/5 — A credible, FSA-backed milestone by Japan's three largest banks on a joint bank-issued stablecoin (governance novelty, large strategic TAM), but purely a closed 6-day PoC with a captive affiliate, zero live transactions, and already trailing JPYC on live deployment. Strategically notable, operationally still "announced, not adopted".

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits in regulated yen stablecoins / tokenized bank money — a subvertical created by legislation, not product demand. Japan's Payment Services Act (stablecoin amendments effective June 2023, further clarified 2025) restricts issuance to banks, funds-transfer operators and trust companies and mandates ≥100% reserve backing in yen deposits / JGBs (per news.bitcoin.com, Stripe, as of 2026). The pilot token is a "specified trust beneficiary right" backed by bank deposits — i.e. tokenized deposits dressed as a stablecoin, not an algorithmic or crypto-collateralized one. Two drivers + "why now": (1) regulatory unlock — in Aug 2026 the FSA removed the ¥1,000,000 per-transaction cap that had confined stablecoins to retail micropayments, opening B2B/corporate settlement, the exact use case this pilot targets (per techtimes.com, fystack.io, 2026); (2) the end of Japan's negative-rate era makes deposit-backed instruments economically live. "Why now": the ¥1m cap lift (Aug 2026) → corporate cross-border is now legal at scale → megabanks race to own the rails before non-bank issuers (JPYC) do. **Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for payment rails: settlement volume / value, number of corridors, participating institutions, per-transaction cost vs correspondent banking (SWIFT). Players & basis of competition: (a) bank consortia issuing deposit-tokens — the direct global comp is the JPMorgan/Citi/BofA/Wells Fargo Tokenized Deposit Network via The Clearing House (targeting 1H2027, per Forbes/cryptonews, 2026) and Citi Token Services + the DBS–Citi 24/7 cross-border USD tokenized-deposit pilot launched 2026-09-08; (b) non-bank regulated issuers — JPYC Co., Japan's first licensed yen stablecoin, launched Oct 2025 on ETH/Polygon/Avalanche (per theblock.co); (c) the infra layer — MUFG's Progmat platform (reportedly on Avalanche, per ethnews/kucoin, 2026), with Mitsubishi UFJ Trust as trustee and TISI as tech provider. Basis of competition is distribution + regulatory standing, not price. Protagonist position: ahead domestically on the bank-issued track (three megabanks jointly cover the bulk of Japanese corporate deposits; moat = network effects + switching costs of incumbent corporate banking relationships `(analysis)`), but behind the US consortium on timeline — the JPM-led network and Citi already have live/near-live corporate products, while Japan's trio has only completed a closed pilot (July 17–23, 2026) with live transactions still ahead, targeted for fiscal 2026 (ends March 2027).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Pilot ≠ live — execution/timeline risk.** Only the full cycle (issuance/transfer/redemption) was verified in a 7-day closed test for a single counterparty (Mitsubishi Corporation HQ ↔ overseas offices). Live commercial transactions are still unproven and gated to fiscal 2026 (to March 2027); slippage here would let the US consortium and JPYC set the corporate standard first.
2. **Stack ambiguity / consortium governance.** The Oct-2025 story credited fintech Progmat as builder; the pilot credits SMBC's platform built by TISI, with a single trust company still to be chosen (per Ledger Insights, 2025). Three rival megabanks co-governing one token is a known failure mode (cf. the US four-bank network, where Forbes flags "history is the hard part") — disagreement on economics/liability can stall the rollout.
3. **Disintermediation by non-bank issuers.** JPYC is already live (Oct 2025) and benefits from the same ¥1m-cap removal; if corporates adopt a cheaper multi-chain non-bank token first, the megabanks capture the compliance burden but not the rails — margin captured a layer away `(analysis)`.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Falsifiable thesis: the Aug-2026 FSA cap removal turns yen stablecoins from a retail curiosity into a corporate cross-border settlement rail, and bank-issued deposit-tokens (not JPYC) win the enterprise segment because treasurers value deposit-equivalent credit standing over multi-chain reach. Trigger/catalyst to watch: the first *live* (non-pilot) commercial transaction before March 2027 and the naming of the single trust company; what would make the thesis wrong — a major Japanese corporate settling cross-border on JPYC (or on the US JPM/Citi network) before the megabank token goes live, signaling the bank consortium missed its window.
