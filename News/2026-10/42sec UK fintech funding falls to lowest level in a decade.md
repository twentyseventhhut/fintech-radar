## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)

A **data/analysis story**, not a corporate event: KPMG's **Pulse of Fintech H1 2026** report (released ~2026-08, retold by 42sec/Bloomberg on 2026-10-19) says total UK fintech investment hit **£1.8bn in H1 2026 — the lowest since 2016**, when KPMG began the series. That is **down ~two-thirds (~-67%) from £5.0bn in H1 2025**. The figure spans M&A + PE + VC, not venture alone. Deal count also fell to a decade low: **205 UK deals in H1'26 vs 281 a year earlier**.

The one bright spot KPMG foregrounds: **AI-related UK fintech investment rose to £445m across 79 deals (25% of total)**, up from £382m / 67 deals (16%) in H1'25 — so AI's *share* roughly doubled even as the absolute pool collapsed.

**Why framed this way / what it reveals.** Two things to de-PR. (1) **Numbers diverge by methodology and currency** — the KPMG/Bloomberg "£1.8bn, -67%, decade low" headline is a GBP, M&A-inclusive measure; the earlier **Innovate Finance H1 2026** cut reported **$1.8bn, only -5% HoH, UK still #2 globally** (see [[UK regains second spot in global fintech investment]]), and Tracxn put it near **$1.5bn / £1.1bn, -35%**. Same half-year, three very different "declines" — the "decade low" drama is partly a base-and-definition artifact (KPMG compares against a huge H1'25 that was inflated by mega-deals). (2) The AI "bright spot" is a *mix shift*, not growth of the pie — AI is winning share by cannibalizing the rest, which is the real story (see [4]).

## [1] Competitors / peers (other regions & data series)

- **US:** the Americas took **>80% of global H1'26 fintech investment** (~£63.8bn, of which US ~£59.4bn / 933 deals). US dominance is the mirror image of the UK's fall — capital is concentrating, not disappearing.
- **EMEA:** UK stays #1 in Europe/EMEA but its EMEA share **cratered from ~68% (end-2025) to 22% in H1'26** — a far steeper relative decline than the headline £ figure alone implies.
- **Global trend series (corpus):** [[Global fintech funding fell 18% QoQ to $11B in Q3]] (CB Insights, Q3'26, $100M+ rounds = 61% of funding on 4% of deals) and [[Global fintech investment rebounds to $116 billion in 2025, KPMG says]] (the H2'25 rebound this H1'26 drop reverses). [[Innovate Finance UK FinTech Investment Landscape 2025]] / [[UK regains second spot in global fintech investment]] show the 2025 full-year high ($3.6bn UK, #2) that H1'26 fell off from.

**Why the lay of the land is this way (analysis).** Fintech funding is now **bimodal and mega-round-driven** (Q3 global: 4% of deals = 61% of capital). When the handful of mega-rounds that a given half-year lands cluster in the US (OpenAI-era AI, US stablecoin/crypto raises), a mid-sized geography like the UK shows a violent % swing off a lumpy base. The 68%→22% EMEA share drop signals the UK isn't just shrinking — **capital is actively re-sorting toward the US and toward AI-native names**, wherever domiciled.

## [2] Company/sector history & fit

The UK has been the structural **#2 global fintech hub** (Revolut, Monzo, Wise, Zilch, ClearBank, Thought Machine) and routinely #1 in Europe. The H1'26 reading fits the broader down-cycle: after the 2021 ZIRP peak, funding fell for three years, **rebounded in 2025** ([[Global fintech investment rebounds to $116 billion in 2025, KPMG says]]), then re-cracked in H1'26. The UK's own 2025 recovery to #2 ([[UK regains second spot in global fintech investment]]) makes the H1'26 "decade low" read as a sharp reversal rather than a steady decline.

**Why (analysis).** Structural pressure: higher-for-longer rates raised the cost of growth capital; the mega-round engine (which the UK rode in 2021–22) migrated to AI/US; and UK late-stage names increasingly **raise privately/offshore or via secondaries** (e.g. [[Moneybox eyes £800M valuation in PISCES secondary share sale]]) rather than in primary VC rounds counted here — so some "decline" is capital routing around the metric, not pure demand collapse.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction

Novelty of the *story* is low — it is a scheduled half-yearly report, and the UK-weakness narrative has run all year ([[Innovate Finance UK FinTech Investment Landscape 2025]]). The genuinely new, citable datapoint is the **£1.8bn / decade-low / -67% KPMG print and the AI share jump to 25%** — a cleaner "AI is eating fintech's funding" signal than prior quarters.

**Why the value-add is real-but-partial (analysis).** The "decade low" is a true data fact but a **soft signal**: (a) methodology/currency makes the magnitude elastic (-5% to -67% depending on source); (b) H1 is a lumpy base dominated by whether mega-rounds happened to land; (c) the metric misses secondaries/offshore raises. The durable insight is the **structural one**: AI is pulling share within fintech (16%→25%) while the absolute pool shrinks — the question shifts from "is UK fintech funding down?" to **"is 'fintech' funding being reclassified as 'AI' funding, and does the UK capture the AI layer or just host it?"**

## [4] What's next / market sentiment

Expect commentary to stay "cautious optimism": KPMG/Innovate Finance frame scaling-business-model deals and AI as green shoots; bears point to US/AI capital concentration, geo-economic instability, and rate uncertainty. Watch H2'26 and the full-year print to see whether £1.8bn was a trough or a new baseline, and whether UK AI-fintech ($445m) compounds.

**Second-order (analysis).** Capital concentration cuts two ways: the mega-round regime (61% of global capital in 4% of deals) means a *single* UK AI-fintech mega-round could swing the H2 number back up — the series is fragile to one deal, not to broad demand. The real risk isn't a bad half; it's **persistent re-sorting of UK fintech talent/capital into US-domiciled AI vehicles**, which no half-yearly £ figure captures until it is already structural.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** UK fintech investment hit £1.8bn ($2.5bn) across 205 deals in H1 2026 — the lowest since KPMG's Pulse of Fintech began in 2016 (vs the £735m 2016 full-year base), down ~two-thirds from £5.0bn in H1 2025 (per KPMG UK, via Finextra/FintechFutures, Aug 2026). Deal count fell to 205 from 281 YoY. Structure: fintech VC is late-cycle and bifurcating — capital concentrates into fewer, larger, growth-proven names while early/mid-stage dries up; the barrier shifting from capital to a credible path-to-profit. Driver / "why now": a hard rotation of venture dollars into AI — AI-related UK fintech took £445m across 79 deals (25% of total, up from £382m/67 deals in H1'25), the one line rising while the aggregate collapses. Second-order: capital isn't leaving the UK so much as leaving *pure* fintech for AI-labelled plays. KPMG's Hannah Dobson framed it as "on a par with… the first wave of the pandemic" — i.e. a confidence shock, not just valuation reset.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs that gate funding now: path-to-profitability / Rule-of-40, revenue growth durability, and AI-attach. Players in the "ranking" are *countries*, not companies: US #1 at $17.2bn, UK #2 at $1.8bn, India #3 at $1.6bn (H1'26, Innovate Finance). Basis of competition = ecosystem gravity (talent, capital depth, regulatory clarity, exit routes). UK's EMEA position: still the regional leader ($2.5bn / 205 deals, ahead of Germany's $1.6bn / 36 deals; Europe total ~$5bn / 509 deals) — but share of EMEA investment collapsed to 22% from 68% at end-2025, so its *dominance* is eroding even as the #2 global rank holds. Position: slipping from strength, not from weakness — a relative de-rating of the UK as a fintech destination `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** This is a sector-funding data point, not a company round — no EV/Revenue or P/S to compute; multiples = N/A. Internal trend comps from the base:
- [[Innovate Finance UK FinTech Investment Landscape 2025]] — FY2025: global +21% to $53bn / 5,918 deals; UK reclaimed #2 at $3.6bn (Innovate Finance figure).
- [[UK regains second spot in global fintech investment]] — Jan 2026, UK back to #2 globally.
- [[Connecting the Dots Sokin raises $100M amid fintech funding slump]] — slump narrative already live early-2026.
Arithmetic on the UK trajectory: $3.6bn FY2025 (Innovate Finance) vs an H1'26 run-rate of ~$2.5bn×2 = ~$5bn annualised — but note H2 is typically back-loaded and the YoY *half-over-half* is the red line: £5.0bn → £1.8bn ≈ **−64%**. Caveat: KPMG (£1.8bn) and Innovate Finance ($1.8bn ≈ £1.4bn) report slightly different bases/scopes — treat the magnitude, not the decimal, as sourced.

**Risk flags.**
1. **AI cannibalisation of the fintech capital pool** — if 25% of "fintech" funding is really AI, headline fintech ex-AI fell even harder than −64%; a structurally smaller addressable VC pool for payments/neobank/lending, not a cyclical dip.
2. **Ecosystem-competitiveness / exit-route risk** — EMEA share 68%→22% signals capital mobility away from the UK; weak IPO/exit visibility (a long-standing London-listings concern) risks a self-reinforcing talent-and-capital drift `(analysis, drivers not in the cited data)`.
3. **Measurement / comparability risk** — "lowest in a decade" rests on one firm's methodology from a 2016 baseline; scope changes (M&A+PE+VC blended) can overstate the cliff. Read as direction, not precision.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a late-cycle de-rating of the UK as a fintech *destination*, with AI acting as the drain `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: if H2'26 UK fintech funding doesn't recover toward the ~£5bn/yr run-rate AND EMEA share stays near 20%, the shift is structural, not cyclical. Triggers to watch: a marquee UK fintech IPO (would reopen the exit channel), a rate-cut-driven venture thaw, or AI-labelled fintech deals proving they are fintech vs AI tourists. What breaks it: a large H2 round or two re-inflating the half and a bounce in EMEA share — then this was a seasonal/base-effect trough.
