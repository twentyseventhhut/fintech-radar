# Standard Chartered launches custody for Ripple's RLUSD on XRP Ledger

The bank is working with Ripple and LMAX Group to enable RLUSD as eligible collateral for certain LMAX products under its collateral mirroring model.

By Timmy Shen • Edited by James Hunt

#### Quick Take

*   Standard Chartered announced Monday that it has launched institutional custody for Ripple USD on the XRP Ledger.
*   The bank is working with Ripple and LMAX Group to enable RLUSD as eligible collateral for certain LMAX products under its collateral mirroring model.

Standard Chartered has launched institutional custody for Ripple USD Ripple USD RLUSD+0.01% on the XRP Ledger, as the bank continues to expand its crypto-related services.

In a Monday statement shared with The Block, Standard Chartered said its custody support for RLUSD is now live, available through its DIFC branch regulated by the Dubai Financial Services Authority.

The bank is working with Ripple and LMAX Group to enable RLUSD as eligible collateral for certain LMAX products under its collateral mirroring model. The arrangement would allow the value of eligible RLUSD collateral to be mirrored to LMAX Group without requiring the underlying assets to be transferred directly to the trading venue.

The planned integration remains subject to final documentation, testing, regulatory requirements, and internal approvals, with no launch date announced.

Advertisement

Standard Chartered noted that the arrangement is expected to "give institutional clients greater flexibility" in how they hold and trade crypto assets.

"Institutional clients want to participate in digital asset markets without compromising on how their assets are safeguarded or how risk is managed," said Waqar Chaudry, global head of digital assets at Standard Chartered. "The ecosystem must therefore make it easier for clients to hold assets securely, access liquidity and deploy collateral efficiently within trusted frameworks."

Jack McDonald, Ripple's SVP for stablecoins, said that RLUSD was built for "exactly this kind of institutional use case."

"Bringing together RLUSD on the XRP Ledger, Standard Chartered's custody capabilities and LMAX Group's institutional trading infrastructure creates a practical path for institutions to put stablecoins to work across global markets," McDonald added.

## Expanding crypto custody operations

The latest Ripple arrangement comes as Standard Chartered continues to expand its institutional crypto custody operations across multiple markets.

Last week, the bank's Singapore unit said it plans to launch institutional crypto custody services by the end of this year, extending its existing crypto custody footprint in the UAE, Luxembourg, and Hong Kong.

In April, Bloomberg reported that Standard Chartered was weighing folding parts of Zodia Custody, the institutional custodian it incubated with Northern Trust in 2020, into its corporate bank division. In May, the bank said its non-binding offer had been accepted.

Zodia has maintained a presence in Singapore since September 2023, when it began offering institutional crypto asset services in the city-state. It later expanded into Hong Kong. In June, Zodia secured a payment institution license in Luxembourg, enabling it to provide regulated stablecoin custody and transfer services across the EU.

[0] **What happened.** On 2026-10-12 Standard Chartered announced it has gone live with institutional custody for Ripple USD (RLUSD) on the XRP Ledger, offered through its DIFC branch in Dubai (regulated by the Dubai Financial Services Authority). Alongside custody, the bank disclosed a *planned* integration with Ripple and LMAX Group to make RLUSD usable as eligible collateral for certain LMAX products via a "collateral mirroring" model — the value of custodied RLUSD is recognised by LMAX without the underlying tokens leaving the bank's custody. The collateral piece is explicitly not yet live: it remains "subject to final documentation, testing, regulatory requirements, and internal approvals, with no launch date announced." Quotes come from Waqar Chaudry (SC global head of digital assets) and Jack McDonald (Ripple SVP, stablecoins). Source: The Block, 2026-10-12.

[1] **Why it matters.** This is a concrete, live custody mandate — not a plan or MoU — for a specific regulated USD stablecoin at a global systemically-important bank, plus a collateral-mobility design (custody + mirroring) that is the real institutional unlock: it lets funds post stablecoin collateral to a venue while keeping safekeeping at a bank. It deepens the SC–Ripple relationship (both now anchored in the DIFC after [[Ripple opens new regional headquarters at the DIFC]]) and converts the January framework in [[LMAX Group and Ripple partner on RLUSD institutional adoption]] into bank-grade infrastructure. Caveat: the headline "custody" is live but the more differentiated collateral feature is only planned.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is anything here actually new, or is "custody live" just a press framing of capabilities SC already had via Zodia? The news is the specific RLUSD asset + DIFC go-live + collateral design — confirm the custody itself is genuinely newly live vs. a rebrand.
2. The differentiated feature (LMAX collateral mirroring) is only *planned* — "subject to documentation, testing, regulatory requirements, internal approvals, no launch date." How much of the "importance" rests on a feature that may slip or never ship?
3. Scope is one branch (DIFC/Dubai) for one stablecoin. Is this a narrow pilot being marketed as a strategic milestone?
4. Does custody availability translate into real AUC/flows, or is this an announcement with no disclosed client commitments or volumes?
5. "Collateral mirroring" — is this materially different from standard tri-party collateral arrangements, or repackaged language? What's the actual legal/settlement novelty?
6. RLUSD is ~$2.3–2.5B — small vs. USDT/USDC. Is bank custody for a mid-size stablecoin genuinely significant, or is the brand (SC + Ripple) inflating perceived importance?
7. Regulatory durability: DFSA-regulated today — does this travel to EU (MiCA), Singapore (MAS), HK? Each is a separate approval; none confirmed for RLUSD specifically.
8. Counterparty/conflict risk: SC holds a stake in GSR and is deep in the Ripple ecosystem (DIFC, prime brokerage). Is this ecosystem lock-in rather than neutral custody?
9. Sourcing: much rests on SC's own statement to The Block plus Ripple's press release — both interested parties. What independent confirmation exists of the go-live? (CoinDesk/Cointelegraph largely restate the same announcement.)
10. XRP is explicitly excluded. Does that limit strategic weight — i.e., SC is comfortable with the regulated stablecoin but not the native token?
11. Timing cluster: SC made three custody announcements within a week (Singapore 10-08, RLUSD 10-12). Is this a coordinated PR push padding a thin news period rather than independent milestones?
12. Competitive moat: BNY Mellon, Citi (Token Services), HashKey/BitGo are all moving on institutional digital-asset custody same month. Is SC genuinely ahead or just keeping pace?
13. What happens to RLUSD custody economics if GENIUS Act implementation (~2027) reshapes US stablecoin rules — is the offering durable across the regime change?
14. Is there any execution/operational risk flagged (XRPL dependency — note the Oct 2026 XRPL "decade-old bug" patch) that could affect custody of an XRPL-native asset?
15. Could this be double-counted with the Singapore custody notes in the digest? Confirm it is treated as a distinct story (different asset, jurisdiction, mechanism).

**Importance: 3/5** — A live, bank-grade custody mandate for a specific regulated stablecoin at a GSIB, built on a credible prior partnership (LMAX/Ripple) and a real custody platform (Zodia), with a genuinely interesting collateral-mobility design. Marked down from 4 because the differentiating collateral-mirroring feature is only *planned* (no date), scope is a single branch/single asset, there are no disclosed client commitments or volumes, and most sourcing traces to the two interested parties. Fresh and distinct from the Singapore custody expansion and from the separate BNY/XRP story.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Bank-grade digital-asset custody is the plumbing layer this news sits in — not stablecoin issuance. Market sizing is noisy and mostly vendor-report driven: the specialist "crypto custody & institutional digital-asset management" segment is pegged at ~$8.4bn in 2026 → ~$48.7bn by 2034 at ~24.6% CAGR (per Stratistics MRC, via marketresearch.com; treat as directional, single-vendor) `[UNSOURCED precision]`. Structure: fragmenting-then-consolidating — pure-play custodians (Zodia, BitGo, Fireblocks, Anchorage) are being absorbed or out-muscled by G-SIBs entering with balance-sheet trust and existing licences. Barriers are regulatory (per-jurisdiction licences: DFSA/DIFC, MiCA, HK, Singapore MAS) and trust/scale, not tech. Why now: (i) the US GENIUS Act regulated-stablecoin regime (100% HQLA reserves, monthly audits, yield ban; compliance deadline 2027-01-18 — RLUSD/PYUSD qualify directly) de-risks banks touching stablecoins; (ii) a wave of banks launched custody in 2026 (BNY, Citi, Absa, StanChart's own Singapore/UAE/Lux/HK footprint). The real prize here is the LMAX "collateral mirroring" leg — RLUSD as eligible collateral whose value is mirrored to the venue without moving the underlying — i.e. capital-efficiency, not storage.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: assets-under-custody (AUC), number of supported assets/chains, jurisdictions/licences, and — for the collateral angle — eligible-collateral breadth. Key players & basis of competition: G-SIBs competing on licences + trust (StanChart, BNY, Citi); pure-plays on tech/breadth (BitGo, Fireblocks). Recent moves: BNY added USDC mint/burn/custody on its Digital Asset Custody platform and expanded in the EU under MiCA (2026-10) [[BNY expands digital asset custody in EU under MiCA]]; Citi extended Citi Token Services into Japan+UAE (2026-10) [[Citi expands Citi Token Services into Japan and UAE]] and targets Bitcoin custody via "Custody+" by end-2026 (per CNBC); Absa launched digital-asset custody across Africa (2026-10) [[Absa launches digital asset custody across Africa]]. StanChart's position: **ahead among G-SIBs** — multi-jurisdiction custody live (UAE, Luxembourg, HK; Singapore by YE2026) plus the Zodia asset it incubated (2020, w/ Northern Trust; non-binding offer accepted May 2026, Luxembourg PI licence June 2026 for EU stablecoin custody). Moat `(analysis)`: licence stack + institutional relationships (switching costs), not proprietary tech.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/revenue in this news — it is a product launch, so **trading multiples = no data / not applicable**. Qualitative internal comps (same bank-custody wave, Oct 2026): [[BNY expands digital asset custody in EU under MiCA]], [[Citi expands Citi Token Services into Japan and UAE]], [[Absa launches digital asset custody across Africa]], [[BNY Mellon reportedly adds XRP to corporate payments roadmap]]. Distinction vs peers: BNY/Citi have anchored on USDC; StanChart is the first major G-SIB to custody **RLUSD specifically** — a bet on Ripple/XRPL rails over Circle's. StanChart Group market cap / P/E `[UNSOURCED]` (not computed — immaterial to a single-product launch).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Announcement vs. live — the economic leg is NOT live.** Custody is live (DIFC); the value-driving LMAX collateral-mirroring integration is explicitly "subject to final documentation, testing, regulatory requirements and internal approvals, with no launch date" — the headline over-states what customers can actually use.
2. **RLUSD scale/concentration risk.** RLUSD is ~$2.1–2.5bn mcap, ~0.5% of the ~$300bn+ stablecoin market, dwarfed by USDT (~59%) and USDC (~24%); custodying a sub-1%-share, single-issuer coin tied to XRPL concentrates StanChart's offering on Ripple's adoption curve — second-order: limited near-term AUC and fee upside.
3. **Single-jurisdiction gating.** Live only via the DIFC branch under DFSA; cross-border institutional use needs per-jurisdiction rollout (the same friction that makes the segment a licence race) — expansion risk, not a given.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Signals the custody battle is moving from "do you hold the coin" to "can the coin be put to work as collateral without leaving custody" — mirroring is the differentiator. Falsifiable thesis: if the LMAX leg goes live with real eligible-collateral volumes in H1 2027, StanChart leapfrogs BNY/Citi on institutional stablecoin capital-efficiency; **what breaks it** — the integration stays "planned" past mid-2027, or RLUSD fails to grow past its sub-1% niche, leaving this a PR-grade custody checkbox rather than a revenue line. Trigger to watch: a dated LMAX go-live + RLUSD XRPL supply breaking meaningfully above ~$1.1bn.
