 Visa and the Singapore Tourism Board (STB) have signed a three-year MoU to promote high-value tourism, with Visa Destinations set to launch in Singapore in November 2026.
Singapore will be the programme’s second Asia Pacific market after Thailand, which introduced it in July.
The partnership covers three areas: developing tourism experiences, using spending insights to identify high-value visitors, and running joint marketing campaigns.
 Visa cardholder benefits across attractions and nightlife 
Visa and STB will introduce cardholder benefits across attractions, dining, entertainment, retail and tours, building on existing partnerships with Pelago by Singapore Airlines and Grab.
Repeat visitors spend approximately four times more than first-time visitors.
During the 2026 Singapore Grand Prix race week, restaurant and entertainment spending increased by around two-thirds.
Spending between 7pm and 11pm rose by almost half, while spending between 11pm and 5am nearly doubled.
 Payment data to guide tourism campaigns 
Visa identified Australia, Indonesia, Mainland China, Malaysia and the United States among Singapore’s largest inbound travel markets for its cardholders.
Both organisations will use market insights to identify high-value tourism opportunities and develop campaigns under Visa Destinations and STB’s Passion Made Possible brand.
 “Our partnership with the Singapore Tourism Board is about converting that appeal into deeper engagement,” 
said Adeline Kim, Group Country Manager, Regional Southeast Asia, and SVP, Global Clients, Asia Pacific at Visa.
 “Through this collaboration, we aim to create more opportunities for our tourism businesses and advance STB’s Tourism 2040 priorities through impactful, high-quality tourism growth,” 
said Melissa Ow, Chief Executive, Singapore Tourism Board.
The partnership will also support STB’s Tourism 2040 priorities.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Visa and the Singapore Tourism Board (STB) signed a **three-year MoU** (a non-binding memorandum, not a commercial contract with disclosed money terms) to promote "high-value tourism." The operational deliverable: **Visa Destinations launches in Singapore in November 2026**, making Singapore the **second Asia-Pacific market** for the programme after **Thailand** (which launched in July 2026 with the Tourism Authority of Thailand). Three workstreams: (1) develop/curate tourism experiences, (2) use Visa's card-spend insights to identify high-value visitor segments, (3) run joint co-branded marketing (Visa Destinations × STB's "Passion Made Possible" brand). Cardholder perks will span attractions, dining, entertainment, retail and tours, building on Visa's existing tie-ups with **Pelago by Singapore Airlines** and **Grab**. No fee, revenue-share, GMV, or volume commitment was disclosed — this is a marketing/data-sharing framework, not a payments-rails or acceptance deal per se.

## [1] Why it matters / what's the real mechanism
This is a textbook move in Visa's **"own demand above the point of payment"** strategy (see [[Analysis Visa pushes into lifestyle travel to own demand pre-payment]]). Visa Destinations is a mobile-first discovery/recommendations layer ([[Visa launches Visa Destinations travel recommendations service]], [[Visa launches travel platform across 10 global destinations]]) whose commercial logic is: insert Visa earlier in the trip-planning funnel so the card stays the default rail as payments become invisible, give issuers a differentiated benefit, and drive merchant traffic. Partnering a **national tourism board** adds a government-backed distribution and data channel: STB gets Visa's anonymized cross-border spend telemetry (which cohorts spend, where, when) to target campaigns; Visa gets brand placement inside Singapore's official tourism marketing and a reference win in a wealthy, high-throughput APAC hub.

## [2] Numbers / magnitude (verify)
- **Repeat visitors spend ~4x first-timers** (Visa's own stat; the headline commercial rationale).
- **2026 Singapore F1 Grand Prix week**: restaurant/entertainment spend up ~two-thirds; 7–11pm spend +~half; 11pm–5am spend nearly doubled (Visa data — supports the "night economy / high-value" framing).
- **Top inbound markets for Visa cardholders**: Australia, Indonesia, Mainland China, Malaysia, United States.
- **Singapore tourism context (external)**: 2025 = **16.9M visitors**, **record S$32.8B tourism receipts**; STB guides **17–18M arrivals and ~S$31–32.5B receipts for 2026**. Top receipt markets 2025: Mainland China (~S$3.68B), Indonesia (~S$2.09B), Australia (~S$1.54B) — closely matching Visa's cardholder list. **Tourism 2040** roadmap targets **S$47–50B** receipts, backed by an extra S$740M Tourism Development Fund over five years.

## [3] Who's affected / competitive angle
- **Visa**: deepens the Destinations footprint (also live/announced in London, New York, Paris, Thailand) and locks in a government partner. Low-cost, high-optics.
- **STB**: data-driven targeting of high-yield segments, supports Tourism 2040 and MICE/night-economy goals.
- **Merchants / existing partners**: Pelago (Singapore Airlines), Grab benefit from funnel traffic.
- **Competitors**: Mastercard runs parallel travel/lifestyle plays (e.g. [[Mastercard expands airport Fast Track to Madrid and Barcelona]]); Amex is the incumbent in premium travel perks. A tourism-board MoU is cheap turf Visa can plant before rivals.

## [4] Open questions
- Is there ANY money attached (campaign co-funding, data-licensing fee), or is it purely in-kind marketing? Not disclosed.
- Does "spending insights" imply a formal data-sharing/analytics product (Visa Consulting & Analytics) or just ad-hoc campaign inputs?
- Does Singapore get the full merchant-acceptance/digital-payments-enablement angle that Thailand's version emphasized (helping SMEs accept cards), or only the discovery/perks layer?
- Attribution: how will either party measure that incremental high-value spend came from this MoU vs. secular tourism recovery?

New market for the programme, but the programme itself is old news ([[Visa launches Visa Destinations travel recommendations service]], [[Visa launches travel platform across 10 global destinations]]). Incremental, not novel.
3. Are the spend stats (4x repeat, F1 night-economy jumps) independently verifiable, or Visa-supplied marketing figures? All are Visa's own data with no methodology or sample disclosed — treat as directional PR, not audited stats.
4. Does "payment data to guide campaigns" raise privacy/data-governance questions? Visa frames it as aggregated/anonymized insights, but the note doesn't specify consent, PDPA compliance, or data-sharing scope.
5. Could this be double-counted against the Thailand/Dubai/global launch notes? No — distinct market MoU; but importance should not inflate given the near-identical playbook already logged.

7. What does STB actually give Visa — budget, brand, or just a logo? Co-branded marketing under "Passion Made Possible" plus official-channel distribution; no funding disclosed.
8. Is the F1 Grand Prix stat cherry-picked? Race week is an extreme-outlier event; using it to justify a year-round "high-value tourism" thesis is selective.

15. Freshness/duplicate check: fresh — distinct, dated (2026-10-23/26) market MoU; related to but not a duplicate of the global/Thailand launch notes.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits in the card-network tourism-data vertical: networks (Visa, Mastercard) monetising aggregated cardholder spend data as a marketing/loyalty asset for destinations, not just moving transactions. Market "why now" is pointed: Singapore forecasts 17–18m visitor arrivals and S$31.0–32.5bn tourism receipts in 2026 — more arrivals but softer spend vs the record S$32.8bn in 2025, with Q2 2026 receipts down 6.2% YoY to S$7.21bn (per STB/Travel And Tour World, Travelandtourworld, 2026). That spend softening is exactly why STB is leaning into "high-value tourism" and Tourism 2040's S$47–50bn-by-2040 receipts target: when headcount growth alone won't lift receipts, per-visitor yield (dining, nightlife, attractions) becomes the lever — and that is a data problem Visa's cross-border spend footprint can address. The note's own datapoints (repeat visitors spend ~4x first-timers; Grand Prix week dining/entertainment +~two-thirds; 11pm–5am spend nearly doubled) are the proof-of-concept for selling spend-insight-driven targeting.

**Competitive landscape.** Network KPIs: cross-border volume growth, payments volume, processed transactions, and (for travel specifically) travel-related cross-border volume. Visa reported fiscal Q3 2026 (to 30 Jun 2026) net revenue $11.6bn +14%, cross-border volume ex-Europe +12% including +10% travel-related cross-border (per MarketBeat/Yahoo on Visa Q3 FY26, 2026-07-28); FY26 Q4/full-year was due 2026-10-27 (per Visa newsroom). This is a classic Visa "data as marketing" play — the direct comp is Mastercard, whose equivalent is its Economics Institute / Tourism Insights and loyalty-data partnerships; Mastercard reported +12% cross-border volume in Q2 2026 (per Tickeron/AlphaSpread, 2026). American Express competes on the premium-traveller segment but with a closed loop. Basis of competition here is data breadth + destination-marketing distribution, not price. Visa's position: incumbent-ahead — Visa Destinations is now rolling to Singapore as its second APAC market after Thailand (July), i.e. a replicable productised programme, not a one-off. Moat is network-scale data (intangible: spend visibility across inbound source markets Visa named — Australia, Indonesia, Mainland China, Malaysia, US) `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** The two listed pure-play networks:
- **Visa (V):** market cap ~$700–757bn (gurufocus/stockanalysis, Oct 2026); P/E ~31.5x (macrotrends, 2026-10-16); TTM EV/Sales ~15.4x (financecharts). Arithmetic check vs FY run-rate: annualising Q3 FY26 net revenue $11.6bn → ~$46bn → EV/Rev ≈ $700bn / $46bn ≈ 15x, consistent with the quoted TTM figure.
- **Mastercard (MA):** market cap ~$546bn, EV ~$560bn; P/E 34.7x (stockrow, 2026-10-22); EV/Sales ~15.8x on ~$35.1bn revenue ($560bn / $35.1bn = 16.0x) (AlphaSpread/companiesmarketcap/stockrow, 2026).
Both trade in-line with each other (~15–16x EV/Sales, ~31–35x P/E) — rich on an absolute basis but normal for a duopoly with mid-teens revenue growth and high margins (growth justifies the multiple; not an outlier flag). American Express is not a clean comp (lender + closed loop) → excluded. Internal comps for card-network / travel-payments partnerships: [[Worldline and YeePay partner on China cross-border travel payments]], [[Getnet and UnionPay expand acceptance in Spain and Portugal]], [[Checkout.com and Visa partner to launch card issuing]]. This deal carries no disclosed economics, so a deal-specific multiple = no data.

**Risk flags.**
1. Immaterial to the P&L — this is a 3-year MoU with no committed spend disclosed; at Visa's ~$46bn revenue run-rate, one destination-marketing partnership is a rounding error. Read it as brand/data-moat positioning, not a revenue event. The "who's silent": no fee or revenue-share terms disclosed.
2. Demand backdrop is weakening, not strengthening — Singapore receipts fell 6.2% YoY in Q2 2026 even as arrivals grew; the partnership's thesis (convert appeal into higher per-visitor spend) is being launched into a yield-compression environment, so execution risk is real and the payoff is unproven.
3. Data/privacy and regulatory exposure — monetising aggregated cardholder spend for destination targeting invites scrutiny (consent, cross-border data flows across the named source markets, incl. Mainland China); a tightening here would blunt the core value proposition.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Signals the networks' pivot from moving money to selling the data exhaust — destination tourism boards become a new B2B2C channel for Visa/Mastercard value-added services (the higher-margin, faster-growing segment both tout). Falsifiable thesis: if Visa Destinations expands to a 3rd+ APAC market within ~12 months on similar terms, it confirms a scalable VAS product line; if it stalls at Thailand+Singapore with no disclosed uptake metrics, it's marketing optics. Watch Visa's FY26 Q4 print (2026-10-27) for value-added-services revenue growth and any Destinations/tourism-insight callouts.

The relevant financial context is Visa's most recently filed quarter.

**Latest reported quarter: fiscal Q3 2026 (ended June 30, 2026; reported July 28, 2026) — a beat.**

- **Net revenue:** $11.6B, up 14% (13% constant-dollar).
- **GAAP net income:** $5.6B, up 7%; **GAAP EPS $2.97**, up 10%.
- **Non-GAAP net income:** $6.3B, up 8%; **non-GAAP EPS $3.32**, up 11% — vs. Street consensus ~$3.22, a ~$0.10 (~3%) beat. Revenue of $11.6B also topped the ~$11.38B consensus (~1.9% beat).
- **Payments volume:** +10% YoY constant-dollar, crossing **$4 trillion** for the first time in Visa's history in a single quarter.
- **Processed transactions:** 71.7B, +10% YoY.
- **Value-added services revenue:** +34% YoY.

**Directly relevant to the partnership — cross-border/travel:**

- **Cross-border volume +13% total / +12% ex-intra-Europe** (constant-dollar) in Q3 FY2026. This accelerated from Q2 FY2026's **+12% total / +11% ex-intra-Europe**, signalling resilient international travel and e-commerce spend — the exact volume Visa Destinations programs (Thailand, now Singapore) are designed to capture and grow.

**Sequential/prior-period comparison (Q2 FY2026, ended Mar 31, 2026):** net revenue $11.2B (+17%); GAAP EPS $3.14 (+36%); non-GAAP EPS $3.31 (+20%); payments volume +9%; processed transactions 66.1B (+9%). So Q3 showed steady double-digit revenue and continued cross-border acceleration.

**Guidance:** On the Q3 call (CFO Chris Suh) Visa raised its full-year FY2026 outlook — revenue growth to the "low end of the low teens" and EPS growth to the "low end of the mid-teens." The Q3 8-K earnings release itself carried no numeric guidance.

**Thesis flags for this story:** Cross-border is Visa's highest-yield revenue driver, and it is accelerating (+13%). Destinations-type programs (loyalty perks across dining, attractions, retail) are a low-cost way to steer high-value inbound spend — Singapore note cites repeat visitors spending ~4x first-timers and sharp late-night spend lifts during the 2026 Grand Prix. Partnership scale is modest relative to Visa's $4T/quarter volume, so impact is strategic/data-driven (spending insights, campaign targeting) rather than materially moving the P&L near-term. Watch Visa's fiscal Q4/FY2026 results (late October 2026) for whether Asia-Pacific cross-border momentum holds.
