## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Zilch, the UK consumer BNPL lender, has **invited investment banks to pitch for roles** on a planned flotation (per the Financial Times, Oct 2026). Key de-PR'd facts:
- **Stage:** banker *selection/pitch* process — underwriters are NOT yet mandated. This is the earliest formal step of IPO prep, not a filing, price range or roadshow.
- **Timeline:** most likely H2 2027 or early 2028 ("as soon as next year" in optimistic framing). Earlier Zilch messaging pointed to a **2026** IPO ([[Zilch raises over $175M ahead of potential IPO]]), so the window has quietly **slipped ~1–2 years**.
- **Venue:** London is favoured; New York "not ruled out." Belamant had publicly reaffirmed London before ([[Zilch eyes hot fintech IPO market amid product launch]]).
- **Valuation:** last private mark was **$2B** (held since a 2021 round, reaffirmed in the Nov 2025 raise). **Two sources close to the talks say an IPO is unlikely to reach $2B** — the single most important de-PR'd fact, buried under the "calls in bankers" headline.
- **Financials cited:** FY to Mar 2025 — revenue **~£110.3M (+93%)**, pre-tax loss cut **79% to £10.5M** (from ~£50M). ~5M customers ([[Zilch doubles revenue and reaches 5 million customers]]). Belamant wants the business profitable *before* listing.

**Why framed this way:** "inviting banks to pitch" lets Zilch signal momentum and test appetite without committing to a price. The PR anchors on the flattering loss-reduction and revenue-growth figures and on the legacy $2B number, while the genuinely new information — a slipped timeline and insider doubt about the valuation — is the real signal. Inviting pitches is cheap and reversible; it is a long way from a priced book.

## [1] Competitors / peers
BNPL public-market comps, and the reason this news is a hard sell:
- **Klarna** — IPO'd NYSE Sept 2025, priced $40 / ~$15B ([[Klarna prices IPO at $40, debuts on NYSE near $15B]], [[Klarna targets $13 billion pre-money valuation in IPO]]). Since then the stock is **down ~55% YTD (2026)**, trading ~$14, after a guidance cut (2026 revenue trimmed to $4.08–4.16B on weak German spend). Klarna's rocky debut is explicitly cited as having made the **whole BNPL category a harder sell**.
- **Affirm** — ~$24B market cap (Oct 2026, ~$74), recently hit GAAP profitability; relatively flat YTD vs Klarna's slide — the market now rewards *profitability*, not growth.
- **PayPal** — incumbent BNPL at scale; stable.
- **Chime** (consumer fintech, not BNPL) IPO'd well in 2025 — shows the IPO window *was* open for profitable-ish consumer fintech, but BNPL specifically re-rated down.

**Position:** Zilch is a **follower**, roughly 2 years behind Klarna/Affirm into public markets, entering a window that has *closed* for BNPL multiples precisely because the leaders disappointed. At ~£110M (~$140–150M) revenue, Zilch is an order of magnitude smaller than Klarna/Affirm. **Why the gap matters:** the $2B last-round mark implies ~13x revenue — a growth multiple from the 2021 ZIRP era; public BNPL now trades far tighter. The insider doubt is simply the gap between a 2021 private multiple and 2026 public comps.

## [2] Company history / fit
Trajectory: founded 2018 (Philip Belamant); Visa physical card 2025 ([[Zilch and Visa launch physical BNPL card]]); FCA payments licence Dec 2025 ([[Zilch secures FCA payments license]]); EU licence via Fjord Bank (Lithuania) acquisition 2026 ([[Zilch acquires Lithuania's Fjord Bank for EU license]]); governance build-out — ex-Microsoft UK chief Dame Clare Barclay to the board ([[Zilch appoints Microsoft veteran Dame Clare Barclay to board]]), new CCAO ([[Zilch appoints Florence Quirici as Chief Corporate Affairs Officer]]); Arsenal FC partnership for brand ([[Zilch becomes official payment partner of Arsenal FC]]); retail-media / ad-network build ([[Zilch builds retail media network with Intelligent Commerce]]); $175M+ raise Nov 2025 ([[Zilch raises over $175M ahead of potential IPO]]).

**Why it acts this way (analysis):** every move over the past 18 months — licences, board of blue-chip names, brand spend, a retail-media revenue line — reads as **IPO scaffolding**: de-risking the regulatory story, adding a higher-margin (ad/data) revenue narrative on top of a commodity BNPL take-rate, and building the governance a listed company needs. The banker-pitch step is the logical next rung. The slipped timeline (2026 → 2027/28) shows reality (BNPL re-rating + "profitable before listing" goal) overriding the earlier ambition.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Really new here?** Only the *process step* (banks invited) + the *insider valuation doubt* + the *revised timeline*. The IPO intent itself is ~1 year old. So novelty is **incremental**, not a new event.
- **Traction (real numbers):** revenue +93% to £110.3M, loss cut 79% to £10.5M, ~5M customers — genuinely improving unit economics, and the loss narrowing is the credible part of the story (path to the stated "profitable before IPO").
- **Why the value-add is fragile (analysis):** Zilch's core is a commodity BNPL take-rate — the exact model the market just punished in Klarna. The differentiated piece is the **ad-funded / retail-media model** (merchant-funded commission + a data/ad network), which *could* earn a software-like multiple. But that line is unproven at scale, and **who captures the margin** in BNPL is contested: card networks (Visa — Zilch's own partner) and merchants hold leverage. The question shifts from "will Zilch IPO?" to **"can it convince public markets it's an ad-tech/data business, not a sub-scale BNPL lender, in a tape where BNPL multiples have collapsed?"**

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Next concrete milestones to watch: **mandated underwriters** (vs today's pitch), confirmed **venue** (London vs NY — a live political question for LSE, which badly wants fintech listings), **FY Mar 2026/27 results** showing actual profitability, and any pre-IPO crossover round that would reset the private mark.
- **Sentiment:** cautious-to-skeptical. The dominant market read is that the $2B tag won't survive contact with public BNPL comps; a **down-round IPO** relative to the last private mark is plausible.
- **Second-order (analysis):** a London listing would be a symbolic win for the LSE's fintech ambitions regardless of size — so there may be *venue* pressure/incentives that don't map to *valuation* reality. Conversely, if BNPL stocks stay depressed, "inviting banks to pitch" could quietly lapse again, as the 2026 target did. The news is best read as a **soft market test**, not a commitment.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** BNPL is a large but methodology-split market: 2026 estimates range from ~$166bn to ~$750bn in size, with transaction volume commonly cited around $576–647bn for 2026 and ~$1tn forecast by 2031 (per ResearchAndMarkets / GlobeNewswire syntheses, as of 2026 — wide dispersion, so treat any single TAM as indicative, not precise). CAGR cited ~16–22%. Structure: still fragmented at the long tail but consolidating at the top around two scaled, now-listed/profitable leaders (Klarna, Affirm) plus regional champions (Tabby/Tamara in MEA, Zip in APAC/US, Sezzle in US). Barriers are rising and now capital- and funding-driven: BNPL is a balance-sheet lending business, so access to cheap securitisation/warehouse funding (Zilch's Deutsche Bank-led £150m facility) and credit-loss underwriting are the real moats, not the checkout button. Why now: Klarna's Sep-2025 NYSE debut re-opened the BNPL IPO window; Zilch is explicitly timing a H2-2027/early-2028 float to that re-rating, and CEO Belamant has repeatedly said the listing is "inspired by Klarna's strong debut" ([[Zilch eyes hot fintech IPO market amid product launch]]).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: GMV/sales volume, take rate (revenue/GMV), net credit-loss rate, and path-to-profit (adj. operating margin). Zilch's own KPIs (FY y/e 31 Mar 2025): revenue £110.3m (+93%), GMV £1.9bn (+73%) → implied take rate ≈ £110.3m / £1,900m = 5.8% (analysis; richer than classic BNPL ~2–4% because of ad-subsidised "commerce media" model, transaction+ad revenue +81% to £31.5m); gross margin 49%; pre-tax loss narrowed 79% to £10.5m; 5m+ customers. Basis of competition: distribution (merchant network + card rails) and funding cost, increasingly layered with retail-media/ad monetisation. Recent peer moves: Klarna Q1-2026 revenue $1.0bn (+44%), GMV $33.7bn, 119m consumers, adj. op profit $68m ([[Klarna reports Q1 2026 revenue up 44% to $1 billion]]); Tabby $4.5bn secondary valuation Oct-2025, up from $3.3bn eight months prior ([[Gulf Startup Tabby nabs $4.5 billion valuation in secondary sale]]); Zip +38.7% transaction volume, +32.8% revenue Q1-FY26 with buyback ([[Zip shares jump 12% on strong update and buyback]]). Zilch's position: niche/sub-scale vs Klarna/Affirm (GMV £1.9bn vs Klarna's $33.7bn per quarter — roughly 15–20x smaller), but growing faster off a low base and differentiated by the ad-funded, zero-merchant-fee model (analysis). Moat = funding access + ad monetisation; weak on scale and brand reach vs the leaders.

**Comps & multiples.** Public anchors (as of Oct 2026, via multiples.vc / companiesmarketcap / stockanalysis):
- Klarna (KLAR): mkt cap ~$5.0bn, TTM revenue ~$4.0bn → EV/Revenue ≈ $5.0bn / $4.0bn = ~1.2x (one source cites ~0.9x on $4b/$6bn mkt-cap basis; another ~1.9x EV/Rev). Range ~1–2x — a sharply de-rated leader (stock fell from a ~$40 debut toward ~$13).
- Affirm: mkt cap ~$14–16bn; cited ~5x EV/Revenue; priced at ~27x adj. op profit vs Klarna's ~16x.
- Tabby (private): $4.5bn round valuation (Oct-2025) — valuation, not market cap; revenue not public, so EV/Rev = no data.
- Zilch (private): last equity valuation ~£1.45bn (Jan-2025); 2021 peak $2bn. On FY2025 revenue £110.3m → implied P/S ≈ £1,450m / £110.3m = ~13.1x (analysis; private round pricing, not a market multiple). That is far above Klarna's ~1–2x and above Affirm's ~5x. The gap is only partly defensible by growth (+93% rev vs Klarna +44%): classic growth-multiple logic says faster growers earn richer multiples, but a ~13x private mark into a market where the scaled, profitable leader trades at ~1–2x is a clear re-pricing risk at IPO. Distribution across 3 public-ish names: Min ~1x, Median ~5x, Max ~13x (Zilch private) — not a clean comp set; qualitative read: Zilch looks RICH vs listed BNPL on last-round pricing.

**Risk flags.**
1. **IPO re-rating / valuation gap** — Zilch's last private mark (~£1.45bn, ~13x sales) sits well above Klarna's ~1–2x EV/Rev public multiple; unless Klarna/Affirm re-rate upward before H2-2027, a float risks printing below the last round (down-round-on-listing), the same trajectory Klarna's own stock took post-IPO.
2. **Credit-loss / funding dependence** — the business runs on securitisation (Deutsche Bank facility) and absorbs rising credit-loss provisions as GMV scales; a funding-cost spike or consumer-credit deterioration compresses the thin ~£10.5m-loss path-to-profit and hits the lending engine directly, not just the product.
3. **Sub-scale vs the leaders + timing risk** — at £1.9bn GMV Zilch is ~15–20x smaller than Klarna per quarter; the window is being set by a peer's debut (Klarna), so a soft listing market or a weak Klarna/Affirm tape in 2027 removes the "why now," and the London-vs-NY destination is still undecided (liquidity/valuation discount risk for a London listing).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** The invite-banks step is a signal, not a listing — it says the BNPL IPO cohort (Klarna done, Zilch, possibly Tabby/Tamara) is forming, and the sector's next re-rating will be set by whether Klarna/Affirm hold their multiples into 2027 (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: Zilch floats at or below its last ~£1.45bn private mark unless (trigger) Klarna's EV/Rev re-rates back above ~3–4x or Zilch reaches full-year statutory profit at materially higher GMV first; sustained Klarna weakness or a credit-loss spike would confirm the down-mark; a Klarna/Affirm re-rate + Zilch profitability print would break it.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (IPO-readiness read, no fresh print in the news).** The news item is an IPO-mandate story, not an earnings release — there is no new results print. But Zilch is IR-covered, and its most recent audited filing (Annual Report FY2025, year ended 31 March 2025, published 2025-07-25) gives the actuals that matter for an IPO-grade read: **revenue £110.3m (+93% YoY), GMV £1.9bn (+73% YoY), statutory net loss £10.5m (-79% YoY).** Direction: strongly improving — revenue near-doubling while losses shrink ~5x. Still loss-making on a statutory basis, so "IPO-grade financials" are a trajectory, not yet a destination.

**Key figures (Zilch's own FY2025 audited accounts, y/e 31 Mar 2025).**
- Revenue: **£110.3m, +93% YoY** (near-doubling).
- Transaction & advertising revenue: **£31.5m, +81% YoY** (the ad-subsidised network line — the strategic monetisation engine).
- GMV: **£1.9bn, +73% YoY**.
- Statutory net loss: **£10.5m, down 79% YoY** (a ~5x reduction in losses).
- Gross profit margin: **49% in FY2025, up ~10 ppts from FY2024**.
- Provision for credit losses: **£27.4m, more than doubled YoY** — the figure management is quietest about (see thesis-flags).
- Customers: **>5m** (crossed the 5m mark; FY2024 base was ~4.5m at Dec-2024).
- Average order frequency: **57x in FY2025 vs 51x in FY2024** — engagement rising, supports the ad-network flywheel.

**By segment / driver.** Growth is led by the transaction-and-advertising revenue line (+81%), i.e. the ad-subsidised monetisation model, not just lending spread. Margin expansion (49% gross) + frequency (57x) + loss reduction (-79%) together are the "scaling into profitability" story. The drag is credit cost: loan-loss provisions more than doubling to £27.4m as the book grows.

**vs expectations / prior period.** No equity consensus exists — Zilch is private (UK, not EDGAR), so there is no Street number; beat/miss is framed qualitatively vs prior year. **vs FY2024:** revenue +93%, GMV +73%, losses -79% — unambiguous acceleration on every top-line metric. Caveat on the prior-year narrative: the much-cited FY2024 "$130m revenue / surges to profitability" (businesswire, 2024-09-02) was a **July-2024 annualised run-rate / milestone claim**, not the audited y/e Mar-2024 statutory figure; the audited FY2025 accounts still show a statutory net loss of £10.5m. So "already profitable" (PR framing) vs "still loss-making statutorily, but closing fast" (filing framing) — de-PR: the filing governs. No public FY2025-specific whisper or preview frame in the base.

**Guidance / forward.** No financial guidance — this is a private company; the only forward signal in the news is the **IPO timetable: flotation targeted H2 2027 or early 2028, London favoured (New York not ruled out)**, banks invited to pitch. Independent read (analysis): on current trajectory (revenue ~2x, losses -79%, margin 49%), a maiden statutory profit on the FY2026 or FY2027 accounts is plausible and would be the natural gating event for an IPO-grade track record — consistent with the H2-2027/2028 window. Funding runway is supported by the Nov-2025 **$175m+ raise** and a **£150m securitisation facility with Deutsche Bank** (2024) backing book growth.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. **Statutory profitability is the IPO gate, not yet cleared.** Fact: FY2025 still a £10.5m statutory loss despite +93% revenue. Why: ad-network economics + credit provisioning absorb the top-line. Why it matters: a London listing in H2-2027/28 realistically needs a clean statutory profit print first. Second-order: the IPO timing is effectively a bet that the loss curve crosses zero in FY2026/27 — any stall resets the window.
2. **Credit provisions more than doubled to £27.4m — the quiet line.** Why: book growth + a BNPL/consumer-credit cohort in a soft UK consumer environment. Why it matters: this is the one metric that can invert the margin story if loss rates rise faster than GMV. Second-order: UK BNPL regulation (FCA affordability rules phasing in) could force higher provisioning / lower approval rates right as Zilch wants clean pre-IPO numbers.
3. **Monetisation mix is the differentiator.** Transaction & advertising revenue +81% to £31.5m validates the ad-subsidised model vs pure-interest BNPL peers — a higher-quality, more defensible revenue story for IPO investors than Klarna/Affirm-style spread income. Second-order: if this line keeps outgrowing GMV, take-rate and margin compound favourably into the listing.
4. **Listing-venue optionality (London leaning, NY open).** De-PR: "London favoured" is a signal to UK policymakers as much as a decision; NY remains a live hedge if valuation/liquidity disappoints — watch for a venue flip, which would itself be a thesis tell on how FY2026/27 numbers land with UK investors.

Verdict label: **no-data on a fresh quarterly print** (IPO-mandate news, not an earnings release). Latest *audited* actuals (FY2025): strongly improving — revenue +93%, losses -79%, GMV +73%, but still statutory loss-making.
