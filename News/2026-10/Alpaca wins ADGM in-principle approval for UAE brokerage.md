## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On **2026-10-26** Alpaca announced that its new UAE entity, **Alpaca Clearing & Custody ME Ltd ("Alpaca Middle East")**, received **in-principle approval (IPA)** from the **Financial Services Regulatory Authority (FSRA)** of the **Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM)** for a **Financial Services Permission** covering **brokerage services and custody for virtual assets**. (Business Wire / alpaca.markets blog, 2026-10-26.)

**The load-bearing caveat the headline/digest buries:** an IPA is **not a licence**. Alpaca's own disclosure states the IPA **does NOT authorise it to conduct regulated activities in or from ADGM**; any launch is **conditional** on satisfying FSRA requirements and being granted the **full Financial Services Permission**. The exact scope ("within the scope ultimately approved by the FSRA") is **undefined until final permission**. So the correct read is: *Alpaca cleared the first gate of a multi-step licensing process* — a milestone, not go-live. The digest line "plans to offer brokerage and virtual asset custody after final permission" is accurate; "wins ADGM approval" in the title over-reads it.

**No numbers disclosed:** no capital committed, headcount, launch date, local capital requirement, or partner names for the UAE entity. (ADGM broker-dealer VA licences typically carry ~USD 250k base capital per secondary licensing sources — not confirmed for Alpaca.)

- **Brokerage-as-a-service infra peers** chasing the same embedded-investing layer: **DriveWealth** → [[OnePay chooses DriveWealth to power embedded investment platform]], [[DriveWealth CEO departs for ICE; Naureen Hassan succeeds him]], [[DriveWealth and Ripple partner on institutional digital asset investing]]; **Apex Fintech Solutions** → [[Apex Fintech Solutions debuts Apex AI Suite]]; **Upvest** → [[Quirin Privatbank and quirion migrate investment business to Upvest]].

The "custody for virtual assets" scope hints Alpaca wants to anchor its tokenized-equities/onchain roadmap in a credible crypto-licensing jurisdiction — but that is a plan contingent on the final permission, not a shipped capability.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
**Next gate:** satisfy FSRA conditions → full Financial Services Permission → only then can Alpaca Middle East conduct regulated brokerage/custody in/from ADGM. No timeline given (ADGM IPA→full-licence cycles commonly run several months to a year). **Watch:** the final *scope* granted (which activities, which assets), local capital commitment, and first announced UAE partner. **Risks:** (1) IPA can lapse or narrow — no guarantee of full permission; (2) scope undefined, so the headline promise is unpriced; (3) regional competition (DriveWealth/Apex and local players) for the same embedded-investing layer; (4) virtual-asset custody adds compliance/capital burden consistent with the debt-heavy balance-sheet build flagged in the Jul note. **Sentiment:** a credibility-positive but modest step — meaningful for Alpaca's global-standard narrative, immaterial to near-term financials.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector context.** Alpaca sells brokerage-as-a-service: API-driven trading, clearing, custody and self-clearing infrastructure that partner fintechs, banks and exchanges embed into their own apps. The ADGM in-principle approval (IPA) is a geographic-expansion move into the UAE, extending a regulatory footprint the company already holds across the US, UK, EU, Japan, India and The Bahamas, serving partners in 50+ countries. The pitch for MENA is structural: retail investing penetration is low (reporting cites only ~6% of UAE residents currently investing) against rising demand, GCC fintech projected to grow at a ~15% CAGR through 2030, and the UAE capturing a large share of MENA fintech funding. An infra player that lets local apps offer US equities/ETFs (plus virtual-asset custody) into that underpenetrated base is a plausible wedge.

**What the IPA actually is — read carefully.** This is not a license. ADGM's FSRA grants in-principle approval first, then issues a Financial Services Permission (FSP) only after the applicant meets conditions (legal entity setup via the Registration Authority, share capital deposit, local bank account, staffing/senior-management fitness, systems and controls). The IPA explicitly does NOT authorise Alpaca to conduct regulated activities in or from ADGM; any launch is conditional on the full permission. The applicant entity is Alpaca Clearing & Custody ME Ltd (dba "Alpaca Middle East"), which plans brokerage services and virtual-asset custody "within the scope ultimately approved." So headline framing of "wins approval for UAE brokerage" overstates it — the firm has cleared the first gate, not the finish line, and no go-live date is given.

**In-base comps (semsearch down → grep company/alpaca).** The vault shows a clear, consistent expansion-via-infrastructure pattern: $150M Series D at $1.15B (Jan 2026, 300+ partners / 40+ countries, Bank Muscat and Derayah Financial among Series D participants — i.e. Gulf strategics already on the cap table, which contextualizes this UAE push); closed acquisition of WealthKernel (Apr 2026) for licensed UK/EU brokerage+custody; $135M raise for agent-first/prime-brokerage infra (Jul 2026); plus distribution wins — BingX US stock trading via Alpaca (Oct 2026, 7,000+ stocks, USDT/USDC funding), trii in Chile (Mar 2026), Kraken Embed, SBI. Read together, ADGM is one more "acquire/obtain the local license, then onboard partners" step — consistent with strategy, not a new business line. Revenue reported as more than doubling YoY at the Series D, but Alpaca is private and unaudited; no UAE revenue guidance exists.

**Competitive landscape.** In the same embedded-investing / brokerage-as-a-service category Alpaca competes with Apex Fintech, DriveWealth, Upvest and Lemon.markets (EU/MiFID II), and WealthKernel (now owned). The sharper UAE-specific threat is on the demand side: Robinhood has already secured DFSA authorisation (Dubai/DIFC) to launch direct retail investing in the Middle East with a stated 2027 target across 10 asset classes including equities, crypto and tokens. That matters because Alpaca's model depends on partner apps; if large consumer brands enter the UAE directly (or build in-house), the pool of partners needing Alpaca's rails is contested. ADGM (Abu Dhabi) vs DIFC (Dubai) also means Alpaca and Robinhood are, for now, in different free-zone regulators.

**Risk flags.**
1. **IPA ≠ FSP / no revenue yet.** Approval is preliminary and conditional; regulated activity is not authorised, no launch date, and conditions (capital — FSRA typically wants ~6 months of projected opex — staffing, controls) could slip or fail. Treat any UAE P&L contribution as speculative.
2. **Headline overstatement.** "Wins ADGM approval for UAE brokerage" / virtual-asset custody framing risks being read as a live license; the note's own aggregated text ("after final permission") is more accurate than the title.
3. **Demand-side competition from direct players.** Robinhood (DFSA/DIFC, 2027) and incumbent UAE brokers can bypass or shrink the partner-channel Alpaca sells into.
4. **Private/unaudited financials.** "Revenue doubled YoY," $1.15B valuation and partner/country counts are company-reported; no independent verification and the $135M mid-2026 raise so soon after a $150M Series D invites questions on burn/capital intensity of a multi-jurisdiction licensing strategy.
5. **Regulatory/VASP scope risk.** Virtual-asset custody is the regulator-sensitive piece; FSRA could approve a narrower scope than planned, and crypto-custody rules in ADGM are stringent.
