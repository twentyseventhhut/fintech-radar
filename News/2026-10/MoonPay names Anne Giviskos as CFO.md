Former Navan, NYSE and Euronext executive joins as MoonPay scales across financial markets 
 NEW YORK , Oct. 13, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- MoonPay , the global financial technology company powering the movement of value across fiat and digital assets, today named Anne Giviskos as Chief Financial Officer. Anne most recently served as Interim CFO and Chief Accounting Officer at AI-powered business travel and expense platform Navan.
The appointment comes as MoonPay continues to build the operating system for value. In recent months, the company launched MoonPay Trade , giving applications and institutions access to more than 200 blockchains and protocols through a single API. It also acquired  DFlow, a Solana trading infrastructure platform that has processed $45 billion in volume so far in 2026. DFlow transactions appear in more than 95% of Solana blocks daily, and MoonPay's trading infrastructure now powers some of the largest crypto platforms, enabling Solana trades for Coinbase and cross-chain swaps in Ledger Wallet . The company also introduced PayBox , expanding its agentic finance offerings. In September, MoonPay entered into an agreement  to acquire North Capital, expanding into onchain capital markets.
"Anne knows how to help ambitious companies navigate defining moments," said Ivan Soto-Wright, founder and CEO of MoonPay. "She brings experience from public markets and fast-growing technology companies, along with the judgment and discipline to help MoonPay expand responsibly across payments, stablecoins and financial markets."
With over two decades building and safeguarding the infrastructure of traditional markets, Anne brings deep expertise in scaling regulated financial businesses and integrating acquisitions. As CFO, Anne will lead the finance organization, set financial and capital strategy and build the infrastructure to support MoonPay's next phase of growth.
Anne joins an expanding leadership team. MoonPay recently welcomed board members and advisors  with experience spanning Wall Street, national security, Silicon Valley and digital assets.
"The way money moves is being rebuilt and MoonPay sits at the center of that shift, with the products, partners and global reach to lead it," said Anne Giviskos. "I'm thrilled to join the team as MoonPay scales into the company this moment calls for."
 About MoonPay 
MoonPay is a global financial technology company that provides infrastructure for moving value across traditional and digital financial systems. Founded in 2019, MoonPay serves more than 35 million customers in 180 countries and more than 1500 enterprise clients across crypto and fintech . Its products support payments, trading, commerce, stablecoin infrastructure and other services that connect conventional payment networks with blockchain-based financial rails. MoonPay maintains regulatory licenses and authorizations in multiple jurisdictions, including the United States and European Union. For more information, visit www.moonpay.com .

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-10-13 MoonPay named **Anne Giviskos** as its Chief Financial Officer (titled CFO for Global Business). Giviskos most recently served as Interim CFO and Chief Accounting Officer at business-travel/expense platform Navan, and brings ~two decades in regulated-markets finance — roughly a decade across the New York Stock Exchange and Euronext. At MoonPay she will lead the finance organisation, set financial and capital strategy, and "build the infrastructure to support MoonPay's next phase of growth." A separate interim CFO (Mouna Ammari Siala) reportedly continues to cover US operations.

**[1] Why it matters / significance.** The hire is a textbook "institutionalise the finance function" move at a crypto-fintech that has spent 2026 aggressively building an infrastructure stack and an institutional business. The profile — NYSE/Euronext exchange operator + the interim CFO who sat through a public-company travel platform — is squarely a public-markets-readiness profile, not a growth-stage bookkeeping hire. It signals MoonPay is professionalising capital strategy, acquisition integration, and regulated-business scaling ahead of a potential capital event (IPO or a large strategic round such as the reported ICE investment).

**[2] Internal context (prior MoonPay trajectory — cite).** MoonPay (founded 2019, ~35M customers / 1,500 enterprise clients / 180 countries) has executed a dense M&A + product run building "the operating system for value":
- [[MoonPay acquires crypto security startup Sodot for $100 million]] (2026-05) — basis for **MoonPay Institutional** (banks/asset managers), led by former CFTC Acting Chair Caroline Pham.
- [[MoonPay acquires DFlow for on-chain trading infrastructure]] (~$100M all-stock, 2026-05) — Solana execution layer cited in the CFO release as $45B 2026 volume.
- [[MoonPay launches MoonPay Trade digital asset platform]] (2026-05) — single-API access to 200+ chains; execution layer for MoonPay Institutional.
- [[MoonPay acquires Dawn Labs and launches AI trading agent Dawn CLI]] (2026-05) — AI-native trading infra.
- [[MoonPay acquires Entendre for on-chain finance operations]] (2026-06) — reconciliation/treasury/close workflows (finance-ops automation — notably adjacent to a CFO's remit).
- [[MoonPay acquires Glide for crypto deposits infrastructure]] (2026-07) — fiat/bank integration.
- [[Ether.fi moves core payments infrastructure onto MoonPay stack]] (2026-10) — flagship full-stack infra customer.
- Per this release, MoonPay also launched PayBox (agentic finance) and in Sept 2026 agreed to acquire **North Capital**, expanding into onchain capital markets. Board/advisors recently added spanning Wall Street, national security, Silicon Valley and digital assets.
This cadence — regulated institutional unit + capital-markets M&A (North Capital) + a new finance-chief from the exchange world — is a coherent "grow into a regulated, investable financial institution" arc.

**[3] Comparable context (CFO-hires-at-scaling-fintechs — cite).** The pattern mirrors other 2026 fintech CFO installs: [[OnePay names Patrick O'Connell as CFO from AMC Networks]] (ex-Goldman, hired "amid rapid growth / pivotal time") and [[Marqeta appoints Patti Kangwankij as CFO]] (ex-Stripe/JPMorgan, at a listed issuer). Most directly relevant: Giviskos was interim CFO at Navan after [[Navan announces CFO Amy Butte transition following IPO]] (2025-12) — Butte departed following Navan's ~$6.2B **October 2025 IPO**. So Giviskos steps in from a company she helped steward through/after a public listing, reinforcing the public-markets-capability read.

**[4] External context & verdict (WebSearch — cite URLs).** MoonPay was last priced at a **$3.4B** valuation in its Nov-2021 Tiger Global/Coatue round; as of end-2025 it reportedly still carried that mark. Crucially, NYSE-owner **Intercontinental Exchange (ICE)** was reported (Dec 2025) to be in talks to invest in a round valuing MoonPay near **$5B** (~47% step-up) — ICE also owns Bakkt and recently backed Polymarket. On the IPO question itself, MoonPay **publicly dismissed IPO rumours** at the time of this hire: a spokesperson said the focus is building an "iconic / flagship private company" and expanding payments, stablecoins and financial-markets businesses "rather than pursuing a public listing at this time." **Assessment:** the denial is credible as near-term guidance, but the totality — exchange-veteran CFO with Navan post-IPO experience, a capital-markets acquisition (North Capital), an institutional unit, and an NYSE-parent strategic investor circling — reads as deliberate IPO/capital-event *readiness*, whether the exit is a listing or a large strategic round. Treat "no IPO" as present-tense messaging, not a structural signal.

15. For the digest reader: does a CFO appointment at a private company clear the relevance bar vs product/funding/M&A stories the same week?

**Importance: 3/5** — A credible, well-sourced senior hire at a scaling, strategically active crypto-fintech, with a genuinely interesting IPO/capital-event subtext (exchange-veteran CFO + ICE stake talks + North Capital). But it is ultimately a leadership/"people" move at a private company with an explicit IPO denial and single-release sourcing — meaningful signal, not a market-moving event. Mid-tier.

**Sector & drivers.** MoonPay sits in crypto fiat on/off-ramp + stablecoin payment infrastructure. Market sizing is noisy and mostly from secondary trackers: the crypto on-ramp segment was put at ~$10.2bn (2025) growing to ~$17.7bn by 2034 (~6.3% CAGR) per Dataintelo, via dataintelo.com — treat as a vendor estimate, not a verified figure. The adjacent, far larger pull is stablecoin payments: ~$482bn in Q3 2026, +45% QoQ, with total stablecoin cap ~$308bn (Aug 2026) per secondary trackers (kucoin.com) — directionally the reason MoonPay is repositioning from "buy crypto button" to "operating system for value." Structure: fragmented at the retail ramp layer (MoonPay, Coinbase Onramp, Transak, Ramp Network, Mercuryo, Simplex, Banxa all compete on the same embedded-widget surface), consolidating at the infrastructure/regulatory layer where licenses, blockchain coverage and enterprise distribution are the real barriers. Why now: MoonPay disclosed [IR] a NYDFS Limited Purpose Trust Charter (2025-11-01, moonpay.com/newsroom/nytrust) and launched enterprise stablecoin + virtual-account rails — the moat is shifting from consumer UX to regulated custody + B2B2C distribution, which is exactly what a public-markets CFO (ex-NYSE/Euronext/Navan) is hired to run.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: on-ramp volume (fiat→crypto GMV), take rate (~0.5–4% retail), enterprise-client count, blockchain/chain coverage, stablecoin volume share. Players & basis of competition: Coinbase Onramp (distribution + balance sheet), Transak and Ramp Network (embedded/white-label API, country+chain breadth), Stripe (fiat rails + Bridge stablecoin acquisition, the most dangerous entrant because it owns card acquiring). Recent peer/own moves: MoonPay launched MoonPay Trade (200+ blockchains via one API), acquired DFlow (Solana infra, $45bn 2026 volume, in >95% of Solana blocks daily), agreed to acquire North Capital (onchain capital markets, Sep 2026), and introduced PayBox agentic finance (all per the 2026-10-13 PR). Position: ahead of pure retail ramps on scale and licensing, catching up to Coinbase/Stripe on distribution; moat = regulatory licenses (US + EU) + integration into counterparties' stacks (powers Solana trades for Coinbase, cross-chain swaps in Ledger Wallet) — i.e. network/switching-cost moat built via acquisitions, not organic product.
