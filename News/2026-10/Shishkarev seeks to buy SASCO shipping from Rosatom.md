 Сергей Шишкарев хочет выкупить у «Росатома» SASCO 
Основатель ГК «Дело» Сергей Шишкарев, завершивший 7 октября сделку по продаже контрольного пакета группы «Росатому», планирует выкупить у госкорпорации 58,67% Сахалинского морского пароходства (SASCO). Об этом он сообщил в интервью “ Коммерсанту” . «На определенном этапе мы с партнерами из госкорпорации “Росатом” не договорились о консолидации 100% SASCO, и более 30% пароходства сейчас принадлежит связанной со мной компании,— пояснил он.— Я сделал предложение выкупить еще 58,67%, которыми владеет ГК “Дело”».
Группа приобрела SASCO в 2022 году. Пароходство занимается линейными (Владивосток—Корсаков, Владивосток—Магадан), трамповыми, паромными, мультимодальными перевозками. В составе флота SASCO — восемь собственных универсальных и специализированных контейнерных судов общим дедвейтом 71 тыс. тонн, три дизель-электрохода типа «Сахалин» и один портовый буксир. Все суда имеют ледовый класс не ниже Arc 4, что позволяет осуществлять круглогодичную навигацию и обслуживать арктические порты.
Как пояснил господин Шишкарев, если не выкупить SASCO, то она и уже принадлежащая «Росатому» FESCO будут конкурировать на Дальнем Востоке между собой. «Ожидаю позицию коллег на это предложение»,— добавил он.

# Context-enrichment: Shishkarev seeks to buy SASCO shipping from Rosatom
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
In a Kommersant interview dated around 2026-10-08, Sergei Shishkarev — founder of Delo Group, who **one day earlier (7 Oct 2026) completed the sale of his 51% controlling stake in Delo Group to Rosatom for RUB 77bn / ~$900M** (see [[Rosatom completes buyout of Delo Group for 77bn rubles]]) — said he wants to **buy back the 58.67% of SASCO (Sakhalin Shipping Company / Сахалинское морское пароходство) that sits inside Delo**. His stated words: "At a certain stage we and our partners from Rosatom did not agree on consolidating 100% of SASCO, and more than 30% of the shipping company now belongs to a company linked to me. I made an offer to buy out the remaining 58.67% owned by Delo Group." He added: "I await my colleagues' position."

**This is an OFFER, not a closed deal — nor even an agreed negotiation.** Rosatom has made no public response. Price, valuation method, financing, and timeline are all undisclosed.

**Why structured this way / what it reveals:** This is the inverse of the eight-month Delo saga. For ~8 months (Feb–Oct 2026) Shishkarev lost his flagship holding to the state via a "Russian roulette" shotgun clause he could not out-finance; now, within 24 hours of cashing out at RUB 77bn, he is trying to pull ONE asset — SASCO — back out of the state's hands. The structure is telling: he already holds >30% of SASCO personally (outside Delo), so he only needs the 58.67% block to re-consolidate it. The ">30% already mine" detail confirms SASCO was never fully absorbed into the Rosatom-controlled perimeter — there was a pre-existing split ownership the two sides "did not agree" to resolve. So the 7 Oct close of the parent deal mechanically forced the SASCO question: with Delo now 100% Rosatom, SASCO's 58.67% block is a loose end sitting under the same owner as FESCO. (analysis)

**The anti-competition framing is the PR cover; watch the counter-logic.** Shishkarev argues SASCO and Rosatom's FESCO "would compete on the Far East" if left under one roof — presenting the buyback as a clean-up favour. But the obvious alternative for Rosatom is to **keep SASCO and MERGE it into FESCO**, which also eliminates the competition — without handing an asset back to a departed founder. So "they'd compete" is a one-sided argument for why Rosatom *should sell to him*, not a neutral statement of Rosatom's interest. Acceptance is far from assured. (analysis)

## [1] Competitors / peers
The direct peer/overlap is **FESCO (ДВМП / Far-Eastern Shipping Company)** — nationalized after owner Magomedov's conviction and transferred to Rosatom by presidential decree (92.4% of state shares, Nov 2023). FESCO is Russia's largest diversified transport group (~25 ships, ~19 boxships) and runs Far East container/liner services including a **Vladivostok–Magadan shuttle** that directly duplicates a core SASCO route; both also serve Sakhalin/Kamchatka/Chukotka. That overlap is the literal basis of Shishkarev's argument.

Other Far East / Arctic shipping context: Rosatomflot (nuclear icebreakers), Rosmorport (non-nuclear icebreakers being consolidated under Rosatom), and COSCO (resumed seasonal NSR voyages in 2025). **Position:** SASCO is a small, specialized niche player (~11 vessels, sole ferry link to Sakhalin, ~2/3 of Russian cabotage by some accounts) next to FESCO's scale. **Why the lay of the land is this way:** post-2022 sanctions re-domesticated Far East shipping and funneled strategic assets into Rosatom's NSR-operator orbit; SASCO's Arc4 ice-class fleet (year-round Arctic navigation) is exactly the kind of asset the state wants for the NSR — which is itself an argument AGAINST Rosatom selling it. (analysis)

## [2] Company history / fit
- **SASCO**: HQ Kholmsk (Sakhalin). Ice-class ferry/cargo/tramp/multimodal. Fleet ~11: 8 universal/specialized container ships (total DWT ~71k t), 3 Sakhalin-type diesel-electric vessels, 1 port tug; all Arc4+ ice class for year-round Arctic ports. Routes: liner Vladivostok–Korsakov, Vladivostok–Magadan; ferry Vanino–Kholmsk. US-sanctioned (OFAC).
- **Acquisition by Delo**: Aug 2022 (closed ~15 Aug 2022); 58.67% held via TransContainer. Value undisclosed.
- **Delo Group**: founded 1993; Global Ports (~23% of Russian container turnover), TransContainer (~50% of Russian rail container transport), DeloPorts (NUTEP + KSK grain terminal, Novorossiysk). Rosatom entered 2019 (30% → 49% via option) → 100% on 7 Oct 2026.

**Why Shishkarev acts this way:** After the forced full exit, he said his future remains "with Russian logistics, sport and native Novorossiysk." SASCO is a tractable re-entry vehicle: he already owns >30%, it is small enough to fund from the RUB 77bn proceeds, and it keeps him in shipping. Structurally, this is a founder who just received a large cash settlement reaching for the one piece of his old empire with a clean path back — the piece the state never fully consolidated. (analysis)

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
What is genuinely NEW vs the corpus: the prior three notes ([[Shishkarev drops plan to buy 49% of Delo Group from Rosatom]], [[Rosatom decides to buy out Shishkarev's stake in Delo Group]], [[Rosatom completes buyout of Delo Group for 77bn rubles]]) all tracked the state ABSORBING Delo. This note is the **first instance of the reverse vector**: Shishkarev trying to buy an asset BACK from Rosatom. New asset (SASCO, not the Delo parent), new direction, new counterparty dynamic. So it is a fresh development, not a reprint.

**Why the value-add is thin at this stage:** there is zero traction — it is a verbal offer in an interview, with no price, no financing confirmation, no Rosatom assent, no timeline. "Who captures the value" is entirely unresolved: if Rosatom keeps SASCO and merges it into FESCO, the value (NSR-capable ice-class tonnage) stays with the state; if it sells, Shishkarev re-consolidates a niche carrier he already half-owns. The economics of the SASCO stake are undisclosed; the only observable fact is the >30%/58.67% ownership split. (analysis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch for: (1) any Rosatom response (silence so far); (2) whether Rosatom opts to MERGE SASCO into FESCO instead of selling (the competing resolution to the same "overlap" problem); (3) a disclosed price/financing if talks advance; (4) FAS/regulatory treatment of a sanctioned asset changing hands. Backdrop: NSR cargo actually FELL 2.3% YoY to 37.02m t in 2025 — sanctions are undercutting the Arctic-growth thesis, which cuts both ways (less commercial pressure to retain SASCO, but also the state's strategic-tonnage logic for keeping it).

**Why / second-order:** This is the rare counter-current in an otherwise one-way re-statisation story. A founder trying to carve an asset back out of a state corporation inverts the 2026 Russian pattern — but the same structural forces (the state's NSR mandate, the value of ice-class tonnage, Rosatom's preference for consolidation over divestment) argue the offer is more likely to be declined or converted into a FESCO merger than accepted. The counterintuitive read: Shishkarev's "avoid competition" pitch hands Rosatom the exact argument it needs to KEEP and absorb SASCO rather than sell. (analysis/hypothesis)

## Top challenge / red-team questions (second-order)

1. **Is it really new vs the parent-deal notes?** Yes — [[Rosatom completes buyout of Delo Group for 77bn rubles]] (7 Oct) covers the state absorbing Delo to 100%; this is the FIRST reverse-vector move: Shishkarev offering to buy SASCO BACK from Rosatom. New asset, new direction. Fresh.
2. **Is this a deal or just an offer?** Only an offer, stated in a Kommersant interview. Shishkarev himself: "I await my colleagues' position." No Rosatom response. Announced ≠ agreed ≠ live.
3. **What is the price / valuation of the 58.67%?** Open — undisclosed. No valuation method, no implied SASCO equity value in any source.
4. **How would Shishkarev finance it?** Open — plausibly from the RUB 77bn Delo proceeds, but he also pledged a "significant portion" of those to national security/defense, so available cash is unquantified. (hypothesis)
5. **Will Rosatom even sell?** Doubtful by the counter-logic: Rosatom can eliminate the SASCO/FESCO overlap by MERGING SASCO into FESCO, keeping the ice-class NSR tonnage. Selling hands a strategic asset to a departed founder. Acceptance not assured. (analysis)
6. **Is "they'd compete" a neutral fact or self-serving framing?** Self-serving — it is an argument for why Rosatom *should sell to him*, not evidence Rosatom wants to. The same overlap argues equally for a FESCO merger.
7. **What exactly is the ownership split?** Shishkarev-linked company >30%; Delo (now Rosatom) 58.67%; so SASCO was never fully consolidated into the Rosatom perimeter — a pre-existing unresolved split. Confirmed per the note.
8. **How was SASCO held inside Delo?** Via TransContainer (per external research); acquired Aug 2022, value undisclosed. The 58.67% is the Delo/TransContainer block.
9. **Does SASCO's sanctioned status (OFAC) complicate a transfer?** Plausibly material — both SASCO and Shishkarev are sanctioned, constraining financing/payment routes, as with the parent Delo deal. (hypothesis)
10. **Does the NSR thesis support selling SASCO?** No — SASCO's Arc4+ year-round Arctic-capable fleet is exactly the NSR-strategic tonnage Rosatom (the NSR operator) wants. That argues for retention, against the sale. And NSR cargo actually fell 2.3% in 2025, so commercial urgency is weak either way.
11. **What is the fintech relevance?** Thin — Russian infra/M&A, a proposed stake transaction. Belongs in corpus as Far East shipping consolidation, not a payments/fintech-product story. Caps importance.
12. **Could this just be post-exit positioning / signalling?** Possibly — a founder publicly reasserting a future "in Russian logistics" one day after exiting. The interview framing suggests as much. (hypothesis)
13. **What-if Rosatom accepts?** Shishkarev re-consolidates SASCO (>30% + 58.67% → ~90%+), re-entering Far East shipping as a niche private operator alongside the state's FESCO — a rare private carve-out.
14. **What-if Rosatom declines / merges SASCO into FESCO?** The likelier outcome; SASCO becomes part of the state logistics champion, and this offer is a footnote. (analysis)
15. **Source robustness?** Single thin primary (mergers.ru, 291 words) relaying a Kommersant interview; the surrounding facts (Delo close, FESCO→Rosatom, SASCO fleet/routes) are well-corroborated, but the offer itself rests on Shishkarev's own words with no second-party confirmation.

**Importance: 3/5 — rationale.** A genuinely NEW and analytically interesting development — the first reverse-vector move in an eight-month state-consolidation saga, a tycoon trying to pull an asset back OUT of the state one day after being bought out, with a clean read on Far East route competition (SASCO vs Rosatom's FESCO). But standalone it is capped at 3: it is only a verbal OFFER with no price, no financing, no Rosatom assent and an obvious counter-logic (a FESCO merger) that may kill it; it is a small niche asset relative to the ~$900M parent deal; it is infrastructure/M&A with only adjacent fintech relevance; and it rests on a single thin primary relaying one man's interview. Fresh, not a duplicate of the parent-deal chain.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Russian shipping / coastal (cabotage) and Far-East multimodal logistics — not a fintech vertical; included as a Russian M&A / infrastructure data point. This is a sequel event inside the Delo/Rosatom saga already tracked in-corpus (see internal comps below): having just sold his 51% of Delo Group to Rosatom (closed 2026-10-07, 77bn ₽ / ~$900M), founder Sergei Shishkarev now wants to buy BACK one discrete asset — **58.67% of SASCO (Sakhalin Shipping Co.)** — from the state corporation. Note the ownership map stated in the note: >30% of SASCO already sits with a Shishkarev-connected company, and the 58.67% he is bidding for is held by Delo (and, per web, specifically by the sanctioned subsidiary **TransContainer**), so a successful deal would take him to a majority. Structure of the subvertical: highly consolidated and state-anchored post-2022 — Rosatom now controls Delo + FESCO + TransContainer under one logistics champion (Likhachev: ~50% of container transshipment, ~44% of rail container volume — self-reported). Barriers: capital, sanctions, Northern Sea Route (NSR) statutory control sits with Rosatom. **Why now:** Shishkarev's own stated rationale is competition avoidance — "if SASCO is not bought out, it and Rosatom-owned FESCO will compete against each other in the Far East" (per Kommersant/mergers.ru, 2026-10-07). Second-order: the post-Delo consolidation deliberately merges FESCO into a single Far-East operator, so an independently-controlled SASCO is an anomaly the state would plausibly prefer to keep inside its own perimeter — giving Rosatom, not Shishkarev, the stronger negotiating hand ("I await my colleagues' position," he said). (analysis)

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs (shipping, not fintech): fleet DWT / vessel count, cabotage market share, ice-class capability, route coverage, revenue & net margin. SASCO data (per Wikipedia/TAdviser/magicport, 2024–25): revenue **6.9bn ₽ in 2025, +8.7% YoY** (from 6.3bn ₽ in 2024); net profit **315.6m ₽ in 2025, +163.8%** (from 119.6m ₽); fleet per the note = **8 own container ships (71k DWT total), 3 "Sakhalin"-type diesel-electric vessels, 1 port tug, all ice-class ≥ Arc 4** (year-round Arctic-port capable); third-party trackers show ~12 managed / 14 sanctioned vessels. SASCO provides ~2/3 of Russian cabotage and is the sole Sakhalin ferry link (Vanino–Kholmsk), plus Vladivostok–Korsakov and Vladivostok–Magadan lines. Key players / basis of competition: **FESCO** (Rosatom-controlled) is the direct Far-East overlap SASCO's buyer wants to de-conflict; competition here is route/regulatory access and ice-class fleet, not price. Recent moves (dated): Aug–Sep 2022 Delo/TransContainer acquired 58.51% of SASCO; 2023-07-20 SASCO added to **US sanctions** (State Dept, Arctic-energy logistics role); 2026-10-07 Delo/Rosatom deal closes and Shishkarev makes the SASCO buy-back offer. Protagonist's position: Shishkarev is the **bidder/minority-adjacent holder** (>30% via a related company), seeking control of a niche but strategically unique cabotage operator; moat is intangible/regulatory (sole-ferry monopoly to Sakhalin, ice-class fleet), not a commercial network-effect moat. (analysis)

**Comps & multiples.** Deal value for the 58.67% SASCO stake = **not disclosed** (the note and all sources give no price; Shishkarev has only made an offer, Rosatom has not responded). **Risk flags.**
1. **Sanctions (binding constraint).** SASCO has been on the **US sanctions list since July 2023** (14 vessels + subsidiaries named), and the 58.67% is held via the US-sanctioned **TransContainer**. Any transfer, financing, or future monetization is OFAC-exposed; a sanctioned buyer acquiring a sanctioned target from a sanctioned seller is a closed-loop that forecloses international financing or resale — the same trap that is delaying the FESCO–DP World JV for the parent group. Second-order: this is a domestic, cash-only reshuffle with no external capital path.
2. **Counterparty / weak-bidder leverage.** Shishkarev has only *made an offer* ("I await colleagues' position"); Rosatom holds the asset and has a stated strategy of consolidating ALL Far-East logistics (incl. FESCO) under one roof — so selling SASCO out of that perimeter cuts against its own de-duplication logic. Announced ≠ agreed; the state has the stronger hand and no obligation to sell. He also just failed to finance the far larger Delo buy-back, raising doubt he can/will fund this one on terms Rosatom accepts.
3. **Strategic-asset / monopoly sensitivity.** SASCO is the sole Sakhalin ferry link and ~2/3 of Russian cabotage — a politically sensitive chokepoint. Even if Rosatom is willing, FAS/state approval and the "transport sovereignty" framing that justified the Delo consolidation could argue for keeping SASCO state-controlled, not returning it to a (sanctioned) private owner.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Not a re-rating — a small carve-out attempt at the tail of a large state consolidation. Falsifiable thesis: Rosatom declines or slow-walks the offer, because pulling SASCO out undercuts the just-announced "single Far-East logistics management center" (FESCO + Delo) it built the whole 77bn ₽ deal to create; a sale would only happen if the state decides SASCO's niche cabotage/ferry role is non-core to the Arctic-corridor strategy. Trigger to watch: any official Rosatom response and, critically, a disclosed price — until then this is a one-sided announcement. Thesis breaks if Rosatom quickly agrees, signaling it views SASCO as non-strategic and is willing to monetize the stake to a sanctioned founder. (analysis)
