# Финтехно: Russian Islamic banking grows to 38 participants

> [!info] 2026-10-07 · 1 упоминаний · 0 источника(ов) с текстом

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Финтехно] За три года исламский банкинг в России прошёл путь от регионального правового эксперимента к растущему рынку с 38 участниками и востребованными продуктами. И на этой волне ЦБ предлагает перевести партнёрское финансирование из экспериментального режима в постоянный. Рассматриваются два варианта: либо сделать бессрочным действующий специальный закон, либо — и этот вариант ЦБ считает более перспективным — выделить наиболее востребованные продукты банков и МФО и встроить их непосредственно в обычное финансовое законодательство. После выбора модели должен появиться законопроект. Рынок пока небольшой, но быстро растёт: на конец первого полугодия портфель размещённых средств достиг 10,4 млрд рублей, привлечённых — 16 млрд, а число участников эксперимента к августу выросло до 38. Также в этом году появился первый отраслевой стандарт партнёрского финансирования, а ВЭБ․РФ разместил первые рыночные партнёрские облигации на 3,5 млрд рублей — спрос превысил предложение более чем в 2,5 раза. # Context-enrichment: Russian Islamic banking grows to 38 participants
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
This is a **status-and-roadmap report** (source: Финтехно, aggregating early-October 2026 coverage — Lenta, RIA, РБК/Реальное время, 06.10.2026), not a single new event. Three years after launch (the partner-finance "experimental legal regime" ran from **1 Sep 2023** across four regions — Tatarstan, Bashkortostan, Dagestan, Chechnya), the Bank of Russia (CBR) is signaling it wants to move partner finance **from the time-limited experiment into permanent regulation**.

De-PR'd facts:
- **Two models on the table**, not a decision: (a) make the existing special experiment law open-ended/perpetual, or (b) — the option CBR calls more promising — cherry-pick the most-demanded bank/MFI products and embed them directly into *ordinary* financial legislation. A draft bill comes **only after** the model is chosen. So the headline "CBR proposes permanent mode" is still **"intent to legislate," not a signed law**.
- **Scale (de-PR'd):** market is small but fast-growing. The note cites portfolio of **placed funds 10.4bn ₽ and attracted 16bn ₽** at end-H1 2026, participants up to **38 by August**. External sources cite slightly lower H1-point figures — **placed 9.3bn ₽ (from 3.4bn at year-start, ~2.7x) and attracted 14.7bn ₽ (from 7.5bn)** — the discrepancy is a **measurement-date/metric mismatch** (cumulative vs period; the note's 10.4/16 likely a later/August read). Flag: the note and primary press give different numbers for the "same" period — treat exact figures as soft.
- **38 participants = 21 non-financial + 7 non-credit financial + 10 credit institutions** (per iz.ru/Реальное время). So only ~10 are actual banks; the headcount is padded by non-bank/non-financial registrants. "38 participants" ≠ "38 Islamic banks."
- **First industry standard** for partner finance approved **July 2026** by the Association of Banks of Russia (АБР) committee and agreed by CBR — it covers **commodity installment/murabaha-style** sale with mark-up, built on **AAOIFI** requirements. Further standards (investment instruments, equity/mudaraba, leasing) planned by end-2026. So "first standard" is real but narrow (one product type).
- **ВЭБ.РФ partner bonds:** debut **3.5bn ₽** placement on **Moscow Exchange, 9 June 2026**, 2-year tenor, proceeds to an industrial park ("Северные ворота," Tatarstan); demand oversubscribed **>2.5x**. This is the first market partner-finance ("Islamic") bond on MOEX — a genuine capital-markets first, but single-issuer and small.

**Why structured this way / what it reveals:** CBR prefers option (b) because a standalone perpetual "Islamic" law is politically and doctrinally awkward in a secular state — embedding "partner finance" products into existing banking/MFI law **normalizes it without naming religion** and avoids a parallel regulatory regime. The persistent use of **"партнёрское финансирование" (partner finance)** rather than "Islamic banking" is the tell: the state wants the economic substance (attract Gulf/OIC capital, serve ~25M Muslim citizens) while sidestepping the confessional framing.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Global Islamic-finance hubs** (Malaysia, UAE, Saudi, Qatar, Pakistan) are decades ahead with full Shariah-banking stacks — cf. [[Pakistan's SBP approves Samba Bank's Islamic conversion]] (full-bank conversion, Oct 2025), [[Bank Muamalat taps Backbase for AI-driven Islamic banking]] (digital Islamic bank, 2025), [[Qatar Islamic Bank and PayLater launch Shariah-compliant BNPL]], [[Dubai DFSA proposes updates to Islamic finance regulatory framework]] (May 2026), [[Two Islamic digital banks enter Labuan sandbox]]. Russia is a **late entrant playing catch-up**: it is only now approving its *first* commodity-murabaha standard, a product live for decades elsewhere.
- **Domestic "competitors":** the main domestic players are the same large RU banks — **Sberbank** (cooperation with the Spiritual Administration of Muslims, products Amana account / Adafa card / Murabaha mortgage) and **Ak Bars Bank** (joined AAOIFI). These aren't competitors to the experiment; they **are** the experiment. No Russian pure-play Islamic bank at scale.
**Why the lay of the land is this way:** Russia's pivot is **geopolitically driven** (post-2022 sanctions → reorient toward OIC/Gulf capital), not organic retail demand, which is why it is top-down (CBR/Duma/АБР) rather than fintech-led, unlike the Malaysia/UAE digital-first wave.

## [2] Company history / fit
Not a company item — a **jurisdiction/regime** item. Trajectory: Duma adopted the experiment bill (TASS, 2023) → launch Sep 2023, 4 regions → 2nd stage (ВЭБ.РФ, Банк ДОМ.РФ enter register) → extension of the experiment proposed to **1 Sep 2028** → July 2026 first АБР standard → June 2026 ВЭБ bond → Oct 2026 CBR "make it permanent" signal. Logical and monotonic: pilot → standards → capital markets → permanence. **Why the state acts this way:** sanctions make Gulf/Islamic capital a strategic funding channel; estimates floated of **up to 1 trillion ₽** attracted and **+10M users by 2030** — aspirational官方 targets, not booked flows.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Real new bits:** (1) CBR's stated *preference* to embed products in ordinary law vs a perpetual special law — a regulatory-philosophy signal; (2) the July-2026 **first АБР standard** (narrow: commodity installment); (3) the June-2026 **ВЭБ 3.5bn ₽ bond**, >2.5x oversubscribed — the only hard "traction" datapoint with external demand.
- **Traction reality:** portfolio ~10–16bn ₽ is **tiny** for a 38-entity, 4-region, 3-year program (rounding error vs RU banking assets). Growth rate (~2.5–2.7x YoY) is high only off a near-zero base. "38 participants" overstates depth (only ~10 credit institutions).
**Why the value-add is / isn't real, deeper:** the durable value is **not** the product menu (murabaha/ijara are commoditized globally) but **regulatory certainty** — moving from a sunset experiment to permanent law is what would let large banks commit balance sheet and let Gulf LPs underwrite RU partner-bonds. The margin in the stack sits with **the few large banks (Sber, Ak Bars) and ВЭБ** who can structure AAOIFI-compliant instruments at scale; the 21 non-financial registrants capture little. What breaks the thesis: the law is still only *proposed*; a sanctioned Russia may struggle to attract the headline "1 trillion ₽" of Gulf capital regardless of domestic framing.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Next:** CBR/government to finalize the permanent-regulation model "soon," then a draft bill; a government expert council to present first contours of the bill by **year-end 2026**; experiment extension to **2028**; more АБР standards (investment, equity, leasing) by end-2026.
- **Sentiment:** officially bullish (1 trln ₽ / +10M users by 2030 — treat as policy ambition, not forecast). Surveys cited that up to 80% of Russians want to know about Islamic-banking tools (iz.ru) — awareness ≠ adoption.
**Why the market may go this way / second-order:** permanence + standards + a working bond precedent create a credible on-ramp; but the second-order constraint is **external** — the whole point (Gulf/OIC capital inflow) depends on counterparties willing to transact with sanctioned Russian issuers. Domestic normalization is necessary but **not sufficient**; the binding constraint is geopolitical, not legal. So the real question shifts from "will Russia legalize Islamic finance?" (likely yes) to "will foreign Islamic capital actually show up at scale?" (open, sanctions-gated).

## Red-team / challenge questions

6. **Is the "first industry standard" broad or narrow?** — Narrow: July 2026 АБР standard covers commodity installment (murabaha-style) only; equity/leasing/investment standards still pending end-2026.

7. **What already did the same, and when?** — Malaysia/UAE/Saudi/Qatar/Pakistan have full Shariah stacks for decades (cf. internal notes). Russia is approving its *first* commodity-murabaha standard now — deep catch-up, not frontier.

8. **Is demand organic or geopolitical?** — Primarily geopolitical/top-down (post-2022 sanctions → reorient to OIC/Gulf capital), driven by CBR/Duma/АБР, not fintech or retail pull.

9. **Is the "up to 1 trillion ₽ / +10M users by 2030" credible?** — These are official aspirational targets, not forecasts or booked flows. Treat as policy ambition.

10. **What's the binding constraint — law or sanctions?** — Sanctions. Domestic legalization is necessary but not sufficient; the inflow thesis depends on foreign Islamic counterparties transacting with sanctioned RU issuers (open).

11. **Who captures the margin?** — The few large structurers (Sberbank, Ak Bars, ВЭБ.РФ) who can build AAOIFI-compliant instruments at scale; the 21 non-financial registrants capture little.

12. **How material is ~10–16bn ₽?** — Negligible vs RU banking assets; high growth only off a near-zero base. Not yet systemically relevant.

13. **Is there a duplicate in the corpus?** — No. The only RU Islamic/partner-finance note is this target itself; no prior note covers the 38-participant update, the June ВЭБ bond, or the "permanent mode" signal. FRESH.

15. **What would change the verdict upward?** — A signed permanent law, a named large foreign Islamic-capital commitment, or multi-issuer partner-bond volume on MOEX. None present yet.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russia's "partner (Islamic) financing" is a Shariah-compliant niche inside banking, run since Sep-2023 as a legal experiment in four Muslim-majority regions (Tatarstan, Bashkortostan, Chechnya, Dagestan); the special law was extended through 2028 (per RealnoeVremya/TASS, via web, 2025). Per the note's own Финтехно text: H1-2026 placed portfolio ₽10.4bn, attracted funds ₽16bn, participants up to 38 by Aug-2026; VEB.RF placed first market partner (sukuk-style) bonds of ₽3.5bn with >2.5x oversubscription. These H1-2026 figures trace only to the digest text — no independent CBR release confirmed them in search (web returned older data: ~31-33 participants, H1-2024 volume ~₽5.4bn), so treat the 38/₽10.4bn as single-sourced. Structure: tiny and fragmented vs the ₽140tn+ Russian banking system — portfolio of ₽10.4bn is a rounding error (<0.01% of system assets) (analysis). Entry barriers are regulatory, not capital: the experiment regime + need for a Shariah board. Drivers / why now: (1) the CB wants to graduate the experiment into permanent rules — either make the special law perpetual or, its preferred path, fold the most-used products into mainline financial law; (2) a 2026 industry standard + first market bond issue signal early institutionalization. Global backdrop: Islamic finance assets heading toward ~$6tn in 2026, banking ~72% of that, financing +17% YoY in 2025 (per Khaleej Times/ICD citation, via web) — Russia is a late, sub-scale entrant to a large global pool; KPMG earlier pegged Russia's medium-term potential at $7-9bn (via web).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: participant count, placed vs attracted portfolio (₽), product mix (murabaha/ijara-style trade & mortgage finance), and oversubscription/spread on partner bonds. Basis of competition = distribution + regulatory first-mover, not price. Key players surfaced via web: Sberbank (in Islamic finance since 2016; its structured-deal book is small — cited at ~$262m + ₽30m, via RealnoeVremya, undated) and AK BARS Bank (partner-finance branch since 2019, mortgage-focused); plus regional MFOs and investment platforms. Recent moves: VEB.RF's ₽3.5bn partner bond (H1-2026, per note); first sector standard adopted 2026 (per note). Protagonist here is the *market/regulator*, not one firm — the CB is the de-facto architect. Position: nascent/niche; the moat is regulatory licensing + Shariah-board intangibles, not scale (analysis). No disclosed take rates, margins or default data — [UNSOURCED].

**Comps & multiples.** This is a regulatory/market-structure story, not a deal with a valuation — no round, no revenue, no EV. Trading multiples: **not applicable / no data** (no transacting entity, no price). Internal comps from the base (all *foreign* Islamic-finance moves — no prior Russia partner-finance note exists in the corpus, itself a signal of how thin coverage is): [[Dubai DFSA proposes updates to Islamic finance regulatory framework]] (regulatory-framework parallel, 2026-05), [[Mobilink Microfinance Bank launches Islamic banking in Pakistan]] (2025-12), [[Mashreq launches Pakistan's first Islamic digital banking platform]] (2025-11), [[Bank Muamalat taps Backbase for AI-driven Islamic banking]] (2025-06). Read-across: peer markets (UAE, Pakistan, Malaysia) are moving to digital-first / standardized frameworks while Russia is still debating *whether* to make its regime permanent — i.e. roughly a regulatory generation behind (analysis). Multiple distribution not computed — no comparable financials.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Model/regulatory uncertainty (primary).** The CB's preferred option — dissolving the special law and embedding only "most-used" products into general law — risks dropping less-popular but structurally necessary instruments, and no bill exists yet; a wrong model choice could stall the whole segment just as it gains traction.
2. **Sub-scale / single-source growth.** ₽10.4bn is trivially small and the headline growth rests on one undated digest figure not corroborated by a CBR release in search; if the 38/₽10.4bn numbers are soft, the "fast-growing" framing is overstated.
3. **Geographic + demand concentration.** Confined to four regions and a faith-defined customer base; outside Tatarstan/Bashkortostan/Caucasus, organic demand is thin, capping TAM regardless of the legal regime (analysis).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Falsifiable thesis: moving partner finance from "experiment" to permanent law — plus a sector standard and a liquid partner-bond market — is the gating step that lets large banks (Sber, AK BARS) scale beyond pilot books; watch for the actual draft bill and whether product scope is preserved or trimmed. Trigger = publication of the legislative model choice / draft; thesis breaks if the portfolio stays sub-₽20bn and participant growth flattens after the experiment lapses. (analysis)
