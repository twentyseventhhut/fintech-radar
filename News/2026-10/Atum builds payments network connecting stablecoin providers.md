Atum raises $13.5m to build the world's first  open payments network.
 SAN FRANCISCO , Sept. 22, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Atum  today emerged from stealth having built the  open payments network, a first of its kind, for global money movement. Atum connects payment companies, developers, and enterprises through a single coordination layer, without issuing a currency, operating a blockchain, favoring a rail, or taking custody of customer funds. The company is launching with $13.5 million of funding from Variant , PayPal  Ventures, Abstract Ventures , Road Capital , Mirana Ventures , First Commit, Credibly Neutral , and strategic advisor Charlie Songhurst.

 Payments are fragmented across currencies, chains, apps, and local rails that do not interoperate, and every handoff adds cost, delay, and complexity. Stablecoins offer fast, low-cost global rails, but are spread across many blockchains, which pushes that complexity onto users. Atum is the coordination layer between them. Any integrator, developer, or application can submit a payment request, while settlement providers compete to fulfill the payments across any supported chain and stablecoin , with native authorization, reversible payments, and identity. Senders specify what they will send; receivers get what they asked to receive.
Atum is incentivized only by volume, and connects participants through a marketplace where settlement providers compete to fulfill each payment. That structure is what makes the network credibly neutral: its interests are aligned with everyone building on it. It is an open-loop payments and commerce platform.
The network serves businesses moving money: card issuers and acquirers, payment service providers, card networks, stablecoin orchestrators, wallets, fintechs, and enterprises—plus developers building on top. Atum supports financial institutions and stablecoin card issuers, for both non-agentic stablecoin payments and agentic payments through agentic protocols including x402 and MPP. Both humans and agents are completing payments on Atum today.
"This is my life's work," said Pete Cooling, founder and CEO of Atum. "In 2014 I saw that blockchains were payments networks, and everything since has pointed to the same conclusion. Onchain accounts are the future bank accounts, and new infrastructure is needed to make money move more seamlessly than it does today. Atum is that infrastructure."
Cooling formerly led the crypto product team at Visa and has been researching and building blockchain-based payments systems over the past decade. He served as Visa's representative on Linux's OpenWallet Foundation, helping guide standards for digital wallets across identity, financial accounts, payment cards, and stablecoins .

## [0] What happened

Atum emerged from stealth on **22 Sep 2026** (surfaced in Fintech Brainfood's 18 Oct digest) with a **$13.5M seed** and an "open payments network" — a credibly-neutral **coordination layer** for global money movement across stablecoins and chains. Atum does not issue a currency, run a blockchain, favor a rail, or take custody. Integrators (wallets, PSPs, card issuers/acquirers, networks, fintechs, enterprises, developers) submit a payment request; **independent settlement providers compete in a marketplace** to fulfill it across any supported chain/stablecoin. Senders specify what they send; receivers get the asset they asked for. The network adds native authorization, identity, and reversible payments, and supports **agentic payments via x402 and MPP** — "both humans and agents are completing payments on Atum today." Seed led by **Variant** with **PayPal Ventures**, Abstract Ventures, Road Capital, Mirana Ventures, First Commit, Credibly Neutral; strategic advisor **Charlie Songhurst**. Reporting also cites **~$125M in committed deployments** beyond the raise. Founder/CEO **Pete Cooling** formerly led Visa's crypto product team and was Visa's rep on Linux's OpenWallet Foundation. Open to builders at atum.xyz.

## [1] Why it matters

Stablecoins are now a credible global rail, but liquidity is fragmented across dozens of chains and issuers (USDC, USDT, PYUSD, EURC, RLUSD…), pushing routing/FX/settlement complexity onto every integrator. Atum's bet is the same one SWIFT, card networks, and Circle's CPN make: the value sits in a neutral **coordination/clearing layer**, not in any single token or chain. By not taking custody and competing settlement providers on price/speed, Atum positions as "credibly neutral" infrastructure — distinct from custodial orchestrators and single-issuer networks. The agentic angle (x402/MPP) stakes an early claim on machine-to-machine stablecoin payments, a fast-emerging 2026 theme.

## [2] Prior art / internal context

- [[ClearBank to join Circle Payments Network for USDC and EURC]] — Circle's **CPN** is the closest conceptual rival: a governed, member-gated network, but single-issuer (USDC/EURC). Atum is multi-issuer, multi-rail, and permissionless to builders — the open-vs-governed contrast.
- [[Mastercard and BVNK launch global fiat-stablecoin money platform]] — BVNK (being acquired by Mastercard, up to ~$1.8B) is full-stack, custodial orchestration; Atum explicitly avoids custody and competing with participants.
- [[Euronet chooses Fireblocks for cross-border stablecoin payments]] — Fireblocks is an orchestration/custody-tech layer; same "abstract the chains" problem, different (custodial) posture.
- [[Cybrid raises $10M for stablecoin infrastructure]] — comparable seed-stage infra bet on abstracting stablecoin rails for fintechs/developers.

Also adjacent: Bridge (Stripe, ~$1.1B) as embedded developer orchestration API. Atum's differentiator is the **competitive settlement-provider marketplace** + non-custodial neutrality.

- **~$125M committed deployments** cited in coverage (not in the PR body — treat as reported, unverified).

## Challenge / red-team

1. **Is "open payments network" real tech or positioning?** Mostly positioning at launch — the primary claim is a non-custodial coordination layer with a competitive settlement marketplace. Core protocol/standards depth is unverified beyond the PR. Open.
2. **Who actually settles?** Atum doesn't move money; third-party settlement providers do. Its value depends on recruiting enough credible, well-capitalized providers to create real price competition — a classic cold-start. Open.
3. **Is the $125M "committed deployments" figure solid?** It appears in secondary coverage (KuCoin), not the PR body. Could be LOIs/pipeline, not live volume. Treat as reported, unverified. Open.
4. **Named integrators live today?** No specific marquee integrator named in the PR; "humans and agents are completing payments today" is unquantified (no TPV, no counts). Likely very early/test volume. Open.
5. **How is this different from Circle CPN?** CPN is governed/single-issuer (USDC/EURC); Atum is multi-issuer, multi-rail, permissionless to builders, non-custodial. Genuine differentiation — but CPN has Circle's balance sheet, compliance, and bank membership. Answered.
6. **vs Bridge/BVNK/Fireblocks?** Those are custodial/full-stack orchestration (Bridge in Stripe, BVNK into Mastercard). Atum's non-custodial neutrality is the wedge, but incumbents have distribution + capital Atum lacks. Answered.
7. **Does "credibly neutral, volume-only incentive" survive contact with economics?** Hard to bootstrap: no custody/float means thin take-rate; needs large volume to be viable. Monetization durability is unproven. Open.
8. **Regulatory exposure?** Claiming no custody / no counterparty is a deliberate licensing-avoidance posture, but "authorization, identity, reversible payments" edge close to regulated activity (money transmission, consumer protection) depending on jurisdiction. Open.
9. **Reversible stablecoin payments — technically how?** Stablecoin/chain settlement is natively final; "reversible" must live in Atum's coordination/escrow logic, not on-chain. Mechanism undisclosed; a key trust claim to scrutinize. Open.
10. **Agentic (x402/MPP) — substance or buzzword?** 2026 agentic-payments hype is real but nascent; Atum supporting the protocols ≠ meaningful agent volume. Early flag-planting. Open.
11. **Founder credibility?** Strong — Pete Cooling ex-Visa crypto lead + OpenWallet Foundation gives network/standards credibility and likely the VC access. Answered.
12. **Investor signal?** Variant + PayPal Ventures is a strong stablecoin-infra signal (PayPal has PYUSD). Suggests strategic interest, possibly PYUSD distribution. Answered.
13. **Is this a duplicate of any prior note?** No — no prior Atum note exists; this is the first coverage. Fresh. Answered.
14. **What would make this a $1B company vs a feature?** Needs to become the default routing layer across issuers before Circle/Stripe/Mastercard close the open-network gap themselves — a narrow, contested window. Open.
15. **Biggest risk?** Incumbent orchestration + issuer-native networks (CPN) capturing the coordination layer first, leaving Atum as a thin marketplace without volume. Open.

Importance: 3/5 — Credible team, strong backers (Variant/PayPal Ventures), and a differentiated non-custodial/open-network thesis in a hot category (stablecoin coordination + agentic payments). But it's a $13.5M seed just out of stealth with no named live integrators, unverified volume, and formidable incumbents (Circle CPN, Stripe/Bridge, Mastercard/BVNK). Notable to watch, not yet market-moving.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Stablecoin payments are moving from issuance/custody toward an orchestration/routing layer. Context: stablecoin payment volume ran ~$401–527bn Jan–Aug 2026, up ~42–63% YoY (per BCG / multiple 2026 trackers, via web searches as of 2026-10); total stablecoin market ~$315bn as of Jun 2026 (per CoinCentral/trackers). TAM estimates for "stablecoin payment infrastructure" vary widely and come from secondary research shops ($300bn 2025 → $1,715bn 2034 @ ~22% CAGR per ResearchIntelo) — treat as indicative, not verified `[UNSOURCED]` on methodology. Structure: fragmented across chains, stablecoins, apps and local rails; value today is captured by issuers (Circle, Paxos), API/off-ramp platforms (Bridge, BVNK, Conduit, Zero Hash) and custody (Fireblocks). Atum targets the gap *between* them — a credibly-neutral coordination layer that issues no currency, runs no chain, takes no custody. Why now: post-GENIUS/MiCA regulatory clarity + hyperscaler M&A (Stripe/Bridge, Mastercard/BVNK) are consolidating the issuance + API layers, which raises demand for a *neutral* router that is not owned by one acquirer — the thesis Atum is selling.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/settlement volume, take rate (bps), number of integrated rails/chains, and for a marketplace model — provider liquidity/fill rate (analysis). Players & basis of competition: issuer networks (Circle, Paxos), payment-API/off-ramp platforms (Bridge→Stripe, BVNK→Mastercard, Conduit, Zero Hash, Fipto, Due), custody-first (Fireblocks), and rail-neutral settlement layers (Frame, now Atum). Recent moves: Stripe closed Bridge Feb 2025 (~$1.1bn); Mastercard agreed to buy BVNK Mar 2026 for up to $1.8bn (close expected late 2026); Conduit raised a $36m Series A (Dragonfly/Altos), $53m cumulative. Atum's position: niche / new entrant (seed-stage, $13.5m, emerged from stealth Sep 2026). Moat claim = network effects + credible neutrality (it doesn't compete with participants and is "incentivized only by volume") — intangible/distribution moat, unproven `(analysis)`. Founder Pete Cooling ex-Visa crypto product lead is the main distribution asset.

**Comps & multiples.** Atum has no disclosed valuation/post-money, so no multiple can be computed — "no data" on Atum's own multiple. Peer anchors (all external, mostly M&A not public-market):
- Bridge — acquired by Stripe ~$1.1bn (Feb 2025); reported ~$454m revenue cited → ~2.4x EV/Rev `[UNSOURCED]` (revenue figure third-party, unverified); an alternate read put it at >100x on a much smaller run-rate — the two figures are irreconcilable, so **treat the Bridge multiple as not reliably computable**.
- BVNK — Mastercard deal up to $1.8bn (Mar 2026). BVNK had surpassed $20bn processing volume → implied ~9% price/volume = `$1.8bn / $20bn = 0.09x` price-to-TPV `(analysis)`; revenue undisclosed, so no EV/Rev.
- Conduit — ~$53m raised, valuation undisclosed; >$10bn annualized volume → no multiple ("no data").
- Internal comps (base): [[BVNK gets strategic investment from Citi Ventures]], [[BVNK surpasses $20bn in processing volume]], [[Coinbase and Mastercard in talks to buy stablecoin firm BVNK]], [[Coinflow raises $25M to challenge Stripe]], [[Brex launches stablecoin payment platform supporting Circle USDC]].
Distribution not computed (fewer than 3 comparable, verifiable multiples) — qualitative only. At $13.5m seed Atum is pre-revenue vs peers already at $10–20bn volume; valuation gap reflects stage, not an over/under-valuation flag.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Disintermediation by its own participants** — Atum sits *above* issuers and settlement providers who are themselves well-capitalized (Circle, Stripe/Bridge, Mastercard/BVNK); any of them can build the routing layer in-house and route around a thin coordination fee. "Incentivized only by volume" = a low, squeezable take.
2. **Cold-start / two-sided network risk** — the marketplace needs both integrators submitting requests AND settlement providers competing to fill; without density, fill rates/pricing degrade. "Open to builders today" ≠ live production volume (de-PR: PR says humans and agents pay "today" but gives no TPV).
3. **Regulatory + fraud-liability ambiguity** — it claims authorization, identity, and *reversible* payments without holding funds; who bears fraud/chargeback liability and AML obligation across a competitive settlement marketplace is unstated — a real exposure as GENIUS/MiCA enforcement tightens.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** If issuance (Bridge, BVNK) is being absorbed into closed stacks (Stripe, Mastercard), a *neutral* router is the natural counter-structure — but neutrality only has value if no single stack dominates routing `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: Atum is a bet that stablecoin rails stay multi-polar. Trigger to watch: first disclosed production TPV / a named large integrator (wallet or PSP) going live; what breaks it: a Stripe or Mastercard shipping an equivalent in-stack router, or Atum being acqui-hired before reaching volume.
