SaaScada and Zumo have launched a strategic partnership to provide UK financial institutions with a turnkey solution for regulated crypto services. By integrating Zumo’s digital asset infrastructure with SaaScada’s cloud-native core banking, firms can now offer crypto trading and custody alongside traditional cash accounts, capturing volume that previously sat outside the banking ecosystem.

SaaScada and Zumo have introduced a joint infrastructure solution that allows UK-based banks, neobanks, and wealth managers to integrate regulated cryptoasset services into their existing propositions. The partnership is designed to tackle the fragmentation of the customer experience, where users typically hold cash in a bank account but move those funds to external exchanges to purchase digital assets. According to FCA data, approximately 10% of UK adults hold cryptoassets, with the average value of those holdings on the rise.

The technical delivery is split between the two providers to ensure regulatory and operational compliance. Zumo, which is registered with the FCA as a cryptoasset business, manages the digital asset lifecycle, including custody, trading, and mandatory KYC/AML checks. SaaScada provides the underlying cloud-native core banking engine that manages the fiat cash accounts used to fund these purchases and receive proceeds from sales.

The service is available immediately and is built to be modular, allowing institutions to tailor the offering to their specific client base rather than adopting a one-size-fits-all package. Key features include the ability for clients to view their cash and crypto holdings in a single interface and for institutions to track all payment flows in real-time. This integration aims to help firms capture new revenue streams and improve retention by keeping crypto activity within the primary banking relationship, rather than committing to a multi-year internal build.

> "The UK is bringing cryptoassets within the FCA's regulatory remit, with a comprehensive regime set to take effect in October 2027. This is a game changer for our sector, unlocking pent-up institutional demand and helping the UK build its position as a leading digital assets hub. The question banks and wealth managers are asking has shifted from whether to offer digital assets to how fast they can move."

Nick Jones, Chief Executive Officer & Co-Founder at Zumo.

Unlike monolithic legacy systems, it operates on a modular basis, allowing banks to integrate specialist services through APIs. The company has established itself as a challenger to traditional core providers, focusing on reducing the operational overhead and technical debt that often hampers innovation in established firms. It provides the technological and regulatory framework required for financial institutions to offer crypto services, including custody, brokerage, and compliance checks. Based in the UK, Zumo has positioned itself as a bridge between the emerging digital asset economy and traditional finance, specifically targeting the institutional demand created by evolving UK regulations. The partnership combines SaaScada’s ledger and cash management capabilities with Zumo’s specialized crypto infrastructure, allowing wealth managers and neobanks to offer a unified financial experience without the need for multiple, disconnected platforms.

Original Press Release

**LONDON, UK, 13 October 2026:**SaaScada, the cloud-native core banking platform, and Zumo, the UK digital assets platform, today announced a strategic partnership giving UK financial institutions a ready-built route to offering regulated crypto services, alongside the cash accounts used to fund them.

Almost one in 10 UK adults hold cryptoassets, according to the FCA, and the typical value held is rising. Yet most of that activity happens outside the banking relationship, with customers keeping their assets on the exchanges they buy from.

The partnership between SaaScada and Zumo is designed to bring that activity inside banking relationships. Zumo, registered with the FCA as a cryptoasset business, handles the crypto side, from custody and trading to KYC/AML checks. SaaScada runs the cash accounts that fund purchases and receive sale proceeds.

Through the partnership, banks and wealth managers can:

*   Open a new revenue line and improve client retention by capturing crypto activity that currently happens elsewhere
*   Launch a regulated crypto offering on infrastructure that is already built and connected, rather than committing to a multi-year build
*   Give clients one view of their cash and crypto, rather than two disconnected experiences
*   Track every payment in and out in real time, alongside the rest of the client's banking

Nick Jones, Founder and Chief Executive Officer of Zumo, said: "The UK is bringing cryptoassets within the FCA's regulatory remit, with a comprehensive regime set to take effect in October 2027. This is a game changer for our sector, unlocking pent-up institutional demand and helping the UK build its position as a leading digital assets hub. The question banks and wealth managers are asking has shifted from whether to offer digital assets to how fast they can move."

With just over a year until the new regime takes effect, the partnership is open now to banks, fintechs, neobanks and wealth managers serving UK customers, and is built around each firm's existing proposition rather than offered as a fixed package.

Jones added: "Regulatory developments are baked into our architecture rather than added as an afterthought, which is what an institution needs when it moves on digital assets. Working with SaaScada means a bank or wealth manager can bring regulated crypto services into their proposition without stepping outside the core they've already selected."

Steve Round, Co-founder and President of SaaScada, said: "We do one thing, core banking, and we bring in specialists for everything around it. Zumo has done the regulatory and operational work that lets UK institutions offer digital assets with confidence. For a wealth manager or a bank whose clients are already invested elsewhere, that is the difference between those holdings sitting outside the relationship and becoming part of it."

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Two converging markets: cloud-native core-banking SaaS and embedded/regulated crypto infrastructure (crypto-as-a-service, CaaS). "Why now" is a hard regulatory clock: the UK's comprehensive cryptoasset regime goes live **25 October 2027**, with the authorisation application window closing **28 Feb 2027** — firms need custody/safeguarding/trading permissions to operate (per FCA / Freeths / Eversheds, as of 2026). Demand side is softer than the PR implies: the release cites "~10% / 1-in-10 UK adults" holding crypto, but the FCA's own 2025 survey shows ownership **dipped to ~8% from a 12% peak in 2024** (per FCA via Cointelegraph/Investors Centre, 2025) — so the retail TAM is flat-to-declining, not rising, and the "average value rising" framing (£1,595→£1,842) is the more defensible leg of the thesis. Structure: core banking is consolidating around a few scaled platforms (Thought Machine, Mambu, 10x, FIS/Finastra legacy) while crypto-infra is fragmented across custody specialists — SaaScada+Zumo bolt the two together via API rather than either building the other's stack.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: core-banking = ARR / NRR / accounts-on-platform; crypto-infra = AUC (assets under custody), trading volume, # institutional clients. Basis of competition = regulatory coverage + distribution/integration depth, not price. Players: on core banking — [[Thought Machine lands GBP 45M funding as losses widen]], Mambu, [[Philip Barnett named executive vice chair at 10x Banking|10x Banking]], Finastra ([[Finastra offloads Universal Banking division to Pollen Street Capital]]); on the crypto side the real competitive threat is bank-grade custody/CaaS providers — **Zodia Custody** (Standard Chartered-backed, FCA-registered, UK — the closest like-for-like competitor to Zumo), Fireblocks, BitGo, Bitpanda Technology Solutions (powers N26/Deutsche Bank), plus [[Binance introduces crypto-as-a-service for institutions]], [[OKX launches Rubix digital-assets-as-a-service stack]] and [[WisdomTree appoints BNY for banking-as-a-service infrastructure for digital assets|BNY digital-asset BaaS]]. Recent moves: Zumo/Quantum Chain quantum-secure infra partnership (7 Oct 2026); Zumo's UK Cryptoasset Regulation Tracker (Sep 2026). Protagonist position: **niche / catching-up** `(analysis)` — SaaScada is sub-scale as a core vendor (see comps), and Zumo is a small CaaS player versus Standard Chartered-owned Zodia; the moat is a *bundled* fiat-ledger-plus-crypto integration (switching cost once a bank adopts the joint stack), not standalone scale.

**Comps & multiples.** All relevant names are private; no public market caps, so EV/Rev and EV/EBITDA = **no data / [UNSOURCED]**. Last-round valuations (round valuation, not market cap):
- SaaScada — total funding only **~$5.9m** across seed/angel/VC rounds (TX Ventures round undisclosed); no post-money disclosed → valuation **no data** (per PitchBook/Tracxn, 2026).
- Zumo — **~$32m** raised over 7 rounds, Series B; earliest post-seed valuation flagged at only **£5–10m**, recent valuation redacted → **no data** (per Tracxn, 2026).
- Thought Machine — Series D doubled valuation to **$2.7bn** on ~$75m ARR 2025 → implied P/S ≈ `$2,700m / $75m ≈ 36x` (rich, but on a hyper-growth, loss-making SaaS base; losses widening per [[Thought Machine lands GBP 45M funding as losses widen]]).
- Mambu — **$5.5bn** valuation (2024) on ~$129m revenue → implied P/S ≈ `$5,500m / $129m ≈ 43x` (per getLatka/PitchBook, 2024; stale, likely marked down since).
Read: the core-banking peers carry 35–43x revenue multiples set in the 2021–22 cycle and are likely overstated today; SaaScada and Zumo are two to three orders of magnitude smaller than these comps — this is a minnow-pair partnership, not a scaled-incumbent move. Distribution not computed (only 2 comparable multiples, both stale private marks).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory timing / execution** — the whole thesis hinges on the Oct-2027 FCA regime; Zumo is "FCA-registered" under the *current narrow AML regime*, which is NOT the same as being authorised under the new comprehensive regime (safeguarding/trading permissions still to be won by Feb-2027). If Zumo's authorisation slips, the "ready-built route" stops being ready. Second-order: banks that adopt now bear migration risk if the partner doesn't clear authorisation.
2. **Partner scale / counterparty concentration** — both are sub-scale (SaaScada ~$5.9m raised; Zumo ~$32m) versus Zodia (Standard Chartered) and Fireblocks/BitGo. A bank picking a joint minnow stack over a bank-owned custodian is making a bet on two small balance sheets; survivorship/funding risk is real.
3. **Soft/declining retail demand + bank appetite** — UK crypto ownership fell to ~8% (2025) and nine major UK banks still cap or block exchange payments (per crypto.news, 2026). The new regime does NOT compel banks to offer crypto — so "pent-up demand" is a seller's claim, and conversion from "announced" to "live/adopted" is unproven (no named bank client in the PR).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The move is a *packaging* play, not a re-rating: it signals that core-banking vendors will increasingly embed third-party crypto-infra via API rather than build custody in-house — pressure on legacy cores (FIS/Finastra) that can't bridge fiat↔digital. Falsifiable thesis: SaaScada+Zumo will convert this into at least one *named, live* UK bank/wealth-manager deployment before the Feb-2027 authorisation deadline; trigger to watch = Zumo securing full FCA authorisation under the new regime (not just registration). What breaks it = Zumo failing/slipping authorisation, or a Standard Chartered-backed Zodia-type stack winning the same wealth-manager accounts on counterparty strength.
