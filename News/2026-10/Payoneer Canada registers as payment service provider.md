Payoneer Canada registers with Bank of Canada as payment service provider


 
 Maria Nikolova 
 
 
 
 0 Comments 
 
 
 October 13, 2026 
 
 
Fintech company Payoneer Global Inc (NASDAQ:PAYO) today announced that Payoneer Canada Limited has been registered with the Bank of Canada as a payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA).
The registration strengthens Payoneer’s ability to serve businesses based in Canada, and businesses looking to expand into the Canadian market, and reaffirms Payoneer’s long-term commitment to the region. The registration supports Payoneer’s continued provision of regulated payment services in Canada, including local-currency collection capabilities.
This new license delivers value for businesses in Canada operating globally, as well as for companies outside the country transacting with Canadian counterparties, by:
Expanding regulatory footprint: Payoneer is among Payment Service Providers (PSPs) that have completed the regulatory approval process under Canada’s new Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), marking an important milestone for the company’s local compliance and global regulatory expansion.
Supporting banking relationships: Registration reinforces the regulatory foundation supporting Payoneer’s relationships with its Canadian banking partners and cross-border payment services.
Enabling continued customer support: Businesses onboarding with Payoneer Canada Limited can continue to access Payoneer services available in Canada, supported by locally tailored onboarding and compliance processes.
 “Achieving registration under the RPAA marks an important milestone for Payoneer in Canada,” said Tsafi Goldman, Chief Legal & Governance Officer at Payoneer. “It reinforces the global regulatory foundation supporting our customers and partners and reflects Payoneer’s commitment to maintain compliance with Canada’s regulatory requirements.” 
Payoneer supports customers in over 190 countries and territories, leveraging a robust network of nearly 100 banking and payment service provider partnerships. Payoneer maintains a strong global regulatory foundation, with licenses across multiple jurisdictions, ensuring customers can trust that their funds are secure, accessible, and supported by the highest compliance standards. Payoneer’s expanded regulatory footprint strengthens its ability to support customers operating globally with consistency, reliability, and scale.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On **13 Oct 2026** Payoneer Global Inc. (NASDAQ:PAYO) said its subsidiary **Payoneer Canada Limited** was **registered with the Bank of Canada as a Payment Service Provider (PSP) under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA)**. De-PR'd: this is a **registration, not a license** — Payoneer's PR and several outlets loosely call it a "new license", but the RPAA regime is explicitly a **registration/supervision regime, not an authorization/licensing one** (BoC itself frames it as registration + oversight). What it concretely enables: continued provision of regulated retail-payment services in Canada, **local-currency collection**, and — the real point — it **shores up the legal basis for Payoneer's existing Canadian banking-partner relationships**.

**Why framed this way / what it reveals.** The RPAA created a **mandatory** regime: any PSP doing retail payment activity touching Canadian end-users had to apply in the Nov 1–15, 2024 transition window and must be registered to operate after **8 Sep 2025**; non-registration → fines / cease-activity. So Payoneer "achieving registration" is **table-stakes compliance that it could not legally avoid**, dressed up as "expanding regulatory footprint" and "global regulatory expansion". The genuinely load-bearing line is the banking-partner one: registration de-risks the correspondent/bank sponsorship Payoneer relies on in Canada. The "local-currency collection" capability is the only tangible customer-facing item, and it is a **continuation**, not a new product.

**Second order:** the announcement is best read as **de-risking ahead of the pending Nuvei take-private** (business-as-usual regulatory hygiene while the deal is in its long ~12-month regulatory close) rather than a growth catalyst.

## [1] Competitors / peers
The RPAA registry is **crowded, not exclusive**: the BoC published its **first ~300 fully-registered PSPs in Oct 2025** with **1,500+ applications pending** (first cohort included Wealthsimple, Koho, Shopify's payments arm, Trolley, Zum Rails, etc. — see [[Bank of Canada registers non-bank fintechs as payment providers]]). Payoneer is **one of many** entrants completing the same mandatory step, i.e. **parity, not differentiation**.

Direct cross-border/SMB peers — **Wise, Airwallex, PayPal/Xoom** — all face the identical RPAA obligation if they serve Canadian users; registration confers **no moat** over them. Airwallex is expanding via stack acquisitions; Wise competes on FX transparency. Payoneer's edge in Canada is its marketplace-seller network (Amazon/Walmart/eBay payouts), not this filing.

**Why the lay of the land is this way:** Canada moved late to supervise non-bank payments (RPAA is a 2021 act, operational only 2024–25). The first wave of registrations is therefore a **one-time regulatory on-ramp the whole industry walks through simultaneously** → the signal value of any single registration is low; the only differentiation is *speed* (early vs pending) and *clean approval*.

## [2] Company history / fit
Payoneer has run a **serial multi-jurisdiction licensing/registration playbook**: in-principle cross-border payment-aggregator authorization in India (Jan 2026 — [[Payoneer receives in-principle authorization as a cross-border payment aggregator in India]]), local collection in Indonesia and Mexico ([[Payoneer launches local collection in Indonesia and Mexico]]), a US National Trust Bank charter filing, and stablecoin rails via Bridge. The company states it supports customers in **190+ countries** with **~100 banking/PSP partnerships**. Canada RPAA fits this pattern exactly: **keep the global regulatory lattice intact so banking partners stay comfortable**.

**Why it acts this way:** Payoneer's profit is structurally dependent on (a) float/interest income on ~$7–8B of customer balances and (b) a slowly commoditizing take-rate. Its durable asset is **trust/compliance infrastructure that lets banks and marketplaces route cross-border flows through it**. Each registration is maintenance capex on that moat — necessary to *retain* access, not to *win* new share. Q2 2026: revenue **$274.3M (+5%)**, ex-interest revenue **$222.2M (+10%)**, volume **$23.7B (+15%)**, B2B volume **$4.3B (+48%)**, ~2M active customers, net loss **$(2.4)M**. The business is intact but ratestill-dependent and modestly growing.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: low.** This is not a new capability, a new product launch, or a new reporting period — it is **completion of a mandatory registration** that Payoneer had to obtain to keep operating in Canada. Traction is **zero new** (no disclosed Canada volume, customer count, or revenue tied to the registration). The only substantive value-add is **legal/continuity**: it firms up banking-partner relationships and permits continued local-currency collection.

**Why the value-add is thin, one level deeper:** in the cross-border stack, margin is captured by the card networks (Visa/MC), by FX spread, and increasingly by stablecoin rails — **not** by holding a Canadian PSP registration. Registration is a cost of doing business (ongoing risk-management, funds-safeguarding, incident-reporting, annual reports), i.e. it **adds compliance overhead**, not margin. It protects the right to compete; it does not improve the economics.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
No market reaction of note — PAYO trades near the **$7.40 cash offer** from Nuvei ([[Nuvei to acquire Payoneer for $2.75 billion]]), which anchors the equity and mutes any news-driven move; deal expected to close **mid-2027**. Regulatory backdrop: the BoC is now in **active supervision/enforcement** mode (first RPAA enforcement decisions published Aug 2026), so being cleanly registered is a **downside-protection** item for Payoneer and, by extension, for the Nuvei combination's Canadian footprint.

**Why the market shrugs (second order):** for an acquisition target trading to a fixed cash price, incremental regulatory hygiene changes nothing about the payout — it only marginally reduces **deal/close risk** on the Canadian perimeter. The counterintuitive read: the most important audience for this press release is **not customers or investors but Payoneer's Canadian banking partners and the Nuvei deal's regulatory reviewers** — it's a confidence signal, not a growth event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Cross-border B2B/SMB payments is a large, structurally growing pool: the cross-border segment is cited as the largest share (~60%) of the B2B payments market, growing ~10–11% CAGR (per Fortune Business Insights/Mordor, via web, as of 2026) — attribute to secondary analyst reports, exact TAM figures vary wildly by methodology (one set: B2B payments $4.97tn→$5.31tn 2025→2026), so treat as directional, not a hard TAM. The value chain is **fragmenting into a licence-heavy middle layer**: marketplace payouts + multi-currency receiving accounts sit between the card networks/banks and the SMB. The moat here is regulatory, not technical — "why now" is that jurisdictions are simultaneously formalising PSP regimes. Canada's **Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA)** went live with a Bank of Canada PSP registry; an initial snapshot showed **~320 PSPs registered, ~900+ applications still pending** (per Bank of Canada registry / NCFA, via web, 2025–26). So a BoC RPAA registration is now **table-stakes to legally operate**, not a differentiator — barrier-to-entry rising, but every serious peer clears it.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: **volume (TPV), take rate (bps), ARPU, active customers**. Payoneer FY2025 (per 8-K earnings release via investor.payoneer.com + SEC 10-K, filed 2026-02-26): revenue **$1,052.8m (+8%)**, revenue **ex-interest $821.2m (+14%)**, volume **$87.5bn (+9%)**, blended take rate **120 bps (−2 bps YoY; Q4 only 111 bps)**, ARPU **$488 (+15%)**, active ICPs **536k (−4% YoY)**, net income **$73.2m (−40%)**, adj. EBITDA ex-interest **$85–95m**. Players & basis of competition: **Wise** (consumer+SMB transfers, competes on price), **Airwallex** (SMB/platform FX+acquiring, competes on product breadth), **PayPal/Xoom, Adyen, Stripe** (adjacent). Recent moves: Wise FY26 (to Mar-26) net revenue **$2.5bn (+19%)**, cross-border volume **$243bn (+31%)**, take rate **cut to 52 bps** from 58 (per Wise FY26 RNS, via web, 2026-06-26); Airwallex Series H **$320m at $11bn valuation**, ~$1.3bn annualised revenue, ~$266bn annualised volume (per Businesswire/CNBC, via web, 2026-06). Payoneer's position: **niche/catching-up** — smaller volume than Wise, slower growth than Airwallex, but a genuinely differentiated **marketplace-payout + licence network** moat (190+ countries, ~100 banking partners, licences across jurisdictions). **Risk flags.**
1. **Licence ≠ moat on its own.** ~320 PSPs already registered under RPAA; a BoC registration is competitive table-stakes. Second-order: the regulatory "moat" narrative only holds in aggregate across many jurisdictions, and raises compliance cost for everyone, compressing the long-tail — but it does not fence out Wise/Airwallex, who clear the same bars.
2. **Take-rate erosion + rate dependence.** Blended take rate slipping (120→111 bps in Q4) while ~22% of revenue is interest income on float. Second-order: a Canadian local-currency-collection licence adds volume at a declining unit economics, and falling rates hit the float layer directly — growth in "regulated services" may not translate into margin.
3. **Change-of-control overhang.** Mid-2027 Nuvei close with a multi-jurisdiction regulatory perimeter; new licence applications/registrations are being pursued by an entity whose ownership is in flux. Second-order: integration/ownership uncertainty can slow onboarding and partner trust in exactly the local-market relationships this registration is meant to shore up.

**What this changes (idea-lens) `(analysis)`.** Marginal, not re-rating: this is defensive compliance infrastructure (protecting Canadian banking-partner relationships and local-currency collection), not a new revenue line — consistent with Payoneer's "expand regulatory footprint" playbook ahead of the Nuvei deal. Watch: whether the combined Nuvei+Payoneer uses this plus Nuvei's existing Canadian acquiring (Interac/IBT) to cross-sell pay-in+pay-out locally. Falsifier: if RPAA registration coincided with a *new* Canadian product/partner launch rather than "continued provision" of existing services, the thesis that this is pure maintenance would be wrong.
