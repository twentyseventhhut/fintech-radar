Taptap Send, a leading cross-border payments platform, officially launched its money transfer services in Bahrain yesterday during Fintech Forward 2026 at Exhibition World Bahrain.
The expansion follows the company’s receipt of a Payment Service Provider licence from the Central Bank of Bahrain (CBB), marking Bahrain as Taptap Send’s second GCC market after the UAE. The platform enables users across more than 35 countries to transfer money to recipients in over 90 destination markets.
“Bahrain has built a strong reputation for financial innovation, supported by the Central Bank of Bahrain’s forward-looking regulatory approach,” said Michael Faye, chief executive and co-founder of Taptap Send. “Securing this licence reflects our commitment to Bahrain’s financial services ecosystem as we continue our regional expansion.”
Dalal Buhejji, executive director of business development for financial services at the Bahrain Economic Development Board (Bahrain EDB), welcomed the move, noting that Taptap Send’s entry will support the continued evolution of cross-border payments and strengthen the kingdom’s fintech sector.
The launch builds on Taptap Send’s recent regulatory approvals from the Central Bank of the UAE, where it secured Stored Value Facilities, Retail Payment Services (Category II), and Exchange Business (Category IV) licences to bolster its footprint across the Gulf.

# Context-enrichment: Taptap Send launches money transfers in Bahrain
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- On 2026-10-13/15 at Fintech Forward 2026 (Exhibition World Bahrain), Taptap Send **launched live** money-transfer services in Bahrain, its **second GCC market after the UAE**. This is a real go-live, not a "will explore".
- Legal basis: a **Payment Service Provider licence from the Central Bank of Bahrain (CBB)**. The local entity is **Taptap Send Bahrain B.S.C.**, licensed as a Payment Service Provider / **Ancillary Service Provider**, registration **185646-1**. "Ancillary Service Provider" is the lighter CBB category — i.e. Taptap operates as a licensed money-transfer/remittance operator, not a stored-value or full PSP tier; it rides existing rails rather than holding client balances.
- Scope claim: users in "more than 35 countries" can send to "over 90 destination markets". **This is the global platform footprint, not Bahrain-specific corridors** — the PR reuses the group-level number to make a single-country send-side launch look larger. The actually-new fact is narrow: **one new SEND country (Bahrain) added to an existing network.**
- Companion announcement at the same event: **Beyon Connect "eKey 2.0" integration** with Taptap Send — i.e. Bahrain's national digital-ID eKYC plugged into Taptap's onboarding. This is the operationally meaningful detail (local KYC/onboarding rail) that the headline buries.
- **Why framed this way:** a single-market diaspora send-side launch is low-drama, so the PR leans on (a) the group's 35/90 reach and (b) the CBB's "innovation" halo + an EDB quote. The de-PR'd substance: Taptap now has a CBB licence and can acquire Bahrain-resident senders (largely South-Asian/African expat labour).

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Incumbent exchange houses:** LuLu Exchange (16 Bahrain branches, LuLu Money app) and BenefitPay (Bahrain's dominant e-wallet, 500k+ users) — the two already integrated for cross-border remittance; Beyon Money also lives inside BenefitPay. These own the resident send-side and cash/branch network Taptap lacks.
- **Digital challengers:** Remitly already serves Bahrain send-side via local partners (Travelex, Zenj). [[Wise gets UAE Central Bank approval for products]] (Oct 2025) shows Wise's parallel GCC push — but via the heavier SVF + Retail Payment Services (Cat 2) CBUAE licences, a broader mandate than Taptap's Ancillary PSP tier.
- Position: **catching up / parity at best.** Taptap's edge is app-first UX + low fees to specific African/South-Asian payout corridors; its disadvantage is no branch/cash network and a late entry into a market already served by LuLu, BenefitPay, Beyon and Remitly.
- **Second-order:** value in GCC remittance is shifting from the transfer itself (fee-compressed) to KYC/onboarding + payout-last-mile. The eKey 2.0 tie-up is Taptap trying to neutralise the incumbents' local-rail advantage.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Taptap Send (founded 2018, CEO/co-founder **Michael Faye**, also behind Sendwave/GiveDirectly lineage) is a **diaspora remittance app**: send from high-income/Gulf countries to mobile-money/bank/cash payout in Africa, Asia, Caribbean, LatAm. Raised a **$65M Series B (Dec 2021, led by Spark Capital; Reid Hoffman, Canaan, Breyer, Flourish, Wamda et al.)**, total funding >$80M. No fresh raise disclosed with this launch.
- GCC is a logical expansion: the Gulf is one of the **largest remittance-SOURCE regions globally**, dominated by expat labour from exactly Taptap's payout corridors. UAE first ([[Wise gets UAE Central Bank approval for products]] era — Taptap secured CBUAE Stored Value Facilities + Retail Payment Services Cat II + Exchange Business Cat IV), now Bahrain.
- Fits the recent corridor-expansion pattern: [[VPBank and Taptap Send launch zero-fee remittances to Vietnam]] (Jun 2026) added a payout corridor; Bahrain adds a send market. **Why:** remittance take-rates are commoditised, so growth = more licensed send geographies × more payout corridors; each new jurisdiction is incremental volume, not a step-change.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** This is geographic expansion of an existing product under a new licence — not a new capability. No new product, no stablecoin/infra angle (contrast [[Zepz launches stablecoin-powered Sendwave Wallet for digital dollars]], where a peer actually changed the rail).
- **Traction disclosed for Bahrain: none.** No corridor list, no fee schedule, no user/volume target for Bahrain. The 35/90 figures are group-level. "Launched" is credible (licence + live app + eKey integration) but adoption is unproven.
- **Value-add reality:** genuine for Taptap (adds a licensed, high-propensity-to-remit expat send base) but marginal for the market — a fifth-plus option in a market already covered by LuLu, BenefitPay, Beyon, Remitly. **Who captures margin:** with fees compressed, the durable value is the onboarding/eKYC rail (eKey 2.0) and payout-corridor economics, not the Bahrain send fee itself.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Expect further GCC build-out (Saudi/Kuwait/Qatar the logical next licences) — the Gulf sent a **record ~$161B in outward remittances in 2025 (+13.6% YoY)**, the highest globally; Bahrain's slice is small (digital-remittance segment ~$43M est.; total outbound low-single-digit $B).
- **Regulatory backdrop:** CBB is actively courting fintech (EDB endorsement, Fintech Forward) — licence access is easy; the hard part is distribution against entrenched exchange houses.
- **Risks:** fee compression, FX-margin squeeze, incumbent cash-network moat, and dependence on a few payout corridors. **Counterintuitive second-order:** easy CBB licensing lowers the entry barrier for everyone, so a licence is table-stakes, not a moat — it commoditises the send side and pushes the real competition to payout economics and super-app distribution (BenefitPay).

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** GCC is the world's largest source of outbound worker remittances: $161bn in 2025, +13.6% YoY (+~$19bn), ahead of the US at $107bn (per GCC-Stat, via Gulf News / Arab News, as of Oct 2026). UAE led at $62.1bn (+6.3%), Saudi $58bn (+26.9%). Bahrain is a small slice — no standalone 2025 figure published; last firm data point ~$3.2bn in 2022 [UNSOURCED for 2025] — so this is a beachhead, not a size play. Structure: the send side is fragmented (bank branches, exchange houses like LuLu/Al Fardan, digital MTOs) but the *licensed* digital layer is thin — a PSP licence from the CBB is the real moat/barrier here, which is why the story is a licence, not a product. Drivers / "why now": GCC regulators (CBB, CBUAE) are actively courting licensed digital MTOs to move cash-based corridors onto rails they can supervise; the corridor economics (South/Southeast Asian migrant base) are structurally durable as expat employment and non-oil activity expand (analysis).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: send volume (TPV), blended take rate (FX margin + fee), per-transaction margin, corridor count. Players: incumbent cash/exchange houses (LuLu, Al Fardan, BenefitPay domestic rails) vs digital MTOs (Wise, Remitly, Taptap Send) vs bank-led corridors (Mashreq, Careem Pay). Basis of competition: price (FX spread + fee), payout reach / last-mile, and — in the GCC specifically — being licensed at all. Recent moves: Remitly won a CBUAE Stored Value Facilities licence (2026-07, [[Remitly wins Stored Value Facilities licence in UAE]]); Wise got CBUAE product approval (2025-10); LuLu Money Business partnered Mastercard on SME rails (2026-10, [[LuLu Money Business partners Mastercard on SME ecosystem]]). Taptap Send's position: a niche follower in the GCC — Bahrain is its *second* GCC market (after UAE SVF/RPS/Exchange licences), vs Remitly/Wise already live. Moat: its edge is underserved-corridor payout depth (90+ destination markets, strong African/Asian corridors), not GCC distribution (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** Taptap Send is private: last round $65m Series B (Dec 2021, led by Spark Capital), ~$715m post-money valuation per PitchBook (via FintechFutures/TechCrunch) — note this is a ~5-year-old round valuation, NOT a current mark, and no newer raise is public. No revenue/TPV disclosed → EV/Revenue, take rate = no data. External public peers for a take-rate read: Remitly (NAS:RELY) blended take rate >2%, FY2025 revenue $1.64bn (+29%), FY2026 guide $1.94–1.96bn (+19–20%); Wise cross-border take rate ~0.50%, $69.3bn cross-border volume in the quarter to Jun-2026 (+26%) (via Motley Fool / xflowpay, Oct 2026). Arithmetic illustration of the margin spread Taptap sits inside: Wise 0.50% vs Remitly >2% = ~4x gap on the same $1 sent — distribution not computed for Taptap (no disclosed figures), qualitative only. Takeaway: Taptap operates in the higher-take, underserved-corridor segment (closer to Remitly than Wise), where fees are higher but FX/compliance costs and CAC are also higher.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Thin, compressing margins.** Transparent-fee players (Wise 0.50% take rate) plus referral-loop scaling compress economics industry-wide; a higher-take model is only defensible while the corridors stay underserved — second-order: Bahrain is a *competed*, licensed corridor (Remitly/Wise present), so Taptap enters at the low-margin end, not a greenfield.
2. **Disintermediation by stablecoin rails.** USDC/stablecoin transfers settle near-instantly at ~cents; even Remitly is piloting USDC. Taptap's protection is last-mile payout, but if payout partners adopt stablecoin settlement, the FX-margin layer Taptap monetises gets squeezed by another stack layer.
3. **Licence/regulatory dependence + sub-scale market.** The whole story rests on one CBB PSP licence; GCC MTO licences carry AML/sanctions and capital obligations, and Bahrain's own outbound pool is small (~low-single-digit $bn). Execution risk: a lot of compliance overhead for a market that is strategic positioning (GCC #2) more than a volume driver.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is incremental GCC footprint-building, not a re-rating — Taptap is catching up to Remitly/Wise on the licensed-digital map rather than opening new ground (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: Taptap's GCC bet pays off only if it lands a high-volume corridor (e.g. a Saudi licence, the $58bn/+27% market) — watch for a next licence or a bank/wallet distribution deal; absent that, the Bahrain launch is a flag-plant that won't move the company's economics. What breaks the thesis: a fresh (post-2021) funding round or disclosed GCC TPV showing real traction.
