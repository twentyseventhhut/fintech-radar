PPRO partners with Damisa for stablecoin-powered treasury and cross-border settlement
 LONDON, 15 October 2026. 
Damisa, the regulated stablecoin-native settlement platform for cross-border business payments, today announced that PPRO, the local payments platform behind global payment service providers and merchants, has partnered with Damisa. What began as a focused deployment in the Latin American region is now scaling with PPRO’s global expansion.
Through the partnership, PPRO taps into Damisa's onramp and off-ramp network to move money. Damisa converts the funds into regulated stablecoins and moves them across borders the same day. The model replaces multi-bank correspondent chains with a single regulated rail, reducing the capital that has to be prefunded in each market and giving PPRO full visibility of every payment.
 STARTING IN LATIN AMERICA 
Latin America was the natural starting point. It is one of the fastest-growing regions for local payments, and one of the hardest for moving money out efficiently. By leveraging stablecoin rails, Damisa gives PPRO a faster and more predictable route to move its funds across Latin American markets.
Following the initial rollout, the partnership is now being extended to additional markets as PPRO grows its global footprint, with Damisa supporting collection, cross-border movement and local-currency settlement.
 WHAT THEY SAID 
 “PPRO is one of the most respected names in local payments, having built their business on getting the local details right. We are proud to be the rail that makes their cross-border money movements faster and more efficient. Latin America was the proving ground: collect locally, move on stablecoin rails, settle in PPRO’s currency of choice the same day.” Jordan Lawrence, CEO and Co-Founder, Damisa 
 
 “As we expand our platform globally, we continue to need fast and reliable ways to move funds across borders. Damisa gives us a regulated, stablecoin-powered solution that lets us settle in our currency of choice. The results in Latin America made the case for expanding it further.” Almir Majcic, VP Treasury, PPRO 
 WHY STABLECOIN SETTLEMENT, AND WHY NOW? 
Correspondent banking has been retreating from many of the corridors where cross-border demand is growing fastest, while regulation of stablecoins has matured through frameworks such as MiCA in the European Union and the GENIUS Act in the United States . Together, those shifts have made regulated stablecoin rails a credible settlement layer for institutional payment flows, not just for crypto-native businesses.
Damisa was built for that moment. It operates as a regulated, multi-jurisdiction platform, holding an Australian Financial Services License with AUSTRAC digital currency exchange and remittance registration, registration as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC in Canada , and a VASP registration in Poland, with Malta CASP and Payment Institution authorisation in progress. Sanctions, AML and transaction screening run on every payment. Damisa settled more than $70 million in the first half of 2026 across more than 90 countries and 70 currencies.
 ABOUT DAMISA 
Damisa is a regulated, stablecoin-native settlement platform for cross-border business payments. It helps payment service providers, FX houses and global trading businesses collect, move and settle funds on regulated stablecoin rails, with local-currency payout across Africa, Latin America and Asia and coverage of more than 90 countries and 70 currencies. Headquartered in London, Damisa operates regulated entities in Australia, Canada and soon to be Europe with further authorisations in progress.

PPRO is a leading local payments platform that helps payment service providers (PSPs) and merchants grow their businesses by letting their customers pay local. With a single connection, PPRO activates, accepts, and manages local payments, giving businesses access to consumers in some of the world’s largest ecommerce markets. # Context-enrichment: Damisa and PPRO partner on stablecoin cross-border payments
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
PPRO (local-payments platform; PSP/merchant connectivity) has made **Damisa** (London-based, ~2025-founded, regulated stablecoin-native settlement platform) its rail for moving its own treasury/collected funds across borders. Flow: PPRO collects locally → Damisa on-ramps and converts to regulated stablecoins → moves cross-border same-day → off-ramps and settles in PPRO's currency of choice. Pitch: replace multi-bank correspondent chains with one regulated rail, cut per-market prefunding, give PPRO full payment visibility. Started as a "focused deployment" in LatAm, now "being extended to additional markets."

**De-PR'd reality:** This is a **treasury/own-funds settlement arrangement for PPRO, not a merchant- or consumer-facing product** — PPRO uses Damisa to repatriate/move money it has collected, not to let shoppers pay in stablecoins. Note the asymmetry in the quotes: PPRO's VP Treasury (Almir Majcic) speaks, not a product/commercial exec — confirms this is a back-office FX/liquidity play.

**What's conspicuously absent (the anti-PR gate):** zero deal-specific numbers — no corridor list, no volume moved for PPRO, no FX-spread or fee economics, no exclusivity, no contract term. The only hard figure ($70M settled H1 2026 across 90+ countries / 70 currencies) is **Damisa's total platform volume**, not PPRO flow — a flattering base borrowed to dress a bilateral announcement. "What began as a focused deployment… is now scaling" is vague; no start date for the LatAm pilot is given, so "proven in LatAm" is unverifiable from the release.

**Why framed this way:** Damisa is an early-stage challenger (pre-seed £2.25M, Fuel Ventures) that needs marquee logos; PPRO is "one of the most respected names in local payments." The PR is engineered to borrow PPRO's credibility for Damisa far more than it signals a strategic shift at PPRO. The "regulated… MiCA/GENIUS Act… AFSL/FINTRAC/VASP" paragraph is trust-signalling to institutional buyers who fear crypto counterparty risk — the real product here is **compliance, not crypto novelty**.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Regulated stablecoin B2B settlement is now a crowded field: **BVNK** (FCA EMI, MFSA EMI + MiCA CASP; 75% of embedded-wallet volume from PSP/fintech clients, ~263x YoY growth — the direct structural competitor for a PSP buyer), **Bridge** (Stripe-owned), **Conduit** (emerging-market B2B payouts, 100+ countries, T+0/T+1), **Circle** (USDC issuer + CPN), plus Fireblocks/Sphere infrastructure. In the corpus PPRO itself already partnered with **[[PPRO and Coinbase partner on stablecoin payments for US merchants]]** (May 2026, merchant-side) — so PPRO multi-sources stablecoin rails rather than committing to Damisa. Damisa's own prior tie-up with **[[dLocal and Damisa partner to expand cross-border settlement in APAC]]** (and the April retelling [[dLocal and Damisa partner on APAC cross-border settlement]]) shows Damisa stitches together third-party local rails rather than owning them.

**Position:** Damisa is **catching up, not leading.** Its edge vs BVNK/Bridge is not technology but **multi-jurisdiction licensing breadth relative to its size** (AFSL+AUSTRAC, FINTRAC MSB, Poland VASP; Malta CASP + PI "in progress"). But against BVNK's actual PSP traction and Circle's distribution, Damisa is sub-scale. **Why PPRO picked a small vendor:** likely because this is low-stakes own-treasury flow (not customer-facing, so blast radius is small), and Damisa may offer sharper pricing / more hands-on corridor coverage in exactly the hard-to-exit markets (LatAm, Africa) where BVNK/Circle are thinner.

## [2] Company history / fit
Damisa: founded 2025 by Jordan Lawrence (ex-Volt), Panos Dandolas, Maximilian Marenbach; £2.25M pre-seed led by Fuel Ventures (early 2025). Trajectory in corpus: [[Damisa launches local payment rails across six GCC markets]] (Jul 2025) → dLocal APAC tie-up (Apr–May 2026) → [[UK fintech Damisa lands Australian Financial Services Licence]] (Jul 2026) → PPRO (Oct 2026). Consistent logic: a **licensing-and-orchestration layer** that resells on/off-ramps and local rails, racking up regulated entities to sell "regulated stablecoin settlement" to institutions. For PPRO the fit is clean: PPRO is a collections/local-methods specialist with a genuine problem — money pooled in hard-to-repatriate markets requires prefunded capital in each one. **Why PPRO acts this way:** its model ties up working capital in local floats; a same-day stablecoin rail directly attacks that trapped-capital cost. That is the one genuinely rational core of the deal.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Not novel.** "Collect local → stablecoin bridge → local payout" is the standard 2025–26 LatAm playbook (B2B stablecoin flow grew from <$100M/mo in early 2023 to >$6B/mo by mid-2025 per industry data; 71% of LatAm firms report using stablecoins). Many vendors do exactly this. **The value-add that is real:** removing per-market prefunding for PPRO — a concrete working-capital saving, if volumes are real. **What's unproven:** no PPRO-specific volume, no corridors named, "scaling to more markets" is aspirational. Traction claimed is Damisa's aggregate ($70M H1), which is modest for the category and not attributable to PPRO. **Who captures the margin:** the FX spread + on/off-ramp fee sits with Damisa and its local liquidity partners; PPRO captures working-capital relief and speed. Because PPRO can (and does) multi-source rails, Damisa has **little pricing power** — it is a swappable rail, not a lock-in — which caps the strategic value of this logo for Damisa.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Backdrop is favourable in principle: correspondent-banking retreat + maturing stablecoin regulation (MiCA, US GENIUS Act) legitimise regulated rails for institutional flows. **But the killer second-order risk the PR hides:** the deal "started in Latin America," and the deepest LatAm corridor — **Brazil** — now **bans stablecoin/crypto as a settlement rail for eFX/cross-border providers**, effective **1 October 2026** (BCB Resolution 561; see [[Brazil's central bank bans crypto settlement in cross-border payments]]). So the headline LatAm proving ground has a hostile regulatory turn in its single largest market *right as this scales*. (analysis) Expect Damisa/PPRO LatAm activity to concentrate in Mexico/Colombia/Argentina and lean on licensed off-ramps, while Brazil forces fallback to conventional eFX — which erodes the "single regulated rail" selling point regionally. Net: a credible-but-incremental treasury optimisation whose marquee LatAm story is partly undercut by regulation; weight is low unless real corridor volumes surface.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Cross-border B2B is the single largest stablecoin lane: ~$137–153bn settled Jan–Aug 2026 (per McKinsey/Artemis, via blindpay compilation); Juniper pegs stablecoin cross-border B2B at $13.4bn in 2026 scaling to $5tn by 2035 (via GlobeNewswire, 2026-04-27). Structure: fragmented at the edge (dozens of onramp/offramp + settlement startups — Conduit, BVNK, Bridge, Damisa), consolidating fast at the top (Mastercard's up-to-$1.8bn BVNK deal; Stripe's Bridge). Value sits in the regulated multi-jurisdiction rail + local-currency payout network — the hard, capital-and-licence-heavy part — not in the stablecoin itself. "Why now": correspondent banking is retreating from exactly the high-growth corridors (LatAm, Africa), while MiCA (EU) and the GENIUS Act (US) gave regulated stablecoin settlement institutional cover. Second-order effect: the prefunding/nostro capital that PSPs lock in each market becomes the margin pool a single rail can attack.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: settled volume (TPV), corridor/currency coverage, prefunding capital released, settlement speed (T+0 vs T+2), take rate on FX+transfer. Key players & basis of competition — not price but regulatory breadth + payout reach + counterparty trust: Conduit (~$10bn annualised volume, 100+ clients, $53m raised, 2025-05), BVNK ($30bn/yr, ~€625m last round, acquired by Mastercard for up to $1.8bn), Bridge (Stripe-owned). Damisa's position: sub-scale new entrant — $70m+ settled H1 2026 across 90+ countries / 70 currencies — but differentiated on the regulatory stack (AFSL/AUSTRAC Australia, FINTRAC Canada MSB, Poland VASP; Malta CASP + PI pending) and local-currency payout across Africa/LatAm/Asia. Moat `(analysis)`: multi-jurisdiction licensing + payout network (switching costs for a PSP once integrated), not technology. The PPRO win is the proof point — PPRO is a tier-1 PSP backbone (Citi, PayPal, Stripe as clients), so a reference customer that scaled from a LatAm pilot to global is meaningful validation for a pre-seed-stage firm.

**Comps & multiples.** Private, early-stage — valuation multiples for Damisa = no data (Series A in progress per Pitchbook/Tracxn as of 2026-07; last priced round £2.25m pre-seed, Fuel Ventures, 2025-04). Peer anchors (round/deal values, NOT market caps): BVNK — ~€625m implied last-round valuation, then Mastercard acquisition up to $1.8bn ($1.5bn base + $300m earnout) on ~$30bn annual stablecoin volume → ~0.06x deal-value/volume `(analysis, crude)`; Conduit — $53m raised, valuation undisclosed, on ~$10bn annualised volume. Partner PPRO (IR-grounded): €1.4bn valuation at the €85m round (2024-03-13, Eurazeo/HPE Growth/PayPal Ventures/J.P. Morgan/Citi Ventures/BlackRock), struck at ~17.7x EV/Revenue on FY2022 revenue of £56m (£56m × 17.7 ≈ £991m ≈ €1.16bn, roughly reconciling to the headline). Distribution not computed — too few comparable, mostly undisclosed figures; qualitative only. Internal comps (Conduit not in base): nearest in-base peers — [[Yuno integrates Coinbase Payments for stablecoin checkout]], [[XTransfer launches cross-border payment service in Chile]], [[Rain launches embedded stablecoin wallets for partners]], [[Noah closes $38M seed after extra $16M raise]].

**Risk flags.**
1. Scale/concentration — $70m settled H1 2026 is tiny vs Conduit's $10bn / BVNK's $30bn; a single marquee client (PPRO) likely drives a large share of volume, so the relationship is both the proof point and the concentration risk if PPRO multi-sources the rail.
2. Disintermediation from above — Mastercard (BVNK) and Stripe (Bridge) now own regulated stablecoin rails and sell directly to PSPs; Damisa's PPRO-type customers are exactly who the networks are courting, risking margin capture by the stack layer above.
3. Regulatory-timing dependency — the moat rests on licences still "in progress" (Malta CASP + PI); EU payout at scale is gated on authorisations not yet granted, and MiCA/GENIUS interpretation remains fluid — a why-flag because the differentiator is unfinished.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` A pre-seed-stage rail landing a tier-1 PSP (PPRO) signals the regulated-stablecoin-settlement layer has crossed from crypto-native to institutional procurement — this is a new-entry / land-grab phase, not yet consolidation at the long tail.
