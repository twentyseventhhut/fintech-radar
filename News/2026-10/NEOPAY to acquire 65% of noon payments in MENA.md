NEOPAY enters into definitive agreement to acquire 65% stake in noon payments
Expanding regional leadership across MENA
 Dubai, UAE –  NEOPAY today announced that it has entered into a definitive agreement to acquire a 65% controlling stake in noon payments (the “Transaction”), marking a significant step in NEOPAY’s ambition to build a leading regional payments platform.  Completion of the Transaction remains subject to customary conditions precedent, including applicable regulatory and antitrust approvals.
Upon completion, the transaction will bring together NEOPAY’s scalable acquiring infrastructure, omnichannel acceptance capabilities and merchant services platform with noon payments’ embedded payments platform, e-commerce gateway and merchant network across the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Egypt. This will expand NEOPAY’s digital commerce capabilities and regional footprint, while strengthening its position in the UAE.
As commerce increasingly moves across physical and digital channels, merchants are looking for payment partners that can support them seamlessly across markets and customer journeys. By bringing together the strengths of the two businesses, NEOPAY will offer merchants a more integrated proposition spanning online payment acceptance, in-store acquiring, faster settlements, data and analytics, and value-added financial services.
 Vibhor Mundhada, CEO of NEOPAY, said: 
 “This is an important milestone in NEOPAY’s journey. We have built a strong payments business in the UAE, and our ambition is now to take that capability across the region. 
 noon payments has built an impressive digital payments platform with a strong presence in some of MENA’s most important e-commerce markets. By combining their strength in digital commerce with NEOPAY’s acquiring and omnichannel capabilities, we have the opportunity to create a truly regional payments platform that can serve merchants seamlessly across channels and markets. 
 This acquisition will reinforce our leadership in the UAE, expand our footprint into Saudi Arabia and Egypt, and strengthen NEOPAY’s position as the payments provider of choice for businesses across MENA.”    
noon payments has established strong integrations with marketplace sellers and online merchants, creating high transaction density in some of the region’s fastest-growing digital commerce segments. Upon completion, the Transaction will give NEOPAY access to additional merchant relationships and payment flows while accelerating the rollout of regional payment innovations, including alternative payment methods and installment options.
 Faraz Khalid, CEO of noon, commented : 
“Payments should be simple, reliable, and built for the markets they serve. NEOPAY brings deep local expertise and strong infrastructure. Together, we can help merchants grow and make paying easier for millions of customers across the region.”
The combined roadmap will focus on making it easier for merchants to do business across the region, including faster merchant onboarding, improved payment performance, advanced fraud capabilities and embedded financial services. The Transaction will also create opportunities to strengthen cross-border payments and settlement capabilities across key regional commerce corridors.
 About NEOPAY 
NEOPAY is one of the UAE's leading digital payments companies, providing end-to-end payment infrastructure for businesses, financial institutions and government entities across the Middle East. Its portfolio includes merchant acquiring, omnichannel payment acceptance, payment orchestration, embedded finance and issuer processing solutions, enabling secure, scalable and intelligent payment experiences.
Backed by a strategic consortium led by Arcapita and Dgpays, with continued support from Mashreq, NEOPAY combines trusted financial services expertise with advanced payment technology to deliver seamless payment experiences, faster settlements, advanced analytics and value-added services. Today, the company serves thousands of merchants across retail, e-commerce, hospitality and the public sector, helping organizations simplify payment operations and accelerate digital growth.
 ABOUT NOON.COM 
noon.com was founded with the objective of fostering an ecosystem of regionally based digital companies in order to secure the region's digital landscape's future. noon's mission is to provide customers and companies in the Middle East region with outstanding value and support. On December 12th, 2017, noon launched its consumer platform in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates. noon debuted in Egypt in February 2019 and has since evolved to become the largest online shopping destination in the Middle East. Primarily a digital e-commerce platform powered by in-house technological talent, noon has swiftly developed strong native capabilities throughout its marketplace, fulfillment, logistics, and payment systems.

# Context-enrichment: NEOPAY to acquire 65% of noon payments in MENA
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 4/5. Freshness: fresh._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- On **2026-10-06** (Zawya PR; Gulf News/EPI date it 5–6 Oct), **NEOPAY** signed a **definitive agreement to buy a 65% controlling stake in noon payments**, the payments unit of Alabbar-backed e-commerce group noon.com. Close is subject to "customary conditions... including regulatory and antitrust approvals." No regulator named (likely CBUAE + Saudi SAMA + Egypt).
- **Terms NOT disclosed:** no price, no valuation of noon payments, no TPV, no merchant count. noon implicitly retains ~35% (not stated explicitly). This is a materially under-disclosed PR — treat all scale claims ("high transaction density", "strong presence") as unquantified marketing.
- **De-PR'd substance:** NEOPAY (UAE acquiring/omnichannel infra) buys control of noon payments (e-commerce gateway + embedded payments + marketplace merchant flows across UAE/KSA/Egypt). The real asset is noon's **captive marketplace payment flow** and its KSA/Egypt footprint — NEOPAY was UAE-centric.
- **Who NEOPAY actually is — critical correction:** NEOPAY is NOT Network International/Brookfield. Per the PR itself it is "backed by a strategic consortium led by **Arcapita and DgPays**, with continued support from **Mashreq**." It was spun out of Mashreq; Arcapita (Bahrain PE) + DgPays (Turkish payments-infra firm) bought the majority for **$385m, completed Jan 2025**. So this is a **DgPays/Arcapita-backed challenger buying scale**, competing directly against Brookfield's Network International–Magnati.
- **Why structured as 65%, not 100%:** noon keeps a 35% stake — retains economics/optionality over its own payment rails and avoids fully ceding the merchant data. For NEOPAY, 65% buys consolidation/control (it books the TPV) while keeping noon aligned as an anchor merchant. Governance/veto rights and any captive-volume commitment are undisclosed — the key silent terms.

## [1] Competitors / peers (MENA acquiring / e-com payments)
- **Network International + Magnati** (Brookfield/FAB): key merger approvals **2025-07-10**, combined **>$400bn TPV, ~240k merchants, 250+ FIs, 50+ markets** — the regional incumbent NEOPAY is scaling against. NEOPAY is buying its way toward relevance.
- **PayTabs** (Saudi): roll-up strategy — [[PayTabs Group acquires checkout startup TAPn'GO in UAE]] (2026-04), and agreed to buy **Amazon Payment Services' MENA ops** (~Sep 2026, "north of $100m"). Direct consolidator peer.
- **Checkout.com:** direct UAE acquiring licence (2023), MENA volume +62% YoY, planning UAE card issuance 2026.
- **Geidea, Tap Payments** (CBUAE Retail Payment Services licence 2025-04), **Telr** (bought by Cashfree 2021), **MoneyHash** (orchestration — [[MoneyHash partners with noon for GCC payments]], 2025-09).
- **Position:** NEOPAY is **catching up**, not leading. Even post-deal it is a fraction of Network International–Magnati's >$400bn TPV. **Why this way:** MENA acquiring is consolidating (PayTabs, NI-Magnati, now NEOPAY) because standalone PSPs lack cross-border licences + scale; the margin accrues to whoever owns both acquiring + captive flow. NEOPAY's edge here is buying a captive flow (noon marketplace) rather than only licences.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **noon payments** trajectory: noon.com launched KSA/UAE Dec 2017, Egypt Feb 2019; payments gateway publicised from ~Jan 2022. First in MENA to launch Mastercard Payment Passkey ([[noon payments and Visa launch first payment passkey for merchants]], 2024/25); partnerships with FAB Misr ([[FAB Misr partners with noon payments on e-commerce]], 2025-10), MoneyHash, valU ([[valU launches digital onboarding on UAE marketplace noon]], 2025-09).
- **Fit:** logical for both. noon.com is monetising/de-risking a non-core infrastructure unit while keeping 35% upside — plausibly cleaning up ahead of its own financing/IPO (hypothesis). NEOPAY needs KSA/Egypt + e-com flow to justify its post-$385m-buyout growth story for Arcapita/DgPays.
- **Why NEOPAY acts this way:** a UAE-only acquirer has a capped multiple; owning a regional e-com gateway with captive marketplace density is the fastest path to a "regional platform" narrative that supports a higher exit valuation for its PE owners.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new event** (new control deal, new entity formed) — not a reprint. But **no disclosed traction numbers**: no TPV, no merchant count, no price. "High transaction density" is unquantified.
- **Real value-add (analysis):** the durable asset is **captive flow** — noon.com's own marketplace GMV routed through the combined entity. That is sticky and hard to replicate. **But the margin question:** if the 35%-retained noon can later re-route its GMV, or if there is no long-term processing lock, the "transaction density" is not actually captured. **Who captures the margin** depends entirely on the undisclosed captive-volume terms. On the rails side, interchange still accrues to Visa/Mastercard — NEOPAY captures acquiring/MDR + value-added services, a commodity-ish take-rate unless it layers embedded finance (cf. [[NEO PAY and Biz2X launch embedded SME financing in UAE]]).

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Close pending multi-jurisdiction approval (UAE/KSA/Egypt) — non-trivial for cross-border acquiring; timeline undisclosed. Combined roadmap: faster onboarding, APMs/installments, fraud tooling, cross-border settlement.
- **Market read:** continuation of MENA payments consolidation (NI-Magnati, PayTabs/Amazon, now NEOPAY/noon). **Second-order (analysis):** this is a sponsor-driven land-grab — Arcapita/DgPays assembling a sellable regional platform. Counterintuitive risk: a payments business whose scale leans on ONE anchor merchant (noon) is more fragile, not safer — concentration risk if noon.com's own GMV softens or it exercises its 35% to pull volume.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** MENA digital-payments spend is estimated at ~$275bn in 2026, projected to ~$462bn by 2031 at a ~10.9% CAGR (per Mordor Intelligence, via researchandmarkets/Yahoo Finance, as of 2026). The deal sits where two secular drivers converge: (1) cashless mandates — Saudi reached ~79% non-cash retail transactions by early 2025, Dubai ~88% (per Mordor, 2026); (2) e-commerce GMV grew ~30% in MENA in 2024 with AOV ~$35.6 (same source). Structurally the acquiring/gateway layer is fragmented and consolidating fast: sovereign-backed incumbents (Network International, Magnati, Geidea) plus a long tail of gateways/orchestrators. Barriers are regulatory (central-bank PSP licences per country — noon payments holds UAE, KSA and a CBE-approved Egypt PSP) and network/merchant density. "Why now": NEOPAY was itself bought by a Dgpays/Arcapita consortium (with Mashreq) in Jan-2025, and this is the growth-capital thesis playing out — bolt on an embedded e-commerce gateway to turn a UAE-centric acquirer into a 3-country regional platform ahead of noon's own IPO push.

**Competitive landscape.** Payments KPIs that matter here: TPV/GPV, take rate, merchant count, transaction density, settlement speed — none disclosed for either party [UNSOURCED]. Key players & basis of competition: Network International LLC (post Magnati merger, Oct-2025, Brookfield-controlled) competes on scale/bank relationships; Geidea and Nearpay on Saudi acceptance; PayTabs and MoneyHash on gateway/orchestration; Checkout.com/Stripe/Adyen on cross-border acceptance. NEOPAY competes on bank-grade acquiring + omnichannel; noon payments on captive e-commerce density (noon marketplace, ~20m MAU; FABMISR/FAB Misr and Visa passkey partnerships in 2025). Recent peer moves: Qashio acquired Sanad Cash (KSA, Oct-2025); PayTabs acquired TAPn'GO (UAE, Apr-2026); Paribu acquired CoinMENA ($240m, Dec-2025); Network/Magnati merger (Oct-2025). Protagonist position: catching up to Network/Magnati on scale but buying a differentiated, hard-to-replicate asset — e-commerce transaction density. Moat (analysis): switching costs + distribution via the captive noon marketplace; vulnerable because the seller keeps 35% and noon remains the retained channel owner.

**Comps & multiples.** No price disclosed for this deal — "no data" on the headline. Benchmarks for a 65% MENA-payments control stake:
- **NEOPAY itself (Jan-2025):** Dgpays/Arcapita bought 65% at ~$385m total enterprise value (per Arabian Post/fintechfutures). Best direct internal/precedent anchor — same target, same stake size.
- **Network International (Brookfield, 2023):** ~$2.8bn EV on ~$438m revenue and ~$178m EBITDA ⇒ EV/Revenue = `$2.8bn / $438m = 6.4x`; EV/EBITDA = `$2.8bn / $0.178bn ≈ 15.7x` (per Bloomberg/Gulf Business). This is a public-to-private take-private of a scaled acquirer — rich end for the region.
- **noon.com parent (Dec-2025):** ~$10bn valuation on a $500m PIF-co-led round (round valuation of the e-commerce parent, NOT the payments unit, NOT a market cap) — context only; the 35% noon retains is a fraction of the much smaller payments subsidiary.
- Internal comps from base: [[Qashio acquires Saudi expense platform Sanad Cash]], [[PayTabs Group acquires checkout startup TAPn'GO in UAE]], [[Paribu acquires crypto exchange CoinMENA for $240M]] — all undisclosed-to-small MENA fintech M&A, consistent with a consolidation wave.
- Distribution not computed (only 2 priced payment comps, different scale/model); qualitative read: a MENA acquiring platform likely transacts well below Network's 6.4x revenue given sub-scale; NEOPAY's own $385m-for-65% precedent is the more apt yardstick. Verdict: **in-line with regional consolidation**, headline multiple unknowable without terms.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Minority/control friction:** seller (noon) retains 35% AND remains the dominant captive channel. If NEOPAY depends on noon's own marketplace for transaction density, the "regional platform" value can be captured or re-priced by the retained parent — dependence on someone else's e-commerce rails.
2. **Regulatory/antitrust completion risk:** explicitly subject to multi-jurisdiction approvals (UAE CBUAE, Saudi, Egypt CBE). "Announced ≠ live" — three central banks must clear a cross-border payments consolidation; timeline undisclosed.
3. **Competitive squeeze from a scaled incumbent:** the Oct-2025 Network International/Magnati merger created a Brookfield-controlled scale player; a sub-scale NEOPAY+noon combination risks margin pressure in acquiring (price-based competition) unless the e-commerce density translates into a genuine take-rate premium.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is consolidation, not re-rating: the MENA acquiring stack is coalescing into two poles — a Brookfield/Network scale camp and a Dgpays/Arcapita/NEOPAY e-commerce-density camp — with gateways/orchestrators (PayTabs, MoneyHash, Geidea) as the next targets. Falsifiable thesis: NEOPAY+noon payments becomes a credible #2 regional acquirer within 18 months. Trigger to watch: regulatory clearance dates and whether noon payments' merchant flows stay committed post-close; thesis breaks if approvals stall or noon diversifies its PSPs away from the captive unit (MoneyHash's 2025 orchestration tie-up with noon is an early signal that noon already multi-homes).
