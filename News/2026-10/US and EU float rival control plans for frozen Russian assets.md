## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] TL;DR.** The fight over ~€260bn of immobilized Russian sovereign assets has escalated from "how to spend the income" to "who controls the principal." A US proposal (floated by Trump envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner at Miami talks on ~Oct 9–10, 2026) would partially unfreeze Russian reserves and move them into a new special-purpose vehicle under Washington's control, as a peace-talks lever. European officials flatly rejected it. In parallel, the European Commission is weighing its own mechanism — a "bad bank"-style SPV that would lift the assets out of Euroclear while keeping them frozen and ringfencing Euroclear's balance sheet. Belgium remains the key blocker on any confiscation-adjacent plan.

**[1] Who / what / when.**
- **~€260bn** total Russian sovereign assets immobilized by Western governments (FT figure in the note). Of this, **~€210bn** of Russian central-bank reserves are frozen in the EU; the central-bank portion held at **Euroclear** (Brussels) is ~€185bn, with ~€202bn of Euroclear's €241bn June-2026 balance sheet tied to sanctioned Russian assets.
- The frozen assets generate income: **~€8bn** as of an Aug 3 estimate (note). As ECB/euro rates fall, that yield shrinks — a cited reason the debate is shifting toward the **principal** rather than just the windfall profits.
- **US side**: only ~$5bn of Russian assets sit in the US (Congressional Research Service est., per note). Washington therefore cannot execute its own plan unilaterally — it needs consent from European jurisdictions, and unfreezing requires **unanimity** across EU member states.
- **Miami meeting** (~Oct 9–10, 2026): Witkoff + Kushner proposed the US-controlled vehicle to officials from Ukraine, France, Germany, UK, EU and NATO. Europeans said no — one source: they "were told that this was just simply not going to happen." German Chancellor Merz: frozen Russian assets must stay in Europe.

**[2] Why it matters (fintech/market-infrastructure angle).**
- **Euroclear** is a systemic EU central securities depository (CSD). Any forced transfer or confiscation risks (a) Russian retaliation/counter-claims against Euroclear's own assets, (b) legal liability cascading to EU states, and (c) reputational damage to the EU as a safe custody/settlement jurisdiction — a concern for sovereign reserve managers globally.
- The "bad bank"/SPV design is explicitly an attempt to protect Euroclear's balance sheet and ordinary settlement/custody operations while redeploying the frozen cash (converted into zero-coupon EU bonds) to fund Ukraine.

**[3] Competing proposals (side by side).**
- **US plan**: partial unfreeze → assets into a Washington-supervised financial vehicle; framed as leverage/collateral in peace negotiations; could imply eventual return of some funds to Russia. Rejected by Europeans.
- **EU/Commission plan**: keep assets frozen but move them out of Euroclear into an EU SPV ("bad bank") that absorbs both assets and matching liabilities; proceeds lent to Ukraine at ~0% (reparations-loan logic, repayable only if Russia pays reparations under a future peace deal — Commission insists "no confiscation").
- **Status quo/fallback**: at end-2025 EU leaders could not agree a reparations loan; Ukraine instead got a **~€90bn** loan for 2026–2027.

**[4] Open risks / unknowns.**
- **Belgium** (PM Bart De Wever) calls outright confiscation "an act of war," demands all EU states share legal liability and a binding answer on who pays if Russia enforces a claim — the main veto point.
- **Litigation**: Russia's central bank is suing the EU over the indefinite immobilization and separately seeks **~$230bn** in damages from Euroclear in a Moscow court.
- Whether the US and EU tracks can be reconciled (US wants control/leverage; EU wants assets to stay in Europe and stay frozen) is unresolved; EU unanimity makes the US plan hard to execute.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a market-plumbing / sovereign-reserves-sanctions theme, not a company deal. "Size": ~€260bn of Russian sovereign assets frozen across the West (FT, via note); ~€210bn of CBR reserves immobilised in the EU, almost all held at Euroclear, the Brussels ICSD (per Kyiv Post / Modern Diplomacy, as of Oct 2026); only ~$5bn sits in the US (Congressional Research Service, via note). Structure: highly concentrated — a single CSD (Euroclear) is the choke-point, with Clearstream (Luxembourg) a distant second, so "control" of the assets is really control of two European settlement venues, which is precisely why Washington cannot execute its plan unilaterally (it holds only ~$5bn). Value in this chain is the *windfall cash flow*: the immobilised CBR cash matures into Euroclear balances that earn the deposit rate. Drivers / "why now": (1) rate cycle — windfall income is falling, €1.1bn in Q1 2026 (-23% YoY) and €2.3bn in H1 2026 (-13% YoY) per Euroclear/Interfax, so ~€8bn cumulative by early Aug 2026 (EEAS) is decelerating → the debate shifts from *income* to *principal*; (2) Ukraine's ~€23bn financing gap beyond the €90bn fallback loan (Kyiv Post) → principal is "the fastest money on the table"; (3) a US peace-track overlay (Witkoff/Kushner, Miami talks 9 Oct, per Meduza/Pravda) that reframes the assets as a negotiating chip rather than reparations collateral.

**Competitive landscape.** The relevant "KPIs" here are custody/settlement economics: assets under custody/retained, windfall net profit, and windfall provision/contribution. Euroclear provisioned €744m windfall contribution in Q1 2026 and €1.5bn in H1 2026 (Euroclear). Two rival "control" designs are on the table (per note / FT): US — partially unfreeze and move reserves into a Washington-controlled vehicle (effectively a partial return/relaxation); EU/Commission — lift the assets *out of Euroclear* into a common European mechanism while keeping them frozen (the "reparations loan": Euroclear lends ~€210bn to the Commission, relent to Kyiv as limited-recourse debt repayable only if Russia pays reparations). Recent moves: 122 MEPs signed a letter (early Oct 2026) backing the reparations loan; Commission sought an extra €25bn of assets (Politico); Belgium reiterated opposition and the Oct 15-16 European Council produced no new decision, positions unchanged (Interfax-Ukraine / ua.news). Camp split: SE/NL/ES/PL + Baltics for; BE/IT/FR against. Protagonist = Euroclear, which is a *reluctant* principal — it does not want balance-sheet or litigation exposure, so its "position" is to resist being made the risk-bearer `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/multiple attaches to this news — it is a policy/plumbing story, so trading comps = no data. Internal corpus comps on the Euroclear / CSD-plumbing angle: [[Euroclear in exclusive talks to acquire Uptevia from BNP Paribas, CACEIS]], [[Euroclear and SG-FORGE to explore USD stablecoin settlement]], [[DTCC selects Canton Network for Treasury tokenization]], [[ClearToken and Consensys bring 24 7 settlement to wholesale markets]]. These frame the backdrop: the same CSD/settlement rails being politicised here are simultaneously racing to tokenise — a reserve-currency trust shock would hit both legacy and tokenised European settlement ambitions. Context comp on counter-sanctions plumbing: [[Russia tightens counter-sanction limits on retail non-resident clients]], [[Russian central bank revokes ALOR+ depository licence]] (shows Moscow's symmetric blocking of non-resident assets in Russian CSDs — the retaliation surface).

**Risk flags.**
- *Legal / reversibility (Euroclear-on-Belgium).* If sanctions lapse or a court orders return, Euroclear could be compelled to repay ~€210bn it no longer holds; Belgium, as home state, is the implicit backstop and is demanding full risk-mutualisation / uncapped guarantees before consenting. Second-order: no EU-wide guarantee → no deal, which is why Oct 15-16 moved nothing.
- *Euro reserve-currency trust (ECB objection).* The ECB warns that touching sovereign reserves could erode confidence in the euro; third-country reserve holders (China, Saudi, Brazil, Indonesia) may diversify away from euro-denominated custody. Second-order: a slow outflow from Euroclear/Clearstream undermines the very European settlement franchise being tokenised in the comps above.
- *Retaliation / rival-stack fragmentation.* Moscow holds symmetric Western assets in Russian CSDs (NSD) and the US plan would fork control across a Washington vehicle vs an EU mechanism. Second-order: competing "control" structures risk double-claims and litigation over the same €210bn, freezing disbursement regardless of who "wins."

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is less about Ukraine financing than about who controls the plumbing of frozen sovereign reserves — and the answer has repricing power over Europe's standing as a neutral custody/settlement hub just as Euroclear/Clearstream pivot to tokenised rails. Falsifiable thesis: the principal does NOT move in 2026 — the gating constraint is Belgian/ECB risk-sharing, not political will, and absent uncapped mutual guarantees the EU will keep tapping only the (shrinking) windfall income. Trigger that would break it: a European Council agreement to a full risk-mutualisation guarantee, or a US-brokered peace deal that converts the assets into a settlement chip. Watch: the next European Council, any ECB formal opinion, and Euroclear's Q3 2026 windfall-provision number (income trajectory is the clock on this debate).
