Zip US Announces Expansion of Banking Partnerships to Support Long-Term Growth
Monday, 19 October 2026
 NEW YORK — October 19, 2026 — Zip Co (ASX: ZIP), the digital financial services company offering innovative, people-centered products for everyday Americans, today announced a second banking partnership with Column N.A., a nationally chartered bank.
Under the new partnership, Column N.A. will serve as a bank partner for Zip’s unsecured U.S. installment lending products. Column N.A. operates under a U.S. national bank charter and is federally regulated.
The expansion of Zip’s bank partnership model through the addition of a nationally chartered partner further diversifies the Company’s banking relationships, supports funding cost efficiencies and strengthens operational resilience, reflecting the significant scale Zip has achieved.
“As Zip continues to grow strongly, maintaining a strong and diversified funding platform is key to delivering for our customers.” said Rory Herriman, President and Interim CEO at Zip US. “Through our partnership with Column, we’re strengthening the foundations of the business to support our next phase of growth and continue expanding access to transparent and responsible credit for millions of Americans.”
“Column supports the largest and most sophisticated financial services companies with a range of consumer lending products, and Zip’s reach makes it a strong fit for our platform,” said Brian Fishbein, Chief Investment Officer at Column N.A.. “We’re looking forward to supporting Zip’s significant scale with our technology, especially as it expands into new products and serves new customers.”
 About Zip 
Zip Co Limited (ACN 139 546 428) (ASX: ZIP) is a digital financial services company, offering innovative and people-centred products. Operating in Australia and the United States, Zip offers access to point-of-sale credit and digital payment services, connecting millions of customers with its global network of tens of thousands of merchants.
Founded in Australia in 2013, Zip provides fair, flexible and transparent payment options, helping customers to take control of their financial future and helping merchants to grow their businesses.
For more information, visit: www.zip.co
 About Column N.A. 
Column N.A., Member FDIC, is a nationally chartered bank that provides regulated financial infrastructure for technology companies. Founded in 2019 by Plaid co-founder William Hockey, Column serves as the banking backbone for modern financial products and powers the world's most sophisticated fintechs, such as Brex, Bilt, Wise, Mercury, and more. With a custom-built banking core, ledger, data model, and a direct connection to the Federal Reserve, Column eliminates the inefficiencies of legacy banks and middleware — creating a faster and more reliable banking platform. Beyond payments and accounts, Column enables national lending and credit products, powering API-based loan-origination partnerships and deploying its balance sheet to fund lenders through debt financing and loan purchases.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What exactly happened (de-PR'd).** On 19 Oct 2026 Zip Co (ASX: ZIP) — the Australian BNPL group operating the former Quadpay US business, NOT the US procurement/spend-management unicorn "Zip" — announced a **second US bank-partner relationship, with Column N.A.**, to originate its **unsecured US installment-lending** products ("Pay-in-Z"). Column is nationally chartered and federally regulated; Zip says the addition "diversifies banking relationships, supports funding-cost efficiencies and strengthens operational resilience." De-PR'd: this is a **structural/plumbing deal, not a product launch and not a disclosed-economics deal** — no volume split, no pricing, no go-live date, no financial terms. The material fact is the move from a **single sponsor bank to two**. Zip's incumbent/first US sponsor is **WebBank**, which originates its loans today (explicitly named as originator in the Zip Cash release, [[Zip plans to launch Zip Cash same-day loans in the US]], and on Zip's US loan terms of service).

**Why a SECOND partner (the real story).** In the US "bank-partner" (a.k.a. rent-a-charter / BaaS-lending) model, a non-bank fintech cannot lend directly; a chartered bank **originates** the loan and exports its home-state interest rate nationwide, sidestepping 50-state usury caps and lending licenses. Running this through a **single** sponsor is a concentrated dependency: if that one bank hits a regulatory consent order, funding constraint, risk-appetite change, or failure, the fintech's entire US origination can halt. Adding Column gives Zip (a) **redundancy / business-continuity** (dual-originate, fail-over), (b) **funding-cost leverage** (play two balance sheets against each other; Column also deploys its own balance sheet to fund lenders and buy loans), and (c) **headroom at scale** — Zip's US book is now large (FY26 US TTV ~US$8.6bn +42.5%, ~4.65m active US customers, per its FY26 results) and a single sponsor's concentration limits / capital can cap growth. The timing is not coincidental: it lands days after the **Zip Cash** same-day-loan launch ([[Zip plans to launch Zip Cash same-day loans in the US]]) and amid a US "AI-native" product push ([[Zip US and Google Cloud build AI-native product factory]]) — Zip is scaling US credit and needs origination capacity to match. Context: single-sponsor risk is a live lesson — the 2024 Synapse collapse and the Evolve Bank & Trust fallout pushed fintechs toward multi-bank redundancy; **Mercury itself migrated off Evolve onto Column + Choice in 2025** (web: Banking Dive / American Banker). (NB per MEMORY: "mercury" in the IR DB = Mercury *Fund* VC, not the neobank — not relevant here.)

**Column N.A. background (fact-checked).** Column is a **nationally chartered, FDIC-member infrastructure bank for fintechs**, founded by **Plaid co-founder William Hockey (with Annie Hockey)**, who self-funded ~US$50m to **acquire Northern California National Bank (Chico, CA) and rebrand it Column** — it is **founder/employee-owned with no outside VC**. One correction to the note's About text: credible sources (TechCrunch, CrowdfundInsider, Sacra) date the **bank acquisition/launch to ~2021–2022**, not "2019" as Zip's PR states — treat the "Founded in 2019" line as PR imprecision. Column owns the charter outright (own core, ledger, direct Fed connection) rather than acting as middleware; it powers Brex, Bilt, Wise, Mercury, etc., and Forbes (Feb 2026) reported Column >2x'd revenue to >US$200m in 2025. For Zip, Column's edge is **national lending / loan-origination APIs + balance-sheet funding (debt financing, loan purchases)** — i.e. it can both originate and help fund, which is exactly what a scaling lender wants from a second rail.

**Regulatory backdrop (BNPL / bank-partner).** The deal sits in the **deregulated-federal / active-state** window documented in the Zip Cash note: the CFPB in 2025 withdrew its BNPL interpretive rule and deprioritized small-dollar enforcement, but **state usury/UDAP/licensing enforcement is expected to rise in 2026**, and the FDIC has proposed tighter bank-fintech partnership standards. The whole point of a bank-partner is rate-export to neutralize state usury — so a second *nationally chartered* sponsor (vs a state bank) is a defensible, redundant rail. The flip side: more "rent-a-charter" exposure invites the "true lender" / rent-a-charter critique that NCLC and some state AGs press.

**Zip US growth fit.** This is the funding-infrastructure leg of Zip's US scale-up story: US is ~76% of group TTV; FY26 group cash EBTDA ~A$269m (+58%), US cash earnings up sharply, net bad debts ~1.7% of TTV. A mooted **Nasdaq listing** ([[Zip targets Nasdaq listing to fuel US BNPL growth]], [[Zip shares jump 12% on strong update and buyback]]) makes a resilient, diversified US funding/origination stack strategically load-bearing for the equity story.

**Red-team / challenge questions**

1. Is this a product or just plumbing? **Answered — plumbing.** It is a second loan-origination sponsor bank, not a new consumer product, new market, or capital raise. Easy to over-read.
2. Who was the FIRST US bank partner? **Answered — WebBank**, Zip's incumbent originator (named in Zip's US loan terms and in the Zip Cash release). The news is "+1 to 2," not "Zip gets its first bank."
3. Any economics disclosed? **Open/No.** No volume split, pricing, fee split, funding cost, origination-share, or go-live date. "Funding-cost efficiencies" is asserted, unquantified.
4. Does Column *fund* or just *originate*? **Partly answered.** Column can both originate AND deploy its balance sheet (debt financing / loan purchases). How much of Zip's book Column actually funds vs WebBank is undisclosed — the real economic lever, and it's hidden.
5. "Founded in 2019"? **Challenged — likely wrong.** TechCrunch/CrowdfundInsider/Sacra date Column's bank acquisition/launch to ~2021–22 (Hockey bought Northern California National Bank, Chico). PR date is imprecise; do not repeat uncritically.
6. Is the "operational resilience" framing real or boilerplate? **Partly.** It's genuine (single-sponsor concentration is a real risk — see Synapse/Evolve 2024–25, Mercury's Evolve→Column+Choice migration), but Zip gives no concrete continuity/fail-over detail. Directionally true, specifics absent.
7. Does adding a second sponsor increase or decrease regulatory risk? **Open/two-sided.** Decreases concentration/continuity risk; but more bank-partner rate-export deepens "true lender" / rent-a-charter exposure as state AGs and FDIC (proposed partnership standards) lean in during 2026.
8. Why a *nationally* chartered bank specifically? **Answered (analysis).** A national charter exports rate under federal preemption nationwide — cleaner for 50-state unsecured lending than a state-bank sponsor; also signals Zip wants a sophisticated, scale-capable partner (Column serves Brex/Bilt/Wise/Mercury).
9. Is Column financially solid enough to be a critical rail? **Mostly answered.** Founder/employee-owned, no outside VC, Forbes (Feb 2026) reports >US$200m revenue (2x YoY). No public solvency flags; still a young, concentrated-in-fintech bank — key-supplier risk remains.
10. Was this a share-price mover? **Open.** Likely immaterial to the stock (plumbing); unlike the Zip Cash / FY26 catalysts. No evidence of a re-rating.
11. Does this connect to Zip Cash? **Answered (analysis).** Almost certainly — Zip just launched a same-day cash-loan product (WebBank-originated) days earlier; adding origination capacity/redundancy right before scaling US credit is coherent sequencing.
12. Disambiguation risk? **Answered.** "Zip" = Zip Co / Quadpay BNPL (ASX: ZIP), NOT Zip the procurement unicorn. Note and tags are correct.
13. Could this signal WebBank is pulling back or near a cap? **Open/speculative.** Zip frames it as additive, not a replacement; but "diversify + resilience" can also hint at single-sponsor concentration limits being reached. Unconfirmed.
14. Does it change Zip's loss/credit profile? **No (analysis).** Switching/adding an originating bank doesn't change underwriting or the risk of the loans themselves; US bad debts (~1.7% TTV) are a function of Zip's credit model, not the sponsor.
15. Materiality for the Nasdaq-listing narrative? **Partly.** A diversified, resilient US funding/origination stack is a credibility point for US investors, but this single deal is a supporting detail, not a catalyst.

**Importance: 2/5** — A fresh, real, correctly-reported structural move: a top-6 US BNPL player goes from one sponsor bank (WebBank) to two by adding a credible nationally chartered infrastructure bank (Column), de-risking single-sponsor concentration (a live concern post-Synapse/Evolve) and adding funding/origination headroom as its US book scales ahead of a mooted Nasdaq listing. Caps importance: it is infrastructure plumbing with **zero disclosed economics** (no volume split, pricing, or funding share), not a product, market-entry, or capital event; incremental to Zip's already-known US growth and Zip Cash story rather than a surprise. Newsworthy for fintech-infra watchers, minor for the broad market.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US BNPL/installment lending is a scaled but structurally bank-dependent subvertical: a non-bank originates and underwrites, but a chartered partner bank is the legal lender-of-record, then the fintech buys back the receivables (originate-to-distribute). Zip itself is now at meaningful US scale — FY26 (reported Aug 2026) US TTV +42.5% to US$8.6bn, US revenue +44% to US$613m, group cash EBTDA +57.9% to A$268.9m, margin 20.0% (per Zip FY26 results, via Investing.com / Kalkine, as of Aug 2026). The sponsor-bank layer is consolidating and under scrutiny post-Synapse (2024 collapse locked ~100k users out of ~$265m deposits; Fed C&D on Evolve Jun-2024); top-5 sponsor banks back ~29% of disclosed relationships and the top-4 are projected to absorb ≥75% of new US BaaS launches by end-2027 (per rupturelabs/CCG Catalyst). "Why now": at Zip's scale, single-partner dependence is a funding-cost and resilience liability — diversifying bank rails is the natural de-risking step, and comes days after Zip announced Zip Cash same-day cash loans (13-Oct-2026, 100k+ waitlist) that will need balance-sheet funding.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TTV/GMV, net transaction margin, cost of funds, and charge-off rate (consumer credit). Column N.A. is not a classic BaaS middleware play — it owns a de-novo national charter (founded 2019 by Plaid co-founder William Hockey), with a direct Fed connection and no middleware; Sacra estimates ~$153.1m 2025 revenue (+219% YoY) and $1.8bn assets as of Jun-2026, serving Brex, Bilt, Wise, Mercury, Affirm. Basis of competition on the fintech side: cost of funds, speed of integration (API-direct vs middleware), and regulatory robustness. Recent peer moves: Affirm applied for a Nevada ILC bank charter (Jan-2026) to internalize funding away from Cross River/Celtic; Klarna powers Walmart/OnePay installments via WebBank. Zip's position: catching up on the bank-stack sophistication curve — it is adding a second rail (Column) alongside its existing partner WebBank (not named in the release; per WebBank brand-partner disclosures) rather than pursuing its own charter like Affirm `(analysis)`. Moat is modest: distribution/merchant network, not funding infrastructure.

**Comps & multiples.** Internal comps: [[Affirm applies for Nevada banking charter]] (opposite strategy — vertical integration into a charter vs Zip's multi-partner diversification); [[Zip plans to launch Zip Cash same-day loans in the US]] (the product that raises funding needs); [[Zip shares jump 12% on strong update and buyback]] (FY26 Q1 TPV +38.7%, revenue +32.8%). External multiple arithmetic: no data — the release carries no deal value, no economics, and Column is private (last-round valuation not disclosed in sources). Zip (ASX:ZIP) market cap not sourced here → EV/Revenue not computed. Distribution not computed; qualitative comparison only. Column's ~$153m revenue at +219% is a growth outlier but with no public valuation the multiple is `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Concentration shifting, not eliminated** — adding one partner (Column) reduces single-bank dependence vs WebBank, but Column is a small bank ($1.8bn assets) absorbing a large originator; sponsor-bank capacity and its own concentration (Brex, Affirm, Wise, Mercury all on Column) is the second-order risk — a Column-level regulatory or liquidity event would now hit Zip's core US lending rail.
2. **Regulatory/liability ambiguity (post-Synapse)** — regulators now penalize banks directly for third-party fintech risk; the release is silent on who holds fraud/credit liability and how receivables are funded, exactly the economics that broke in prior BaaS failures. "Announced" ≠ live volume.
3. **Funding-cost thesis unproven** — Zip claims "funding cost efficiencies," but discloses no rate, no capacity commitment, no volume split between WebBank and Column; the benefit is asserted, not quantified.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a maturation/de-risking move, not a re-rating: large US BNPL originators are being forced to engineer redundancy into the bank-partner layer (multi-rail like Zip, or own-charter like Affirm). Falsifiable thesis: if Zip reports Column-originated volume and a measurable cost-of-funds improvement by FY27 results, the diversification is real; trigger that breaks it — if Column remains a named-but-dormant partner with no volume disclosed, it is resilience optics ahead of Zip Cash scaling.
