# Context-enrichment: Nasdaq Ventures invests in One Trading
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Nasdaq Ventures (the strategic/VC arm of Nasdaq Inc.) made an **early-stage strategic equity investment** in **One Trading** — a Netherlands-regulated (Dutch AFM/DNB) MiFID II OTF venue for crypto perpetual futures, formerly Bitpanda Pro. Announced **8 Oct 2026** (Finextra; The TRADE; fintech.global; Dealroom).
- **What is NOT disclosed (the key tell):** deal size, stake %, valuation — all undisclosed. There is no named syndicate and no priced round; this is a solo strategic cheque. Contrast with Nasdaq's other 2025–26 crypto bets where numbers WERE given ([[Gemini taps Nasdaq as $50M strategic investor ahead of IPO]] = explicit $50M; Nasdaq–Payward/Kraken Sep 2026 = $100M at a $21B valuation). Undisclosed + "early-stage" signals a **small, optionality cheque**, not a headline position.
- **What was actually agreed:** an investment plus an intent to *"explore"* next-gen derivatives infrastructure — "24/7 equity futures trading and integrated trading, risk management and settlement." → Why framed this way: the substance is a **term sheet + MoU to explore**, not a launched product. "Explore" and "will" are the load-bearing verbs. De-PR'd: Nasdaq bought a cheap call option on a MiFID-licensed perps engine; nothing is live between the two.
- → Why it matters (2nd order): the value here is **regulatory/structural, not financial**. One Trading holds a scarce asset — a MiFID II OTF licence that lets it offer crypto (and now equity) perpetual futures to EU retail. Nasdaq is buying proximity to the only EU-regulated 24/7 perps rail, consistent with its "build crypto into Wall Street's plumbing" thesis.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **EU-regulated perps / derivatives:** Kraken (CySEC MiFID II + UK MTF, EEA retail perps — ahead on scale/brand), OKX (MiCA via Malta + MiFID II, ~10x retail perps — ahead), Robinhood Europe (EEA retail perps — ahead on distribution). Bybit (Austrian FMA MiCA, May 2025) is **spot/margin in EU — MiCA does not cover derivatives**, so not a like-for-like EU-perps rival. Deribit dominates BTC/ETH options (EU perps status unclear). D2X (Amsterdam), Eurex, CME = traditional listed euro/BTC futures; incumbents dwarf newcomers (CME ~3.95M BTC-futures lots in 2024 vs Eurex ~30.8k — FOW).
- → Position: **regulatory frontrunner, commercial minnow.** One Trading was plausibly first with an EU-regulated crypto-perps venue for retail (live Apr 2025), but on liquidity/volume/brand it trails Kraken/OKX/Bybit/Deribit badly.
- → 2nd order: the EU "regulated perps" niche exists because MiFID II (not MiCA) governs derivatives — a licensing moat more than a product moat. Whoever wins is decided by **distribution + liquidity**, which is exactly what One Trading lacks and Nasdaq (and SC Ventures earlier) can supply.

## [2] Company history / fit
- 2019 launched as **Bitpanda Pro**; 2021 ex-JPMorgan Josh Barraclough joins (now CEO). **Jun 2023** spun out of Bitpanda, rebranded One Trading, raised **~€30M (~$33M)** led by Peter Thiel's Valar Ventures (MiddleGame, Speedinvest, Keyrock, Wintermute) — reported as **Series A** (prompt's "Series B" is a mislabel). **Jul 2024** MiFID II OTF licence from Dutch AFM/DNB (prompt's "Oct 2024" is off ~3 months). **Sep 2024** SC Ventures (Standard Chartered) led a further strategic investment (terms undisclosed). **Apr 2025** went live with first regulated EU BTC/ETH-EUR perps (settle ~60s, 24/7); retail ~May 2025. **Jul 2026** added leveraged **equity** perpetual futures (24/7 US-equity exposure). **8 Oct 2026** Nasdaq Ventures invests.
- → Why Nasdaq now: One Trading's Jul 2026 pivot into *equity* perps is the hook — it directly overlaps Nasdaq's 24/7-equities/tokenized-equities agenda. The fit is strategic, not financial.
- → Pattern: One Trading survives by **stacking blue-chip strategic backers** (Valar → SC Ventures → Nasdaq) rather than scaling volume. Each new name is a credibility prop for a venue that still has to prove liquidity.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novel?** The *deal* is incremental — it is Nasdaq's Nth crypto-rails bet (see [1]/[4]), just the first in the EU/MiFID perps context. The *asset* (EU-regulated 24/7 perps licence) is genuinely scarce.
- **Traction — thin.** No official One Trading volume/user/revenue figures exist, in this release or elsewhere. The only independent data (CoinGecko) shows a **spot** One Trading entity doing ~$98K/24h, 3 pairs, "Low" liquidity — likely NOT the Dutch OTF perps venue, so a mismatch; but crucially **no credible perps volume/open-interest figure could be found at all.** → "Europe's first regulated perps venue" is a **regulatory milestone, not market share.**
- → Who captures the margin: in derivatives the venue with deepest liquidity takes the fees. One Trading has the licence but not the book; Nasdaq brings distribution/clients. If it works, Nasdaq effectively owns the relationship and the flow — One Trading risks being the **licensed-rail supplier**, not the franchise.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Stated next steps are exploratory: upgrade derivatives infra, broaden instruments (longer-maturity futures), test whether 24/7 equity-futures trading can work under MiFID II oversight. **No listing/IPO angle stated** (do not assume).
- → Why the market goes this way: this is one data point in a clear 2025–26 Nasdaq campaign to wire crypto/24-7 rails into TradFi — [[Nasdaq files with SEC to trade tokenized securities]], [[Nasdaq partners with Kraken for tokenized stock trading]], [[Nasdaq partners Talos to build crypto into market infrastructure]], the $100M Payward/Kraken stake (Sep 2026), plus the Gemini $50M placement. One Trading is the **EU leg** of that thesis.
- Risks: EU regulators tightening retail-perps leverage/appropriateness; One Trading's liquidity may never reach escape velocity; an "explore" MoU can quietly lapse (anti-PR gate: nothing is live).

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** One Trading sits in European regulated crypto/derivatives trading infrastructure — specifically the thin slice of *onshore, MiFID II-regulated* perpetual futures. It holds a Dutch AFM Organised Trading Facility (OTF) licence (granted Jul 2024) plus a MiCA CASP licence, making it the first — and essentially only — EU venue offering cash-settled crypto perpetual futures to both institutional and retail clients (per SC Ventures PR, GlobeNewswire 2024-09-17). "Why now" is a regulatory-arbitrage window: MiCA's first year closed with ~102 approved CASPs (per EU, via `[[EU's MiCA marks first year with 102 approved CASPs]]`), but perps are a MiFID II (not MiCA) product, so an OTF licence is the scarce asset — a capital/regulatory entry barrier more than a tech one. The second driver is the "24/7 markets" theme now crossing from crypto into traditional equities: CME is launching 24/7 crypto futures in early 2026 and Nasdaq filed to extend US equity hours to 23h/day (per Reuters/Yahoo Finance, 2025-2026). Structure: the regulated-derivatives layer is consolidated around incumbents (Eurex/Deutsche Börse, CME, ICE); crypto perps are fragmented across offshore venues (Binance, Bitget, OKX). One Trading is trying to bridge the two — regulated venue + crypto-native 24/7 rails. No reliable public TAM for "EU regulated crypto perps" — **no data**; the segment is too new to size credibly.

**Competitive landscape.** Venue KPIs: traded volume / open interest, number of listed contracts, number of regulated jurisdictions, and (for an OTF) membership/liquidity-provider count — One Trading discloses none of these publicly → `[UNSOURCED]`. Players split three ways: (1) regulated TradFi derivatives incumbents — Eurex/Deutsche Börse, whose CEO has publicly *warned against* 24/7 trading as liquidity-fragmenting (Reuters, 2026), signalling incumbents will not rush this lane; (2) EEA-authorised retail perp venues — Kraken Pro, OKX (X-Perps), Robinhood Europe, capped ~3x-10x with appropriateness tests (per Finance Magnates/CoinPerps, 2026); (3) offshore giants (Binance/Bitget) with 100x+ leverage but no EU regulatory standing. Recent moves: Kraken rolled out 24/7 perpetuals on tokenized US stocks (`[[Kraken rolls out 24 7 perpetuals for tokenized US stocks]]`, Feb 2026); Nasdaq itself partnered Kraken on tokenized stocks and Talos on crypto market infrastructure (`[[Nasdaq partners with Kraken for tokenized stock trading]]`, `[[Nasdaq partners Talos to build crypto into market infrastructure]]`, Mar 2026) — i.e. Nasdaq is placing *multiple* bets in this space, One Trading is one of several. Protagonist position: **niche leader** on the narrow regulatory moat (only EU MiFID II perps venue), but **a challenger** on liquidity/scale vs both offshore depth and TradFi incumbents. Moat = regulatory licence (intangible/barrier-to-entry), weak on network effects until volume compounds `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal is a strategic investment, amount and valuation **undisclosed** → no entry multiple computable, "no data". Internal reference points for scale and prior pricing:
- `[[Bitpanda 2025 revenue rises 16% to €371 million]]` — One Trading's former parent (spun out of Bitpanda Pro). Bitpanda adjusted revenue €371m (2025, +16% YoY), EBITDA fell ~75%; reportedly eyeing a Frankfurt IPO at €4-5bn (`[[Bitpanda gears up for Frankfurt IPO in H1 2026]]`). This is the closest-geography/origin comp but a much larger, retail-brokerage model — **not** a clean comp for a perps venue.
- `[[Architect raises $35M Series A for AX perps exchange]]` (Jan 2026) — $35m Series A for an *institutional perps exchange* (crypto + TradFi). Closest business-model comp; implies the institutional-perps venue category is at Series-A / early-growth pricing, not public-market scale.
- One Trading's own history: €30m Series A in 2023 (Valar Ventures / Thiel-led, per CoinDesk 2023-06-28, rebrand from Bitpanda Pro), then a 2024 round led by SC Ventures / Standard Chartered (undisclosed, 2024-09-17). The Nasdaq cheque is a further strategic top-up, not a priced growth round as framed.

No ≥3 comparable public multiples → **distribution not computed; qualitative comparison only.** EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA → `[UNSOURCED]` (private, no financials disclosed).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Liquidity / scale is the whole game and it's unproven.** A regulated perps venue with no disclosed volume or open interest is a licence without a market; offshore venues hold the liquidity, and liquidity begets liquidity (network effect works *against* a new entrant). Second-order: if volume doesn't compound, the regulatory moat is a cost centre, not a franchise.
2. **"Explore" vs "live".** De-PR: Nasdaq and One Trading say they will *explore* next-gen derivatives infra and 24/7 equity futures — this is an option, not a committed product or integration. The headline implies more than the agreement delivers; 24/7 *equity* futures in the EU faces active incumbent resistance (Deutsche Börse CEO on record against it).
3. **Regulatory concentration / single-licence dependence.** The entire thesis rests on the Dutch OTF + MiCA regime remaining favourable to retail-accessible crypto perps. A rule change (ESMA leverage caps, retail-access restrictions) would hit the differentiator directly. Also single-regulator (AFM) dependence.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is Nasdaq buying a cheap option on EU 24/7/derivatives infrastructure rather than a conviction consolidation — consistent with its parallel Kraken/Talos/Boerse Stuttgart bets. Falsifiable thesis: if One Trading is a real re-rating candidate, we should see *disclosed* volume/open-interest growth and new regulated-jurisdiction expansion within ~12 months; absent that, it stays a niche licence play. Trigger to watch: a named Nasdaq product integration (vs. "exploring"), a priced growth round with a stated valuation, or a Bitpanda IPO that re-prices the whole Austrian crypto-exchange cohort. What breaks the thesis: ESMA/AFM tightening retail perp access.
