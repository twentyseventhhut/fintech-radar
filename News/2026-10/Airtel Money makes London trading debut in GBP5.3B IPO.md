## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Sub-Saharan Africa mobile money moved **~$1.43tn** in transaction value in 2025, +27% y/y, ~66% of the $2.09tn global total, on ~1.2bn registered and 347m active-30-day accounts (per GSMA State of the Industry 2026, via connectingafrica.com). East Africa (~$806bn) and West Africa (~$498bn) dominate — Airtel Money's core footprint. Structure: **oligopolistic by market, fragmented across the continent** — each country is effectively a duopoly/oligopoly between a telco-led wallet and the incumbent bank rails; barriers are distribution (agent networks + SIM base), regulation (per-country e-money/PSP licences, central-bank float rules) and network effects (two-sided payer/merchant liquidity). Why now: the listing is the first pure-play African mobile-money float on a major Western exchange — a test of whether international institutional capital will underwrite the asset class (the IPO priced at $7bn, **below the $8–9bn initially sought**, per Forbes/intellinews, as of 2026-10-01), at a time when LSE has been starved of large listings (biggest London IPO in five years).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: total transaction value (TPV), active/MAU wallets, revenue take-rate on TPV, and ARPU. Airtel Money reported **56.5m customers, ~$245bn annualised transactions and ~$404m quarterly revenue as of June 2026** (web); FY2026 (to Mar) revenue **~$1.3bn (+28% cc), EBITDA ~$676m (~50% margin), op. FCF ~$638m** (web; corp. H1-26 release). Implied take-rate ≈ $1.3bn / ~$245bn TPV ≈ **0.53%** `(analysis)`. Key players and basis of competition (distribution + price): **[[M-PESA reaches 40 million active customers in Kenya|M-Pesa]]** (Safaricom/Vodacom) — East-Africa leader, ~40m Kenya users, FY26 M-Pesa revenue ~$1.41bn, ~45.6% of Safaricom Kenya service revenue; **MTN MoMo** — largest by country count (~17 markets); **Orange Money** — Francophone strength; **[[OPay hires banks for US IPO at about 4 billion valuation|OPay]]** (Nigeria, 40m+ users, targeting ~$4bn US IPO). Airtel's position: **clear #2–#3 by scale, catching up on share** — per the prior Airtel note, Airtel Money lifted Kenya share from 7.6%→8.9% while M-Pesa slipped, evidence of real contestability. Moat = agent-network distribution + SIM base (switching costs modest; interoperability mandates erode lock-in) `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** At the $7.0bn IPO mark (unit has minimal standalone net debt, so EV≈equity):
- **Airtel Money:** EV/Revenue = `$7.0bn / $1.3bn ≈ 5.4x`; EV/EBITDA = `$7.0bn / $0.676bn ≈ 10.4x` (web attributes 5.2x / 10.4x on their revenue figure).
- **Safaricom (M-Pesa parent, listed NSE):** LTM EV/Revenue ~**3.4x** on ~$3.3bn group revenue / ~$1.6bn EBITDA, market cap ~$10.8bn (web, multiples.vc / stockanalysis, as of 2026-09). Note: Safaricom is a blended telco+M-Pesa, so a drag vs a pure wallet.
- **[[OPay hires banks for US IPO at about 4 billion valuation|OPay]]:** ~$4bn targeted round valuation (not market cap), 40m+ users → **price-per-user ≈ $100**; Airtel Money ≈ `$7.0bn / 56.5m ≈ $124/user` `(analysis)`.

Flag: Airtel Money prices **rich vs Safaricom on EV/Rev (5.4x vs 3.4x)** but Airtel grows revenue ~28% vs Safaricom M-Pesa ~13%, so the premium is partly growth-justified — within the growth-adjusted norm, not an automatic over-valuation flag. The muted debut (shares slipped to £1.93 vs £1.96 offer despite oversubscription) says the market sees it as **fully priced, not cheap**. EV/EBITDA 10.4x is mid-range and reasonable for a ~50%-margin, cash-generative asset.

**Risk flags.**
1. **FX / soft-currency revenue.** Revenue is earned in naira, shilling, kwacha, franc etc. — historically volatile vs the GBP/USD the equity is priced in; a repeat of 2023–24 African currency devaluations would compress reported dollar revenue and the multiple regardless of local-currency growth.
2. **Regulatory / float concentration.** Per-country central-bank rules on e-money float, pricing caps and interoperability mandates can cut the ~0.53% take-rate; a single large market (Nigeria/East Africa) carries outsized regulatory and macro exposure.
3. **Priced-for-perfection + thin LSE liquidity.** Below-range pricing and a sub-offer debut leave little cushion; as the first pure African mobile-money float on LSE, secondary liquidity and index inclusion are unproven, raising a de-rating risk if growth decelerates.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is the sector's **price-discovery event**: a successful float sets the valuation benchmark (~5x revenue) that MTN MoMo and OPay listings will be measured against — a re-rating catalyst if it trades up, or a chilling signal for the whole African-fintech IPO pipeline if it fades. Falsifiable thesis `(analysis)`: if Airtel Money holds above the £1.96 offer through the lock-up and first results print, expect MTN to accelerate a MoMo carve-out/listing; trigger to watch — unconditional trading from Oct 14 and the first post-IPO quarterly TPV/take-rate print. Thesis breaks if the stock drifts well below offer on FX or share-loss news.
