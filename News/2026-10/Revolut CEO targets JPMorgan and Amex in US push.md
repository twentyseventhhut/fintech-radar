## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
In a Bloomberg TV interview (~8 Oct 2026), CEO Nik Storonsky said Revolut's US offering will be **"at par with Amex or JPMorgan Chase"**, that he wants to **"dominate"** the US, and that he favors a **primary US listing** ("that was always the target, given the deep liquidity of the US market") **if** Revolut ever goes public — reaffirming an IPO **not before 2028**, via a UK+US dual listing where the US is now the primary leg. Credit cards and loans are to roll out **only once Revolut holds a full US banking charter**.

**+ Why this way / what it reveals:** This is a CEO **comment**, not a decision or a filing — "if it goes public," no S-1, no float size, no price range, no date beyond "not before 2028." The naming of JPMorgan and Amex is a soundbite, not a strategy delta. The one concrete, verifiable datapoint behind it — OCC **preliminary/conditional** approval for a US national bank charter (3 Sep 2026, Revolut Bank US N.A.) — was already reported in September and is still gated on FDIC + Fed + final OCC sign-off. Nothing in the US is live; cards/loans are contingent on a charter that is not yet granted.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Incumbent targets:** JPMorgan/Chase and Amex measure US customers in the tens of millions with trillion-/hundreds-of-billion-dollar balance sheets. Revolut has **~1m US customers** (2026) and no live US bank — the "at par" framing is aspiration, not a near-term competitive threat (analysis).
- **US neobank field:** Chime IPO'd on Nasdaq 13 Jun 2025 (~$11.6bn, ~8.6-9.5m active members); Cash App/Block is the US scale leader; SoFi is the closest chartered full-suite digital bank; Varo/Dave/Current are niche. Revolut is a minor US player today.
- **European-fintech public comps (the listing rationale):** Klarna listed NYSE Sept 2025; Nubank (NYSE, ~$65bn, FY2025 rev $16.3bn) trades ~4x sales vs Revolut's implied private ~12.5x and IPO-target ~33x — see [[This Week in Fintech Nubank worth more than Revolut]].
- Internal: [[Revolut explores dual listing in London and New York]], [[Revolut raises $3B at $75 billion valuation]].

**+ Why:** The US-venue tilt is a read-through from Klarna/Nubank — deep US liquidity supports a richer multiple than a post-slump London book. The real pressure is the gap to Nubank (profitable, public, scaling); a US listing is partly about matching Nubank's investor access and defending a $150-200bn ask.

## [2] Company history / fit
US ambitions are a **6-year-old, slow-delivering story**: US launch via partner bank (Metropolitan Commercial) in Mar 2020; a 2021 charter bid withdrawn; strategy switched from acquiring a US lender to a de-novo OCC application in Jan 2026 ([[Revolut to seek own US banking license, drops acquisition]]); charter filed Mar 2026 ([[Revolut files US bank charter application, names US CEO]]); 2027 US launch targeted ([[Revolut plans to launch US bank in 2027]]); OCC preliminary approval Sept 2026. Venue stance drifted UK-cool → "dual US+UK widely discussed" → "US more likely, 2-3 yrs out" ([[Revolut signals US IPO two to three years away]]) → "$150-200bn, not before 2028" → now "US primary."

**+ Why:** Revolut wants a software/platform multiple, not a bank multiple — which pushes it toward the deepest capital market (US) and toward owning the full lending stack (charter → cards/loans → net-interest + fee income) to justify the valuation. UK base rate is a warning: 2021 application → restricted licence Jul 2024 → full licence Mar 2026 (~5 yrs), and the US needs three regulators.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Low-to-zero novelty.** The US-lean, the 2028 timeline, the $150-200bn target, the OCC preliminary approval and the 2027 launch were all previously covered; the "par with Amex/JPMorgan" line is a fresh soundbite but not a fresh fact. **No new terms, no new figures, no filing.** Traction on the IPO: zero. Traction in the US: ~1m users, debit/app/FX/crypto via partner, no charter-backed credit or deposits yet — announced, not launched.

**+ Why it's thin:** Venue preference and incumbent-naming carry no economic value-add until there is a filing, price range and float structure. The margin question (who captures value) hinges on the US **charter**, not the listing venue or the rhetoric — and that charter is still only preliminary.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch: (a) full OCC charter + FDIC/Fed sign-off → 2027 US bank launch → actual cards/loans go-live; (b) the H2-2026 secondary at >$100bn (a reported ~$115bn July-2026 employee secondary) as the real near-term price signal; (c) whether London formally loses the primary slot (a UK political sore point). Sentiment: "most anticipated European IPO," but repeatedly pushed out.

**+ Second-order:** By making the US the *primary* venue and inviting comparison to JPMorgan/Amex, Revolut raises the bar — US public markets will price it on hard bank metrics against JPMorgan/Amex/Nubank, where a software multiple is harder to defend than in a sympathetic London listing. The $75bn→$150-200bn aspiration is a private/secondary mark, not a public-tested price; execution risk is a US bank with no US lending track record.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US retail banking is the target, not a greenfield: Revolut is positioning to compete with incumbents JPMorgan Chase and American Express (per Bloomberg TV interview, 2026-10-12), i.e. a frontal push into the deepest, most-contested deposit/cards pool rather than an underbanked niche. Revolut's own FY2025 Annual Report (IR, dated 2026-04-29) frames the scale behind the ambition: group revenue +46% to $6.0bn (£4.5bn), profit before tax +57% to $2.3bn (38% PBT margin), net profit $1.7bn, retail customers +30% to 68.3m, customer balances +66% to $67.5bn — a fifth consecutive profitable year (per Revolut, annualreport2025.pdf). "Why now": Revolut is cash-rich after a ~$3bn 2025 raise at a $75bn valuation ([[Revolut raises $3B at $75 billion valuation]]) and explicitly wants the US as a PRIMARY listing venue "given the deep liquidity of the US market"; the IPO is gated to no earlier than 2028, so the US build-out is a multi-year ground game, not a listing event. Structure: US retail banking is consolidated at the top (JPMorgan, BofA, Wells, Citi, Amex) with regulation + charter/deposit-insurance as the hard entry barrier — a neobank must either rent a sponsor bank's rails or pursue its own charter.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for neobanks: funded/active customers, ARPU/ARPAC, customer balances/deposits, and GAAP profitability. Key players in the US-facing neobank race: Chime (listed, US-native), Nu Holdings (listed, LatAm-native, applying for a US national bank charter — [[Nubank applies for US banking license]]), and Chime pursuing its own national bank charter ([[Chime CEO says national bank charter is 'when, not if']]) — the whole cohort is converging on charters to own economics vs. renting sponsor-bank rails. Basis of competition in the US: distribution and trust, not price — incumbents own primary-account relationships and Amex owns premium credit + rewards, exactly the two names Revolut called out. Revolut's position: globally ahead on profitability and scale (68.3m retail, $6.0bn revenue, GAAP-profitable) but a near-zero-share NEW entrant in the US specifically — catching up on US licensing, brand and credit. Moat is intangibles/product velocity + a multi-product super-app, not US deposit stickiness, which it does not yet have `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Price-to-sales on FY2025 revenue (comparable neobanks):
- **Revolut** — $75bn last-round valuation / $6.0bn FY2025 revenue = **~12.5x P/S** (private round valuation, NOT a market cap; per [[Revolut raises $3B at $75 billion valuation]] + annualreport2025.pdf).
- **Nu Holdings (NU)** — ~$75.6bn market cap (2026-10-06) / $16.3bn FY2025 revenue = **~4.6x P/S** (listed).
- **Chime (CHYM)** — ~$12bn market cap (Nov 2025) / $2.19bn FY2025 revenue = **~5.5x P/S** (listed).
Median of the three ≈ 5.5x. Revolut at ~12.5x is **rich / an outlier vs. listed peers** (~2.3–2.7x the listed median). Partly justified by growth — Revolut revenue +46% vs. Chime +31% — but the gap is wider than the growth premium alone explains, and it is a private, selective-liquidity round mark, not a price-discovered public tape. EV/EBITDA and forward multiples for the private name → [UNSOURCED]. Qualitative read: listed neobank marks sit near ~5x revenue, so a 2028 US IPO would have to argue Revolut deserves a large premium to NU/Chime.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Valuation vs. listed comps.** The $75bn mark (~12.5x P/S) sits ~2.5x above the listed-neobank median (~5.5x); if public multiples hold into a 2028 IPO, the private round could prove the high-water mark, pressuring the eventual listing price (second-order: insiders took liquidity at today's mark, IPO buyers face tomorrow's).
2. **US execution / licensing gap.** Competing head-on with JPMorgan and Amex requires a US charter or sponsor-bank dependence plus years of trust-building; Revolut still lacks a full UK banking licence after a long wait ([[Revolut's full UK banking licence held up by risk concerns]]), a read-through on how slowly regulators clear it. Second-order: without a charter it rents rails and surrenders deposit/interchange economics to the sponsor.
3. **Reputation / fraud & compliance scrutiny.** Revolut has topped UK bank fraud complaints ([[Revolut tops UK bank fraud complaints for third year]]); US OCC/CFPB oversight and Amex/JPMorgan-grade compliance bars raise both the cost of entry and the risk of enforcement friction in a new, litigious market.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Framing the US push as "compete with JPMorgan and Amex" signals a primary-banking and premium-cards ambition — wallet share, not a side account. Falsifiable thesis: if Revolut is serious, the next 12–18 months should show a concrete US charter path or sponsor-bank partnership plus a US credit product, not just PR; **watch for a US bank-charter application or a licensed-partner announcement** as the catalyst. What breaks the thesis: a 2028 listing that prices below the $75bn private mark would confirm the round — not the US story — drove the valuation.

The figures below are the most material earnings context against which that US-push thesis has to be read, taken from Revolut's OWN latest filing: the **Annual Report 2025** (consolidated FS for the year ended 31 Dec 2025, Revolut Group Holdings Ltd), published 2026-04-29.

**Verdict (context, not an earnings print).** BEAT / record year on the company's own reporting — revenue $6.0bn (+46% YoY) · PBT $2.3bn (+57% YoY), PBT margin 38% (up from 35%) · net profit $1.7bn · ~68.3m retail users (+30%). Growth is broad-based (11 product lines each >$135m) and fee-driven, not interest-rate-driven — this is the balance-sheet strength Storonsky is leaning on to pitch the US fight vs JPMorgan/Amex and a primary US listing. No conventional Street consensus exists (private, not listed), so beat/miss is vs the prior year, not vs analysts.

**Key figures (FY2025 vs FY2024).**
- Revenue: **$6.0bn (£4.5bn), +46% YoY** (from $4.0bn / £3.1bn in 2024). 2024 itself was +72%, so growth is decelerating off a larger base but still fast.
- Pre-tax profit: **$2.3bn (£1.7bn), +57% YoY** (from $1.4bn in 2024). PBT margin **38%**, up from 35% — operating leverage, not just top-line.
- Net profit: **$1.7bn**, up from ~$1.0bn in 2024 — fifth consecutive year of net profit.
- Interest income: **£974m ($1.3bn), +23% YoY**; lending portfolio **£2.2bn ($2.9bn), +120% YoY**.
- Total customer balances: **£50.2bn, +66% YoY**.
- Retail users: **~68.3m, +30% YoY**; business clients **767K, +33% YoY** (Revolut Business ~16% of income).

**By segment / driver.** Revenue mix (FY2025 shares): card payments **22.2%** ($1.3bn / £1.0bn, +45%), interest income **21.6%** (£974m, +23%), subscriptions **15.7%** ($936m / £708m turnover, +67%), wealth **14.7%** (£663m / $876m, +31%), FX **13.4%** (£606m / $800m, +43%). Subscriptions (+67%) and FX/wealth pull the mix away from interest dependence.
