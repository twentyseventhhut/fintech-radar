## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Финтехно] Банковский сектор остаётся высокоприбыльным даже при охлаждении экономики. Три квартала 2026 года принесли больше чистой прибыли, чем весь прошлый год: 3,7 трлн рублей против 3,5 трлн. Это происходит прежде всего благодаря высокой процентной марже: ставки по привлечённым вкладам снижаются быстрее, чем падает доходность кредитов. ⬛️ По абсолютной прибыли Сбер остаётся в отдельной категории. За девять месяцев он заработал примерно в 3,6 раза больше ВТБ и почти в 10 раз больше Т-Банка. ⬛️ По темпам роста лидирует Т-Банк, но его результат нужно оценивать с учётом сравнительно низкой базы 2025 года. Тогда прибыль составляла 48,1 млрд рублей. ⬛️ Пять банков заработали суммарно почти 2,47 трлн рублей — около двух третей прибыли сектора. То есть финансовый результат достаточно сильно сконцентрирован. ⬛️ В сентябре основной банковский бизнес принёс банкам больше прибыли, но совокупный результат ухудшился из-за нестабильных источников дохода. ## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What this is.** The Bank of Russia's monthly "Development of the Banking Sector" review for September 2026 (data cut-off 19.10.26). Aggregated by the Fintexno channel. Headline: the sector stays highly profitable even as the economy cools — 9M 2026 net profit of **RUB 3.7 trln already exceeds the RUB 3.5 trln earned in all of 2025**. The driver is a wide net interest margin: deposit rates are repricing down faster than loan yields fall (CBR held the key rate at 14% on 2026-10-23 — see [[Bank of Russia holds key rate at 14%]]).

**September monthly figures (from the CBR PDF, cross-checked vs TASS/Reuters/Frank Media).**
- Net profit fell **5.9% MoM to RUB 414 bln**, mainly from a drop in non-core/volatile income; *core* profit actually rose on smaller loan-loss provisions (~RUB 165 bln of provisions, down from August). Total comprehensive result was RUB 401 bln, slightly below profit due to a -RUB 13 bln negative revaluation of debt securities in capital. Annualized ROE eased to ~23% from ~24%.
- **Corporate lending:** claims on companies +0.7% MoM / +13.3% YoY; corporate loans +0.8% MoM / +13.5% YoY to RUB 104.7 trln. Growth cooled partly from quarter-end interest payments. Metals and real-estate firms drove ~a third of the gain.
- **Mortgages accelerated sharply** to +1.4% MoM (vs +0.5% in Aug) — borrowers rushed to beat the 01.10.26 tightening of "Family Mortgage." Originations hit a 2026-record RUB 617 bln (+62% MoM); subsidized share 70%.
- **Unsecured consumer lending slowed** to +0.9% MoM (vs +1.7%); auto loans +0.4%. Problem corporate loans rose +0.5pp to 12.9% in August (coal/power utilities stress).
- **Funding tightened:** client funds -0.2% MoM (household deposits roughly flat -0.1%; corporate -0.4%), after active inflows in August. This echoes earlier deposit-flow volatility — see [[Russians withdrew 550bn roubles from banks in May]].
- Banks absorbed 77% of the RUB 1.4 trln OFZ auctions (MinFin resumed auctions in Sept); state funding +10.5% MoM.

**Concentration.** Sber earned ~3.6x VTB and ~10x T-Bank over 9M; T-Bank leads on growth rate but off a low 2025 base (RUB 48.1 bln). The top 5 banks earned ~RUB 2.47 trln — roughly two-thirds of sector profit. Sberbank's RAS 9M net profit was ~RUB 1.5 trln (+18.2% YoY). State dominance of the sector is a recurring theme — cf. [[Bank of Russia keeps Raiffeisen, UniCredit systemically important]].

**Why it matters.** High headline profit masks accumulating internal contradictions: corporate credit still growing while retail slows, loan quality deteriorating in pockets (coal/power), and deposit inflows fading. The profit base is also concentrated and partly margin-driven, so it is vulnerable to rate cuts compressing NIM.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. "9M profit already beats all of 2025" is true in nominal rubles — but after inflation (double-digit in Russia) and ruble depreciation, is real-terms profit actually higher, or is this a nominal illusion?
2. If the margin tailwind is "deposit rates falling faster than loan yields," what happens to that NIM when the CBR eventually cuts the key rate from 14% and loan repricing catches up? Is this a peak-earnings quarter?
3. September net profit *fell* 5.9% MoM — the "record" framing is about the cumulative 9M figure, not the trend. Is the headline burying a deceleration?
4. Core profit rose only because provisions shrank (RUB 165 bln). Is lower provisioning prudent, or is it under-reserving against the admitted deterioration in corporate loan quality (12.9% problem loans, coal/power stress)?
5. Problem corporate loans data is August; September figures "will appear after this material." Is the review flattering the picture by lagging the worst segment?
6. Mortgage surge (+1.4%, record RUB 617 bln originations) is a one-off pull-forward before the 01.10.26 Family Mortgage tightening. Does this inflate Sept and set up an October cliff?
7. Concentration: top 5 = ~two-thirds of profit, Sber ~10x T-Bank. Does "sector is profitable" really just mean "Sber is profitable"? How fragile are the smaller 300+ banks?
8. T-Bank "leads on growth" off a RUB 48.1 bln 2025 base — is this base-effect spin, and are the numbers RAS or IFRS, standalone or group? (comparability risk)
9. Client funds fell 0.2% while lending grew — is the sector's funding gap widening, and is it being plugged by CBR/state money (+10.5% state funding)? Is that sustainable?
10. Banks bought 77% of RUB 1.4 trln OFZ. Does rising sovereign exposure concentrate duration/credit risk on bank balance sheets as the state finances deficits?
11. The source is an aggregator channel (Fintexno) summarizing a CBR PDF — are the bank-by-bank multiples (3.6x, 10x) from CBR or the channel's own calc? Provenance of those ratios is unverified.
12. "Unrecognized negative revaluation" on amortized-cost bonds grew to RUB 0.7 trln (~3% of capital). How large is hidden MTM loss if rates stay high and repo access tightens?
13. ROE eased to ~23% — still very high globally. Is this a wartime-economy distortion (subsidized lending, captive OFZ demand) rather than durable franchise quality?
14. Does the review address sanctions/FX constraints at all, or is it deliberately "not for monetary analysis," limiting what conclusions are safe to draw?

**Importance: 3/5** — A clean, numbers-dense read on the whole Russian banking sector from the primary regulator (profit, lending mix, deposits, asset quality) with real signal: margin-driven record profit masking slowing retail, fading deposits, and pocketed corporate-loan stress. But it is a routine monthly macro review, not a company-specific or market-moving event, and Russia is a sanctioned, lower-priority market for this feed — hence mid-tier, not high.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** CBR's September 2026 banking review: the Russian banking sector earned ₽3.7tn net profit over 9M 2026 — already above the ₽3.5tn booked for all of FY2025 (per note / Interfax, via CBR). The CBR has raised its FY2026 sector-profit forecast to ₽3.9–4.4tn, explicitly citing "persistently high margins" (per Interfax, as of Oct 2026). Monthly September net profit was ₽414bn, down 5.9% m/m, driven by weaker non-core income while core profit rose on lower provisioning (per CBR review). The structure is highly consolidated: five banks earned ~₽2.47tn, roughly two-thirds of the sector's profit; Sber sits in its own tier (~3.6x VTB, ~10x T-Bank over 9M per note). The "why now": the sector is riding a positive-carry window — deposit rates reprice down faster than loan yields, so net interest margin stays wide even as the lending cycle cools. The CBR held its key rate at 14.00% on 23 Oct 2026 and raised its 2026 inflation forecast to 7.1–7.6% (annual inflation 6.8% as of 19 Oct), via Reuters/TASS — a prolonged high-rate plateau that sustains the margin tailwind but also seeds asset-quality strain.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs that matter here: net interest margin (NIM), return on equity (ROE), cost of risk/provisioning, and problem-loan share. Structure by profit concentration: Sber (9M 2026 RAS net profit ~₽1.5tn, +18.2% y/y, ROE ~23% per akm.ru/Reuters) >> VTB (8M IFRS ~₽314.8bn, −4% y/y per TASS) >> T-Bank/T-Technologies (growth leader off a low 2025 base of ₽48.1bn per note). Basis of competition: scale and funding cost (state banks' cheap sticky deposits) vs. T-Bank's higher-margin retail/digital franchise. Recent dynamics in the review: corporate lending still growing (+0.7% m/m, +13.3% y/y), retail/unsecured consumer lending slowing (+0.9% vs +1.7% prior), mortgage spiking +1.4% m/m as borrowers rushed ahead of the 01.10.26 tightening of "Family Mortgage." Protagonist's position: the sector as a whole is "late-cycle profitable" — earnings are peak-ish and margin-driven, not volume-driven (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** This is a sector/regulator story, not a single traded name; no per-company valuation in the news, so multiples are "no data." Qualitative cross-base comps:
- [[Thai banks' profitability weakens in 2026 on margin compression]] — contrast case: Thai NIMs compressing in 2026, whereas Russian NIMs are still expanding; the Russian margin tailwind is cycle/rate-specific, not universal (analysis).
- [[Indian listed banks post record ₹4.11 lakh crore profit in FY26]] and [[Public sector banks post record ₹1.98 lakh crore net profit in FY26]] — parallel "record bank-sector profit" prints in another high-rate EM; shows record profits can coincide with either improving (India) or deteriorating (Russia corporate NPLs) credit quality.
- [[SCBX posts consolidated net profit for the period]] — EM digital-bank-group earnings comp.
Peer trading multiples not computed — most Russian majors are state-controlled / sanctioned, so EV/Rev, P/E, P/B are `[UNSOURCED]` from free sources here.

**Risk flags.**
1. Earnings quality / peak-margin risk — September profit already −5.9% m/m, and the profit depends on the deposit-vs-loan repricing gap; once the CBR eventually cuts from 14%, the asymmetry narrows and NIM compresses, so current record profit is not a durable run-rate (why: margin, not volume, is doing the work).
2. Corporate asset quality deteriorating — problem corporate loans rose to 12.9% (₽13.3tn, +0.5pp m/m in Aug), concentrated in coal/power companies; coverage of corporate loans by reserves+collateral slipped to ~45% with ₽7.2tn uncovered (~70% of pre-norm capital buffer). A credit event in these sectors would hit provisioning and the sector's capital cushion (why: second-order drain on the very profit cushion the CBR says will absorb losses).
3. Concentration risk — two-thirds of profit in five banks means sector resilience is really the resilience of Sber/VTB et al.; funding already softened (client funds −0.2% m/m, corporate current accounts −1.7%), so a deposit outflow or a problem at one systemic name is systemic (why: the "stable and profitable" sector narrative masks tail concentration).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Thesis (analysis): Russian bank profitability is at/near a cyclical peak powered by the rate plateau, not a structural re-rating; the mix is shifting from reported profit to building credit risk (corporate NPLs up, consumer/funding slowing). Watch triggers: (a) first CBR rate cut → NIM compression and provisioning normalization; (b) Q4 corporate loan-quality data (noted as not-yet-released in the review) — a jump in IV–V category loans among coal/energy names would falsify the "manageable risk" line. Falsifier: if NIM holds and corporate NPL coverage rebuilds into 2027, the strain thesis is wrong.
