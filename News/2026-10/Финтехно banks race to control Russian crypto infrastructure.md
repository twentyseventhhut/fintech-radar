[Финтехно] Вчера стартовала гонка банков за контроль над инфраструктурой российского крипторынка, и различие стратегий уже видно. С 5 октября вступили в силу положения ЦБ, которые впервые позволяют компаниям официально попасть в новые реестры криптообменников и цифровых депозитариев. # Context-enrichment: Финтехно — banks race to control Russian crypto infrastructure
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5. Freshness: FRESH (distinct bank-strategy commentary on the same registry event; see duplicate note below)._

**[0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd).** This is a Финтехно COMMENTARY (2026-10-06), not a registry announcement. The underlying event — CBR publishing its first crypto-infrastructure registers (5 digital depositaries + 4 exchangers) — is covered canonically by [[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]]. The value-add HERE is the de-PR'd read of DIVERGING BANK STRATEGIES on day one of the admission window (CBR admission mechanism in force 2026-10-05; umbrella law № 282-ФЗ core provisions in force 2026-09-01; law signed early Aug 2026):
- **«Т-Технологии» (T-Bank group) — full-stack, both links.** Filed for BOTH registers: «Атомайз» (Atomyze) → digital-depositary register; ООО «Т-Инвест Лаб» (T-Invest Lab) → exchanger register. Launch planned AFTER an internal employee pilot — i.e. phased, product-led.
- **Сбер (Sber) — depositary-first, distribution-led.** Applied for digital-depositary status only; plans to start with BTC/ETH/USDT custody and embed operations into «СберБанк Онлайн», «СберИнвестиции», «СберБизнес»; launch target ~1 Dec 2026.
- **ВТБ (VTB) — widest ambition, both registers.** Flagged brokerage, a digital depositary AND crypto exchange; is the ONLY entity on BOTH the depositary and exchanger registers. Trading via «ВТБ Мои Инвестиции» "as early as November", own exchange December (per cryptodaily/crypto.news, 2026-10-06).
- **Альфа-Банк (Alfa-Bank) — earliest mover, already testing.** Began a LIMITED pilot in summer — crypto trading in the Alfa-Investments app for a small group of qualified investors (reported assets: BTC, ETH, USDT, USDC, SOL, LTC, ZEC); wants its own digital depositary + ruble-exchange gateways completed in 2026, broader rollout ~Q4 2026 (crypto.news).
- **Why structured this way / what it reveals:** the register is a licence GATE calibrated to balance-sheet + compliance scale (reported min equity ~RUB 15m, scaling by activity), so the "race" is structurally an INCUMBENT race — the banks that already own retail/HNW distribution are racing to also own the custody + exchange layer. The strategy split is the real signal: Sber bets custody-first on its distribution monopoly; T-Bank and VTB go full-stack (same firm holds exchange AND custody — opposite of Western segregation-of-duties); Alfa trades its early-pilot head-start for first-mover learning. (analysis)

**[1] Competitors / peers (the land-grab).** Intra-Russia incumbent contest, not a global-exchange story. Ordering on the "race" axis: Alfa (earliest live pilot, qualified-only) > T-Bank/VTB (both-register, product pipelines) > Sber (largest distribution, Dec target). Non-bank first-wave entrants — «Зефир» (RUB 10k-capital shell, no public product), «Система-Крипто», «Вольтари», «Облачная инфраструктура» — sit on the registers but are the opposite of the land-grab story: likely SPVs/placeholders, not competitors (see [[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]] on «Зефир»). **Second-order:** because CBR lets one bank hold BOTH licences (VTB), the competitive moat is distribution + licence, NOT product — so the winner is whoever converts an existing banking/brokerage funnel fastest, which favours Sber's reach and T-Bank's digital-native UX over everyone else. The multiple-license concentration raises self-dealing/counterparty risk the West deliberately separates.

**[2] Company / regime history & fit.** This continues the multi-year EPR debate (Finance Ministry vs CBR on cross-border crypto settlement). Sber had already pushed crypto (see [[Russia's Sberbank plans crypto-backed loans for corporate clients]], 2026-02); Alfa flagged a depositary earlier in 2026 (see [[Alfa-Bank plans own crypto depositary after peers]]). **Why they act this way:** sanctioned state banks need an ONSHORE, supervised venue — both to capture tax/oversight and to build a cross-border-settlement rail insulated from Western correspondent banking. The law permits crypto for cross-border TRADE settlement while keeping DOMESTIC crypto payments BANNED and retail throttled (RUB 300k/yr per intermediary for non-qualified, after a suitability test; qualified uncapped). The commentary's "strategy divergence" is downstream of this: every bank is optimising for the same regulatory shape, differing only on sequencing (custody-first vs full-stack vs pilot-first).

**[3] Novelty / value-add / traction.** Novelty of the EVENT = high (first legal crypto-infra licences) but that is the sibling note's claim. THIS note's incremental novelty = the comparative strategy map (who filed for which link, launch sequencing, Alfa's live-but-tiny pilot). Traction de-PR'd = essentially ZERO live retail volume: Alfa's pilot is qualified-only and "small group"; Sber/T-Bank/VTB launches are ANNOUNCED (Nov–Dec), not live. **Who captures the margin:** the incumbent that owns distribution — adoption will track the app funnel, not the licence. What breaks the thesis: if the walled-garden (sanctions + domestic-payment ban + RUB 300k cap) keeps the venue illiquid, the "race" is for a small, supply-pushed market (cf. the digital-ruble pattern — [[Russians open 220,000 digital ruble accounts in first month]]: high accounts, low activity).

**[4] What's next / sentiment / risk.** Watch the first REAL de-PR'd volume: Sber custody go-live (~1 Dec), VTB retail trading (Nov–Dec), T-Bank post-pilot launch, Alfa Q4 broader rollout. Risks: (a) **sanctions** — Sber/VTB (OFAC/EU-designated) custodying BTC/ETH/USDT = sanctions-adjacent rails with no path to Western fiat liquidity; (b) **concentration/self-dealing** (VTB on both registers); (c) **demand** — domestic-payment ban + retail cap limit organic uptake. **Counterintuitive second-order:** the "race" narrative overstates competition — the registers formalise a CONSOLIDATION (state/incumbent banks capture crypto custody+exchange) and a deliberately parallel onshore system unreachable by the global crypto stack: legitimacy at home, isolation abroad.

This note adds genuine incremental analysis of the bank land-grab (per-bank filings, launch sequencing, Alfa's live pilot, Sber's distribution-first integration) — which the canonical note even cites BACK to this note for. Kept FRESH on that distinct angle, but importance is downweighted because the core event is already covered and nothing here is live-volume.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russia's crypto market is large but grey: ~20mn users holding an estimated $40–44bn in digital assets per Deputy Finance Minister Chebeskov, Sep 2026 (via tokenpost/digitaltoday); the Bank of Russia itself is more conservative, pegging holdings on global exchanges at ~933bn rubles / ~$11.9bn in mid-2025 (via cryptobriefing) — a 3–4x spread that is itself a data-quality flag. Sberbank projects ~4tn rubles (~$46bn) of regulated volume in year one (via parameter/bitcoinfoundation). Structure: this is a regulated-rails land-grab, not an open market. Law 282-FZ (effective 1 Sep 2026) created the framework; the admission mechanism for the two new registries — digital depositaries (custody) and digital-currency exchangers — went live 5 Oct 2026, and the Bank of Russia named the first cohort on 6 Oct: 5 depositaries (Sberbank, VTB, Voltari, Atomyze, Oblachnaya Infrastruktura) and 4 exchangers (VTB, T-Invest Lab, Zefir, Sistema-Kripto) (via akm.ru/cryptonomist). Why now: regulation is the gating event — the barrier to entry is a Bank of Russia license, which structurally hands the infrastructure to incumbent banks rather than crypto-natives. Retail is capped at 300,000 rubles (~$3,700) per intermediary per year after a suitability test; qualified investors uncapped; domestic crypto *payments* remain banned, only trading/custody/cross-border settlement permitted (via bitcoinethereumnews).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for this layer: assets under custody (AUC) in the depositary, number of registry slots held (depositary + exchanger), and eventual trading volume / take rate — none disclosed yet, all `[UNSOURCED]`. Basis of competition = regulatory speed + distribution (existing banking app installed base), not price. Players and positioning: **T-Technologies** is the only non-Sber player to grab *both* links — Atomyze (depositary) + T-Invest Lab (exchanger) — the fullest stack in the first cohort (analysis), launching after an employee pilot. **Sber** has one slot (depositary), targets a 1 Dec 2026 go-live with BTC/ETH/USDT embedded in SberBank Online / SberInvestments / SberBusiness — i.e. betting on distribution. **VTB** holds slots on *both* registries. **Alfa-Bank** started a limited pilot over summer but is not in the first named cohort (per [[Alfa-Bank plans own crypto depositary after peers]]) — behind the leaders. Position: Sber/VTB lead on distribution scale; T-Technologies leads on stack completeness; moat = regulatory slot + app distribution (switching costs once assets are custodied), a classic incumbent-advantage setup (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** This is a regulatory-admission event with no round/valuation attached, so trading multiples are not computable — "no data" on EV/Revenue, P/E etc. Internal comps (same market/theme): [[Russia names first crypto-market depositaries and exchangers]] (6 Oct, the registry-naming event this piece commentates), [[Russia registers first crypto exchange operators and custodians]], [[Финтехно Sber plans full crypto operations under new law]] (2 Jul, Sber's intent), [[Sberbank plans crypto wallet and custody service by December]] (Dec target precedent), [[Alfa-Bank plans own crypto depositary after peers]]. Cross-border custody analog: [[Absa launches digital asset custody across Africa]]. Valuation reference for one protagonist's parent: [[Tochka Bank valued at 84.8bn rubles ahead of share consolidation]] (not a crypto comp — included only as a Russian-fintech valuation datapoint). Distribution not computed — qualitative comparison only; no public financials on the custody/exchange units.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Market-size uncertainty.** The $44bn (MinFin) vs ~$12bn (Bank of Russia) spread means the revenue pool these banks are racing for is unverified; much of it sits offshore and may not migrate onshore under a 300k-ruble retail cap (second-order: the TAM justifying the land-grab could be a fraction of headline).
2. **Regulatory/sanctions overhang.** Entire framework is a single-regulator construct that bans domestic payments and leans on cross-border settlement — any sanctions-driven correspondent/stablecoin (USDT) cut-off directly impairs the use case the banks are building for.
3. **Concentration + disintermediation of crypto-natives.** Value is captured by ~9 licensed incumbents; registry membership (not product) is the moat, so an unregistered-but-superior UX player is locked out, but equally a registered bank with weak crypto execution can sit on a slot it can't monetize (execution risk).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** The pick frames it as a "race," and the first cohort confirms a two-track split: distribution-led (Sber/VTB, embed-in-superapp) vs full-stack control (T-Technologies/Atomyze). Falsifiable thesis (analysis): whoever ships first with live AUC — watch Sber's 1 Dec 2026 target and T-Technologies' post-pilot launch — sets the default custody rail and compounds switching costs. Breaks if the 300k-ruble cap + domestic-payment ban keep volumes offshore, in which case the registry becomes a compliance shell, not a market. Trigger to watch: first disclosed AUC / trading-volume numbers from any registrant, and whether USDT survives as a retail-eligible asset.
