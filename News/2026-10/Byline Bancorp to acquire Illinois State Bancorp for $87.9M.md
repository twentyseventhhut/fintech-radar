 Byline Bancorp, Inc. and ILLINOIS STATE Bancorp, Inc. Announce Definitive Merger Agreement 
   
 Chicago, IL, October 6, 2026 – Byline Bancorp, Inc. (“Byline”) (NYSE: BY), and Illinois State Bancorp, Inc. ("Illinois State Bancorp"), today jointly announced that they have entered into a definitive merger agreement pursuant to which Illinois State Bancorp and its wholly owned banking subsidiaries will merge with and into Byline Bancorp, Inc. in a cash and stock transaction valued at approximately $87.9 million, based upon Byline's closing stock price on October 5, 2026. 
 The transaction enhances Byline's presence throughout the Chicago market by adding a stable core deposit base, expanding customer relationships, and increasing market density across key communities. Upon closing, the combined franchise will operate 48 branches with approximately $10.5 billion in assets, $8.0 billion in loans, and $8.4 billion in deposits. 
 Illinois State Bancorp, Inc., headquartered in Chicago, Illinois, is the parent company of First Nations Bank and The Bank of Bourbonnais. As of June 30, 2026, on a consolidated basis, Illinois State Bancorp had total assets of approximately $617.3 million, total loans of $470.5 million, and total deposits of $510.5 million. For more than 35 years, Illinois State Bancorp has been committed to providing relationship-driven commercial and community banking services across the Chicagoland and Kankakee County markets. Through its banking subsidiaries, Illinois State Bancorp serves customers from three branch locations in Chicagoland and one branch location in Bourbonnais, Illinois. 
 Roberto R. Herencia, Executive Chairman and Chief Executive Officer of Byline Bancorp, Inc., stated, “This partnership represents another meaningful step in Byline's growth strategy and further strengthens our position in the Chicago market. Illinois State Bancorp has developed deep customer relationships and a strong community banking franchise. Together, we will be well-positioned to continue delivering the local decision-making, personalized service, and financial expertise our customers value while advancing our goal of becoming the preeminent commercial bank in Chicago.” 
 “At Byline, we have always believed that successful partnerships begin with shared values, strong cultures, and a common commitment to serving customers and communities. Illinois State Bancorp embodies those qualities and has established an outstanding reputation across the markets it serves,” said Alberto J. We believe this partnership will drive sustainable growth, enhance long-term stockholder value, and reinforce our commitment to the local markets and communities we proudly serve.” 
 Florian J. We found that partner in Byline, whose strong Chicago market presence, relationship-driven approach to banking, commitment to local decision-making, and continued investment in technology, innovation, and fraud management closely align with our vision for the future. We believe this partnership will create meaningful opportunities for our customers, employees, and communities while building on the strengths of both organizations.” 
   
   
 Transaction Details 
 Under the terms of the definitive merger agreement, at the closing of the transaction, Byline will issue approximately 1.4 million shares of common stock and $28.9 million in cash to Illinois State Bancorp, Inc. shareholders. In addition, Byline will pay approximately $5.1 million in cash in connection with the settlement of all outstanding stock options. 
 Based upon the closing price of Byline’s common stock of $37.63 on October 5, 2026, this represents a fully diluted transaction value of approximately $87.9 million or $261.23 per Illinois State Bancorp common share. 
 The transaction has been approved unanimously by each company’s board of directors and is expected to close during the first quarter of 2027, and is subject to regulatory approvals, the approval of Illinois State Bancorp, Inc.’s shareholders, and the satisfaction of certain other closing conditions. 
 Vedder Price P.C. served as Byline’s legal advisor. D.A. Davidson & Co. served as financial advisor to Illinois State Bancorp, and Barack Ferrazzano Kirschbaum & Nagelberg LLP served as Illinois State Bancorp’s legal advisor.

# Context-enrichment: Byline Bancorp to acquire Illinois State Bancorp for $87.9M
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-06 Byline Bancorp (NYSE: BY), a Chicago commercial bank (~$9.9B assets, 44 branches), signed a **definitive** merger agreement to acquire Illinois State Bancorp — parent of First Nations Bank and The Bank of Bourbonnais (~$617.3M assets, $470.5M loans, $510.5M deposits as of 2026-06-30; three Chicagoland branches + one in Bourbonnais). Cash-and-stock, ~$87.9M total, i.e. $261.23/share.
- Consideration mix (de-PR'd): ~1.4M Byline shares (≈$52.7M at the $37.63 close on 2026-10-05) + $28.9M cash to shareholders + $5.1M cash to settle options → ~$86.7M, rounded by Byline to ~$87.9M fully diluted. So the deal is **~60% stock / ~40% cash** — Byline is conserving equity.
- **Pricing:** one aggregator (GuruFocus) reports ~1.07x tangible book value, minimal ~1.2% TBV dilution, <1.5-yr earnback, mid-single-digit EPS accretion. These accretion/earnback figures are **from Byline's own investor deck, not independently verified** — treat as management guidance. A ~1.0–1.2x TBV entry (vs. mid-2010s community-bank deals at 1.5–2x+) is a **cheap, low-risk tuck-in**, not a premium strategic grab.
- **Why structured this way:** small target, mostly stock → limits cash outlay and TBV dilution so the deal is immediately "clean" for Byline shareholders. The PR anchors to "stable core deposit base" and "market density" — i.e. this is a **deposit-and-branch infill**, not a capability/technology acquisition. Herencia's quote ("preeminent commercial bank in Chicago") is the real thesis: scale consolidation in a fragmented home market.
- Pro forma: 48 branches, ~$10.5B assets, $8.0B loans, $8.4B deposits. Close targeted Q1 2027, subject to regulatory + ISB shareholder approval.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Direct comps (US community-bank M&A, 2026, from corpus):** [[Bank First to acquire PSB Holdings in $202.9 million stock deal]] (WI, all-stock, →$7.6B pro forma), [[Prosperity Bancshares to acquire Stellar Bancorp in $2 billion deal]] (TX, Houston density play), [[OppFi to acquire BNC National Bank for $130 million]] (fintech-buys-charter). Byline's deal is the **smallest and cheapest** of these — a bolt-on, not a transformational merger.
- **In-market rivals:** Wintrust, Old Second, First Midwest/Old National legacy franchises, and the money-center branches (JPMorgan/BofA/BMO) all compete for Chicago deposits. External reporting ("Why is Chicago still 'overbanked'?") frames Chicago as a persistently fragmented, over-branched market → a long runway of small targets for roll-ups like Byline.
- **Position:** Byline is a **serial consolidator ahead of most same-size peers** in deal cadence, but behind Wintrust in absolute Chicago scale. The deal is parity-to-slightly-ahead on consolidation strategy, not differentiated.
- **+ Why:** the Chicago fragmentation is structural (dense legacy community/thrift charters), so density economics (overlapping branches → cost takeout) favor whoever buys locally. Byline's edge is a repeatable integration playbook, not price.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Byline roots to 1978, rebranded "Byline" 2015, IPO 2017. Acquisition track record: **First Evanston (2019)**, **Inland Bancorp (completed 2024, ~$150–165M value)**, now Illinois State Bancorp. A clear, consistent **Chicago in-market roll-up strategy**.
- ISB founded 1991, 35+ yr relationship-banking franchise, Kankakee County + Chicagoland. Logically a **clean fit**: overlapping geography, core deposits, commercial/community mix mirroring Byline.
- **+ Why Byline acts this way:** a mid-cap commercial bank gets its multiple from loan growth + efficient deposit funding. Organic deposit growth is slow and expensive in a high-rate environment; **buying a $510M low-cost core-deposit book at ~1x TBV is cheaper than raising it organically.** Hence the structural pressure: fund the loan book with acquired sticky deposits, take out duplicate overhead, lift ROA. This is the fit — ISB is deposit fuel, not a new business line.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** Nothing new in product, technology, or geography — it is Byline's 3rd Chicago-area bank acquisition on a known template. ISB's own PR cites Byline's "investment in technology, innovation, and fraud management" as a draw → confirms ISB is the one *lacking* scale/tech, i.e. a classic sub-scale community bank selling to survive compliance/tech cost curves.
- **Traction = the real question:** value-add depends entirely on execution of cost synergies and deposit retention through close (Q1 2027). The <1.5-yr earnback is credible given the small size and low premium, **but is management's own projection** — no independent confirmation, and customer/deposit attrition risk is explicitly flagged in Byline's own forward-looking statement ("customer disintermediation").
- **+ Who captures the margin:** Byline captures it — a small seller at ~1x TBV has little leverage; option-holder cash settlement ($5.1M) is the main sweetener for ISB insiders. The deal creates value mainly via **overhead elimination + funding-cost arbitrage**, not revenue growth.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Path: S-4 filing, ISB shareholder vote, regulatory approval, Q1 2027 close. Regulatory risk is **low** given small size and the generally lighter 2025–26 US bank-M&A approval backdrop (cf. [[US bank deregulation unlocks $2.6 trillion lending capacity]]).
- Sentiment: minor/positive for Byline (accretive tuck-in), neutral market event overall. This is a **routine regional-bank consolidation**, not a fintech or structural story.
- **+ Second-order:** the real signal is macro, not idiosyncratic — small community banks keep selling into regional consolidators because standalone compliance/tech/fraud costs no longer pencil out at <$1B assets. Expect more Chicago tuck-ins from Byline/Wintrust. Counterintuitively, cheap ~1x-TBV pricing signals **seller weakness / scarce buyers**, not a hot M&A market.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US community-bank M&A, Chicago/Illinois. Pricing has re-rated up in 2026: Q1 2026 saw 34 deals at a median 1.52x P/TBV; Q2 2026, 45 deals at 1.45x (per WCF Advisors FI updates). Willingness to pay has risen — 44% of acquirers now say they'd pay up to 1.5x TBV for the right target vs 40% a year ago (2026 Bank M&A Survey, Bank Director). Structure is highly fragmented in Chicago specifically: the metro is persistently "overbanked" — a glut of sub-$1bn community banks and a shortage of buyers willing to absorb them, which has kept consolidation slow despite the apparent opportunity (American Banker). Why now: the $10bn-asset threshold. This deal pushes Byline pro-forma to ~$10.5bn assets, crossing a line that brings the Durbin interchange cap and heavier regulatory/compliance burden — a classic trigger to add scale to dilute that fixed cost. Barriers to entry are capital + regulatory approval + deposit-franchise acquisition cost, which favor serial in-market consolidators.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: P/TBV, core-deposit premium, TBV earnback (yrs), EPS accretion. Byline is a serial Chicago consolidator — 11 lenders acquired since 2011, five in the past decade (American Banker) — and the state's dominant SBA 7(a) lender (17th consecutive year as Illinois SBA Lender of the Year; $136.9m 7(a) originations FY2025). Basis of competition for these targets is distribution/deposit density, not price or product. Named Chicago-market peers also buying: First Financial Bancorp (FFBC) closed its Chicago-entry BankFinancial deal Jan 1 2026 [[First Financial Bancorp acquires BankFinancial in all-stock deal]]; larger incumbents (Wintrust, Fifth Third, JPMorgan, BMO, Huntington) compete for SBA/CRE but are not chasing sub-$1bn tuck-ins. Byline's position: ahead/niche as the preeminent-Chicago-commercial-bank aggregator; moat = local decisioning + accumulated in-market scale (switching costs on relationship deposits) `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal terms (Byline 8-K investor deck, ex99_2):
- Price/TBV = **1.07x**; core-deposit premium = **1.6%**; LTM earnings multiple = **13.1x**.
- Consideration ~84% stock / 16% cash; TBV dilution ~1.2%, earnback <1.5 yrs; mid-single-digit EPS accretion; ~$16.6m pre-tax merger costs; cost saves 75% in 2027, 100% 2028+.
- Implied value: $87.9m / $261.23 per ISB share.
Pay-to-trade check: deal 1.07x P/TBV ÷ Byline's own ~1.37x P/TBV = **~0.78** (well below the 100% buyers target) → accretive, buyer paying below where it trades. The 1.07x is also well below the 2026 sector medians (1.45–1.52x) — Byline is buying **cheap vs peers**, not rich. ISB earnings multiple 13.1x vs Byline's own ~10.6–12.2x P/E — modestly above, consistent with a clean small target.
Internal comps (in-base `type/m-and-a`, banking): [[First Financial Bancorp acquires BankFinancial in all-stock deal]] (Chicago entry, all-stock, Jan 2026); [[Mid Penn Bancorp to buy 1st Colonial Bancorp for $101 million]] ($101m cash+stock, similar small-cap community tuck-in); [[Prosperity Bancshares to acquire Stellar Bancorp in $2 billion deal]] (larger regional roll-up). Per-deal P/TBV not disclosed in-base for these → "no data"; used only as structural comps.

**Risk flags.**
1. **$10bn-threshold step-up.** Crossing ~$10.5bn triggers Durbin interchange caps + heightened supervisory/compliance costs — the accretion math depends on out-earning those recurring costs, not just the one-time $16.6m. Why it matters: sours if fee income and cost saves underperform.
2. **Regulatory-approval + closing risk (Q1 2027).** Needs regulatory and ISB shareholder approval; ~2 quarters of close risk. Why: 84% stock means the $87.9m value floats with BY's share price until close (fixed 4.5208 exchange ratio), so a BY drawdown re-prices the deal down for sellers / goodwill up relative to plan.
3. **Core-deposit / customer attrition.** Press release explicitly flags "customer disintermediation" as a risk; the entire thesis is the "stable core deposit base" — if relationship deposits run off post-close, the 1.6% deposit premium and earnback thesis weaken. Why: deposit franchises are the only real asset in a $617m community bank.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Confirms Byline as the default consolidator mopping up Chicago's overbanked long tail; a cheap (1.07x) below-trade tuck-in that adds density without stretching capital. Falsifiable thesis: if cost saves hit (75%/100%) and deposits stick, this is a textbook accretive scale-builder and Byline keeps buying sub-$1bn Illinois banks. What breaks it: TBV earnback slipping past ~1.5 yrs, deposit attrition, or $10bn-threshold compliance drag eroding the mid-single-digit accretion. Trigger to watch: S-4/proxy (deposit beta, intangibles) and the Q1 2027 close.
