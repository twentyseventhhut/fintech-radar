# Nuvei enters Israeli payments market as Payoneer deal expands its reach

## The Canadian fintech company has received a local payment clearing license, allowing it to serve international retailers and airlines processing transactions from Israel. Nuvei also plans to target Israeli technology exporters as it prepares to combine with Payoneer in a $2.75 billion deal.

Sophie Shulman

09:08, 21.10.26 

Following its $2.75 billion acquisition of Payoneer, Canadian fintech company Nuvei is expanding its presence in Israel after receiving a payment clearing license from the Israel Securities Authority.

According to Nuvei, the decision to apply for a local clearing license was driven by demand from its clients, primarily large retailers, seeking to expand their operations in Israel. The company began the application process about a year and a half ago and has now received approval. The license will allow clients including online retail giants Shein and Temu, as well as airlines such as Lufthansa and KLM, to process payments in local currency, among other benefits, while reducing costs.

Nuvei already has a strong position among large retailers and plans to target Israeli high-tech companies, most of which are exporters that need clearing services for payments received from abroad. The license covers payment clearing, not card issuance, meaning Nuvei will not compete with credit card companies in issuing cards. The company will also connect to SHVA, or Automated Bank Services, as required of relevant financial entities operating in Israel.

“One shouldn't underestimate the purchasing power of Israelis; they are very active online and also travel extensively abroad,” Yuval Ziv, Nuvei’s president, told Calcalist. “That is why our clients are very interested in this market, despite its small size. We will also contribute to the Israeli economy, as, until now, merchants from abroad had to go to great lengths to process payments originating from here.”

For consumers, Nuvei’s service is expected to operate behind the scenes. Online merchants serving Israeli customers, however, could benefit from lower payment processing costs, savings that Ziv says should ultimately be passed on to consumers.

“When an Israeli shops on a site like Shein, it is considered an international transaction from the merchant's perspective, which makes it more costly for them. Switching to Nuvei will allow Israelis to pay using local credit cards (rather than international ones), and it will also reduce the rate of transactions flagged as potential fraud, which generally boosts revenue. These cost savings should be passed on to the customers,” Ziv said.

Nuvei already has a local presence in Israel, largely through its 2019 acquisition of SafeCharge, the company founded by Teddy Sagi, for $890 million. In 2021, it also acquired Simplex, a provider of cryptocurrency payment solutions, for $250 million. That acquisition has been considered less successful.

Nuvei currently employs 350 people at its Tel Aviv hub, out of a global workforce of approximately 2,600. The company is headquartered in Montreal. Its Israeli presence is expected to grow significantly following the completion of its acquisition of Payoneer, an Israeli fintech company, under a deal signed in June. Nuvei is set to pay $2.75 billion for Payoneer, which is of a similar scale in terms of revenue and headcount. The combined company is projected to generate an annual revenue run rate of approximately $3 billion and employ nearly 5,000 people.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
### [0] What happened
On 21 October 2026, Nuvei announced it secured a **local payment acquiring/clearing licence in Israel**, granted by the **Israel Securities Authority (ISA)** under the Regulation of Payment Services and Payment Initiation Law, 5783-2023. Nuvei's own PR frames it as a **"direct acquiring licence"** adding Israel as its **53rd local-acquiring market**; the Israeli business press (Calcalist) frames it as a **"clearing licence"** that lets international merchants — notably Shein, Temu and airlines Lufthansa and KLM — process Israeli-shekel transactions **locally** rather than as cross-border card payments. The licence covers **clearing/acquiring, NOT card issuance**, so Nuvei will not issue cards or directly compete with Israel's credit-card issuers. As required of financial entities operating locally, Nuvei will connect to **SHVA (Automated Bank Services)**, the domestic card switch. Nuvei president **Yuval Ziv** told Calcalist the application process began ~18 months ago and was driven by demand from large-retailer clients. (Sources: https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/6t07bknbi ; https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/nuvei-secures-direct-acquiring-license-in-israel-302915340.html ; https://www.stocktitan.net/news/NVEI/nuvei-secures-direct-acquiring-license-in-vsfft56pfemz.html )

### [1] Who / the actor
**Nuvei** — Montreal-headquartered global payments/acquiring fintech ("the infrastructure for every payment, everywhere"). Founder/Chair & CEO **Philip Fayer**. Formerly dual-listed (TSX/Nasdaq: NVEI), it was **taken private by Advent International on 1 April 2024 in an all-cash deal valuing it at ~US$6.3bn** (US$34.00/share, ~56% premium), alongside Canadian holders Fayer (~24%), Novacap (~18%) and CDPQ (~12%); the take-private completed June 2024. Nuvei already has deep Israel roots: it bought **SafeCharge (founded by Teddy Sagi) for ~US$889m in 2019**, and crypto-payments firm **Simplex for US$250m in 2021** (widely viewed as less successful). It runs a **~350-person Tel Aviv hub** (of ~2,600 globally). (Sources: https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-news/nuvei-to-be-taken-private-by-advent-international-in-63-billion-deal ; https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-canadas-nuvei-to-buy-sagis-safecharge-for-889m-1001286741 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuvei )

### [2] Why it matters / stakes
Israel is a small but affluent, highly e-commerce-active market where cross-border giants (Shein, Temu, airlines) currently pay international-card interchange and face elevated fraud flagging. A local-acquiring licence lets these merchants route via local Israeli cards — **cutting interchange, lifting authorisation rates, reducing false-positive fraud declines**, savings Ziv says flow to consumers. It deepens Nuvei's **"Every Payment Everywhere"** local-acquiring land-grab (South Africa was market #52 just a week earlier, see below). Strategically it precedes and complements Nuvei's **US$2.75bn acquisition of Payoneer** (an Israeli-founded fintech), which would roughly double Nuvei's Israel headcount and lift combined annual revenue run-rate to ~US$3bn / ~5,000 staff.

### [3] Context / landscape
Israel's card-clearing market is dominated by three issuers/acquirers — **Isracard, Max (owned by Clal Insurance) and Cal (Israel Credit Cards)** — products of the banking-competition reform that forced banks (Hapoalim, Leumi) to divest card arms. A parallel reform is **opening the payment system**: the Bank of Israel expanded the SHVA identification code to three digits (Jan 2026) and admitted new players (Gama, Max, **Rapyd**) to supervised retail payment systems (Apr 2026). **Rapyd** has applied to the ISA for an expanded clearing licence and intends to **issue cards**, directly challenging Isracard/Max. Nuvei enters this liberalising landscape as a clearing-only, merchant-side global acquirer, competing more with **Adyen and Checkout.com** on international-merchant local acquiring than with domestic card issuers. (Sources: https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/hylast0eje ; https://www.boi.org.il/en/communication-and-publications/press-releases/the-banking-competition-reform-moves-one-step-further-as-isracard-becomes-a-public-company-with-65-percent-of-its-shares-held-by-the-public-in-israel/ )

## Челлендж / ред-тим

### Red-team questions
1. **Framing mismatch:** Nuvei's PR says "direct acquiring licence" (market #53); Calcalist says "clearing licence" from the ISA. Are these the same regulatory object, or is one outlet overstating scope? (Likely same ISA licence; "clearing/acquiring" is terminology, not a second approval.)
2. Is this genuinely a *new* licence, or just a formalisation of acquiring Nuvei could already partly do via its SafeCharge-era Israeli presence since 2019?
3. The note says "enters Israeli payments market" — misleading? Nuvei has operated a ~350-person Tel Aviv hub since 2019. This is a *licence upgrade*, not a market debut.
4. Revenue impact is unquantified. What incremental volume/revenue does local Israeli acquiring actually add for a ~US$3bn-run-rate group? Likely immaterial near-term.
5. Who are the committed merchants vs. illustrative? Shein/Temu/KLM are cited as *eligible beneficiaries* — are any contractually signed to route through Nuvei locally, or is this prospective?
6. Shein and Temu face regulatory/tariff/consumer-protection scrutiny in multiple markets. Concentration risk if Nuvei's Israel thesis leans on a handful of Chinese-origin marketplaces.
7. Competitive reality: Adyen and Checkout.com already offer local acquiring to global merchants in many markets. What is Nuvei's differentiated edge in Israel specifically?
8. Does connecting to SHVA impose cost/latency, and is three-digit code access (Jan 2026 reform) actually granted to Nuvei yet, or still pending?
9. Payoneer linkage: the deal is expected to **close only ~mid-2027** pending regulatory clearance — so synergy claims (double Israel headcount, ~5,000 staff) are forward-looking and not yet realised.
10. Is the "costs passed to consumers" claim verifiable, or vendor spin? Interchange savings typically accrue to merchants first.
11. Any antitrust/foreign-ownership sensitivity to a foreign (Advent-owned, Montreal) acquirer entering a market mid-liberalisation?
12. Simplex (crypto, 2021) was flagged "less successful" — does Nuvei's Israel execution track record support optimism here?
13. Rapyd is pursuing issuance + clearing in parallel; could a better-capitalised local champion crowd out Nuvei's merchant-acquiring niche?
14. Source concentration: the substantive detail is a single Calcalist article + Nuvei's own PR. Is there independent confirmation of licence terms from the ISA?
15. Timing: announced days before this note's date and amid Payoneer deal — is this partly a narrative/PR beat rather than a standalone material event?

**Importance: 3/5** — A concrete, confirmed regulatory licence that extends Nuvei's local-acquiring footprint to its 53rd market and strengthens its Israel position ahead of the material Payoneer deal. But it is incremental (Nuvei already operated locally since 2019), near-term financially immaterial for a ~US$3bn-run-rate group, clearing-only (no card issuance), and heavily single-sourced with illustrative-not-contracted merchant names. More strategically interesting as a marker of Israel's payments liberalisation than as a standalone needle-mover.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Global card acquiring / merchant-payments is large and consolidating around a handful of scale processors; Adyen alone processed €1,285.9bn (~$1.4tn) of volume in FY2024 at €1,996m net revenue (per Adyen H2-2024 results, 2025-02). The structural story here is narrower: Israel in 2023–24 opened its domestic acquiring market to foreign-licensed acquirers under the Regulation of Payment Services and Payment Initiation Law, 5783-2023, and mandated connection to SHVA (the national card-routing switch) — a regulated, capital/compliance-gated entry rather than a greenfield TAM. Why now: Bank of Israel's "open access" reform plus the Strum-Committee-era push to break the bank-owned acquiring oligopoly (Isracard / CAL / Max) has triggered a wave of foreign entrants — Revolut, Rapyd, Mesh Payments, Airwallex and Wise have all obtained Israeli payment licences (per Fintech News UAE; BoI), and Rapyd completed its SHVA connection in April 2026. Nuvei's licence (Israel Securities Authority, 2026-10-21) is one more node in that opening, applied for ~18 months ago per Nuvei president Yuval Ziv (Calcalist, 2026-10-21). TAM for Israeli acquiring specifically: no clean free figure — "no data" (GlobalData/Verified Market Research reports are paywalled).

**Competitive landscape.** Acquiring KPIs: TPV/total volume, take rate (net revenue / volume), EBITDA margin. Nuvei's own scale (last public FY2023): total volume $203bn (+59% YoY), revenue $1.2bn (+41%), Adjusted EBITDA $437m (~36% margin) — per Nuvei FY2023 release. Implied blended take rate ≈ $1.2bn / $203bn ≈ 0.59% (analysis, mixes segments). Israeli incumbents: Isracard, CAL (ICC-Cal), Max, plus licensed acquirer Tranzila — bank/insurer-owned and being restructured (Delek took ~40% of Isracard in 2025; Union+Harel buying 72% of CAL). Basis of competition for Nuvei is NOT consumer cards (the licence is clearing/acquiring only, no issuance — explicitly won't compete with the card companies) but cross-border merchant economics: letting Shein, Temu, Lufthansa, KLM settle Israeli shoppers in local shekel rails rather than as costlier "international" transactions, cutting interchange/FX and false-positive fraud declines. Nuvei's position: niche-but-advantaged entrant — it already has a Tel Aviv hub of 350 (of ~2,600 global) via its 2019 SafeCharge acquisition ($890m) and its 2021 Simplex crypto buy ($250m, described as less successful); the pending $2.75bn Payoneer combination (an Israeli-rooted business) deepens that local footprint. Moat (analysis): existing enterprise-merchant distribution + incumbent in-country ops, not a regulatory monopoly — the same licence door is open to Rapyd/Revolut/Airwallex.

**Comps & multiples.**
- **Payoneer acquisition (direct, pending):** $2.75bn for FY2025 revenue $1,052.8m → EV/Revenue ≈ 2.75 / 1.05 = **2.6x**; vs Adj. EBITDA $272m → EV/EBITDA ≈ 2.75 / 0.272 = **10.1x** (Payoneer FY2025 release; deal value per Calcalist). Low-to-mid for payments — consistent with Payoneer's single-digit (~8%) revenue growth. Internal comps: [[Nuvei to acquire Payoneer for $2.75 billion]], [[Payoneer reports Q4 and full year 2025 results]], [[Linas Newsletter Nuvei-Payoneer completes Advent's payments empire]].
- **Nuvei take-private (2024):** Advent valued Nuvei at EV ≈ $6.3bn (2024-04). Against FY2023 revenue $1.2bn → EV/Revenue ≈ **5.3x**; against Adj. EBITDA $437m → EV/EBITDA ≈ **14.4x** (Nuvei press release). In-line for a scaled, profitable, lower-growth acquirer. No public market cap now (private since 2024) — current multiples "no data".
- **Adyen (external, listed peer):** EV/Revenue ≈ **7.9x** (stockanalysis.com, recent). Richer than Nuvei/Payoneer — justified by ~23% net-revenue growth and 50% EBITDA margin vs Nuvei's ~36% / Payoneer's 26%; not an outlier once growth-adjusted.
- **Checkout.com (external, private peer):** last marked at a **$12bn** valuation in an employee share buyback (2025-10, [[Checkout.com runs employee share buyback at $12bn valuation]]); revenue undisclosed → EV/Rev `[UNSOURCED]`.
Distribution: 3 comparable EV/Rev points (Adyen 7.9x, Nuvei 5.3x, Payoneer 2.6x) — wide spread tracking growth, so qualitative rather than a tight median; Payoneer deal screens cheap, Adyen rich, both explained by growth differential.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Entry is open, not exclusive — margin compression.** The same Israeli licence regime has already admitted Revolut, Rapyd, Airwallex, Mesh and Wise; Israeli acquiring is a small market (Ziv concedes its size) now crowded with scaled foreign acquirers, so this is share-grab in a thin pool, not a moat. Second-order: price-led competition caps the take rate Nuvei can earn locally.
2. **Execution dependence on SHVA + regulator, and on the Payoneer close.** Economics hinge on connecting to SHVA and ongoing ISA/BoI compliance (AML, local-entity obligations); the growth case leans on the still-pending $2.75bn Payoneer deal, which carries regulatory/close risk. If the combination slips, the "significantly larger Israeli footprint" thesis weakens.
3. **No current financial transparency + prior integration track record.** Nuvei is private (Advent, since 2024) with no post-2024 public figures, so volume/economics of this entry are unverifiable; management's own framing that the 2021 Simplex (crypto) deal was "less successful" is a caution on how well bolt-on expansions convert to results. (De-PR: announcement emphasises client demand and cost savings "passed to consumers" — the actual take rate, onboarded-merchant count and fraud-liability split are undisclosed.)

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Marginal for Nuvei globally (one small market), but a clean data point that Israel's acquiring oligopoly is being structurally unbundled by foreign-licensed entrants (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: within ~12 months, cross-border giants (Shein/Temu/airlines) shift a meaningful share of Israeli-shopper volume onto foreign acquirers, pressuring Isracard/CAL/Max take rates. Trigger to watch: how many of the licensed cohort (Rapyd, Revolut, Airwallex, Nuvei) actually complete SHVA integration and announce live merchant volume — licence ≠ live. What breaks the thesis: incumbents retain enterprise merchants and the foreign cohort stays sub-scale in a market "too small to matter."
