MassPay Partners with Bottomline to Add Paymode Virtual Card and Premium ACH to Its Platform 
 Paymode capabilities will expand secure U.S. vendor payment options for MassPay customers 
MassPay is embedding Paymode's virtual card and Premium ACH capabilities directly into its payment orchestration platform.
Customers can pay vendors through a secure payment network without adding another system to manage.
Organizations gain greater control and visibility over vendor payments while reducing exposure to payment fraud.
 
 PORTSMOUTH, NH and LAS VEGAS, NV – October 13, 2026 – Bottomline, a global leader in business payments, today announced a partnership with MassPay, a payment orchestration and global financial platform, to embed Paymode’s virtual card and Premium ACH capabilities directly into the MassPay platform. This will allow MassPay customers to pay U.S. vendors through Paymode’s secure network alongside the payments they already manage through MassPay.
As payment fraud targeting businesses continues to rise, finance teams need vendor payment methods that protect both sides of the transaction. MassPay customers will gain access to Paymode's wide network of enrolled vendors and can use virtual card and Premium ACH as secure vendor payment options. Businesses can reduce fraud risk, keep better control over who gets paid and how, and manage it all on the platform they already use. For organizations still paying some vendors by check, it also offers a simple path to secure electronic payments.
"Security is at the center of every payment our customers make," said Ran Grushkowsky, Co-Founder and CEO of MassPay. "Adding Paymode's services gives our customers secure ways to pay vendors, backed by a trusted network, without adding another system to manage."
"Finance teams are under pressure to protect against fraud while doing more with fewer resources," said Gunita Bindra, Vice President, Partnerships and Business Development at Bottomline. "MassPay has built a strong platform for moving money across a wide range of payment scenarios. Together, we're making it easier for their customers to automate vendor payments securely and move away from paper checks."
The partnership expands Bottomline’s embedded payments ecosystem by extending access to Paymode through strategic partners that help businesses pay and get paid digitally.
Paymode capabilities will be available to MassPay customers starting November 2026.

 About MassPay 

 MassPay is a payment orchestration and global financial platform that helps businesses move money efficiently and securely. Through a single integration, MassPay connects platforms to local rails, stablecoins, mobile wallets and real-time payout methods in 180+ countries, with built-in compliance and recipient account verification. MassPay processes tens of billions of dollars a year, with no contract lock-in and fees only on successful transactions.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] TL;DR.** On **2026-10-13** Bottomline (Portsmouth, NH) and MassPay (payment orchestration / global payouts) announced a partnership embedding **Paymode's virtual card and Premium ACH** disbursement rails directly into the MassPay platform, so MassPay customers can pay U.S. vendors through Paymode's secure network without onboarding a new system. GA to MassPay customers from **November 2026**. Framed as fraud reduction + a check-to-electronic migration path. This is MassPay extending from cross-border/consumer payouts into **domestic B2B accounts-payable (AP)** vendor payments.

**[1] What's actually new.**
- MassPay is primarily a **global payout orchestrator** — one API to local rails, stablecoins, wallets and real-time methods in 180+ countries. Paymode adds the missing piece: a **U.S. AP vendor-payment network** with pre-enrolled suppliers.
- Two Paymode payment methods are embedded: **virtual card** (single-use card numbers → interchange rebate to payer, strong fraud control) and **Premium ACH** (ACH enriched with remittance data + network validation, i.e. payments to Paymode's authenticated supplier directory rather than raw bank details).
- Positioning quotes: Ran Grushkowsky (MassPay Co-Founder/CEO) stresses security; Gunita Bindra (Bottomline VP Partnerships & Business Development) frames it as part of Bottomline's **embedded-payments ecosystem** — extending Paymode reach through partner platforms rather than only direct Bottomline customers.

**[2] Why it matters / stakes.**
- For MassPay, this closes the "money movement loop": after adding **Visa Direct** (2025-12 [[MassPay integrates Visa Direct for global B2B payouts]]), **Cash App payouts** (2026-10 [[MassPay adds Cash App to its direct payout network]]), **Coinbase USDC cross-border payouts** ([[Coinbase and MassPay enable cross-border stablecoin payouts in USDC]]), and expanded US rails, Paymode gives it domestic AP vendor payments — the one B2B segment it lacked. MassPay reports a **third straight record quarter (Q3 2026): payout volume +510% YoY, revenue +112%**, so it is bolting AP onto fast-growing throughput.
- For Bottomline, this is the **distribution strategy for Paymode under Thoma Bravo** ownership (Bottomline taken private for ~$2.6B, closed May 2022). Rather than win AP clients one-by-one, it seeds Paymode into third-party orchestration/ERP-adjacent platforms. Same play as [[Edenred Pay expands B2B network via Bottomline and Paymode]] (2026-01) and [[Bottomline and Koxa partner to deliver ERP-banking]] (2026-02) — a clear partner-led land grab.
- Economics: virtual-card interchange rebates + Premium ACH network fees are Bottomline's monetization; MassPay gets a new revenue line and stickier finance-team relationships.

**[3] Spin / what's missing.**
- **No disclosed economics**: no rev-share, pricing, or volume commitments. "Access to Paymode's network" ≠ guaranteed vendor acceptance — virtual card/Premium ACH only work where the vendor is enrolled or will enroll; check-bound vendors still must be converted.
- Network size framed vaguely. Bottomline's "$16 trillion in payments annually" is the whole company (payments + cash management + bank messaging), NOT Paymode; Paymode's own scale is ~$300B/yr across 500,000+ businesses / 550,000+ authenticated suppliers (per prior Bottomline/Thoma Bravo disclosures). Don't conflate the two.
- US-only, vendor-pay-only for now; not reciprocal (doesn't add inbound/collections). Announced capability, **not yet live** — GA Nov 2026, so adoption/volume unproven.

**[4] Watch next.**
- Nov 2026 GA — does it actually ship on time, and what does pricing look like to MassPay clients?
- Volume signal: whether domestic AP shows up in MassPay's Q4 2026 metrics.
- Competitive response: this puts MassPay+Paymode against AP-automation networks — **AvidXchange, BILL, Tipalti, Stampli, Melio, Ramp** — most of which run their own virtual-card + ACH payment rails. Paymode's edge is a pre-authenticated supplier directory; the threat is these incumbents owning the invoice/approval workflow upstream of the payment.
- Whether Bottomline announces further orchestrator/ERP partners (pattern after Edenred, Koxa).

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. **Is any money or exclusivity disclosed?** No rev-share, pricing, or minimum-volume terms. Treat as a capability/distribution announcement, not a financial event.
2. **Live or vaporware?** Not live at announcement — GA to MassPay customers is Nov 2026. No customer logos or pilot volume cited.
3. **Does "access to Paymode's network" guarantee reach?** No. Virtual card/Premium ACH only hit vendors already enrolled (or willing to enroll) in Paymode. Check-bound vendors still require conversion — the hard part.
4. **$16T vs $300B — which is Paymode?** Neither press figure is Paymode-specific in the release. $16T = whole Bottomline; Paymode is ~$300B/yr, 500K+ businesses, 550K+ suppliers (prior disclosures). Don't overstate network size.
5. **Is this reciprocal?** No — US vendor *pay-out* only. Doesn't add collections/AR or non-US AP. One-directional extension.
6. **New TAM for MassPay or cannibalization?** Domestic AP is genuinely new vs its cross-border/consumer-payout core; but is it a strategic pivot or an opportunistic add-on riding Bottomline's distribution push?
7. **Who owns the customer?** MassPay keeps the platform relationship; Bottomline supplies rails + supplier network. If Paymode is the value, is MassPay a channel or a true co-seller?
8. **Why would a MassPay client not just use BILL/AvidXchange/Tipalti?** Those own the invoice/approval workflow upstream. Paymode-in-MassPay only wins if the client already orchestrates payouts in MassPay and wants AP in the same pane.
9. **Fraud-reduction claim — evidence?** "Reduce fraud exposure" is standard virtual-card/validated-ACH marketing (single-use numbers, authenticated directory). No quantified fraud metric; it's a category claim, not a MassPay result.
10. **Is Bottomline's partner strategy working?** This is the 3rd partner-led Paymode distribution deal in ~10 months (Edenred 2026-01, Koxa 2026-02). Repetition suggests strategy, but none has disclosed resulting volume — pattern unproven.
11. **Interchange-economics risk?** Virtual-card rebates depend on vendor card acceptance; large vendors often refuse card (interchange cost) and push ACH. Rebate upside may be thin in practice.
12. **Regulatory/compliance angle?** Domestic US AP ACH + card — mature, low regulatory novelty. Nothing new here; the compliance story is MassPay's existing payout KYB/recipient verification.
13. **Timing — real or PR cadence?** Lands amid MassPay's record-quarter PR blitz (Visa Direct, Cash App, Coinbase, Validate). Could be momentum-manufacturing as much as a product milestone.
14. **Does it move either company's numbers?** Both private; MassPay figures are self-reported % growth off an undisclosed base. Not independently verifiable.
15. **Dedup check:** Distinct from [[Edenred Pay expands B2B network via Bottomline and Paymode]] (different partner), from [[MassPay adds Cash App to its direct payout network]] (consumer payout rail, not AP), and from [[MassPay integrates Visa Direct for global B2B payouts]] (push-to-card payouts, not Paymode AP network). Fresh.

**Importance: 2/5** — A credible, strategically coherent capability/distribution partnership (MassPay fills its domestic-AP gap; Bottomline extends Paymode via a partner channel, consistent with its Edenred/Koxa pattern). But: both parties private, no disclosed economics, not yet live (Nov 2026 GA), US-vendor-pay-only, and no customer/volume proof. Incrementally meaningful for both companies and a datapoint on Bottomline's partner-led Paymode strategy, but not market-moving.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** B2B/AP payments automation — the "last paper mile" of corporate finance (US still ~1/3+ of B2B spend paid by paper check). Sector sizing is noisy and source-dependent: one vendor report pegs the broad B2B payments market at ~$1.48tn (2025) → ~$1.61tn (2026) (per Straits Research, via marketresearch pages, as of 2026) — this is flow, not software revenue, so treat as context, not TAM. The virtual-card slice is the growth engine: ~$565bn → ~$674bn flow, ~19% CAGR (per The Business Research Co., as of 2026). AP-automation software proper is a much smaller pool (low-double-digit $bn). Drivers + "why now": (1) rising B2B payment fraud (BEC/vendor impersonation) pushing finance teams off checks onto verified networks — the explicit hook in this PR; (2) virtual-card economics — card interchange (~1.5–2.5% of spend) funds rebates to buyers, giving a "free/profit-center" path off checks, which is the real monetization engine behind "Premium ACH" + vcard. Why it matters: the economic pull isn't efficiency, it's the interchange rebate — second-order effect is that AP migration is self-funding as long as interchange holds.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: network size (enrolled/verified vendors), annual payment volume/TPV, take rate (interchange capture on vcard + per-txn ACH fee), digital-conversion/attach rate. Players + basis of competition — it's a network-effects game (enrolled-vendor density), not a price game:
- **Bottomline/Paymode** (protagonist's partner): >600k verified businesses, >$500bn processed annually, ~3,000 new vendors/mo (per Bottomline, as of 2026); Thoma Bravo portfolio co.
- **BILL (BILL)**, **AvidXchange** (now Corpay/TPG), **Corpay**, **Edenred Pay**, **Finexio**, **Basware** — mix of AP SaaS + payment networks.
- Recent moves (dated): Edenred Pay integrated Paymode (Jan 2026) [[Edenred Pay expands B2B network via Bottomline and Paymode]]; Bottomline+Koxa ERP-banking (Feb 2026) [[Bottomline and Koxa partner to deliver ERP-banking]]; Extend+WEX put vcards into SAP Concur (May 2026) [[Extend and WEX enable virtual card payments in Concur Invoice]]; AvidXchange taken private by Corpay/TPG, $2.2bn, closed 15 Oct 2025 [[Anchorage Digital acquires Routable for corporate payment rails]] (corpus references Corpay/AvidX context).
- **Protagonist position (MassPay):** niche-but-complementary (analysis). MassPay is a global-payout orchestrator — 180+ countries, local rails/stablecoins/wallets, "tens of billions/yr" processed, fees only on success (per note). It is NOT a domestic AP-automation vendor; it is **buying distribution to Paymode** to plug its US-vendor gap. Moat for MassPay = cross-border breadth + multi-rail orchestration (incl. stablecoins, cf. [[Coinbase and MassPay enable cross-border stablecoin payouts in USDC]], [[MassPay adds 35+ local fiat funding currencies for payouts]]); moat for Paymode = the 600k verified-vendor network (switching cost / density). Note is silent on economics — no revenue share, no interchange split, no MassPay volume broken out.

**Comps & multiples.** This is a partnership (no round/valuation), so protagonist multiple = n/a. Reference anchors for the AP-payments stack:
- **BILL (BILL):** mkt cap ~$4.05bn (19 Mar 2026), FY2026 revenue $1,653.2m (per BILL 8-K / stockanalysis, as of 2026) → mkt-cap/revenue = $4.05bn / $1.65bn ≈ **2.5x** (P/S proxy; not clean EV, cash/debt not netted → `[UNSOURCED]` EV). In-line-to-cheap for ~13% growth.

- **Bottomline:** private (Thoma Bravo, $2.6bn all-cash, closed May 2022, $57/sh; per Thoma Bravo / fintechfutures) — stale 2022 mark, not a current multiple → **no data** on current value; EV/EBITDA for Bottomline/MassPay → no public figures → `[UNSOURCED]`.
- Distribution not computed (only 2 current public revenue multiples, 2.5x–4.5x, different growth/situations); qualitative read: AP-payments software trades ~2–5x revenue in 2025–26, well below hyper-growth fintech — a mature, cash-flowy, consolidating pool.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Materiality/scale is unquantified (both sides).** "Tens of billions/yr" (MassPay) and ">$500bn" (Paymode) are totals, not the partnership's incremental US-vcard volume. No disclosed revenue share or ramp. Why: easy headline, but this could be a thin distribution add-on; live-adoption (available Nov 2026) is announced, not proven.
2. **Interchange pressure = the P&L under the product.** Virtual-card economics depend on commercial-card interchange funding rebates. Any regulatory/scheme compression of commercial interchange (an active global theme) directly erodes the "move off checks for free" pitch. Why: the migration incentive is interchange-funded; squeeze it and vcard attach stalls, pushing volume to thinner-margin ACH.
3. **Disintermediation / dependence on someone else's rails.** MassPay is renting Paymode's domestic network rather than owning US-vendor density — it captures orchestration, Bottomline captures the network economics. Second-order: in US AP, MassPay stays a guest on Bottomline's rails, and Bottomline could prefer/price-favor its own front-ends (BILL, Edenred Pay, direct) over MassPay.
4. (minor) **Competitive crowding of the same network.** Paymode is simultaneously distributing through Edenred Pay, Koxa banks, and others — MassPay is one of several front doors, limiting exclusivity/moat.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Marginal, not structural: confirms Bottomline's "Paymode-as-embedded-network" land-grab (sign up every orchestrator/AP front-end) and MassPay's pivot from pure cross-border/stablecoin payouts toward a fuller US B2B footprint. Falsifiable thesis: if the partnership is real distribution, expect MassPay to later disclose US vcard/ACH volume or a named-logo vendor-payment win by mid-2027; trigger/what breaks it — if no volume/customer metric surfaces and it stays a press-release-only integration, treat as table-stakes feature parity, not a growth driver. Watch: commercial-interchange regulation as the sector's swing factor.
