## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** The Information (Michael Roddan, Oct 23, 2026) reports that after a proposed Ramp–Airwallex payments partnership collapsed in April 2025, Ramp's politically connected backers "went nuclear": they lobbied U.S. lawmakers to investigate Airwallex's China ties. Airwallex says it withdrew from the talks for commercial reasons and disputes the national-security allegations. The story reframes an ordinary B2B-fintech deal break-up as a geopolitically weaponised rivalry between two of the hottest corporate-spend / cross-border-payments players. ([theinformation.com](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/ramp-investors-went-nuclear-airwallex-doomed-deal), [gokhshtein.com](https://gokhshtein.com/news/2026-10-25-ramp-backers-pushed-lawmakers-to-probe-airwallex-after-deal))

**[1] Who the players are.** Ramp is the U.S. spend-management / corporate-card leader, last valued at **$44B** (June 2026 Series F, $750M, led by ICONIQ Growth/GIC/Ontario Teachers') — a near-quadrupling from $13B in early 2025 and $16B at its [[Ramp raises $200M Series E at $16bn valuation]] round, driven by an AI-spend narrative and its deepened [[Ramp and Visa deepen partnership on payments AI automation]] tie-up. Airwallex is the Melbourne-founded, Singapore-HQ global cross-border-payments/B2B platform, last valued at **$12B** ([[Airwallex raises new funding at $12 billion valuation]], May 2026, led by Addition) — up from $8B at its [[Airwallex raises $330M Series G at $8bn valuation]] round, having [[Airwallex crosses $1B annualized revenue]]. Airwallex counts Tencent and HongShan (ex-Sequoia China) among minority holders. ([cnbc.com](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/04/ramp-valuation-funding-ai-spend.html), [axios.com](https://www.axios.com/pro/fintech-deals/2026/05/28/airwallex-addition-valuation))

**[2] The deal and why it collapsed.** Ramp and Airwallex explored a payments partnership (terms/scope undisclosed; likely Airwallex supplying cross-border rails to Ramp's bill-pay/global-money-movement). Ramp CEO Eric Glyman learned in **April 2025** the deal was falling apart. Per The Information, Airwallex pulled out "partly amid investor pressure"; Airwallex's public line is that it ended talks "for commercial reasons." The timing matters: Ramp's own Visa issuing/AI partnership and global ambitions give it reasons to build or source cross-border capability elsewhere rather than deepen reliance on a China-linked rival. ([theinformation.com](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/ramp-investors-went-nuclear-airwallex-doomed-deal))

**[3] The "nuclear" escalation — dated timeline.** The break-up triggered a public campaign by Ramp-aligned investors. **Dec 2025:** Khosla Ventures MD and Ramp board director **Keith Rabois** posted on X that Airwallex is a "Chinese backdoor into sensitive American data," alleging ~40% of staff in mainland China/Hong Kong and >20% Chinese ownership legally oblige data hand-over to Beijing. **8VC's Joe Lonsdale** joined the push on lawmakers. Senator **Tom Cotton** wrote to Treasury Secretary Bessent urging a **CFIUS** review of Tencent's and HongShan's minority stakes (and earlier a DOJ review). Airwallex CEO **Jack Zhang** called the claims "patently false" / "conspiratorial," said no U.S. customer data goes to China (stored in U.S./Singapore, citing a Coalfire assessment), and publicly asked whether Ramp's founders are "comfortable with politically connected investors… using a founder's Chinese ethnicity and national-security as weapons against a competitor." Airwallex subsequently **relocated staff out of China**. ([cxtoday.com](https://www.cxtoday.com/security-privacy-compliance/fintech-feud-erupts-after-rabois-chinese-backdoor-jab-at-airwallex-ceo-zhang-fires-back/), [capitalbrief.com](https://www.capitalbrief.com/briefing/airwallex-ceo-denies-allegations-that-us-user-data-is-being-sent-to-china-926c5a94-5d49-47cc-be83-c32899fd7b92/), [cryptobriefing.com](https://cryptobriefing.com/airwallex-china-staff-relocation-security/))

**[4] Why it matters.** This is a landmark case of U.S.–China geopolitics being deployed as a competitive weapon inside fintech. For Airwallex it is an acute **IPO/regulatory overhang**: a live CFIUS/DOJ cloud and a possible forced divestment of Chinese shareholders would complicate a U.S. listing (its president Lucy Liu has said the firm wants to be "IPO-ready," while flagging it's "not the best time"). For Ramp, the aggressive-investor playbook risks reputational blowback even as it defends its home turf. For the wider B2B-payments market, it signals that cross-border infrastructure with China exposure now carries a national-security discount, pushing U.S. buyers toward "clean" domestic rails and raising the bar for any partnership/M&A involving China-linked payments firms. ([fortune.com](https://fortune.com/article/airwallex-fundraise-lucy-liu-ai-fintech-ipo-08-20-2026/), [x.com](https://x.com/awxjack/status/2101361635437015114))

**Red-team Q&A**

1. **Is the causation (deal collapse → investor campaign) established or inferred?** Partly inferred. The Information frames the lobbying as a reaction to the April-2025 break-up, but the public Rabois/Cotton attacks are dated Dec 2025+ — an 8-month gap. The link is journalistically asserted, not proven. OPEN.

2. **Did Airwallex really pull out "amid investor pressure," or for commercial reasons?** Disputed. The Information says "partly amid investor pressure"; Airwallex says purely commercial. Both can be true. Unresolved — single-sourced on the "pressure" angle. OPEN.

3. **Are the China-data allegations substantiated?** No independent proof cited. Rabois asserts legal data-handover obligations; Airwallex cites a Coalfire security assessment and U.S./Singapore data residency, and relocated China staff. No regulator has published a finding. OPEN.

4. **Is there an actual CFIUS/DOJ investigation, or only requests for one?** As reported, these are *requests/letters* (Cotton → Bessent; DOJ ask) — not confirmed open investigations with outcomes. Status of any formal review is unclear. OPEN.

5. **What exactly was the proposed partnership?** Terms and scope undisclosed. Likely Airwallex supplying cross-border/FX rails to Ramp, but not confirmed. OPEN.

6. **Could this be a paywall-sourced single-origin story?** Yes — the primary is The Information (paywalled); corroboration comes from secondary aggregators (gokhshtein, Capital Brief, cxtoday) and X posts. Core new claim rests heavily on one outlet. Treat specifics with caution.

7. **Conflict of interest for the accusers?** Rabois (Ramp board/Khosla) and Lonsdale (8VC) are direct beneficiaries of Airwallex being hobbled; Rabois is also tied to Stripe, another cross-border rival. The security framing is not disinterested. ANSWERED.

8. **Are the valuations correct?** Ramp $44B (June 2026, confirmed CNBC/Bloomberg/TechCrunch); Airwallex ~$12B (May 2026, Axios). ANSWERED.

9. **Is this note duplicative of the earlier funding notes?** No. Prior corpus notes cover funding rounds/revenue milestones; none covers the deal collapse or the investor/CFIUS campaign. This is a net-new development. ANSWERED → fresh.

10. **Does the aggregated blurb misstate anything vs sources?** The blurb says "April 2025" withdrawal and "investor pressure," consistent with sources. Accurate. ANSWERED.

11. **Could the campaign backfire legally/reputationally on Ramp?** Possible — Zhang's framing (weaponising a founder's ethnicity) invites a defamation/ethics counter-narrative; no litigation reported yet. OPEN.

12. **Does Airwallex actually have material U.S.-government/defence customers at risk?** Rabois claims payroll data for defence contractors flows through Airwallex; unverified. Airwallex's U.S. footprint vs claims is not independently quantified. OPEN.

13. **Is the Tencent/HongShan ownership still above the thresholds cited?** Airwallex diluted Tencent below 10% in late-2025 round per earlier reporting; HongShan stake size unclear. The ">20% Chinese-owned" claim may be stale or aggregate. OPEN.

14. **IPO impact — concrete or speculative?** Speculative but material: a live CFIUS overhang plausibly delays/complicates a U.S. listing; Liu has publicly hedged IPO timing. Directional, not quantified. PARTIAL.

15. **Any sign U.S. regulators have acted against Airwallex to date?** No public enforcement/finding reported as of Oct 2026 — only political letters and self-remediation (staff relocation). OPEN.

**Importance: 4/5 — rationale.** A high-signal, named-player story at the intersection of the two leading corporate-spend/cross-border fintechs, a $44B vs $12B rivalry, and U.S.–China national-security politics being used as a competitive weapon — with real IPO/CFIUS stakes for Airwallex. Capped below 5 because the core new claim is largely single-sourced (The Information, paywalled), the deal terms are undisclosed, and the "investigations" are so far requests rather than confirmed enforcement.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits at the collision of two adjacent B2B-fintech subverticals: US corporate-spend/expense management (Ramp) and global cross-border payments (Airwallex). Cross-border B2B flows are a very large but low-take-rate base — estimated ~$198tn in 2026 growing ~6% CAGR (per Mordor Intelligence, via researchandmarkets/mordorintelligence listings, as of 2026); the narrower cross-border payments *revenue* pool is far smaller (~$237bn in 2026, ~7.4% CAGR per Spherical/360iResearch listings). Structure: fragmented at the edges (hundreds of fintechs) but increasingly consolidating as scaled players stack cards + FX + treasury + AP into one platform. Barriers are regulatory (multi-jurisdiction licensing, money-transmission, banking rails) and data/network driven. Why now: (1) the "AI-agent finance stack" narrative is re-rating spend/payments names at a premium (Ramp tripled its valuation in ~12 months on this thesis, per TechCrunch 2026-06-04); (2) US-China national-security politics has become a live competitive weapon in fintech — the novel driver in this story.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for spend/cards — interchange/card volume, ARR, net revenue retention (Ramp ~70% of revenue is card interchange per valueaddvc); for cross-border — TPV, take rate on FX/payments, ARR. Players: Ramp and Brex (now being acquired by Capital One for $5.15bn, see internal comp) in US spend; Airwallex, Wise, Nium, Payoneer in cross-border. Basis of competition: product breadth + distribution, increasingly plus the AI-automation story. Recent moves with dates: Airwallex scrapped a proposed Ramp payments partnership in **April 2025**; Ramp investors (Joe Lonsdale, Keith Rabois) subsequently pushed US lawmakers / CFIUS to probe Airwallex's China ties (reported by The Information, 2026-10-23); Airwallex CEO Jack Zhang called the allegations "false" and said data is stored domestically; Airwallex raised a $320M Series H at **$11bn** (2026-06, airwallex.com newsroom). Protagonist position: Ramp is the clear scale leader in US spend (ahead); Airwallex is a scaled cross-border challenger whose moat (licensing + FX network) is partly offset by a "China-backed" governance overhang `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Private names → round/secondary valuations, not market caps; revenue figures are annualized run-rate / ARR, so EV/Rev here is a valuation-to-ARR proxy, not a clean EV/Revenue multiple.
- **Ramp:** $60bn valuation (Oct 2026 $1.85bn round) on >$2bn ARR → `$60bn / $2bn = ~30x` ARR. Prior: $44bn / ~$1bn (Jun 2026) = ~44x; valuation-to-ARR has actually compressed as revenue caught up. [IR: funding_announcement "Ramp at $44 Billion", ramp.com/blog/ramp-at-44-billion-the-third-pillar.]
- **Airwallex:** $11bn (Series H, Jun 2026) on ~$1.5bn ARR (May 2026) → `$11bn / $1.5bn = ~7.3x` ARR; ~$1.3bn ARR / $287bn annualized TPV in Mar 2026 implies a blended take rate of only ~0.45% `(analysis)`. [IR: funding_announcement $330M Series G at $8bn, airwallex.com newsroom 2025-12-08.]
- Internal comps: [[Ramp raises $750M Series F at $44B valuation]]; [[Ramp raises $200M Series E at $16bn valuation]]; [[Airwallex raises new funding at $12 billion valuation]]; [[Capital One to acquire Brex for $5.15 billion]].
- Flag: Ramp looks **rich** vs Airwallex (~30x vs ~7x ARR) — only defensible if interchange-heavy spend revenue is valued far above thinner cross-border take-rate revenue; the AI-agent story is doing real multiple work here. Distribution not computed (only 2 directly comparable scaled privates); qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **National-security / CFIUS overhang on Airwallex** — a CFIUS probe or forced divestment of Chinese investors would impair its US growth and cap its valuation; second-order: it chills any US fintech from partnering with China-backed rails.
2. **Governance/reputational risk for Ramp** — politically-connected backers weaponizing a competitor's founder's ethnicity (per Jack Zhang's public rebuttal) invites blowback and regulatory/press scrutiny on Ramp itself.
3. **Valuation vs growth for Ramp** — ~30x ARR at $60bn leaves no margin for an AI-narrative de-rating; if spend/card growth normalizes, the multiple is the risk, not the business.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This marks national-security politics becoming a mainstream competitive lever in fintech, not just in chips/telecom — a new, non-economic moat/weapon. Falsifiable thesis: if CFIUS formally opens an Airwallex review, expect it to delay/discount an Airwallex IPO and push it toward a capital raise or partial Chinese-investor exit; the trigger to watch is any Senate/CFIUS action in the next 2 quarters. Thesis breaks if lawmakers decline to act and Airwallex IPOs cleanly at or above its $11-12bn mark.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (headline read).** NOT AN EARNINGS PRINT — this is a deal-collapse / corporate-drama story (The Information, Oct 23, 2026), not a quarterly report. No results/guidance are reported in the news. But both parties are growth-stage private companies with disclosed metrics, so this layer adds: disclosed run-rates + growth + the competitive financial standing that frames why the Ramp–Airwallex B2B-payments fight turned "nuclear." Both are scaling fast and raising at rich marks; neither discloses profit beyond Ramp's one "positive free cash flow" claim.

**Key figures (disclosed; both private — no GAAP P&L).**
- Ramp: annualized revenue crossed $1.0bn (Sep 2025, IR press release), then "more than doubled to over $2bn" by Oct 2026 — roughly +100% in ~4 months / well over +100% YoY. 85,000 customers; ~$275bn annualized purchase volume, up +175% YoY. CEO Eric Glyman has claimed "positive free cash flow"; no margin figure disclosed (analysis: profitability claim is unaudited, treat as management color). Valuation: $16bn (Jun 2025) → $22.5bn (Jul 2025) → $32bn (Nov 2025) → $44bn (Jun 2026, $750m Series F) → ~$60bn (Oct 15, 2026, $1.85bn round led by Dragoneer/Thrive/Founders Fund) — ~36% step-up in four months. At ~$60bn on >$2bn ARR ≈ ~30x revenue.
- Airwallex: $720m annualized revenue (Mar 2025, +90% YoY) → $1.3bn (Mar 2026, +74% YoY, per IR); Sacra estimates ~$1.5bn ARR by mid-2026; company targeting ~$2bn ARR by YE2026. >$130bn annualized payments volume; ~150,000+ business customers. Regional: APAC +83% YoY revenue / +49% volume (Q3 2024 IR); Southeast Asia +108% YoY. Surpassed US$1bn global AUA in its Yield/treasury product (Mar 2026). Valuation: $6.2bn (May 2025) → $8bn (Dec 2025, $330m Series G) → $11bn (Jun 2026, $320m Series H led by Addition) ≈ ~8.5x ARR.

**By segment / driver.** Ramp: growth driven by enterprise adoption and AI-finance product expansion; purchase volume (+175%) is outrunning revenue, consistent with a spend-platform take-rate model scaling customer count (25k→40k→85k over the funding arc). Airwallex: growth led by cross-border payments volume, geographic expansion (EMEA >$1bn invested Mar 2026, dual HQ in SF Dec 2025) and newer treasury/Yield + acquisitions (Leapfin Jun 2026, Indonesia/Korea deals). Both are layering AI-agent finance narratives onto the core.

**vs expectations / prior period.** No consensus exists (private, no analyst estimates) → [UNSOURCED] for any "beat/miss." On YoY momentum: Ramp is the faster grower off a similar/larger base (>+100% vs Airwallex ~+74%) and carries a ~5.5x richer valuation (~$60bn vs $11bn) and ~3.5x the revenue-multiple (~30x vs ~8.5x ARR) — the market is pricing Ramp's US spend-management + AI story far above Airwallex's global-payments infrastructure. Airwallex grows off a broader international/volume base but at a lower multiple.

**Guidance / forward.** No forward guidance in the news (not an earnings event). Only disclosed forward marker: Airwallex's internal ~$2bn ARR YE2026 target (management color, not guidance). Ramp gives none beyond the FCF-positive claim. (analysis) Expect both to keep raising and pressing the AI-agent-finance pitch; the capital arms race (Ramp +$2.6bn across two 2026 rounds; Airwallex ~$650m across Series G+H) is itself the competitive signal.

**Thesis-flags (from the failed deal).**
1. Competition has gone adversarial, not just commercial. Ramp investors reportedly pushed U.S. regulators to probe Airwallex's China links after the Apr-2025 partnership collapse (Airwallex says it exited for commercial reasons and disputes the allegations).
