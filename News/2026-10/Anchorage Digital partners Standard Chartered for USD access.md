Anchorage Digital Expands Global Services with Standard Chartered
 TL;DR: 
Anchorage Digital now offers USD accounts and access to SWIFT wires, including transfers to and from third parties, to eligible international institutional clients through a partnership with Standard Chartered
Institutional clients of Anchorage Digital outside the United States can hold USD, move money across borders, and settle 24/7 with in-network counterparties, all on the same platform they currently use for digital asset custody and trading
 Financial infrastructure that keeps pace with innovation 
Anchorage Digital is building toward a financial system where traditional rails and blockchain networks converge, where fiat and digital assets sit side by side in the same account, and where value moves anywhere in the world at any hour of any day.
Today, many institutions still operate across a divide. Global businesses need to move money around the clock while the banking system beneath them runs on cutoff times and multi-day settlement windows, and companies in the digital asset economy carry the added cost of limited access to institutional USD accounts and separate providers for payments, custody, and settlement.
We are closing that gap, so institutions can move fiat and digital assets 24/7 on one regulated platform, at global scale.
“As client needs evolve, financial infrastructure has to advance to facilitate greater speed, access and reliability. At Standard Chartered, we believe in banking the digital ecosystem by providing the regulated banking, payments, liquidity and risk infrastructure that digital asset firms need to operate at institutional scale. By bringing Standard Chartered’s trusted banking systems and global network together with Anchorage Digital’s regulated platform, we are enabling institutional firms the ability to move and manage fiat and digital assets more seamlessly, securely and efficiently across borders.” — Luke Boland, Global Head of Fintech, Standard Chartered. 
 Expanding B2B payments to global businesses 
Anchorage Digital Singapore Pte. Ltd. now offers clients access to USD accounts and SWIFT transfers, extending a capability already available through Anchorage Digital Bank to institutions worldwide. 
Enabled by a partnership with Standard Chartered, the offering complements the digital asset custody, trading, and settlement infrastructure Anchorage Digital is known for. Clients onboarded through Anchorage Digital Singapore can also move USD around the clock, including weekends and holidays, with counterparties that bank with Standard Chartered Singapore. 
Whether an institutional client onboards through Anchorage Digital Bank in the United States or Anchorage Digital Singapore internationally, they gain access to one place to manage both fiat and digital assets – a single platform that powers modern, global businesses.
"Global businesses should not have to choose between the speed of digital assets and the reliability of traditional banking rails. With this expansion, clients around the world can hold USD alongside digital assets, send and receive fiat wires, and settle around the clock, all on one platform." — Nathan McCauley, CEO and Co-Founder, Anchorage Digital 
The expansion deepens Anchorage Digital's presence in Asia Pacific, a region where banks, asset managers, family offices, and corporates are building digital asset strategies at scale. These institutions will have access to the same secure, compliant infrastructure Anchorage Digital delivers to clients globally.
 Core capabilities 
Within a single integrated account structure, eligible institutional clients can access:
 USD accounts: institutional accounts for operating funds and global payments
 Named virtual accounts: spin up accounts in your name with unique routing details to segregate funds, attribute flows, and streamline reconciliation
 SWIFT payments: send and receive international USD wires, including transfers to and from third parties
 24/7 settlement: move funds at any hour through Anchorage Digital’s Atlas settlement network
 Integrated digital asset services: secure digital asset custody and trading on the same platform 
 Built for institutions operating across borders 
The expansion is designed for companies whose operations do not stop at borders: 
 Global businesses moving money across markets, including digital asset exchanges and global trading businesses managing high-volume USD flows.
 Digital asset companies that need fiat rails to match their onchain operations, including protocols and neobanks.
 Financial institutions building digital asset strategies, including banks, asset managers, family offices, and corporates.
 One partner for cash and digital assets 
The expansion continues Anchorage Digital's work of consolidating institutional finance onto a single regulated platform. Rather than holding USD with one provider, digital assets with another, and settling across a third, clients can bring those workflows together, reducing fragmentation, counterparty exposure, and operational overhead.
That is the standard Anchorage Digital is building toward globally: fully integrated payments, custody, trading, and settlement.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened (2026-10-07).** Anchorage Digital partnered with Standard Chartered to offer institutional USD accounts and SWIFT wire access (including third-party transfers) to eligible **non-US** institutional clients through **Anchorage Digital Singapore Pte. Ltd.** (MAS-licensed). Clients get named virtual accounts, international USD wires, and 24/7 USD settlement via Anchorage's Atlas network with counterparties that bank at Standard Chartered Singapore — on the same platform they already use for digital-asset custody and trading.

**Why it matters.** The core pitch is closing the fiat↔crypto "divide": digital-asset businesses run 24/7 while correspondent banking runs on cutoff times and multi-day settlement, and crypto-native firms have historically faced limited access to institutional USD accounts (de-banking / banking-access risk). StanChart supplies the regulated USD banking, SWIFT and liquidity rails; Anchorage supplies the regulated crypto platform. For Anchorage this extends APAC reach beyond its US bank, consolidating payments + custody + trading + settlement onto one regulated platform.

**Positioning read (skeptical).** This is an incremental distribution/rails deal, not a new charter or balance-sheet event. Anchorage is effectively re-selling StanChart USD banking to non-US clients via its Singapore entity — valuable for stickiness and APAC expansion, but the underlying banking infrastructure is StanChart's. Note the FDIC/SDIC disclaimer: Singapore custody assets are not under the SG Deposit Insurance scheme. Timing is notable: announced the same week The Information reported Anchorage cut ~17% of staff amid a crypto downturn, so the "global expansion" framing runs alongside cost-cutting.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits at the intersection of two subverticals: institutional crypto custody and crypto-adjacent banking/fiat rails. Custody market sizing varies wildly by definition — per The Business Research Company (via web, as of 2026) the "crypto custody provider" market is ~$3.52bn in 2026 (from ~$2.1bn in 2025); Grand View / sector reports put the broader institutional digital-asset custody market at ~$3.3bn in 2025 rising to ~$15.8bn by 2034 (~14.5% CAGR) — treat all as illustrative, no single verifiable TAM. Structure: the custody layer is consolidating around ~5 scaled players (Coinbase Custody, BitGo, Fireblocks, Anchorage, Copper), with incumbent banks (BNY, Fidelity, State Street) entering — high barriers from regulation (charters/licences), security infra, and institutional trust. The relevant "why now": the deal is NOT about custody itself but about bolting regulated fiat rails (USD accounts, SWIFT, 24/7 settlement) onto custody — closing the fiat/crypto operational divide for non-US institutions. Driver = post-GENIUS-Act regulatory clarity pulling banks toward "banking the digital ecosystem" (SC's own framing); second-order effect = custodians racing to become one-stop fiat+crypto platforms rather than pure vaults.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: assets under custody (AUC), number/quality of institutional clients, regulatory licence footprint; for the banking layer, settlement speed and fiat-currency coverage. Players & basis of competition — Coinbase Custody (~$300bn AUC, ~84% of US spot-BTC-ETF assets per web, Apr 2026 — competes on scale/ETF rails); BitGo (>$90bn secured on platform per web); Fireblocks (~$90bn on platform, competes on orchestration/MPC tech + M&A — bought Dynamic for $90m, [[Fireblocks acquires wallet platform Dynamic for $90 million]]); Anchorage competes on regulation (only federally chartered US crypto bank) + the fiat-rails convergence play. Recent peer moves: Coinbase itself partnered Standard Chartered for multi-currency fiat rails, 2026-06 ([[Coinbase partners with Standard Chartered for multi-currency fiat rails]]) — i.e. SC is wiring the same capability to Anchorage's largest rival; Binance added Anchorage's Atlas as a Triparty banking partner, 2026-07 ([[Binance expands Triparty Network with Anchorage Atlas integration]]). Protagonist position: ahead on regulatory moat (federal charter + MAS licence + BitLicense), catching-up/niche on raw AUC scale vs Coinbase. Moat = intangibles (charter/licences) + switching costs of a single integrated custody+fiat account; `(analysis)`. Proprietary KPIs (exact AUC, client count, Singapore flows) → `[UNSOURCED]`, Anchorage discloses only "tens of billions" in custody.

**Comps & multiples.** Anchorage is private — no public trading multiples. Last valuation marks (web): Series D $350m led by KKR at >$3bn; Feb-2026 $100m Tether strategic investment + employee tender at $4.2bn; self-reported ~$85m revenue at last round → implied ~49x revenue ($4.2bn / $85m = ~49.4x) — rich even for crypto infra, but `(analysis)` on a single stale self-reported revenue figure, not a verified trading comp. Internal comps (private/round marks, NOT market caps): [[Standard Chartered takes stake in GSR at $1 billion valuation]] (crypto liquidity, 2026-05, $1bn round mark); [[Crypto custody firm Copper explores $500 million sale]] (custody consolidation, 2026-05, $500m ask). Distribution not computed — only ~1 Anchorage mark is a revenue-bearing figure; qualitative comparison only. No EV/EBITDA, P/E, or clean forward multiples → no data.

**Risk flags.** (1) Competitive disintermediation by its own banking partner — Standard Chartered is simultaneously the fiat-rail provider to Coinbase (2026-06), is building its own digital-asset custody in Singapore (2026-10, [[Standard Chartered plans digital asset custody in Singapore]]), and takes direct crypto equity stakes (GSR) — SC captures the regulated-bank layer across the whole sector, so Anchorage's edge is non-exclusive and the bank could favour rivals or compete directly. (2) Concentration/dependency on someone else's rails — the 24/7 USD settlement works only with counterparties that also bank Standard Chartered Singapore, narrowing the network effect and making the offering hostage to one bank relationship. (3) Valuation-vs-fundamentals — a ~$4.2bn mark on ~$85m self-reported revenue leaves little room for execution slips if crypto-cycle flows or stablecoin momentum (USDtb/USAT) stall.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is custodians land-grabbing the fiat-settlement layer: the thesis is that the moat is migrating from "who holds the keys" to "who owns the regulated fiat+crypto account," and 2026 is a consolidation/one-stop-platform year (Copper sale, Fireblocks M&A, banks entering). Falsifiable thesis: Anchorage out-grows pure custodians by bundling banking — watch for a published Singapore client/flow metric or an exclusive deepening with SC. What breaks it: SC extends identical rails to Coinbase/others at scale (already underway) or launches its own Singapore custody competitively, collapsing Anchorage's differentiation to a thin reseller margin.
