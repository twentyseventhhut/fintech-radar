StraitsX Partners Visa to Expand its Reach to over 100 Markets Globally on Stablecoin-Backed Card Programs
 SINGAPORE , 15 October 2026 – StraitsX , the stablecoin-native settlement layer powering global finance, today announced that it is partnering with Visa, the world’s leader in digital payments to expand its card issuing capabilities globally to more than 100 markets. 
The milestone extends StraitsX’s card issuing infrastructure beyond its established footprint in Asia, enabling the company to support eligible card issuance programs globally and is subject to applicable regulatory, compliance and Visa requirements. This expanded capability is designed for fintechs, Digital Payment Token (DPT) service providers, wallets, and other businesses looking to build card programs that connect stablecoin balances and digital asset payment flows with Visa’s global acceptance network.
 From rapid growth in Asia to global card issuing 
The expansion follows a period of significant growth for StraitsX’s card issuing business in Asia. StraitsX reached an annualised card GTV of more than US$6 billion across its stablecoin-backed card programs.
With the partnership with Visa, StraitsX can now extend the infrastructure and experience behind this growth to businesses building card programs across global markets.
“Stablecoin-backed cards have become one of the clearest examples of how stablecoins can move beyond settlement and into everyday payments,” said Tianwei Liu, CEO and Co-Founder of StraitsX . “We have seen significant growth in this use case across Asia. Expanding our issuing capabilities globally allows us to bring the same infrastructure to businesses looking to build and scale card programs across markets.”
 Connecting stablecoins with global card acceptance 
Stablecoin-backed cards provide a practical bridge between digital value and existing payment networks, allowing businesses to extend the utility of stablecoins without requiring merchants or consumers to adopt an entirely new payment experience.
StraitsX enables fintechs, DPT service providers and wallets to build card programs where stablecoins can support the underlying funding and settlement flow, while cardholders continue to transact through familiar Visa credentials and its global acceptance network.
For businesses operating across multiple markets, this brings together stablecoin-native infrastructure and global card issuing capabilities through a single infrastructure partner.
This collaboration will also take into account the continuously evolving nature of money-laundering and terrorism financing typologies and ensure that such risks arising from this integration are properly mitigated.
“At Visa, we are focused on bringing the benefits of new payment technologies into the global financial system in a way that is secure, scalable and trusted,” said Rubail Birwadker, Global Head of Growth Products & Partnerships, Visa. Working closely with our partner, StraitsX, we are unlocking more ways to use stablecoins for real-world payment flows, while connecting them to the reach and reliability of Visa’s global network.”
 Building global payment infrastructure on stablecoin rails 
The expansion of StraitsX’s card issuing capabilities is part of a broader strategy to connect stablecoins with the financial and payment infrastructure businesses already use today.
Beyond card issuing, StraitsX provides stablecoin-native infrastructure for payments and settlement, enabling businesses to move and use digital value across markets. As new forms of commerce emerge, including payments initiated by AI agents, StraitsX is building the infrastructure to connect these new payment flows with existing financial networks.
By expanding its Visa card issuing capabilities globally, StraitsX is extending this infrastructure to businesses looking to launch and scale stablecoin-backed card programs across markets.
 About StraitsX 
StraitsX is the stablecoin-native settlement layer driving global finance, and a Major Payment Institution licensed by the Monetary Authority of Singapore. As the issuer of XUSD and XSGD stablecoins, StraitsX leverages the power of blockchain and stablecoin technology to drive payments interoperability while offering innovative tools for liquidity management, stablecoin integration, and cross-border transactions. Partnering with leading financial institutions, global liquidity providers, and payment technology partners, StraitsX delivers cutting-edge payment solutions in a highly regulated and reliable environment, bridging the gap between traditional finance and the digital economy.

# Context-enrichment: StraitsX and Visa take stablecoin card programs to 100+ markets
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- **Announced 15 Oct 2026 (Singapore).** StraitsX — a MAS-licensed Major Payment Institution and issuer of the XSGD (SGD) and XUSD (USD) stablecoins — said it is partnering with Visa to extend its **card-issuing infrastructure** "to over 100 markets," beyond its existing Asia footprint. The one concrete number: StraitsX's stablecoin-backed card programs reached an **annualised card GTV of >US$6bn**.
- **Capability/eligibility, not a live 100-market rollout.** Read the PR verbs precisely: StraitsX "can now extend," is "designed for," supports "eligible card issuance programs" that are "subject to applicable regulatory, compliance and Visa requirements." There is **no launch date, no live-market count, no named client** in the new geographies. The product is the *ability to issue* in markets where Visa's rails + local rules permit — the actual live count today is almost certainly still Asia-centric. (de-PR: "100+ markets" = addressable reach, not deployed.)
- **The customer is not the consumer — it's the platform.** StraitsX issues infrastructure for *fintechs, DPT (Digital Payment Token) providers and wallets* to launch their own card programs. StraitsX funds/settles the card in stablecoins (XSGD/XUSD) behind the scenes; the end-user swipes a normal Visa credential. So this is a **B2B2C issuer-processor play**, not a consumer card.
- **Why framed this way:** anchoring to a flattering, already-earned Asia number (US$6bn GTV) and a big addressable geography ("100+") lets StraitsX sell *momentum* while the heavy lifting (per-market licensing, BIN sponsorship, AML) remains future/conditional. The PR even pre-empts the obvious objection by flagging evolving ML/TF typologies — a tell that the compliance surface is the real bottleneck, not technology.

## [1] Competitors / peers
The stablecoin-card-issuing layer is suddenly crowded and the "100+ countries with Visa" headline is not original:
- **Bridge (Stripe-owned) + Visa** — announced essentially the *same* "stablecoin-linked cards to 100+ countries" expansion in **2026**, scaling from ~18 live markets (LatAm-first: Argentina, Colombia, Mexico, Peru, Chile, Ecuador) toward EMEA/APAC/Africa by year-end; settles via Visa's on-chain pilot (Lead Bank). StraitsX is explicitly walking the **same Visa playbook, from the opposite (Asia) corner**.
- **Rain** — ~US$1.95bn valuation (Series C, Jan 2026); **Visa *principal* member** (issues directly, no sponsor bank) and added **Mastercard** credit/prepaid in May 2026. Structurally ahead of StraitsX on issuing autonomy.
- **Reap** (Payward/Kraken; Visa principal issuer), **Immersve** (Mastercard principal), **Baanx** (self-custody wallet cards) — the active issuing platforms behind most programs.
- **Mastercard side:** acquired **BVNK** (up to US$1.8bn, closed Aug 2026) to own this stack directly.
- **Visa itself:** stablecoin *settlement* run-rate ~US$7bn (Apr 2026) and four stablecoins across four chains — see [[Visa expands stablecoin settlement network to $7bn run rate]], [[Visa adds four stablecoins across four blockchains]].
- **Position:** StraitsX is **catching up / at parity on the headline, but behind on issuing structure** (Bridge and Rain already have principal/network-direct status; StraitsX's PR does not claim principal membership). Its genuine edge is **regional**: Asia depth, SGD-native XSGD, MAS license.
- **Why the land lies this way (analysis):** Visa is deliberately signing *multiple* non-exclusive issuer partners (Bridge, StraitsX, Rain) — commoditising the issuer layer while keeping the acceptance network and settlement rails. The partners compete on geography and compliance coverage; Visa captures the durable toll.

## [2] Company history / fit
- StraitsX (Xfers lineage; MAS Major Payment Institution) has built methodically: institutional settlement infra and Dedicated Virtual Accounts (mid-2025, [[StraitsX unveils institutional stablecoin settlement infrastructure]]); **US$10bn cumulative volume** rebrand (2025); Coinbase XSGD launch ([[Coinbase and StraitsX launch Singapore dollar stablecoin XSGD]]); US$10M from UQPAY for Asia expansion ([[StraitsX raises $10M from UQPAY for Asia stablecoin expansion]]); Thailand–Singapore QR/cross-border with KBank/Grab ([[KBank partners with StraitsX and Grab on Thailand-Singapore payments]]); multichain issuance (Monad [[StraitsX launches XSGD and XUSD stablecoins on Monad]], Solana planned early 2026). Combined XSGD+XUSD on-chain volume cited at **~US$18bn** cumulatively; XSGD >70% SE-Asia exchange share (web, May 2026).
- **Fit:** logical. StraitsX's thesis is "stablecoin-native settlement layer." Cards are the consumer-facing *off-ramp* that turns settlement balances into everyday spend — the clearest monetisable use case. Going global on issuing is the natural next rung after owning the Asia settlement + QR + multichain base.
- **Why act now (analysis):** stablecoin issuance/float economics are commoditising and regulated (MAS caps on reserves/yield). To avoid being a low-margin issuer-of-record, StraitsX needs *volume-generating distribution* — card GTV it can settle in its own XSGD/XUSD. Visa is the fastest way to buy global acceptance without building 100 bank relationships. The US$6bn Asia GTV is the proof point it's leveraging into a global pitch.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low on the mechanism, real on the regional angle.** Stablecoin→Visa card issuing to "100+ markets" is a 2026 template (Bridge/Visa did it first with an actual live base). What's distinctive here is an **Asia-rooted, SGD-native, MAS-licensed issuer** extending outward — i.e. a non-US, non-LatAm entrant with a real regional franchise.
- **Traction: one hard number, and it's backward-looking.** US$6bn *annualised* card GTV is genuinely substantial and is *existing* (mostly Asia) traction — not vapor. BUT it is pre-expansion. The new 100-market capability has **zero disclosed live clients/markets/volume**. So: real business, announcement-stage expansion.
- **Who captures the margin (analysis):** in a stablecoin card, value accrues to (a) Visa (interchange/network + settlement), (b) the card program owner / interchange holder, and (c) the stablecoin issuer (float + settlement). StraitsX's leverage is being **both the issuing infra AND the stablecoin issuer** — it can capture settlement + float on XSGD/XUSD flows, not just a processing fee. That vertical integration is the real value-add. The risk: if it is *not* a Visa principal member (not claimed), it depends on sponsor BINs and local acquirers, thinning its take and slowing per-market launches — exactly where Rain/Bridge are ahead.
- **What breaks it:** commoditisation of the issuer layer (Visa courting many partners) + the binding constraint being 100 separate regulatory/AML regimes, not tech. "100 markets" could take years to become even 20 live.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Next:** expect staged, market-by-market activation (likely APAC/Middle East first where StraitsX has relationships), named wallet/fintech clients as the real proof, and continued multichain issuance (Solana early-2026) to widen the funding base. Watch whether StraitsX pursues Visa principal membership.
- **Sentiment:** strongly positive macro backdrop — 2026 is the year stablecoin cards went mainstream (Stripe/Bridge, Mastercard/BVNK US$1.8bn, Rain US$1.95bn, Visa US$7bn settlement run-rate). Networks have concluded stablecoins are *additive* (they settle them), not a threat.
- **Why the market goes this way (analysis):** stablecoins are migrating from settlement rail to **spend rail**; the card is the bridge that needs zero merchant/consumer behaviour change. Second-order, counterintuitive effect: because Visa signs *every* issuer and keeps acceptance+settlement, a flurry of "100-market" partner announcements makes the **issuers more fungible and fragile**, not stronger — the headline that sounds like a moat is actually evidence the moat belongs to Visa. StraitsX's durable defensibility is therefore its *regional/licensing* position and its float-capture as a native issuer, not the Visa tie-up itself.
- **Risks:** regulatory/AML drag per market (PR-flagged); reliance on sponsor banks if not principal; XSGD/XUSD depeg or reserve-yield rule changes; being out-distributed by better-capitalised Bridge/Rain.

**Thesis-flags (for the StraitsX-Visa item).**
1. Cross-border +12–13% cc is the money line → it is Visa's highest-yield flow and precisely the global, multi-market usage a StraitsX stablecoin-backed card drives → more StraitsX card GTV across 100+ markets routes incremental high-margin cross-border volume onto Visa → reinforces why Visa partners on stablecoin issuance rather than resisting it.
2. $7bn stablecoin settlement run-rate vs $4tn/quarter payments volume → stablecoins are ~0.04% of flow today → second-order: for Visa, stablecoin-card deals like StraitsX are TAM-expansion optionality and a defensive hedge (keep the acceptance network in the loop as funding moves on-chain), not near-term revenue.
3. "No revenue clarity" on stablecoins/agentic → management touts the stack but is silent on monetization → the StraitsX "$6bn annualized card GTV / 100+ markets" headline is credible traction for StraitsX, but for Visa it is still strategic positioning, not a reported earnings contributor.
4. Opex +17–19% and incentives +18% outpacing revenue +14% → margin/operating-leverage watch → if new-flows investment (stablecoin, agentic) keeps opex running hot without clear monetization, the quality of the beat erodes even as top-line accelerates.
