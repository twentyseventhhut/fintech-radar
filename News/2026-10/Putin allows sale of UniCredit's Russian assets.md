Президент разрешил проводить реорганизацию акционерного общества «ЮниКредит Банка» и совершать сделки по покупке 100% его долей. Дополнительных разрешений на продажу российских активов не требуется, отмечается в документе. Об этом пишет " Коммерсант ".
«ЮниКредит Банк» входит в список кредитных организаций, для сделок с акциями и долями которых необходимо специальное разрешение президента России. В Кремле говорили, что рассмотрят вопрос о продаже российских активов группы, «когда поступит соответствующее обращение».
Продажа российских активов UniCredit задумана не как передача всего банка одному покупателю: российский «ЮниКредит Банк» предполагается разделить на два юридических лица. Несмотря на отмену указом необходимости в дополнительном президентском согласовании, для продажи российских активов западной компании по-прежнему требуется разрешение Центробанка России. При этом под контролем UniCredit должен сохраниться бизнес трансграничных переводов в долларах и евро для корпоративных клиентов, не находящихся под санкциями.

# Context-enrichment: Putin allows sale of UniCredit's Russian assets
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**Freshness verdict: FRESH.** This is the **Putin decree of 2026-10-05** (reported 2026-10-06) that authorizes UniCredit to **reorganize AO UniCredit Bank and sell 100% of the "Remaining Bank"** — a real "talks → authorized" follow-up to the **May-2026 non-binding agreement** with a UAE investor. It is a DISTINCT event from the sibling note [[Bank of Russia keeps Raiffeisen, UniCredit systemically important]] (that is the 2026-10-07 CBR annual SIB-list refresh — a "nothing changed" regulatory notice). This note is the sale-authorization decree itself. No prior corpus note covers the decree; related UniCredit/foreign-exit notes are context, not duplicates.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On **2026-10-05** Putin signed an order permitting the **reorganization of AO UniCredit Bank** and transactions with 100% of its shares, **without the need for further presidential sign-off** on the sale itself. AO UniCredit Bank sits on the list of credit institutions whose share deals require a special presidential decision; this decree is that decision.

- **The structure is the story (de-PR'd):** The Russian bank is split into TWO legal entities, NOT sold whole to one buyer:
  - a **"New Bank"** that **UniCredit keeps 100%** — carved out to run **cross-border euro/USD payments for non-sanctioned corporate clients**;
  - a **"Remaining Bank"** (the shrunken lending/retail business) sold **100% to the UAE investor**.
- **What is still NOT done:** The decree removes the **Russian-side presidential veto**, but closing still requires (a) a **binding agreement** (the May deal is non-binding), (b) **physical asset separation** in Russia, and (c) **Bank of Russia (CBR) approval** of the sale to a Western-owned seller. Close is **targeted H1-2027** — a target, not a signing. The UAE buyer remains **unnamed** ("well-established private UAE investor").
- **The money (verified):** UniCredit guided a **cumulative €3.0–3.3bn earnings hit** from the deal (Bloomberg, 2026-05-07), of which **€1.6–1.8bn is a non-cash FX-reserve recycling** through P&L — i.e. the true incremental cash/economic loss is smaller than the headline. UniCredit says the hit **does not change shareholder distributions or 2028–2030 profit goals**.

**Why structured exactly this way / what it reveals:** The two-entity split is a deliberate value-and-control device, not a formality. (1) **Keep the crown jewel:** the euro/USD cross-border payments rail for non-sanctioned corporates is the one piece that is strategically valuable and relatively sanctions-robust — UniCredit retains it rather than hand it to a stranger. (2) **Minimize the discount:** Russia's exit regime for "unfriendly" sellers imposes a **mandatory discount (raised to ~60%) plus an exit levy (~35%)** on market value. By selling only the **"Remaining Bank"** (the already-gutted lending shell) rather than the whole franchise, UniCredit shrinks the base on which that punitive discount bites and eases cash extraction (the mergers.ru source states explicitly the construction "may let the seller reduce the discount and ease getting the funds"). (3) **Who still holds the door:** removing the presidential veto is necessary but not sufficient — the **CBR retains the final sale approval**, so Moscow keeps a lever over the pace and terms. (analysis)

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Citigroup — the exit precedent (done):** Citi **completed** the sale of its last Russian unit AO Citibank to **Renaissance Capital**, closing **Jan-2026 at a ~$1.2bn loss** ([[Citigroup to sell remaining Russian operations at $1.2bn loss]], [[Citi board approves sale of AO Citibank to Renaissance Capital]]). Read-across: Western exits **crystallize large losses and sell to local/friendly-jurisdiction buyers** — exactly UniCredit's pattern (UAE investor, €3.0–3.3bn hit).
- **Societe Generale:** exited outright in 2022 (Rosbank sold; now absorbed into T-Bank) — the early, clean full exit.
- **Raiffeisen (RBI) — the true laggard:** NO confirmed sale; pursuing ECB-mandated attritional run-off of the loan book (~60–65% cut target). Its Russian unit was still ~**38% of RBI group profit in Q3-2025** — the lucrativeness is exactly why it has NOT sold.
- **Position of this deal:** UniCredit is **ahead of Raiffeisen** (it has a named structure, a buyer, and now a decree) but **behind Citi/SocGen** (which already closed). It is the first of the two remaining Western SIBs to get a Kremlin green light. **Why the laggards lagged:** trapped Russian profits were extraordinarily high-margin (NIMs multiples of group), so "stay and earn" beat "crystallize a loss" until ECB pressure + a workable split structure tipped the calculus. (analysis)

## [2] Company history / fit
- **ECB pressure trajectory:** ECB ordered UniCredit to scale down its Russian exposure (Apr-2024); UniCredit litigated, **lost a Nov-2024 suspension bid**, then **withdrew its EU appeal in Jan-2026**, pivoting to an orderly solvent wind-down.
- **May-2026:** signed the **non-binding agreement** to sell most of the Russian business to the UAE investor, keeping only payments — [[Финтехно UniCredit Russia sale requires asset-split first]] and client-side throttling already underway ([[UniCredit Russia tightens card, deposit limits amid pullback]]).
- **Oct-2026:** this decree authorizes the reorganization.
- **Fit — logical and consistent:** UniCredit's broader strategy is **capital-return + selective M&A in Europe** (Banco BPM clearance [[UniCredit gets EU clearance for Banco BPM acquisition]], Commerzbank stake to 37.7% [[UniCredit lifts Commerzbank stake to 37.7% in takeover bid]], Alpha Bank, Aion/Spain). Russia is a **trapped-capital drag** that complicates ECB sign-off on those ambitions. Shedding the Russian lending book while keeping the payments rail removes the regulatory overhang **without** forfeiting the one asset that still has strategic optionality. (analysis)

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty of THIS item: moderate-to-real.** Unlike the sibling SIB-list "nothing changed" note, this is a genuine **procedural unlock** — the Kremlin veto is lifted and the transaction can now proceed to binding stage. That is a real state-change, not a reprint.
- **But traction ≠ done:** no binding agreement, no asset separation, no CBR approval, no named buyer, no disclosed price. The decree is **permission, not completion**. Calling it "UniCredit sold its Russian bank" would overstate it.
- **Where the value/margin sits:** The economically interesting piece is what UniCredit **keeps**, not what it sells. The cross-border euro/USD payments franchise for non-sanctioned corporates is a scarce, defensible rail (few Western banks can still legally offer it); the "Remaining Bank" being sold is a **run-off lending shell** (UniCredit local loans already down ~90%). So the real value-add of the whole maneuver is **de-risking the parent's ECB relationship while retaining a niche payments monopoly-ish position** — the margin is captured by UniCredit holding the New Bank, and by Moscow via the discount/levy on the sold piece. (analysis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Path to close:** binding agreement → asset separation → **CBR approval** → H1-2027 target close. Any of these can slip; Russian-exit deals routinely stall on the discount/levy and regulatory choreography.
- **Sentiment:** Markets read the decree as **mildly positive de-risking** for UniCredit (removes an overhang; loss largely pre-guided and partly non-cash; distributions/2028–2030 targets reaffirmed). Not a re-rating event.
- **Regulatory backdrop:** The same decree regime that **banned** "unfriendly" owners from freely transacting bank shares is what made this slow; Putin's case-by-case authorization is the only door. The CBR simultaneously keeps UniCredit on its **SIB list** (sibling note) — Moscow preserves supervisory visibility over the payments rail it wants retained.
- **Second-order / the central question:** The frame is NOT "a Western bank is leaving Russia." It is **"who captures what in a controlled, discounted exit"** — UniCredit keeps the strategic payments rail and offloads a gutted lending shell; Moscow extracts a ~60% discount + ~35% levy and keeps a supervisory lever; the UAE buyer gets a cheap run-off book with optionality on a thaw. The decree is the moment the Kremlin **priced and permitted** that split, not the moment the exit happened. (analysis)

The decree is the moment that split was priced and permitted. (analysis)

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Western-bank exit from Russia (banking, cross-border M&A under a dual-regulator veto regime). Structure: the exit is gated on BOTH sides — the ECB pushes EU parents to shrink/leave, while Russia requires presidential + CBR clearance for any sale of a "strategic" credit institution's shares. This decree (2026-10-05/06) removes only the Russian-side presidential veto for UniCredit's reorganization; CBR approval and binding documentation are still pending, close targeted H1-2027 (per RTE/Euronext, 2026-10-06). The deal structure is a two-way split: a "New Bank" (kept 100% by UniCredit, confined to international EUR/USD payments for non-sanctioned corporates) and a "Remaining Bank" sold 100% to the buyer — a design that both satisfies Moscow's interest in keeping payment rails and lets the seller narrow the asset actually sold to cut the discount (per note text; corroborated by UniCredit PR, May-2026). "Why now": UniCredit withdrew its EU appeal against the ECB scale-down order in Jan-2026, pivoting from litigation to an orderly solvent exit; the May-2026 non-binding UAE deal needed the Kremlin's door opened to progress. Barrier to exit is regulatory capture on both ends, not price discovery. (analysis)

**Competitive landscape.** KPIs here are prudential/structural, not commercial: local loan-book run-off %, share of balance sheet parked at the CBR, trapped (non-repatriable) profit, and the P&L hit on crystallisation. Basis of "competition" = speed and cleanliness of exit, not market share. Protagonist UniCredit: local lending down ~90% (note channel text; magnitude not independently web-confirmed → [UNSOURCED] on the exact figure), now a shrinking deposit/payments franchise; the Oct decree is a genuine "talks → authorised" step. Peers by exit status (dated): Citigroup — fully exited, AO Citibank sold to Renaissance Capital (now AO RenCap Bank), closed Q4-2025 at a **$1.2bn pretax loss** ([[Citigroup to sell remaining Russian operations at $1.2bn loss]], [[Citi board approves sale of AO Citibank to Renaissance Capital]]); Societe Generale — exited outright 2022. Raiffeisen (RBI) — the direct laggard-peer: Russia unit's 9M-2025 operating result was **€1.558bn, ~half of RBI's total**, on a NIM of **10.05% vs 2.99% group** (per marketscreener/oninvest, 2025-26), loan book cut ~60% under ECB order (target ~65%), but **no confirmed sale** and profits trapped by Russian capital controls. Position: on commercial metrics neither UniCredit nor RBI "competes" — both are managed run-offs. On *exit progress* UniCredit is now ahead of RBI (authorised sale vs attritional run-off) but behind Citi/SocGen (already out). Moat: none commercially; the only residual stickiness is the EUR/USD payments plumbing both Moscow and the parents want preserved — exactly the piece UniCredit keeps. (analysis)

**Comps & multiples.** No clean valuation metric attaches to this decree — price, buyer identity and the sold unit's book value are all undisclosed, so EV/Rev, EV/EBITDA and P/E are **"no data"** (the sold entity is an unlisted local sub; a sale price, not a market cap, would govern). "Distribution not computed, qualitative comparison." The relevant comps are exit-loss precedents, which are consistently negative:
- [[Citigroup to sell remaining Russian operations at $1.2bn loss]] (2026-01) — **$1.2bn pretax loss** on sale of AO Citibank; direct read-across that foreign-bank exits crystallise losses, not gains.
- [[Citi board approves sale of AO Citibank to Renaissance Capital]] (2026-01) — buyer was a local/friendly-jurisdiction house (Renaissance Capital); pattern mirrored by UniCredit → UAE investor (reportedly representing Alfa Group, per Kommersant via Euronews, 2026-10 — treat attribution as reported, not confirmed).
- [[UniCredit Russia tightens card, deposit limits amid pullback]] (2026-07) — same-entity prior step; retail run-off was already underway.
UniCredit's own guided hit: **cumulative P&L impact ~€3.0–3.3bn**, of which **~€1.6–1.8bn is a non-cash FX-reserve recycle** (no capital impact), and the deal is expected to **improve CET1 by ~35bps** (per Bloomberg 2026-05-07 / UniCredit PR). So the "€3bn hit" overstates the economic/capital loss — roughly half is accounting FX recycling. Arithmetic on a multiple is not computable absent a disclosed price → next hard datapoint is the sale price at binding signing.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Undisclosed price + forced-seller discount.** The decree clears the Russian veto but neither price nor the "Remaining Bank" book value is public; precedent (Citi −$1.2bn, UniCredit's own −€3.0–3.3bn guidance) shows these exits realise steep losses. Second-order: the split-bank structure is explicitly designed to "lower the discount" (note text) — a tell that a large discount is otherwise expected.
2. **Execution/timeline slip.** H1-2027 close still needs binding documentation, the spin-off's actual implementation, and CBR clearance; the carve-out + licensing of a standalone payments bank is an extra step that can slip. Second-order: as long as it is open, UniCredit keeps carrying a Russian balance sheet (and, per the related CBR note, SIB-buffer obligations) it wants off the books.
3. **Buyer/governance opacity.** Buyer is an unnamed UAE private investor reportedly tied to Alfa Group; a Kremlin-friendly counterparty raises the chance the "exit" leaves the parent's former franchise in politically connected hands and invites EU/sanctions scrutiny of the structure. Second-order: reputational/sanctions tail-risk on an otherwise capital-accretive deal.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Not a re-rating — it is a procedural unlock that moves UniCredit from "stuck" to "cleared to transact," ahead of RBI which still has no sale. The falsifiable thesis: the Western-bank Russia exit is paced by regulators, not markets, so the decree's value is optionality (a path to close), not realised value — which only lands at binding signing with a disclosed price. Triggers to watch: (a) binding agreement + first disclosed price/loss figure (the next Citi-style loss-crystallisation datapoint); (b) CBR sign-off and whether the carved-out New payments bank is itself kept on/added to the SIB list; (c) any read-across pressure on RBI to follow. Thesis is wrong if a binding deal closes at or above book with no incremental loss — which the Citi precedent and UniCredit's own guidance argue against. (analysis)
