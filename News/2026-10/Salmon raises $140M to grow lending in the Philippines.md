News and updates
Salmon secures $140 million (₱8.8 billion) to double down on the growth opportunity in the Philippines
 Manila, Philippines, October 19, 2026 – Salmon Group, a technology-driven banking and lending platform in the Philippines, today announces that it secured additional $140 million of financing to continue its growth in the Philippines.  
 Proceeds from the financing round will fund the scaling of profitable lending (Salmon Credit Line and in-store lending) and nationwide roll-out of new products, including motorcycle and MSME lending. 
 In less than four years of operations, Salmon has become one of the fastest-growing technology companies in the Philippines, operating through a BSP-licensed bank, Salmon Bank (Rural Bank) and an SEC-licensed financing company, Salmon Finance. 
 Today, the companies in Salmon Group serve millions of Filipinos through its Salmon app – one of the highest rated financial services apps in the Philippines with 9 million downloads, 4.8 rating on App Store and Google Play, 99.9% uptime, and dedicated 24/7 customer support. Through the AI-powered credit engine of Salmon Finance, the group's financing arm, Salmon is able to serve customers with limited credit history and provide credit decisions within minutes. 
 "Everyday financial services come with unnecessary friction for many Filipinos. We’re removing these barriers and building innovative products with the reliability and security customers expect in today’s world," said Pavel Fedorov, co-founder of Salmon . "We are doubling down on the opportunity in the Philippines as we believe deeply in its scale and in the importance of focus to deliver a differentiated execution. This financing lets us do more: extend banking and credit access to more Filipinos, roll out new products, and keep raising the bar for the financial services industry in the country. We are grateful to our existing investors for backing the next stage of our mission." 
 
 ABOUT SALMON   
 Salmon Group is a technology-driven financial services holding company building a banking and lending platform across Southeast Asia, starting with the Philippines. The Group operates a BSP-licensed bank, Salmon Bank (Rural Bank) Inc.; an SEC-licensed financing company, Salmon Finance Inc.; and a growing suite of financial products – all delivered through the Salmon app, a modern money app built for everyday Filipinos. Founded in July 2022 by banking and technology veterans Pavel Fedorov, George Chesakov, and Raffy Montemayor, Salmon is backed by leading US venture investors, IFC (World Bank Group), ADQ/Lunate, Antler as well as prominent Filipino investors. The Salmon app is rated 4.8 in both the App Store and Google Play, one of the highest ratings for a financial services app in the Philippines.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- Salmon Group secured $140M (₱8.8bn), announced 2026-10-19. Structure (NOT in salmon.ph PR, surfaced via DealStreetAsia/e27): **$90M equity + $50M bond**. Equity commitments from Spice Expeditions, Washington University Investment Management (WashU), Moore Strategic Ventures "and other existing backers." (per DealStreetAsia / e27, 2026-10-19).
- **Use of funds:** scale the two lines Salmon calls *profitable* — Salmon Credit Line + in-store (POS) lending — and roll out NEW products: **motorcycle loans and MSME lending**. This is a de-PR'd pivot toward *secured* lending (e27 framing: "from cash loans to motorbikes").
- **+ Why structured this way / what it reveals:** The PR deliberately says only "additional financing" from "existing investors" — **no lead, no valuation, no equity/debt split disclosed**. That silence is the tell. Mixing $90M equity with $50M public bond lets Salmon raise sizeable capital without printing a (possibly flat/down) equity mark — a pattern it has used repeatedly (Nordic bonds in 2025). The shift to *secured* collateral (motorcycles) + MSME is a credit-quality / unit-economics move: secured books carry lower loss-given-default, which both de-risks the loan tape and makes the bond/debt side easier to fund.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Philippine digital-credit / neobank cohort (dates where available):
- **Home Credit** — incumbent in POS / pay-later consumer credit; Salmon's most direct in-store rival.
- **Skyro** — closest analog (POS installments, product loans ≤₱100k, cash loans ≤₱250k, credit lines); launched SkyroCredit nationwide ([[Skyro launches SkyroCredit nationwide across the Philippines]], 2026-07).
- **Billease** — BNPL/consumer credit; 2025 revenue topped $151M ([[Billease profit jumps as 2025 revenue tops $151M]], 2026-07).
- **Tonik** — first licensed digital-only bank; reached profitability Q1 2026 (per e27/web).
- **GoTyme / Maya / UnionDigital / UNOBank** — digital banks competing on deposits + credit; GoTyme took a $13.9M injection ([[GoTyme receives fresh $13.9 million capital injection]], 2026-07).
- **Mastercard + FinVolution (Luvit Card)** — adjacent digital-credit entrant ([[Mastercard and FinVolution launch Luvit Card for digital credit]], 2026-04).
- **Position:** Salmon is ahead of most on *balance-sheet* scale (9M app downloads, dual BSP+SEC licenses, a profitable lending core) but is NOT the profitability pioneer (Tonik got there first as a standalone digibank). On motorcycle finance it is a *new entrant* against established bank/captive-finance players.
- **+ Why the landscape is this way:** The Philippine market is still land-grab for the underbanked long tail; capital + a banking license is the moat. Salmon's edge is being one of the few holding BOTH a rural-bank license (cheap deposit funding) AND an SEC financing license (higher-yield lending) — vertical control of cost-of-funds-to-loan-yield spread.

- **+ Why it acts this way:** Lending is capital-hungry — every peso lent must be funded. Salmon raises roughly twice a year in escalating ticket sizes to feed loan-book growth. The repeated equity-plus-bond blend keeps the equity cap table from being marked too often while the debt scales the tape. Founded Jul-2022 by Fedorov/Chesakov/Montemayor (ex-banking/tech).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Real traction (numbers, not PR):** 9M app downloads, 4.8 store rating, 99.9% uptime, AI credit engine with sub-minute decisions, dual regulated licenses, and a *profitable* core lending book (company claim; corroborated directionally by the +83% H1-2025 bank net income).
- **What's genuinely new here:** the **secured motorcycle + MSME lending** expansion, not the capital itself. Motorcycle finance = collateralized, lower-loss book; MSME = larger tickets, longer duration.
- **+ Why value-add is real/not, deeper:** Salmon captures margin because it owns both the deposit license (funding) and the lending license (yield) — it is the one that *gets paid*, not a referral layer. The risk: moving into motorcycle/MSME means NEW underwriting models and repossession/collateral ops it hasn't proven at scale; the AI engine was trained on unsecured consumer behaviour, not asset-backed vehicle loans. Traction on the NEW lines = zero so far (announced, not live-at-scale).

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Plans:** nationwide rollout of motorcycle + MSME lending; continued scaling of Credit Line + in-store.
- **Market backdrop (sourced):** MSME loans were ₱574.8bn at end-Mar-2026 — just 4.73% of the ₱12.1tn base vs a 10% statutory allocation (per Kilde/KenResearch via web) — a large structural gap fintechs target. Philippine fintech-lending market growing (multiple market reports, exact TAM "no data"/paywalled).
- **+ Why / second-order:** No disclosed valuation + "existing investors only" equity signals the external equity market is cautious on Philippine consumer-credit marks after the 2025 APAC fintech-funding decline ([[KPMG Asia-Pacific fintech funding falls to a decade low]]). Counterintuitive effect: raising via bonds + inside-round equity makes Salmon look well-funded, but heavy reliance on debt funding raises refinancing/rate risk if asset quality turns. Regulatory tailwind: thrift-bank license ambition could cut cost-of-funds further.
