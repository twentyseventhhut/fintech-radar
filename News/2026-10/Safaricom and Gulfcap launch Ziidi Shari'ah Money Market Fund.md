## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] TL;DR.** On 2026-10-06 (Nairobi), Safaricom and GulfCap Investment Bank launched the **Ziidi Shari'ah Money Market Fund**, a Shari'ah-compliant (interest-free, riba-free) MMF distributed to M-PESA's ~41M customers via My OneApp / `*334#`. Minimum entry KES 100, top-ups from KES 10. GulfCap manages the fund with oversight from its Shari'ah Supervisory Board; KCB Bank Kenya is cited as trustee. It is a standalone Islamic-finance sibling to Safaricom's existing conventional Ziidi MMF. CMA approval for the Shari'ah fund dates to Feb 2025. (Source: TechMoran, TechAfrica, The Standard, Business Watch, CMA.)

**[1] Why it matters.** Extends Safaricom's mobile-first wealth push into Islamic finance, tapping an under-served Muslim-Kenyan and ethical-investing segment. With this license Kenya had 7 Shari'ah-compliant unit trusts (Feb 2025 count). The conventional Ziidi MMF has grown explosively — AUM reportedly ~KES 15.1bn by HY2026 (up ~1,061% y/y from KES 1.3bn), ~1.15M active customers (from 130k), net yield ~6.13% (Mar 2026) — so a Shari'ah variant aims to replicate that distribution-led land-grab in a new compliance niche. Capital-preservation, daily-liquidity structure held only in Shari'ah-compliant banks.

**[2] Key players.**
- [[Safaricom clarifies automatic M-PESA health levy deductions]] — Safaricom/M-PESA is the distribution rail (also [[M-PESA reaches 40 million active customers in Kenya]]).
- [[This Week in Fintech The case for M-PESA becoming a bank]] — strategic context for M-PESA's push into banking/wealth products.
- [[SumUp partners Upvest to launch in-app money market investing]] / [[N26 launches Flexible Cash Fund with Fidelity International]] — comparable fintech-embedded MMF distribution plays (DE/EU).
- [[Egypt's FRA licenses GRANITE money market fund]] — adjacent African MMF licensing/regulatory precedent.
- GulfCap Investment Bank (fund manager + Shari'ah Supervisory Board); KCB Bank Kenya (trustee, per Oct 2026 reporting); CMA (regulator, approved Feb 2025).

**[3] Numbers.** Min invest KES 100; top-up KES 10; CMA approval Feb 2025; launch 2026-10-06; reach ~41M M-PESA customers; 7 Shari'ah unit trusts in Kenya (Feb 2025). Context (conventional Ziidi MMF, NOT this fund): HY2026 AUM ~KES 15.1bn, ~1.15M users, ~6.13% net yield (Mar 2026).

**[4] Unknowns / to watch.** No disclosed AUM/target/yield for the Shari'ah fund itself (all AUM figures above are the conventional Ziidi MMF). Management-structure discrepancy in sources: Oct 2026 launch reporting says GulfCap manages with KCB as trustee, whereas older/alternate CMA-filing coverage cited Genghis Capital (manager), Co-operative Bank (trustee), KCB (custodian) — the exact service-provider line-up needs confirmation. Shari'ah MMFs structurally cannot hold interest-bearing instruments, so yield sourcing (sukuk, Shari'ah deposits) and whether returns match conventional MMFs is open. Watch adoption vs the conventional fund, fee structure, and Shari'ah board composition.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Kenya's collective-investment-scheme (CIS) market reached KES 851.7bn AUM in March 2026, +13% q/q, of which money-market funds (MMFs) held KES 442.2bn — though MMF share has slipped to ~51.9% as fixed-income/special funds grow (per CMA data, via mmfskenya.com, Mar-2026). The market is fragmented across ~49 MMFs but concentration is high at the top: Sanlam (~KES 72–161bn across its scheme) and CIC (~KES 81.8bn) lead. Barriers are regulatory (CMA licensing, trustee requirement — here KCB Bank) plus distribution — which is exactly where Safaricom's network effect dominates. "Why now": (1) M-PESA's April-2026 consolidation into the "My OneApp" super-app (80+ mini-apps, Fuliza, M-Shwari, KCB, Ziidi) turns distribution into near-zero-marginal-cost cross-sell; (2) an underserved Islamic-finance TAM — a Shari'ah-compliant MMF (no interest-bearing instruments; profit-sharing structures, GulfCap Shari'ah Supervisory Board) targets Kenya's Muslim population previously excluded from conventional MMFs.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: AUM, net yield, minimum ticket, customer count, cost-to-acquire (distribution). Key players: incumbents CIC, Sanlam, Standard Investment Bank; the dedicated Islamic peer is Etica (~KES 9.1bn AUM, 2.9% share). Basis of competition shifts from yield to **distribution + ticket size**: Ziidi entry is KES 100 (~$0.77) with KES 10 top-ups via *334# / My OneApp. Recent moves: the parent Ziidi (conventional) MMF scaled from KES 1.3bn (Sep-2024) → KES 15.1bn (Sep-2025) → KES 18.2bn (Mar-2026, +18.6% q/q), reportedly capturing ~half of Kenya's unit-trust investors by count, ~1.15m customers (Sep-2025) (per Business Daily / mmfskenya.com). The Shari'ah sub-fund is tiny but fast: KES 68m → 114m (+67% q/q, Mar-2026) — this Oct-2026 relaunch/expansion opens it to all 41m+ M-PESA customers. Protagonist position: **ahead on distribution, niche on product**. Moat = network effects + distribution scale (41m users, M-PESA = 45.6% of Safaricom service revenue, KES 182.7bn FY26, +13.4%); product/yield differentiation is thin `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** This is a product launch, not a financing — no round/valuation disclosed, so no deal multiple. **No data** on valuation. AUM comparison (arithmetic): Ziidi Shari'ah KES 114m = **~0.026% of the KES 442.2bn MMF pool** and ~1.3% of Etica's KES 9.1bn — i.e. negligible today; the bet is on conversion of the 41m M-PESA base. Conventional Ziidi's KES 18.2bn ÷ ~1.15m customers ≈ **KES 15.8k average balance (~$122)** — confirms a micro-ticket, mass-retail model. Internal comps (grep; semsearch DOWN — OpenRouter 402): [[SumUp partners Upvest to launch in-app money market investing]] (same in-app-MMF-distribution playbook, EU), [[M-PESA reaches 40 million active customers in Kenya]] (the distribution base). Distribution not computed as a formal multiple set — qualitative.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Yield/net-return opacity** — Shari'ah MMFs avoid interest-bearing instruments; PR is silent on the actual profit rate vs conventional MMFs or on management fees. If the halal-structured return materially trails CIC/Sanlam yields, the product competes on faith-alignment alone, capping AUM at the Islamic-finance niche. Second-order: AUM stays sub-scale (today ~KES 114m).
2. **Distribution/platform concentration** — the fund is only as durable as M-PESA access; any regulatory or super-app-economics shift (recall the KES 1bn stake-sale inquiry, M-PESA health-levy deductions) hits acquisition directly. Safaricom controls the rails, GulfCap only manages money — thin fee capture for the asset manager.
3. **Shari'ah-governance/execution** — compliance rests on GulfCap's Shari'ah board; any lapse is reputational in a trust-sensitive segment. Execution risk that the 41m→AUM conversion stalls as it did for the sub-fund (KES 114m after launch).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Signals super-app monetization moving from payments/lending (Fuliza KES 1.47tn disbursed) into asset-gathering — Safaricom is becoming a distribution layer that disintermediates asset managers, who are reduced to white-label manufacturers. Falsifiable thesis: if Ziidi Shari'ah clears ~KES 1bn AUM within ~4 quarters, it validates faith-based micro-investing at platform scale and pressures Etica; trigger = next CMA quarterly CIS report. What breaks it: flat AUM growth would show the Islamic-MMF TAM is distribution-independent and small.
