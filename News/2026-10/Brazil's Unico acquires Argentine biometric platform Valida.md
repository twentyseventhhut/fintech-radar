Gracias a frenar más de **13 millones de ataques fraudulentos** en 23 sectores económicos que usan verificación de identidad, la firma **Unico****bloqueó u$s$3.500 millones** en intentos de fraude en América Latina durante el **primer semestre de 2026**, un**23%** más que en el mismo período del año pasado.

La plataforma combina **biometría facial**, **modelos de IA personalizados** y **protocolos de seguridad multicapa** para verificar la identidad en tiempo real.

Su tecnología de **prueba de vida (liveness)** confirma la presencia real del usuario en apenas 2 segundos, con una**tasa de falsa aceptación (FAR) de cero**.

La compañía somete sus sistemas a **pruebas de estrés** continuas y **simula nuevos vectores****de ataque** antes de que se generalicen.

Además, combina **efectos de red** con **metadatos** para identificar y bloquear a **defraudadores en serie**.

## Unico llega ahora a la Argentina con la compra de Valida

Unico concluyó la adquisición de **Valida**, plataforma argentina de **verificación biométrica** con el **50%** del mercado bancario del país.

**José Rolandi**, cofundador de Valida, será gerente general de Unico en la Argentina, y Valida pasará gradualmente a operar bajo la marca Unico.

La operación marca su**entrada oficial en la Argentina** y es su **cuarta compra internacional** desde 2024, tras **Trully.AI** (México), **Oz Forensics** (Emiratos Árabes Unidos) y**OwnID** (Israel).

Unico planea ampliar el alcance de Valida a **salud, seguros y telecomunicaciones**.

Los clientes bancarios de Valida accederán a la tecnología de **liveness** de Unico, y el equipo local seguirá liderando la operación durante la **transición**.

Unico busca aumentar cerca de **60%** el volumen de transacciones de la operación argentina en un año.

## Qué es el fraude como servicio

**Fernando Paulin**, Gerente General de Unico en México, advirtió que el **cibercrimen** se profesionalizó y opera bajo un modelo de **Fraude como Servicio (FaaS)**.

Las redes criminales utilizan herramientas automatizadas e **inteligencia artificial** (IA) para lanzar oleadas masivas de ataques.

Los sectores de alta liquidez, como los **servicios financieros**, absorben los mayores volúmenes.

Según Paulin, la **verificación de identidad** pasó a ser un pilar estratégico para la **continuidad empresarial** en la economía digital.

Un informe de **LexisNexis Risk Solutions** indica que las **identidades falsas** representan el 48% de los casos de fraude en la región, la mayor proporción del mundo.

Unico opera en más de **20 países** y **23 industrias**, con cobertura del 96% de la población económicamente activa de **Brasil**.

## Auge del fraude sofisticado y los deepfakes

Tras un aumento de más de 1.000% a fines de 2025, el **fraude sofisticado** subió otro **500% interanual** en el primer semestre de 2026.

Su volumen en ese semestre ya superó el total de todo 2025: se **multiplicó por 24** en la comparación.

Combina **deepfakes** con técnicas que explotan vulnerabilidades de los motores de prueba de vida, como la **profundidad de imagen**, la textura y el movimiento natural.

Los atacantes recurren con frecuencia a la **inyección de software** para simular transmisiones de video en vivo.

**Fernanda Beato**, General Manager de Unico para Brasil, sostuvo que ninguna empresa puede combatir esta amenaza sin**socios tecnológicos**.

A mediados de 2025, el fraude sofisticado era cerca de **61%** de las amenazas y en el primer semestre de 2026 fue poco más de **14%**.

## Cómo fue el estallido de los deepfakes caseros

Los expertos precisaron que en 2026 surgió un nuevo vector: los**deepfakes caseros** o DIY, de bajo costo.

Los ejecutan actores sin conocimientos técnicos, que usan **aplicaciones de consumo** para atacar a conocidos y familiares.

Estos videos **no tienen la sofisticación para vulnerar motores robustos** de **detección de prueba de vida** y son bloqueados rápidamente.

Aun así, impulsaron un aumento de **400%** en el **fraude simple** frente al primer semestre de 2025.

Ese volumen explica la caída del peso relativo del fraude sofisticado, que no refleja una baja de las **amenazas avanzadas**.

Beato describió un nuevo tipo de defraudador: **personas comunes** que usan apps de deepfakes para suplantar a familiares y aprobar registros o transacciones.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
### [0] What happened
Unico — the São Paulo-based digital identity / fraud-prevention unicorn (fka Acesso Digital) — closed the acquisition of **Valida**, the Argentine biometric identity-verification platform. The deal was signed 27 Aug 2026, announced early Sept (Bloomberg 10 Sept), and closed in October 2026. Terms were **undisclosed**. Valida co-founder **José Rolandi** becomes Unico country GM for Argentina; the Valida brand will migrate to Unico over a transition. It is Unico's **4th international acquisition since 2024** (after Trully.AI — Mexico; Oz Forensics — UAE; OwnID — Israel/US) and its official entry into Argentina, where Unico plans a Buenos Aires office in Jan 2027 as a regional talent hub.

### [1] Who is Unico
Founded 2007 (Brazil), Unico became a unicorn in 2021 and was later valued at **~$2.6B** across rounds led by SoftBank, General Atlantic and Goldman Sachs (Series D $100M in 2022; prior $346M round). It runs facial biometrics + liveness + custom AI for real-time identity verification, covering ~96% of Brazil's economically active population and operating across 20+ countries and 23 industries. Internal history: [[Unico launches Trust Network anti-fraud platform in Mexico]] (collaborative anti-fraud network, Nov 2025); [[Unico names ex-Brazil digital government chief Monteiro as LATAM CEO]] (May 2026 — Luis Felipe Monteiro, ex-architect of Gov.br, hired to lead Brazil/Mexico/Argentina).

### [2] Who is Valida
Buenos Aires, founded ~2015. Biometric onboarding/reauthentication for banks: pairs FaceTec facial-liveness biometrics with a DNI (national ID) scan and a bureau check. Claims **~50% of Argentina's banking market** (reportedly incl. Galicia, Nación, Provincia, Macro) and 25M+ validations run. The 50% claim is a market-access thesis — Unico is buying distribution/customer base as much as tech.

### [3] Market & why it matters
LatAm is the global epicenter of identity fraud: **synthetic/fake identities = ~48% of regional fraud cases** (highest worldwide, per LexisNexis Risk Solutions), and deepfake/GenAI synthetic-identity attacks surged 350%+ YoY in 2026, with DIY "homemade" deepfakes driving a 400% jump in simple fraud. Unico reports it blocked ~$3.5B of fraud attempts in LatAm in H1 2026 (+23% YoY) across 13M+ blocked attacks. The deal is consolidation play #2 in LatAm biometric ID in 2026 after [[Experian's Serasa acquires Brazilian identity startup IDwall for ~$70 million]] (Experian/Serasa buying IDwall, ~$70M / R$400M) — signaling a land-grab in regional identity infrastructure.

### [4] Competitive read / what to watch
- Unico now owns the Argentine banking channel + stack (liveness from Unico pushed to Valida's bank clients); plans to extend Valida into **health, insurance, telco** and grow Argentine transaction volume ~60% in a year.
- Rivals/adjacent: IDwall (now Experian/Serasa), global IDV players (Persona, Proof/Veriff, Incode, Sumsub, FaceTec as a Valida supplier), plus DuckDuckGoose-style deepfake-detection vendors deployed at LatAm identity hubs.
- Watch: Argentine macro/FX risk on a USD-undisclosed deal; integration/brand migration execution; whether Unico's pending Asia/Nigeria ambitions dilute LatAm focus.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** LatAm digital-identity / KYC / anti-fraud verification. Market size estimates diverge sharply by scope: Latin America digital-identity solutions put at ~$1.6–4.1bn for 2026 with ~14–20% CAGR to 2030 (per MarketsandMarkets / Coherent / Fortune Business Insights, via secondary citations, as of 2026) — treat the absolute TAM as soft given the spread; the direction (double-digit growth) is the sourced takeaway. Structure: fragmented at the edges (dozens of national KYC/biometrics vendors) but consolidating fast around a few platform players selling unified identity + fraud across geographies. Barriers: regulatory (local data residency, banking-grade liveness), network effects (shared-fraud intelligence), and bank distribution — Valida sits behind ~50% of Argentine banks, i.e. an installed-base moat Unico is buying rather than building. Why now: (i) Brazil's open-finance mandate pushes digital ID authentication (per Fortune Business Insights); (ii) "Fraud-as-a-Service" + deepfakes — the note's primary source cites sophisticated fraud +500% YoY in H1-2026 and false identities = 48% of regional fraud cases (LexisNexis Risk Solutions, via iProUP) — makes liveness/biometrics a bank continuity spend, not a nice-to-have.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: verification transaction volume, false-acceptance rate (FAR) / liveness accuracy, bank/enterprise coverage, international revenue mix. Players: incumbents (Trulioo, Jumio, Socure, Veriff, Experian, Persona, LexisNexis) vs LatAm-native platforms; basis of competition = accuracy + bank distribution + breadth of unified suite, not price. Recent moves: Unico — Trully.AI (Mexico) + Oz Forensics (UAE) Sep-2024, OwnID passkeys (US entry) Sep-2025, now Valida (Argentina) signed 27-Aug-2026, closing ~Oct-2026 — fourth cross-border buy in ~2yr; Unico reports 84% transaction growth and claims 96% coverage of Brazil's economically active population. Peer activity in-base: [[Experian acquires KYC360 to boost fraud and financial crime solutions in the UK]], [[Themis acquires fraud platform Pasabi, expands social monitoring]], [[Trulioo posts 134% jump in US verification volume]], [[Resistant AI raises $25M Series B against financial crime]]. Position: Unico is the clear LatAm consolidator — ahead regionally via roll-up + bank penetration; moat = shared-fraud-network effects + switching costs of bank integrations `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Unico is private; last marked ~$2.6bn valuation (Series D, Goldman Sachs/General Atlantic/SoftBank, 2022) — a round valuation, not market cap, and ~4yr stale `[UNSOURCED]` for a current mark. Valida deal price undisclosed by both parties → **EV/Revenue, P/S = no data**; no transaction multiple computable. Internal comps are stage-/model-mixed, so distribution not computed, qualitative only: Resistant AI (private, $25M Series B — early-stage, not comparable on scale), Trulioo (private, scale leader, no public multiple), Experian/KYC360 (strategic bolt-on by a listed data group, price undisclosed). Public anchors: Experian trades on a data-services multiple; none map cleanly to a LatAm biometrics roll-up. Verdict: no reliable comp set — valuation claims rest on a stale private round, flagged `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Integration / roll-up execution risk** — fourth acquisition in ~2yr across four jurisdictions (BR/MX/UAE/IL/US now + AR); undisclosed prices mean no visibility on whether deals are value-accretive, and brand migration (Valida → Unico) plus rebuild of a local team mid-transition can leak the 50% bank share Unico just paid for.
2. **Geographic concentration in a fragile macro** — Argentina (FX controls, inflation, peso volatility shown in the source's own crypto-dollar ticker) is the growth bet; a single-country shock or regulatory shift hits the thesis, and revenue is likely peso-denominated against a USD-marked franchise.
3. **Deepfake/liveness arms race = perpetual R&D tax** — the "zero FAR / 2-second liveness" claims are vendor PR (announced, not independently audited); DIY/home deepfakes drove simple fraud +400% and attack vectors mutate, so the moat requires continuous spend or it erodes — classic disintermediation risk if an incumbent (Socure/Jumio) out-invests on model accuracy.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is consolidation, not a re-rating: Unico is assembling the de-facto pan-LatAm identity rail by buying installed bank bases faster than competitors can win them organically — the scarce asset is bank distribution, not tech. Falsifiable thesis: if Unico sustains >60% Argentine transaction-volume growth in-year (its stated target) and holds Valida's ~50% bank share through rebrand, the roll-up is working; trigger to watch — a disclosed deal price or a down-round refinancing (would signal the $2.6bn mark is unsupported) or a major bank defecting to Jumio/Socure post-migration (would falsify the moat).
