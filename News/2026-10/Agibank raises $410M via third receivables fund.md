Agibank Concludes Third FIDC Issuance, Totaling R$ 2.1 Billion 



 The transaction supports Agi’s funding diversification strategy and expands credit origination capacity 



Agibank (the “Bank”), a bank that operates a hybrid platform combining the efficiency and scalability of digital with the proximity and service of a physical presence, announces the closing of the issuance of Class A shares ( quotas ) of the Fundo de Investimentos em Direitos Creditórios Agibank III Responsabilidade Limitada (“FIDC Agibank III”), totaling R$ 2.1 billion. Agibank is a subsidiary of Agi Inc. (NYSE: AGBK) (“Agi”).



The issuance, carried out in two series, features a 132-month maturity and an interest rate equal to the CDI rate + 1.05% per annum. The fund, structured as a Brazilian limited liability closed-end investment fund, is backed by credit rights originating from INSS (Brazilian National Social Security Institute) payroll-deductible loans. The structure is administered by Oliveira Trust DTVM, with management by Oliveira Trust Servicer S.A. and co-management by Agibank Asset Management LTDA. The offering was conducted for public distribution under the automatic registration procedure and private placement in Brazil.



“The completion of this issuance confirms our ability to access different funding sources efficiently and on a recurring basis. The consistency in executing these operations enhances our financial flexibility and strengthens our expansion in a segment where we possess deep expertise, payroll-deductible loans,” highlights Marcello Dubeux, Chief Financial Officer and Investor Relations Officer at Agi.



The transaction, aimed at professional investors, reflects the market’s confidence in the quality of the company’s assets and the resilience of its business model. The FIDC Agibank III quotas were assigned an ‘AAA.br’ rating by Moody’s Local.



“This transaction is a fundamental strategic step in our liability management. Ensuring diversified, long-term funding sources is essential to guarantee predictability and sustainable growth capacity, while maintaining our non-negotiable focus on credit origination with discipline and profitability,” states Glauber Correa, CEO of Agibank.



 About Agi 



Agi stands for a banking experience that welcomes and empowers all Brazilians through a business model that is unique in Brazil. Designed to serve a customer base that represents the majority of the Brazilian population, our model addresses needs that remain outside the priorities of traditional large banks and purely digital banks. We fill a gap in the market by serving, with quality and dignity, customers who are often overlooked.



Our hybrid model combines the best of both worlds: a fully digital bank that is light, fast, and easy to use, complemented by physical branches that offer a welcoming, agile, and accessible in-person experience for all Brazilians. We develop tailored solutions and provide a simple, inclusive customer journey for non-digital-native clients, creating a meaningful competitive advantage. This approach enables us to attract more customers, build long-lasting relationships, and strengthen our growth trajectory.

# Context-enrichment: Agibank raises $410M via third receivables fund
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Agibank (parent Agi Inc., NYSE: AGBK) closed **FIDC Agibank III**, a R$2.1bn (~US$403–410M) receivables fund, PR-dated 2026-10-01. De-PR'd specifics (all confirmed): Class A (senior) quotas only, two series, 132-month maturity, coupon CDI + 1.05% p.a., collateral = INSS payroll-deductible (consignado) loan receivables to retirees/pensioners, rated **AAA.br by Moody's Local**, admin Oliveira Trust DTVM / manager Oliveira Trust Servicer / co-manager Agibank Asset Management, sold to professional investors.
- This is **funding, not equity** — it monetizes an existing loan book, it is not a valuation event. The PR frames it as "funding diversification" and "expanding credit origination capacity," which is accurate: a bank selling receivables to a fund gets term liquidity cheaper than deposits and off-balance-sheet relief to keep originating.
- **Why structured this way → what it reveals:** CDI+1.05% on AAA.br seniors is a *tight* spread, implying real demand. But note "AAA.br" is a **national-scale** Moody's Local rating, NOT globally comparable — the PR leans on the rating to signal quality to local institutional buyers. The structure keeps Agibank Asset as co-manager and (per the standard FIDC pattern) the originator retains the subordinated first-loss tranche — related-party involvement that the PR does not quantify. The real tell is *timing*: this is the third such fund in ~16 months while the equity story is broken (see [3]/[4]) — Agibank needs the capital-markets funding channel precisely because deposits/equity are not doing the job.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Brazilian securitization is booming — 2025 FIDC issuance ~R$90.8bn (+9.5%), the largest structured-credit segment, projected to approach R$1tn in 2026 (Forbes Brasil, Diário do Comércio). Relevant peers:
- **Banco BMG** — closed R$1.5bn "Consig Premium II" FIDC in Aug 2026, same INSS-consignado-card asset class — the closest direct comp; Agibank III is larger.
- **Banco Pan** — major consignado/vehicle lender (book ~R$55bn), securitizes via Brazilian Securities (CRIs); a direct scale incumbent.
- **Asaas** — [[Asaas raises $55-58M in largest FIDC to date]] — same *instrument* (FIDC, Oct 2026) but DIFFERENT asset (SMB card-receivable advances, ~R$300M), NOT consignado. Useful peer for the FIDC-as-funding trend, not a lending competitor.
- Broader Brazilian FIDC issuers in-corpus: CloudWalk ([[Brazil's CloudWalk raises $780 million via FIDC for receivables]], [[CloudWalk raises over 1B via FIDC issuance in Brazil]]), SumUp ([[SumUp raises R$850 million FIDC for receivables financing]]), iCred ([[iCred raises R$1.15bn in FIDCs, hires Vinci Compass]]) — shows FIDC is now the default Brazilian fintech funding rail.
- Private-payroll (CLT) lending entrants: Nubank, Mercado Pago (launched Aug 2026), PicPay, C6, Creditas — these crowd the broader payroll-loan space but target working CLT employees, not Agibank's INSS-beneficiary niche.
- **Why the landscape is this way / second-order:** Agibank's moat is the *hybrid* model (1,100+ physical hubs) reaching non-digital-native retirees — a segment pure neobanks underserve. The R$2.1bn fund is bigger than BMG's R$1.5bn, signalling Agibank is the aggressive consolidator in INSS consignado securitization. But the asset's quality now depends on INSS-origination integrity (see [4]) — a regulatory, not competitive, variable.

## [2] Company history / fit
- 1999 founded (Campinas). Hybrid digital + physical bank specialized in secured consignado lending to INSS beneficiaries. CEO Glauber Correa; CFO/IRO Marcello Dubeux.
- **FIDC trajectory:** [[Agibank raises $350M via first FIDC issuance]] (FIDC I, mid-2025, ~R$2.0bn), then FIDC II (R$2.5bn, 2025), now FIDC III (R$2.1bn) — cumulative ~R$6.6bn across three funds since 2025. Also [[Agibank launches asset management subsidiary in Brazil]] (Mar 2026) — which is exactly the Agibank Asset co-manager now plugged into FIDC III: vertical integration of its own funding machine.
- **Equity history:** [[Agibank's US IPO plan hits hurdle over payroll loan suspension]] (Jan 2026, INSS suspended new consignado registrations) → [[Agibank goes public on NYSE raising $240 million]] (Feb 2026: priced at $12, bottom of a range SLASHED ~65% from the original $15–18 / 43.6M-share, ~$3.3bn-valuation ambition; ~$1.9bn implied valuation).
- **Why it acts this way:** A monoline secured lender with a thin-spread asset (consignado) must grow origination to grow earnings, and origination needs funding. With equity de-rated (stock ~$6.76 on 2026-10-09, ~-44% from IPO) and deposits insufficient, recurring FIDC issuance is the structurally forced funding path. Building its own asset manager (Mar 2026) captures the fees and control of that pipeline in-house.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Not novel as an instrument** — this is Agibank's THIRD FIDC and a routine securitization; the honest read is "recurring funding execution," not innovation. Value-add = demonstrated repeat market access on a tight spread despite the 2025 INSS scandal overhang.
- **Traction (Q2 2026, de-PR'd but sell-side-sourced):** loan book R$37.1bn (+21% YoY, 88% secured), 7.6M active customers, INSS share 9.6%, NPL 90+ 3.3% (coverage 182%), Q2 net income R$200.3M, revenue R$3.2bn (+26%). These are real numbers, not announcements — the funding has fed actual book growth.
- **Why value-add is real BUT capped / who captures the margin:** The FIDC lets Agibank originate faster than deposits allow, and keeping Agibank Asset as co-manager captures the fund-management economics. The margin risk: consignado is a commodity, low-spread asset; the AAA.br seniors are cheap for investors because the *collateral* carries the risk, and Agibank retains the first-loss subordinated tranche — so Agibank keeps the credit risk while handing seniors the safety. The value-add breaks if INSS origination is curtailed (the fund is only as good as the receivables flow behind it).

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **The dominant overhang is regulatory, not market:** In Dec 2025 INSS suspended Agibank's new consignado registrations after a CGU audit flagged 30,000+ irregular contracts (incl. 1,192 signed after borrowers' deaths), within the broader 2025 INSS "descontos indevidos" scandal (Operation Sem Desconto, Apr 2025). The suspension already dented the IPO. **Whether that suspension is lifted as of Oct 2026 is open/unverified** — and it is the single biggest question, because the FIDC's whole premise (expanding origination) depends on INSS taps being open.
- **Market sentiment:** equity de-rated hard (~-44% from IPO); sell-side "buy" targets should be treated skeptically given the slashed IPO. The *debt/securitization* market, by contrast, is confident — AAA.br, tight spread, closed in two series.
- **Why / counterintuitive second-order:** The divergence (strong FIDC demand vs. weak equity) makes sense — FIDC investors are protected by subordination and are indifferent to equity upside; they only need the receivables to perform. But it means Agibank is increasingly funding a *contested* asset (INSS consignado under regulatory scrutiny) via instruments that push first-loss back onto Agibank's own balance sheet. Concentration in one regulator-dependent asset class is a fragility, not a strength: a further INSS clampdown would simultaneously choke origination AND stress the retained subordinated tranches.

15. **Is it incremental funding or balance-sheet neutral?** Expands credit-origination capacity per PR and fits the R$37.1bn book (+21%), so net-incremental to origination — but off-balance-sheet relief plus retained first-loss means the risk does not fully leave Agibank.

But it is a *recurring, non-novel* securitization (third fund), equity funding not raised, and value is capped by a commodity asset whose origination is under an unresolved INSS regulatory cloud. ## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Brazilian FIDCs (credit-rights investment funds) are the dominant non-real-estate securitization vehicle: the market grew from ~$15bn (2015) to an estimated ~$110bn (2025), a ~27% CAGR, and now account for roughly one-quarter of Brazil's structured-credit market (per Euromoney / Vinod Kothari, as of 2025). Structure: a fragmented, fast-institutionalizing funding layer sitting between fintech/bank originators and capital markets — barriers are low on the origination side but higher on asset quality and rating (capital + regulatory discipline). Driver / "why now": digital lenders need to diversify off balance-sheet and term out funding; INSS (federal pension) payroll-deductible receivables are among the lowest-risk pools available, which is exactly what backs FIDC Agibank III (R$2.1bn / ~$410M, CDI + 1.05% p.a., 132-month maturity, 'AAA.br' from Moody's Local). Second-order effect: recurring FIDC access converts origination capacity into a repeatable funding flywheel rather than a one-off capital raise.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for a payroll-lending hybrid bank: credit portfolio size, gross origination, net interest income (NII), ROE, and funding cost vs CDI. Agi Inc (NYSE: AGBK, parent of Agibank) Q2 2026 (ended 30-Jun-2026, reported 5-Aug-2026): total loan portfolio R$37.1bn (+21% YoY; 88% secured), gross credit origination >R$7bn in the quarter, active customers 7.6M (+36% YoY), NII R$1.3bn (+11% YoY), net income R$200.3M, ROE (LTM) 21.6% (per AGBK Q2 2026 release via Yahoo Finance). Basis of competition in Brazilian receivables funding is funding cost + asset quality + rating, not product. Agibank's position: a recurring, programmatic FIDC issuer — this is its THIRD issuance after FIDC Agibank I ($350M, Jun-2025), so it is ahead of most peers on repeatability. Moat `(analysis)`: scale in a specialized, low-default INSS payroll niche plus a hybrid digital+branch distribution reaching non-digital-native customers (switching costs / underwriting intangibles), which supports the 'AAA.br' tranche rating and the thin CDI + 1.05% spread.

**Comps & multiples.** Internal FIDC comps from the base (receivables-fund funding, Brazil):
- [[Agibank raises $350M via first FIDC issuance]] — same issuer, first FIDC, $350M (Jun-2025); III at ~$410M is a ~17% step-up (~$410M / $350M = 1.17x).
- [[Brazil's CloudWalk raises $780 million via FIDC for receivables]] — $780M (Nov-2025), ~1.9x Agibank III.
- [[SumUp raises R$850 million FIDC for receivables financing]] — R$850M (Nov-2025), smaller than III's R$2.1bn.
- [[iCred raises R$1.15bn in FIDCs, hires Vinci Compass]] — R$1.15bn (Dec-2025), ~0.55x III by size.
- [[Asaas raises $55-58M in largest FIDC to date]] — Asaas's own largest FIDC (Oct-2026), far smaller in absolute terms; illustrates the fragmentation of issue sizes.
Distribution: issue sizes R$850M–R$2.1bn among closest peers — FIDC Agibank III (R$2.1bn) is at the large end; no median computed on absolute size (not a valuation multiple).
Equity valuation: Agi Inc market cap ~$1.08bn at ~$6.75/share (per Simply Wall St / public.com, Oct-2026), vs IPO 11-Feb-2026 at $12.00 (now ~44% below issue; 52-wk high $12.21). Rough P/E `(analysis)`: annualizing Q2 net income R$200.3M × 4 ≈ R$801M; market cap ~$1.08bn ≈ R$5.9bn (at ~5.45 BRL/USD) → ~R$5.9bn / R$801M ≈ 7.4x trailing-run-rate P/E — cheap vs typical bank/fintech names, consistent with a sharp post-IPO de-rating. Cross-currency and annualized, so `(analysis)`, not a clean multiple; clean EV/EBITDA and forward consensus → `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Risk flags.**
- Concentration in INSS payroll-deductible receivables: a single government-program asset class means any change to INSS deduction rules, margin caps, or the pension framework hits both origination and the collateral backing every FIDC at once — correlated funding + credit risk.
- Funding-cost / rate sensitivity: tranches priced at CDI + 1.05% mean the spread is thin and the cost floats with Brazil's policy rate; a higher-for-longer SELIC/CDI squeezes NIM on CDI-funded, often fixed-rate consumer paper.
- Equity de-rating / market confidence: the stock trading ~44% below its Feb-2026 IPO price signals the public market is skeptical despite +26% revenue and 21.6% ROE — a soft cost-of-equity constraint on growth even as FIDC debt funding stays open.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is not a new entry but deepening institutionalization of Brazilian receivables funding: repeat, AAA-rated, pension-backed FIDCs are becoming a standing funding channel for specialized lenders, decoupling origination growth from deposit/equity capital. Falsifiable thesis: Agibank can keep terming out funding via FIDC regardless of its weak equity, so loan-book growth (next: FIDC IV or a larger III follow-on, and sustained >R$7bn/qtr origination) continues even with the stock depressed. What breaks it: an INSS regulatory shift or a CDI spike that widens required FIDC spreads and chokes issuance.
