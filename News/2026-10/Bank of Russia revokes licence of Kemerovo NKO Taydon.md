## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 22 October 2026 the Bank of Russia, by Order No. OD-1864, revoked the banking licence of OOO NKO "Taydon" (non-bank credit organization, reg. No. 2085-D, based in Kemerovo). A provisional administration was installed the same day (Order No. OD-1865), suspending the executive bodies' powers until a receiver/liquidator is appointed. By assets the entity ranked 355th in the Russian banking system — small and not systemically important. The stated grounds: Taydon failed to meet its obligatory payment obligations for more than 14 days, having accumulated very low-quality assets that could not generate the cash flow needed to keep operating, resulting in a full loss of solvency. ([finance.mail.ru](https://finance.mail.ru/article/cb-otozval-licenziyu-u-nebankovskoj-kreditnoj-organizacii-69233638/), [1prime.ru](https://1prime.ru/20261022/tsb-874499680.html), CBR English wire via [kurzy.cz](https://zpravy.kurzy.cz/879155-banking-licence-of-nco-taidon-llc-revoked-22-10-2026/)).

**[1] Why it's notable / what's genuinely new.** The revocation rationale is the unusual part. Most 2026 Russian licence pulls cite either repeated breach of CBR prudential norms or — most commonly — AML/CFT violations under Federal Law 115-FZ (suspicious transit, failure to report mandatory-control operations). Taydon is a rarer case: the licence was pulled directly for insolvency/inability to pay, not for laundering or norm breaches. That is a textbook "balance-sheet failure" of a small non-bank credit organization rather than an enforcement action.

**[2] Prior coverage in the vault (internal).** Semsearch index was down (OpenRouter 402), so these are grep-sourced. Directly comparable recent CBR revocations: the AML-driven collapse of [[Финтехно QIWI Bank collapse rooted in laundering-channel risk]] and the depository-licence pull in [[Russian central bank revokes ALOR+ depository licence]]. Adjacent regulatory-tightening items that frame CBR's current posture: [[Bank of Russia proposes new AML internal-control rules]] and [[Bank of Russia introduces quality rating system for e-money operators]]. The QIWI/ALOR cases were enforcement-driven (laundering channels, conduct), which sharpens the contrast with Taydon's solvency-only grounds.

**[3] Market context — the cleanup wave.** 2026 marks a return to active market "cleansing" after a 2022–2025 stretch dominated by mergers (M&A was ~70% of bank exits in 2025). The year's first forced revocation came in January; before Taydon, CBR had already pulled licences from several players including Bank RMP (10 April) and KB "NMB" (23 January). A closely analogous non-bank case: on 27 August 2026 CBR revoked RNKO "Tsifrovye Resheniya" (Digital Solutions, 288th by assets) — but that one was explicitly for repeated legal/normative breaches AND 115-FZ AML failures (prolonged suspicious client transit, late/false mandatory-control reporting), i.e. the opposite rationale to Taydon. The Russian banking system is down to roughly 305 institutions and is widely expected to fall below 300 as the regulator weeds out weak/undisciplined players. ([brobank.ru](https://brobank.ru/zakrytye-banki-2026/), [vedomosti.ru on Tsifrovye Resheniya](https://www.vedomosti.ru/finance/news/2026/08/27/1224039-tsb-otozval-litsenziyu), [investfuture.ru](https://investfuture.ru/articles/kolichestvo-bankov-v-rossii-sokratitsya-do-300-k-2026-godu-iz-za-otzyva-litsenziy-1175841474)).

**[4] What to watch.** Next step for Taydon is liquidation or bankruptcy, decided by the provisional administration / court. Whether retail/corporate clients had funds and how recoveries proceed (NKOs are settlement/payment entities, typically with limited or no deposit base — Taydon was not described as a DIA-insured deposit-taker). Watch whether CBR publishes further detail on the asset-quality problem, and whether this slots into a broader run of small-NKO failures as the sub-300 bank-count target approaches.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Subvertical: Russian non-bank credit organisations (НКО / NKO) — small, narrow-licence settlement/payment institutions that cannot take retail deposits or lend like full banks. The licence revocation is one event inside the Bank of Russia's decade-long sector "cleanup" that began under governor Nabiullina in 2013. Scale of that cleanup: the count of Russian credit institutions fell from 900+ in the early 2010s to ~305 banks by mid-2026 (per brobank.ru / sravni.ru citing CBR data, as of 1 Jul 2026); roughly half of all licences were revoked in Nabiullina's first six years, and at the 2019 peak ~40% of revocation grounds cited AML-law violations (per bne IntelliNews / Moscow Times, 2018–19). Pace has slowed sharply: 12 revocations in 2024, only ~6 in 2025 (three forced, three via M&A), and by YTD 2026 just two forced exits plus six absorptions (per brobank.ru). Structure: highly consolidated at the top (state banks dominate), with a long tail of sub-scale NKOs being steadily culled — "why now" is the late-stage, mop-up phase of that cleanup, where the surviving small players fail on economics rather than fraud.

**Competitive landscape.** NKO sector KPIs are capital adequacy, payment-settlement throughput and asset quality (NKOs are barred from the maturity-transformation lending that drives bank NIM). Taydon ranked 355th of ~305 banks + remaining NKOs by assets — i.e. bottom-tail, immaterial to settlement infrastructure. Basis of "competition" among NKOs is niche settlement/e-money utility, not scale; most retail payment volume has migrated to systemic banks and the SBP/digital-ruble rails the CBR itself runs. Protagonist position: niche, failing — the regulator explicitly called its assets "extremely low quality" with "complete loss of solvency" (CBR order OD-1864, 22.10.2026, via finance.mail.ru / 1prime.ru). Notably the stated ground was insolvency/non-payment >14 days, NOT an AML or prudential-norm breach — a less common exit route than the AML-driven revocations that defined the 2014–19 cleanup (analysis).
