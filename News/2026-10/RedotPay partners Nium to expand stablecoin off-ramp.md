Nium and RedotPay partner on stablecoin off-ramp payouts
Claudia Pincovski
08 Oct 2026 / 5 Min Read
 More than nine million RedotPay users will be able to convert stablecoins into traditional currencies through the payout infrastructure of Nium, a provider of cross-border money movement services, following a partnership between the two companies. RedotPay, a stablecoin-based payment fintech that links blockchain technology with conventional banking systems, gains access to Nium's coverage across 100 currencies and 190 countries. It plans to use this connectivity to extend its off-ramp services into new markets, as part of its focus on compliant adoption of digital asset payments. 
 Nium and RedotPay integration enables cross-border payments 
 Under the arrangement, Nium connects to stablecoin rails through partnerships with regulated blockchain infrastructure providers, while payouts are processed across its own licensed payment network. This structure separates blockchain connectivity, handled by third parties, from the fiat payout layer, which Nium operates directly. Funds can be disbursed to bank accounts, wallets, and cards, with more than 100 of its payout markets settling in real time. Co-headquartered in the US and Singapore, the company holds regulatory licences in over 40 countries and moved more than USD 84 billion across borders during the past year. 
 Nium is also a principal member of card schemes including Visa, Mastercard, Discover, and UATP, and issues more than 41 million card credentials annually. Its platform allows banks, fintechs, and enterprises to collect, convert, send, and spend money across currencies and blockchains. For RedotPay, whose app lets customers spend and transfer crypto holdings, the integration adds a further route for turning those balances into local currency. RedotPay states that it aims to widen financial access for unbanked people while also serving existing crypto users. 
 Stablecoins shift towards everyday spending 
 Nium noted that stablecoins are increasingly used for routine payments rather than only for trading, and that RedotPay's users often move funds between digital and conventional money. Its infrastructure is designed to support these transfers across the corridors and currencies those users need. For RedotPay, the off-ramp capability is intended to let users worldwide apply their digital assets to everyday expenses, as the company extends its services into new markets.

# Context-enrichment: RedotPay partners Nium to expand stablecoin off-ramp
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**Freshness: FRESH.** Announced 2026-10-08 at TOKEN2049 Singapore (Nium/PRNewswire wire; The Paypers, Fintech Singapore, technode). This is a NEW, distinct tie-up — RedotPay (demand side / consumer front-end) adopting Nium (supply side / payout rails) as an off-ramp provider. It is NOT a reprint of the adjacent priors: [[Nium and Circle connect USDC settlement with global payouts]] (May-2026, Nium joining Circle's CPN — the stablecoin-settlement INBOUND side) and [[RedotPay picks OpenPayd for stablecoin payment infrastructure]] (Jun-2026, a different fiat-BaaS/IBAN supplier). Here RedotPay specifically wires Nium's last-mile payout network as an off-ramp. New counterparty + new capability framing → fresh, but it is RedotPay's Nth rails-supplier announcement of a familiar type (Circle, Ripple, OpenPayd, Tempo all preceded it).

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- **Who:** RedotPay (Hong Kong-based stablecoin card/wallet fintech, now >9M registered users across 100+ countries, ~$12B annualized payment volume, unicorn, Visa card at 130M+ merchants) adopted **Nium** (Singapore/US cross-border payments infrastructure) as a **stablecoin-to-fiat off-ramp** provider. Announced 2026-10-08 at TOKEN2049 Singapore.
- **What RedotPay gets:** access to Nium's payout footprint — **100 currencies, 190 countries**, 100+ markets settling in real time, disbursing to bank accounts, wallets and cards. RedotPay says it will use this to extend off-ramp services into NEW markets.
- **Mechanism (the key de-PR'd detail):** Nium **does not touch blockchain directly** — it "connects to stablecoin rails through partnerships with regulated blockchain infrastructure providers" (third parties, unnamed), while **payouts are processed across Nium's own licensed payment network**. So the deal explicitly **separates the crypto-connectivity layer (outsourced) from the fiat payout layer (Nium-operated)**.
- **Nium scale cited:** moved >USD 84B cross-border in the past year; licensed in 40+ countries; principal member of Visa/Mastercard/Discover/UATP; issues 41M+ card credentials/yr.
- **What is NOT disclosed:** no contract value, no volume commitment, no exclusivity, no go-live date, no named target markets, no FX/fee economics, no named "regulated blockchain infrastructure providers" that supply the stablecoin leg. Wording ("will be able to," "plans to extend") is a **signed partnership, not demonstrated production traffic** → treat as announced, not proven-live-at-scale.
- **Why structured this way:** RedotPay's own edge is the consumer card/wallet + a crypto-native user base; the hard, jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction part is **licensed fiat last-mile payout** across many corridors. Rather than build/license that everywhere, it rents Nium's network. Nium, mirror-image, keeps itself out of direct blockchain custody/handling (regulatory and risk reasons) by outsourcing the on-chain leg and monetizing only the fiat payout rails it is licensed for. The PR anchors to "9 million users" and "190 countries" to imply scale the deal itself does not guarantee flows for.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Rails/off-ramp supply side (who else RedotPay could have used, and does):** Circle (already a RedotPay partner — [[RedotPay adds crypto-to-MXN payouts via Circle in Mexico]], [[RedotPay and Circle enable crypto-to-fiat transfers to Brazil]]); Ripple ([[RedotPay partners with Ripple for naira payouts]]); OpenPayd ([[RedotPay picks OpenPayd for stablecoin payment infrastructure]]); Tempo ([[RedotPay integrates Tempo for agentic stablecoin payments]]). So RedotPay runs a **multi-rail supplier strategy** — Nium is one more corridor source, not a sole-source dependency. Other payout/off-ramp networks: Thunes, dLocal, Paysend/Nodu ([[Paysend and Nodu enable stablecoin payouts to banks and wallets]]), MoneyGram (Kraken deal), Western Union.
- **RedotPay's own category peers:** Bybit Card, KAST, Bleap, Wirex, Crypto.com — crypto-card/wallet players. RedotPay is a scale leader (9M users).
- **Position:** For RedotPay — parity/breadth-extension, not a leap; a rational widening of its payout corridor map. For Nium — a notable DEMAND-side logo win: having spent 2025-26 building the stablecoin INBOUND rails (Circle CPN, Visa pilot, Coinbase USDC, stablecoin card issuance, the Cypher acqui-hire), Nium now lands a 9M-user crypto consumer app routing off-ramp flows onto its payout network. That is the point of its build-out being monetized.
- **Why the landscape looks this way + second order:** The stablecoin stack has bifurcated into crypto-rails (Bridge, BVNK, Zero Hash) vs licensed fiat payout/BaaS (Nium, OpenPayd, Thunes). Nium deliberately positions as the **fiat payout half** and refuses to own the on-chain leg (outsourced to "regulated blockchain providers") — keeping it a neutral, Visa/Mastercard-principal payout utility rather than a crypto-risk-bearing entity. Second order: that neutrality lets Nium serve many crypto wallets at once (it has no exclusivity with RedotPay), so the margin it captures is a thin, repeatable payout take-rate — volume game, not a moat on any single client.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **RedotPay trajectory:** launched HK 2023 → Series A $40M (Mar-2025) → unicorn $47M ([[RedotPay reaches unicorn status with $47M round]], Sep-2025) → **$107M Series B** (Dec-2025, [[RedotPay raises $107M Series B for stablecoin payments]], Circle Ventures among backers) → explores US IPO at ~$4B ([[RedotPay explores US IPO at $4 billion valuation]], [[RedotPay plans $1bn US IPO at $4bn valuation]], Feb/Mar-2026) with executive churn ([[RedotPay churns executives ahead of $4 billion US IPO push]]). User base went 6M (mid-2026) → **9M (Oct-2026)**; annualized volume ~$10B → ~$12B. Launched Connect merchant settlement ([[RedotPay launches Connect for instant fiat stablecoin settlement]], Jun-2026), VASP registrations (Mexico, [[RedotPay secures Mexico virtual asset service provider registration]]).
- **Nium:** Singapore/US cross-border payments infra, Temasek/GIC-backed, 190+ countries, FY24 revenue S$167.2m (~US$124m, +13.3%), net loss S$88.1m ([[Nium, Singapore-based fintech, narrowed annual loss to S$88M ($69M) with 13.3% revenue]]); heavy 2025-26 stablecoin build-out: Visa settlement pilot ([[Nium joins Visa's stablecoin settlement pilot]]), stablecoin card issuance ([[Nium debuts stablecoin card issuance platform with Visa]], [[Nium launches stablecoin card issuance across 190+ countries]]), Coinbase USDC ([[Nium partners with Coinbase for USDC stablecoin payments]]), Circle CPN ([[Nium and Circle connect USDC settlement with global payouts]]), Cypher acqui-hire ([[Nium acquires crypto infrastructure startup Cypher]]). US IPO targeted ~end-2026.
- **Fit / why each acts this way:** For RedotPay — ahead of an IPO narrative, it needs to show a widening "turn crypto into local fiat anywhere" footprint; renting Nium's 190-country payout reach is the fastest way to claim new-market off-ramp coverage. For Nium — it has spent a year assembling stablecoin plumbing while staying loss-making; converting that into real consumer off-ramp volume (9M-user app) is exactly the proof-of-strategy it needs for its own IPO. Mutual IPO-narrative reinforcement (analysis).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low-to-moderate.** "Crypto wallet rents a licensed cross-border payout network for off-ramp" is a standard integration; RedotPay has done it 4+ times (Circle, Ripple, OpenPayd, Tempo) and Nium's payout network already powers many clients. The only genuinely interesting structural detail is the explicit **crypto-leg-outsourced / fiat-leg-owned** split — a deliberate regulatory firewall, not a new technical capability.
- **Real value-add (if it goes live):** broader corridor coverage (190 countries, 100 currencies, real-time in 100+ markets) → RedotPay users in more markets can convert stablecoins to local fiat into bank/wallet/card. Genuinely useful for EM/remittance users, but it is **corridor breadth, not a new mechanism**, and overlaps capability RedotPay already had via Circle/Ripple in specific corridors.
- **Who captures the margin (analysis):** Nium takes a per-transaction payout/FX take-rate on RedotPay's off-ramp flows and keeps the regulated fiat relationships; RedotPay keeps the customer, the card interchange and the wallet. RedotPay **rents, does not own, the last-mile fiat leg** — a margin leak and a dependency, mitigated by its multi-rail sourcing (it can shift corridors between Nium/Circle/Ripple). Because Nium has no exclusivity, the same rails serve any crypto wallet → no durable moat for either side on this deal specifically.
- **Traction: none disclosed.** Announcement only; no volume, no live corridors, no go-live date. The "9M users" figure is addressable base, not users actually off-ramping via Nium.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Watch for:** named target markets/corridors, go-live confirmation, any volume disclosure, and WHICH "regulated blockchain infrastructure providers" supply Nium's stablecoin leg (that unnamed third party is where custody/AML risk and a chunk of economics actually sit).
- **Market backdrop:** stablecoins shifting from trading to everyday payments is the explicit thesis both cite; stablecoin-infra consolidation is intense (Stripe→Bridge, Mastercard→BVNK up to $1.8B). Licensed fiat-payout networks (Nium, Thunes, OpenPayd) are the picks-and-shovels beneficiaries as wallets bolt on compliant off-ramps rather than build them.
- **Why the market goes this way + counterintuitive second order (analysis):** As more crypto wallets outsource the fiat leg to a handful of licensed payout networks, value migrates to the regulated middle (Nium) and the consumer apps (RedotPay) become increasingly commoditized distribution skins competing on UX/fees. Counterintuitive: RedotPay's multi-rail strategy looks like resilience, but it also signals that no single off-ramp partner is strategic to it — so each such "partnership" is individually low-weight; the weight is in the aggregate trend, not this deal.
- **Risks:** announced-not-live; undisclosed economics; dependency on Nium's (and its unnamed blockchain partners') licensing/AML posture; Nium itself loss-making and IPO-pending; no exclusivity means commoditized rails.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits in stablecoin-rail cross-border payout / off-ramp infrastructure — the "fiat-edge" layer where digital-dollar balances convert into local currency. Sizing: genuine stablecoin *payment* activity (ex-trading/bot flows) was ~$135bn in 2025, up from ~$82bn in 2024, still only ~0.31% of the ~$44.3tn retail cross-border market (per Allium/FXC Intelligence sizing, via allium.so, 2026). The stablecoin-infra-and-cross-border-platform submarket is pegged at ~$7.4bn (2025) → ~$9.2bn (2026), ~23–24% CAGR to ~$21.3bn by 2030 (per The Business Research Co., via giiresearch/einpresswire, 2026) — treat these vendor TAMs as directional, not audited. Structure: fragmented and bifurcating into (a) fiat-native payout networks that own last-mile local-currency coverage (Nium, Airwallex, Thunes) and (b) stablecoin-native networks that treat USDC/USDT as the working balance (Bridge, BVNK, Eco). Barriers are regulatory (licenses in dozens of jurisdictions) and network/last-mile scale, not code. "Why now": post-GENIUS-Act regulatory clarity plus incumbents buying the pipes — Stripe/Bridge ($1.1bn, late-2024), Mastercard/BVNK (up to $1.8bn, closed 2026-08-03, per CoinDesk/S&P Global) — pushes standalone fintechs toward partnering rather than self-building the fiat edge.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: payout TPV/cross-border volume, currency/country coverage, take rate per corridor, real-time settlement share. Players & basis of competition: Nium competes on breadth of *licensed fiat payout* (100 currencies / 190 countries, licenses in 40+ countries, 100+ markets settling real-time) rather than on owning the stablecoin rail itself — note the architecture explicitly separates third-party blockchain connectivity from Nium's own fiat layer. Peers on the payout side: Thunes, Airwallex, Wise, plus incumbents SWIFT/Visa/Mastercard (Visa USDC settlement for US banks live Dec-2025, >$3.5bn annualized). Recent Nium moves: joined Visa stablecoin pilot (2025-11), Coinbase USDC partnership (2026-04), Circle Payments Network / USDC settlement (2026-05), dual-network stablecoin card issuance (2026-03), acquired crypto-infra startup Cypher (2026-07). RedotPay's position: a consumer-facing stablecoin wallet/card app (9M+ users, 100+ countries, ~$10bn annual payment volume per its Dec-2025 Series B) that does NOT own global fiat last-mile — this deal rents Nium's rails rather than building them. Moat: for Nium, licensing + payout network scale `(analysis)`; for RedotPay, user base + card issuance, but off-ramp is outsourced.

**Comps & multiples.** Private-market reference points (round valuations, not market caps):
- Nium: ~$1.4bn (June-2024 Series E *down round*, led by Brunei Investment Agency; ~37% below 2021 peak). FY2024: revenue S$167.2m (~US$124m, +13.3%), net loss S$88.1m (flat YoY), >US$84bn moved across borders in the past year. Implied P/S ≈ $1.4bn / $0.124bn ≈ **~11x** on FY24 revenue `(analysis)` — rich for a +13% grower still loss-making; flag as stretched vs growth. (per kapronasia, note [[Nium, Singapore-based fintech, narrowed annual loss to S$88M ($69M) with 13.3% revenue]])
- RedotPay: unicorn Sep-2025 ($47M round); $107M Series B Dec-2025; reportedly exploring a ~$1bn US IPO at >$4bn valuation (2026-02/03, per Bloomberg via CoinDesk). On ~$10bn annual payment volume, $4bn ≈ ~0.4x volume `(analysis)` — but take-rate undisclosed, so revenue multiple = no data. ([[RedotPay plans $1bn US IPO at $4bn valuation]], [[RedotPay raises $107M Series B for stablecoin payments]])
- BVNK: acquired by Mastercard for up to $1.8bn (2026), >2x its $750m Dec-2024 Series B — the clearest external read on how incumbents price the fiat-edge layer.
- Internal partnership comps: [[RedotPay and Circle enable crypto-to-fiat transfers to Brazil]], [[RedotPay adds crypto-to-MXN payouts via Circle in Mexico]], [[RedotPay partners with Ripple for naira payouts]], [[Nium and Circle connect USDC settlement with global payouts]].
Distribution not computed (<3 comparable revenue multiples on the same basis); qualitative read: Nium's ~11x P/S looks rich for its growth, BVNK's ~$1.8bn shows strategic-buyer premium for the same corridor layer.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Disintermediation / stacked rails for RedotPay** — RedotPay already runs crypto-to-fiat via Circle (Brazil/Mexico), Ripple (naira) and OpenPayd; adding Nium means the economics of the fiat edge accrue to multiple third parties, compressing RedotPay's own payout margin and ceding the regulated layer it doesn't control. Second-order: in an IPO story, "we rent our off-ramp" is a weaker moat narrative.
2. **Concentration on someone else's licenses** — the off-ramp depends on Nium's licenses in 40+ countries and on third-party "regulated blockchain infrastructure providers"; a license loss or corridor pull in any key market (e.g. HK/APAC) directly caps RedotPay's reach.
3. **Margin squeeze at Nium itself** — Nium is still loss-making with processing costs that surged 77% YoY (FY24); each new high-volume partner adds the very variable cost that has kept it in the red, so volume ≠ profit. Regulatory risk also live: stablecoin off-ramps are exactly the AML/KYC chokepoint supervisors scrutinize.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Confirms the "fiat-edge-as-a-service" thesis: stablecoin-native consumer apps are standardizing on buying last-mile payout rather than building 40+ licenses, so licensed payout networks (Nium, Thunes) become toll-collectors on the stablecoin boom `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: if RedotPay's IPO filing shows off-ramp cost-of-revenue rising faster than payment volume, the "rent the rails" model is margin-dilutive and the >$4bn tag is hard to defend. Watch next (trigger): RedotPay S-1/F-1 disclosures on take rate and third-party rail costs; and whether Nium's next results show processing-cost growth finally decoupling from volume.
