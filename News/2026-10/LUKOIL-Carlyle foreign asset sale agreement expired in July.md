 Соглашение о продаже зарубежных активов "ЛУКОЙЛа" инвесткомпании Carlyle истекло в июле 
Соглашение "ЛУКОЙЛа" с американской инвестиционной Carlyle о продаже LUKOIL International GmbH (100% дочернего общества российской НК, владеющего зарубежными активами группы) в конце июля утратило силу, поскольку не было одобрено OFAC, сообщается в отчете LIG за 2025 год, он датирован 30 сентября. Об этом пишет Интерфакс . 
"В настоящее время ведутся интенсивные переговоры с другими заинтересованными сторонами относительно потенциального приобретения группы компаний LIG", - говорится в документе.
О потенциальной сделке с Carlyle "ЛУКОЙЛ" объявил в январе, не сообщив о каких-либо сроках действия соглашения с компанией. В дальнейшем НК не делала объявлений о судьбе договоренностей с Carlyle.
LUKOIL International в отчете напоминает, что с одобрения OFAC от 31 марта 2026 года LUKOIL Mid-East Limited (Кипр) (через LUKOIL International Upstream Holding B.V., Нидерланды, 100% часть группы LIG) может передать свою 75% долю в нефтяном месторождении Западная Курна-2 в Ираке компании Basra Oil Company в соответствии с контрактом от 23 февраля 2026 года. При этом отмечается, что "в настоящее время невозможно оценить, какие другие дочерние компании или проекты LIG в сфере добычи нефти и газа могут быть приобретены потенциальными покупателями отдельно, и в какой степени дочерние компании или проекты в сфере добычи нефти и газа могут быть проданы напрямую другим покупателям".
"Руководство ожидает успешного завершения переговоров о продаже и подписания соглашения о покупке акций, в результате чего LIG и ее дочерние компании перейдут к новому владельцу. До завершения сделки руководство также рассчитывает на непрерывное продление необходимых лицензий", - отмечается в отчете.
Согласно информации компании, с 30 июня 2026 года с поста управляющего директора LUKOIL International Holding GmbH ушел Роберт Гулла.
OFAC 22 октября 2025 года внесло в санкционный список (SDN List) "ЛУКОЙЛ" и ряд его дочерних обществ, после чего НК объявила о намерении продать зарубежные активы, оцениваемые в $20 млрд, и приняла предложение о выкупе от международного сырьевого трейдера Gunvor. Однако осуществление сделки стало невозможным из-за позиции Минфина США, который её не одобрил. В конце января 2026 года "ЛУКОЙЛ" заключил соглашение с Carlyle.
НК на момент введения санкций участвовала в проектах в Азербайджане, Казахстане, Узбекистане, Ираке, Египте, Камеруне, Нигерии, Гане, Мексике, ОАЭ, Республике Конго. Компании принадлежат НПЗ в Болгарии, Румынии, Нидерландах, а также сеть АЗС в 19 странах в количестве около 2,5 тыс. заправок. Доказанные запасы нефти и газа по международным проектам "ЛУКОЙЛа" на конец 2024 года составили 1 трлн 345 млрд барр. н.э. Добыча нефти с газовым конденсатом по международным проектам без учета проекта "Западная Курна-2" в 2024 году оценивалась в 3,9 млн т, добыча газа - 16,2 млрд куб. м. Объем переработки на европейских НПЗ группы в 2024 году составил 13,5 млн т, снизившись на 18% к предыдущему году в связи с продажей НПЗ ISAB в Италии в мае 2023 года. Розничные продажи нефтепродуктов за рубежом в 2024 году были на уровне 4,2 млн т. "ЛУКОЙЛ" по итогам 2025 года полностью списал инвестицию в LIG и признал убыток от обесценения в 1,66 трлн руб.
США неоднократно продлевали действие временных лицензий, позволяющих активам продолжать работу на период решения вопроса об их будущем. Срок действия последнего продления истекает 22 октября.
Недавно Financial Times сообщила, что бывший владелец футбольного клуба "Челси" финансист-миллиардер Тодд Боэли заручился поддержкой правительства США и влиятельных лиц из стран Персидского залива для подачи заявки на покупку зарубежных активов "ЛУКОЙЛа". По информации издания, в консорциум Боэли входят Корпорация США по финансированию международного развития (US International Development Finance Corporation) и шейх Тахнун бен Заид Аль Нахайян - брат президента ОАЭ. В числе участников также катарская семья миллиардеров Аль-Хайят, тесно сотрудничавшая с Белым домом и членами семьи Трампа в сфере недвижимости и энергетики. Ожидается, что DFC - федеральное агентство США, инвестирующее в зарубежные проекты, - получит долю в новой компании в размере около 15%, сообщили два источника FT. Агентство возглавляет Бен Блэк, сын Леона Блэка, соучредителя частной инвестиционной фирмы Apollo. Семья Аль-Хайят получит меньшую долю, в то время как Боэли и DFC будут контролировать большинство мест в совете директоров.

# Context-enrichment: LUKOIL-Carlyle foreign asset sale agreement expired in July
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 4/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
De-PR'd: LUKOIL's late-January 2026 agreement to sell **LUKOIL International GmbH (LIG)** — the 100% subsidiary that holds ALL of the group's foreign assets — to US PE firm **Carlyle** **lapsed at the end of July 2026** because it **was never approved by OFAC**. The lapse was disclosed only now, buried in LIG's FY2025 report dated **30 Sep 2026** (via Interfax). LIG says it is "in intensive negotiations with other interested parties." So the headline is not a new deal — it is the quiet confirmation that the *second* attempted exit route (Gunvor was the first, blocked Nov 2025) has also failed.

**Why structured this way / what it reveals:** The Carlyle agreement was **non-exclusive** (per S&P Global) and carried **no disclosed term length** (the note explicitly says LUKOIL never announced a deadline). That non-exclusivity is the tell: LUKOIL kept shopping the assets the entire time, and Carlyle was never a committed buyer so much as a placeholder while OFAC was lobbied. The deal did not "fail" on price or diligence — it failed because **the entire structure is unworkable under sanctions**: OFAC's licenses (GL 131-series, latest **GL 131J** per the agent, expiring **22 Oct 2026** — the note's "22 October" deadline) authorize only *negotiating/signing contingent contracts*, NOT closing; every actual asset transfer needs a *separate specific license* that has never been granted; and LUKOIL (the sanctioned seller) **cannot be paid** — funds must be blocked, no money may flow to Russia. A PE buyer like Carlyle, whose model depends on an eventual resale, faces the problem that any *future* exit would itself need fresh OFAC review (analysis, supported by Disruption Banking 5 Oct 2026). That is why a financial sponsor walked into a wall.

Second data point in the note: with OFAC's 31 Mar 2026 approval, LIG's Iraqi vehicle may transfer its **75% in West Qurna-2** to Iraq's Basra Oil Company (contract dated 23 Feb 2026) — i.e. the portfolio is already being **dismembered piecemeal** by host states, which undercuts any single-buyer, whole-portfolio deal.

Scale (note + web): assets valued ~**$20bn** (write-off basis) to ~**$22bn** (deal-framing basis); LUKOIL took a **1.66 trn RUB (~$19.8bn)** full impairment on LIG for FY2025. Portfolio: refineries in **Bulgaria (Neftohim Burgas, ~190k bpd, largest in the Balkans), Romania (Petrotel), Netherlands (Zeeland JV)**; a retail network of **~2,500 stations across 19 countries** (web sources diverge: ~2,000 to ~5,300); upstream in Iraq, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Egypt, UAE, and across Africa/Mexico.

## [1] Competitors / peers (bidders & comparable forced divestitures)
- **Bidders for these assets (chronological):** Gunvor (offered ~$22bn, Nov 2025; **withdrew** after US Treasury branded it a "Kremlin puppet"); Carlyle (Jan 2026, **lapsed** end-July 2026); the **Todd Boehly consortium** (FT, 23 Sep 2026 — reported/exploratory, not a submitted binding bid).
- **Piecemeal competitors:** Chevron and Quantum reportedly circling **West Qurna-2** specifically (Arab Weekly); host governments (Bulgaria via special administrator, Iraq via operational takeover) are carving off in-country assets.
- **Comparable sanctions-driven Russian divestitures:** BP/Rosneft (~$25bn charge, 2022, effectively written off), Shell (Sakhalin-II etc.), ExxonMobil (Sakhalin-1). **Why the lay of the land is this way:** forced Russia-exposed sales clear at deep discounts or outright write-offs because the buyer universe is tiny. The LUKOIL case is *different and harder*: the assets sit OUTSIDE Russia and are genuinely valuable (EU refining, Iraqi oil), so bidding is competitive — **but the seller is barred from being paid**, which is the structural choke point no bidder can engineer around. Second-order: this inverts the usual "fire-sale" dynamic — there is demand, but the transaction rails are broken, so value leaks to host-state seizures rather than to either LUKOIL or a buyer.

## [2] Company history / fit
Trajectory: **22 Oct 2025** OFAC SDN-lists LUKOIL + subs → LUKOIL announces intent to sell ~$20bn of foreign assets → **Gunvor** deal blocked (Nov 2025) → **29 Jan 2026** Carlyle agreement → **~20 Mar 2026** FY2025 results: 1.66 trn RUB impairment, LIG written to zero → **end-July 2026** Carlyle lapses → **30 Sep 2026** LIG report discloses lapse → **23 Sep 2026** Boehly consortium reported. Governance churn: LUKOIL International Holding GmbH MD **Robert Gulla departed 30 Jun 2026** (note) — consistent with a stranded, distressed unit.
**Why LUKOIL acts this way:** it has no choice — sanctions make the foreign unit a wasting asset (host states nationalizing/administering, licenses on a short clock), so LUKOIL has already *accounted* for a total loss. Its only remaining interest is an *orderly* exit that preserves some residual (blocked) value versus the worse outcome of outright expropriation when licenses lapse.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
What's genuinely new (vs PR): **confirmation of failure**, not progress. The real signal is that **after ~12 months and three buyer processes (Gunvor, Carlyle, Boehly), ZERO asset transfers have closed** and OFAC has issued only negotiation-only licenses, never a closing authorization. "Intensive negotiations with other interested parties" is management-speak for "we are back to square one." Traction = negative: the one *completed* action is the piecemeal hand-back of West Qurna-2 to Iraq, i.e. value draining out of the portfolio, not a sale realizing it.
**Why the value-add question shifts:** the central question is no longer "who buys LUKOIL's assets and at what price" but **"can ANY transaction clear OFAC's separate closing-license requirement before host states seize the assets outright"** — a regulatory/political question, not a commercial one. Whoever "captures" these assets will be decided in Washington and Gulf capitals, not in a data room.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **22 Oct 2026** is the binding date (GL 131J expiry). Base case (analysis): **another negotiation extension, not a closing** — OFAC has extended ~11 times and never granted a closing license; a fresh Boehly bid surfacing only ~4 weeks before expiry argues against a clean close.
- **If licenses lapse without extension:** accelerated asset freezes and host-state takeovers (Bulgaria already runs Burgas via a special administrator; Iraq controls West Qurna-2 operations; Romania/Moldova/Hungary contemplating steps) → de facto nationalization, the worst value outcome.
- **Boehly consortium** (reported): Boehly + US **DFC** (~15%, led by **Ben Black**, son of Apollo's Leon Black) + Gulf money (Sheikh **Tahnoun bin Zayed**, Qatari **Al-Khayyat** family). **Counterintuitive second-order risk:** a US government arm (DFC) bidding for assets whose sale another US arm (OFAC/Treasury) must bless is an explicit conflict of interest and politicizes the whole process — tying it to US-Russia diplomacy and Trump-adjacent actors. That raises, not lowers, execution risk and optics risk, even as it might grease OFAC approval.

1. **Is this actually new, or a reprint of the Jan 2026 Carlyle deal?** New. The Jan deal was never in the corpus; the news here is its *lapse* (end-July 2026), disclosed only in LIG's 30 Sep 2026 report. A genuine "talks → failed" development. Freshness = fresh.

3. **Why would a PE firm even bid for assets it cannot resell cleanly?** Analysis: any future exit by Carlyle would itself need fresh OFAC review — a structural misfit for the PE flip model. Likely Carlyle was positioning for a distressed-value entry betting on sanctions easing via US-Russia diplomacy. Open whether Carlyle had a committed thesis or was opportunistic.

4. **Can ANY transaction close before 22 Oct 2026?** Base case: no. OFAC has granted ~11 negotiation-only extensions and never a closing license. GL 131J is negotiation-only and explicitly revocable. (web + analysis)

5. **What happens to asset value if licenses lapse?** Downside trigger: freezes + host-state seizures (Bulgaria's Burgas special administrator, Iraq's West Qurna-2 takeover) → de facto nationalization, worst value outcome. (web)

6. **Is the ~$20bn headline real or aspirational?** It is an *accounting write-off* figure (1.66 trn RUB impairment, LIG written to zero), i.e. LUKOIL already books these as worth ~$0 on a net basis. Deal-value framing (~$22bn) is gross. The realizable cash value to LUKOIL is effectively blocked/zero under current rules. (note + web)

7. **Who actually captures these assets in the end?** Open. Candidates: Boehly consortium (whole portfolio), host states (piecemeal seizure), or a frozen limbo. Decided in Washington/Gulf capitals, not a data room.

8. **Is the DFC's ~15% stake a conflict of interest that helps or hurts approval?** Both (analysis): a US government arm bidding for assets another US arm must license is a textbook conflict that could grease OFAC approval while poisoning the optics and inviting legal/political challenge. Net effect open.

9. **How much of the portfolio is already gone before any sale?** West Qurna-2 (75%, LUKOIL's single biggest upstream asset) is being handed to Basra Oil Company; Bulgaria's Burgas is under special administration. The "whole-portfolio" sale premise is eroding in real time. (note)

10. **Does the Gunvor precedent tell us Treasury will block Boehly too?** Partly: Treasury blocked Gunvor on *who the buyer was* ("Kremlin puppet"). Boehly/DFC/Gulf is the opposite profile — US-govt-aligned — so the buyer-identity objection reverses. But the payment/closing-license problem is buyer-agnostic and remains. (web)

11. **Why disclose the lapse in a dry FY2025 report dated 30 Sep rather than a press release?** Analysis: LUKOIL has no incentive to publicize failure; the disclosure is compliance-driven (LIG's statutory report), which is why it surfaced via Interfax, not an official announcement. Signals LUKOIL has nothing positive to say.

12. **Is there any fintech/financial-markets angle justifying this in a fintech corpus?** Weak-direct, strong-indirect: the story is sanctions-finance mechanics (OFAC licensing, blocked funds, PE/sovereign-fund M&A, DFC) — payments-and-sanctions plumbing rather than a product. Importance rests on scale and geopolitics, not fintech novelty. (analysis)

13. **What-if sanctions ease via a US-Russia deal — does Carlyle come back?** Open. If OFAC closing-licenses became grantable, the whole buyer calculus resets; Carlyle's non-exclusive agreement is dead but could be re-struck. Highly contingent on diplomacy.

14. **Does the retail-station count discrepancy (2,000 / 2,500 / 5,300) matter?** Marginally: it affects valuation granularity but not the central thesis (transaction is blocked). Flag as unresolved; the note's 2,500/19-countries is mid-range. (web)

15. **Who needs whom more — LUKOIL or the bidders?** LUKOIL needs an orderly exit more than any bidder needs these specific assets (bidders have alternatives; LUKOIL faces expropriation). That weak hand, plus blocked payment, means LUKOIL cannot extract value — it is a price-taker trying to avoid a zero. (analysis)

Importance: 4/5 — A ~$20bn forced divestiture of a Russian major's entire foreign footprint, with a hard 22 Oct 2026 OFAC deadline, US-government and Gulf-sovereign bidders, and live nationalization risk across the EU/Iraq — geopolitically major and genuinely fresh (second failed exit confirmed). Capped below 5 because the fintech-product relevance is indirect (sanctions-finance plumbing, not a fintech launch), nothing has actually closed, and the key development is a *failure* rather than a completed transaction.

**Sector & drivers.** This is a sanctions-forced divestment in international oil & gas (upstream + European downstream/retail), not a fintech deal — comps in this base are thin. Asset perimeter per note: LUKOIL International GmbH (LIG) holding — upstream stakes (Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Iraq, Egypt, Nigeria, Ghana, Mexico, UAE, Congo), refineries in Bulgaria/Romania/Netherlands, and ~2,500 retail stations across 19 countries. Headline value ~$20bn (per the sale perimeter LUKOIL announced after the 22-Oct-2025 SDN listing; web pegs the Carlyle deal at ~$22bn, per cryptobriefing.com, as of 2026-10). Structure: a single bilateral forced sale of a blocked asset — the "market" here is OFAC's licensing regime, not a competitive M&A process. The binding driver is the sanctions clock: OFAC added LUKOIL to the SDN List on 22-Oct-2025; wind-down/negotiation General Licenses have been rolled over repeatedly (GL 131H → Aug-22; GL 131J → **22-Oct-2026**, per interfax.com / fesenkolaw.com), and the note's own last-extension expiry (22-Oct) matches. "Why now": the Carlyle agreement (signed late-Jan-2026) lapsed end-July because OFAC never approved it; LIG's 2025 report (dated 30-Sep) confirms the lapse and says "intensive negotiations with other interested parties" are underway.

**Competitive landscape.** The relevant "KPIs" are not take rates but: reserves (1.345bn boe intl proved, end-2024), intl output (~3.9mn t oil + 16.2bn m³ gas, 2024, ex-West Qurna-2), European refining throughput (13.5mn t, 2024, −18% YoY after the ISAB/Italy sale in May-2023), and intl retail fuel sales (4.2mn t, 2024) — all from the note. "Buyers," not peers, compete here: (1) **Gunvor** — original buyer, rejected by Treasury as a Kremlin "puppet" (per thebell.io); (2) **Carlyle** — signed Jan-2026, agreement lapsed end-July for lack of OFAC approval, though a later report (late-Sep) says Carlyle got OFAC clearance but still awaits Trump-administration sign-off (per seenews.com); (3) **Todd Boehly-led consortium** — backed by US DFC (~15% stake expected), UAE's Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed and the Qatari Al-Khayyat family, described as "advanced" (per FT via note; oilandgasmiddleeast.com). **Risk flags.**
- **Regulatory kill-switch (primary).** The deal's existence depends on an OFAC license expiring 22-Oct-2026; absent renewal or a closing authorization, the assets risk full freeze/standstill — European refineries (Bulgaria/Romania) and ~2,500 stations face operational disruption (per cryptobriefing.com: "refineries idle"). Second-order: feedstock/payment counterparties pre-emptively exit, eroding asset value before any sale.
- **Serial deal failure / buyer risk.** Two buyers already blocked (Gunvor rejected; Carlyle lapsed end-July despite later partial OFAC nod but no Trump sign-off). A third attempt (Boehly) is unconfirmed → pattern suggests the binding constraint is political approval, not buyer availability; each failed round re-opens the clock and signals the asset may not clear at all.
- **Geopolitical entanglement.** Reports tie the LIG sale to US–Russia talks on ending the Ukraine war (per meduza.io / NYT) — the price and closing are hostage to a diplomatic track LUKOIL cannot influence; terms could change for reasons unrelated to asset fundamentals.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is a precedent-setting test of whether a Western buyer can cleanly carve a ~$20bn asset block out of a sanctioned Russian major — if structured to OFAC's satisfaction it becomes a template for future forced divestments; if it fails again, it signals such assets are effectively stranded/unsellable at any price. Falsifiable thesis: a sale closes only if/when a buyer with explicit White-House political backing emerges (Boehly consortium with DFC is the current best-fit). Trigger to watch: whether OFAC renews past 22-Oct-2026 and whether the Trump administration grants the closing authorization Carlyle reportedly still lacks — no renewal = asset-freeze scenario; what breaks the thesis = a close under a buyer without visible US-government sponsorship.
