# Ripple expects $900 million annualized product revenue run rate as institutional business expands

The firm says customers using multiple Ripple products have tripled this year, as its payments network nears $150 billion in lifetime volume.

By Jason Shubnell • Edited by Kyle Baird

Business•October 28, 2026, 9:00AM EDT

#### Quick Take

*   Ripple said customers and partners using multiple products have tripled this year, as its payments network approaches $150 billion in lifetime volume.
*   That push is extending beyond payments and stablecoins, as financial institutions increasingly use Ripple’s infrastructure for tokenized assets and trading.

Ripple expects to exit 2026 with a $900 million annualized product revenue run rate, citing growth across its payments, custody, stablecoin, prime brokerage, and treasury businesses, the firm announced Wednesday.

The digital asset infrastructure firm said customers and partners using multiple Ripple products have tripled this year, as its payments network is expected to approach $150 billion in lifetime volume by year-end and its RLUSD stablecoin reaches a $2.6 billion market capitalization.

"As traditional and digital finance converge, market infrastructure is migrating to modern rails," Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse said in a statement. "Settlement is moving to real-time, 24/7, and tokenized assets can be traded, financed and used as collateral."

On Monday, Ripple also received final approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency to establish a national trust bank, while expanding its partnership with BNY on tokenized trade finance using the XRP Ledger.

That push is extending beyond payments and stablecoins, as financial institutions increasingly use Ripple's infrastructure for tokenized assets and trading.

## Ripple's big year

Aviva Investors just launched a tokenized share class of its £22 billion sterling liquidity fund on XRPL, investing £50 million ($66.7 million) in the new share class. The hedge fund Brevan Howard expanded its relationship with Ripple to include prime brokerage, clearing and financing.

Meanwhile, Ripple Prime earlier this month added swaps referencing more than 100 markets, with 24/7 trading and settlement. Institutional clients can use RLUSD as collateral.

On a more cultural note, Ripple also became the Kansas Jayhawks' sports sponsor — the first time a cryptocurrency would be displayed on the jerseys of a major college sports program. That was followed by similar deals with Florida Gators athletics and the University of Louisville.

"Others focus on individual pieces of the puzzle," Garlinghouse said Wednesday. "Ripple is bringing all of that together, with our partners, to enable the Internet of Value."

Despite a spike higher in early August, the XRP token remains down nearly 30% this year with an $83 billion market cap.

Meanwhile, spot XRP ETF volumes have increased in recent months, although they still pale in comparison to their BTC, ETH, and SOL ETF counterparts. Total net assets for the XRP funds sit around $1.5 billion as of Oct. 26.

# Context-enrichment: Ripple targets $900M annualised product revenue run rate
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-28 Ripple put out a corporate update saying it expects to **exit 2026 at a ~$900M annualised PRODUCT revenue run rate** — i.e. a point-in-time exit-year run-rate (annualised December-ish figure), NOT $900M of booked 2026 revenue. The number is Ripple's own, unaudited; Ripple is a private company and publishes no audited financials. Supporting metrics given: customers/partners using **multiple** Ripple products "tripled" this year; payments network "approaching" $150B **lifetime** (cumulative, not annual) volume by year-end; RLUSD market cap $2.6B; 1,700+ institutions/enterprises served; 85+ licences.

**Why framed this way, and what it reveals.** "Product revenue run rate" is the load-bearing phrase. The term exists specifically to carve out XRP token sales / escrow releases, which have historically been the real engine of Ripple's P&L. Garlinghouse has for ~2 years anchored to a "$1B recurring revenue excluding XRP by end-2026" target; $900M is the progress check-in against that self-set goal — slightly short of $1B and still a *target/expectation* ("expects to exit"), not a reported result. Because no external party can audit the split between service fees and token-linked revenue, "product revenue" is a definition Ripple controls end-to-end. The update bundles genuine commercial wins (OCC national trust bank final approval, BNY tokenised trade finance, Brevan Howard prime-brokerage expansion, Aviva £22B fund tokenised share class on XRPL) with vanity/cultural items (college-sports jersey sponsorships) — a tell that the quantitative core is thin enough to pad.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Stablecoin issuance (RLUSD $2.6B):** dwarfed by Tether (~$170B+ class) and Circle USDC (tens of $B); RLUSD is a small, institution-targeted entrant. Revenue here is reserve interest — directly rate-sensitive.
- **Custody / tokenisation infra:** competes with Coinbase (Prime/custody), BNY (now also a *partner*), Fireblocks, Anchorage, plus Ripple's own Metaco acquisition stack. Circle (Arc), Stripe/Bridge and Stripe's stablecoin push are the fast-moving peers on payments+stablecoin convergence.
- **Prime brokerage / trading (Ripple Prime, ex-Hidden Road):** competes with FalconX, Galaxy, Cumberland; Brevan Howard expansion is a real institutional signal.
- **Position:** Ripple is assembling a full-stack "institutional crypto bank" (payments + custody + stablecoin + prime + treasury + trust charter). Breadth is ahead of most single-product rivals; depth/scale in any one line is mid-pack. Second-order: breadth is a moat only if cross-sell compounds — hence the "multi-product customers tripled" stat is the one that actually matters, yet it's given as a ratio with no base.

## [2] Company history / fit
Ripple's trajectory: XRP sales / escrow → regulatory overhang (2020–2023 SEC case) → post-GENIUS-Act pivot to institutional infra. Acquisitions built the stack: Metaco (custody), Hidden Road (prime brokerage, repackaged as Ripple Prime), GTreasury (treasury, Oct 2025). Jan 2026 it raised $500M at a $40B valuation (Fortress/Citadel Securities) [[Ripple raises $500m at $40bn valuation]]. The $900M "product revenue" narrative is the logical capstone: a private company at a $40–50B valuation needs a software/infra revenue story — not a token-sales story — to justify the multiple and to tee up an eventual IPO. Structural pressure: XRP is down ~30% YTD ($83B mcap), so leaning on a token-independent revenue line is both credibility-building and self-serving.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
What's genuinely new in THIS note: the specific **$900M exit-year run-rate figure** and the fresh metric bundle (tripled multi-product customers, ~$150B lifetime payments volume, $2.6B RLUSD cap). The underlying building blocks (BNY custody/trade finance, RLUSD, Ripple Prime, OCC trust bank) are each covered in prior notes — see [[Ripple selects BNY to custody RLUSD stablecoin reserves]], [[Ripple expands partnership with TJM for trade execution]], [[Ripple launches platform for traditional and digital assets]]. Traction that is real (named clients/volumes): Brevan Howard, Aviva (£50M committed), BNY, OCC charter. Traction that is soft: "$900M run rate" is a forward expectation; "tripled" and "approaching $150B lifetime" are unbaselined/cumulative. **Who captures the margin:** stablecoin reserve income is rate-sensitive and commoditising; payments take-rate is thin and competitive; the durable, high-multiple revenue is custody + prime + treasury SaaS/fees — but Ripple doesn't break out the mix, so the quality of the $900M is unverifiable.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Drivers to watch: whether the $900M is actually hit and, more importantly, its composition (recurring fees vs. reserve interest vs. anything token-linked); RLUSD growth vs USDC/USDT; conversion of the OCC trust charter into live custody/settlement revenue; an eventual IPO where audited financials would finally expose the split. Sentiment is bifurcated: the business narrative is "best year ever," while XRP (the token the market actually prices) is having its worst year since 2022. Second-order/counterintuitive: a strong *product* story that de-couples Ripple's P&L from XRP is bullish for the company but arguably bearish for XRP holders, since it removes the "Ripple needs XRP to go up" reflexivity. Regulatory tailwind (GENIUS Act, OCC charter) is the real structural enabler.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
_Importance: 3/5._ A credible, well-timed corporate update with real institutional wins, but the headline number is a self-set, unaudited, forward run-rate target — incremental rather than event-defining.

## Top challenge / red-team questions

1. **Run rate vs. booked revenue?** $900M is an *exit-year annualised run rate* Ripple "expects to" hit, not $900M of 2026 revenue. Answered: it's a forward/point-in-time figure — far weaker than a reported annual result.

2. **What does "product revenue" exclude?** It is explicitly the "excluding-XRP" line Garlinghouse has pushed for ~2 years (his standing target was ~$1B recurring revenue ex-XRP by end-2026). So $900M = progress toward a self-set goal, and is *below* that $1B. Open: exact carve-out rules undisclosed.

3. **Can anyone verify it?** No. Ripple is private, publishes no audited financials; external parties cannot split fees vs. reserve interest vs. token-linked revenue. The number is entirely Ripple-defined.

4. **Why $900M now, when the earlier target was $1B?** Likely a reset/realistic check-in just under the round $1B, or a deliberately conservative framing. Open — Ripple doesn't reconcile the two.

5. **Revenue composition — durable vs. rate-sensitive?** Big share is likely RLUSD reserve interest (directly exposed to falling rates) + thin payments take-rate. The high-multiple durable part (custody/prime/treasury fees) is not broken out. Open — this is the key unanswered question for valuation.

6. **Is the $150B figure impressive?** It's *lifetime/cumulative* payments volume "approaching" year-end, not annual throughput — a materially softer stat than it reads.

7. **"Multi-product customers tripled" — from what base?** No absolute numbers, no starting base. 3x off a tiny base is meaningless; 3x of a large base is huge. Open.

8. **Is any of this new vs. prior notes?** The $900M figure and the metric bundle are new; the components (BNY, RLUSD, Ripple Prime, OCC trust bank) are each previously covered. Verdict: fresh as a corporate-update data point, not a brand-new deal.

9. **Brevan Howard / Aviva — real traction?** Yes, these are named, concrete institutional commitments (Aviva £50M into a tokenised share class; Brevan Howard prime-brokerage expansion). The strongest substance in the update.

10. **RLUSD $2.6B — competitive relevance?** Tiny vs. USDT (~$170B+) and USDC. Growing but not yet a scale revenue driver.

11. **Why bundle college-sports sponsorships into a revenue update?** Marketing padding — a tell that the hard quantitative core is thin.

12. **Decoupling from XRP — for whom is it good?** Good for the *company* (IPO-readiness, valuation independent of token). Counterintuitively bearish for *XRP holders*, as it weakens the "Ripple needs XRP up" reflexivity, while XRP is down ~30% YTD.

13. **Does the OCC national trust bank change the revenue picture yet?** Approval is secured but revenue is prospective — a licence, not yet live custody/settlement income. Open.

14. **Why disclose now and in this form?** Positioning for an eventual IPO and to counter the weak-XRP narrative with a strong operating story — self-serving framing to watch.

15. **What would confirm or break the thesis?** Confirm: audited breakout at IPO showing a large recurring-fee (non-token, non-reserve) share. Break: $900M turns out to be dominated by rate-sensitive reserve income or soft/one-off items, or the run rate is quietly missed.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Ripple sits in crypto market-infrastructure for institutions — a bundle of cross-border payments, custody, a fiat-backed stablecoin (RLUSD), prime brokerage and corporate treasury. Sector context: total stablecoin market cap was ~$300bn as of 28 Oct 2026 (Tether ~$183.8bn, USDC ~$74.1bn; the two = ~82% of supply — per DefiLlama/trackers via fool.com & onebullex, as of Oct 2026). RLUSD at a stated $2.6bn market cap is a ~0.9% slice — a distant challenger, not a leader. Structure: the stablecoin layer is a consolidated duopoly (Tether + Circle) while the surrounding institutional-infra layer (custody, prime, tokenization rails) is still fragmented and up for grabs — which is precisely where Ripple is aiming the capital it raised. "Why now": the US GENIUS stablecoin framework plus Ripple's final OCC approval (26–27 Oct 2026) for Ripple National Trust Bank (to hold RLUSD reserves + offer fiduciary custody) lowers the regulatory barrier for a bank-grade institutional offering (per Morningstar/CoinDesk, Oct 2026). Barriers to entry here are capital, licensing (OCC trust charter, MiCA CASP, EU EMI, FCA) and institutional trust — all of which Ripple has been buying/earning.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for a stablecoin issuer, circulating supply × reserve yield drives revenue (Circle's model); for custody/prime, AUC, financing balances and trading volume; for payments, lifetime/annual settled volume and take rate. Key players and basis of competition: **Circle** (USDC issuer — competes on scale + distribution; USDC ~$74bn, settled ~$36tn in H1 2026, 77% of global stablecoin tx volume by June — per fxstreet/stablecoininsider, Oct 2026); **Coinbase** (vertically integrated — custody, prime, and the dominant USDC distribution partner, earning the majority of USDC economics); **Tether** (largest issuer, offshore). Recent peer moves: Coinbase itself added RLUSD across retail/exchange/Prime on 28 Oct 2026 (per note's own source list) — a competitor distributing Ripple's stablecoin, which cuts both ways. Ripple's own position: a multi-product institutional stack assembled by M&A — Hidden Road (prime brokerage, closed Oct 2025), GTreasury ($1B, corporate treasury, Oct 2025), Rail ($200M, stablecoin payments, Aug 2025), Palisade (wallet/custody, Nov 2025) [[Ripple acquires wallet provider Palisade]], plus BNY as RLUSD reserve custodian [[Ripple selects BNY to custody RLUSD stablecoin reserves]]. Position: **catching up / niche** on pure stablecoin scale (RLUSD is ~1/28th of USDC), but **ahead/differentiated** on the bundled "one-stop institutional infra" thesis. Moat (analysis): switching costs from multi-product integration (multi-product customers tripled YoY, 1,700+ institutions) + a growing licensing/regulatory moat (OCC trust bank, MiCA, FCA, EMI) that few crypto peers hold.

**Comps & multiples.** This is a private company reporting a *forward exit-run-rate* ($900m annualized by YE 2026), not booked GAAP revenue — so no EV/EBITDA or P/E is computable; valuation is a private round mark, not a market cap.
- **Ripple:** last primary round Nov 2025 = $40bn post-money ($500m led by Fortress/Citadel) [[Ripple selects BNY to custody RLUSD stablecoin reserves]] (IR, below); a Mar 2026 buyback reportedly implied ~$50bn (per coindesk/dlnews, 2026). Against the $900m exit run-rate: $40bn / $0.9bn ≈ **44x run-rate revenue**; at $50bn ≈ **56x**. These are round-valuation multiples, not market caps, and sit far above the hyper-growth revenue-multiple range (~0.5–20x) — justified only if the run-rate compounds and converts to real, margined revenue (see risks).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Disclosure / de-PR gap.** $900m is a self-reported *forward annualized exit run-rate*, not audited revenue; no segment split, no margin, no net-vs-gross on prime/treasury. A private co can frame run-rate generously — treat as management guidance, not a result.

3. **Valuation stretched on a forward number.** ~44–56x run-rate (private mark) vs Circle's ~9x P/S on realized revenue; execution across five bolted-together businesses (payments/custody/stablecoin/prime/treasury) must compound for the mark to hold — integration and crypto-cycle risk (XRP down ~30% YTD) cut against it.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Ripple is explicitly positioning as the bundled, bank-chartered alternative to the Circle/Coinbase-and-Tether stack — infrastructure, not a single stablecoin. Falsifiable thesis: if RLUSD supply and multi-product attach keep compounding and the OCC trust bank goes live, Ripple re-rates as "institutional crypto infra" rather than "the XRP company." What would break it: RLUSD stalls well below $5bn, or the $900m run-rate doesn't convert into disclosed, margined revenue in 2027 — in which case the $40–50bn mark looks like cycle froth. ## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (headline read).** NOT AN EARNINGS RELEASE — Ripple is private and files no audited results. This is a self-reported, forward-looking **exit-year run-rate target**: ~$900M annualized **product** revenue by end-2026, delivered in a corporate-update press push, not a reported quarterly/annual figure. Read it as **management guidance**, not a print: treat the number [UNSOURCED-to-audit] and beat/miss is not applicable.

**Key figures (as disclosed by the company; none audited).**
- **$900M** annualized product revenue **run rate**, which Ripple says it is "approaching" / "expects to exit 2026 with." Run rate = latest-period rate × annualization, not booked FY revenue; the actual trailing figure is not disclosed. No prior-year revenue base disclosed, so **no YoY growth rate can be computed** — "tripled customers using multiple products" is an adoption metric, not revenue.
- Scope stated as payments, custody, stablecoin (RLUSD), prime brokerage, and treasury. **Critically: "product revenue" explicitly EXCLUDES XRP held on the balance sheet / XRP sales** — Garlinghouse frames a parallel ~**$1B** operating run-rate "not counting the XRP on its balance sheet" (cryptonews.net, cryptoslate). This is the single most important caveat: historically Ripple's economics were dominated by programmatic XRP sales; the $900M "product" figure is the deliberately de-XRP'd number meant to position Ripple as an infrastructure business, not a token treasury.
- Supporting operating metrics (company-stated, unaudited): payments network nearing **$150B lifetime** cumulative volume by year-end (cumulative, not annual throughput); RLUSD market cap **$2.6B** (co. says +~88% YTD); 1,700+ institutions/enterprises served; customers using multiple products "tripled" this year.

**By segment / driver.** Growth narrative leans on inorganic expansion: Hidden Road / Ripple Prime (prime brokerage, acquired ~$1.25B), Rail (~$200M, stablecoin payments), GTreasury (~$1B, corporate treasury), plus the OCC national trust bank final approval (Oct 26) enabling fiduciary digital-asset custody and RLUSD reserve management. So a large share of the $900M run-rate is **bought, not organically grown** — the "tripled multi-product customers" partly reflects cross-sell across acquired units. No per-segment revenue split is given, so the custody/RLUSD/payments/prime/treasury mix inside the $900M is **no data**.

**vs expectations / prior period.** No public consensus exists (private, no sell-side model) and **no prior Ripple operating-revenue figure has ever been formally disclosed** — this ~$1B/$900M run-rate guidance is among the company's first specific operating-revenue targets. Ripple's only recurring "quarterly" disclosures were the **XRP Markets Reports**, discontinued after Q1 2025 ([[q1-2025-xrp-markets-report_715c50]]), which covered XRP sales/escrow, not product P&L. Net: there is **no comparable baseline** to measure a beat/miss against; momentum is assertable only via the Nov-2025 $40B valuation round led by Fortress/Citadel ([[ripple-announces-500-million-strategic-investment-led-by-fortress-citadel-securities-valuing-the-company-at-40-billion-f_ae2bdd]]), i.e. a priced-in growth expectation, not a reported result.

**Guidance / forward.** This item IS the guidance: a new, introduced forward target (~$900M product / ~$1B operating exit run-rate, end-2026, XRP-excluded). Plausibility: **directionally credible but unverifiable.** Credible drivers are real and documented (OCC trust-bank approval, RLUSD at $2.6B with Moody's investment-grade reserve ratings, BNY tokenized-trade-finance tie-up, Aviva £50M XRPL tokenized fund, Brevan Howard prime expansion). But a "run rate" quoted at the moment of peak momentum, with no audited base, no YoY, and no segment split, is a **self-serving framing** — likely IPO-preparatory positioning (Ripple pre-IPO secondary interest is active). Management tone: confident/promotional ("Internet of Value," "others focus on individual pieces"). What they stay silent on: absolute current revenue, profitability/margins, how much of the run-rate is acquired vs organic, and RLUSD net interest economics.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. **XRP-exclusion is the real signal (fact → why → matters → 2nd-order).** Fact: Ripple now headlines a product-revenue number that excludes XRP. Why: it reframes the investment case from a token-sales treasury to a regulated-infrastructure fintech. Matters: de-risks the thesis from XRP price (XRP is down ~30% YTD, ~$83B mcap, its worst year since 2022) — the business story is decoupling from the token story. 2nd-order: strengthens an IPO narrative at/above the $40B mark and supports a revenue-multiple (not token-mcap) valuation frame.
2. **"Run rate" ≠ realized revenue (de-PR).** A run-rate annualizes a recent peak; absent the trailing-twelve-month figure and the organic/inorganic split, the $900M overstates durable earning power. Risk: a chunk is freshly-acquired (Hidden Road, GTreasury, Rail) revenue that may not fully recur or integrate.
3. **Custody/RLUSD economics are rate-sensitive.** Much "product revenue" (RLUSD reserve yield, trust-bank custody, treasury) rides on short-term rates and stablecoin float; a rate-cut cycle or RLUSD-cap stall compresses it. Flag because RLUSD growth is the most-cited driver yet its revenue is the least disclosed.
4. **No auditable baseline.** Every figure is company-sourced; there is no 8-K, no audited statement, no consensus. Appropriate stance: track whether these metrics reappear in a future S-1 before treating $900M as fact.
