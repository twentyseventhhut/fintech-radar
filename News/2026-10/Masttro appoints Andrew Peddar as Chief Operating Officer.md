Masttro has appointed Andrew Peddar as Chief Operating Officer to lead its global data operations and client experience teams. For fintech professionals, this move signals a scaling phase for a wealth tech firm reporting 43% ARR growth, as it seeks to professionalize its operational backbone to support AI-driven cross-border wealth management.

Andrew Peddar joins Masttro as Chief Operating Officer, a role that places him in charge of the company’s Data Operations and Client Experience teams. Based at the firm’s New York City headquarters, Peddar reports directly to CEO Jay McNamara. His remit focuses on unifying global operations, onboarding, and client service functions to maintain data reliability and seamless delivery as the platform scales.

Peddar’s career spans over 25 years in financial technology and enterprise software. He previously served as Group Chief Operating Officer at StatPro, where he managed commercial and delivery functions across North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific. He later held the position of Vice President and General Manager at Broadridge Financial Solutions, overseeing a global business serving major financial institutions. Most recently, he was Chief Revenue Officer at Next Matter, an AI and data orchestration platform.

The appointment follows a period of significant growth for Masttro, which reports a 43% increase in annual recurring revenue over the last 18 months. Since the beginning of 2025, the company has expanded its workforce by 20%, with staff operating across seven countries and serving clients in more than 40 markets. The firm’s technical infrastructure currently supports more than 750 direct custodial connections and tracks over 300,000 alternative investments. In the first nine months of 2026, Masttro’s AI-driven tools saw high engagement, with the Intelligence Assistant processing over 32,000 client requests. Additionally, its Documents AI and Alternatives AI products now support workflows across more than 10,000 funds. The company also secured ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification in September 2026.

Masttro provides a global operating system designed for the management of complex wealth. The platform is built to consolidate financial data across various institutions, entities, and asset classes, specifically targeting the needs of ultra-high-net-worth individuals and family offices. By integrating direct custodial connections with alternative investment tracking, the firm aims to automate the reporting and analysis of diversified portfolios that often include non-traditional assets.

The company operates from its headquarters in New York City and maintains a global presence with employees in seven different countries. Masttro has positioned itself as a specialist in the "complex wealth" niche, where traditional retail or institutional tools often struggle with the lack of transparency in private equity, hedge funds, and other alternative holdings. Its market strategy relies heavily on the integration of artificial intelligence to handle document processing and data orchestration. This focus on security and operational standards was recently formalized through the achievement of ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification, reflecting its role as a critical infrastructure provider for global wealth owners and their advisors.

FF News has tracked Masttro’s expansion and its focus on AI-driven automation throughout 2025 and 2026. In early 2025, the publication covered the leadership transition when the firm Masttro Appoints Jay McNamara as Chief Executive Officer. Since then, the reporting has shifted toward the company's product evolution and technical integrations.

In August 2026, FF News reported that Masttro Launches Cash Projection Hub to Automate Closed-End Fund Forecasting, a move designed to assist wealth managers with liquidity planning. This was followed in September 2026 by the news that Masttro and Truewind Launch AI Integration to Automate UHNW Wealth Accounting. That partnership specifically targeted the movement of complex wealth data into family office accounting systems, illustrating the firm's ongoing strategy of reducing manual data entry for high-net-worth clients.

The appointment of a COO with deep enterprise experience suggests that the wealth tech sector is moving beyond the initial "visibility" phase into a period of industrial-scale operational maturity. As ultra-high-net-worth portfolios become increasingly weighted toward complex alternative assets, the pressure on legacy reporting providers to match the speed of AI-driven platforms is intensifying. The industry now faces a critical question: can automated data orchestration fully replace the manual reconciliation processes that have long defined family office operations? This move indicates that for platforms to win, they must pair advanced AI tools with the institutional-grade operational stability typically associated with much larger financial incumbents.

Original Press Release

Masttro, the global operating system for complex wealth, today announced the appointment of Andrew Peddar as Chief Operating Officer. Peddar will oversee the company’s Data Operations and Client Experience teams, strengthening the operational support behind its platform and reinforcing Masttro's capacity to deliver AI-powered wealth solutions to a growing, cross-border client base.

Peddar brings more than 25 years of experience in financial technology and enterprise software, with expertise in investment data, analytics and reporting. He will report to CEO Jay McNamara and work closely with the team at Masttro’s New York City headquarters. The appointment comes as Masttro reports 43% growth in annual recurring revenue over the past 18 months. Since the start of 2025, Masttro has grown its workforce by 20%, with employees now based in seven countries, serving clients in over 40 markets globally.

“As Masttro grows, it is critical that we ensure the quality of our data and the support our clients receive grows with it,” said McNamara. “It is also essential that our tools and support keep pace with our growth. Andrew has spent his career connecting technology, operations and client service in complex financial software businesses. He will help us scale those functions together, so clients can rely on Masttro as their portfolios and reporting needs evolve.”

Peddar previously served as Group Chief Operating Officer of StatPro, where he led commercial, client-facing and delivery functions across North America, Europe and Asia-Pacific. He later joined Broadridge Financial Solutions as Vice President and General Manager, overseeing a global financial technology business serving major financial institutions. Most recently, he was Chief Revenue Officer at Next Matter, an AI and data orchestration platform serving asset and wealth managers.

**Strengthening data operations and client support**

Peddar will oversee Masttro’s global operations, onboarding and client experience functions, bringing these teams together to deliver a seamless and consistent experience across the client lifecycle. His focus will be on strengthening collaboration, improving operational efficiency and ensuring that service quality, data reliability and client outcomes remain central as the business scales.

Central to this approach is Masttro’s commitment to working closely with clients as long-term partners, understanding their evolving needs and incorporating their feedback into ongoing platform innovation. This collaboration helps ensure Masttro continues to deliver solutions that create lasting value as both its clients and the business grow.

“For clients managing wealth across institutions, entities and asset classes, reliable data and responsive support are essential,” said Peddar. “My focus will be on building upon Masttro’s strong foundation, further strengthening how our teams work together to deliver the connectivity, reporting and support clients depend on every day.”

**Growing global connectivity and AI adoption**

Masttro continues to expand its global data network and AI capabilities, with more than 750 direct custodial connections worldwide and over 300,000 alternative investments tracked on its platform. This extensive network, combined with its integrated AI tools, enables clients to consolidate complex financial data, automate time-consuming processes and gain deeper insights across their holdings. During the first nine months of 2026, clients submitted more than 32,000 requests to the Masttro Intelligence Assistant, while Documents AI and Alternatives AI support document and alternative investment workflows across more than 10,000 funds. Masttro also achieved ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification in September 2026, reinforcing its commitment to data security as its global operations expand.

# Context-enrichment: Masttro appoints Andrew Peddar as Chief Operating Officer
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Masttro — the New York-based "operating system for complex wealth" (portfolio aggregation / reporting for UHNW families, family offices and the institutions serving them) — hired **Andrew Peddar as COO**, effective October 2026. He runs **Data Operations + Client Experience** (onboarding, client service, delivery) and reports to CEO **Jay McNamara**. This is a single senior-exec operational hire, not a round, product or deal. ([masttro.com](https://masttro.com/insights/masttro-appoints-andrew-peddar-chief-operating-officer), [fintechfutures.com](https://www.fintechfutures.com/job-cuts-new-hires/us-wealthtech-masttro-names-andrew-peddar-as-new-coo))

**Why structured this way / what it reveals:** the remit is deliberately *operations + client experience*, not product or GTM — i.e. the role exists to industrialise **data reliability and onboarding** as volume scales, the classic choke-point for an aggregation platform whose value is "one clean real-time view across 750+ custodians and 300k+ alts." The PR anchors to flattering, cherry-picked stats: **"43% ARR growth over 18 months"** (growth-rate, not absolute ARR — the absolute number is withheld, so you can't size the business), "20% headcount growth since start of 2025," "40+ markets," "750+ direct custodial connections," "300k+ alts tracked," "32,000 Intelligence Assistant requests in 9M26," "10,000+ funds" across Documents AI / Alternatives AI, and **ISO/IEC 27001:2022 cert (Sept 2026)**. None of these are independently audited; all are self-reported. The genuinely load-bearing fact is small: a scaling-stage private wealthtech is adding enterprise operational muscle. (analysis)

## [1] Competitors / peers
Direct comps in UHNW/family-office aggregation and reporting:
- **Addepar** — the category gorilla: ~1,400 client firms, 60 countries, **~$9T on platform** (40%+ alts), **$230M Series G at $3.25B** (2025). ([wealthtechstrategy.com](https://www.wealthtechstrategy.com/post/addepar-raises-230m-series-g-at-3-25b-valuation-a-private-credit-milestone-wealthtech-signal)) See [[Addepar launches ADX data exchange built on Databricks]].
- **Arch** — alts data aggregation, ~800 portals, **$500B+** on platform, **$52M Series B** (2025). ([aleta.io](https://aleta.io/addepar-alternatives)) See [[Arch raises $52 million led by Oak HC FT]]. Note: Masttro and Arch have *partnered* on UHNW/family-office data (familywealthreport), so Arch is partly complement, partly comp.
- **Canoe Intelligence** — alts document/data automation, now **acquired by Bloomberg** (2026, rumoured ~20x revenue, ~$750M–$1B). See [[Bloomberg completes Canoe Intelligence acquisition]].
- Adjacent: Wealth.com (estate), iCapital (alts marketplace), CircleBlack (advisor aggregation — see [[CircleBlack appoints Vikram Chugh as CEO]]).

**Why the lay of the land / second-order:** the category is **consolidating around data + alts** — Bloomberg buying Canoe, Addepar launching a data exchange (ADX) — because the durable moat is the **custodial-connection graph + clean alts data**, not the UI. Masttro's $43M total raised (2023) is an order of magnitude below Addepar's war-chest; it competes on the "complex wealth / alternatives depth" niche rather than scale. The COO hire is a *parity* move (every scaling peer professionalises ops), not a *lead* move. (analysis)

## [2] Company history / fit
Founded **2010** by operators inside a multigenerational single-family office; bootstrapped until its **first and only disclosed raise: $43M growth equity led by FTV Capital, with Citi Ventures (March 2023)** — pitched as its North America push. ([ftvcapital.com](https://ftvcapital.com/2023/masttro-secures-43-million-growth-equity-investment-led-by-ftv-capital/), [institutionalinvestor.com](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bdjwejr10tkktxabwtfk/ria-intel/wealthtech-platform-masttro-raises-43-million-from-ftv-capital-citi)) **Jay McNamara** (ex-Morningstar/S&P/MSCI data-business leader) became CEO in early 2025; since then the narrative has shifted founder-led → professionalised-management. Recent product cadence: **Cash Projection Hub** (Aug 2026), **Truewind AI integration** for UHNW accounting (Sept 2026). Pricing is **flat, not AUM-based** — a deliberate wedge against Addepar's AUM-scaling fees.

**Why the company acts this way:** an outside CEO (2025) brings in an outside COO (2026) — a textbook **founder-company → scale-up** transition, almost certainly **investor-driven (FTV)** ahead of either a larger Series B-equivalent or an eventual exit. With one modest round four years old, Masttro must prove **operational durability and net retention** to justify a growth round at a decent multiple. (hypothesis)

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty of the event: essentially zero** — it's a hire. The *interesting* signal is Masttro's self-reported traction: 43% ARR growth, 40+ markets, 750+ custodian connections, ISO 27001. But every figure is **self-reported and unaudited**, absolute ARR/revenue/client count is withheld, and "43% over 18 months" (~27% annualised) is **solid-but-not-hypergrowth** for SaaS — arguably modest given the alts-wealthtech tailwind.

**Where the margin sits / what breaks it (analysis):** Masttro's value-add is the **direct-custodial-connection graph + alts coverage** — the same moat Addepar and Arch are racing to deepen and that Bloomberg just paid up for (Canoe). The risk: data-plumbing can be **commoditised or disintermediated** if a Bloomberg/Addepar-scale incumbent bundles it cheaper, squeezing a flat-priced niche player. The COO's job (onboarding + data reliability) is exactly the **retention/gross-margin lever** that decides whether 43% growth compounds or churns. Hiring a StatPro/Broadridge operator signals they know the next battle is **execution, not features**. Peddar's most recent role (CRO at Next Matter) was revenue, not ops — a slight mismatch worth flagging.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect Masttro to keep shipping AI data-ops features (Documents AI, Alternatives AI, Intelligence Assistant) and likely tee up a **growth round or strategic exit** within ~12–18 months (one 2023 round, new CEO+COO = classic pre-raise/pre-exit grooming). (hypothesis) Market backdrop is **favourable but consolidating**: UHNW alts allocations rising, incumbents (Bloomberg, Addepar) buying/building the data layer.

**Counterintuitive second-order:** the same consolidation that validates Masttro's niche also **shrinks its runway to stay independent** — as Bloomberg/Addepar absorb the data-aggregation layer, a sub-scale specialist's best outcome tilts toward **being acquired** rather than winning share. A flat-fee pricing model that undercuts AUM-based rivals is attractive to clients but **caps revenue upside per account**, which can depress the multiple relative to Addepar. (analysis)

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Sub-vertical: wealthtech portfolio-aggregation / reporting software for UHNW individuals and family offices — consolidating data across custodians, entities and asset classes, with a heavy skew to alternatives (PE, hedge funds, direct deals). Market size: family-office *software* TAM is small and estimate-dependent — ~$1.2bn in 2026 growing to ~$2.3bn by 2035 (CAGR ~8%) per Research Nester, or ~$1.8bn in 2025 at ~11% CAGR per Verified Market Reports (as of Oct 2026, via secondary market-research aggregators — treat as directional, methodologies differ; `[UNSOURCED]` for a single consensus TAM). The broader family-office market is ~$21.6bn (2026). Structure: fragmented and specialist — several sub-$1bn vendors each owning a niche (aggregation/visibility vs institutional analytics vs alternatives-document extraction) rather than one dominant suite; entry barriers are the custodial-connection network (Masttro cites 750+ feeds) + alternatives data coverage, both of which compound with scale (network/data moat). Drivers + "why now": (1) secular growth of the UHNW population (~+4.2% in 2024) and the shift of their portfolios toward hard-to-aggregate alternatives, which legacy retail/institutional tools handle poorly; (2) the AI document-extraction wave turning manual reconciliation of PE/hedge statements into an automatable workflow — the thing every vendor here is racing on.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs (SaaS): ARR growth, NRR, assets-on-platform / custodial-feed coverage (reach proxy, not revenue), funds/documents processed. Masttro disclosed: 43% ARR growth over 18 months, 750+ custodial connections, 300k+ alternatives tracked, 10,000+ funds, 32,000 Intelligence-Assistant requests in 9M26, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (Sep 2026). It does NOT disclose ARR $, NRR, CAC or client count beyond "400+ family offices" (per vendor marketing) — those are `[UNSOURCED]`. Key players & basis of competition: **Addepar** (institutional analytics/risk benchmark, scale), **Canoe Intelligence** and **Arch** (alternatives document/data workflows), plus FundCount, Aleta, Eton, Asora. Competition is on product depth + data coverage, not price. Recent moves: Bloomberg completed its **Canoe Intelligence** acquisition (Oct 2026) — a strategic-buyer consolidation of the alternatives-data layer; **Arch** raised $52M Series B (Sep 2025, Oak HC/FT); **Addepar** raised $230M Series G at a $3.25bn valuation (May 2025). Masttro's position: niche specialist in "complex wealth" visibility/aggregation — ahead on breadth of custodial + alternatives coverage, behind Addepar on institutional analytics scale (analysis). Moat = custodial-feed network + alternatives data (switching costs once a family office's full picture lives on the platform).

**Risk flags.**
