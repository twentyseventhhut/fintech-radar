Российский рынок средств разработки и управления данными к 2031 г. по сравнению с 2025 г. вырастет в 2,3 раза до 198 млрд руб. Такой прогноз содержится в исследовании аналитической компании iKS-Consulting, которое есть в распоряжении «Ведомостей». Аналитики ожидают, что этот сегмент будет расти быстрее остальных крупных классов программного обеспечения (ПО) – в среднем на 15,1% в год.
Сегмент ПО информационной безопасности будет ежегодно расти на 13,1%, системного и инфраструктурного ПО – на 10,5%, прикладного – на 9,5%. Весь рынок ПО в 2025–2031 гг. будет прибавлять в среднем 11,2% ежегодно, оценивают авторы исследования.

# Context-enrichment: Vedomosti — AI adoption lifts demand for corporate data protection
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**Type:** market-trend / research-report (analyst forecast relayed by Vedomosti), not a company deal. Subject: Russia. **Freshness:** FRESH — first fintech-corpus coverage of this specific iKS-Consulting study (published ~2026-10-06); no prior note carries these figures.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Vedomosti relayed a new iKS-Consulting study (first full-scale look at the segment, per iKSMEDIA). Headline numbers:
- Russian market for **data-development and data-management software** grows **2.3x vs 2025, to ~198 bln RUB by 2031**, CAGR **~15.1%/yr** — the fastest-growing large software class.
- **Information-security software: +13.1%/yr**; system/infrastructure SW +10.5%; application SW +9.5%; total SW market +11.2%/yr over 2025–2031.
- Framing: AI adoption drives demand for BOTH data management AND data protection.

De-PR caveats:
- The 198-bln figure and "AI drives it" headline are about the **data-development/management** class, NOT the data-protection/DLP class specifically. The note's English title conflates the two. The infosec-SW class grows SLOWER (13.1%) than the data class (15.1%). So "AI lifts demand for corporate data protection" is the softer, secondary claim; the hard number belongs to data tooling.
- A single-vendor analyst forecast to 2031 (5+ years out) "in Vedomosti's possession" — directional, not audited. Treat CAGRs as vendor marketing-grade. **(analysis)**
- Why framed this way: iKS positions itself on a segment it calls under-studied ("first full-scale research"), and the AI hook is the fashionable demand narrative — but the real structural driver in RU is regulatory (turnover fines) + import substitution, which the clipped note omits. → The press frame ("AI") understates the harder driver (law + forced domestic migration).

## [1] Competitors / peers (the RU data-protection / infosec landscape)
This is a market piece, so "peers" = the vendors who capture the forecast spend.
- **DLP / data-leak:** InfoWatch (long-time RU/CIS DLP leader, also named in global DLP vendor lists), Solar (Rostelecom's Solar / Dozor), SearchInform, Garda. Demand for DLP reportedly +35–40% after turnover-fine law; DLP ~10% of RU infosec market, forecast to ~17% by 2027; segment ~17 bln RUB (Indeed/"Индид" cited by CNews, 2026-10).
- **Broad infosec / SOC:** Positive Technologies (said to dominate a ~180-bln-RUB RU cybersecurity market), Kaspersky, BI.ZONE, Group-IB/F.A.C.C.T. RU cybersecurity market ~USD 7.75 bln (2026), CAGR ~8–11%.
- **AI-specific protection:** a NEW micro-segment — RU market for securing LLM/AI workflows (chatbot leaks, training-set compromise) forecast to pass **~1 bln RUB by end-2026** (InvestFuture/anti-malware). Tiny but the genuine "AI" angle.
- Position of the item: it is the macro wrapper over all of the above; it does not pick winners. Second-order: the data-MANAGEMENT spend (Arenadata, Postgres Pro, Yandex/VK data platforms, Base/Базис on infra) grows faster than the SECURITY spend — i.e., AI first drives data-plumbing capex, with protection as the lagging follow-on. **(analysis)**

## [2] Fit / why this appears now
Not a company, so "history" = the macro trajectory:
- Structural pressure 1 — **regulation:** FZ-420 (30.11.2024) added turnover fines to the 152-FZ regime from **30 May 2025**: repeat leaks 1–3% of annual revenue, floor 20 mln / cap 500 mln RUB; large leaks 10–15 mln; biometric 15–20 mln; non-notification up to 3 mln. This is the hard demand driver — firms buy DLP partly "to prove to the regulator that measures were taken," not only to stop leaks. **(cited)**
- Structural pressure 2 — **import substitution:** Dec-2024 decree + exit of CrowdStrike/Palo Alto/Fortinet forced migration to domestic stacks → captive, structurally protected RU vendors (Positive, Kaspersky, Solar). Links to the corpus talent squeeze (see [[RBK Russia faces shortage of 42,000 cybersecurity specialists]]): demand is real but supply-constrained on engineers.
- Structural pressure 3 — **AI:** LLMs in business processes create new data-exposure surfaces (chatbot leaks, training data) → net-new protection demand, but currently sub-1-bln RUB, so a 2031 story not a 2026 one. → The iKS report bundles a decade-long regulatory/sovereignty trend under a fresh "AI" label. **(analysis)**

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- Novelty of the EVENT: low-medium. It is a forecast, not a deal or a product. The 198-bln / 15.1% numbers are new to the corpus (fresh), but the underlying trend (RU data-sovereignty + infosec boom) is well established.
- Real traction behind the trend (the citable part, NOT the forecast): DLP customer count +25% H1-2026, revenue +30%, full-year guidance >40% (secpost/CISO survey); data-leak-protection demand +40% post-fines (www1.ru). These are observed, not projected — the credible core.
- Who captures the margin: domestic security/data vendors in a near-closed market. The multiple/durability rests on the FINE regime and import ban persisting; if either eased (unlikely near-term) the "compliance-forced" portion of demand softens. The AI-security slice is where genuine NEW value could form, but it is still <1 bln RUB. **(analysis)**

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Direction: all cited sources agree — strong double-digit growth through 2027–2031; RU cybersecurity market reportedly approaching a "trillion-ruble" frame in bullish 2026 trend pieces (kubnews), more conservative CAGRs (8–11%) from Western trackers (Mordor/Grand View).
- Counterintuitive second-order: growth is driven more by COMPELLED spend (fines + no foreign alternative) than by organic security maturity → it is defensive/mandated capex, which makes revenue durable for incumbents but also means the "AI tailwind" narrative is partly a re-label of a sanctions/regulation tailwind. A thaw in either driver, not an AI slowdown, is the real downside trigger. **(hypothesis)**
- Risk to the number: single analyst, 6-year horizon, RUB-denominated (inflation/FX flatters nominal growth) → read the CAGR, not the 198-bln headline.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is not a company story — it is an iKS-Consulting market-forecast report (via Vedomosti, 2026-10-06) on two adjacent Russian software segments: data-management/development tools and information security (IS).
- Size/growth (data-management & dev tools): per iKS-Consulting via Vedomosti (2026-10-06), the segment grows 2.3x from 2025 to **₽198bn by 2031**, ~**+15.1%/yr** — fastest of the big software classes. IS software: **+13.1%/yr**; system/infra: +10.5%; application: +9.5%; whole SW market ~+11.2%/yr (same source). Figures confirmed across RIA/Prime/Kommersant reprints of the same iKS release.
- Adjacent sourced anchor — the broader IS/cybersec market (CSR forecast, Nov 2025, via Kommersant/TASS): **₽364bn in 2025 (+16.1%)** → **~₽968bn–₽1trn by 2030–31**, ~+21%/yr; foreign-vendor share forecast to fall **below 5% by 2030** (from ~7% end-2024, ~11% in 2023 per TAdviser). Note the inconsistency: CSR's whole-market +21%/yr is faster than iKS's IS-software +13.1%/yr — different scope (CSR includes services/hardware; iKS is software-only), so don't conflate.
- Structure: domestically-oriented and consolidating around a few scaled Russian vendors; high entry barriers now are regulatory (import-substitution mandates, certification) rather than pure tech. Russian suppliers hold >94% of the IS-tools market (TAdviser).
- Drivers + "why now": (1) **AI adoption** — data must be prepared, access-controlled and protected for model use (the explicit iKS thesis); (2) **import substitution / tech-sovereignty** — the structural tailwind that handed the whole domestic install base to local vendors post-2022. Second-order effect: demand is policy-driven, not purely organic, so it is partly a one-time stock-replacement wave that normalizes once substitution completes.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: revenue growth vs market (2x market is the benchmark Positive sets), shipments/"отгрузки", recurring/licence mix, R&D headcount. There is no protagonist company in this item — it is a sizing report — so positioning is of the sector's named leaders:
- **Positive Technologies** — FY2025 revenue **₽30.9bn (+26%)**, IFRS net profit ~₽7.3bn (2x); FY2026 shipments guided ₽40–45bn (TAdviser). Grew ~2x the market rate.
- **InfoWatch** — DLP leader among Russian vendors; FY revenue **₽3.77bn** (down y/y, per TAdviser) — much smaller scale and shrinking, a caution against treating "the market is booming" as uniform.
- Others named across sources: Kaspersky, GC Solar (Solar Dozor DLP), Group-IB/BI.ZONE, SearchInform, Zecurion. DLP is ~10% of the IS market. Basis of competition: certification + regulatory fit + distribution into state/enterprise, not price.
- Recent moves (dated, in-base): [[FRII and Metascan launch 300M-ruble cybersecurity fund]] (2026-07) — ₽300m fund for IS startups, signalling capital rotating into the segment; [[RBK Russia faces shortage of 42,000 cybersecurity specialists]] (2026-10) — a **42k-specialist deficit** = the key execution constraint on realizing the forecast growth.

- [[RBK Russia faces shortage of 42,000 cybersecurity specialists]] — labour-supply datapoint, no valuation.

Trading multiple (only verifiable listed name): **Positive Technologies EV/Revenue** — market cap alone is not a free, dated figure here, so a clean EV/Rev is **no data / [UNSOURCED]**; what is sourced is growth (+26%) and profit (₽7.3bn). Distribution not computed — only one listed comparable; qualitative read: Positive is the scaled, profitable bellwether, InfoWatch the sub-scale laggard — the segment average masks a wide dispersion.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Policy-dependence / one-time substitution wave** — much of the growth is import-substitution stock-replacement, not organic demand; once foreign share nears the forecast <5% floor, the structural tailwind fades and growth should decelerate toward GDP+IT-budget rates. Over-extrapolating the 2.3x/+15% headline risks pricing in a one-off as a perpetuity.
2. **Talent bottleneck** — a 42k-specialist shortfall (2026-10) caps delivery capacity; a forecast that assumes +13–21%/yr may be supply-constrained, inflating prices but not necessarily installed capability.
3. **Forecast-source & scope fragility** — the headline is a single analyst house (iKS-Consulting) on a paywalled report, and the IS numbers differ materially between iKS (+13.1%/yr SW) and CSR (+21%/yr whole market). Treat the specific ₽198bn/₽968bn figures as single-source, scope-dependent, not triangulated hard data.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The signal is that **data-governance/DLP is being re-rated from a compliance line-item to an AI-enablement layer** — "you can't deploy AI on ungoverned data." Falsifiable thesis: if AI is the real driver (not just import-substitution), data-management SW should keep outgrowing IS SW even *after* foreign share bottoms out (~2029–30). What to watch / trigger: Positive Technologies' FY2026 shipments landing in the ₽40–45bn guide, and whether sub-scale vendors like InfoWatch return to growth — divergence would confirm consolidation; continued InfoWatch decline alongside sector "growth" would confirm the boom is concentrated, not broad.
