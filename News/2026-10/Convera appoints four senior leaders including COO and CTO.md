Convera appoints four senior leaders to accelerate its global money movement platform
Senior appointments strengthen Convera’s ability to help customers move money globally with greater speed, visibility, and control. 
 
 
 
 SEATTLE and LONDON, [October 13, 2026] — Convera, a global leader in commercial payments, today announced four senior leadership appointments: Mike Cahill as EVP and Chief Operating Officer; Jon Southall as SVP and Chief Technology Officer; Tui Allen as SVP and Chief Product Officer; and Chris Steinig as Chief Information Officer. Together, they bring extensive experience in building and scaling global payments , product, technology and operational platforms. They will lead the execution of Convera’s roadmap for an integrated global money movement platform that combines cross-border payments , FX risk management and multi-currency capabilities for businesses.
Convera’s strategy is built on a simple premise: as agentic commerce emerges and volatility persists , businesses operating across borders need integrated solutions. They need to send, receive and hold money in the currencies they trade in, manage FX exposure at the point of payment, and route transactions across the most appropriate payment rail for speed, cost and visibility.
“Businesses don’t just need faster or less expensive cross-border payments. They need to know where their money is, what it is worth and how to protect it as it moves,” said Patrick Gauthier, Group CEO at Convera. “That is the opportunity in front of Convera. Our global reach, FX expertise, regulatory depth and customer relationships enable us to solve that problem in a way few providers can. Mike, Jon, Tui and Chris are proven leaders with the experience, judgement and execution discipline to help us deliver for customers. Each brings a distinct perspective, and together they give us the leadership depth to advance our roadmap for integrated cross-border money movement.”
Advancing customer-led execution
 Mike Cahill has been appointed EVP and Chief Operating Officer, leading Convera’s combined Product, Technology and Operations organization. Since joining Convera in June 2026 as Interim Chief Product and Technology Officer, he has focused on bringing these functions together to deliver a more connected customer experience. Cahill has scaled mission-critical financial platforms and led complex transformations at Early Warning Services, the company behind Zelle, as well as at PayPal, Boku and American Express.
 Jon Southall joins as SVP and Chief Technology Officer from PayPal, where he managed the Large Enterprise Platform, which supports more than $1.5 trillion in annual transaction volume. He has also built scaled payment platforms at Boku and Braintree. His experience in high-volume infrastructure will help Convera deliver faster, more reliable and more visible money movement for customers.
 Tui Allen joins as SVP and Chief Product Officer from Ticketmaster/Live Nation, where she helped modernize product experiences at scale. She previously built new solutions at Shopify. Allen brings experience translating complex customer needs into scalable products that are easier to use, integrate and adopt.
 Chris Steinig has been appointed Chief Information Officer after leading Convera’s technology operations. A 25-year veteran of the company, he brings deep knowledge of Convera’s systems, infrastructure and operating environment. Steinig will connect platform modernization, enterprise technology and operational resilience to help ensure the company delivers its roadmap with the reliability and control customers expect.
“This team brings complementary strengths across product, technology and operations, backed by experience delivering at global scale,” said Mike Cahill, EVP and Chief Operating Officer at Convera. “Our focus is disciplined execution: translating customer needs into products, modernizing the technology that supports them and delivering consistently in every market we serve. That is how we will make cross-border money movement simpler, more connected and more valuable for our customers.”
These appointments build on Convera’s continued investment in technology, product delivery and commercial capabilities. This includes global currency accounts and APIs that allow banks, fintechs and business platforms to embed Convera’s payments and FX risk management capabilities into their own customer experiences.

# Context-enrichment: Convera appoints four senior leaders (COO, CTO, CPO, CIO)
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-13 (SEATTLE/LONDON) Convera — the ex-Western Union Business Solutions B2B FX/cross-border payments business — named four senior leaders:
- **Mike Cahill — EVP & COO**, now running a combined Product + Technology + Operations org. NOT a fresh outside hire: he joined Convera in **June 2026 as Interim Chief Product & Technology Officer**, so this is a promotion/confirmation + mandate expansion. Prior: Early Warning Services (Zelle), PayPal, Boku, Amex.
- **Jon Southall — SVP & CTO**, from PayPal (ran the Large Enterprise Platform, ">$1.5T annual volume"); also Boku, Braintree.
- **Tui Allen — SVP & CPO**, from Ticketmaster/Live Nation, earlier Shopify. Note: a consumer/commerce product background, not core B2B-FX.
- **Chris Steinig — CIO**, a **25-year company veteran** promoted internally (was leading Convera's tech operations).

**Why structured this way / what it reveals:** This is a reorg dressed as four hires. Two of the four are internal elevations (Cahill from interim, Steinig a lifer); only Southall and Allen are genuinely new external talent. The real signal is the **consolidation of Product+Tech+Ops under one COO (Cahill)** — a classic move to break silos and force "one platform" delivery after a period of fragmented execution. The PR framing ("accelerate its global money movement platform", "agentic commerce emerges") is aspirational cover for what is fundamentally org plumbing. No revenue, customer, or product-milestone number is attached to the announcement — the tell that this is a capability/execution story, not a commercial one.

## [1] Competitors / peers
B2B cross-border FX/payments is a crowded, consolidating field:
- **Corpay** (NYSE: CPAY) — the public bellwether, aggressive M&A (BVNK stablecoin wallets May 2026, Kinexys/JPM blockchain FX), board/exec moves (see [[Corpay appoints European Operating Executive David Bunch to its Board of Directors]]). Public-company scale and stablecoin rails push.
- **Ebury** (Santander-backed, ~£2B IPO ambitions) — Lumon FX takeover attempt, geographic expansion, repeated capital raises ([[Santander's Ebury eyes £100m Lumon FX takeover]], [[Ebury launches Ebury Connect for cross-border solutions]]).
- **Currencycloud, Moneycorp, Okoora** — embedded-FX/API peers.
- Convera's own ecosystem moves: [[Convera taps Ripple for stablecoin cross-border payments]] (Apr 2026) and [[dLocal partners with Convera for cross-border payments]] (Dec 2025).

**Position:** Convera is large by turnover (~$190B/yr, 140+ currencies, 30k+ customers) but structurally a **private, PE-owned (Goldfinch/Baupost) legacy carve-out** racing to modernize while Corpay compounds publicly and Ebury chases an IPO. **Why the lay of the land is this way:** the category's margin is migrating from commodity FX spread toward embedded APIs + value-added treasury/risk + (nascent) stablecoin rails. Everyone is hiring platform/engineering leadership from PayPal/Shopify/Braintree because the fight is now about **developer-grade infrastructure**, not sales desks. Convera bringing in PayPal's large-enterprise-platform CTO is on-trend but **catching up**, not leading.

## [2] Company history / fit
Convera = Western Union Business Solutions, carved out in a ~$910M deal (closed fully July 2023) to Goldfinch Partners + Baupost, CEO **Patrick Gauthier** (ex-Amazon Pay, ex-PayPal). Trajectory: legacy WU corporate-FX desk business → repositioning as "global money movement platform" with global currency accounts (60+ currencies) and REST APIs for banks/fintechs/platforms to embed. ~$170B turnover 2024 → ~$190B 2025 (company figures, unverified independently).

**Why the company acts this way:** A carve-out of a mature, commodity-take-rate FX business has one structural problem — **declining spread economics and a relationship-sales cost base**. To justify private-equity value creation and an eventual exit, Convera must re-rate from "FX broker multiple" to "payments-infrastructure multiple", which requires software/platform delivery it historically lacked. Hence: interim CPTO (June 2026) → now a permanent COO owning Product+Tech+Ops, plus a PayPal-scale CTO. The hires are the **execution arm of a de-risking-before-exit thesis**, logically consistent with the Ripple/stablecoin and dLocal/emerging-markets-payout partnerships that broaden the rail story.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: low.** Nothing here is a product, a customer, or a number. It is a leadership announcement — the most common and lowest-signal fintech news type. Compare [[Corpay appoints European Operating Executive David Bunch to its Board of Directors]] and [[Send Payments appoints Jason McClintock chief commercial officer]]: leadership items rarely move the thesis.

**Where value-add could be real (analysis):** The one non-trivial element is the **structural consolidation** (one COO over P+T+O). If it actually dissolves the product/eng/ops silos typical of a WU legacy carve-out, that is a prerequisite for shipping the embedded-FX platform faster. But "could enable faster delivery" is a hypothesis; there is **zero traction evidence** (no shipped product, no API adoption metric, no customer win tied to this team). **Who captures the margin:** in embedded FX, margin accrues to whoever owns the rail + the point-of-payment hedging + the developer surface. Convera owning its bank network and regulatory licenses is a moat, but the stablecoin/agentic-commerce rails it name-checks are controlled by others (Ripple as partner, card networks for agentic payments) — so a leadership team alone does not change who captures the margin.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch for: (a) actual API/currency-account product launches and *named* embedded-platform customers under Cahill; (b) whether Convera's private revenue growth (claimed record H1 in prior years) sustains; (c) PE-exit signals (IPO or sale) — strong platform leadership is typically assembled 12–24 months pre-exit. **Why the market goes this way:** B2B cross-border is consolidating and re-rating toward infrastructure; volatility + "agentic commerce" are the industry's current narrative hooks (Convera explicitly leans on both). **Counterintuitive second-order:** loading a legacy carve-out with PayPal/Shopify platform talent raises the burn and cultural-integration risk; if the modernization stalls, the same hires become the evidence of an expensive, slow transformation rather than a fast one. For a private, non-reporting issuer, this item is **informative but low-weight** — a milestone on a transformation path, not a result.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** B2B cross-border payments are projected to grow from ~$39.3tn to ~$56.1tn of flow by 2030 (+43%), per FXC Intelligence via BVNK (as of 2024-25). Revenue pool from cross-border payments is cited at ~$280bn by 2030 (per Convera's own blog, promotional — treat as directional). Structure: fragmented at the long tail (hundreds of FX/payments brokers) but consolidating at the top via acquisition (Corpay/Alpha, Santander/Ebury). Value sits in the FX spread + float, not the rail; entry barriers are regulatory licensing across many jurisdictions and correspondent-banking access, not technology. Drivers / "why now": (1) persistent FX volatility pushing corporates toward hedging-at-payment; (2) "agentic commerce" + stablecoin rails (Convera's own Ripple tie-up, 2026-04 [[Convera taps Ripple for stablecoin cross-border payments]]) reshaping settlement economics. Second-order: as speed/transparency commoditize, the defensible margin migrates to FX risk management and multi-currency account balances — exactly where Convera is repositioning.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/turnover, FX take rate (bps on volume), client count, net revenue retention. Key players: Corpay Cross-Border (listed, largest), Santander-backed Ebury, Convera (ex-Western Union Business Solutions), plus Airwallex/Wise (SMB-digital), iBanFirst/Lumon (Europe). Basis of competition: FX pricing, corridor/currency breadth, regulatory licensing footprint, and increasingly platform/API embedding. Recent moves: Corpay bought Alpha Group (~$2.2bn EV, 2025) and took Mastercard's $300m stake in its cross-border unit; Ebury raised ~£550m (Centerbridge-led, Santander 55%, 2026-04 [[Santander to invest $67 million in Ebury via funding rounds]]) and targets a ~£2bn London IPO [[Ebury revives London IPO targeting £2B valuation]]. Convera scale: ~$190bn turnover in 2025 with double-digit revenue growth, >$0.5bn revenue, 140+ currencies to 200+ countries (per Convera disclosures/press). Position: Convera is a scaled incumbent (volume on par with or above Corpay's cross-border unit) but catching up on platform/tech — this leadership refresh (COO/CTO/CPO/CIO, all 2026 hires ex-PayPal/Zelle/Shopify) is itself the tell that product/tech modernization lagged. Moat `(analysis)`: scale + regulatory licensing + 40-yr FX book (switching costs for hedging corporates); weak on network effects vs digital-native peers.

**Comps & multiples.** Private; no public market cap. Qualitative/peer comparison:
- **Corpay (CPAY, listed):** mkt cap ~$26.2bn, FY25 rev $4.53bn (+13.9%); implied P/S ≈ $26.2bn / $4.53bn = ~5.8x (whole company). Cross-border unit EV ~$10.7bn @ ~20x forward EBITDA (per Apr-2025 Mastercard-stake disclosure), ~$13.0bn EV after Alpha. [UNSOURCED] clean segment revenue denominator → segment EV/Rev not computed.
- **Ebury (private):** ~£2bn targeted IPO valuation; serves 27,000 businesses, >30% rev p.a. growth since 2020. Round valuation, not market cap; revenue undisclosed → EV/Rev "no data".
- **Convera:** last public price was the 2021 WUBS purchase at ~$910m. Against ~$190bn TPV + >$0.5bn revenue today, a mark anywhere near Corpay's cross-border unit (~$10-13bn) would be a steep step-up; absent a fresh round, Convera valuation = "no data". Distribution not computed (<3 comparable disclosed revenue multiples) → qualitative only. Flag: Ebury's ~5x+ revenue step-up and Corpay's ~20x EBITDA mark look rich vs a slower-growth, PE-owned ex-WU asset — a comp mismatch, not a read on Convera itself.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Execution / integration on legacy tech.** Four simultaneous senior hires (COO, CTO, CPO, CIO) plus a prior interim CPTO signal the platform modernization off Western Union infrastructure (Temenos/AWS migration) is not finished; re-platforming a $190bn-volume book while swapping leadership is material operational risk — why: slippage hits client retention in a market where rivals ship product fast.
2. **Disintermediation of the FX-spread margin.** Convera's own Ripple/stablecoin move and the broader shift to real-time rails compress the FX spread + float that is its core P&L; why: if settlement commoditizes faster than Convera monetizes risk-management/accounts, the net take rate erodes structurally.
3. **PE ownership / exit overhang.** Owned by Goldfinch Partners + The Baupost Group since 2021 with no IPO path disclosed while peers (Ebury) move to list and Corpay consolidates; why: a leveraged, sponsor-owned asset competing against a listed consolidator and a bank-backed rival may be capital-constrained on M&A and pricing.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The hire wave reframes Convera from a WU carve-out into a would-be "integrated money-movement platform" — a defensive re-positioning, not a re-rating. Watch next: whether these appointments precede a funding round / IPO file or a sale to a consolidator (Corpay-style). Falsifiable thesis: if Convera ships a unified multi-currency-account + hedging product within ~12 months and holds double-digit revenue growth, the modernization narrative is real; it breaks if churn rises or a fresh raise lands below the implied scale-adjusted mark of peers.
