Arab Sea Agrees to Sell SeaPay for 35M SAR
 Riyadh, Saudi Arabia — Arab Sea Information Systems Company has signed a Memorandum of Understanding with PayTabs Group (Growth Makers Company) to sell its entire 100% stake in Arab Sea Financial Company (SeaPay) for 35 million SAR. The MoU was signed on October 22, 2026. SeaPay is a wholly owned subsidiary of Arab Sea Information Systems Company.
Deal Details
The proposed transaction covers a full ownership transfer of SeaPay from Arab Sea Information Systems Company to PayTabs Group. The agreed consideration stands at exactly 35 million SAR. The MoU runs for six months from the signing date or until both parties execute a definitive Sale and Purchase Agreement (SPA), whichever comes first. No related parties are involved in the transaction.
Before any deal closes, both sides must complete a due diligence process. After that, they will negotiate and sign a binding SPA. Finally, the transaction requires regulatory approvals and no-objection confirmations from the relevant authorities. Arab Sea Information Systems Company has committed to disclosing material developments as they arise.
Why It Matters
Arab Sea Information Systems Company is divesting its fintech payments subsidiary, SeaPay, signaling a potential strategic refocus on its core information systems business.
PayTabs Group, an established regional payments player, adds a licensed Saudi fintech entity to its portfolio through this deal.
The 35 million SAR price tag sets a concrete valuation benchmark for SeaPay ahead of formal due diligence.
The MoU imposes no immediate financial impact on Arab Sea Information Systems Company — the accounting effect triggers only upon closing.
What This Means for Investors
This remains a preliminary agreement, not a done deal. Due diligence, SPA execution, and regulatory sign-off can each delay or derail the transaction.
If the sale closes at 35 million SAR, Arab Sea Information Systems Company will recognize a financial impact — positive or negative — depending on SeaPay’s carrying value on its books.
Investors should watch for the SPA announcement and any regulatory decisions as the clearest signals of deal progress.
The six-month MoU window means any material update should surface before April 2027.

 THE SAUDI STANDARD’S VIEW: ARAB SEA’S SEAPAY EXIT SIGNALS FINTECH CONSOLIDATION IN THE KINGDOM 
The agreement between Arab Sea Information Systems Company and PayTabs Group to transfer full ownership of SeaPay for 35 million SAR is more than a single corporate transaction — it reflects a broader pattern of strategic repositioning and market consolidation reshaping Saudi Arabia’s fintech and payments landscape. Investors should read this deal carefully, because what appears on the surface as a straightforward divestiture carries meaningful implications for capital allocation, sector dynamics, and regulatory maturity.
 • A STRATEGIC PIVOT, NOT A RETREAT 
Arab Sea Information Systems Company’s decision to exit its payments subsidiary points to a deliberate narrowing of focus toward its core information systems competencies. Holding a licensed fintech entity requires sustained investment in compliance, technology, and capital buffers — resources that a parent primarily oriented around information systems may deploy more productively elsewhere. This divestiture, if completed, would sharpen the company’s operational profile and potentially improve return metrics for shareholders.
 • PAYTABS STRENGTHENS ITS LICENSED FOOTPRINT 
For PayTabs Group, acquiring a Saudi-licensed fintech entity like SeaPay represents a meaningful regulatory and operational asset in one of the region’s most competitive payments markets. Obtaining a payments license in Saudi Arabia independently is a time-intensive and resource-heavy process, making an acquisition of an existing licensed entity strategically efficient. This move positions PayTabs to deepen its Saudi market penetration with an established regulatory standing already in place.
 • VALUATION DISCIPLINE AND BALANCE SHEET RISK 
The 35 million SAR consideration establishes a hard valuation reference point for SeaPay ahead of due diligence — a figure the market can now measure against what formal scrutiny ultimately confirms or revises. The financial outcome for Arab Sea Information Systems Company will hinge entirely on SeaPay’s carrying value relative to the sale price, meaning the deal could produce a gain, a loss, or a negligible impact on the income statement. Investors should reserve judgment on the financial benefit until the SPA is signed and the carrying value is disclosed.
 • REGULATORY MILESTONES ARE THE REAL TIMELINE 
The six-month MoU window and the multi-stage closing process — due diligence, SPA execution, regulatory approvals — mean this transaction carries execution risk at each checkpoint. Saudi financial sector regulators maintain rigorous standards for ownership transfers in licensed entities, and no-objection clearances are neither automatic nor instantaneous. Investors tracking this deal should treat regulatory confirmation, not MoU signing, as the definitive measure of deal certainty.
Transactions like this Arab Sea-PayTabs arrangement are a direct expression of Vision 2030’s ambition to build a sophisticated, consolidated, and internationally competitive Saudi financial technology sector. The Kingdom’s push to diversify its economy has deliberately cultivated a fintech ecosystem dense enough to generate organic M&A activity, license transfers, and sector specialization — all signs of a maturing market. As consolidation accelerates among Saudi fintech players, investors who understand which companies are sharpening their focus and which are scaling through acquisition will be better positioned to identify durable value in the Kingdom’s evolving digital economy.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-10-22, Arab Sea Information Systems Company signed a non-binding MoU with PayTabs Group (operating entity "Growth Makers Company") to sell its entire 100% stake in Arab Sea Financial Company — brand name **SeaPay** — for **SAR 35 million (~US$9.3M)**. The MoU runs six months from signing (so through ~April 2027) or until a definitive SPA is executed, whichever is first. Closing is conditional on due diligence, a binding SPA, and regulatory/no-objection approvals. No related parties; no immediate accounting impact until close. Disclosed via Saudi market (Tadawul) announcement, 2026-10-25.

**[1] Who the parties are.**
- **SeaPay (Arab Sea Financial Co.)** — launched 2023, a SAMA-licensed B2B payment gateway providing POS and SoftPoS acceptance, collection and settlement across online and in-person channels. Small, Saudi-only footprint; the price tag (SAR 35M) confirms this is a tuck-in, not a platform deal.
- **PayTabs Group** — Saudi-founded (Abdulaziz Al Jouf, 2014) regional payments infrastructure player, HQ Saudi Arabia, active across MEA. Framed the deal as a way to "diversify revenue streams, improve transaction economics, and reduce dependencies," feeding a stated 2030 target of 200,000 Saudi merchants and US$100B+ in annual processed volume.
- **Arab Sea Information Systems Co.** — the listed parent, divesting fintech to refocus on its core information-systems business.

**[2] Why it matters / strategic logic.** For PayTabs, the real asset is SeaPay's existing **SAMA payments licence** — organically obtaining one is slow and capital-heavy, so buying a licensed entity is a regulatory shortcut to deepen its home-market (Saudi, its largest) presence. For Arab Sea, it's a clean exit from a sub-scale, compliance-heavy fintech unit. The gain/loss on Arab Sea's P&L depends entirely on SeaPay's carrying value vs. the SAR 35M price, undisclosed as of the MoU.

**[3] Pattern — PayTabs' 2026 acquisition streak.** This is PayTabs' **third disclosed 2026 deal**, confirming an aggressive roll-up strategy:
- Apr 2026: acquired UAE checkout startup **TAPn'GO** — see [[PayTabs Group acquires checkout startup TAPn'GO in UAE]].
- Sep 2026: agreed to buy **Amazon Payment Services' MENA operations** for US$100M+ (deal would make PayTabs "the largest MENA payments infrastructure provider," combined ~SAR 150B / ~US$40B annual volume). [No internal note yet — external: fintechfutures.com, wamda.com.]
- Oct 2026: SeaPay MoU (this note).
PayTabs has also been building Saudi/Gulf distribution via partnerships — see [[Doha Bank and PayTabs partner on Qatar digital commerce]] and [[PayTabs partners with Valu and Doha Bank in Qatar]].

**[4] Market backdrop.** Saudi payments is consolidating under Vision 2030. SAMA's payments register held 33 licensed entities as of 2026-09-10 (14 EMIs, 19 payment institutions). Licensed competitors in SeaPay's acquiring/gateway lane include **Geidea**, **HyperPay**, and **Moyasar**; infrastructure peers include Visa's locally hosted Saudi acceptance platform — see [[Paymentology expands in Riyadh with Oracle and AWS integrations]]. The SAR 35M consideration is a notable size reference for a small, 2023-vintage licensed Saudi acquirer.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

**Red-team questions**

1. This is an **MoU, not a binding SPA** — what's the historical completion rate for MENA payments MoUs, and could due diligence reveal SeaPay's licence/book issues that kill or re-price the deal?
2. Is SAR 35M (~$9.3M) actually cheap or expensive? Without SeaPay's revenue, GMV, merchant count, or carrying value, the "valuation benchmark" claim is unverifiable.
3. Does SeaPay's SAMA licence **transfer** automatically on a change of control, or does the acquirer need fresh SAMA no-objection that could take months or be denied?
4. How much of SeaPay's value is the licence vs. an operating business? If merchant volumes are negligible, PayTabs is paying a licence-acquisition premium — is that the real story?
5. Could SeaPay's licence be duplicative with PayTabs' existing Saudi authorisation, making the acquired licence redundant?
6. The "200,000 merchants / $100B by 2030" target is PayTabs' own framing — what's its current actual Saudi merchant base and processed volume? Is SeaPay material to that, or rounding error?
7. Does the Amazon Payment Services MENA acquisition (also pending, ~$100M) already give PayTabs the Saudi scale SeaPay offers, making SeaPay marginal?
8. Both the Amazon PS and SeaPay deals are **pending regulatory approval simultaneously** — could regulators scrutinise PayTabs' rapid consolidation on competition grounds?
9. Is "Growth Makers Company" the correct PayTabs operating entity, or a naming artifact? Confirm it's the acquirer of record.
10. For Arab Sea (the seller) — will the sale produce a gain or a loss? If SeaPay was carried above SAR 35M, this is a write-down disguised as a divestiture.
11. Six-month MoU window: what happens if no SPA is signed by ~April 2027 — does the deal lapse, auto-extend, or is there a break clause?
12. Are there competing bidders? A single-counterparty MoU at a fixed price suggests a negotiated/pre-arranged exit rather than a competitive process — any conflict given "no related parties" was explicitly flagged?
13. Is the ~$9.3M USD conversion contemporaneous (SAR/USD ~3.75 peg) — yes, consistent; but confirm no earn-out or deferred consideration hidden beyond the headline SAR 35M.
14. Does this deal change the competitive position vs. Geidea/HyperPay/Moyasar, or is it too small to move market share?

**Importance: 3/5** — Confirmed, well-sourced (Tadawul disclosure + multiple fintech trade outlets), and it fits a clear, strategically significant pattern: PayTabs' third 2026 acquisition and continued Saudi/MENA payments consolidation under Vision 2030. But standalone it is small (SAR 35M, non-binding MoU, 2023-vintage sub-scale target) with execution risk at every gate; materiality derives mainly from the acquirer's roll-up trajectory (the ~$100M Amazon PS MENA deal is the bigger story), not from SeaPay itself.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Saudi payments is a large, fast-consolidating market: the broad Saudi payments market was ~$181bn in 2025 and is projected at ~$200bn in 2026 growing to ~$327bn by 2031 (CAGR ~10.3%), per Mordor Intelligence (via researchandmarkets, as of 2025). The policy driver is Vision 2030's cash-lite push — non-cash share rose from 32% (2019) to 63% (2023), with a 70%-cashless-by-2030 target (per Ken Research). Structure: fragmented at the acquirer/gateway/SoftPoS layer (SeaPay, PayTabs, Geidea, HyperPay, Amazon Payment Services, Moyasar, Paymentology) but consolidating fast — "why now" is that organically obtaining a SAMA payments license is slow and capital-heavy, so buying a licensed entity is the efficient entry/scale path (de-PR'd: the article's own thesis, and consistent with SeaPay being pitched as a "licensed footprint" asset).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: annual processed volume (TPV), take rate, merchant count, licensed scope. PayTabs is explicitly running an M&A roll-up to hit >US$100bn annual TPV by 2030 (per Entrepreneur ME / fintechfutures, Oct 2026). Recent peer/own moves with dates: PayTabs agreed to buy Amazon Payment Services' MENA arm for >$100M (Sep 2026), lifting combined volume to ~SAR 150bn (~$40bn)/yr and ~3,500 merchants (per fintechfutures/zawya); PayTabs bought UAE checkout startup TAPn'GO (Apr 2026) → [[PayTabs Group acquires checkout startup TAPn'GO in UAE]]. Peer consolidation: NEOPAY to take 65% of noon payments (Oct 2026) → [[NEOPAY to acquire 65% of noon payments in MENA]]; Qashio/Sanad Cash (Oct 2025) → [[Qashio acquires Saudi expense platform Sanad Cash]]; Ateon just won a SAMA license → [[Ateon receives Saudi SAMA payment services license]]. SeaPay is a B2B gateway/PoS/SoftPoS provider (per fintechfutures); its position is a niche sub-scale licensed target being absorbed, not a standalone competitor — moat is the SAMA license itself, not product/network `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** This is an all-in SAR 35M (~$9.3M) 100% buyout; no disclosed SeaPay revenue/TPV, so EV/Revenue and EV/TPV = no data (multiple arithmetic not computable). Internal comps for Saudi/MENA payments M&A: PayTabs–Amazon MENA (>$100M for a ~$40bn-TPV, 3,500-merchant book) vs SeaPay (SAR 35M) implies SeaPay is a very small tuck-in — roughly 1/10th the Amazon price for an unquantified book `(analysis)`; [[PayTabs Group acquires checkout startup TAPn'GO in UAE]] (undisclosed), [[NEOPAY to acquire 65% of noon payments in MENA]], [[Qashio acquires Saudi expense platform Sanad Cash]] (all undisclosed consideration). PayTabs' own revenue is only loosely estimated at ~$22–50M (third-party trackers LeadIQ/ZoomInfo — `[UNSOURCED]`, treat as directional). Verdict: price looks like a small license-and-tech tuck-in; distribution not computed — qualitative only, too few disclosed figures.

**Risk flags.**
- Deal certainty: this is an MoU, not a signed SPA — contingent on due diligence, binding SPA and SAMA no-objection; the 6-month window (to ~Apr 2027) can lapse with no deal. Second-order: the SAR 35M "benchmark" can be revised down after diligence.
- Carrying-value / impairment asymmetry for the seller: Arab Sea's P&L impact (gain/loss) is undisclosed and depends on SeaPay's book value vs SAR 35M — a sub-carrying sale would signal the asset was overvalued on-book.
- Roll-up execution/overlap risk at PayTabs: stacking SeaPay on top of the much larger Amazon MENA integration (still itself pending) within ~12 months risks integration strain and overlapping Saudi gateway rails — paying for a license/merchants it may partly duplicate.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Confirms a PayTabs-led consolidation wave in Saudi acquiring where incumbents buy licensed/merchant books rather than build — SeaPay is a cheap bolt-on, not a re-rating event `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: if PayTabs closes both SeaPay and Amazon MENA, it becomes the dominant MENA acquirer and smaller licensed gateways (Geidea, Moyasar, HyperPay) become next targets or get squeezed; watch the SPA/SAMA approval as the real trigger — failure to convert the MoU within the window would falsify the "consolidation accelerating" read.
