Arab Sea Agrees to Sell SeaPay for 35M SAR
 Riyadh, Saudi Arabia — Arab Sea Information Systems Company has signed a Memorandum of Understanding with PayTabs Group (Growth Makers Company) to sell its entire 100% stake in Arab Sea Financial Company (SeaPay) for 35 million SAR. The MoU was signed on October 22, 2026. SeaPay is a wholly owned subsidiary of Arab Sea Information Systems Company.
Deal Details
The proposed transaction covers a full ownership transfer of SeaPay from Arab Sea Information Systems Company to PayTabs Group. The agreed consideration stands at exactly 35 million SAR. The MoU runs for six months from the signing date or until both parties execute a definitive Sale and Purchase Agreement (SPA), whichever comes first. No related parties are involved in the transaction.
Before any deal closes, both sides must complete a due diligence process. After that, they will negotiate and sign a binding SPA. Finally, the transaction requires regulatory approvals and no-objection confirmations from the relevant authorities. Arab Sea Information Systems Company has committed to disclosing material developments as they arise.
Why It Matters
Arab Sea Information Systems Company is divesting its fintech payments subsidiary, SeaPay, signaling a potential strategic refocus on its core information systems business.
PayTabs Group, an established regional payments player, adds a licensed Saudi fintech entity to its portfolio through this deal.
The 35 million SAR price tag sets a concrete valuation benchmark for SeaPay ahead of formal due diligence.
The MoU imposes no immediate financial impact on Arab Sea Information Systems Company — the accounting effect triggers only upon closing.
What This Means for Investors
This remains a preliminary agreement, not a done deal. Due diligence, SPA execution, and regulatory sign-off can each delay or derail the transaction.
If the sale closes at 35 million SAR, Arab Sea Information Systems Company will recognize a financial impact — positive or negative — depending on SeaPay’s carrying value on its books.
Investors should watch for the SPA announcement and any regulatory decisions as the clearest signals of deal progress.
The six-month MoU window means any material update should surface before April 2027.

 THE SAUDI STANDARD’S VIEW: ARAB SEA’S SEAPAY EXIT SIGNALS FINTECH CONSOLIDATION IN THE KINGDOM 
The agreement between Arab Sea Information Systems Company and PayTabs Group to transfer full ownership of SeaPay for 35 million SAR is more than a single corporate transaction — it reflects a broader pattern of strategic repositioning and market consolidation reshaping Saudi Arabia’s fintech and payments landscape. Investors should read this deal carefully, because what appears on the surface as a straightforward divestiture carries meaningful implications for capital allocation, sector dynamics, and regulatory maturity.
 • A STRATEGIC PIVOT, NOT A RETREAT 
Arab Sea Information Systems Company’s decision to exit its payments subsidiary points to a deliberate narrowing of focus toward its core information systems competencies. Holding a licensed fintech entity requires sustained investment in compliance, technology, and capital buffers — resources that a parent primarily oriented around information systems may deploy more productively elsewhere. This divestiture, if completed, would sharpen the company’s operational profile and potentially improve return metrics for shareholders.
 • PAYTABS STRENGTHENS ITS LICENSED FOOTPRINT 
For PayTabs Group, acquiring a Saudi-licensed fintech entity like SeaPay represents a meaningful regulatory and operational asset in one of the region’s most competitive payments markets. Obtaining a payments license in Saudi Arabia independently is a time-intensive and resource-heavy process, making an acquisition of an existing licensed entity strategically efficient. This move positions PayTabs to deepen its Saudi market penetration with an established regulatory standing already in place.
 • VALUATION DISCIPLINE AND BALANCE SHEET RISK 
The 35 million SAR consideration establishes a hard valuation reference point for SeaPay ahead of due diligence — a figure the market can now measure against what formal scrutiny ultimately confirms or revises. The financial outcome for Arab Sea Information Systems Company will hinge entirely on SeaPay’s carrying value relative to the sale price, meaning the deal could produce a gain, a loss, or a negligible impact on the income statement. Investors should reserve judgment on the financial benefit until the SPA is signed and the carrying value is disclosed.
 • REGULATORY MILESTONES ARE THE REAL TIMELINE 
The six-month MoU window and the multi-stage closing process — due diligence, SPA execution, regulatory approvals — mean this transaction carries execution risk at each checkpoint. Saudi financial sector regulators maintain rigorous standards for ownership transfers in licensed entities, and no-objection clearances are neither automatic nor instantaneous. Investors tracking this deal should treat regulatory confirmation, not MoU signing, as the definitive measure of deal certainty.
Transactions like this Arab Sea-PayTabs arrangement are a direct expression of Vision 2030’s ambition to build a sophisticated, consolidated, and internationally competitive Saudi financial technology sector. The Kingdom’s push to diversify its economy has deliberately cultivated a fintech ecosystem dense enough to generate organic M&A activity, license transfers, and sector specialization — all signs of a maturing market. As consolidation accelerates among Saudi fintech players, investors who understand which companies are sharpening their focus and which are scaling through acquisition will be better positioned to identify durable value in the Kingdom’s evolving digital economy.
