## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened (2026-10-20).** The Initiative Deutsche Zahlungssysteme published an Institut für Demoskopie Allensbach survey (fieldwork 1 Jul–13 Aug 2026; n≈1,039 face-to-face, Germans 16+). Headline: a majority of Germans have never heard of **Wero**, the European Payments Initiative (EPI) account-to-account wallet, and uptake is minimal. The note's cited figures (55% never heard, 5% have used it; 54% aware of the digital euro, 7% would probably use it) are consistent with press coverage, though German outlets frame awareness inversely — Börse Express reports only ~39% "know" the app, and among those first hearing of Wero, ~61% do not want to use it vs ~13% interested. The incumbent girocard remains dominant at ~98% penetration.

**Why it matters — the sovereignty frame.** Wero and the digital euro are Europe's twin answer to dependence on US rails. ~61% of eurozone card payments run through Visa/Mastercard (by value ~47% in 2025), and 13 European countries have no relevant domestic card alternative. EPI positions Wero as a European counterweight to PayPal/Visa/Mastercard, targeting a "fully-fledged alternative" by 2027. A consumer-awareness gap in Germany — EPI's launch market and largest economy — is a direct threat to that thesis: political and bank backing cannot substitute for end-user pull.

**Adoption reality vs. awareness gap.** The survey's pessimism sits awkwardly against EPI's own momentum numbers: ~52.5M users disclosed in March 2026, ~58M by September 2026 (from ~43.5M at the one-year mark, >€7.5bn transferred in year one). Reconciliation: most "users" are banking-app customers auto-provisioned with Wero P2P transfers (it rides inside existing bank apps), so registered reach ≠ active/aware brand recognition. That explains how Wero can have tens of millions of nominal users while a majority of Germans say they've never heard of the brand — the brand is largely invisible behind the bank apps that carry it.

**Rollout timeline (dated).**
- 2024-07-02 Germany P2P launch; 2024-09-01 France; 2024-10-01 Belgium.
- Late 2025: e-commerce payments launch in Germany; Belgium early 2026; progressive France rollout.
- 2026-09-01 Luxembourg launch (see [[Wero to launch in Luxembourg with Payconiq and EPI]], [[Luxembourg banks confirm instant euro payments live]]).
- Netherlands: iDEAL phasing into Wero from 2026 (see [[iDEAL to phase into Wero starting in 2026]], [[Wero launches in Dutch online stores in October 2026]]).
- ~48,000 merchants had processed Wero transactions by Sep 2026.
- EPI + EuroPA MoU (Bizum/Bancomat/SIBS MB WAY/Vipps MobilePay) → ~130M combined users across 13 countries; Poland's BLIK joining the pan-European push ([[Poland's BLIK joins pan-European cashless payments push]]).

**Internal priors.** EPI ecosystem build-out is well covered in the DB: acquirer/PSP onboarding ([[Mollie joins EPI Company to accelerate Wero]], [[payabl. joins European Payments Initiative to expand Wero]], [[Unzer completes first Wero transaction on production system]]), bank/app integrations ([[Nickel integrates Wero into its app]], [[France's Fortuneo partners with Wero for instant transfers]]), and the public-sector push ([[Berlin plans to introduce the European payment system Wero]]). This survey is the first DB item that quantifies the **consumer-demand side** rather than supply-side rollout — a genuinely new angle, not a duplicate.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. **Awareness numbers conflict.** The note says 55% "never heard of Wero" (so ~45% aware); Börse Express reports only ~39% "know" it. Different wording ("heard of" vs "know/recognize") and the same vs. a different wave? Which figure is apples-to-apples?
2. **Survey vintage.** Fieldwork was 1 Jul–13 Aug 2026 but published 20 Oct 2026. Two-plus months of Wero marketing/rollout (incl. Luxembourg launch 1 Sep) are not reflected — is the awareness gap already partly stale?
3. **Sponsor bias.** The commissioner, Initiative Deutsche Zahlungssysteme, is the girocard lobby — a direct competitor to Wero at POS. Does the framing ("98% girocard dominance") serve an agenda? Treat the pessimistic spin skeptically.
4. **Users vs. awareness paradox.** EPI claims ~58M users (Sep 2026) yet a majority say they've never heard of Wero. Are "users" just auto-enrolled bank-app P2P customers who never recognize the brand? What is *active* monthly usage, not registered reach?
5. **The 5% "used it" figure** — used for what? P2P transfers inside a bank app (where users may not even see "Wero" branding) vs. standalone/e-commerce? Usage definition is load-bearing.
6. **Representativeness.** n≈1,039 face-to-face is standard for Allensbach but is Germany-only. Does low German awareness generalize to France/Belgium, where Wero launched differently and earlier P2P traction was stronger?
7. **Digital euro conflation.** The note bundles Wero (private EPI product) with the digital euro (ECB CBDC). They're distinct; does mixing their awareness numbers muddy the read? 54% aware of digital euro vs. 45% of Wero — why is a not-yet-launched CBDC more recognized?
8. **E-commerce vs. P2P.** Awareness is low, but the strategic bet is POS + e-commerce (launched Germany late 2025). Does consumer brand awareness even matter if Wero rides inside trusted bank apps at checkout?
9. **Counterfactual.** What awareness did girocard, Paydirekt, or PayPal have at a comparable 2-year post-launch stage? Is 45% awareness in 2 years actually poor, or normal for a new rail?
10. **"Would use" vs. "aware."** 7% "would probably use" the digital euro and ~13% interested in Wero — is stated intent meaningful, or will habit/girocard default dominate regardless?
11. **Merchant side.** ~48k merchants accept Wero — how does that compare to girocard/PayPal acceptance footprints? Acceptance ubiquity, not awareness, may be the real bottleneck.
12. **Does this change the investment/strategic thesis?** EPI/banks are committed through 2027 regardless of one survey. Is this a signal or just a predictable early-adoption-curve data point?
13. **Primary-source check.** Figures are from a German-language press release + secondary German outlets; the English note rounds/translates. Have the exact percentages been verified against the original Allensbach tabulation?

**Importance: 2/5** — A credible, well-sourced data point quantifying Wero's consumer-awareness deficit in its anchor market, which is a genuine wrinkle in the European-payments-sovereignty narrative and the first demand-side read in the DB. But it is single-market, sponsored by a competing incumbent (girocard lobby), based on summer fieldwork that predates recent rollout, and does not alter EPI's committed trajectory or any near-term financials. Context-building, not market-moving.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Account-to-account (A2A) / instant payments is the fast-growing layer EPI's Wero sits on: per Mordor Intelligence, the Europe real-time payments market is ~$7.96bn in 2026 (from $7.21bn in 2025), to ~$13.1bn by 2031 at ~10.5% CAGR; Europe mobile payments are larger and faster — ~$174.05bn (2026) to ~$587.95bn (2031) at ~27.6% CAGR (per Mordor Intelligence, as of 2026). Structure: the European retail-payments stack is fragmented at the wallet layer (national schemes — BLIK/PL, Bizum/ES, iDEAL/NL, Swish/SE) but consolidated at the card-rail layer, dominated by Visa/Mastercard — the sovereignty gap EPI exists to close. Barriers are network effects (two-sided consumer+merchant) and distribution via bank apps. Why now: the EU Instant Payments Regulation now obliges eurozone banks to execute A2A transfers in seconds (per Mordor Intelligence), giving Wero free rails; and the digital euro moved to its next phase — ECB selected 36 PSPs for a pilot planned H2-2027, with potential first issuance ~2029 (per ECB, 14 Jul 2026 release). Wero's own traction: ~58M registered users as of Sep 2026, up from ~40-43M in mid-2025; >100M transactions and >€5-7.5bn transferred (per EPI/press, 2026).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: registered users, active users, transaction volume/value, merchant acceptance footprint, P2P→e-commerce→POS funnel progression. Key players and basis of competition: (a) card incumbents Visa/Mastercard (distribution, ubiquity, Click-to-Pay); (b) national A2A schemes now folding into / federating with EPI — iDEAL phasing into Wero from 2026 [[iDEAL to phase into Wero starting in 2026]], BLIK joining the pan-European push [[Poland's BLIK joins pan-European cashless payments push]], plus the EuroPA network linking Bizum/Swish et al. [[European wallets form European Network for Payments]]; (c) the forthcoming digital euro — a public-sector A2A instrument that is simultaneously a tailwind (legitimizes A2A) and a direct substitute. Recent moves: Austria's Erste + Raiffeisen joined EPI as shareholders (Jun 2026); Deutsche Bank group completed full Wero adoption incl. norisbank (Aug 2026); e-commerce launches in Belgium (Mar 2026) and Dutch online stores (Oct 2026). Protagonist position: Wero is ahead on bank distribution (16 bank/payments shareholders, Sparkassen committed €150M) and user registration, but behind on usage — its moat is network effects via mandatory bank-app integration, not yet consumer habit (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** No deal/valuation/round in this item — it is a survey, so no trading multiples apply ("no data"; EPI is a bank consortium, not listed, no market cap). Internal comps (the Wero adoption/expansion arc in-base): [[Wero launches e-commerce payments in Belgium]], [[Wero launches in Dutch online stores in October 2026]], [[Nickel integrates Wero into its app]], [[BoursoBank integrates Wero into its payment solutions]], [[Van Lanschot Kempen becomes Principal Member of EPI, adds Wero]], [[Mollie joins EPI Company to accelerate Wero]], [[Digital Euro clears key hurdle in EU parliament]]. Pattern: 12+ months of supply-side wins (banks, merchants, markets) — this Allensbach datapoint is the first hard demand-side read, and it is weak: in Germany, Wero's anchor market, 55% have never heard of it and only ~5% have used it despite near-universal bank-app reach. Price-per-user / revenue multiples: no data (no disclosed revenue or raise here).

**Risk flags.**
- Awareness/adoption gap in the home market (why): Germany is EPI's largest shareholder base and launch market, yet 55% unaware / ~5% used — network effects run backwards if the consumer flywheel never starts, stranding the heavy bank-distribution investment and ceding P2P→POS ground back to cards.
- Public-sector substitution (why): the digital euro (ECB pilot H2-2027) targets the same A2A sovereignty use-case; if it arrives with ECB legitimacy before Wero achieves habit, Wero risks being out-marketed by its own stated rationale — the 54% digital-euro awareness vs 45% Wero awareness in this survey already shows the public brand leading.
- Supply-led, demand-light execution (why): continued announcements (banks, markets, merchants) without usage growth is a classic "announced vs adopted" trap — acceptance footprint is necessary but not sufficient; the silent metric is active (not registered) users.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** The story shifts Wero's risk from "can it get bank/merchant coverage" (largely solved) to "can it get consumers to switch off cards" (unproven) (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Wero cannot lift German awareness above ~60% and active usage into double digits by the time the digital-euro pilot starts (H2-2027), it concedes the mindshare it was built to capture. Trigger/catalyst to watch: next EPI active-user disclosure (vs registered), POS (in-store) launch timing, and whether iDEAL migration (2026) converts Dutch iDEAL's deep habit into Wero usage — the single best natural experiment for whether Wero can inherit, rather than rebuild, consumer adoption.
