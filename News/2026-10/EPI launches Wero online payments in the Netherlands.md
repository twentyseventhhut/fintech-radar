EPI Company initiates Wero roll-out in the Netherlands
As part of the migration process from iDEAL to Wero announced earlier this year, EPI enters a new phase with the roll-out of webshops' payment use case with all participating Dutch banks. Through a single integration of Wero, merchants will be able to reach a larger scale at European level while offering independent, sovereign payments to their customers.
 Brussels / Amsterdam, 26 October 2026 – European Payments Initiative (EPI) today announced the ignition of the next phase of the iDEAL to Wero migration with the launch of online commercial payments with all participating Dutch banks from 26 October. The ignition of the Wero payment service now allows payment service providers (PSPs) to progressively allow the deployment of Wero for Dutch merchants. From now on merchants will be welcomed to transition from iDEAL to Wero. Our strong ambition is to finalise the migration before the end of 2027 as this timeframe is the best guarantee to ensure a successful and smooth transition.
The launch of the Wero ecommerce use case will be effective for customers of ABN AMRO, ASN Bank, bunq, BUUT, Finom, ING, Knab, Nationale-Nederlanden, Rabobank, Triodos Bank, Van Lanschot Kempen on 26 October. Commercial payments will be extended in Q2 2027 with new payment management options such as subscriptions and pay on delivery.
Through its phased approach, EPI is allowing every merchant to work on its own deployment calendar and that of its’ PSP, while also offering additional time to integrate purchase protection which will be embedded in Wero for Dutch consumers by January 2028. This protection is being discussed to craft a solid solution with the support of all parties involved, from merchants to unions, federations and PSPs as well as consumer associations, banks and relevant institutions. The solution will be aligned through a series of workshops which will be hosted over the fourth quarter of 2026, and announced in the first quarter of 2027.
Smoothness, security and trust have been the guiding principles of the iDEAL to Wero migration from the beginning, to truly build on the foundation with the established use cases and simplicity of the solution while developing additional use cases and value-added services.
To support the ignition of Wero payments in webshops and pursue awareness initiatives, a new marketing campaign has been designed by DEPT Agency and orchestrated by Havas Media, and will be launched on 26 October, running until March 2027.
 Seeing Wero in the banking app of our Dutch shareholders and members is definitely the milestone we were looking forward to for the past year. Thanks to the continuous cooperation of all parties involved in the migration, we are entering this new phase ahead of the peak season, with the first merchants now joining the Wero journey - and we couldn’t be happier to observe the first transactions coming through. We are not just championing European sovereignty in payment, we are building it, creating new opportunities for commerce and empowering merchants to thrive. 
Martina Weimert
CEO of EPI
The deployment of Wero doesn’t require any action from consumers. Banks will keep their customers informed through its official banking channels. Neither Wero nor the bank will ever ask consumers to provide personal or banking information outside the secure banking environment. No new service should be subscribed to.
 About EPI 
 EPI  (or European Payments Initiative) is supported by 18 European banks and payment service providers. Beyond the shareholders, over 50 institutions in Europe are now members of EPI. They have joined forces with a common goal: to offer a unified mobile payment service, to all European companies and citizens, Wero. EPI intends to enable European consumers and merchants and to carry out all types of retail transactions simply, via a resolutely sovereign digital wallet. 
Find out more at  epicompany.eu and follow us on  LinkedIn 
 About Wero 
Based on instant account-to-account (A2A) payments, Wero further streamlines payments in Europe by eliminating intermediaries in the payment chain and the associated additional costs. Wero already supports payments between individuals, and progressively unveils payments to professionals (P2Pro). Wero is live for P2P payments in Belgium, France, Germany and Luxembourg, currently serving 60 million users. Wero also plans to expand into Austria. 
For retail payments, Wero is live in Germany, Belgium and Luxembourg with progressive roll-out in France throughout 2026. Point-of-sale payments are already live in Belgium and Luxembourg with further rollouts planned across other markets. The migration from iDEAL to Wero in the Netherlands is expected to involve around 15 million consumers by 2027. Additional value-added services will include merchant loyalty programmes, recurring payments and subscription management.  
EPI/Wero is also a founding member of the European Network for Payments (ENP), which aims to progressively connect payment solutions collectively serving around 130 million users across 13 European countries.

### [0] What happened
On 26 October 2026 EPI (European Payments Initiative) "ignited" the next phase of the iDEAL-to-Wero migration: the **online commercial (e-commerce) use case went live** across **all 11 participating Dutch banks** — ABN AMRO, ASN Bank, bunq, BUUT, Finom, ING, Knab, Nationale-Nederlanden, Rabobank, Triodos Bank and Van Lanschot Kempen. Payment service providers (PSPs) can now progressively enable Wero for Dutch merchants, who begin transitioning from iDEAL. EPI reaffirms the target to **finish the full migration by 31 December 2027**. Commercial payments extend in **Q2 2027** with subscriptions and pay-on-delivery; **purchase protection** is to be embedded for Dutch consumers by **January 2028** (design workshops in Q4 2026, announced Q1 2027). A marketing campaign (DEPT Agency / Havas Media) runs 26 Oct 2026–March 2027. Consumers need take no action. (Source: [epicompany.eu press release](https://epicompany.eu/media-insights/epi-company-initiates-wero-roll-out-in-the-netherlands); [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/epi-starts-wero-ecommerce-roll-out-with-dutch-banks))

### [1] Why it matters
iDEAL is the dominant Dutch online-payment scheme (~60%+ of Dutch e-commerce, ~15 million users set to migrate by end-2027). Folding iDEAL's volume into Wero is the single biggest lever for EPI's pan-European account-to-account (A2A) ambition: it instantly gives Wero scale and merchant reach that its Belgium/France/Germany launches could not. It is also Europe's clearest "sovereign payments" play against US card networks (Visa/Mastercard) and PayPal. Execution risk is high — this is a managed replacement of critical national infrastructure, not a greenfield launch.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
### Red-team questions
1. Is this a genuinely new event or a rehash of the Jan-2026 announcement ([[Wero launches in Dutch online stores in October 2026]])? — New: Jan was forward-looking; 26 Oct 2026 is the actual go-live ("ignition") across all 11 banks. Distinct, verified by press release date.
2. "Launched" vs. "available to switch": the e-commerce use case is *technically live*, but merchant onboarding is progressive via PSPs — not an overnight cutover. Headline risks overstating real-world availability on day one.
3. How many merchants actually accept Wero on 26 Oct? The release says "first merchants now joining" — i.e. a handful, not broad coverage. Adoption number is effectively zero-to-small at launch.
4. Is iDEAL being switched off? No — iDEAL continues during a dual-brand migration through end-2027. No forced cutover yet.
5. The "15 million consumers by 2027" figure is a migration *target*, not current Wero usage in NL. Don't present as adoption.
6. User-count discrepancy: this release says 60M Wero users; recent coverage cites 56M. Which is current? Likely PR rounding/recency — treat as ~56-60M, note ambiguity.
7. Consumer awareness risk: Germans largely haven't heard of Wero ([[Allensbach survey...]]). Does strong iDEAL brand recognition actually transfer to Wero, or create confusion?
8. Merchant economics: are Wero's merchant fees genuinely lower than iDEAL/cards, or is "sovereignty" the main selling point? Release is silent on pricing — a real adoption lever left unquantified.
9. Purchase protection (Jan 2028) is still "being discussed" via Q4-2026 workshops — a key consumer feature is unbuilt at launch. Execution risk.
10. Regulatory/competition: does replacing the dominant national scheme raise any central-bank/DNB or EU competition scrutiny? Not addressed in source.
11. Competitive response: how do PayPal, Visa/Mastercard, Klarna react to Wero capturing iDEAL volume? No evidence of displacement yet.
12. Timeline credibility: is end-2027 full migration realistic given merchant/PSP dependencies and that P2P/POS use cases in NL aren't even covered here (e-commerce only)?
13. Digital euro overlap: could the ECB digital euro undercut Wero's rationale before migration completes?
14. Source bias: primary source is EPI's own press release — self-serving framing ("championing European sovereignty"). Independent verification limited to trade press echoing the release.
15. Is "all participating Dutch banks" = full market? 11 banks is broad but not every Dutch bank; coverage gaps possible.

Importance: 4/5 — Migrating iDEAL (the dominant Dutch online-payment scheme, ~15M users) onto Wero is the single most consequential step for EPI's pan-European A2A sovereignty play and materially reshapes a major national payments market. Not a 5 because day-one real-world availability is thin (progressive merchant onboarding), key features (purchase protection, subscriptions) are not yet live, and it is one phase of a long, execution-heavy multi-year migration rather than a completed shift.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector context — European sovereign payments / A2A.** EPI's Wero is the pan-European account-to-account (A2A) wallet backed by 18 shareholder banks/PSPs and 50+ member institutions, positioned as a "sovereign" alternative to Visa/Mastercard and PayPal. Wero is live for P2P in Belgium, France, Germany and Luxembourg, reporting ~60M users (56–57M cited mid-2026), with retail/e-commerce live in Germany, Belgium and Luxembourg and rolling out in France through 2026. The Netherlands launch (26 Oct 2026) opens the e-commerce use case to customers of 11 Dutch banks (ABN AMRO, ING, Rabobank, bunq, Finom, Knab, Triodos, etc.), beginning the migration away from iDEAL — targeted for completion before end-2027, with subscriptions and pay-on-delivery due Q2 2027 and embedded purchase protection by Jan 2028. This is the single most consequential market for Wero: iDEAL holds ~70% of Dutch online payments and ~15M consumers are expected to migrate by 2027, so a clean handover materially de-risks Wero's pan-European scale ambition (EPI/Wero also co-founds the European Network for Payments / ENP, targeting ~130M users across 13 countries).

**In-base comps (news DB).** The base tracks a dense Wero/EPI build-out: the original migration announcements ([[News/2025-10/iDEAL to phase into Wero starting in 2026]], [[News/2025-11/Dutch iDEAL to rebrand as iDEAL Wero in 2026]]); prior e-commerce launches ([[News/2026-03/Wero launches e-commerce payments in Belgium]], [[News/2026-04/Nexi implements Wero for eCommerce in Germany]]); infrastructure/processing partners ([[News/2026-06/ACI Worldwide and EPI to power instant payments in Europe]], [[News/2026-06/ACI Worldwide powers Rabobank's Wero instant payments]]); acquirer/PSP onboarding feeding the Dutch rollout ([[News/2026-01/Mollie joins EPI Company to accelerate Wero]], [[News/2025-11/Airwallex becomes EPI Principal Member to bring Wero]], [[News/2025-10/payabl. joins European Payments Initiative to expand Wero]]); and cross-border interop ([[News/2026-04/Bancomat, EPI and SIBS demonstrate cross-border payment interoperability]], [[News/2025-09/EuroPA and EPI prepare to expand cross-border payments in Europe]]). The Netherlands note is the next predictable step in that sequence — scale-in via the Dutch bank cohort, not a new business model.

**Risk flags (skeptical):**
1. **Adoption lag at large merchants.** External coverage (EuroShop, trade press) flags that despite bank/user growth, major e-commerce retailers (e.g. Otto, Zalando, Rewe in Germany) remain hesitant; without top merchants, Wero struggles to be a genuine PayPal/iDEAL replacement. Dutch rollout faces the same acceptance-side risk.
2. **Displacing an entrenched incumbent.** iDEAL is a ~70% online-payments quasi-monopoly used by ~9 in 10 Dutch shoppers; replacing a trusted, cheap, well-understood rail by end-2027 is an aggressive timeline with consumer-habit and merchant re-integration friction.
3. **Terms differ from iDEAL.** Wero reportedly differs on pricing, consumer/purchase protection, data return and merchant control; purchase protection is not embedded until Jan 2028 (still being negotiated in Q4 2026 workshops), i.e. a protection gap during the core migration window.
4. **Execution/PSP dependency.** Migration pace is gated by each PSP's and merchant's own deployment calendar ("phased approach"), so EPI does not fully control timing — the end-2027 target is a stated ambition, not a hard commitment, and slippage would be unsurprising.
5. **Press-release sourcing.** The note is sourced entirely from EPI's own announcement (CEO quote, no independent transaction volumes, no merchant count, no revenue/economics). Treat milestone framing ("first transactions coming through") as promotional until independent adoption/volume data appears.
