Argentina Rejects Near Future Changes In Crypto Banking Integration
While Argentina has reached one of the largest cryptocurrency adoption levels in Latam and the world, the government does not contemplate digital assets’ integration into its banking and payment system, at least in the near term.
At an event organized by the Argentine Payment Chamber, Juan Curutchet, Superintendent of Financial and Foreign Exchange Institutions at the Central Bank of Argentina (BCRA) reiterated that there would be no changes regarding crypto and digital assets in banking and payment channels in the near future.
 “You didn’t ask me about the dollar or crypto. That’s fine, because nothing is going to happen this year. But I do see it as a possibility during a second Milei presidency,” Curutchet revealed before leaving the stage of the event.
The statement contradicts alleged leaks hinting at a possible deregulation of digital assets, with bank sources claiming a draft to allow banks to offer crypto services was in the works.
This means the 2022 BCRA’s ban on financial institutions offering crypto services will hold, with its elimination depending on the state of the economy before an upcoming presidential election.
Curutchet acknowledged that Argentina’s economic standing offered limited leverage to advance these solutions, but stressed that these were valid issues to discuss.
 “In a country where the perception of vulnerability and fragility has decreased significantly, more than today, and with full economic freedom, these are issues on which progress must be made. Today we have a level of fragility that does not allow us to advance as much. But I think there is a pending agenda for later,” Curutchet concluded.
Even so, banking groups are advancing their own stablecoins projects to offer programmable money features for institutional money management, featuring treasury management operations, payments conditioned on on-chain events, and collateralized credit management.
These conglomerates are developing these initiatives through separate companies to respect the current ban. Banking integration would be delayed until the central bank lifts the ongoing prohibitions.
Even with this prohibition, Argentina became the second-largest crypto hub in Latam , receiving over $88.5 billion in crypto value in the 12 months ending June 30, according to Chainalysis.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** At the Pulsa 2026 payments event (Cámara de Medios de Pago), Juan Curutchet, BCRA Superintendent of Financial and Foreign Exchange Institutions, said there will be NO integration of crypto/digital assets into Argentina's banking and payment channels "this year," pausing it for roughly the next two years and leaving the door open only to a hypothetical second Milei presidency. The 2022 ban (Communication "A" 7506, May 2022) on financial institutions offering/facilitating crypto operations therefore stays in force. Cited reason: economic "fragility" limits room to advance. Banking groups continue to build institutional stablecoin projects via separate legal entities to respect the ban.

**[1] Why it matters / the reversal.** This is a notable walk-back of a late-2025 narrative. In December 2025 CoinDesk, The Block and Bitcoin Magazine reported the BCRA was "set to greenlight" bank crypto services in 2026 (draft framework floated for ~April 2026: blue-chip coins only — BTC/ETH/USDC/USDT/XRP — custody + trading via ring-fenced units with higher capital/KYC under CNV). Curutchet's October 2026 statement explicitly contradicts those "leaks" of a deregulation draft. So the story is less "ban upheld" (status quo) and more "the much-reported liberalization has been deferred," tying bank crypto to the political/economic cycle rather than a near-term regulatory timetable.

**[2] Market backdrop.** Argentina is Latam's #2 crypto hub (~$88.5B received in the 12 months to 30 Jun per Chainalysis) and leads the region on adoption (~20% usage / ~8.6M users in 2026). Usage is overwhelmingly stablecoins — ~94% of peso crypto volume — a dollarization/inflation hedge that persisted even after most personal USD-purchase controls were lifted (April 2025). So the formal banking sector is locked out of a very large, stablecoin-dominated on-chain economy that keeps growing around it. Local players already serve this demand outside banks: see [[Argentina crypto fintech Lemon raises $20M Series B round]], [[Argentina's Cocos launches standalone Cocos Crypto app]], and [[Berry raises $2M seed for Argentine US-stock investing]].

**[3] Regional comparables.** The region is diverging. Brazil has moved to a strict regime — VASP licensing (BCB Resolutions 519/520/521, effective Feb 2026; stablecoin transfers treated as FX) and Resolution BCB 561 (eff. 1 Oct 2026) barring eFX providers from stablecoin cross-border settlement except via BCB-overseen entities; see [[Binance to tighten Brazilian crypto transfer rules Nov 1]] and [[Brazil tightens crypto self-custody wallet reporting rules]]. Mexico permits public crypto use but restricts regulated institutions (Fintech Law). Meanwhile banks elsewhere in Latam are being onboarded via vendors — see [[Gennius brings Coinbase-powered stablecoin services to LatAm banks]]. Argentina's choice to keep banks out is thus a deliberate outlier given its adoption lead.

**[4] What to watch.** (i) Whether a formal BCRA/CNV framework is actually published vs. the April-2026 draft that was reported but not enacted; (ii) the political trigger — Curutchet explicitly ties any change to a "second Milei term" and the economy's perceived fragility; (iii) continued workaround via ring-fenced stablecoin entities by banking groups (reportedly Grupo Bind, Grupo Petersen) — i.e. shadow institutional stablecoin rails growing while bank balance sheets stay excluded; (iv) IMF linkage, since the 2022 ban was widely read as IMF-influenced.

3. The Dec-2025 CoinDesk/TheBlock "set to greenlight in 2026" reports — were those ever official, or sourced to anonymous "bank sources"/La Nación leaks? If the latter, is the "reversal" overstated?
4. "Next two years" — is that an actual stated horizon or a paraphrase? Sources vary between "this year," "near term," and "two years." Which is sourced?
5. Does tying change to a "second Milei term" reflect BCRA policy or Curutchet's personal political read? Milei's current term runs to Dec 2027; a "second term" implies no change before ~2028.
6. The $88.5B Chainalysis figure and the ~94% stablecoin / ~20% adoption stats come from different reports/periods — am I conflating metrics? Verify each stat's window before leaning on them.
7. If banks are already running institutional stablecoin projects via separate entities, how material is the ban in practice? Is this regulatory theater that the market has routed around?
8. Counterfactual: would lifting the ban even move adoption, given retail already uses Lemon/Cocos/exchanges? Who actually benefits from bank crypto — custody-seeking institutions, not retail?
9. IMF angle: is the ban genuinely IMF-conditioned, or is that a recurring media trope? A new IMF program could make this a hard constraint, not a political choice.

11. Translation/source quality: much coverage is aggregator/secondary (bitcoin.com, kucoin, phemex). Is there a primary Spanish-language BCRA or La Nación source corroborating Curutchet's exact words?
12. Is there a conflict between Superintendencia (Curutchet) and BCRA presidency/CNV? Could another arm still publish a framework, making this statement non-final?
13. Duplicate check: do we already have a note on the Dec-2025 "BCRA to allow bank crypto" story? If so, is this the *contradiction* update (fresh) rather than a restatement?

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Latam stablecoin/crypto rails. Argentina received over $88.5bn in crypto value in the 12 months to June 30, 2026, the second-largest Latam market behind Brazil, per Chainalysis (via news.bitcoin.com). Regional stablecoin transaction volume reached ~$324bn in 2025, +89% YoY (Brazil ~$252.5bn, Argentina ~$88.5bn, Mexico ~$77.6bn), per soulbit.io citing Chainalysis. In Argentina USDC/USDT account for >70% of crypto purchases (rankingslatam/Chainalysis) — demand is structurally a dollarization hedge against inflation and capital controls, not speculation. "Why now": the policy split is the story — Brazil is pushing a *mandatory* VASP licensing regime (BCB Resolution 519, effective Feb 2, 2026; existing providers must file by Oct 30, 2026, after which licensed banks may not transact with unauthorized VASPs), while Argentina's BCRA is keeping its 2022 bank-crypto ban *in place* and explicitly deferred any change past this year. Structure: in Argentina the regulated on-ramp is a parallel, non-bank VASP channel (CNV-registered exchanges/wallets), i.e. crypto activity is high but structurally *outside* the banking perimeter — a regulatory, not demand, barrier.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: on/off-ramp volume, stablecoin share of flows, registered-user base, custody AUC. Because banks are barred, the competitive field is non-bank VASPs and fintechs: Lemon ([[Argentina crypto fintech Lemon raises $20M Series B round]]), Cocos ([[Argentina's Cocos launches standalone Cocos Crypto app]]), Belo, Ripio, plus global exchanges. Basis of competition: distribution + FX/dollar access, not bank balance sheets. Banking groups are not absent — they are routing institutional stablecoin projects (programmable money, treasury, collateralized credit) through *separate legal entities* to respect the ban (per primary source). Recent peer context: a draft reportedly circulated to let banks offer crypto as early as April 2026 (bitcoinmagazine/coindesk, Dec 2025), which Curutchet's statement now publicly contradicts. Protagonist position: the BCRA here is the *gatekeeper*, not a competitor; its stance keeps incumbent banks on the sidelines (catching-up at best) while non-bank VASPs retain the moat of being the only legal rail — a regulatory moat that evaporates the day the ban lifts. `(analysis)`

**Risk flags.**
- Regulatory/political binary: the ban's removal was explicitly tied to a hypothetical "second Milei term" and the state of the economy before an election — timing is political and non-linear, so any bank-crypto roadmap is unfundable until an election outcome is known. Second-order: capex/partnership decisions by banks stay frozen.
- Shadow-structure / arbitrage risk: banks building stablecoin products via separate entities to sidestep the ban creates a regulatory-arbitrage gray zone — supervisory perimeter and consumer-protection liability are ambiguous, and a strict reading could force unwind.
- Divergence vs Brazil: if Brazil's licensed-bank model captures institutional stablecoin flows while Argentina keeps banks out, Argentine banks cede the programmable-money/treasury layer to fintechs and foreign platforms — disintermediation of domestic banks from a $88.5bn flow.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Short term this entrenches non-bank VASPs (Lemon, Cocos, Ripio) as the sole legal crypto rail in Argentina — a durable, policy-granted moat. Falsifiable thesis: the ban holds through 2026 and does not lift before the mid-2027 electoral window; it breaks if the BCRA publishes the leaked draft permitting bank crypto services (watch for a BCRA resolution or CNV/BCRA joint rule) — that would re-rate incumbent banks into the space and compress the VASP regulatory moat. Trigger to watch: any formal BCRA draft/consultation on lifting the 2022 prohibition.
