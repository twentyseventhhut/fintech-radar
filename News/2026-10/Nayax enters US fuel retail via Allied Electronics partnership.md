HERZLIYA, Israel, Oct. 06, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- Nayax Ltd. (Nasdaq: NYAX; TASE: NYAX), a global commerce enablement, payments, and loyalty platform, today announced a distribution partnership with Allied Electronics, Inc. to bring Nayax Fuel’s VPOS Media 5 outdoor payment solution to fuel retailers across the United States.
The US fuel market operates at exceptional scale, with convenience stores generating over $450 billion in fuel sales in 2025 and selling an estimated 80% of the fuel Americans buy, according to the National Association of Convenience Stores (NACS). Drivers increasingly pay for that fuel by card, which now represents 81% of transactions at the pump, up from 64% a decade ago . That shift has made modern payment infrastructure at the pump a necessity for US fuel retailers, central to how they serve customers.
This partnership meets that need by bringing Nayax payment infrastructure to US fuel retailers alongside Allied, a partner retailers know and trust. Allied has served the retail petroleum industry since 1978, and its systems, including the NeXGen PRIME forecourt controller, operate at tens of thousands of locations nationwide. Nayax Fuel, a dedicated vertical within the Nayax group, extends that foundation with a comprehensive portfolio spanning payment at the dispenser, forecourt control, and station management, building on the successful integration of OTI PetroSmart and Roseman.
"Allied and Nayax share an important focus on making the forecourt work better for retailers," said Eli Bar, General Manager, Nayax Fuel. "Allied is one of the most respected names in US fuel retail, and our payment technology and solution give its customers a fuel-specific option built to meet the same high standard."
"The US fuel industry is moving through a modernization cycle, and operators need both reliable forecourt systems and modern payment solutions at the pump" said Bob Danford, Director of Engineering & Development at Allied Electronics, Inc. “Nayax complements our solutions with a purpose-built payment terminal backed by global payments infrastructure, allowing us to deliver the complete package to retailers together.”
Nayax Fuel will present its fuel retail solutions at the NACS Show, October 7-9, 2026, at the Las Vegas Convention Center. Visitors can find Nayax at booth C6385.

From a corner store in Tennessee to American military bases overseas, Allied’s core business is supplying best-in-class forecourt solutions. Specifically, Allied Electronics, Inc. proudly manufactures and sells the NeXGen PRIME forecourt controller.

# Context-enrichment: Nayax enters US fuel retail via Allied Electronics partnership
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- Nayax (Nasdaq/TASE: NYAX) announced a **distribution partnership** with **Allied Electronics, Inc.** on **2026-10-06**, under which Allied will distribute Nayax Fuel's **VPOS Media 5** outdoor payment terminal to US fuel retailers. This is Nayax's first dedicated US-fuel go-to-market move.
- **What was actually "done" is thin.** This is a **channel/distribution agreement**, not a product launch, a signed retailer rollout, or an acquisition. No terms, no exclusivity, no revenue commitment, no unit/site targets, no live-install count disclosed. The deliverable is "Allied will carry our terminal alongside its forecourt controller." Timing is transparently a **trade-show play**: the PR exists to seed the **NACS Show, Oct 7-9 2026, booth C6385** — this is marketing calendar, not a deal milestone.
- Allied's asset is its **NeXGen PRIME forecourt controller**, in service since 1978 and installed at "**tens of thousands**" of US sites (no precise figure). Nayax rides that installed base as a route to market; the forecourt controller is the system that talks to the dispenser, and VPOS Media 5 is the card-reading outdoor payment terminal that must integrate with it.
- **Why structured this way / what it reveals:** Nayax has essentially no US fuel presence and no incumbent OPT certification muscle, so the fastest entry is to piggyback on a trusted forecourt-controller vendor rather than win retailer-by-retailer. The PR anchors to **flattering, unquestionable macro stats** — $450B C-store fuel sales (2025, NACS), 81% of pump transactions on cards (up from 64% a decade ago) — because it has **no deal-specific traction to show**. The macro TAM is real; Nayax's capture of it is entirely unproven. Classic "announced, not adopted."

## [1] Competitors / peers
- US **outdoor payment terminal (OPT) / forecourt** market is an **oligopoly**: **Gilbarco Veeder-Root** (clear leader) and **Verifone** jointly ~39% of fuel/parking OPT installs; top five (Gilbarco, NCR, **Dover Fueling Solutions**, Verifone, PDI) ~52-55% of 2025 revenue; top five ~71% of deployments (per OPT market-research reports, directional). **Invenco by GVR** (Gilbarco's open-platform arm) makes FlexPay terminals, factory-installed in Gilbarco Encore dispensers, and has an EMV interoperability cooperation with Verifone.
- **Position: new entrant, bottom of the stack.** Nayax Fuel is a sub-scale challenger against entrenched incumbents who (a) own the dispensers (Gilbarco/Dover), (b) own the in-store POS (Gilbarco Passport, Verifone Commander), and (c) already hold the EMV/OPT certifications and oil-brand approvals that take years. Nayax's VPOS Media 5 is a credible terminal but faces a certification + oil-major-approval moat it has not shown it has cleared in the US.
- **Why the landscape is this way / second-order (analysis):** Fuel payments lock-in runs through the **dispenser + forecourt controller + brand network approvals**, not the terminal alone. Incumbents win because switching the OPT usually means touching certified dispenser integrations. Nayax's Allied route is smart precisely because it **borrows an incumbent's controller relationship** — but it also means Nayax is a **component supplier dependent on Allied's channel**, not the system owner. The margin/standard-setting power stays with whoever owns the controller + certifications.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Nayax fuel lineage is **not new**: it acquired **OTI (with the PetroSmart fuel unit) in 2022** and **Roseman Engineering (fuel/EV fleet, AVI) on 2024-04-01**; "Nayax Fuel" is the branded vertical assembled from those. So fuel is an existing global line — the news is purely **US market entry for it**, via a distributor.
- Broader arc: IPO ~**July 2025 (~$145M)**; two layoff rounds (**6% Jul 2025, 3% Apr 2026**); serial M&A — **Lynkwell** (EV, Dec 2025), **Unipaas** card-present partnership (Dec 2025), **IPS Group** smart-parking ($350M, closed 2026-10-01). 2026 guide rev ~$510-520M but still **net-loss-making** with cut FCF conversion. See [[Nayax completes acquisition of IPS Group]], [[Nayax announces acquisition of Lynkwell]], [[Nayax raises about $145 million in Wall Street IPO]], [[Nayax cuts 3% of workforce in second layoff round]], [[Nayax cuts 6% of workforce in first major layoffs]], [[Nayax partners with Unipaas on card-present payments]], [[Nayax launches VPOS Media 4 terminal for UK self-service market]].
- **Why the company acts this way (analysis):** Nayax's core is a **commodity-ish transaction take-rate** business that must bolt on recurring, vertical software (EV, parking, fuel) to justify a software multiple. US fuel is a large, card-heavy, modernization-cycle market — attractive on paper. But a **distribution partnership is the cheapest possible way to plant a flag**: zero capital, zero disclosed commitment, maximal PR. It fits the "add a vertical, tell a TAM story" pattern, consistent with the VPOS Media 4 UK self-service launch — a geographic/vertical extension of the same terminal family, not a new capability.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** The product (VPOS Media, fuel vertical) already exists; the only new element is a **US distribution channel via Allied**. No new technology, no new economics disclosed.
- **Traction: essentially none shown.** No signed retailers, no committed sites, no units, no revenue — only Allied's own "tens of thousands" of controller installs, which are Allied's footprint, **not** confirmed Nayax terminal placements. "Announced" ≠ "live." The real proof point (not present) would be certified oil-brand approvals and a first multi-site rollout.
- **Where the value-add is / isn't (analysis):** If it works, Nayax captures **payment-processing take-rate** on fuel transactions routed through its terminals + its payfac rails — that is the margin it controls and the reason to do this. BUT the value is bottlenecked by (a) **certification/brand-approval timelines** it hasn't demonstrated, (b) **dependence on Allied's sales motion**, and (c) incumbents who can bundle dispenser + controller + POS + OPT and undercut a standalone terminal. **Who captures the margin:** the processing take-rate can accrue to Nayax, but customer ownership and the forecourt standard stay with Allied/the controller vendors — so Nayax risks being a **swappable terminal supplier**, not the platform.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Plans:** present at NACS (Oct 7-9 2026); convert Allied's installed base into VPOS Media 5 placements over time. Tailwind: an estimated **30,000+ US fuel sites still not fully EMV-compliant** as of early 2026 (per OPT market research) — a genuine replacement/upgrade cycle Nayax is trying to ride.
- **Sentiment:** this is a **minor, low-information announcement** for an issuer the market is already skeptical of (the much larger $350M debt-funded IPS deal and loss-making profile dominate the NYAX story; see [[Nayax completes acquisition of IPS Group]]). A distribution tie-up with no numbers is unlikely to move the stock.
- **Risks / why it may underdeliver (analysis):** incumbent lock-in (dispenser + certifications), long US fuel sales cycles, dependence on a single distributor, and the EMV-upgrade window partly already served by Gilbarco/Verifone/Invenco. **Second-order:** the EMV laggard sites that remain are disproportionately **small, price-sensitive independents** — exactly the hardest, lowest-margin segment to convert, which may be why incumbents left them and why a low-cost challenger terminal is positioned here. A flag-plant, not a share-shift — until a real rollout number appears.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US fuel retail is a large, mature, fragmented channel: convenience stores generated >$450bn in fuel sales in 2025 and sell ~80% of US fuel (per NACS, via the GlobeNewswire release). The store count is still growing — 122,620 fuel-selling c-stores in 2026, an eight-year high (+768 locations / +0.6% YoY, per NACS via marketscale.com, Sep 2026). The relevant demand driver is a payments-modernization cycle at the forecourt: card share at the pump reached 81% of transactions vs 64% a decade ago (per NACS, via the release). "Why now": the automated-fuel-dispenser EMV liability shift took effect April 2021 (Visa/Mastercard), pushing fraud liability onto non-compliant retailers; EMV upgrades run ~$30k/store and new EMV pumps $20k+ (per csnews.com / cspdailynews.com), so a long replacement/retrofit tail is still working through the installed base — the structural opening Nayax is targeting. Barriers to entry are distribution and trust (installed forecourt-controller relationships, certifications), not capital — which is exactly why Nayax entered via a distribution partner rather than direct.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: installed device/site count, attach of payment+software per site, and (for Nayax group) TPV, processing take rate and recurring-revenue mix. Basis of competition in US forecourt payments = distribution + certified integration with the forecourt controller, not headline price. Incumbents: **Gilbarco Veeder-Root** (Invenco "FlexPay 6" outdoor terminals, contactless standard) and **Verifone** (Commander POS; Verifone claims ~55,000 sites / ~40% of US c-stores, per gasstationcompliancehub.com) dominate dispenser-integrated payments; **NCR** competes mainly at the in-store POS layer. Recent move: Nayax's 2026-10-06 distribution deal with Allied Electronics (petroleum forecourt supplier since 1978; its NeXGen PRIME controller runs at "tens of thousands" of US sites) brings the VPOS Media 5 outdoor terminal to US fuel; Nayax Fuel is the vertical built on the integrated OTI PetroSmart + Roseman assets (per release / investing.com). Position: **new entrant / niche challenger** in US forecourt payments — late vs entrenched Gilbarco/Verifone, but leveraging Allied's existing base as distribution. Moat is Allied's distribution reach plus Nayax's global payments/acquiring stack (80+ acquirer integrations), not yet US fuel scale `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Nayax group economics (not US-fuel-specific) from Q2 2026 (per globenewswire.com / investing.com, 2026-08): revenue ~$123M (+28% YoY, +21% organic); TPV $2.06bn (+29%); processing margin 40.5% (from 39.1%); recurring revenue 72% of sales; installed base 1.55M devices / 125k customers; net loss $10.1M (vs +$11.7M PY) as SBC rose; FY2026 guidance rev $510–520M and adj EBITDA $85–90M; market cap ~$2.58bn.

- External peers Gilbarco Veeder-Root (Vontier segment) and Verifone (private, owned by Britton/formerly public) — no clean, comparable standalone forecourt-payments revenue/EV publicly sourced → **no data**; distribution not computed, qualitative comparison only.
- Internal comps (same company, trajectory): [[Nayax raises about $145 million in Wall Street IPO]], [[Nayax completes acquisition of IPS Group]], [[Nayax announces acquisition of Lynkwell]], [[Nayax launches VPOS Media 4 terminal for UK self-service market]], [[Nayax cuts 3% of workforce in second layoff round]].
At ~5x forward sales on ~28% growth the group screens broadly in-line for a profitable-at-EBITDA, GAAP-loss payments platform — not obviously rich, but not cheap given GAAP losses `(analysis)`.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Distribution-only entry, not direct demand** — this is an announced distribution agreement, not committed site volumes; economics (Nayax's share per terminal, who carries fraud liability, hardware vs recurring split) are undisclosed. Pump-through into Allied's base is unproven → revenue contribution could be immaterial for years.
2. **Entrenched incumbents + late entry** — Gilbarco/Verifone own the dispenser-integrated relationships and certifications; a new EMV terminal must win on already-equipped forecourts (EMV shift was 2021), so the near-term TAM is retrofit/replacement, not greenfield. Disintermediation risk: value may stay with the forecourt-controller/dispenser layer, leaving Nayax a thin payments slice.
3. **Group profitability drift** — Q2 flipped to a GAAP net loss on rising SBC while FY free-cash-flow outlook was cut (per stocktitan.net); fuel is one more acquisition-fed vertical (IPS, Lynkwell, OTI PetroSmart, Roseman) layering integration/execution risk on a company already running layoffs (2025 and 2026).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` A toe-hold, not a re-rating: Nayax converts its unattended-payments playbook into US forecourt via partner distribution, probing whether Gilbarco/Verifone's grip is loosening as the modernization cycle extends. Falsifiable thesis: if Nayax Fuel US becomes material, expect it to disclose named site wins or a US-fuel revenue line within ~2–4 quarters. Trigger/catalyst: NACS Show follow-through (booth C6385, Oct 7–9 2026) and any first multi-site retailer rollout. What breaks the thesis: no disclosed US fuel traction by FY2027 while incumbents retain share → the deal was a distribution footnote, not a channel entry.
