# Context-enrichment: M-KOPA hits $600M record revenue and 10 million customers
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
M-KOPA's 15-year-anniversary PR (2026-10-08) reports **FY2025 revenue of $600M (+45% YoY)**, **10 million cumulative customers** (milestone actually hit July 2026), **3 million customers added in the past year**, across **5 African markets — Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, Uganda, South Africa**. Operationally: ~10,000 new customers/day, 2,500+ staff, ~50,000 agents, 10,000+ e-motorcycles/3-wheelers in Kenya.

**What the PR buries (the real story).** The headline from a year ago — M-KOPA's "first-ever profit of $9.2M in 2024" (see [[M-KOPA posts first profit of $9.2 million in 2024]]) — **has been restated to a $21.2M LOSS** after the first PwC-audited accounts corrected expected-credit-loss (ECL) accounting, interest recognition on impaired loans and loan-population completeness. The 2025 result is a **$4.8M net profit on $600M revenue — a sub-1% net margin.** So "maintained profitability" is literally true but razor-thin, and last year's celebrated profit never really existed.

**Why structured this way.** The anniversary frame (15 years / 10M / $600M) anchors to flattering cumulative milestones and the top line, steering attention away from the restatement and the margin compression. The deeper tell: **device-product gross margin nearly halved, 27.0%→15.9%**, while interest income rose +45% to ~$236M — but ECL ate ~$91M (38.5% of interest income) and the company ran a **$69M operating CASH OUTFLOW** despite the accounting profit. The hardware is no longer the profit engine; lending is, and lending is where the credit stress sits (single-source: Launch Base Africa's reading of UK filings — high-confidence but not independently cross-checked against Companies House).

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Sun King** (ex-Greenlight Planet): FY revenue **~$814.7M**, ~10M customers, ~35% global PAYG solar share, $156M–$286M securitizations + $40M equity (Dec 2025). **Larger than M-KOPA by revenue** but pure off-grid solar.
- **d.light**: 2025 **$300M** raise (largest African startup round of the year), ~$1B financing capacity. PAYG solar.
- **Watu Credit**: 2025 net profit ~KES 4.8B (~$37M), +14x YoY — **more profitable in absolute terms than M-KOPA's $4.8M** on a motorcycle-financing/EV model.
- **Mogo Kenya**: boda/logbook loans; hit with a predatory-lending class action (Oct 2025).
- Adjacent fintechs: **Moniepoint** (unicorn, $110M Series C, >$1B valuation — see [[CNBC names Moniepoint, PiggyVest, M-KOPA among top 300 fintechs]]), **FairMoney** (~$750M annual revenue), **Tala**, **Branch** — payments/pure-digital-lending lane, not device-locked.

**Why the lay of the land is this way (analysis).** M-KOPA's moat is not scale (Sun King is bigger) but the **device-locked PAYG + cross-sell flywheel**: the phone is both collateral and distribution for digital loans, insurance and e-mobility. The vertical, Finnish KilpiTek acquisition (see below, [[M-KOPA acquires KilpiTek Oy for $8M]]) is a deliberate move to own the lock-on-default enforcement tech — the hard power that makes thin-file lending underwritable. Second-order: as device margins commoditize, the company's value increasingly rests on the lending book, which makes credit quality — not customer count — the number that matters.

## [2] Company history / fit
Founded 2011 as financed solar-home-systems. Diversification ladder: smartphones (2020) → digital loans (2022) → e-mobility (2023) → health insurance via Turaco (Jan 2024). 2023 raised **$255M ($200M debt + $55M equity**, Sumitomo-led). Reached 5M customers Sept 2024; >$1.5B credit disbursed cumulatively. Nigeria crossed 1M customers in 2025 after ₦320B (~$234M) disbursed since 2019 (see [[M-KOPA surpasses one million customers in Nigeria]], [[M-KOPA Nigeria targets informal economy after $169 million disbursed]]). 2025 **$160M Series F** (Sumitomo-led, ~half primary / ~half secondary/ESOP exits). $22.5M credit line unlocked for South Africa, Apr 2026 (see [[M-KOPA unlocks $22.5M credit for South African operations]]).

**Why the company acts this way (analysis).** Commodity solar take-rate → needs recurring, higher-margin lending revenue → hence the push into smartphones + digital credit + e-mobility, each cross-sold onto the same locked device and daily-payment rail. The business is structurally capital-intensive (receivables grow faster than cash), which explains the perpetual debt raising and the $69M operating cash outflow — growth is funded, not self-financing.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Nothing *new* in the model here — this is a milestone/earnings re-statement, not a product launch. Genuine traction: real customer growth (+3M/yr, 10M total) and real revenue scale ($600M, +45%). But the de-PR'd value-add is **weaker than the headline**: product margins halved, 2024's profit was an accounting mirage, and 2025's $4.8M profit is dwarfed by the $91M ECL charge and $69M cash outflow.

**Who captures the margin (analysis).** Increasingly the lending book, not hardware — but that margin is being handed straight back as credit losses (ECL = 38.5% of interest income; 47% ECL coverage of gross receivables). What underpins any multiple is underwriting edge on thin-file customers + enforcement (device lock); what breaks it is FX stress across KES/NGN/GHS/UGX/ZAR and rising defaults in a weakening consumer environment.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Continued diversification (e-mobility scaling, insurance), deeper South Africa and Nigeria pushes, and in-housing enforcement tech via KilpiTek. Sentiment is positive on growth but should be tempered: valuation is **unconfirmed** (the circulated "$1.5B" is cumulative credit disbursed, not equity value; M-KOPA is not a confirmed unicorn). Governance overhangs are live: a racial-equity share-scheme petition (Njoki, May 2025 — see [[Former employee accuses M-KOPA of racism in share scheme]], [[M-KOPA faces lawsuit alleging racial equity disparity]]) and **co-founder Chad Larson's CMA complaint (Nov 2025) alleging an ESOP buyback at a ~95% discount.**

**Why it matters second-order (analysis).** The deeper the lock-on-default capability (KilpiTek), the more acute the reputational/regulatory risk — Kenyan complaints of phones staying bricked after full repayment are already public. A thin-margin, cash-outflow lender scaling credit to low-income borrowers is more fragile the faster it grows: the customer-count story and the credit-risk story point in opposite directions.

**Sector & drivers.** African embedded finance / asset-financing (PAYGo). Market size: Africa embedded finance put at ~$13.2bn in 2025, projected to $18bn by 2030 at ~8.1% CAGR (per ResearchAndMarkets, via BusinessWire, 2025-11-26); the BNPL/device-financing slice ~$6.5bn in 2026, ~$16.8bn by 2029 (per ResearchAndMarkets, via Yahoo Finance, 2026). Structure: fragmented, capital-intensive (balance-sheet lending funded by equity + debt facilities), high regulatory and FX exposure across 5 markets (Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, Uganda, South Africa). Barrier = proprietary alternative-data underwriting (mobile usage / repayment patterns) + agent distribution (~50k agents, ~10k onboarded/day). Why now: PAYGo model pivoting from solar to smartphones, digital credit and e-mobility as device demand among the underbanked grows; but the sector is under scrutiny — one MAF Lab dataset finds only ~51% of PAYGo customers fully repay at 1.5x loan tenor, and bad debt is the second-largest cost after manufacturing (per Acumen "PAYGo at a Crossroads", 2026-04). (analysis)

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: active customers, GMV/credit disbursed, interest income, expected-credit-loss (ECL) rate, product gross margin. Key players — M-KOPA (protagonist, solar→device→EV), [[Watu posts record $37 million profit from smartphone financing]] (Kenya, smartphone/boda financing, record $37m profit FY, now eyeing LatAm per [[Watu Credit plans Latin America expansion]]), Lipa Later (BNPL, named anchor in Africa BNPL report), plus mobile-money incumbents (M-PESA/Faraja) layering credit. Basis of competition: underwriting quality + cost of capital + distribution reach, not price. Recent moves: M-KOPA crossed 1m customers in Nigeria and disbursed $169m there in 2025 ([[M-KOPA Nigeria targets informal economy after $169 million disbursed]], 2026-03). Protagonist position: scale leader (10m cumulative customers, 15yr track record) but transitioning its profit engine — ahead on distribution, moat = alt-data underwriting + agent network. (analysis)

**Comps & multiples.** De-PR the headline first: the $600M "record revenue" = $363.4m product revenue + $236.3m interest income; the "10 million customers" is CUMULATIVE/lifetime, while ACTIVE customers end-2025 were 3.4m (+49% YoY) (per Launch Base Africa, 2026-10-08). Profitability is thin and lending-driven:
- Net profit $4.8m (vs restated 2024 loss of $21.2m).
- Product gross margin collapsed 27.0%→15.9%; product gross profit $57.9m (down from $67.9m) despite revenue +45% — cost of sales +66.6%.
- Lending now the main engine: interest income $236.3m, less ECL charge $90.9m → risk-adjusted lending contribution $145.4m, >2x product gross profit.
- ECL provision balance $155.5m on $328.4m receivables ≈ 47% coverage.
Internal comp (private): Watu record profit $37m ([[Watu posts record $37 million profit from smartphone financing]], 2026-05) vs M-KOPA net profit $4.8m — Watu far more profitable at smaller scale, i.e. M-KOPA is trading scale/growth for margin. Valuation: M-KOPA has raised ~$865m total; Series F $166m closed 2025-11-13 (per PitchBook, via WebSearch) — post-money market valuation not publicly disclosed → EV/Revenue and EV/EBITDA = no data (private, round terms not published). Trading multiples [UNSOURCED]. Qualitative: priced as a growth lender, not a hardware retailer.

**Risk flags.**
1. Credit risk / rising ECLs — $90.9m credit-loss charge is 38.5% of interest income and the ECL balance covers ~47% of receivables; if repayment deteriorates (sector repayment at 1.5x tenor ~51%), the lending contribution that now carries the P&L erodes fast. Second-order: a thin $4.8m net profit flips to loss on a small ECL uptick.
2. Funding dependence / cash burn — operating cash flow was -$69.0m in 2025 (worse than -$61.7m in 2024); borrowings $237.7m, interest paid $37.9m, equity only $118.4m. The model consumes external equity ($59.6m raised) + debt ($61.8m new) each year; any funding-market tightening or rate rise directly throttles originations.
3. Margin disintermediation on hardware — product gross margin halving to 15.9% means the device-sale leg barely covers itself; M-KOPA is effectively a subprime consumer lender wearing a solar/phone retailer's clothing, concentrating risk in the loan book and FX across 5 currencies.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a re-rating of the PAYGo story from "asset-financier" to "alternative-data consumer lender" — the question is no longer hardware volume but loan-book quality and cost of capital. (analysis) Falsifiable thesis: if the next disclosure shows ECL charge/interest income holding ≤~40% while active customers keep compounding, the lending model is durable; trigger to watch — credit-loss ratio, operating-cash-flow trajectory, and whether the Series F cash lengthens runway or just funds originations. What breaks it: a step-up in defaults in Nigeria (newest, fastest-growing market, +$169m disbursed) combined with local-currency depreciation.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (headline read).** GROWTH-BEAT on scale, QUALITY-MISS on economics · FY2025 revenue $600M (headline, +45% YoY; management's own number) · net profit just $4.8M on $599.8m gross income (net margin ~0.8%) · driver: lending now out-earns hardware, but a $90.9m credit-loss charge gutted the bottom line · no formal guidance (private company, self-reported). M-KOPA is private and not IR-covered: all figures are self-reported (impact report / press release) with secondary reporting from Launch Base Africa's read of the accounts; where unverifiable, marked [UNSOURCED].

**Key figures (with growth; FY2025 vs FY2024 self-reported).**
- Headline revenue: $600M (+45% YoY vs ~$416M in 2024). This is the top-line the company markets.
- Revenue from contracts with customers (product sales): $363.4m (+45% YoY). Cost of sales +66.6% to $305.6m, so gross profit on goods FELL to $57.9m from $67.9m in 2024. Product gross margin collapsed from 27.0% to 15.9%.
- Interest income (EIR method): $236.3m (+45% YoY). After $90.9m expected-credit-loss charge, risk-adjusted lending contribution $145.4m — more than 2x the $57.9m product gross profit.
- Net profit: $4.8m, vs a RESTATED 2024 loss of $21.2m. (Flag: the 2024 press cycle reported a $9.2m first-ever profit — see [[M-KOPA posts first profit of $9.2 million in 2024]]; the FY2025 accounts restate 2024 as a loss, so the "first profit" narrative does not survive restatement.)
- Customers: 10m cumulative (+3m in the past year), reached ~6 years after the first million; ~10,000 onboarded/day; micropayments at 23 txns/sec. ACTIVE customers 3.4m at end-2025, +49% YoY — the more economically meaningful base.
- Credit book: gross trade/loan/lease receivables $328.4m; ECL provision $155.5m → ~47% coverage ratio. FY2025 ECL charge $90.9m = 38.5% of interest income.
- Operating footprint: 5 markets (Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, Uganda, South Africa); >40,000 agents; >2,500 staff; ~50,000 income-earning agents.

**By segment / driver.** Growth mix has flipped from hardware to credit. Product sales still the largest revenue line ($363.4m) but their gross profit shrank in absolute terms as cost of sales outpaced revenue — margin halved to 15.9%. The profit engine is now lending: interest income $236.3m, risk-adjusted contribution $145.4m, >2x product gross profit. Geographically, M-KOPA now acquires more customers OUTSIDE Kenya than inside it; Nigeria is the fastest-growing market (>1m customers in 2025, ₦320bn disbursed since 2019 — see [[M-KOPA surpasses one million customers in Nigeria]], [[M-KOPA Nigeria targets informal economy after $169 million disbursed]]). Expansion beyond solar into smartphones (since 2020), digital credit, and e-mobility (>10,000 e-motorcycles/3-wheelers financed in Kenya).

**vs expectations / prior period.** No public consensus exists — private, no analyst coverage, so beat/miss is purely qualitative vs the company's own trajectory. Scale momentum is real and decelerating in % terms but accelerating in absolute terms: revenue +66% in 2024 → +45% in 2025 (growth rate slowing, base now ~4x larger); customers took ~6 years to go 1m→10m. Active base +49% YoY (3.4m). On economics the YoY read is NEGATIVE despite revenue growth: product gross margin 27.0%→15.9%, and net profit is thin ($4.8m) with a prior-year loss RESTATEMENT (2024: reported +$9.2m → restated -$21.2m). Trend vs base: [[M-KOPA posts first profit of $9.2 million in 2024]] (the restatement reverses this), [[M-KOPA unlocks $22.5M credit for South African operations]], [[East African fintechs turn profitable rather than unicorns]].

**Guidance / forward.** None given — private company, no formal outlook. Management tone (self-reported) emphasizes scale milestones (15 years, 10m customers, $600M, daily onboarding, e-mobility) and goes quiet on: unit economics, the margin collapse, the 47% ECL coverage, and the 2024 restatement. Independent read (analysis): at +45% off a larger base, 2026 revenue likely lands in the $800m–$900m range if lending-led growth holds, but net profitability is fragile and hostage to credit losses and FX across frontier markets. Funding context: ~$650m raised over ~21 rounds, latest Series F July 2025 ([UNSOURCED] for exact amount/valuation); CB Insights cumulative ~$649m. Also disclosed alongside results: $8m acquisition of Finnish software firm KilpiTek — see [[M-KOPA acquires KilpiTek Oy for $8M]].

**Thesis-flags.**
1. Credit losses are the whole story. ECL charge $90.9m = 38.5% of interest income, and provisions cover ~47% of the gross book — M-KOPA expects to lose nearly half of what it is owed. Why it matters: with net profit only $4.8m, a modest further deterioration in repayment (Nigeria NPL ~7%, above the 5% regulatory threshold) wipes out profitability entirely. Second-order: growth and credit quality are coupled — pushing 10,000 new sub-prime borrowers/day is also pushing the loss rate.
2. Margin mix shift = de facto a lender, not a hardware financier. Product gross margin halved to 15.9%; profit now comes from interest, not devices. Why it matters: valuation/multiple should be a frontier-market consumer-lender's (credit-cycle, provisioning risk), not a growth-hardware fintech's. Second-order: the moat is repayment enforcement (phone lockout as collateral), which invites regulatory and reputational risk.
3. 2024 restatement erodes trust in self-reported figures. The marketed "first-ever profit of $9.2m" (2024) became a restated $21.2m loss. Why it matters: with no auditor-of-record visibility and no IR coverage, headline numbers (incl. the $600M top line vs $363.4m product revenue + $236.3m interest) require skeptical handling — "revenue" blends product sales and interest income.
4. FX / frontier concentration. Growth is increasingly Nigeria- and non-Kenya-led; naira and multi-currency exposure compound credit risk. No specific FX-loss disclosure found in the 2025 results [no data]. Second-order: a currency shock in a top market hits both the dollar revenue line and repayment capacity simultaneously.
