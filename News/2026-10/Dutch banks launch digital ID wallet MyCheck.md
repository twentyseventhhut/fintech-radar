 A group of major Dutch companies is working on a digital identity wallet called MyCheck . This app will let people prove their identity online, log in to services, verify their age, and sign documents, all in one place. It is designed to share as little personal data as possible. 
Who is behind it 
MyCheck is backed by banks ABN AMRO, Rabobank, and ASN Bank, telecom company KPN, insurer Achmea, and consultancy Deloitte. Insurers a.s.r. and Nationale-Nederlanden also plan to use the wallet. The companies announced the project on 13 October.
What it does 
According to its creators, MyCheck is a secure and easy-to-use app for handling digital interactions, documents, and personal data. It lets users log in to different organisations with one trusted app instead of many accounts and passwords. People can use it to prove their age, share address details when applying for things like a driving licence or passport, and review or sign agreements digitally within the app.
The main idea is that users control what information they share and only provide what is needed. The app shows which data an organisation wants, and users can choose to allow or deny access. For age checks, for example, someone can prove they are old enough for a product or service without revealing their name, address, or full date of birth.

The EU rules 
This project is a response to new European rules on digital identity, called eIDAS 2.0. These rules require every EU country to have a system by the end of 2026 that lets residents share their identity and personal data online. The Dutch government has said it supports organisations testing how eIDAS 2.0-compliant wallets could work in the Netherlands.
The companies have offered to launch MyCheck in partnership with the government and say it would be free for both the state and users.
A ‘strategic’ Dutch alternative 
The companies say MyCheck is not just about meeting regulations. They see it as a Dutch alternative to similar services from big international tech firms. In their view, it is strategically important to keep control of key digital infrastructure and citizens’ identity data within the Netherlands.
For businesses and public organisations, the wallet could change how identification, applications, and contracts are managed. By sharing pre-approved data directly, users would not have to fill out the same forms as often, according to the companies.
What is not yet clear 
Right now, MyCheck is still being developed and is not yet available to download. Questions remain about when it will launch, how widely it will be accepted, and how a privately built identity system will be governed and overseen. The project comes at a time when the nation is having a broader discussion about digital identity and data protection, including the government’s DigiD login system and recent debates about online age verification and privacy risks.

# Context-enrichment: Dutch banks launch digital ID wallet MyCheck
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**FRESHNESS: fresh.** First surfacing in the corpus of the MyCheck wallet / this Dutch private-sector consortium. No prior note on MyCheck; the adjacent [[Visa white paper links digital ID wallets to payments]] (2026-03) is a thematic precedent on citizen wallets + payments, not the same event. Verdict: a genuine NEW development (consortium formation + wallet build announced 13 Oct 2026), not a re-run.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- On **13 Oct 2026** a coalition of Dutch firms — banks **ABN AMRO, Rabobank, ASN Bank**, telecom **KPN**, insurer **Achmea**, consultancy **Deloitte** (insurers a.s.r. and Nationale-Nederlanden flagged as prospective users) — announced they are *developing* a digital identity wallet called **MyCheck**: prove identity online, log in to services, verify age, share address attributes (driving licence/passport flows), and sign documents — with data-minimisation ("share only what's needed").
- **De-PR'd reality: this is an ANNOUNCEMENT, not a launch.** The app is "still being developed and is not yet available to download" (dutchbrief). No firm launch date; one secondary source floats a Q2 2027 target (unconfirmed, treat as hypothesis). No user numbers, no merchant/relying-party commitments, no governance/oversight model disclosed.
- **Why framed this way:** the consortium explicitly positions MyCheck as a "Dutch/sovereign alternative" to Big-Tech identity services, and offers to run it **free** for the state and users. → Why structured so: a free, sovereignty-branded pitch is a bid to pre-empt the lagging government wallet and lock in the position of national ID infrastructure before eIDAS 2.0 forces the market open. The free price signals they expect to monetise elsewhere (relying-party fees, cross-sell into banking/insurance) — the economics are deliberately unstated.
- **Regulatory anchor:** eIDAS 2.0 requires every EU state to provide an EUDI-wallet system; the legal deadline is **24 Dec 2026** (member states must make a wallet available). This is the "why now".

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Government NL-Wallet** (Dutch Ministry of the Interior reference wallet) — the designated first *certified* EUDI wallet. BUT the NL rollout has slipped: NL **postponed to late 2027 after a pilot drew only 57 users** (Euronews, Sep 2026). → MyCheck is racing into the vacuum left by a stalled public wallet.
- **Big-Tech identity** (Apple/Google wallet ID, Sign-in-with-Apple/Google) — the explicit foil in the PR.
- **Incumbent Dutch rails:** **DigiD** (government login) for public services; **iDEAL**-ecosystem identity and existing bank-ID flows. The banks already own trusted-login real estate — MyCheck is an attempt to extend that into eIDAS-grade attributes.
- Peer private/consortium ID efforts: [[Persona launches Persona Connect at Money20 20]], [[Didit raises $7.5 million for identity fraud infrastructure]], [[Glide Identity raises over $20 million Series A]], [[UK regulators say digital ID could fight financial crime]] — but those are KYC/fraud-infra vendors, not a bank-backed consumer EUDI wallet.
- **Position: catching up / parity-by-default.** Announced-stage only; no live product. → Why the lay of the land is this way: eIDAS forces both a public wallet and certified private wallets, so the real contest is over distribution and relying-party acceptance, not features. Second-order: whoever gets accepted by banks, telcos and government services first captures the default — and this consortium *is* those acceptors, which is its one structural edge.

## [2] Company history / fit
- ABN AMRO and Rabobank are the two large Dutch retail banks; both already run digital-rail / AI-payments initiatives in the corpus: [[Worldline and ABN AMRO extend Dutch payments partnership]], [[ABN AMRO completes first blockchain-based smart derivatives contract]], [[Mastercard and Rabobank complete Netherlands' first AI agent payment]], [[ACI Worldwide powers Rabobank's Wero instant payments]].
- Fit: logical. Banks are the natural KYC/identity custodians; eIDAS + AMLR will *require* banks to accept EUDI wallets (bank compliance from ~Jul 2027). → Why they act now: rather than be forced to *consume* a government or Big-Tech wallet, the banks would rather *own the wallet* — controlling the identity layer protects their customer relationship and KYC moat from disintermediation. Building it as a free consortium spreads cost and signals "public-good", easing a government blessing.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty is modest.** eIDAS-compliant wallets with data-minimisation, age-proof-without-DoB, and attribute sharing are the mandated template, not a MyCheck invention. The differentiator is *who* is building it (the trusted bank/telco/insurer bloc) and the sovereignty framing — distribution, not technology.
- **Traction: zero so far.** No downloads, no live relying parties, no user numbers. Everything is "announced / will". Per the anti-PR gate: piloted/framed ≠ live — MyCheck isn't even piloted yet.
- → Why value-add is real-or-not, deeper: the value is a *distribution option*, not a product. If the banks + KPN + insurers mandate MyCheck for their own login/onboarding, it instantly gets a mass relying-party base the government wallet lacks — that's the real asset. What breaks it: eIDAS interoperability means a user can bring *any* certified wallet, so acceptance can't be exclusively captured; and if the state certifies/pushes NL-Wallet, MyCheck becomes one of several, commoditised. Who captures value then shifts to whoever controls the issuance of high-value attributes (government source documents).

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Next: the consortium seeks a government partnership and certification; watch for a firm launch date (unverified Q2 2027 target), first relying parties, and whether a.s.r./Nationale-Nederlanden convert from "plan to use" to committed.
- Backdrop: EU-wide, **24 of 27 members are expected to miss the 24 Dec 2026 deadline** (Euronews); NL is among the laggards (rollout to 2027). Business acceptance mandated by Dec 2027; banks earlier (~Jul 2027) under AML rules. → Why the market goes this way: the deadline is legally hard but operationally missed everywhere, creating an 18–24 month window where private wallets can establish footholds. Counterintuitive second-order: a *stalled* government wallet is bullish for MyCheck's land-grab, but also raises governance/oversight questions (a privately built national ID system) that could trigger regulatory pushback and slow it.
- Risks: governance/oversight of a private identity rail; data-concentration in a bank-led consortium; competition/antitrust optics of the two big banks + incumbents co-owning national ID; dependence on government certification; monetisation opacity.
