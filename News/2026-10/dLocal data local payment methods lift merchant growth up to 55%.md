New dLocal data reveals how adding local payment methods can drive merchant transaction growth of up to 55%
 
3 min. read
 Montevideo, Uruguay, 19th October 2026 — Merchant data from dLocal’s 2026 research shows that a second local payment method has driven significant new transaction volume for merchants, rather than taking share from existing payment options. This seems to be a consistent pattern across several markets, highlighted by merchant case studies in the publication, The Local Code , launched today by the leading cross-border payment platform connecting global enterprise merchants to emerging markets.
The Local Code is a playbook for payments in markets of the future, and draws from dLocal’s own merchant transaction data, third-party market data including the standardized payment and commerce market intelligence platform PCMI, as well as dLocal’s 2026 retail consumer survey, across 17 markets in Africa, Asia, the Middle East and Latin America. 
A merchant in Brazil saw total transaction volume grow 55% within five months of adding Pix, the country’s instant payment system, alongside its existing payment options. A merchant in Peru had a similar result with Yape, which overtook cards as its leading payment method within three months and drove total transaction volume up more than 25% over six months. Colombia tells the same story from a different angle: Nequi’s share of one merchant’s transaction volume grew from 63% to 68% within six months, almost entirely from new spend rather than from existing cardholders switching to Nequi.
This points to local payment methods as a growth lever for merchants in emerging markets, not just a checkout function. Each additional local method reaches a different slice of the customer base, whether through an instant payment system, a local eWallet or a Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL) provider, rather than simply splitting share between options already on offer. For merchants weighing the cost of another integration against the return, these figures suggest that return often shows up as volume that wouldn’t otherwise exist.
The effect extends beyond total volume. In Mexico, a merchant that added Kueski, a local Buy Now, Pay Later  provider, alongside its existing payment options saw average order value rise 6.1% compared with debit card purchases.
Local payment methods also reduced processing costs for merchants in the data set. Processing costs fell 19% in Colombia following the addition of Nequi, and 30% in Peru after Yape became the merchant’s leading payment method.
Pedro Arnt, CEO at dLocal said, “Across the markets we work in, we keep seeing the same pattern: a second local payment method doesn’t eat into the first, it reaches customers the first one never touched. That matters beyond conversion. Millions of people across these markets have a phone and no credit history, which shouldn’t stop them buying things online. When merchants accept the payment methods people already use every day, that barrier goes away, and it becomes a real route into the digital economy for people who didn’t have access before.”
For merchants and payments leaders evaluating expansion in emerging markets, The Local Code reveals the local payment behaviors and market dynamics shaping these markets, helping them spot opportunities, guide payment strategy, and get ahead of the curve.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 19 Oct 2026 (Montevideo), dLocal launched *The Local Code*, a vendor "playbook" whose headline claim is that adding a second local payment method can lift a merchant's total transaction volume by "up to 55%." The 55% figure is a single Brazilian merchant that added Pix (five months); other cherry-picked case studies: a Peru merchant where Yape overtook cards within three months and drove total volume +25% over six months; Colombia where Nequi's share of one merchant grew 63%→68% in six months; Mexico where adding Kueski (BNPL) lifted average order value +6.1% vs debit; plus processing-cost cuts (−19% Colombia after Nequi, −30% Peru after Yape). Core thesis: a second local method *adds* new volume rather than cannibalizing existing options.

**[1] Who/scale.** dLocal (NASDAQ: DLO) is a cross-border emerging-markets payments platform ("One dLocal": one API/platform/contract) spanning 60+ countries in LatAm, Africa, Asia, the Middle East, with ~1,000 payment methods. It is a sell-side data drop timed to the firm's own momentum: Q2 2026 TPV hit US$17.7B (+92% y/y), revenue US$399.7M (+56% y/y), net profit ~US$55M, guidance raised — i.e. the "study" doubles as marketing for a business that benefits directly when merchants bolt on more local methods (each integration = more dLocal volume). Previously: [[dLocal reports Q1 2026 results with payment volume up 73%]].

**[2] Methodology (skeptical).** Draws from (a) dLocal's own merchant transaction data, (b) third-party PCMI (Payments & Commerce Market Intelligence) data, (c) dLocal's 2026 retail consumer survey, across 17 markets. Red flags: the top-line "up to 55%" is an *n=1* best-case merchant, not a cohort median; "up to" framing hides the distribution; survivorship/selection bias (merchants who adopted and stayed); correlation ≠ causation (merchants adding Pix/Yape may already be in high-growth verticals like ride-hailing/e-commerce that drove dLocal's own +92% TPV). No control group, confidence intervals, or sample size disclosed in the release.

**[3] Why it's directionally plausible anyway.** Independent PCMI data corroborate the macro trend: digital wallets + A2A are ~46% of LatAm e-commerce turnover (projected >51% by 2027); Pix ~22% of LatAm online purchases and taking share from debit; Yape/Nequi are "banks-as-wallets." So the *direction* — local methods expand the addressable base (unbanked/thin-file consumers with a phone, no credit history) — is well-supported even if the specific 55% is marketing. CEO Pedro Arnt frames it as financial inclusion, not just conversion.

**[4] Competitive/ecosystem read.** The playbook is a land-grab for merchant mindshare vs rivals: EBANX (dLocal's closest emerging-markets peer), PayU, Flutterwave (Africa), and global PSPs Adyen/Stripe moving down-market; dLocal also partners up-stack (recent ACI Worldwide orchestration deal). Related internal context: [[dLocal expands BNPL Fuse financing for 500 million buyers]], [[Kueski and dLocal collaborate on BNPL services in Mexico]], [[dLocal enables instant transfers via Colombia's Bre-B]]. Net: credible thought-leadership + an unaudited self-serving stat; useful as a signal of local-method adoption momentum, not as a benchmark.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. "Up to 55%" is one Brazilian merchant (n=1). What is the *median* uplift across all merchants studied, and how wide is the distribution? The release never says.
2. Is this causation or selection? Merchants that add Pix/Yape may already be fast-growing e-commerce/ride-hailing players — the same verticals behind dLocal's own +92% TPV.
3. No control group. How much of the "55% in five months" would have happened anyway from organic/market growth without adding Pix?
4. Who funded and designed the study? It is dLocal's own publication; dLocal earns more when merchants add methods — direct conflict of interest.
5. What is the sample size and selection rule for the 17 markets and the case-study merchants? Were losers/churned merchants excluded (survivorship bias)?
6. "New spend, not cannibalization" — how was incrementality measured? Without a holdout, attributing all new volume to the added method is unverifiable.
7. Time-window cherry-picking: 55% over "five months," 25% over "six months," AOV +6.1% — inconsistent windows suggest each stat was chosen to look best.
8. Processing-cost claims (−19%/−30%) depend on interchange/fee structures that vary by merchant and contract — are these representative or best-case?
9. How much of PCMI's independent data is actually cited vs used as decoration? The headline stat is dLocal's own, not PCMI's.
10. Does "total transaction volume +55%" mean count or value? Mixing of volume/value/AOV metrics across examples muddies comparison.
11. Macro-timing: wallet growth (Pix/Yape/Nequi) is a rising tide — merchants adding them ride the trend regardless of dLocal.
12. Marketing function: released same day as the playbook and amid raised guidance — is this research or demand-gen for more integrations?
13. Generalizability: results are LatAm-wallet-heavy; does "up to 55%" hold in Africa/Asia/Middle East markets also claimed by the 17-market scope?
14. Competitive durability: EBANX/PayU/Flutterwave offer the same local methods — is the uplift about dLocal specifically or about local methods generically (a thesis that commoditizes dLocal)?

**Importance: 2/5** — Directionally useful signal that local payment methods (Pix/Yape/Nequi/BNPL) drive incremental merchant volume in emerging markets, corroborated by independent PCMI data. But it is an unaudited, self-serving vendor press release built on cherry-picked single-merchant case studies and "up to" framing; no hard financial disclosure, no methodology rigor. Low standalone news weight; matters mainly as context for dLocal's strong Q2 2026 momentum and the broader LatAm A2A/wallet shift.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** dLocal plays in cross-border payments into emerging markets (EM), a market variously sized at ~US$237bn in 2026 growing to ~US$365bn by 2032 at ~7.3% CAGR (per Fortune Business Insights/researchnester, via web, as of 2026). The structurally relevant slice is high-growth EM corridors: LatAm cross-border was ~US$30.7bn in 2025 heading to ~US$52.5bn by 2033 (~7.4% CAGR), and Africa is the fastest grower at ~11.4% CAGR to ~US$1tn by 2035 (per Grand View/industry, via web). Structure: fragmented at the local level — each market has its own rails (Pix in Brazil, Yape in Peru, Nequi in Colombia), so the value is in aggregating hundreds of local methods behind one API ("One dLocal"); barriers are regulatory licensing in 60+ countries, local settlement/treasury, and network effects with global merchants. Why now: the secular shift from cards to instant-payment systems and local eWallets in EM — dLocal's own data (The Local Code, 19 Oct 2026) shows a second local method adds net-new volume (+55% in Brazil via Pix, +25% in Peru via Yape) rather than cannibalizing cards, turning local-method coverage into a growth lever, not just a checkout feature.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV (total payment volume), take rate (revenue/TPV), gross profit and gross-profit retention. dLocal Q1 2026: TPV US$14.1bn (+73% YoY), revenue US$335.9m (+55% YoY), implied take rate ≈ US$335.9m / US$14.1bn ≈ 2.4%; gross profit US$118.7m (+40% YoY), a record (per SEC 6-K / earnings, via web, 14 May 2026). Note the take-rate is compressing as TPV outgrows revenue (volume +73% vs revenue +55%) — typical of mix-shift to lower-margin large-merchant / payout flows. Key players: global infra (Adyen, Stripe, PayPal) going down-market into EM; EM specialists EBANX (Brazil), Flutterwave and Razorpay (Africa/India), and Rapyd (post-PayU). Basis of competition: breadth of local-method coverage + reliability + price. Recent moves: Rapyd closed its ~US$610m PayU GPSP acquisition (2025, at a ~US$4.5bn Rapyd valuation) to push >US$1bn combined revenue; dLocal itself has been bolting on BNPL (Fuse aggregator), remittances (Western Union, Cameroon) and stablecoin rails through 2026 (see internal comps). dLocal's position: ahead on LatAm depth and breadth of One-API coverage; moat = regulatory licensing stack + global-merchant switching costs (analysis), but thin on any single local method where domestic specialists dominate.

Adyen's H1 2026 net revenue grew ~19% YoY — slower than dLocal, yet richer on EV/Revenue, reflecting DM scale and mature margins.

Read: dLocal screens CHEAP vs Adyen on EV/Revenue (~2.5x vs ~5.8x) despite materially faster growth (rev +55% vs ~19%), and its forward P/E (~13.5x) sits well below trailing — consistent with the market pricing in take-rate compression and EM / FX / regulatory risk rather than a structural de-rating. Distribution not computed (only 2 clean public comps); qualitative comparison only.
Internal comps: [[dLocal reports Q1 2026 results with payment volume up 73%]] · [[dLocal reports $41B volume and $1B+ revenue in latest earnings]] · [[EBANX brings Colombia's Bre-B instant payments to global firms]] · [[dLocal debuts BNPL Fuse aggregator for emerging markets]]

**Risk flags.**
1. Take-rate compression — TPV (+73%) is outrunning revenue (+55%), so blended take rate (~2.4%) is eroding as mix shifts to large merchants and payout flows; the "add-a-local-method grows volume" story must be watched for whether it grows profit, not just processed TPV.
2. Disintermediation by local rails — Pix/Yape/Nequi are themselves low- or zero-cost instant systems; as they commoditize, the economics of merely routing to them can thin, pushing dLocal to defend margin via value-added (BNPL, FX, stablecoins) rather than pure processing.
3. EM concentration / FX & regulatory risk — revenue is tied to EM currencies and 60+ local licensing regimes; a single-market regulatory shift or devaluation hits reported revenue directly, which is part of why the stock trades below DM peers.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** The Local Code reframes local-method coverage as a top-of-funnel growth lever for merchants, not a defensive checkout box — a tailwind for aggregators like dLocal if it converts into retained gross profit (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if the +55%/+25% "net-new volume" effect is real at scale, dLocal's TPV growth should keep decoupling from (and leading) cannibalization fears and sustain gross-profit growth even as take rate drifts down. What would break it: if subsequent quarters show gross profit growth decelerating faster than TPV (i.e. the added volume is structurally low-margin), the "growth lever" narrative is really margin dilution. Trigger to watch: next earnings take-rate and gross-profit-retention trend.

## Earnings Review

> The news item itself is a product/research release (The Local Code), **not** an earnings report — "no full earnings report in the news." The following is dLocal's latest *reported* quarter as material context (dLocal is public, NASDAQ: DLO; IR-covered).

**Verdict (headline read) — Q2 2026 (reported 2026-08-14, call 2026-08-20): MIXED, lean BEAT on top line / miss on EPS.** Revenue $399.7M (+56% YoY; vs public consensus ~$366.8M, a ~$33M / ~+9% beat) · diluted EPS $0.18 (vs consensus $0.19, missed by ~$0.01 / ~5%) · driver = record TPV +92% YoY lifting revenue, offset by opex growth + net-take-rate compression on a large low-margin merchant · **guidance RAISED** on TPV and gross profit. Stock fell ~2-5% after-hours on the EPS miss and opex jump. irdb/semsearch was down (OpenRouter 402) — figures from WebSearch of the SEC 6-K / IR release + public aggregators.

**Key figures (with growth).**
- TPV $17.7bn (+92% YoY) — 7th consecutive quarter of 50%+ growth; record. (analysis) outsized vs the 55-73% run-rate, flagged by mgmt as partly one large merchant.
- Revenue $399.7M (+56% YoY, +19% QoQ) — record.
- Gross profit $127M (+29% YoY) — record, but growing far slower than TPV/revenue → margin mix shift.
- Net take rate (GP/TPV) 0.72%, down from 1.07% YoY and 0.84% QoQ. Mgmt: excluding one very large (low-take-rate) merchant and FX volatility, take rate ~flat QoQ with underlying TPV still +65% YoY.
- Operating profit $64M (+15% YoY); Operating profit / gross profit ratio 50% (+6pp QoQ) — operating leverage improving sequentially.
- Net income $55M (+28% YoY); diluted EPS $0.18.
- NRR 153% — 5th consecutive quarter above 140%.

**By segment / driver.** Growth broad-based across ride-hailing, travel, e-commerce and remittances; geographic breadth cited. The TPV surge is disproportionately a single very large merchant with a low take rate — explains why TPV (+92%) massively outran gross profit (+29%). This is the quarter's central tension: volume quality < volume quantity.

**vs expectations / prior period.** Revenue beat public consensus (~$366.8M, as of Aug 2026) by ~+9%; EPS $0.18 missed the ~$0.19 consensus by ~$0.01. Sequentially vs 1Q26 (TPV $14.1bn +73% YoY, revenue $335.9M +55% YoY, GP $118.7M +40% YoY, net take rate 0.84%): TPV and revenue accelerated, but gross-profit growth *decelerated* (40%→29% YoY) and net take rate compressed further (0.84%→0.72%). So the top-line beat came with weaker margin conversion QoQ. No in-base prior dLocal earnings note found (semsearch down); comparison drawn from public 1Q26 release.

**Guidance / forward.** RAISED vs prior FY2026 guidance. Prior (set/affirmed at 1Q26): TPV +50-60%, GP +22.5-27.5%, operating profit +27.5-32.5%. New FY2026: TPV +60-70% (midpoint ~65%), GP +25-30% (midpoint ~27.5%), operating profit maintained +27.5-32.5%. So TPV and GP ranges lifted on 1H strength; operating-profit range held, with mgmt citing non-recurring tax items and FX headwinds — the tell that the bottom-line conversion is where caution sits. Management tone: confident on volume/breadth; more guarded (silent-ish) on take-rate trajectory and opex, which is exactly where the EPS miss landed.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. Net take rate 1.07%→0.72% YoY — fact: GP growth (+29%) is a fraction of TPV growth (+92%). Why: concentration in one very large low-margin merchant + FX. Why it matters: the bull case rests on monetizing volume, not just moving it; sustained compression means revenue/TPV optics overstate earnings power. Second-order: single-merchant concentration is also a churn/renegotiation risk — if that merchant renegotiates or leaves, both TPV and the growth narrative snap back.
2. Guidance raised on TPV/GP but operating-profit range HELD — fact: they lifted volume/GP, not profit. Why: non-recurring tax + FX + opex. Why it matters: market read it as an EPS-quality miss (stock -2 to -5%). Second-order: signals the incremental TPV is low-incremental-margin; "growth at any take rate" invites a de-rating if investors reprice toward profit conversion.
3. NRR 153% (5th quarter >140%) and 50% OP/GP ratio (+6pp QoQ) — the genuine positives: existing-merchant expansion and sequential operating leverage remain strong, supporting the structural LatAm/EM cross-border thesis the news item (local payment methods driving +55% merchant volume) illustrates qualitatively.
