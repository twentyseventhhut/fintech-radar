# BlackRock files for third Bitcoin ETF, still no Solana or XRP

Latest NewsPublished Oct 20, 2026

BlackRock’s proposed Bitcoin fund would use options to limit potential losses and gains, adding another strategy to its existing Bitcoin and Ether ETF lineup.

Copy link Copied to clipboard Copy link

BlackRock, the world’s largest asset manager, is looking to expand its crypto fund lineup with a Bitcoin exchange-traded fund (ETF) that offers limited downside protection in exchange for capped gains.

The world’s largest asset manager on Monday filed a preliminary prospectus with the US Securities and Exchange Commission for the iShares Bitcoin Managed Protection ETF (PBIT).

The proposed ETF would use options linked to BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF (IBIT) rather than holding Bitcoin (BTC) directly, according to the preliminary prospectus filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC).

IBIT is the world’s largest spot Bitcoin ETF by assets. It holds Bitcoin directly and gives investors exposure through brokerage accounts. The fund had $65.7 billion in net assets as of Monday, according to BlackRock.

BlackRock also runs the iShares Ethereum Trust ETF (ETHA), a spot Ether fund with $8 billion in net assets. The asset manager has yet to launch US spot ETFs tracking other cryptocurrencies such as Solana or XRP.

ETF Store president Nate Geraci questioned BlackRock’s continued absence from the market for spot ETFs tracking other cryptocurrencies.

“Still shocked they’re sitting out on spot ETFs for SOL, XRP, HYPE, etc,” Geraci wrote on X on Monday. “BlackRock clearly has high conviction in only sticking [with] BTC [and] ETH. For now,” he added.

## BlackRock adds ETF strategies instead of new crypto assets

BlackRock entered the US spot Bitcoin ETF market in January 2024 with the launch of IBIT, followed by the ETHA in July that year.

In March 2026, its offerings expanded with the iShares Staked Ethereum Trust ETF (ETHB), which offers exposure to Ether and staking rewards. It was followed by the iShares Bitcoin Premium Income ETF (BITA) in June, designed to generate income through options trading.

BlackRock’s crypto ETF lineup includes two Bitcoin funds and two Ether funds. Source: BlackRock

Bloomberg ETF analyst James Seyffart said that PBIT would mark BlackRock’s third Bitcoin ETF filing, although similar “buffered” Bitcoin funds already exist from Calamos, First Trust and Innovator.

PBIT would use three overlapping sets of options contracts, typically replacing one each month, to seek downside protection while limiting potential gains.

Each options tranche would initially target a maximum loss exposure of about 10% to the underlying Bitcoin-linked asset, before fees and expenses. BlackRock cautioned that the fund “does not provide a fixed Tranche Protection Start Value, Floor or Tranche Cap Value,” implying investors could face greater losses.

# Context-enrichment: BlackRock files for iShares Bitcoin Managed Protection ETF (PBIT)
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

**Freshness verdict: FRESH.** This is a genuinely new SEC filing for a distinct product (a "buffer"/managed-protection Bitcoin ETF, ticker PBIT) filed ~Oct 19–20, 2026. It is NOT a reprint of BlackRock's spot fund IBIT, nor of the covered-call income fund [[BlackRock launches iShares Bitcoin Premium Income ETF (BITA)]]-type product (no such enriched prior note exists in corpus). Closest internal priors are tangential: [[UniCredit to offer product tied to BlackRock's Bitcoin ETF]] (a European wrapper over IBIT, Jul 2025) and [[Texas buys $5M in IBIT to start state bitcoin reserve]] (Dec 2025). Neither covers a BlackRock defined-outcome/buffer product. New product, new mechanism, new regulatory track → fresh.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- BlackRock filed a **preliminary prospectus** (485APOS-style) with the SEC for the **iShares Bitcoin Managed Protection ETF (PBIT)**. This is a FILING, not a launch — effective ~75 days post-submission, i.e. early **January 2027** absent SEC action. "Will offer" not "offers".
- Mechanism (de-PR'd): PBIT does **not hold BTC directly**. It builds synthetic exposure via **options on IBIT** (BlackRock's own $65.7B spot ETF) plus **fixed-income/cash collateral**. Per the filing it buys **deep in-the-money calls** (to replicate IBIT upside), **sells higher-strike calls** (which caps gains), and uses bonds to cushion losses. Three overlapping options tranches, each ~monthly roll.
- Protection is **soft, not guaranteed**: each tranche *targets* ~90% floor (initial max loss ~10% before fees). BlackRock explicitly warns there is **"no fixed Tranche Protection Start Value, Floor or Tranche Cap Value"** → investors can lose more than 10%. This is the key anti-PR point: "managed protection" ≠ Calamos-style 100% defined-outcome guarantee. It's a best-efforts rolling buffer.
- **Why structured this way:** stacking PBIT on top of IBIT options (rather than holding BTC) keeps AUM and fee flow inside BlackRock's own iShares stack and reuses IBIT's deep, liquid options market. The soft/"managed" floor (vs a hard defined-outcome floor reset annually) lets BlackRock market protection while avoiding the cap/fee drag and rigidity of a true buffer reset — i.e. a marketing-forward middle product.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Prior art clearly exists.** Calamos launched the self-styled "world's first downside-protected Bitcoin ETF" (CBOJ, 100% protection; plus CBXJ 90% and an 80% tier) in **Jan 2025** — ~21 months before this filing. First Trust and Innovator also already run defined-outcome/buffer Bitcoin funds (confirmed by Bloomberg's James Seyffart). So BlackRock is a **late follower** in buffered BTC, not a pioneer.
- Within BlackRock's own shelf, PBIT would be the **third Bitcoin ETF**: IBIT (spot, Jan 2024, $65.7B), BITA (premium income / covered-call, launched Jun 16 2026, targeting ~15–25% yield), and now PBIT (downside-protection). Plus two Ether funds: ETHA (spot) and ETHB (staked, Mar 2026).
- Position: **catching up / parity** on product type; **ahead on distribution**. The edge is not novelty but BlackRock's brand, iShares shelf, and IBIT's liquidity — the standard "fast-follower with the biggest distribution" play.
- **Why the landscape is this way (analysis):** defined-outcome equity ETFs (Innovator, First Trust) are a mature, crowded category; porting the structure to BTC was obvious once a liquid underlying (IBIT + listed options) existed. Calamos got there first on structure; BlackRock wins on gathering assets. The second-order dynamic: incumbents' first-mover edge erodes fast once BlackRock enters, because advisors default to iShares.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Trajectory: IBIT (Jan 2024) → ETHA (Jul 2024) → ETHB staked (Mar 2026) → BITA income (Jun 2026) → PBIT protection (Oct 2026 filing). A clear pattern: **land the spot vehicle, then layer options-overlay "outcome" products** (income, protection) on top.
- Notable gap: still **no spot SOL/XRP/HYPE** ETF despite rival filings. ETF Store's Nate Geraci flagged this publicly — BlackRock is doubling down on BTC/ETH monetization rather than breadth of assets.
- **Why the company acts this way:** a plain spot ETF is a commodity with a fee that only compresses (IBIT at 0.25%, and race-to-zero pressure). To defend/raise the fee multiple, BlackRock adds **engineered-outcome overlays** (income via BITA, protection via PBIT) that justify higher expense ratios and capture advisor/retail money that wants crypto exposure without full volatility. It is monetizing the IBIT franchise deeper rather than wider.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** The buffer-ETF structure is ~2 years old (Calamos Jan 2025); options-on-IBIT overlays are BlackRock's own recent template (BITA). The only genuine delta is "BlackRock's name + IBIT options liquidity behind a soft rolling buffer."
- **Traction: zero.** Nothing is live — it is a preliminary filing, effective ~Jan 2027 at earliest. No AUM, no flows, no fee disclosed in the summary. Importance must reflect announcement-not-adoption.
- **Value-add reality check:** the soft/"managed" floor is weaker than Calamos' hard defined-outcome guarantee, yet markets a similar promise. Investors give up meaningful upside (capped) and pay options/roll costs + a management fee, for protection that is NOT contractually fixed. In a flat-to-up BTC market the cap + roll drag likely leaves holders behind plain IBIT; the product mainly serves drawdown-averse/advisory allocations.
- **Who captures the margin (analysis):** BlackRock does, twice — IBIT fees on the underlying exposure plus PBIT's own fee on the overlay. The options counterparties and market-makers capture spread. The end investor trades upside for a best-efforts cushion and layered fees.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Path: ~75-day SEC effectiveness → potential launch ~**January 2027**; ticker PBIT. No guaranteed approval timeline; defined-outcome crypto funds are an established category so approval risk is modest but not zero.
- Backdrop: filing lands amid a soft tape — BTC ~$82.8K at time of coverage (down from the Oct 2025 ATH ~$126K per [[Bitcoin hits all-time high of $126,000 on October 6]]). A drawdown environment is exactly when "downside protection" marketing sells best — likely why BlackRock is pushing a protection product now (analysis).
- Risks/counterintuitive second-order: (1) the "protection" label invites regulatory/investor-expectation risk given the explicit no-fixed-floor caveat — mis-set expectations if BTC gaps down >10%; (2) product proliferation (spot + income + protection) risks **cannibalizing IBIT flows** and confusing advisors rather than growing the pie; (3) in a bull run the cap makes PBIT structurally underperform IBIT, limiting durable AUM to the risk-averse segment.
- Sentiment: analysts (Seyffart) treat it as routine line-extension, not a landmark — "third Bitcoin ETF," buffered peers already exist. Geraci's critique shifts the real question from "is this innovative?" (no) to "why is BlackRock monetizing BTC/ETH ever deeper while ceding the SOL/XRP spot race to rivals?"

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** PBIT sits at the intersection of two fast-growing niches: crypto ETFs and defined-outcome ("buffer"/"managed-protection") products. The defined-outcome ETF segment held ~$85.2bn AUM across ~473 ETFs / ~20 issuers in 2026, up from ~$280bn total "outcome ETF" assets BlackRock cited in Jan-2026 for the broader derivatives-ETF category (per BlackRock, via ifre.com, as of Jan-2026 — note the $85bn and $280bn figures use different definitions, so not directly additive). BlackRock itself projects outcome-ETF assets reaching ~$650bn by 2030; Cerulli projects buffer ETFs quadrupling to ~$334bn by 2030 (~35% CAGR) (per Morningstar/Cerulli). Structure: the crypto-ETF side is concentrated (IBIT ~59% spot-BTC share, $65.7–69bn net assets as of 20-Oct-2026 per BlackRock/TheBlock), while the buffer side is led by First Trust (~$40bn, 110 ETFs, end-2025) and Innovator/Goldman Sachs (~$37bn as of 30-Sep-2026). "Why now": record IBIT demand (biggest single-day inflow in ~12 months, $665m on 19-Oct-2026) + retail appetite to own volatile BTC with a loss cap drives the product-line extension; this is BlackRock monetizing its own IBIT franchise twice (fee on IBIT + fee on PBIT's options overlay).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: AUM/net flows, expense ratio, and (for buffer funds) the buffer % / cap % that define the payoff. Basis of competition: distribution + brand + fee, not product uniqueness — "buffered" BTC funds already exist. Key players and recent moves: Calamos (CBOY, 100%-protection BTC buffer, ~0.69% avg defined-outcome fee), First Trust and Innovator already ship buffered Bitcoin funds (per Bloomberg's James Seyffart, via Cointelegraph). BlackRock's own adjacent line: IBIT (spot, Jan-2024), ETHA (spot ETH, Jul-2024), ETHB (staked ETH, Mar-2026), BITA (Bitcoin Premium Income / options-income, Jun-2026), now PBIT (third BTC ETF). Protagonist's position: late entrant on the buffer mechanic (catching up on product) but dominant on distribution/scale (ahead) — moat is distribution + the captive IBIT reference asset, not novelty `(analysis)`. Notably BlackRock still sits out spot SOL/XRP/HYPE ETFs (ETF Store's Nate Geraci, 19-Oct-2026).

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation multiple applies — this is a product filing, not a deal/round; BlackRock's own group metrics (record $12.5tn AUM, Jul-2025) dwarf the fund and aren't comparable to a single ETF. Relevant comps are product-economics, not trading multiples:
- Calamos CBOY — 100% BTC downside protection, defined-outcome fee ~0.69% (Calamos/Morningstar).
- First Trust — largest buffer issuer, ~$40bn / 110 ETFs (end-2025).
- Innovator (now Goldman Sachs) — ~$37bn AUM, buffered BTC products live.
- IBIT (reference asset) — $65.7bn net assets, 20-Oct-2026; ETHA $8bn.
PBIT's quantified terms: three overlapping options tranches on IBIT, ~one rolled/month, each targeting ~10% max initial loss exposure (before fees) — buffer, not a hard floor. Fee and listing exchange still blank in the filing; effective ~75 days post-filing → earliest launch ~Jan-2027. Internal comps: [[Morgan Stanley files for Bitcoin and Solana ETFs]], [[Derivatives-based ETFs become Wall Street's fastest-growing segment]], [[UniCredit to offer product tied to BlackRock's Bitcoin ETF]], [[Bitcoin ETFs draw $2.7 billion as altcoins slip]]. "Rich/cheap" flag: fee = "no data" (blank in prospectus), so cost-competitiveness vs Calamos/First Trust cannot yet be assessed.

**Risk flags.**
1. SEC approval + timing uncertainty — the fund is a preliminary/post-effective filing; fee, floor and listing venue are blank, and BlackRock can revise terms. "Filed" ≠ "approved" ≠ "live"; launch is earliest ~Jan-2027, not now.
2. Options-cost drag + no hard floor — the buffer is funded by selling calls (caps upside) and is explicitly NOT a fixed floor ("does not provide a fixed Tranche Protection Start Value, Floor or Tranche Cap Value"); BTC's extreme vol makes options expensive, so the cap may be low and realized protection can lag a true -10% buffer. Second-order: a disappointing cap/fee could blunt demand.
3. Me-too / cannibalization — buffered BTC ETFs already exist (Calamos, First Trust, Innovator) and BlackRock already runs BITA (options-income on BTC); PBIT risks splitting its own franchise's flows rather than expanding the pie, and competes on fee it hasn't yet disclosed.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Signals the "institutionalization of crypto payoff engineering" — the growth is moving from owning BTC to shaping its return profile with derivatives, favoring scaled issuers (BlackRock, First Trust, Goldman/Innovator) over the asset itself `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: if PBIT launches and gathers >$1bn within ~12 months of listing, buffered-crypto becomes a durable category; trigger to watch = SEC effectiveness + the disclosed fee/cap. What breaks it: a low cap or high fee at launch, or a BTC bull run that makes capped-upside products look unattractive, leaving PBIT sub-scale like most niche buffer funds.
