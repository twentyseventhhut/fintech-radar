## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened.** On 2026-09-23 the Bank of Algeria (via its Monetary and Banking Council) authorized **Djezzy Payment Services**, a subsidiary of **Optimum Telecom Algérie** (the legal entity operating the Djezzy mobile brand), to *constitute itself as a Payment Service Provider (PSP)*. This is the **first PSP incorporation authorization ever granted** since the category was created — i.e. an *initial establishment approval*, not yet an operating licence. (Note date 2026-10-08 reflects the digest pickup; the regulatory event is 23 Sep.)

**Two-stage process (important nuance).** The approval only lets Djezzy *incorporate* the PSP entity. Djezzy then has **12 months from notification** to file a full licence application with the Bank of Algeria. Only after that licence is granted can it actually process transactions. So "payment services" are not live yet — this is step 1 of ≥2.

**Who Djezzy is.** Djezzy is one of Algeria's three mobile operators (alongside Mobilis/state-owned and Ooredoo). It is operated by Optimum Telecom Algérie; the state (Fonds National d'Investissement) holds the controlling stake after Algeria nationalized the former Orascom/VimpelCom-era asset. Large subscriber base = ready distribution rail for a wallet.

**Regulatory framework.** Algeria's **2023 Monetary and Banking Law** created the PSP status. The Bank of Algeria set the incorporation/licensing/operating rules via **Regulation No. 25-02 (April 2025)** and **Instruction No. 06-2025 (published 17 Aug 2025)** — the country's first dedicated PSP rulebook covering digital wallets, agent networks and consumer-fund protection. A regulatory sandbox is targeted for 2026. PSPs may offer payment accounts, execute payments/transfers and provide merchant payment services.

**Market context — why it matters.** Algeria is a deeply cash-heavy, under-banked market: financial inclusion is among the lowest in MENA, and MSME inclusion is the lowest in the region (~57% of adults cited as unbanked in local coverage). Yet digital-payment momentum is real — Algeria recorded **15.43M online transactions in H1 2026, +52% YoY**, with electronic-terminal payments up **81%**. Djezzy's licence is widely read as the **first step toward telco-led mobile money in Algeria** and a template signalling to other applicants what the central bank expects.

**Internal comps (telco-fintech / mobile-money licensing playbook).**
- [[Vodafone Egypt and Tunisie Telecom launch cross-border mobile payments]] — telco-led mobile payments elsewhere in North Africa (Egypt/Tunisia), the regional analogue Djezzy would aim at.
- [[Orange Money secures separate licence in Liberia]] — the Orange Money model of operators carving out separately-licensed payment entities, exactly the structure Djezzy is now building (separate PSP subsidiary).
- [[Airtel Africa targets London IPO for mobile money unit]] — illustrates how valuable a ring-fenced telco mobile-money unit can become once scaled; the long-run optionality for a Djezzy Payment Services entity.

**Playbook read.** This follows the classic African telco-fintech pattern (M-Pesa, MTN MoMo, Orange Money, Airtel Money): operator spins out a separately-licensed payments subsidiary, leans on its SIM base + agent/airtime network for distribution, and targets the unbanked majority. Algeria is a *late* entrant to this regulatory model, so Djezzy is first-mover in a greenfield, state-controlled market.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** North Africa is the least-developed mobile-money sub-region in Africa: ~30m accounts and ~$15bn annual transaction value, but it posted the continent's fastest transaction-volume growth at +60% (per GSMA "Mobile Economy MENA / State of the Industry", via connectingafrica.com & ecofinagency.com, 2025). Context: sub-Saharan Africa alone moved ~$1.4tn in 2025 and global mobile-money volumes doubled to ~$2tn over four years ([[Global mobile money volumes double to $2 trillion]]) — i.e. North Africa is a thin slice of a large, cash-displacing wave. Algeria specifically is a greenfield: ~57% of adults unbanked and ~90% of e-commerce still paid cash-on-delivery, against ~115% mobile penetration / ~95% of population with a cellular connection and ~73% internet penetration (per transfi.com, algeriatech.news, datareportal-style figures via search, Q2-2025). Structure: nascent and regulator-gated — Algeria's 2023 Monetary & Banking Law created the PSP status and the Bank of Algeria only set incorporation/licensing rules in April 2025; Djezzy is the FIRST PSP authorisation issued under the regime (per Bank of Algeria / techcabal.com, launchbaseafrica.com, 2026-09-23). "Why now": the gap between near-universal mobile reach and near-zero digital-payment adoption is exactly the wedge telco-led wallets exploited across Africa, now unlocked in Algeria by a fresh regulatory rail.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: active wallets, transaction value (TPV), take rate, and agent/merchant network density. Basis of competition in telco-led mobile money = distribution (SIM base + agent footprint) and regulatory licence, not product. Key players: this is an *initial* approval, not a live product — Djezzy is Algeria's #2 mobile operator and faces state-owned Mobilis and Ooredoo Algeria, plus the incumbent bank/card rails (CIB card, the slow bank-led e-payment push). Regional telco-fintech precedents frame the playbook: Airtel Africa sought a ~$2bn London IPO valuing its mobile-money unit at ~$10bn ([[Airtel Africa targets London IPO for mobile money unit]], 2026-05), and VEON — Djezzy's former parent — has pivoted into telco-banking elsewhere, e.g. a $20m injection into Mobilink Microfinance Bank in Pakistan ([[Veon invests $20m in Mobilink Microfinance Bank]], 2026-01). Protagonist's position: niche / earliest-mover with a 12-month runway to a final licence — ahead on regulatory sequencing, unproven on execution (analysis). Moat: a first-PSP licence + nationwide SIM/agent distribution is a real intangible+network moat *if* converted; today it is only optionality.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation, round, or revenue/TPV figure is attached to this news — it is a regulatory milestone, so trading-comp multiples are **no data / [UNSOURCED]**. For scale context only (NOT comparable, different stage/geo/disclosure): Airtel Africa's mobile-money unit implied ~$10bn enterprise value against a ~$2bn raise ([[Airtel Africa targets London IPO for mobile money unit]]) — a mature, pan-African, revenue-generating unit vs Djezzy's pre-launch single-market licence, so no multiple is computed. Internal comps (telco→fintech, emerging markets): [[Veon invests $20m in Mobilink Microfinance Bank]], [[Airtel Africa targets London IPO for mobile money unit]]. Distribution not computed — fewer than 3 comparable figures; qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
- **Approval ≠ product.** This is an *initial establishment* approval; Djezzy has up to 12 months to build infrastructure, governance and apply for the *final* operating licence (per Bank of Algeria, via techcabal.com). Nothing is live — "authorised" must not be read as "launched"; adoption, agent build-out and the final licence are all unproven second-order gates.
- **Ownership / grounding mismatch.** VEON exited Djezzy — FNI (Algeria's state fund) completed the buyout of VEON's stake in 2022, so Djezzy is now wholly state-owned (per ecofinagency.com / Wikipedia). Parent-level IR for VEON therefore does NOT ground Djezzy's economics; state ownership adds political/fiscal-priority risk and removes a listed-parent disclosure anchor. (No `djezzy`/`veon` entry in ir_latest.json — IR skipped.)
- **Deep cash dependence / adoption risk.** With ~90% of e-commerce paid cash-on-delivery and ~57% unbanked, habit and cash-infrastructure inertia — not technology — are the binding constraint; high SIM penetration does not auto-convert to active wallets (analysis).
- **Rails dependence & competition.** Interoperability with bank/card rails and against state-owned telcos Mobilis/Ooredoo is undefined; margin could be captured by whoever controls settlement/switch infrastructure.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a *new-entry / market-opening* signal, not a re-rating: Algeria's PSP regime is now operational and the first licence is out, so expect a sequence of follow-on telco and bank applications over 12–24 months (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Djezzy reaches a *final* operating licence and reports its first active-wallet/TPV numbers within ~12–18 months, Algeria becomes the next North-African mobile-money growth story after Egypt; trigger to watch = final-licence grant and first disclosed wallet/agent metrics. What breaks it: the 12-month window lapses without a final licence, or cash-on-delivery share fails to move — in which case this stays a paper milestone.
