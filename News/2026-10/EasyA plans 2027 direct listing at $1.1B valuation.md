EasyA, a platform for building blockchain and artificial intelligence applications, received an independent valuation of $1.1 billion as it prepares for a direct listing in the first half of next year.

EasyA, which says its app has more than 1.3 million users, is considering offering tokenized shares and is expanding into tokenization projects and applications involving AI agents.
EasyA, a platform that helps developers build blockchain and artificial intelligence applications, reached a $1.1 billion valuation as it prepares to go public in the first half of next year, co-founder and CEO Philip Kwok told CoinDesk in an interview Tuesday.
The platform, which was founded by brothers Philip and Dominic Kwok, plans to pursue a direct public listing, which would allow its existing shareholders to sell their stock publicly without the company raising fresh capital through a traditional initial public offering (IPO).
EasyA has already submitted a draft registration to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). The company has completed two years of audits by the accounting firm PKF and received an independent valuation based on its financial performance and growth prospects. The valuation does not necessarily indicate what investors will pay for EasyA once its shares begin trading.
The planned listing would give public-market investors access to a business that helps blockchain networks and AI companies attract developers and build applications. It would also test investor appetite for companies that sell the infrastructure and services behind those industries, rather than for cryptocurrencies or AI models themselves.
"Given we are going public, and we're going public via a direct listing, that means that we of course need to have an independent valuation," Phil Kwok said. He explained that the $1.1 billion figure was determined by a third party using audited financial statements, operating performance, growth plans and comparisons with publicly traded companies.
EasyA says its app has more than 1.3 million users, ranging from software developers to people using AI tools to create applications. It works with companies including Ripple and Solana,, helping them introduce their technology to developers through educational programs and hackathons.
The company also wants its shares to be available in tokenized form, potentially from the first day of public trading.
"That's definitely on the roadmap," Dominic Kwok, EasyA's co-founder and chief operating officer, said of the tokenization plans.
Tokenized equities are gaining attention from major financial firms. Robinhood built a blockchain for tokenized stocks , while analysts expect the SEC's work on tokenized securities to open opportunities for brokers, custodians and blockchain infrastructure providers. EasyA has yet to disclose how its proposed tokenized shares would be issued or traded.
EasyA is also expanding beyond its developer community through EasyA Labs, which is working on tokenization projects and applications involving AI agents.
Walden Yan, who went on to co-found Cognition A, the company behind Devin AI coding agent, was among the many developers who participated in its early hackathons, including at 2026 Consensus in Hong Kong . # Context-enrichment: EasyA plans 2027 direct listing at $1.1B valuation
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-14 EasyA — a London-based (founded 2022) blockchain/AI developer-education app — told CoinDesk in an interview it has (a) filed a **draft** (confidential) registration with the SEC, (b) obtained an **independent $1.1B valuation**, and (c) intends a **direct listing in H1 2027**, possibly with tokenized shares from day one. That is the whole announcement: an *interview*, not a public S-1, not a priced deal, not a trading date.

**What this actually is, de-PR'd:** a forward-looking *aspiration*, not an event. A direct listing lets **existing shareholders sell** without the company raising new capital. Nothing has priced; no investor has paid the $1.1B. The valuation came from **Uniqus** (a consulting firm) off financials **audited by PKF** — an accounting attestation of the books, not a market clearing price. EasyA itself concedes the figure "does not necessarily indicate what investors will pay."

**Why structured this way / what it reveals:** The company is **bootstrapped** — the Kwok brothers (CEO Phil, COO Dom) took no VC and hold ~100% equity, funded by upfront payments from ecosystem clients (Ripple, Solana, Google per interviews). A direct listing is the logical exit for founders who own everything and don't need primary capital: it monetizes their stake without dilution and without an underwriter setting a price. But that same structure means there is **no external price validation** anywhere in the cap table — the $1.1B is self-commissioned (EasyA paid for the valuation precisely *because* a listing requires one). The headline reads as a unicorn milestone; the substance is "two founders hired a valuer and told a reporter they plan to list in ~15 months." Separately flag: some outlets (KuCoin flash) printed "$11 billion" — that is a one-decimal typo; every primary source says $1.1B.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Crypto developer-education / learn-to-earn:** Buildspace (shut down 2024), LearnWeb3, Alchemy University, Cyfrin Updraft, Encode Club, Dapp University, Chainlink Bootcamps, Solana/Polkadot official academies. The category is crowded, mostly grant/ecosystem-funded, and has produced **no public company** — EasyA listing would be a category first.
- **Crypto/infra direct listings & IPOs (dated):** Coinbase direct listing Apr-2021; [[Bullish raises ~$1.1 billion in upsized IPO]] (Aug-2025, traditional IPO ~$1.1B raise); [[Lime raises $167M in US IPO with Uber as investor]] (Jul-2026); Circle IPO (2025); Gemini. Note these are exchanges/stablecoin/infra with large revenue — EasyA is an *education app*, a far smaller and unproven public-market archetype.
- **Tokenized-equity angle:** the day-one-tokenized-shares pitch rides a hot wave — [[SEC approves Nasdaq tokenized securities trading]], [[Nasdaq partners with Kraken for tokenized stock trading]], [[NYSE develops tokenized securities trading platform]], [[Bullish to acquire Equiniti for $4.2 billion to lead tokenized securities]], [[Kraken brings tokenized IPO access to retail investors via xStocks]], Robinhood's tokenized-stock chain. EasyA has **disclosed no mechanism** (issuer, chain, transfer rights) — it is narrative alignment, not a built product.

**Why the landscape is this way / second order:** Developer-education has no public comp because its revenue is lumpy ecosystem-marketing spend (networks pay to recruit devs), not durable SaaS. That is exactly why a *direct listing* (no roadshow price discovery, no new primary raise) is the path of least resistance — there is no natural institutional buyer base to underwrite a traditional IPO for this profile. The multiple EasyA is implicitly claiming ($1.1B on an edtech app) only holds if the market treats it as "AI/tokenization infra" rather than "crypto marketing agency." That reframing is the whole bull case — and it is unproven.

## [2] Company history / fit
Founded 2022 by brothers Phil and Dom Kwok. Grew from "earn-while-you-learn" blockchain lessons; hit 1M downloads (notably a key Polkadot education channel), now claims **>1.3M users** across devs and "vibe-coding"/AI-tool users. Works with 350+ academic institutions; runs hackathons (Consensus Hong Kong 2026) whose alumni include Walden Yan, co-founder of Cognition (Devin AI, ~$48B). Expanding via **EasyA Labs** into tokenization projects and AI-agent apps. Bootstrapped throughout — zero VC, clients pay upfront.

**Why the company acts this way:** With no VC clock and 100% founder equity, EasyA has no pressure to raise but *does* want liquidity and a public brand to (a) cash out founders/early staff and (b) re-rate itself from "edtech" to "AI+tokenization infra." Filing confidentially + commissioning a valuation + leaking via interview is a **pre-marketing** move: it manufactures a $1.1B "unicorn" headline ~15 months before any actual price test, seeding investor and ecosystem perception cheaply. The tokenized-shares line is brand positioning that lets a crypto-education firm claim the crypto-infra multiple.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Genuinely novel:** a bootstrapped crypto developer-education app would be a first-of-kind public listing, and day-one tokenized shares would be a real structural first if executed. **Real traction:** 1.3M users (self-reported, unaudited as to activity/MAU), 350+ institutions, named network clients (Ripple, Solana), notable hackathon alumni — these are credible and non-trivial for an edtech.

**But, de-PR'd:** everything load-bearing is **undisclosed or announced-not-live.** No revenue, margins, retention, or user-engagement figures ("won't release until the public filing"). The $1.1B is self-commissioned and unpriced. Tokenized shares have **no mechanism** disclosed and depend on an SEC regime still being built. "1.3M users" is a vanity metric — for a learn-to-earn app, downloads/sign-ups overstate durable, revenue-generating users; the durable question is *who pays EasyA and how repeatably* (networks' dev-marketing budgets are cyclical with the crypto cycle).

**Who captures the margin / what breaks it:** EasyA sits downstream of L1/L2 marketing budgets. Its revenue is a **derivative of crypto-cycle sentiment** — in a bull market networks overspend on dev acquisition; in a drawdown that budget is first cut. So the durable-vs-transactional split is adverse: the asset is a sales/marketing channel, not owned IP or recurring enterprise SaaS. That caps the multiple a *public* market would pay, which is precisely the gap between $1.1B (self-valued) and whatever prints in 2027.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Path: SEC review of the draft → (if cleared) public S-1 with actual financials → H1 2027 direct listing. Sentiment is cheerleading in crypto press ("unicorn," "without VC") but every serious caveat — unpriced valuation, undisclosed revenue, no tokenization mechanism, regulatory/market-condition dependence — is acknowledged even in the primary interview. Risks: (1) the real S-1 financials undercut the $1.1B; (2) a crypto-cycle downturn before H1-2027 compresses both narrative and the network-marketing revenue base; (3) tokenized-share plans slip or get pared to vapor; (4) direct listings with no anchor/greenshoe are **volatile** at open for thin-float, low-institutional-coverage names.

**Why the market may go this way / counterintuitive:** The bootstrapped, no-VC framing reads as strength ("disciplined, profitable") but on a direct listing it is a **fragility** — there is no VC-set reference price, no lock-up-managed float plan, and no institutional syndicate to stabilize trading, so price discovery is entirely at the open on a name with no public comps. Scarcity of a true peer cuts both ways: novelty draws attention, but absence of a comp means the $1.1B anchor is doing all the work and is the first thing a skeptical public market will discount.

**Sector & drivers.** EasyA sits at the intersection of two layers: crypto/Web3 developer education-and-acquisition (its core — it gets paid by protocols/clouds to funnel developers to their tech via courses, challenges and hackathons) and the 2025–26 crypto-firm public-listing wave. Web3 developer-services market is put at ~$5.09bn by end-2026, ~19.2% CAGR (per secondary citation, dev.events/industry roundups, as of Oct 2026) — directional only, treat as `[UNSOURCED]` at the primary level. Structure: EasyA's slice (sponsored dev-relations/education) is fragmented and project-funded — not a durable take-rate business but a budget line protocols cut first in a down-cycle. Barriers are low (content + community), moat is the 1.3m-user funnel and partner relationships (Ripple, Solana, Google, AWS), not technology. **Why now:** two converging catalysts — (1) the crypto-listing window is open (BitGo, Circle, Securitize, Consensys all public or filing in 2025–26), and (2) SEC movement on tokenized securities lets EasyA dangle "tokenized shares from day one" as differentiation. The deeper driver: issuers want to list into demand while the window holds; a direct listing in H1'27 is a forward intention, not a priced deal.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for a sponsor-funded education/DevRel model: active/registered users (1.3m claimed), paying-partner count and concentration, revenue per partner, and renewal/retention of sponsor contracts — none disclosed. EasyA's revenue and profit are explicitly withheld until the SEC filing goes effective (per Inc./CoinDesk, Oct 2026), so every efficiency KPI is `[UNSOURCED]`. Basis of competition is distribution (reach into developers) and partner trust, not price or product. The listing peers it implicitly benchmarks against — crypto-infra names going public — are a different, larger, revenue-disclosed class. Position: EasyA is a **niche** DevRel/education layer dressing itself in infra/tokenization clothing `(analysis)`; its moat (intangible: brand + community funnel) is real but thin versus custody/stablecoin/RWA peers with network effects and balance sheets.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Valuation is appraisal, not market-tested.** $1.1bn came from a third party on audited financials; a direct listing has no underwriter book-building or price-setting, so day-one price discovery is open — the company flags this itself. Second-order: a weak open repriced in public view damages the "unicorn" narrative and any tokenized-share liquidity thesis.
