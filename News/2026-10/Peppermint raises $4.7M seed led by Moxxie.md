Company’s AI-powered platform was purpose-built for clinical trial teams and hospital systems to transform clinical research into a scalable engine for financial growth and scientific discovery 



 Peppermint , an AI-powered platform that streamlines financial operations and accelerates growth for clinical research teams, today announced $4.7 million in seed funding led by Moxxie , with participation from Homebrew , Better Tomorrow Ventures , Layout Ventures , Digital Health Venture Partners , Night Capital , and angel investors Benjamin Mann and Evan Moore. The funding will further enable the founding team, who previously built payments and reconciliation infrastructure for Robinhood, to transform clinical research into a scalable engine for financial growth and scientific discovery.



Despite advances in the science and technology driving novel drug development, the industry still relies on an antiquated and fragmented financial framework. Instead of payments flowing smoothly between sponsors, CROs, health systems, and research sites, clinical research relies on a patchwork of systems that were never designed to work together. Every party absorbs the cost of that complexity. Health systems carry it across an entire research portfolio, where the finances of clinical research remain one of the least visible lines on the balance sheet. Sites feel it most acutely: One-third of sites have more than 90 days’ worth of overdue receivables, and 50% of sites have three months or less of operating capital, according to a 2024 survey by the Society for Clinical Research Sites (SCRS).



Peppermint is the first AI-powered financial operations platform designed exclusively for clinical research, bringing the uncompromising precision and auditable accuracy of clinical science to its own revenue cycle.



Co-founders David Freeman and VJ Thurimella met at X1, where they were the respective first business hire and first engineer. Together, they helped build the financial infrastructure behind the consumer credit card business that Robinhood acquired to launch its Gold Card. When they saw the inefficiency of financial systems behind clinical research – and how it was depriving research teams of not only cash, but visibility into how much a study actually generates in revenue, how much it costs to run, and the metrics behind both – they knew their fintech experience could break open the industry’s financial black box.



“At X1 and Robinhood we helped build systems designed for precision and scale, to process billions of dollars every year,” said Freeman. “We’re bringing that architecture to clinical research, converting earnings into friction-free collections that also provide CROs and sponsors with cleaner data from the source.”



By combining purpose-built AI with expert human billers, Peppermint replaces the manual monthly billing cycle with a system that ingests data from a site's clinical trial management software, cross-references it against payment history and trial contracts, invoices instantly and continuously, and pursues collections on partners’ behalf. Because each invoice is validated against the record before it goes out, CROs and sponsors receive cleaner data and fewer disputes, while research organizations get more time to focus on what matters most: carrying out clinical trials.



These changes help clinical research teams get paid faster while giving finance leaders a live view of the metrics that drive financial health. And by working within a site’s existing software system, Peppermint is able to pull the data it needs to perform tasks without any migration or workflow change.



Peppermint has helped its partners collect revenue 3x faster and reduce overdue invoices by threefold while reclaiming 20% of staff time spent on billing. The number of studies under management by Peppermint has tripled in three months, reflecting growing demand from clinical research teams and hospital systems across the country.



Building on this momentum, Peppermint will use the funding to expand its platform beyond collections into budget negotiation, expense management, and clinical trial analytics — a full financial ledger for research operations. For health systems, that means margin visibility across an entire research portfolio for the first time. As the pace of discovery accelerates, Peppermint is building the financial framework to scale with it.



"There is no standard financial infrastructure for clinical trial billing today — every dollar a site is owed has to be reconstructed by hand from disconnected systems," said Alex Roetter, Managing Director and General Partner at Moxxie. "Peppermint is bringing the discipline of modern fintech to one of healthcare's least-examined back offices."



For more information, please visit getpeppermint.ai .



 About Peppermint
 Peppermint is the first AI-powered financial operations platform designed exclusively for the teams running clinical research. Founded by David Freeman and VJ Thurimella, who built payments and reconciliation infrastructure at Robinhood and X1, Peppermint combines advanced AI with expert human billers to bring the industry’s highly valued rigor to its own revenue cycle. The platform turns months of manual reconciliation into a continuous, audited record of every visit, invoice, and dollar a site is owed. Peppermint is focusing on finance today so that everyone running clinical trials has the capacity, and clarity, to advance new therapies faster.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- $4.7M seed, led by Moxxie (Alex Roetter signs the quote); participation Homebrew, Better Tomorrow Ventures, Layout Ventures, Digital Health Venture Partners, Night Capital, angels Benjamin Mann (Anthropic co-founder) and Evan Moore (DoorDash). Seed stage, small ticket.
- What it actually does (de-PR'd): an **AI + human-biller accounts-receivable / collections tool for clinical-research SITES**. It ingests data from a site's existing CTMS, cross-references contracts + payment history, auto-invoices continuously, and chases collections on the site's behalf. So the real product is site-side RCM/collections, not a sponsor↔CRO payment rail.
- Traction claims (vendor-reported, unaudited): collect revenue "3x faster", overdue invoices cut "threefold", 20% of billing staff time reclaimed; studies under management "tripled in 3 months". → **Why these metrics:** all are percentage/multiplier framings off an unstated tiny base ("tripled in 3 months" from a seed-stage start = small absolute numbers). No GMV, no $ collected, no client count, no logos. Classic seed-PR anchoring.
- **Why structured this way:** founders' edge is Robinhood/X1 payments+reconciliation infra reused on a niche vertical (clinical-trial site finance) where the pain is acute and incumbents weak — a vertical-fintech "pick a neglected back office" play. The $4.7M size signals this is a conviction seed, not a scaled business.

## [1] Competitors / peers
The clinical-trial finance stack splits into two problems Peppermint's PR blurs:
- **Site/participant PAYMENTS (incumbents, outbound money to sites/patients):** Greenphire (ClinCard + eClinicalGPS, 6M+ site payments, ~80 countries; now owned by Suvoda), Medidata CTFM / Grants Manager (PICAS: 30k+ protocols, 350k+ investigator agreements), IQVIA, Advarra, Veeva. Also [[Dash Solutions launches dashClinical clinical-trial payments]] (2025-10, US disbursement rails for trials).
- **Site RECEIVABLES / collections (Peppermint's actual lane) — newer, thinner:** Ledger Run / ClinRun (AI CTA+budget+payments; claims 3 of top-10 pharma, 2 of top-5 CROs, 17k sites — far more traction), and TrialPay (AI agents chasing unpaid CRO invoices; testimonial recoveries $16K–$111K). Adjacent AI-AR: [[Lunos AI launches AI-driven accounts receivable platform]] (2025, B2B AR automation), [[Cylerity raises $4 million seed and $24 million debt facility]] (2025, AI claim-payment prediction). Healthcare-payments neighbors: [[Turquoise Health raises $40 million Series C for healthcare payments]], [[Inbox Health raises $20M for patient billing platform]], [[AblePay Health raises $20M Series A]].
- **Position:** catching up / behind. Ledger Run already claims blue-chip pharma+CRO logos and Greenphire has scale; Peppermint is a seed-stage late entrant to a vertical that already has an "AI-powered" first-mover claim. **Why:** the moat here is data integrations (CTMS access, contract parsing) + payer relationships, both of which incumbents already hold.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Founders Freeman (first business hire) & Thurimella (first engineer) at X1 — built the credit-card financial infra Robinhood acquired for its Gold Card. Logical fit: reconciliation/billing infra is directly transferable to the clinical-site billing black box. **Why this market:** they're applying scaled-fintech reconciliation discipline to a vertical that genuinely lacks standard infrastructure (Roetter: "every dollar a site is owed has to be reconstructed by hand"). The structural pull is real — the SCRS data (below) is a legitimate pain, not invented.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Is it new?** Partially. "AI for clinical-trial finance" is NOT new — Ledger Run explicitly claimed "first to bring AI-powered automation to clinical trial payments" and TrialPay does AI invoice-chasing. Peppermint's narrower wedge (continuous validate-before-invoice off CTMS data, no migration, human-in-the-loop billers for the SITE'S receivables) is a reasonable differentiation but not a category.
- **Real value-add (analysis):** the sharpest insight is attacking the SITE's cash-flow crisis, not sponsor payment ops. SCRS 2024: 1/3 of sites >90 days overdue receivables, 50% have ≤3 months operating capital; monthly→quarterly terms stretch AR from ~45 to ~137 days. A tool that accelerates site collections hits a survival problem → genuine willingness to pay. **But who captures the margin?** Sites are the cash-poorest party in the chain — the exact customers least able to pay SaaS fees. Peppermint likely needs a take-rate/collections-based model, which caps pricing power and makes revenue transactional, not durable SaaS. (hypothesis)
- **Traction reality:** no absolute numbers; all multipliers. "Tripled in 3 months" ≠ scale. Treat as pre-traction.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Plan: expand beyond collections into budget negotiation, expense management, trial analytics — a "full financial ledger for research operations," pitching health systems margin visibility across portfolios. **Why:** collections alone is a thin, transactional wedge; they must climb into the planning/budget layer (where Medidata PICAS + Ledger Run already sit) to justify a software multiple. That is a head-on collision with better-capitalized incumbents.
- Sentiment: investor appetite for vertical AI fintech + healthcare back-office is hot (Moxxie, Homebrew, BTV, Anthropic co-founder angel). **Counterintuitive second-order:** the macro tailwind (site financial distress) is also the demand risk — distressed customers churn and squeeze pricing; and if sponsors/CROs fix upstream payment terms (Greenphire/Suvoda push), the site-collections pain partly evaporates, disintermediating Peppermint's wedge.
- **Freshness:** FRESH. No prior note covers this round or this company; the same-tag March note is a different firm. Only potential-stale risk (none found).

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Peppermint sits at the intersection of clinical-trial financial operations and healthcare revenue-cycle management (RCM) — specifically the *site-side* billing/collections for clinical research sites, CROs and hospital research portfolios (ex-Robinhood/X1 founders). This is a subvertical of two larger markets: the broader healthcare RCM software market (~$40.6bn in 2026, ~9.5% CAGR to ~$57.3bn by 2030, per The Business Research Company / ResearchAndMarkets via GlobeNewswire, as of 2026-09) and the clinical-trial payments market (~$2.1bn in 2025 → ~$4.8bn by 2034, ~9.6% CAGR, per Dataintelo, as of 2026). The adjacent CTMS market (the systems Peppermint ingests from) is ~$2.4–2.8bn in 2026, ~15.6% CAGR (per Towards Healthcare / Coherent Market Insights). **Structure:** the *participant/patient-payment* layer is moderately consolidated — top-5 vendors ≈54–58% of revenue, led by Greenphire (merged with Suvoda, Apr 2025; >$7bn site payments facilitated) and IQVIA/Medidata adding payment modules — but the *site-side AR/collections* layer Peppermint attacks is genuinely greenfield and fragmented ("no standard financial infrastructure for clinical-trial billing," per Moxxie's Roetter in the note). **Why now:** site financial distress is acute — 1/3 of sites carry >90 days of overdue receivables and 50% have ≤3 months operating capital (2024 SCRS survey, cited in the note) — plus the AI-agent wave hitting back-office RCM (IQVIA launched an agentic-AI Clinical Trial Financial Suite in Sep 2025, per intuitionlabs). (analysis) The structural pain is real; the open question is whether it's a standalone platform or a feature incumbents absorb.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: DSO / days-sales-outstanding on site receivables, collection rate, % overdue >90 days, billing-staff hours reclaimed, studies/sites under management, net-revenue-leakage % (providers lose 3–10% of net revenue to inaccurate payments per Kubera's citation). **Players & basis of competition** — two distinct lanes:
- *Participant/patient payments* (disbursing to trial subjects/sites): Greenphire/Suvoda, Medidata Site Payments, IQVIA CTFS, Sitero, [[Dash Solutions launches dashClinical clinical-trial payments]] (80 countries, participant-facing). Peppermint does NOT primarily compete here.
- *Site-side AR / billing automation* (what Peppermint actually does — reconstruct what a site is owed, invoice, collect): TrialPay (Patients & Science) is the closest direct peer — AI invoice reconciliation + automated follow-ups, connects CTMS/ERP/sponsor portals, site-focused. Strategikon/Clinical Maestro covers budget/contract planning upstream.
**Recent peer moves:** IQVIA agentic Financial Suite (Sep 2025, claims ~50% faster payment processing, >$15bn processed); Greenphire–Suvoda merger (Apr 2025).
**Protagonist position:** niche/early, but in a lane with few dedicated players (analysis). Claimed traction: 3x faster collections, overdue invoices cut 3x, 20% billing-staff time reclaimed, studies under management tripled in 3 months — all vendor-reported, pre-de-PR, unaudited [UNSOURCED on absolute base]. Moat is thin at seed: integrations (CRIO, Veeva, RealTime, QuickBooks, NetSuite, Sage Intacct) + "human billers + AI" is a workflow/data-access play, not yet network effects or switching costs (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation disclosed for Peppermint (seed, $4.7M raised; post-money not public → "no data"). Internal seed/early comps in healthcare-payments/RCM infra:
- [[Kubera Health raises $6.5 million seed for healthcare payments]] — $6.5M seed (May 2026), contract-to-payment system of record for US healthcare (payor-provider, not clinical trials).
- [[Cylerity raises $4 million seed and $24 million debt facility]] — $4M seed + $24M debt (Oct 2025), AI predicting claim payment timing.

Distribution not computed (no comparable valuation figures). Qualitative: round size is modest and consistent with a true seed, not a frothy mark.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Feature-vs-platform / disintermediation.** IQVIA and Medidata already own the CTMS/payment rails and are bolting on AI financial suites (IQVIA Sep 2025, >$15bn processed). If sponsors/CROs standardize on an incumbent's native billing module, Peppermint's site-side wedge can be squeezed from above — the classic "will the platform eat the point solution" risk.
2. **Crowded-but-early with a direct twin.** TrialPay does nearly the same site-side AI invoicing/collections; differentiation at seed is unproven, and first-mover advantage in this lane is contestable.
3. **Dependence on others' rails + unaudited traction.** The product only works by reading a site's existing CTMS/accounting systems (CRIO, Veeva, etc.); integration access and data quality are a dependency and a potential gatekeeping risk. All performance metrics (3x faster, tripled studies) are vendor PR off a tiny early base — "tripled in three months" is a low-absolute-number claim, not evidence of scale (de-PR).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Peppermint is a bet that clinical-trial *site finance* is a distinct, underserved RCM vertical worth a standalone system of record — mirroring how Turquoise/Kubera verticalized payor-provider contracts. Falsifiable thesis: if dedicated site-AR tools (Peppermint, TrialPay) can show auditable, multi-site DSO reductions AND resist incumbent bundling, this becomes a fundable new category; **trigger to watch** — a Series A with disclosed site/ARR counts, or conversely an IQVIA/Greenphire launching a native site-collections module (which would validate demand while compressing the standalone opportunity).
