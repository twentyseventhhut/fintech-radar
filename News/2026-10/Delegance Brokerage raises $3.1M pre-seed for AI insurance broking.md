 NEW YORK , Oct. 20, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Delegance Brokerage, an AI-native commercial insurance brokerage challenging the traditional commission-based insurance model, today announced $3.1 million in pre-seed funding led by Grit Capital Partners, with participation from Core VC, Gilgamesh Ventures, and Outside VC.

 Delegance secured its funding after establishing its brokerage operations. The company is licensed in 51 jurisdictions, holds direct appointments with 25 insurance carriers including Chubb, Travelers, and The Hartford, and has secured more than $26 million in premium commitments for 2026.
 Challenging the traditional commission model 

 Delegance founder and CEO Alexander Ledbetter developed the company's business model after working at commercial insurance carrier AXA XL in 2023. During that time, he observed a large business renew its insurance policy year after year for more than a decade. Its broker collected a 20 percent commission annually, even as the coverage remained largely unchanged.
"That commission paid for paperwork, and the paperwork never got faster," said Ledbetter. "I taught myself to code so software could do that work. The business should keep the money the paperwork used to cost."
 How Delegance works 
Businesses can upload their existing insurance policies or answer a brief series of questions to obtain quotes. Delegance supports more than 4,000 business classifications and over 36 types of coverage, including general liability, workers' compensation, property, commercial auto, cyber, professional liability, and directors and officers insurance. Many business classifications qualify for instant quotes, while specialty and excess risks are directed to appropriate insurance markets through wholesale partners.
Delegance also automates much of the work traditionally associated with ongoing policy servicing. Through a single customer account, businesses can obtain certificates of insurance in seconds, request any of 64 types of endorsements and policy changes, file and track claims, and receive proactive renewal reviews. Orin, Delegance's AI agent, answers coverage questions using customers' existing policies through the company's online portal, email, phone, and chat. Licensed insurance brokers remain responsible for complex placements, claims, and coverage decisions.
By automating much of the servicing process, Delegance offers a flat, disclosed 5 percent fee in place of traditional brokerage commissions that typically range from 15 to 25 percent. According to the company, this model reduces total insurance costs by 10 to 20 percent on a typical placement while maintaining access to the same admitted insurance carriers.
 Serving businesses across industries 
Delegance serves businesses across a range of industries, including construction and skilled trades, commercial real estate, ecommerce and consumer brands, healthcare practices, and professional services. Private equity firms also use Delegance to consolidate insurance management across their portfolios through master insurance programs. In one example, an 11-property commercial real estate portfolio with more than $100 million in insured value is now managed under a single insurance program. The company also works with banks and software platforms that refer business customers seeking commercial insurance coverage.
 Supporting continued growth 
Delegance plans to use the new capital to expand its team of licensed insurance brokers and engineers while strengthening partnerships with banks, private equity firms, and software platforms.
 About Delegance Brokerage 
Delegance Brokerage is an AI-native commercial insurance brokerage headquartered in New York. It quotes, places, and services commercial insurance for businesses from single contractors to private equity portfolios, by web, mobile app, email, phone, chat, and API. Delegance charges a flat, disclosed fee in place of a traditional commission. The brokerage is licensed in 51 jurisdictions and appointed directly by 25 carriers. Learn more at delegancebrokerage.com .

# Context-enrichment: Delegance Brokerage raises $3.1M pre-seed for AI insurance broking
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Delegance Brokerage, a New-York AI-native commercial P&C insurance brokerage, closed a **$3.1M pre-seed** (announced Oct 20, 2026) led by **Grit Capital Partners** (Jennifer Byrne), with **Core VC** (Edwin Andrade), **Gilgamesh Ventures** (Andrew Endicott, Miguel Armaza) and **Outside VC** (Ethan Austin). The pitch: replace the traditional 15–25% premium-linked commission with a **flat, disclosed 5% fee**, which the company claims cuts total insurance cost 10–20% per placement while keeping the same admitted carriers.
- **Real substance vs PR.** This is a *distribution/brokerage* play, not a product-tech or carrier play. The de-PR'd reality: Delegance is a licensed retail broker (51 jurisdictions, 25 direct carrier appointments incl. Chubb, Travelers, The Hartford) that has layered an AI agent ("Orin") over standard servicing — quotes across 4,000+ class codes and 36+ coverage types, COIs, 64 endorsement types, claims, renewals. Licensed humans still own complex placements/claims. So the AI is **servicing automation**, not underwriting or risk-selection IP.
- **Why the fee structure is the whole story.** The flat-fee framing exists because the *incentive conflict* (broker paid more when client pays more) is the only genuinely defensible marketing wedge a sub-scale broker has against Marsh/Aon/Gallagher. The "$26M premium committed for 2026" figure is a **commitment, not bound premium** — at a 5% fee that implies ~$1.3M of fee revenue *if* it all converts, so the metric flatters by anchoring to premium not to realized fee income.
- **Why it matters (2nd order):** a 5% fee on a $26M book is ~$1.3M gross vs. the ~$3.9–6.5M a 15–25% commission broker would earn on the same book. Delegance is deliberately **shrinking the revenue per dollar of premium** and betting AI servicing makes the thinner margin profitable at volume. That only works if CAC and servicing cost fall faster than fee compression — unproven at pre-seed.

## [1] Competitors / peers
A crowded 2026 cohort of "AI-native broker, flat fee instead of commission" startups:
- **Coverwatch** — $4.5M pre-seed (Jul 2026, CoFound/Restive); *identical thesis* (AI-native commercial broker, flat fee, claims 20–40% premium reduction, 50+ carriers). Direct comp by stage and model. See external sources.
- [[Novella raises $21 million to expand AI wholesale insurance brokerage]] — but Novella is **wholesale/E&S** and explicitly *keeps* producers + commissions ("AI makes super-producers"), the opposite incentive bet.
- [[Outmarket AI raises $17 million Series A for insurance agencies]] — **software/vendor** to existing brokerages (AMS integration), not a broker; sells the shovel rather than competing on fee.
- [[Corgi raises $160 million Series B for AI insurance]] and [[Liberate raises $50 million Series B for insurance AI]] — far larger AI-insurance rounds, showing where capital actually concentrates.
- Incumbents: Marsh McLennan ($25.3B rev), Aon ($15.4B), Gallagher ($11.1B); big-3 commission-based pricing at 15–20% on $50M+ deals.
- **Why the landscape looks like this:** the 2023–25 mega-deal wave (Gallagher-AssuredPartners $13.5B, Aon-NFP $13B, Brown&Brown-Accession $9.8B, Marsh-McGriff $7.75B) consolidated the top and left the SMB/mid-market long tail fragmented — that tail is exactly what every AI-broker pre-seed is chasing. The flat-fee model is the common differentiator, which means **differentiation is already commoditized** before any of these reach Series A.

## [2] Company history / fit
Founder/CEO **Alexander Ledbetter** built the model after seeing, at carrier **AXA XL (2023)**, a client renew for a decade while a broker took 20% annually on unchanged coverage; he taught himself to code. Delegance is new (brokerage operations established pre-round). Fit is logical: a carrier-side insider attacks the servicing-rent that commissions pay for. **Why structured this way:** starting as a *licensed retail broker* (not a SaaS vendor) lets Delegance capture the fee directly rather than sell tooling — but it also means it owns E&O liability, regulatory load across 51 jurisdictions, and the human broker payroll it claims to automate away.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low-to-moderate.** Neither the AI-servicing layer nor the flat-fee pitch is new — Coverwatch pitched the near-identical model 3 months earlier, and flat-fee advisory (WithCoverage) predates both. The defensible asset is execution: carrier appointments + licensing breadth + a working servicing agent.
- **Traction: thin and PR-shaped.** The only hard numbers are structural (51 jurisdictions, 25 appointments) plus a **$26M premium *commitment*** — no bound premium, client count, retention, or realized revenue. "Many classes qualify for instant quotes" is a capability claim, not usage.
- **Who captures the margin (2nd order):** by voluntarily collapsing the take rate to 5%, Delegance bets the margin moves from *commission arbitrage* to *servicing-cost arbitrage*. But admitted carriers still set premium and own the risk; Delegance is a thin distribution layer. If AI servicing becomes table stakes (Outmarket-type tools sold to every incumbent broker), the 5% fee wedge erodes and Delegance has given up revenue without a durable moat — the central question shifts from "is the flat fee cheaper?" to "**can a sub-scale broker survive on 5% once incumbents bolt the same AI onto a 15% book?**"

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Use of funds: hire licensed brokers + engineers, deepen bank / PE / software referral partnerships (incl. consolidating PE-portfolio insurance under master programs). Market backdrop: US insurance brokerage ~$145.8B (2026), ~3.9% CAGR — a large, slow, consolidating pool. **Why the market may move this way:** softening commercial P&C rates in 2026 make fee-transparency pitches resonate with cost-pressured SMBs, and PE referral channels are a cheap distribution hack. **Counterintuitive 2nd-order risk:** the same consolidation that creates the opening also means any proven flat-fee playbook is an acqui-hire target for a Gallagher/Aon long before it reaches scale — "disruption" here likely exits as an M&A feature, not an independent incumbent-killer.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Market.** Delegance plays in US commercial insurance brokerage, a large, mature distribution layer. The US insurance brokerage market is estimated at roughly $146B in 2026, projected to ~$176B by 2031 (~3.9% CAGR), with retail brokers handling ~60% of placements. Commercial P&C distribution is dominated by incumbents — Marsh McLennan, Aon, WTW, Arthur J. Gallagher, Brown & Brown — that earn carrier-paid commissions typically 15–25% of premium. Delegance's wedge is replacing that commission with a flat, disclosed 5% fee, which it claims cuts total insurance cost 10–20% per placement while keeping the same admitted carriers (Chubb, Travelers, The Hartford; 25 direct appointments, licensed in 51 jurisdictions).

**Thesis tailwinds.** Two structural pressures favor fee-based, AI-native brokers: (1) commission-transparency and remuneration scrutiny is a recognized drag on the traditional model; (2) a talent crunch — ~400,000 insurance professionals set to retire by 2026 against a ~25% replacement-pipeline shortfall — raises the cost of the manual servicing labor Delegance aims to automate with "Orin," its AI agent for quoting, endorsements, COIs, claims tracking and renewal reviews. The flat-fee structure also removes the incentive to steer clients into pricier policies, which becomes more attractive above ~$100k premium.

**Competitive set.** This is a crowded, well-funded 2026 cohort of AI-native flat-fee commercial brokers, several ahead of Delegance:
- **Coverwatch** — closest comparable: $4.5M pre-seed (Jul 2026, CoFound/Restive), flat fee replacing commission, AI policy review + bids from 50+ carriers, claims 20–40% premium savings; targeting all 48 continental states by Q3 2026.
- **Counterpart** — $50M Series C (Apr 2026, Valor) in small-business management liability (adjacent, better capitalized).
- **Newfront** — the transparent-data "modern brokerage" exemplar, now acquired by WTW for ~$1.3B (validates the thesis but signals incumbent consolidation).
- Broader field includes embedded players (Cover Genius, ~$1.9B valuation) and a wave of AI-insurance startups ($2.68B raised across 69 rounds Jan–Sep 2026, already above all of 2025). Delegance's $3.1M pre-seed is small relative to this set; differentiation rests on execution, carrier breadth (25 appointments, 4,000+ classes, 36+ coverage types) and PE/bank/software referral channels (already $26M+ premium committed for 2026).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Thin differentiation vs. a crowded flat-fee cohort.** The "flat fee replacing 15–25% commission, AI-native, ~20% savings" pitch is nearly identical to Coverwatch and others; Delegance is not first and is relatively undercapitalized at $3.1M pre-seed.
2. **Incumbent consolidation / capital gap.** The WTW–Newfront deal shows incumbents absorbing the modern-brokerage thesis; mega-brokers and better-funded peers (Counterpart's $50M) can out-invest a pre-seed challenger on talent and carrier relationships.
3. **Unit-economics inversion of a 5% flat fee.** Flat fee only beats 15–25% commission for large accounts; on small premiums a 5% fee may not cover the cost of licensed-broker oversight and E&O, squeezing margins or pushing toward only large placements.
4. **Regulatory / licensing & E&O burden.** Carrier appointments are non-transferable, state-by-state permissions (unlicensed activity exposes to fines/suspension); maintaining 51 jurisdictions plus surplus-lines compliance and ~$1M+ E&O floors is costly and scales poorly for a lean startup.
5. **AI liability & oversight limits.** An AI agent cannot underwrite, hold authorization, or carry the balance sheet — licensed brokers must still own complex placements, claims and coverage decisions; AI output quality depends on fragmented policy data, and insurers are increasingly excluding AI-related damages, raising governance and E&O exposure.
6. **Carrier-relationship dependency.** The model relies on direct appointments with majors (Chubb, Travelers, Hartford) and wholesale partners for specialty/E&S risks; carriers could restrict appointments or push back on a fee model that pressures the commission economics they control.
7. **Early-stage traction risk.** $26M premium committed and a single 11-property CRE case are promising but unproven at scale; conversion of bank/PE/software referral partnerships into recurring volume is the key unvalidated assumption.
