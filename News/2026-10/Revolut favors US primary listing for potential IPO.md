# Context-enrichment: Revolut favors US primary listing for potential IPO
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 8 Oct 2026, CEO Nik Storonsky told Bloomberg TV that **if** Revolut lists, it would favor a **primary** listing in the **US** — citing "deep liquidity" and a bigger investor pool ("a gigantic market with a huge number of buyers who will compete fiercely"). A London/Nasdaq dual listing is still "under consideration," but the UK leg is now explicitly **secondary**. He reaffirmed the IPO is **"at least two years away" (not before 2028)** — unchanged from his April 2026 statement. Separately: once Revolut obtains a **full US national banking charter** (OCC gave **preliminary** approval in September 2026), it will roll out **credit cards and loans** and aims to "dominate" the US, with an offering "at par with Amex or JPMorgan Chase."

**+ Why this way / what it reveals:** This is a CEO **comment**, not a decision — "if it goes public," no filing, no bank, no date. The framing moved from a *symmetric* dual US+UK listing (widely floated Sept–Oct 2025) to **US-primary**, a signal to London (the Treasury has courted Revolut as a flagship IPO) that the City is now the junior venue. The hard, concrete item here is regulatory-contingent product rollout (cards/loans), which is downstream of a charter that is still only *preliminary*. Nothing is live.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Klarna** IPO'd on NYSE (Sept 2025) — a US-venue precedent for a European fintech; Revolut said then it felt no pressure to raise ([[Revolut says no need to raise capital despite Klarna IPO]]).
- **Nubank** (NYSE-listed, ~$60–80bn range) already overtook Revolut's private mark ([[This Week in Fintech Nubank worth more than Revolut]]) — a live public comp whose US listing underlines the liquidity argument.
- **Chime, Monzo, N26**: no comparable US primary-listing ambitions at this scale.
- Revolut's position: last financed at **$75bn** private ([[Revolut raises $3B at $75 billion valuation]], [[Revolut completes share sale at $75B valuation]]); targeting **$150–200bn** at IPO ([[Revolut targets up to 200B valuation in IPO two years out]]); a new secondary at **>$100bn** was flagged for H2 2026 ([[Revolut plans secondary share sale at $100-120 billion valuation]]).

**+ Why:** The US-venue tilt is a direct read-through from Klarna/Nubank: deep US liquidity supports a higher multiple than a thin London book. The gap vs. Nubank (profitable, public, scaling in LatAm) is the real pressure — a US listing is partly about matching Nubank's investor access.

## [2] Company history / fit
Storonsky's venue stance has **drifted** over 2025–26: previously cool on London → "dual US+UK listing widely discussed" ([[Revolut weighs $75B dual listing in New York and London]], [[Revolut explores dual listing in London and New York]]) → "US more likely than London, 2–3 years out" ([[Revolut signals US IPO two to three years away]]) → "$150–200bn, not before 2028" ([[Revolut targets up to 200B valuation in IPO two years out]]) → now "US **primary**." On licensing: UK full licence secured ([[Revolut secures full UK banking licence]]); US path switched from acquisition to de-novo OCC application ([[Revolut to seek own US banking license, drops acquisition]], [[Revolut files US bank charter application, names US CEO]], [[Revolut plans 2027 US bank launch with FDIC-insured and stablecoin accounts]]).

**+ Why:** Revolut's structural logic — it wants a software/platform multiple, not a bank multiple — pushes it toward the deepest capital market (US) and toward owning the full lending stack (charter → cards/loans → net-interest + fee income) to justify a $150bn+ ask.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Low novelty.** The US-lean was already explicit in Dec 2025 ([[Revolut signals US IPO two to three years away]]); the 2028 timeline and $150–200bn target are from April 2026; the OCC preliminary approval and 2027 US launch were already covered ([[Revolut plans 2027 US bank launch with FDIC-insured and stablecoin accounts]]). The only incremental wrinkle is the word **"primary"** (demoting London) and the "par with Amex/JPMorgan" soundbite. **No new terms, no new figures, no filing.** Traction: zero on the IPO itself; the live fact is a *preliminary* US charter — cards/loans are **announced, not launched**.

**+ Why it's thin:** Venue preference carries no economic value-add until there is a filing, price range, and float structure. The margin question (who captures value) hinges on the US **charter**, not the listing venue — and that charter is still pending full approval.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch: (a) full OCC charter → 2027 US bank launch → actual cards/loans go-live; (b) the H2 2026 secondary at >$100bn as the real near-term price signal; (c) whether London formally loses the primary slot (a UK political sore point). Sentiment is "most anticipated European IPO," but repeatedly pushed out. Risk: valuation inflation ($75bn → $150–200bn aspiration) without a public float to test it; US execution risk (no US banking track record vs. Amex/Chase).

**+ Second-order:** By making the US the *primary* venue, Revolut raises the bar — US public markets will price it against JPMorgan/Amex/Nubank on hard bank metrics, where a "software multiple" is harder to defend than in a sympathetic London listing.

**Sector & drivers.** Neobank/digital-banking is a large, still-fast-growing vertical: multiple secondary-research houses size the neobanking market at roughly tens of $bn and cite ~30-40% CAGRs (e.g. one estimate ~$117.9bn / 38.6% CAGR, per TrendX Insights, as of 2025) — directional only, vendor TAM, treat as `[UNSOURCED]` for a precise figure. Structure: a few profitable at-scale winners (Nubank, Revolut, Wise, WeBank) plus a long tail of thin-margin challengers; the moat is a full banking charter + a multi-product cross-sell engine, and entry barriers are regulatory (licences) and capital, not technology. Why now: Revolut received **preliminary/conditional OCC approval for a US national bank charter on 2026-09-03** (Revolut Bank US, N.A., Stamford CT; ~$95m capital, ~160 staff at launch, targeting 1H 2027) — it still needs FDIC insurance, Fed approval and final OCC sign-off before taking deposits (per Crowdfund Insider / Genfinity). A US charter plus a US *primary* listing is the "become a global bank on US rails" thesis; US listings also tend to earn richer multiples than London — the likely real driver behind Storonsky's preference.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: customers/ARPU, revenue growth, PBT margin, customer balances/deposits. Players: Nubank (LatAm, ~115m+ customers), Revolut (65m+ retail), Wise (cross-border), Chime (US, listed Jun-2025), Monzo (UK), plus US de-novo pushes by Nubank. Basis of competition: product breadth + distribution + licence footprint, not price. Recent moves: Chime IPO'd on Nasdaq 2025-06-12 at $11.6bn (CNBC); Revolut secondary lifted it to **$75bn (finalised 2025-11-24)**, up from $45bn a year earlier (PitchBook); reports of a 2H-2026 secondary at >$100bn / ~$115bn and an eventual IPO targeting **$150-200bn no sooner than 2028** (CoinDesk/TechCrunch). Position: Revolut is a *leader* among at-scale neobanks — record profitability + global licence land-grab — moat = regulatory licences (scale/intangibles) + cross-sell network across 11 product lines each >$135m revenue `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples (IR-grounded — PRIMARY).** Revolut FY2025 (consolidated, Revolut Group Holdings Ltd, year ended 31-Dec-2025, filed 2026-04-29): **revenue $6.0bn (£4.5bn), +46% YoY**; **PBT $2.3bn (£1.7bn), +57%, 38% PBT margin**; **retail customers 68.3m (+30%)**, business 767k (+33%); customer balances $67.5bn (+66%); subscriptions $936m (+67%), card payments $1.3bn (+45%) — per Revolut's own release and annual report (assets.revolut.com/pdf/annualreport2025.pdf), IR DB confirms the filing is indexed.

- Chime (CHYM): IPO mkt cap $11.6bn (Jun-2025), ~3.7x revenue (Multiples.vc).

- Flag: Revolut's private P/S (12.5x) and IPO-target multiple (~33x) are **rich vs listed peers (Nubank 4x, Chime 3.7x)**. Partly justified by faster growth (+46% vs Nubank +~40%) and higher PBT margin (38%), but a >3x premium to a profitable, larger-revenue public comp is a re-rating bet, not an in-line mark.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Valuation gap / private-mark risk** — $75bn (12.5x sales) and a mooted $200bn IPO imply a multiple 3x+ above profitable listed peers; absent a step-change in growth or US monetisation, public-market investors may re-rate toward peer 4-6x, a material down-draft. Why: secondary rounds are illiquid, VC-set marks, not market clearing prices.
2. **US execution / regulatory dependency** — the charter is only *conditional*; deposits/lending wait on FDIC + Fed + final OCC. The whole "US primary listing + US bank" thesis hangs on approvals Revolut doesn't control, and a UK regulator already dragged out its full UK licence. Why: a stalled US bank undercuts the US-listing rationale.
3. **Conduct/fraud overhang** — Revolut has repeatedly topped UK bank fraud-complaint tables; a US consumer-banking footprint raises CFPB/FDIC conduct exposure. Why: compliance friction can delay the very charter and listing it is banking on.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Signals the next phase of neobank maturation: the biggest winners now compete for *US listing venue* and *US charter*, pulling capital and listing prestige away from London — a re-rating/venue-shift story, not a new-entrant one. Falsifiable thesis: Revolut lists in NY at a premium to UK-listed peers *only if* it converts the conditional charter into a live US bank with disclosed US revenue by the 2028 window; trigger to watch = FDIC/Fed sign-off and first US deposit numbers. What breaks it = charter stalls or a down-round secondary (<$75bn), which would reset the IPO narrative.

> [!note] Earnings layer (adds to context [0]–[4]) — the news is an IPO-venue signal, not a print; the relevant "earnings" are Revolut's own FY2025 accounts (year ended 31 Dec 2025), the numbers underwriting any IPO valuation. Results reported 2026-03-24; >6 months old at news date (2026-10-09), so figures are context, not a fresh print.

**Verdict (headline read).** CONTEXT-ONLY (no new print in the news) · but FY2025 is a BEAT on profitability: group revenue $6.0bn / £4.5bn (+46% YoY) · PBT $2.3bn / £1.7bn (+57% YoY), PBT margin 38% (up from 35%) · net profit $1.7bn / £1.3bn (+~70% YoY) · fifth straight year of net profit. No forward guidance given (private company). Growth is strong but decelerating (FY2024 revenue grew +72% vs FY2025 +46%).

**Key figures (FY2025 vs FY2024, from Revolut's own accounts).**
- Group revenue: $6.0bn (£4.5bn), +46% YoY, up from $4.0bn (£3.1bn). (analysis) FY2024 grew +72%, so 2025 is a sharp deceleration in the growth rate — natural at scale, but a thesis-flag for an IPO priced on hyper-growth.
- Profit before tax: $2.3bn (£1.7bn), +57% YoY; PBT margin 38% vs 35% in 2024 — operating leverage, margin expanding faster than revenue.
- Net profit (after tax): $1.7bn (£1.3bn), up from $1.0bn (£790m) in 2024; net margin ~29%. Fifth consecutive year of net profitability.
- Customers: retail base 68.3m (+30% YoY); business customers 767K (+33% YoY).
- Customer balances/deposits: $67.5bn (£50.2bn), +66% YoY — the funding base behind the planned lending push.

**By segment / driver (FY2025 revenue mix, all Revolut-reported).**
- Card payments: $1.3bn (£1.0bn), +45% YoY — ~22.2% of revenue.
- Interest income: ~21.6% of revenue — the single largest rate-sensitive line (balances +66% + still-elevated rates).
- Subscriptions: $936m (£708m), +67% YoY — fastest-growing major line, ~15.7% of revenue; high-quality recurring revenue.
- Wealth (equities/crypto): $876m (£663m), +31% YoY — ~14.7%; crypto-exposed.
- FX: $800m (£606m), +43% YoY — ~13.4%.
- Fee-based revenue = 76% of turnover (+4.2pp YoY): management/a16z frame this "inverted" mix (fees > interest) as reducing rate-cut sensitivity vs a traditional bank. 11 product lines each cleared ~$135m (£100m) — diversification is real, not a single-product story.

**vs expectations / prior period.** No analyst consensus exists (private; no listed equity, no Street estimates) — beat/miss is qualitative vs prior year. On profitability it is a clear beat: PBT +57% outpacing revenue +46%, margin +3pp, net profit near-doubling. The caution: revenue growth decelerated from +72% (FY2024) to +46% (FY2025) [prior figure per CNBC, 2025-04-24]. Within the corpus: deposits/customer momentum is consistent with [[Revolut raises $3B at $75 billion valuation]] (Nov-2025 secondary at $75bn) — and the valuation has since run to a reported $115bn secondary (July 2026), i.e. ~19x FY2025 revenue / ~50x PBT. See also [[How Revolut's UK bank became CEO Storonsky's top priority]], [[Revolut's full UK banking licence held up by risk concerns]].

**Guidance / forward.** None — private company, no issued guidance. (analysis) The news itself is the only forward signal: US-preferred primary listing (likely Nasdaq, Storonsky citing "deep liquidity"), IPO not expected for at least another year (some reports cite 2028), and credit cards + loans to follow a full US banking charter (preliminary approval Sept 2026). These are revenue-mix levers (adding net interest margin from lending) not yet in the FY2025 numbers.

**Thesis-flags (for an IPO / valuation).**
1. Deceleration vs a ~$115bn tag. Revenue growth halved in rate terms (+72% → +46%); at a reported ~$115bn valuation (~19x FY2025 revenue) the multiple assumes re-acceleration. Fact → growth is slowing → a growth multiple needs the US lending/charter story to deliver → second-order: IPO timing is tied to proving the US bank monetizes before pricing.
2. Interest-income dependence (~21.6% of revenue) + crypto/wealth (~14.7%). Both are cyclical: rate cuts compress interest income, crypto volumes swing wealth revenue. Management counters with the 76% fee-based mix. Flag: how durable is the margin if rates fall AND crypto cools — exactly the lines a prospectus will be probed on.
3. Charter/regulatory gating. US national bank charter is only "preliminary" (Sept 2026); UK full licence has repeatedly stalled on risk-control concerns ([[Revolut's full UK banking licence held up by risk concerns]]). The lending upside priced into the valuation is contingent on regulators, not just execution.
4. Primary-listing venue = valuation arbitrage. Choosing the US over London is explicitly a liquidity/valuation call — a signal management believes a US tape supports a richer multiple than UK/EU listings would, consistent with the $45bn→$75bn→$115bn secondary ramp.
