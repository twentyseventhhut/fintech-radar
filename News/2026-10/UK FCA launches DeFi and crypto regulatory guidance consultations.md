We’re consulting on non-Handbook guidance to help firms providing financial services using decentralised finance (DeFi) comply with our rules on operational resilience and financial crime, and with the Money Laundering Regulations (MLRs).

## Why we are consulting

Our guidance covers observations on the current risk landscape relevant to DeFi. It focuses on operational resilience and financial crime risks, and provides illustrative examples of measures firms may wish to consider.

By helping firms better manage these risks, the proposed guidance aims to protect consumers, uphold market integrity and promote effective competition.

Firms should read this guidance consultation alongside our consultation paper on perimeter guidance, CP26/36: Cryptoasset Perimeter Guidance.

Firms will also find our wider publications on the cryptoasset regime, including our policy statements, relevant. They should also consider our other publications on operational resilience and our Financial Crime Guide.

## Who this applies to

FCA regulated firms providing financial services using DeFi that are:

*   In scope of SYSC 15A (Operational resilience).
*   In scope of SYSC 6 (Compliance, internal audit and financial crime).
*   Subject to the MLRs.

It may also interest:

*   Other FCA regulated firms.
*   Those who are not authorised by the FCA but interact with DeFi.
*   Industry groups and trade bodies.
*   Professional advisers.
*   Consumers and groups representing consumer interests.
*   Policy makers and other regulatory bodies.
*   Academics and think tanks.
*   Industry experts and commentators.

## Next steps

Online response form

Please send us your comments by **18 November 2026**.

You can do this using our online response form or by emailing gc26-7@fca.org.uk. If you are responding by email, please tell us if:

*   You want us to treat your response as confidential.
*   You are content to be named as a respondent.

We will consider your feedback and update the proposed guidance as appropriate. We plan to publish the final guidance in January 2027.

## Background

DeFi is a general term for financial services facilitated by distributed ledger technology and featuring high levels of automation, open and interoperable technology, and, in some cases, no traditional intermediary.

Firms using DeFi should do so safely and responsibly, as with other technologies. Its features can expose firms and their clients to increased operational resilience and financial crime risks.

From 25 October 2027, the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026 will bring new cryptoasset activities into our regulatory perimeter.

The United Kingdom’s **Financial Conduct Authority (FCA)** opened two parallel consultations seeking industry input on decentralised finance (DeFi) and the boundaries of its forthcoming cryptoasset regime.

The moves form part of the regulator’s ongoing preparation for the full crypto framework, which is scheduled to take effect on 25 October 2027 under the _Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026_.

The first consultation, GC26/7, proposes non-Handbook guidance aimed at FCA-regulated firms that use or facilitate access to DeFi protocols.

Rather than introducing a new rulebook, the guidance seeks to clarify how existing requirements apply when firms deliver services through highly automated, distributed-ledger-based systems.

It concentrates on operational resilience under SYSC 15A, compliance and financial crime controls under SYSC 6, and obligations under the Money Laundering Regulations.

The FCA observes that DeFi’s open, interoperable and often intermediary-light design can heighten operational and financial-crime risks for both firms and their clients.

The draft guidance outlines the current risk landscape and offers illustrative examples of steps firms may consider to manage those risks. Its stated objectives are to safeguard consumers, maintain market integrity and support effective competition.

Firms are encouraged to read GC26/7 alongside the second consultation, CP26/36, which addresses the cryptoasset regulatory perimeter following recent legislative changes.

The draft Cryptoassets (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2026 introduce several new exclusions from regulated activities.

These cover, among other matters, temporary settlement arrangements, temporary holding of UK qualifying stablecoins in connection with payment transactions, arrangements involving the backing assets of such stablecoins, proprietary trading where a person is not providing a service to another (or is acting as a market maker on a qualifying cryptoasset trading platform), and technical services that merely enable access to services offered by a third party or to a decentralised protocol.

CP26/36 is intended to help firms determine whether they require FCA permission once the regime begins, thereby reducing the chance of inadvertent breaches.

The regulator has acknowledged that the timing of the latest statutory instrument has created uncertainty for a limited number of firms.

In certain circumstances involving the technical-services exclusion, it has indicated it will show forbearance, allowing additional time to complete aspects of an application provided an otherwise complete submission is made during the application window.

That window for existing UK cryptoasset businesses opened on 30 September 2026 and closes on 28 February 2027.

Both consultations close on 18 November 2026, with the FCA aiming to publish final guidance in January 2027 after considering responses.

The perimeter guidance emphasises that the regime is technology-neutral: the use of software, smart contracts, distributed-ledger technology or distributed governance does not, by itself, determine whether a person is carrying on a regulated activity that requires authorisation.

Earlier policy work has indicated that rules will generally apply where an identifiable controlling entity can be identified.

Industry groups have welcomed the opportunity to comment while stressing the importance of proportionality.

The UK Cryptoasset Business Council has described DeFi as one of the most innovative segments of financial services and urged the FCA to establish clear perimeter rules and balanced expectations so that responsible innovation can remain in the United Kingdom rather than migrate elsewhere.

The consultations arrive against the backdrop of a broader crypto roadmap that has already produced final rules for activities such as trading platforms, intermediaries, staking, and lending and borrowing. Together, the new guidance exercises are intended to give firms greater clarity as they prepare for the 2027 implementation date.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 21 October 2026 the UK FCA opened two parallel, linked consultations that together sketch the first formal regulatory boundary around decentralised finance ahead of the mandatory cryptoasset regime. **GC26/7** proposes non-Handbook guidance for FCA-regulated firms that use or facilitate DeFi protocols, focused on operational resilience (SYSC 15A), compliance/financial crime (SYSC 6) and the Money Laundering Regulations. **CP26/36** consults on how recent legislation reshapes the cryptoasset regulatory perimeter — specifically the new exclusions in the draft *Cryptoassets (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2026*. Both close **18 November 2026**, with final guidance expected **January 2027**. The full regime under the *Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026* takes effect **25 October 2027** ([FCA GC26/7](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/guidance-consultations/gc26-7-decentralised-finance-guidance), [FCA CP26/36](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/consultation-papers/cp26-36-cryptoasset-perimeter-guidance), [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/10/319909-uk-financial-conduct-authority-fca-launches-consultations-on-defi-guidance-and-crypto-regulatory-guidelines/)).

**[1] Why it matters / perimeter mechanics.** The headline is the FCA's "substance over form" / technology-neutral line: use of software, smart contracts, DLT or distributed governance does **not** by itself determine whether someone is carrying on a regulated activity. A limited **technical-services exclusion** can take DeFi front-ends out of scope, but only where they are passive access points — the FCA stresses it is "not a general exclusion for services described as technological, technical, software-based or connected with DeFi." Earlier policy signalled that rules bite where an identifiable **controlling entity** exists. The amendment regs also add targeted exclusions for temporary settlement, temporary holding of UK qualifying stablecoins in payment flows, stablecoin backing-asset arrangements, and **proprietary trading / market-making on a QCATP** where no service is provided to another person ([CoinMarketCap on smart-contract line](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/fca-defi-decentralization-crypto-perimeter-guidance), [Linklaters](https://financialregulation.linklaters.com/post/102o67c/uk-fca-proposes-updates-to-crypto-perimeter-guidance), [PwC UK](https://www.pwc.co.uk/industries/financial-services/understanding-regulatory-developments/fca-redraws-crypto-regulatory-perimeter.html)).

**[2] Where it sits in the roadmap (internal history).** This is the latest step in a roadmap first set out Nov 2024 and largely completed on 30 June 2026, when the FCA issued five policy statements with final rules (trading platforms, dealing, arranging, custody, lending/borrowing, staking, plus stablecoin issuance/backing/redemption). See [[FCA publishes approach to UK cryptoasset regulatory regime]] (Jan 2026 approach), [[FCA's new cryptoasset regime expected to take effect October 2027]] (May 2026, confirmed 25 Oct 2027 go-live) and, most directly, [[FCA opens applications for UK cryptoasset regime]] (8 Oct 2026 — the authorisation gateway). The authorisation window for existing UK crypto businesses runs **30 September 2026 → 28 February 2027** ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/07/fca-finalises-core-rules-for-the-uk-cryptoasset-regime), [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/blockchain-crypto-digital-assets/fca-completes-crypto-roadmap-with-publication-of-new-rules), [FCA press release](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-sets-landmark-crypto-rules-cement-uks-place-global-hub)).

**[3] Related internal threads.** Adjacent UK crypto/DeFi items in the vault: [[FCA opens applications for stablecoin sprint]] (Jan 2026, stablecoin policy co-design) and DeFi-market datapoints such as [[Aave and MetaMask bring DeFi to payments with Mastercard]] and [[Stripe-backed Tempo taps DeFi lender Morpho to expand beyond payments]] — the kinds of intermediary-adjacent businesses the perimeter guidance is aimed at. The consultations also sit alongside the FCA's lifting of the retail crypto-ETN ban ([[FCA lifts retail ban on crypto ETNs]]), signalling a broad "open but supervised" posture.

**[4] What to watch.** (a) The 18 Nov 2026 response deadline and whether industry (e.g. the UK Cryptoasset Business Council, which urged proportionality and clear perimeter rules to keep responsible innovation onshore) pushes back on the controlling-entity test. (b) How tightly the FCA draws the technical-services exclusion in final Jan-2027 guidance — the swing factor for whether front-end/UI operators need authorisation. (c) The forbearance the FCA has flagged for a limited set of firms affected by the technical-services exclusion timing, allowing extra time to complete applications within the window. (d) Whether the 25 Oct 2027 go-live and 28 Feb 2027 application cut-off hold, given the "uncertainty for a limited number of firms" the FCA itself acknowledged ([Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/10/319909-uk-financial-conduct-authority-fca-launches-consultations-on-defi-guidance-and-crypto-regulatory-guidelines/), [Pericls Insights](https://insights.pericls.com/p/decoding-the-fca-s-new-defi-perimeter)).

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Are these genuinely "new rules," or consultations on *guidance* about already-finalised rules? (They are consultations on non-Handbook guidance + perimeter interpretation — not new binding rules; core rules were set 30 Jun 2026.)
2. Does GC26/7 create new obligations, or just restate how existing SYSC 15A / SYSC 6 / MLR duties apply to DeFi? If the latter, how much does it actually change firm behaviour?
3. The technical-services exclusion is narrow and fact-specific — will it in practice exempt any real DeFi front-end, or is it a near-nullity that still pulls most interfaces into scope?
4. Who is the "identifiable controlling entity"? For a genuinely permissionless protocol with no admin keys, is there any regulated person at all — and does that just push activity offshore?
5. Is the 18 Nov 2026 deadline (≈4 weeks) adequate for the complexity, or does it signal the FCA has largely decided and is box-ticking?
6. How binding is non-Handbook guidance? Firms can depart from it with justification — so what is its real enforcement weight pre-25 Oct 2027?
7. Does the proprietary-trading / market-maker exclusion on a QCATP create an arbitrage where firms restructure as "principal-only" to escape authorisation?
8. The FCA acknowledges the statutory instrument's timing "created uncertainty for a limited number of firms" and offers forbearance — how many firms, and does ad hoc forbearance undermine legal certainty?
9. Is the 28 Feb 2027 application cut-off realistic given the gateway only opened 30 Sep 2026 and perimeter clarity is still in consultation until Nov?
10. Does the "technology-neutral / substance over form" stance conflict with the UK's stated ambition to be a crypto hub, i.e. does it chill the DeFi segment it claims to welcome?
11. How does this regime compare with MiCA (EU) and the US post-GENIUS/CLARITY landscape — is the UK stricter on DeFi, and does that drive migration?
12. Is this story materially different from the 8 Oct gateway-opening and the Jun-2026 policy statements, or incremental follow-through on an already-known roadmap?
13. Stablecoin backing-asset and temporary-holding exclusions: do they meaningfully help payment firms, or are the carve-outs too conditional to rely on?
14. What is the AML/financial-crime realism of applying MLRs to pseudonymous, intermediary-light DeFi — can firms actually meet the guidance?

**Importance: 3/5 — rationale.** Substantive and well-sourced UK regulatory development that materially shapes how DeFi and crypto-adjacent firms prepare for the mandatory 2027 regime, and the first formal FCA attempt to draw a DeFi perimeter. But it is a *consultation on guidance* (non-binding, closing in weeks), not new law, and it is incremental on an already-public roadmap whose core rules and authorisation gateway are set — so important for UK crypto/compliance practitioners but not a market-moving or regime-defining event in isolation.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a regulatory-infrastructure story in crypto/DeFi, not a company event — there is no valuation or deal attached, so trading-comp arithmetic is "no data". Size proxy: the UK framework (FSMA 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026) covers ~six defined regulated activities (trading platforms, intermediaries, staking, lending/borrowing, custody, issuance) and takes full effect 25 Oct 2027; the authorisation window for existing UK crypto businesses runs 30 Sep 2026 → 28 Feb 2027 (per FCA/cryptoslate, Oct 2026). GC26/7 (DeFi guidance) and CP26/36 (perimeter guidance) both opened 21 Oct 2026 and close 18 Nov 2026, with final guidance due Spring 2027 (per FCA). Structure: global crypto regulation is now a race of competing frameworks rather than a single standard — EU MiCA is fully live (full regime since 30 Dec 2024, USDT delisted on EU venues), while the US GENIUS Act (signed 18 Jul 2025) is a narrower stablecoin-only statute that becomes enforceable 18 Jan 2027 (per blindpay/stablecoinlaws, 2026). "Why now": the UK is deliberately sequencing guidance ~12 months ahead of the 2027 live date to let firms self-assess perimeter scope before the authorisation gate closes — a timing play to retain crypto activity onshore ahead of the EU and US clocks.

**Competitive landscape.** The "KPIs" here are jurisdictional, not financial: perimeter clarity (what needs authorisation), proportionality of capital/compliance burden, and firm migration (net inflow/outflow of licensed entities). Competing regimes: **EU MiCA** — single passport across 27 states, EMT/ART split, EMI-or-credit-institution-only stablecoin issuance, no yield to holders (Art. 50); **US GENIUS Act** — federal payment-stablecoin path but persistent state-level fragmentation; **UK** — technology-neutral, activity-based, with a new "technical-services exclusion" that could spare passive DeFi front-ends from the "arranging deals" trigger if they don't control transactions or hold client relationships. The UK's distinctive move is applying *existing* SYSC 15A (operational resilience), SYSC 6 (financial crime) and the MLRs to DeFi via non-Handbook guidance rather than writing a new DeFi rulebook — a lighter-touch posture than MiCA's bespoke regime. Positioning (analysis): the UK is a fast-follower, not first-mover (EU/US already have statutes live or signed), betting on perimeter flexibility as its edge; its moat is regulatory optionality (keeping intermediary-light DeFi partly outside the perimeter), which is also its biggest execution risk.

**Risk flags.**
- **Firm migration / competitiveness risk.** Critics warn the draft framework favours well-capitalised market makers (Coinbase, Kraken) while smaller/offshore firms may relocate; the UK Cryptoasset Business Council explicitly urged the FCA to keep rules proportional so innovation "remains in the UK rather than migrate elsewhere" (per crowdfundinsider/thecurrencyanalytics, Oct 2026). Second-order: crypto activity is more mobile than TradFi, so an over-tight perimeter risks losing the base the regime is meant to supervise.
- **Perimeter ambiguity / inadvertent-breach risk.** The FCA itself acknowledges the statutory-instrument timing has created uncertainty for "a limited number of firms" and is offering forbearance on the technical-services exclusion. A guidance (non-Handbook) instrument leaves scope for a firm to misjudge whether it needs authorisation before the 28 Feb 2027 cut-off — a hard legal gate with soft guidance underneath it.
- **Regulatory-arbitrage / fragmentation risk.** With MiCA, GENIUS and the UK regime all diverging (EMT splits, yield bans, exclusions), cross-border DeFi/stablecoin firms face a compliance map with no mutual recognition — margin and legal cost are captured by the most restrictive jurisdiction a firm touches.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is the UK staking a "principles-first, technology-neutral" claim in a now-crowded regulatory-competition field — a differentiation bet, not a consolidation move. Falsifiable thesis: if the final guidance (Spring 2027) preserves a workable technical-services/DeFi carve-out, the UK retains intermediary-light crypto activity; the trigger that breaks it is net licensed-firm outflow or public relocation announcements during the 30 Sep 2026 → 28 Feb 2027 authorisation window. Watch the consultation responses (close 18 Nov 2026) for how hard industry pushes on proportionality.
