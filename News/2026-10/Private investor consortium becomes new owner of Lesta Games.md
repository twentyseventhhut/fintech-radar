 Новым владельцем ГК «Леста игры» стал консорциум частных инвесторов АО «Промышленные сооружения» 
Новым владельцем группы компаний «Леста игры» стал консорциум частных инвесторов АО «Промышленные сооружения», рассказали « Ведомостям » в пресс-службе компании. Это юрлицо стало владельцем 100% ключевых юрлиц студии «Леста» – ООО «Леста геймс эдженси», ООО «Леста» и ООО «Леста геймс Москва», соответствующие изменения уже отражены в ЕГРЮЛ.
Структура владения АО «Промышленные сооружения» не раскрывается, его гендиректор – Захар Кислых, указано в ЕГРЮЛе. Управляющая команда «Леста игры» и стратегия ее развития остаются неизменными, она продолжает работать над текущими и новыми продуктами, отметил представитель «Лесты».
«Мы верим в стратегию и менеджмент компании «Леста игры», поддерживаем как текущие подходы к развитию игровой трилогии, так и планы запуска новых проектов», – отметил представитель АО «Промышленные сооружения».
При этом новый собственник, по его словам, планирует инвестировать не только в разработки «Леста игр», но и во внешние проекты. В ближайшие три года АО «Промышленные сооружения», как уточнил его представитель, планирует вложить в развитие российской игровой индустрии до 10 млрд руб., в том числе через создание венчурного фонда. Цель – в общей сложности выпустить за три года не менее пяти новых продуктов, отметил он: «Также мы рассматриваем компанию «Леста игры» как ключевой элемент для дальнейшей консолидации рынка».
О том, что студию «Леста» планируется осенью продать «заинтересованному инвестору», заявил в сентябре в интервью РБК замминистра финансов Алексей Моисеев. Оценка актива «очень дорогая», также отметил он. Конкретную сумму Моисеев не назвал.
Ранее ключевыми юрлицами студии «Леста» – ООО «Леста геймс эдженси», ООО «Леста» и ООО «Леста геймс Москва» – владело государство в лице Росимущества. Выручка ООО «Леста геймс эдженси» в 2025 г. составила 29 млрд руб., а чистая прибыль – 10,1 млрд руб., следует из базы данных «СПАРК-Интерфакса». Выручка ООО «Леста» – 5,7 млрд руб., чистая прибыль – 14,7 млн руб. Выручка ООО «Леста геймс Москва» – 227,3 млн руб., чистая прибыль – 8,9 млн руб.
До 2022 г. студия «Леста» была дочерним подразделением белорусской компании Wargaming, основанной Виктором Кислым вместе с Маликом Хатажаевым (признана российским судом «экстремистским объединением» и запрещена в стране). После начала специальной операции России на Украине Wargaming объявила об уходе из России. Ее долю в «Лесте» в размере 99% тогда получил Хатажаев. В 2025 г. суд по иску Генпрокуратуры наложил арест на основные юрлица «Лесты», а позже обратил компанию в доход государства и запретил в России партнерство Хатажаева и Кислого за «экстремистскую деятельность».

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** The Russian state (Rosimushchestvo) sold the nationalized game studio group "Lesta Games" to JSC "Promyshlennye Sooruzheniya" ("Industrial Constructions"), described as a "consortium of private investors." The buyer now owns 100% of the three key legal entities — OOO "Lesta Games Agency," OOO "Lesta," and OOO "Lesta Games Moscow" — with the change already recorded in Russia's companies register (EGRYUL) as of ~15 Oct 2026. Lesta publishes the Russian versions of World of Tanks ("Мир танков"), World of Warships ("Мир кораблей") and Tanks Blitz. Management team and strategy are kept unchanged.

**[1] Why it matters.** This is the resolution of a high-profile asset nationalization: a state-seized, highly profitable gaming business is being re-privatized to an opaque private vehicle. Lesta Games Agency alone booked RUB 29bn revenue / RUB 10.1bn net profit in 2025 — so this is a large, cash-generative RU media/gaming asset changing hands via a state-directed sale.

**[2] The numbers.** Deal price not officially disclosed; Deputy Finance Minister Alexey Moiseev (Sept 2026, RBC interview) called the valuation "very expensive" and flagged an autumn sale. Press (3DNews, Kommersant et al.) report a discussed figure of ~RUB 50bn. Buyer pledges up to RUB 10bn of investment into the Russian gaming industry over ~3 years (incl. a venture fund), targeting ≥5 new products and further market consolidation. Lesta 2025 financials (SPARK): Lesta Games Agency — rev RUB 29bn, NP RUB 10.1bn; OOO Lesta — rev RUB 5.7bn, NP RUB 14.7m; Lesta Games Moscow — rev RUB 227.3m, NP RUB 8.9m.

**[3] Who/structure.** Buyer AO "Промышленные сооружения" registered Nov 2024; posted a 2025 loss equal to its RUB 15,000 charter capital (SPARK) — a shell-sized balance sheet acquiring a multi-billion-ruble asset. CEO (from 15 Oct): Zakhar Kislyh, who also controls AO "Игровые инвестиции" (reg. Jun 2025) and AO "Общество устойчивого роста" (reg. Jul 2025). The latter's CEO is Alexey Milevsky — owner of investment fund M9 Capital and ex-head of M&A at VK/Mail group; a namesake "Zakhar Kislyh" is cited as an M9 Capital investment manager. Ownership structure of the buyer is NOT disclosed. Reported earlier bidders: Nikita Mazepin (ex-F1 driver, son of Uralkhim's Dmitry Mazepin); AO "Дхамма" (Denis Fonov, also CEO of AO "Хеманга," which took 75% of ad group NMi Group); and a private-investor consortium said to involve VTB (likely as acquisition lender). The Insider frames the new manager as linked to the son of the VGTRK head.

**[4] Background / prior chain.** Lesta was spun out of Belarusian Wargaming (founder Viktor Kislyi, with Malik Khatazhaev) in 2022 after Wargaming exited Russia/Belarus over the war; Khatazhaev took the 99% RU stake. In 2025 the Prosecutor General's suit arrested Lesta's entities, a Moscow court (Tagansky) seized 100% to the state on "extremism" grounds (tied to a 2023 Wargaming fundraiser for Ukraine), and the Kislyi–Khatazhaev partnership was banned in Russia as extremist. Since 11 Jun 2025 the assets sat under Rosimushchestvo. This sale is the privatization/exit of that nationalized asset. Note: Wargaming's Viktor Kislyi and the buyer's CEO Zakhar Kislyh are different people (shared surname, no confirmed relation).

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. "Consortium of private investors" — but the buyer AO "Промышленные сооружения" is a single shell (reg. Nov 2024, RUB 15,000 capital, 2025 loss = its entire capital). Where is the actual consortium, and who funds a ~RUB 50bn purchase through a shell with no disclosed owners?
2. Price is unconfirmed: "~RUB 50bn" is a reported/discussed figure, and Moiseev never named a sum. Do not state the price as a fact — flag it as unverified.
3. Is this a genuine arm's-length privatization or a state-managed transfer to politically acceptable insiders? The Insider links the manager to the son of the VGTRK head — provenance of that claim?
4. VTB's role is secondhand ("two sources," likely as lender, not owner) and VTB declined to comment — is VTB actually involved, or is this now superseded given "Промышленные сооружения" won?
5. Are Zakhar Kislyh (buyer CEO) and Viktor Kislyi (Wargaming founder, banned "extremist") related? Surnames differ by transliteration/spelling (Кислых vs Кислый). Easy to conflate — explicitly not confirmed related.
6. Who really owns M9 Capital / what is Alexey Milevsky's actual mandate here? His tie is only via AO "Общество устойчивого роста," not direct ownership of the buyer.
7. The RUB 10bn "investment into the industry over 3 years" and "≥5 new products" are the buyer's own stated promises — unverifiable forward claims, not commitments.
8. Legal durability: the asset was seized via an "extremism" ruling that is contested internationally (Meduza, bne). Does the new owner get clean title, or litigation/sanctions risk (esp. for anything touching WoT IP outside Russia)?
9. IP question: Lesta runs the Russian versions of WoT/WoWs. What rights does it actually hold vs. Wargaming's global IP — can the new owner monetize/export, or is it RU-ring-fenced?
10. Fintech relevance check: is this a capital-markets/M&A story (yes — nationalized-asset re-privatization, possible bank-financed buyout) or primarily a gaming-industry story? Borderline for a fintech digest.
11. Why the three separate legal entities, and does owning all three equal operational control over game servers/revenue streams?
12. The RUB 29bn revenue / RUB 10.1bn NP are 2025 SPARK figures for the agency entity — are 2026 figures materially different post-nationalization (player attrition, sanctions on payments)?
13. Sanctions/payments angle: how does a nationalized, seized Russian gaming firm process in-game payments and FX — any exposure that makes this a fintech-adjacent story?

**Importance: 2/5**
Large, headline-grabbing RU asset transfer (~RUB 50bn, 29bn-revenue studio) with M&A and possible acquisition-financing angles, but core subject is gaming/industrial-policy, opaque buyer, unconfirmed price, and limited direct fintech/capital-markets read-through. Of niche relevance to a fintech audience.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russian gaming market ~RUB 200bn in 2025, +7% YoY; Russian users spent ~RUB 175bn on games/in-game content end-2025 (per wnhub.io citing industry study, as of 2026). The 2026 domestic game market is cited at ~$2.3bn with a 106–110m audience. The subvertical here is state-seized-asset redistribution to private hands: the acquirer took 100% of three Lesta legal entities (Leesta Games Agency, Lesta, Lesta Games Moscow) that previously sat with Rosimushchestvo after a 2025 Prosecutor-General seizure that turned the firm "into state revenue." **Structure:** consolidated at the top (Lesta/World of Tanks, VK Play/VK's My.Games legacy, Astrum Entertainment) but fragmented in the long tail of studios; barriers are IP (World of Tanks/Warships franchises), distribution rails cut off from Western stores since 2022, and — uniquely here — political clearance to buy a nationalized asset. **Why now:** the state is actively monetizing seized assets (see internal comps below); Deputy FinMin Moiseev flagged the sale for autumn 2026 and called the asset "very expensive."

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: revenue, net margin, franchise ARPU/MAU (not disclosed for Lesta). Key players: Lesta (World of Tanks / World of Warships / Tanks Blitz), Astrum Entertainment, VK Play. Basis of competition: franchise IP + local distribution post-2022 exit of Western platforms. Recent moves: Indie GO launched a RUB 5bn gaming venture fund targeting IPOs (2026-10-07) [[Indie GO launches 5bn-ruble venture fund for gaming IPOs]]; the new Lesta owner itself pledges up to RUB 10bn into the industry over three years, including its own venture fund and ≥5 new products — positioning as a consolidator. **Protagonist position:** dominant on revenue (Leesta Games Agency 2025 revenue RUB 29bn, net profit RUB 10.1bn per SPARK — roughly an order of magnitude above Astrum's ~RUB 3.3–9.4bn) but ring-fenced to the domestic market after losing Western distribution. Moat: entrenched franchise IP + installed player base (switching costs) `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal price undisclosed. Reported valuation markers: originally ~RUB 50bn, Forbes interlocutors put current fair value at RUB 30–35bn; Mazepin bid reportedly cited RUB 30–35bn.
- P/E on seized entity, at RUB 50bn / 10.1bn net profit = **~5.0x**; at RUB 30–35bn = **~3.0–3.5x**.
- P/S, combined 2025 revenue ≈ 29.0 + 5.7 + 0.2 = RUB 34.9bn → RUB 50bn / 34.9bn = **~1.4x**; RUB 30–35bn = **~0.9–1.0x**.
- Internal comps (state-asset redistribution, same "why"): [[Russian state sells 67% of zinc and lead processor Dalpolimetall]]; [[PSB auctions Agropromkredit bank for 2.85bn rubles]]; [[PSB fails third auction for nationalized Vektor Rail]]. Gaming venture-pricing comp: [[Indie GO launches 5bn-ruble venture fund for gaming IPOs]].
- Flag: **cheap** on earnings (≤5x P/E on a RUB 10bn-profit business) — but the discount is the political/liquidity risk premium, not a value bargain; external listed gaming peers are not comparable (sanctioned, non-convertible). Multiple distribution not computed (no ≥3 comparable public multiples); qualitative only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Opaque acquirer, zero substance.** AO Promyshlennye Sooruzheniya was registered Nov-2024, posted a 2025 loss equal to its RUB 15,000 charter capital, and its ownership is undisclosed; CEO Zakhar Kislykh (25yo, ex-M9 Capital) was installed a day before the deal — classic SPV optics. Who actually funds a RUB 30–50bn purchase and whether VTB is the lender (reported but denied) is unknown → true control and leverage unverifiable `(analysis)`.
2. **Reversibility / political-title risk.** Title derives from a Prosecutor-General seizure of an "extremist" asset; nationalized-asset sales can be re-litigated or re-auctioned (cf. [[PSB fails third auction for nationalized Vektor Rail]]) — buyer's title is politically contingent, not clean private M&A.
3. **Structural revenue cap.** Post-2022 loss of Western storefronts confines Lesta to the Russian ~RUB 200bn market; the RUB 10bn investment/"five new products" pledge is announced, not funded or delivered — execution and demand-ceiling risk on a shrinking-margin domestic market.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Signals the state moving from seizing to monetizing tech/IP assets via opaque private consortia, with gaming as a test case for a domestic consolidation + eventual-IPO narrative (echoing Indie GO's IPO-prep fund). Falsifiable thesis: if the acquirer's beneficial owners and the price are never disclosed and the RUB 10bn fund fails to deploy within ~12 months, this is a custodial transfer, not a genuine PE play — watch for ownership disclosure, the promised venture fund's first close, and any new-product launches as the trigger.
