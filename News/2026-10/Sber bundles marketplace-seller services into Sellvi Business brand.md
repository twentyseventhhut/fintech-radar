# Sber bundles marketplace-seller services into Sellvi Business brand

> [!info] 2026-10-27 · 1 упоминаний · 0 источника(ов) с текстом
> Каналы: Финтехно

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Финтехно] Сбер выделил сервисы для продавцов маркетплейсов в отдельный бренд «Селлви Бизнес». По сути, банк объединил уже существующие финансовые и технологические решения для онлайн-торговли: управление продажами на нескольких площадках, создание собственного интернет-магазина с оплатой и доставкой, бизнес-счёт, API-интеграции и экспертную поддержку. Когда банк обслуживает не только денежные потоки селлера, но и его повседневные бизнес-процессы, он получает более полную картину о нём. В том числе, операции, которые обычно остаются внутри маркетплейсов: продажи, возвраты, комиссии, расходы на продвижение и логистику. Такие данные полезны для финансового мониторинга и потенциально — для более точной оценки кредитных рисков и потребности бизнеса в оборотном капитале. Для получения данных отдельный бренд не нужен. Есть API-интеграции с маркетплейсами, в том числе для сокращения количества запросов по 115-ФЗ. Новое комплексное предложение Сбера решает другую задачу — привлечение и удержание селлер

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened (dated).** On 2026-10-26, Sber (largest Russian bank) launched **"Sellvi Business" / «Селлви Бизнес»**, a new brand that bundles its *already-existing* financial and tech products for online-marketplace sellers (selleři) into one offer. The bundle: multi-marketplace sales management, a turnkey own web-store with payments + delivery, a business account (fee/limit-free transfers to personal cards), marketplace API integrations (incl. to cut 115-FZ AML-query friction), and a dedicated expert-support line. Slogan: "Online commerce on your terms." Not a new product — a re-packaging/go-to-market move aimed at seller acquisition and retention. (Source: Финтехно via max.ru/fintexno; corroborated [CNews 2026-10-26](https://www.cnews.ru/news/line/2026-10-26_sber_zapuskaet_sellvi), [Lenta 2026-10-26](https://lenta.ru/news/2026/10/26/v-rossii-zapustili-novyy-instrument-dlya-sellerov/).)

**[1] Why it matters / strategic logic.** This is Sber playing offense in the structural **bank-vs-marketplace war** in Russia. Russia's marketplaces (Wildberries, Ozon, Yandex) bought small banks in 2021 and now run licensed in-house banks (Ozon Bank, WB Bank, Yandex Bank) that are capturing both buyer and **seller** financial flows — the exact relationships Sber is now fighting to keep. By serving not just the seller's money but their daily operations (sales, returns, commissions, ad/logistics spend), Sber gets richer data for financial monitoring and, potentially, better credit-risk and working-capital underwriting. The source note itself concedes a sharp point: *you don't need a separate brand to get the data* (API integrations already provide it) — so Sellvi Business is fundamentally a **customer-acquisition/retention play**, not a data play.

**[2] Competitive landscape (seller-side embedded finance).** Direct rivals already serve marketplace sellers deep inside their own ecosystems:
- **Ozon** — "Money for Purchases" (supplier financing, 30–90-day deferral, no collateral, no credit-history impact); Ozon Bank reached 56.6M clients in Sep 2026; Ozon Insurance/Life Insurance just launched; Sberbank itself was reported (May 2026) in talks for a ~32% Ozon stake.
- **Wildberries / WB Bank** — own MCC lending to sellers (RUB 100k–20M), plus government-backed loans up to RUB 500M (key rate +3%, 18-mo) launched Oct 2026; VTB is taking a Wildberries DFA stake.
- **Yandex Market / Yandex Bank** — seller incentives + embedded finance.
- Third-party seller lenders (Sovcombank, MTS Bank, etc.) also target WB/Ozon/Yandex sellers.
Sber's edge vs the marketplace-banks: scale, a full business bank, and cross-marketplace neutrality (a seller selling on *several* platforms can't be fully served by any single marketplace's captive bank).

Terms/pricing of the business account and support line are not disclosed. Watch for a lending/BNPL-for-sellers follow-on and whether 115-FZ-friction reduction becomes a real differentiator.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Q1. Is this a genuinely new product or just rebranding?** Mostly rebranding/bundling — Sber explicitly "bundled already-existing financial and tech solutions." No new credit product is disclosed. Verdict: go-to-market move, not innovation.

**Q2. The source itself argues the data rationale is weak — is that right?** Yes. The note concedes "you don't need a separate brand to get the data; API integrations (incl. for 115-FZ) already exist." So the honest driver is seller **acquisition/retention**, not new data access. The context must not overstate a "data moat."

**Q3. Any hard numbers (seller count, loan volume, revenue target)?** Open — none disclosed in the source or launch coverage. Importance can't rest on scale figures.

**Q4. Is Sber late vs Ozon/WB/Yandex seller finance?** Arguably yes — marketplace-captive banks (Ozon "Money for Purchases," WB MCC + state-backed loans up to RUB 500M) already embed seller finance inside the sales flow. Sber is the incumbent bank defending turf, not a first mover.

**Q5. What's Sber's actual edge?** Cross-marketplace neutrality (a multi-platform seller can't be fully captured by one marketplace's bank), full-bank scale, and the business account. Credible but unproven.

**Q6. Does the Sberbank–Ozon ~32% stake talk create a conflict?** Possibly coopetition — Sber may compete with Ozon's seller bank while holding a large Ozon stake. Open; worth flagging, not confirmed closed.

**Q7. Can Sber really get marketplace transaction data if marketplaces are rivals?** Open risk — API access depends on the marketplaces (its competitors). They could restrict it. The "fuller picture of the seller" claim is conditional.

**Q8. Is the 115-FZ friction reduction material?** Plausibly a real pain-point (AML query load on sellers), but it's offered via API integration independent of the brand — so it's a feature, not a brand justification.

**Q9. Does bundling actually improve credit underwriting?** Theoretically (returns, commissions, ad/logistics spend aid working-capital sizing) but no lending product is attached yet, so the underwriting benefit is prospective/speculative.

**Q10. Single-channel sourcing risk?** The note has 1 mention (Финтехно) and 0 extracted source text. External coverage (CNews, Lenta, regional outlets) confirms the launch and feature set — launch is real, not rumor. Low fabrication risk; low depth.

**Q11. Is this duplicate of a prior vault note?** No exact prior note on Sellvi Business. Thematically adjacent to marketplace-bank/SME-lending notes but a distinct, new Sber product launch. Verdict: fresh.

**Q12. Macro relevance — does this move the Russian fintech landscape?** Incrementally. It's a notable escalation signal in the bank-vs-marketplace battle for seller relationships, but it's a packaging launch with no disclosed scale, pricing, or new credit product.

**Q13. Any risk of mis-attribution (wrong company/entity)?** No — Sber is clearly the actor; "Селлви Бизнес" is unambiguously Sber's brand. No cross-wiring.

**Q14. What would raise importance?** A disclosed seller-lending/working-capital product, a seller-count or AUM target, or confirmation it's tied to the Ozon stake. None present.

**Q15. Net read.** A real, dated, strategically-logical but low-substance launch: rebranding of existing services aimed at seller retention amid intense marketplace-bank competition. Interesting as a landscape signal, thin on new product or numbers.

Importance: 2/5 — a confirmed, strategically meaningful but substance-light rebranding move by a major player in an important competitive battle (bank vs marketplace for sellers); no new product, no numbers, single-channel sourcing caps it.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russian marketplace-seller banking sits at the intersection of e-commerce and embedded SME finance — the fastest-growing slice of RU retail. The two platforms Sber is chasing with Sellvi are vast: RWB (Wildberries + Russ) reported 2025 turnover of RUB 6.1tn (~$67bn GMV) and Ozon's 2025 e-commerce revenue was ~$49bn (per TASS / ecdb, as of 2026). Structure is a consolidating oligopoly (Ozon + Wildberries dominate, Yandex Market third) with banks now forcing their way into the seller stack: "VTB joins Wildberries, Sberbank joins Ozon" (per bne IntelliNews / Deliver2, May–Oct 2026). Why now: marketplace fintech verticals (Ozon Bank, WB Bank) have grown into deposit/lending businesses that annoy both incumbent banks and the CBR (per bne IntelliNews, 2026) — regulators are pushing for a détente, and banks are responding by building their own seller propositions. Sellvi is Sber's answer: bundle existing SME tools (multi-platform sales management, own web-shop, business account, marketplace API integrations, 115-FZ query reduction) under one brand to acquire and retain sellers before the platforms' own banks lock them in.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: seller GMV through the platform, seller-finance loan book, take rate / fee income on acquiring, and active business-client count. Players: (a) marketplace-captive banks — Ozon Bank (56.6M clients Sep 2026, embedding DFA, insurance, brokerage), WB Bank (RVB); (b) incumbent banks pushing in — Sber (Sellvi), VTB (5% of Wildberries CFA; Ozon stake as collateral); (c) specialist SME neobanks (Tochka, Tinkoff/T-Bank Business). Basis of competition: data access and distribution, not price — whoever sees seller sales/returns/commissions/ad-and-logistics spend can underwrite working-capital better. Recent moves: Ozon set up its own insurers (Oct 2026) and plans Ozon Investments brokerage (autumn 2026); Sber is simultaneously negotiating to buy AFK Sistema's ~31.8% Ozon stake for >RUB 300bn (~$4.2bn) in a debt settlement (per bne IntelliNews / Reuters, May 2026). Sber's position vs the captive banks: catching up on data (the note itself admits platform-internal operations "usually stay inside the marketplaces") but ahead on scale and balance sheet. Moat (analysis): switching costs if a seller runs account + acquiring + web-shop on Sber rails; scale on cost of funding. Seller-level economics (loan yields, attach rates, CAC) are not disclosed → [UNSOURCED].

**Comps & multiples.** Sber is a RUB 1.7tn-net-profit universal bank; Sellvi is a product line, not a standalone valued entity, so a clean multiple on Sellvi = no data. Scale anchor (Sber FY2025 IFRS, filed 2026-02-26): net profit RUB 1.7tn (+7.9% YoY), ROE 22.7%, NII RUB 3,556bn (+18.5%), CIR 30.3%, equity RUB 8.3tn (+16.4%), CAR 13.7%; B2B-segment corporate-customer net interest income RUB 610.2bn in 2025 vs RUB 617.4bn in 2024 (flat — no growth tailwind in the core corporate book the SME push must offset). Reference transaction for the sector: Sber's mooted Ozon stake values ~31.8% at >RUB 300bn → implied ~100% ≈ RUB 944bn (RUB 300bn / 0.318 = RUB 943.4bn), a debt-settlement price, not a market cap — treat as a floor datapoint for the e-commerce-fintech asset, not a trading comp. Internal base comps (RU marketplace-seller / embedded-finance): [[Ozon Bank grows to 56.6M clients in September 2026]], [[Ozon Bank opens DFA purchases to business clients]], [[Ozon to launch brokerage arm Ozon Investments in autumn 2026]], [[VTB takes large Ozon share stake as collateral]]. External seller-finance comps (different geo, directional only): eBay Seller Capital, Flipkart Advertise-Now-Pay-Later / RBI lending licence, NEO PAY–Biz2X ($32bn SME book). Distribution not computed — too few comparable RU figures; qualitative only.

**Risk flags.** (1) Data-access gap is self-admitted: the note says a separate brand is NOT needed to get data (API integrations exist), so Sellvi's credit-underwriting edge depends on sellers volunteering platform operations that "usually stay inside the marketplaces" — if Ozon/WB restrict API data to protect their own banks, the working-capital thesis weakens (disintermediation by the platform layer). (2) Channel conflict / antitrust: Sber courting Ozon (31.8% stake) while Ozon runs its own competing bank — and with FAS already warning Wildberries/Ozon on seller interaction (2026) — creates regulatory and ownership-conflict overhang. (3) Core B2B stagnation: corporate-customer NII was flat YoY (RUB 610.2bn vs 617.4bn), so SME/seller fee income must do real lifting, not just rebranding, to move the segment.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a defensive land-grab, not a new market: incumbent banks re-bundling to stop marketplace-captive banks from owning the seller relationship (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Sber closes the Ozon stake, Sellvi + an owned marketplace = the strongest seller-banking flywheel in RU; watch the AFK Sistema / Ozon deal close (trigger) and whether Sber discloses Sellvi seller count or loan book. Thesis breaks if Sellvi stays a rebrand of existing tools with no new data rights and sellers stick with Ozon Bank's in-platform balance.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (headline read).** IN-LINE/BEAT, momentum intact — but note that this is a product-brand announcement (Sellvi Business), NOT an earnings print; no financials are in the news itself. Grounding it in Sber's own filings: FY2025 IFRS net profit RUB 1,707.4bn (+8.0% YoY), record, ROE 22.7%; and the freshest RAS print (9M 2026, Oct 2026) shows net profit RUB 1.5tn (+18.2% YoY), ROE 23.0% — accelerating. Guidance: reaffirmed ~RUB 2tn FY2026 profit target. The relevant read for Sellvi is that fee/commission and transaction-business growth is the soft spot (fees roughly flat), so bolting SME/seller daily processes onto the bank is a deliberate push to re-accelerate the fee/transaction line that NII strength has been masking.

**Key figures (with growth).**
- FY2025 IFRS (filed 2026-02-26, PRIMARY): Profit attributable to shareholders RUB 1,707.4bn vs RUB 1,581.6bn FY2024 = +8.0% YoY (third straight record year; ~$22bn). ROE 22.7%. Net interest income +18.5% YoY to RUB 3.55tn (the profit driver). Net fee & commission income the weak line — fee income RUB 1,217.1bn vs RUB 1,172.1bn (+3.8%), fee expense RUB 383.4bn vs RUB 329.2bn. EPS RUB 77.8 (+8.0%). CIR 30.3%; equity +16.4% YtD to RUB 8.3tn; CAR 13.7%.
- Retail engine (FY2025 IFRS B2C): NII to individuals RUB 581.6bn vs RUB 441.2bn = +31.8% YoY; corporate (B2B) NII to corporates RUB 610.2bn vs RUB 617.4bn = −1.2% — i.e. the retail/individual side is doing the work, corporate NII is flat-to-down.
- Freshest print — 9M 2026 RAS (released Oct 2026, ~3mo fresher than the IFRS filing): net profit RUB 1.5tn, +18.2% YoY; ROE 23.0%; September 2026 alone RUB 168.5bn, +12.1% YoY, ROE 23.4%; NII +24.1% YoY to RUB 2.8tn; net fee & commission income RUB 534.6bn, −0.8% YoY; CIR 26.0%. Profit growth is re-accelerating (+18.2% RAS 9M26 vs +8.0% IFRS FY25) and efficiency improving (CIR 30.3%→26.0%), but fees are still flat-to-down — the structural gap this Sellvi move targets.
- Operating base (from Q2/Q3 2025 IFRS): retail clients >111.2mn; SberPrime subscribers 21.2mn; retail-lending market share ~48%. A very large installed SME + seller addressable base to cross-sell Sellvi into.

**By segment / driver.** Profitability is carried by net interest income (retail lending), with FX/credit-quality noise; the fee & commission line is the laggard (roughly flat in FY2025, slightly negative in 9M 2026 RAS). Sellvi Business targets exactly this laggard: by owning marketplace sellers' day-to-day processes (multi-platform sales management, own storefront with payments/delivery, business account, API integrations, expert support) Sber adds transaction, account and service fee streams and — per the note — richer behavioural data (sales, returns, commissions, promo/logistics spend) to sharpen SME credit-risk and working-capital underwriting. That both (a) feeds the NII engine via better-priced SME/working-capital lending and (b) builds the fee/transaction line that FY2025 shows as the weak spot.

**vs expectations / prior period.** No consensus applies to a product announcement — [UNSOURCED] for Sellvi itself. On the financials: FY2025 IFRS +8.0% was a record and broadly in line with management's own trajectory; 9M 2026 RAS +18.2% is a clear acceleration vs the +8.0% FY2025 pace and vs 9M 2025 (recall 9M 2025 IFRS operating income before provisions was +17.4% YoY). Press framing: analysts noted Sber "beats market expectations again" in 2026 and the bank maintaining a ~RUB 2tn FY2026 profit path. Momentum is positive, not deteriorating.

**Guidance / forward.** Reaffirmed: management guides FY2026 net profit toward ~RUB 2.0tn and continues to target ROE in the low-20s% (23.0% achieved 9M 2026 RAS). No guidance was attached to Sellvi (no disclosed revenue/seller-count target) — treat Sellvi contribution as [UNSOURCED]; (analysis) it is a retention/attach-rate play on the existing SME/seller base rather than a near-term P&L needle-mover. Tone: confident on profit and ROE; notably quiet on the flat fee & commission line, which is precisely why the SME/transaction-business pushes (Sellvi) matter for the forward fee trajectory.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. Fee/transaction line is the one soft metric (fees roughly flat FY2025, −0.8% 9M 2026 RAS) while NII roars (+18–24%). Why it matters: Sber's growth is interest-rate/retail-lending dependent; Sellvi is a structural attempt to diversify toward fee/transaction income from SME sellers. Second-order: if rate cuts compress NII (CBR at 14%), a bigger SME-services fee base cushions the mix.
2. Data-for-credit flywheel. Owning sellers' marketplace flows (sales/returns/commissions/promo/logistics) improves working-capital and credit underwriting. Why it matters: sharper risk pricing on a huge SME base supports both loan growth and cost-of-risk control. Second-order: deepens lock-in and raises switching costs for sellers — a retention moat, not just an acquisition channel.
3. Scale/attach leverage. 111mn+ retail and a large SME base mean Sellvi is an attach/cross-sell play, not a new-build; low marginal cost, CIR already improving (30.3%→26.0%). Why it matters: incremental fee/transaction revenue at high operating leverage. Second-order: competitive pressure on marketplace-native financial offerings (Ozon, Wildberries/WB, Yandex) and other banks' seller programmes.
4. De-PR caution. The announcement bundles "already existing" solutions under one brand — it is a repackage/retention move, not a new revenue engine; no disclosed seller or revenue target. Treat near-term financial impact as immaterial/[UNSOURCED]; the signal is strategic intent on the fee line, not a 2026 earnings catalyst.
