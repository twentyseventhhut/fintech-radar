МТС запускает оплату по QR-коду в Колумбии и Аргентине
 
 Максимальный платеж через приложение «Мой МТС» в Колумбии составляет 315 тыс. рублей, в Аргентине — 400 тыс. рублей.
 
 МТС запустила для физлиц оплату товаров и услуг по QR-коду в Колумбии и Аргентине через национальные системы мгновенных платежей. Сервис доступен в большинстве торговых точек обеих стран всем пользователям приложения «Мой МТС». Компания стала лидером по числу стран в Латинской Америке среди российских финтех-сервисов с оплатой по местным QR-кодам.

 В обеих странах используются универсальные QR-коды, объединяющие местные платежные сервисы, что упрощает безналичную оплату. В Аргентине и Колумбии оплата по QR-коду — один из основных способов безналичных расчетов.

 Кирилл Пальчун, руководитель кластера «Платежи и переводы» МТС, сообщил, что оплата по QR через «Мой МТС» уже доступна в пяти странах Латинской Америки. По его словам, сервис также работает более чем в 15 странах, включая Египет , Вьетнам и Китай , а компания продолжит расширять список направлений.

 Для оплаты нужно открыть приложение «Мой МТС», выбрать во вкладке «Деньги» пункт «Оплата по QR-коду» и сканировать код на терминале. Платежи можно совершать как с лицевых счетов абонентов, так и с карт российских банков, без комиссии и с конвертацией в местную валюту по курсу на момент операции. Максимальная сумма платежа в Колумбии составляет до 315 тыс. рублей, в Аргентине — до 400 тыс. рублей. Платежи до 5 тыс. рублей проходят без СМС-подтверждения.

 Сервис доступен всем пользователям приложения, в том числе с номером любого оператора. Абоненты МТС могут оплачивать покупки даже при отрицательном балансе, если есть подключение к интернету.

# Context-enrichment: MTS enables local QR payments in Colombia and Argentina
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-13 MTS (via the "Мой МТС" / My MTS super-app, payments run through MTS Bank) switched on QR-code payments for retail users in **Colombia and Argentina**, routed through those countries' national instant-payment rails (Colombia's **Bre-B**, Argentina's **Transferencias 3.0 / interoperable QR**). The user scans a merchant QR in the app's "Деньги → Оплата по QR-коду" tab; funds are pulled from the MTS mobile-account balance **or a Russian-bank card**, with on-the-fly FX at the spot rate and **no commission**. Caps: **RUB 315k in Colombia, RUB 400k in Argentina** per payment; payments under RUB 5k skip SMS confirmation; works for users on any carrier, and MTS subscribers can pay even on a negative balance if they have internet.
- **What this really is (de-PR'd):** not a new MTS product and not a local acquiring deal — it is the **addition of two destination countries** to an existing cross-border-QR feature that already runs in 15+ countries. The underlying rail is the Russian cross-border-QR scheme (CBR / NSPK "СБП за рубежом") that bridges Russian funds into a foreign domestic QR network; MTS is a distribution front-end, not the rail owner. (analysis)
- **Why framed this way:** the headline metric is a **self-defined superlative** — "leader by number of LatAm countries **among Russian fintech services**." That carefully excludes banks: Sberbank already supports foreign-QR in ~12 countries (incl. Argentina and Brazil) and VTB in ~10. So "leader" is true only inside the narrow "Russian non-bank / telco-fintech" slice, and only on the LatAm sub-count. The RUB caps (not local currency) confirm this is an outbound-Russian-traveler tool, not a local-market play.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Russian outbound-QR rivals (the real comp set):** **Sber** (~12 countries incl. Argentina, Brazil, Vietnam, Thailand, Indonesia), **VTB** (~10 countries), **T-Bank** (~5). Industry data: Russian tourists made 400k+ foreign-QR payments worth ~RUB 1.3bn in 2026, up tens-of-x YoY. MTS's edge is a specific LatAm country count, not scale — Sber/VTB already cover the same two countries. (so the "leader" claim is marketing positioning, not a defensible moat).
- **Local LatAm QR rails MTS rides on:** Colombia's **Bre-B** (launched 2025-10-06; ~370M tx, 33M users, 2.8M merchants in first 3 months) and Argentina's **Transferencias 3.0** (62.6M tx in Dec 2024). MTS adds no local infrastructure; it plugs foreign spend into mature domestic rails.
- **Local/foreign-traveler analogues in corpus:** [[Nu Colombia adds QR payments through Bre-B]] (Nubank wiring the *same* Bre-B rail for locals) and [[Satoshi Tango integrates Brazil's PIX for Argentine travelers]] (wallet bridging travelers into a foreign QR rail — the closest structural twin) and [[9Pay opens stablecoin-to-QR corridor for US fintechs in Vietnam]] (another inbound-traveler-into-local-QR bridge).

## [2] Company history / fit
MTS has been serially adding countries to the same feature in 2026: **Brazil (Aug 26) → Peru + Bolivia (Sept 9) → Colombia + Argentina (Oct 13)** = 5 LatAm markets, 15+ globally (Egypt, Vietnam, China, Philippines…). It fits MTS's broader super-app/fintech push visible in the corpus: [[MTS Bank app adds rail and air ticket purchases]], [[MTS Pay adds separate charitable-donation transactions]], [[MTS Bank enables money transfers inside Max chats]]. 
- **Why MTS does this:** a telco monetizing its mobile-account + MTS Bank base by making "Мой МТС" the default wallet for Russians traveling where Visa/Mastercard no longer work for Russian cards. Each new country is low marginal cost (same rail integration) and high PR yield — a cheap way to keep the super-app sticky. (analysis)

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new here:** only the **two incremental countries** and their RUB caps/SMS thresholds. The capability, UX, zero-commission model and Russian-card funding are unchanged from the prior Brazil/Peru/Bolivia rollouts — so novelty is **incremental, not categorical**.
- **Traction evidence:** none MTS-specific disclosed (no MTS tx counts, GMV, or active-user figures for these corridors). The only hard adoption numbers are the *rails'* (Bre-B 370M tx) and the *market's* (400k Russian foreign-QR payments / RUB 1.3bn in 2026 across all providers). MTS's own share is unstated — a classic "announced, not proven" gap.
- **Who captures margin:** MTS runs it commission-free, so it earns on **FX spread** (conversion "at the spot rate" leaves room) and retention, not fees. The domestic rail (Bre-B/Transf. 3.0) and NSPK own the actual settlement economics; MTS is a thin front-end. The value-add to users is real (a working payment method where cards are dead) but the value-add to MTS is marketing/retention, not a new revenue line. (analysis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
MTS says it will keep adding destinations; Russian industry commentary expects foreign-QR coverage to reach ~10 LatAm/other countries in 2026. Structural driver: sanctions killed Russian Visa/Mastercard abroad, and the CBR/NSPK cross-border-QR scheme is the sanctioned-economy workaround — so every Russian bank/telco is racing to bolt more countries onto the same rail. 
- **Second-order:** because all providers share the one NSPK rail and the same foreign domestic networks, **differentiation collapses to country-count and UX** — a feature-parity race with no durable moat. The risk is geopolitical/operational: a single foreign rail or correspondent cutting off the Russian bridge removes a country overnight. (hypothesis) So "leader by LatAm countries" is a fragile, easily-matched title, not a strategic position.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits in cross-border retail payments for Russians abroad — a niche carved out after 2022, when Visa/Mastercard/Apple Pay/Google Pay stopped working on Russian-issued cards outside Russia and SWIFT access was curtailed. The workaround that has emerged is riding destination countries' domestic instant-payment QR rails: MTS routes a payment from a Russian mobile account or a Russian bank card into the local national scheme, converting to local currency at the time of the transaction. The "why now" is a supply-side tailwind — LatAm instant-payment rails only recently became interoperable and QR-first: Colombia's Bre-B launched 2026-10-06 and by mid-2026 had processed 670m+ transactions across 34m+ users with end-user transfers free until at least 2028 (per Banco de la República, via dLocal/Ria, as of mid-2026); Argentina's Transferencias 3.0 QR rail had already reached ~62.6m transactions/month by Dec-2024 (per BCRA, via secondary citations). These are free, universal, merchant-side rails — MTS is a guest on them, not an operator. Structure: on the Russian side this is a small, fragmented land-grab among the few domestic players still able to move money across the border (MTS, Sber, VTB); value here is captured at the FX spread and float, not at the rail. Barriers are regulatory/access, not technological.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for a payments rider: TPV through the corridor, active users, FX take/spread, per-transaction margin — MTS discloses none of these for this service, so position is `(analysis)`. Competition is on country coverage and friction, not price (MTS advertises "no commission," so margin is implied to sit in the conversion rate — the PR is silent on the spread, a de-PR flag). Key players on the Russian side: Sber (QR via AliPay in China and a handful of CIS/Asia markets); VTB (launched phone-number transfers to mobile wallets in six African countries at 0.5% on 2026-10-12 — see [[Transfers to mobile wallets launch across six African countries]], a remittance adjacency rather than a merchant-QR rival). Globally, the closer analogue is traveler-QR apps like Moreta (QR payments for foreigners across ~14 countries). MTS's own recent moves: Brazil via Pix (RUB 800k cap, My MTS; per Telecompaper/akm.ru), Egypt (2026-01-27), Philippines (QRPh) — so Colombia/Argentina extend an already-running playbook, now claimed as 5 LatAm countries and >15 globally. Position: `(analysis)` first-mover among Russian fintechs in LatAm by country count (the company's own claim, unverified), but the moat is thin — the underlying rails are public infrastructure any competitor can also connect to; the only durable edge is the My MTS distribution base.

**Comps & multiples.** No deal/round/valuation attaches to this product launch, so trading multiples are not the right lens — this is a feature, not a financing event. IR grounding (primary): MTS Q1 2026 Press Release (earnings_release, 2026-05-20) — group revenue RUB 201.3bn (+14.7% YoY), OIBDA RUB 74.7bn (+18.0% YoY), with FinTech named among the primary growth drivers alongside Telecom (per MTS IR, 2026-05-20). MTS does NOT break out a discrete fintech/MTS Bank segment revenue figure in the Q1 2026 release surfaced via web, and the IR databook (semsearch down, HTTP 402) could not be queried — so fintech-segment revenue = no data / `[UNSOURCED]`. Internal comps (base): [[Transfers to mobile wallets launch across six African countries]] (cross-border consumer transfers, 0.5% fee — a priced comp vs MTS's "free"); [[MTS Pay adds separate charitable-donation transactions]] (same MTS payments cluster, domestic). Distribution not computed — qualitative only; no ≥3 comparable priced figures exist.

**Risk flags.**
1. Dependence on someone else's rails — MTS is a guest on Bre-B/Transferencias 3.0/Pix. If a central bank restricts foreign-funded or non-resident access (an AML/FX-control lever these schemes retain), the service can be switched off per country with no recourse, and sanctions-era banking relationships make MTS an easy target for such a carve-out.
2. Economics undisclosed — "no commission" shifts the entire margin into the undisclosed FX conversion spread + float; a thin or disclosed spread would make this a cost center that exists for retention/ARPU, not a profit line (second-order: it props up telecom/fintech stickiness, not P&L).
3. Breadth-over-depth execution risk — "available in most merchants" and "5 LatAm / 15+ countries" are coverage claims, not usage; announced ≠ adopted. Low per-country TPV on a rail MTS doesn't own means the whole corridor could stay sub-scale while generating PR.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector, this is a re-tread, not a re-rating: Russian-abroad payments is consolidating around 2-3 domestic incumbents stitching together destination countries' public QR rails, and country count is becoming the vanity metric. Falsifiable thesis `(analysis)`: this is a retention/ARPU play for My MTS, not a standalone revenue engine — it would be WRONG if MTS starts disclosing fintech cross-border TPV or a spread as a monetized line. Trigger to watch: whether any LatAm regulator moves to gate non-resident/foreign-funded access to its instant-payment scheme (would validate risk flag 1 and cap the whole corridor).

Scale vs the group is tiny today: a geographic footprint and active-user-engagement story, not a results mover. Growth here is reported as number of countries (claimed LatAm leader among Russian fintechs; 5 LatAm, 15+ total), not disclosed TPV/revenue — treat adoption/volume as (analysis), no data.

**Thesis-flags.** (1) Fintech revenue +18.4% YoY is solid but net-profit's 2.6x is provision-release-led, not structural — watch reserve normalization.
