OPay IPO: NGX pushes for Nigerian investors to own fintech giant
Nigeria’s technology sector has expanded across digital payments, financial services, electronic commerce and other technology-enabled businesses
The Nigerian Exchange Group ( NGX Group 
) is seeking to widen domestic ownership of Nigeria-focused technology businesses as reports of OPay’s planned initial public offering ( IPO 
) in the United States revive calls for a potential listing on the Nigerian Exchange.
The proposed IPO and a possible secondary listing of OPay on the NGX could create an opportunity for Nigerian investors to acquire stakes in one of Africa’s leading fintech companies, while strengthening the domestic capital market’s participation in the country’s expanding digital economy.
The development comes less than two months after NGX Group’s leadership, led by Chairman Umaru Kwairanga and Group Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer Temi Popoola, met with President Bola Ahmed Tinubu at the Presidential Villa in Abuja to advocate a national strategy for encouraging major Nigerian businesses, particularly technology companies, to list locally or pursue dual listings.
At the 6 August meeting, Popoola specifically identified OPay among the fintech companies whose participation in the domestic capital market could expand investment opportunities for Nigerians and allow them to share in the long-term value created by businesses operating in the country.
The proposal reflects NGX Group’s push to move Nigerians beyond being consumers of digital services to becoming shareholders in the companies benefiting from their patronage. The strategy could broaden retail participation, attract new investors and diversify the ownership base of listed companies.
For OPay, whose business is supported by a substantial Nigerian customer base, a domestic listing could potentially provide local investors with access to its equity, subject to the company’s plans, regulatory approvals and prevailing market conditions.
While a US IPO would give OPay access to international investors and capital, a secondary listing on the NGX could provide an additional route for Nigerian investors to participate in the company’s growth without relying solely on foreign markets.
Kwairanga has consistently positioned the capital market as a platform for financing enterprises, supporting industrialisation and promoting wider participation in economic growth. Bringing major technology companies to the Exchange would, therefore, serve not only to increase the number of listed companies but also to mobilise long-term capital and connect domestic investors with businesses that have significant economic footprints.
Nigeria’s technology sector has expanded across digital payments, financial services, electronic commerce and other technology-enabled businesses. However, opportunities for domestic investors to acquire direct equity stakes in some of these fast-growing companies remain limited.
NGX Group’s proposed approach seeks to bridge this gap by encouraging businesses with substantial Nigerian operations to consider the domestic capital market as part of their funding and ownership strategies.
A successful listing by a major fintech company could also broaden the sectoral composition of the Exchange, potentially attract younger and technology-oriented investors, and deepen market liquidity. These outcomes, however, would depend on the terms of any offering, investor demand, regulatory requirements and the number of shares made available to the Nigerian market.
For the Exchange, attracting technology-driven businesses would complement efforts to strengthen Nigeria’s capital market as a source of long-term funding for companies seeking to expand their operations and compete internationally.
The engagement with President Tinubu formed part of NGX Group’s broader advocacy for closer collaboration among the government, regulators and corporate leaders to increase listings and expand investment opportunities in the domestic market.
The proposed OPay listing also raises a wider question about how Nigeria can translate the growth of its digital economy into broader domestic wealth creation. Where companies build substantial customer bases and business operations in Nigeria but seek capital abroad, a domestic listing could provide an avenue for local investors to participate in their corporate growth.
Nevertheless, OPay’s reported IPO plans and the possibility of a secondary listing on the NGX should not be interpreted as confirmation that the company has committed to listing on the Exchange. Any such transaction would depend on the company’s final decisions, commercial considerations and the necessary regulatory approvals.
As more technology companies explore international capital markets, the potential OPay listing has brought renewed attention to the need to ensure that Nigerian investors can participate in the value generated by businesses serving the domestic economy.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- De-PR'd: OPay's **F-1 (filed 9 Oct 2026)** states it has "obtained the approval of our board and shareholders to pursue a **secondary listing on the Nigerian Exchange Limited**" **after** a primary NYSE listing (ticker reported as OPAY, target valuation ~$4B). That is the hard fact.
- This note's source instead leads with NGX's **advocacy**: at a **6 Aug 2026** meeting at the Presidential Villa (Abuja), NGX Group Chairman Umaru Kwairanga and GMD/CEO Temi Popoola urged President Tinubu to adopt a national strategy pushing Nigerian tech firms to list locally / dual-list; Popoola named OPay specifically.
- **Why framed this way / what it reveals:** the article converts a company decision (already in the F-1) into an NGX "win" narrative. The causality is PR-flattering — it implies NGX advocacy drove OPay's choice, but a secondary listing of a US-listed ADS issuer is primarily OPay's own capital-markets/optics decision (89.5% of H1-2026 revenue is Nigerian; a local listing is defensive against a "foreign-capital-on-local-customers" nationalist critique). The piece itself admits this is "not confirmation that the company has committed to listing" — a hedge that contradicts the fact that the F-1 already records shareholder approval. So the note *understates* reality on the commitment and *overstates* NGX's agency.

## [1] Competitors / peers

- **Why the lay of the land is this way:** NGX is structurally starved of large, young, tech-driven issuers; its push is a supply-side campaign to broaden sectoral composition and retail participation. Second-order: a dual-listed OPay creates a **fungibility/arbitrage problem** — the F-1 warns NGX shares "may not be fungible with or exchangeable into our ADSs," so local demand could push NGX prices to a different level than NYSE with no clean arbitrage path. The "let Nigerians own it" story thus carries a built-in retail-investor risk the PR glosses over.

- **Why OPay acts this way:** ownership is overwhelmingly foreign (Chinese founder, SoftBank, Opera) while ~90% of revenue is Nigerian. A US listing maximises valuation/liquidity; a Nigerian secondary listing is a **legitimacy/political hedge** against the recurring nationalist critique (foreign capital extracting value from Nigerian consumers) and smooths regulatory relations (CBN, SEC Nigeria under the Investments and Securities Act 2025). It costs little and buys goodwill.

## Sector & drivers

Nigerian digital payments is the deepest fintech market in Africa, structured as a **two-layer oligopoly**: (1) agent/merchant acquiring + consumer wallets (OPay, Moniepoint, PalmPay) and (2) B2B/enterprise rails (Flutterwave, Interswitch, HabariPay/GTCO). Entry barriers are regulatory (CBN licensing, geo-tagging mandates) + network effects on the agent footprint — capital alone doesn't buy the distribution.

- **Market concentration / size proxy:** Moniepoint processed ~₦412tn (~$294bn) in-person volume in 2025 and claims 8/10 in-person transactions; OPay disclosed **$558bn GTV TTM** in its F-1. Per TechCabal, Nigeria's top-9 fintechs were valued ~$10.6bn as of Jan 2026 (via Brand Communicator / NCW, as of 2026-01). The sector is large but ownership is almost entirely offshore (Cayman/Singapore holdcos) — the structural gap NGX is targeting.
- **"Why now" driver — the domestic-ownership politics.** Two Nigerian megacaps are simultaneously choosing *foreign* capital markets (OPay → NYSE "OPAY", F-1 filed 2026-10-09; Airtel Money → GBP5.3bn London IPO, see [[Airtel Money makes London trading debut in GBP5.3B IPO]]). NGX's 2026-08-06 meeting with President Tinubu (naming OPay) is a reaction: a push to let 50M+ Nigerian users become shareholders, not just customers. This is **advocacy, not policy** — no primary legislation changed foreign-ownership or listing rules in 2025 (per US State Dept 2026 Investment Climate Statement).
- **FX / repatriation backdrop.** The naira lost ~70% vs USD since the 2023 float; CBN (Cardoso) cleared a ~$7bn FX backlog, easing repatriation. Dual NGX listings have historically served as an FX-repatriation channel during capital-control episodes (per Businessday/Legal500). A secondary NGX line would give OPay a naira-denominated venue aligned with where its revenue is actually earned (**89.5% Nigeria**, F-1).

## Competitive landscape

Sector KPIs: **GTV/TPV, take rate, DAU/MAU, agent-network depth, lending mix.**

- **OPay (protagonist):** 50.1M MAU / 26.8M DAU (F-1, Jul 2026); 20M+ DAU milestone + top-10 global fintech app claim (IR, 2025-11-15, media-outreach); first monthly profit May 2024 (IR, prnewswire). - **Flutterwave:** ~$3bn (Feb-2022 Series D, stale 3+ yrs); B2B rails, *already* pitched a $250M NGX IPO inviting FG to take $75M (see [[Flutterwave invites Nigeria to invest $75M in $250M NGX IPO]]) — the direct precedent for NGX's OPay ask. **Protagonist moat (analysis):** network effects on the Nigerian consumer base + newly-disclosed profitability; weaker on the merchant/PoS flank where Moniepoint leads.
