 Abu Dhabi-based Phoenix Venture Partners has completed fundraising for its debut venture fund, raising about $23 million across the fund and dedicated co-investment vehicles as it prepares to more than double its portfolio. 
 The manager stopped accepting commitments on October 3, almost two years after its first close in October 2024 , when it set a $50 million fundraising target. It didn’t disclose how much of the $23 million belongs to Phoenix Venture Partners Innovation Fund itself. 
 Founded by Steve Khayat, Faris Al-Obaid and Mussaad Al-Razouki, PVP backs early-stage technology companies across MENA, with scope to invest internationally. Its interests span fintech, healthcare, education, logistics and sustainability. 
 The fund has drawn commitments from 42 investors across nine jurisdictions. Backers include Abu Dhabi’s KSH Investments, the international investment platform of the Private Department of Sheikh Mohamed Bin Khalid Al Nahyan, and Kuwait-listed Al-Imtiaz Investment Group. Kuwait’s Fawzi Musaad Al-Saleh Family Office and Lucaslux, the Luxembourg-based private investment fund of Patrick Lucas, also participated. 
 PVP plans to expand the fund’s portfolio from seven companies to between 15 and 20. About 45% of committed capital remains available for new deals and follow-on investments, with deployment expected to continue through 2027 and potentially into 2028. 
 The firm has brought its investors into individual transactions alongside the fund. In September, it led UAE consumer fintech Sav’s $3.5 million pre-Series A , with participation from co-investors drawn from the fund’s backers. 
 Its recently disclosed investment in COFE Tech followed a similar approach, with KSH among the co-investors. PVP bought existing shares before the procurement and commerce software company’s $43 million pre-IPO round, and Khayat joined its board. 
 PVP reported a total value to paid-in capital ratio, or TVPI, of 1.53x, which it said placed the fund above the 1.50x threshold for the top decile of 2024-vintage funds in Carta’s Q2 2026 benchmark data. The manager said it had met the top-decile threshold for three consecutive quarters. 
 The measure includes the value of unsold investments. A TVPI of 1.53x means the fund’s remaining holdings and any distributions together are worth $1.53 for every dollar investors have paid in; it does not mean that amount has been returned in cash.

# Context-enrichment: Phoenix Venture Partners completes ~$23M debut fundraise
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 1/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Abu Dhabi (ADGM)-based **Phoenix Venture Partners (PVP)** stopped accepting commitments on **3 Oct 2026**, closing its debut fund — the **Phoenix Venture Partners Innovation Fund (PVPIF)**, a 2024-vintage vehicle — at **~$23M across the fund AND dedicated co-investment vehicles**. 42 investors across nine jurisdictions.
- **The headline is a soft miss dressed as a milestone.** At first close (Oct 2024) PVP set a **$50M target** (see [[Phoenix Venture Partners reaches third close on debut MENA fund]]). The "completed fundraise" actually lands at **<50% of target**, ~2 years later. The firm did **not disclose how much of the $23M is the fund itself** vs. the co-invest SPVs — a tell: the standalone commingled fund is likely materially smaller than $23M. Calling the blended figure "the raise" flatters a sub-scale close.
- **Why structured this way:** blending fund + co-invest vehicles maximizes the reported number and lets PVP frame a shortfall as a "completion." The TVPI flex (1.53x, "top-decile of 2024-vintage per Carta Q2 2026") is paper mark on 7 early-stage holdings including unrealized gains — not cash returned; a 2-year-old fund with 45% dry powder has had no exits.
- **Live facts:** 7 portfolio companies, targeting 15–20; ~45% of committed capital undeployed; deployment runs through 2027, possibly 2028. Co-invest model in action: led UAE consumer fintech **Sav**'s $3.5M pre-Series A (Sept 2026); bought secondary in **COFE Tech** ahead of its $43M pre-IPO (Khayat took a board seat).

## [1] Competitors / peers
Same-window MENA/debut-fund comps in corpus:
- [[Oraseya Capital doubles venture fund to AED1 billion]] — Dubai (DIFC/Dubai Future District), **$272M**, ~12x PVP's size, same fintech/AI/healthcare/digital-assets thesis. (importance 3)
- [[Antares Ventures secures over $50M at second close of debut fund]] — deep-tech debut, already 2x+ PVP at an interim close. (importance 2)
- [[Pear VC closes $333M fifth fund for pre-seed and seed]] — US, established franchise, scale reference. (importance 2)
- **Position:** PVP is a **sub-scale micro-fund at the bottom of the peer set** — an order of magnitude below Oraseya and even below Antares's *interim* close. **Why this layout:** GCC LP capital in 2025–26 concentrated into sovereign-anchored / larger platforms (Oraseya has Dubai govt backing); a founder-led first-timer with only family-office + HNW backers and no anchor LP struggled to clear its own target, consistent with the broader flight-to-scale in first-time-fund raising.

## [2] Company history / fit
Founded by **Steve Khayat (CEO), Faris Al-Obaid, Mussaad Al-Razouki**; ADGM-domiciled. Timeline: first close Oct 2024 ($50M target) → second close Mar 2025 → **third close 13 Mar 2026** ([[Phoenix Venture Partners reaches third close on debut MENA fund]], which explicitly flagged a **planned final close in Oct 2026**) → **final close 3 Oct 2026 at ~$23M**. Mandate: early-stage, MENA-focused with global/G20 scope, across ~7 verticals — fintech, healthtech, edtech, agrifood, logistics/mobility, energy/sustainability, consumer. **Why it acts this way:** a first-time, multi-sector generalist micro-manager leaning on a co-investment/SPV structure to stretch a thin commingled fund and keep deploying while still "raising" — the blended $23M framing is a structural consequence of not reaching institutional scale.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Nothing novel — a standard first-time GCC micro-VC final close. **Fintech relevance is thin:** fintech is one of seven verticals; the one named fintech bet (Sav, $3.5M) is tiny. Traction is unproven: 1.53x TVPI is an **unrealized paper mark** on 7 names, no exits/DPI, 45% uncalled. **Who captures value:** at this size the GP's economics hinge on co-invest fees/carry from SPVs more than a sub-scale fund's 2%-and-20%; LPs are buying GCC early-stage access, not a differentiated edge.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Portfolio to grow to 15–20, deploy through 2027–28. **Why the market tilts this way:** GCC VC fundraising is bifurcating — sovereign/government-anchored and larger platforms ([[Oraseya Capital doubles venture fund to AED1 billion]]) absorb LP capital while first-time generalist micro-funds undershoot targets. Second-order: PVP's sub-scale close and co-invest reliance make follow-on reserves thin, raising dilution/signaling risk for its startups in down rounds. Materiality to a fintech reader: **marginal** — a small regional house, not a sector-moving event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Phoenix Venture Partners (PVP) is an Abu Dhabi (ADGM-incorporated) early-stage manager whose debut vehicle, PVPIF, closed on ~$23M across the fund and co-investment SPVs (commitments shut 2026-10-03, ~2 years after the Oct-2024 first close) (per fwdstart.me / pvp-me.com, 2026-10-12). This lands in a contracting MENA venture tape: MENA startups raised ~$1.35bn across 214 deals in H1 2026, funding −22% YoY and deal count −41% YoY (per MAGNiTT H1-2026 review; secondary, treat as directional). Structure: the MENA emerging-manager layer is thin and sovereign/family-office-dependent — ~23 micro-VCs plus accelerators/angels serve pre-seed, and the region is a ~$3bn+/yr funding market shaped by sovereign-wealth anchors and localization mandates (per MAGNiTT / valu.vc, 2026). "Why now": closing a debut fund against a −22% regional funding backdrop is counter-cyclical, and the economics favor it — Carta data shows top-decile TVPI is structurally highest in the smallest funds ($1–10M vintage 2018 90th-pct TVPI ~4.03x vs ~1.67x for $100M+ funds) (per carta.com), so a $23M vehicle can post strong early multiples off a handful of marks.

**Competitive landscape.** KPIs for a sub-scale debut fund: deployment pace, dry-powder / reserve capacity, TVPI→DPI conversion (none realized yet), and LP re-up ability for Fund II. PVP: 7 portfolio companies today, targeting 15–20; ~45% of committed capital still available; deployment running through 2027, possibly 2028 (per fwdstart.me). LP base = 42 investors across 9 jurisdictions — KSH Investments (Abu Dhabi), Al-Imtiaz Investment Group (Kuwait-listed), Fawzi Musaad Al-Saleh Family Office, Lucaslux (Luxembourg) — i.e. Gulf sovereign-adjacent + family-office capital, not institutional LPs. Basis of competition for a first-time manager is thesis/relationships + co-invest access, not price; PVP's co-invest model (e.g. leading UAE fintech Sav's $3.5M pre-Series A in Sep-2026; a pre-IPO secondary into COFE Tech ahead of its $43M round, with Khayat taking a board seat) is its differentiator. Position: niche/sub-scale emerging manager with a broad multi-sector mandate (fintech, healthcare, education, logistics, sustainability); moat (analysis) is Gulf LP relationships + deal-by-deal co-invest, not scale or realized track record.

**Comps & multiples.** Not an operating company — no EV/Revenue/EBITDA multiples; compare on fund scale/stage. Debut/emerging-manager peers in-base: [[Antares Ventures secures over $50M at second close of debut fund]] (debut, >$50M at second close, SG deep-tech — ~2x+ larger, still open), [[Oraseya Capital doubles venture fund to AED1 billion]] (~$272M, MEA multi-sector — ~12x larger), [[Pear VC closes $333M fifth fund for pre-seed and seed]] (established US seed, $333M — ~14x larger), [[Union Square Ventures raises $900M across two funds]] (established — ~39x larger). PVP is the smallest of the recent 2026 fund-close cohort (~$23M). Performance: reported TVPI 1.53x, which PVP says cleared the 1.50x top-decile threshold for 2024-vintage funds in Carta's Q2-2026 benchmark, met for three consecutive quarters (per fwdstart.me / pvp-me.com). Arithmetic/meaning: TVPI 1.53x = $1.53 of current value (held + distributed) per $1 paid in — paper marks, NOT cash; DPI (realized) = no data (effectively ~0x this early). **Risk flags.**
1. **Target undershoot — raised ~$23M against a $50M goal (~54% below target).** The debut fund stopped at less than half its stated $50M target after ~2 years of raising. Why: a half-size fund compresses portfolio construction (still aiming 15–20 names) and reserve/follow-on firepower; with only ~45% dry powder left, pro-rata defense across a doubling portfolio looks thin, raising dilution risk in later rounds.
2. **TVPI is unrealized, self-reported, and early-vintage.** 1.53x is paper value with ~0 DPI on a ~2-year-old 2024-vintage fund still in the J-curve; the "top-decile vs Carta 1.50x" claim is manager-selected framing, and for small funds top-decile TVPI bars are structurally low/volatile on few marks (per carta.com). Why: early TVPI on an illiquid, undistributed book is a weak predictor of cash returns, and a single markdown among 7 names could swing it below benchmark.
3. **Concentration + regional/LP dependence.** 7 holdings, Gulf/family-office LP base, co-invest model bringing the same backers into deals — concentrated by company, geography (MENA-tilted) and capital source, into a −22% YoY MENA funding market (per MAGNiTT). Why: correlated exposure — a regional funding/exit slowdown hits portfolio marks and LP re-up appetite for Fund II simultaneously; MENA's thin exit market means DPI (and thus a credible Fund II raise) is long-dated.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) A capital-formation data point, not a re-rating: Gulf family-office/sovereign-adjacent LPs will still seed a sub-scale MENA debut manager counter-cyclically, but the sub-target close signals genuine fundraising friction in a −22% market. Falsifiable thesis: PVP grows to 15–20 companies and raises a larger Fund II — trigger that confirms it is a Fund II announcement with institutional (not just family-office) LPs; what breaks it is deployment stalling, the TVPI mark slipping below 1.50x, or no exits/DPI by the end of the 2027–2028 deployment window.
