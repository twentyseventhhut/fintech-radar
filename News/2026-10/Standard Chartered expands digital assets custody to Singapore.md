## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Institutional digital-asset custody — the regulated "back office" (safekeeping of keys for crypto, stablecoins, tokenised RWAs) that traditional allocators treat as a precondition for exposure. Market sizing is noisy and mostly vendor-sourced: per Mordor Intelligence (via mordorintelligence.com, as of 2026) the digital-asset custody market is ~$0.7tn in 2026 growing at a ~24.7% CAGR to ~$2.12tn by 2031; a separate Research and Markets report cites a larger base at ~17.6% CAGR — treat the absolute TAM as [UNSOURCED]/"no data" (methodologies differ, no independent figure), the ~20%+ growth direction is the usable signal. Structure: **two-tier and consolidating at the top** — a handful of regulated qualified custodians (Coinbase, BitGo, Anchorage, Fidelity Digital Assets, BNY) plus tech/MPC vendors (Fireblocks), now being crowded by chartered banks (Citi, BNY, StanChart). Barriers are regulatory (bank/trust charters, MAS DPT licensing) and trust/balance-sheet, not technology. Why now: post-ETF institutional demand + regulatory green lights (US GENIUS Act, OCC bank charters for crypto custodians, MAS tokenisation push) make a bank-grade APAC custody footprint timely — Singapore is the regional hub where tokenised-RWA demand is concentrating.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: assets under custody (AUC), number of supported assets/chains, insurance coverage, regulatory status (qualified custodian / bank charter). Players & basis of competition — competition is on **trust + regulation + distribution**, not price: Coinbase Custody ~$300bn AUC and primary custodian for 9/11 spot-BTC and 8/9 ETH ETFs (per fireblocks.com/eco.com, 2026); BitGo ~$65.2bn Assets-on-Platform at 30-Jun-2026 (incl. $11.9bn staked) per its FY2026 10-Q, 1,700+ assets, OCC national-bank charter approved Dec-2025; Anchorage (OCC-chartered bank); Fireblocks (MPC tech layer); plus incumbents Fidelity and BNY. Recent moves: StanChart agreed to absorb Zodia Custody's book (May-2026, [[Standard Chartered to absorb Zodia Custody crypto business]]) and took a stake in GSR at a $1bn valuation ([[Standard Chartered takes stake in GSR at $1 billion valuation]]); Citi targeting a 2026 custody launch ([[Citi plans to offer crypto custody services in 2026]]); BNY expanding USDC/crypto custody ([[BNY expands Circle ties for institutional USDC custody]]). Protagonist position: **catching up as a bank-channel entrant, niche-but-credible in APAC** — StanChart already runs live digital-asset custody in UAE, Luxembourg and Hong Kong, so Singapore is incremental geography on an existing (Zodia-consolidated) platform. Moat is distribution + banking licence + Asia/Africa/Middle East footprint (intangible/regulatory), not scale: its AUC is not disclosed, so it is dwarfed by Coinbase/BitGo on volume — StanChart's AUC [UNSOURCED].

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/metrics attach to this news (it is a product/geo expansion), so trading multiples are not computable for StanChart's custody unit — "distribution not computed, qualitative comparison." Reference points for the sub-vertical: Coinbase (COIN, public) and BitGo (BTGO, 10-Q filer) are the only publicly-figured peers; BitGo ~$65.2bn AoP vs Coinbase ~$300bn AUC frames the gap StanChart enters against. Internal comps (base precedent for the bank-custody wave, as `[[wikilink]]`): [[Standard Chartered to absorb Zodia Custody crypto business]], [[Standard Chartered takes stake in GSR at $1 billion valuation]], [[Citi plans to offer crypto custody services in 2026]], [[BNY expands Circle ties for institutional USDC custody]], [[Ripple and Absa Bank launch digital asset custody in South Africa]]. EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E = no data (no standalone unit financials). Growth-vs-multiple check not applicable.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Execution / "announced vs live":** news is an intent to expand pending MAS regulatory sign-off and no confirmed launch date or asset list — classic de-PR gap; the "live in UAE/HK" track record de-risks it but Singapore remains conditional.
2. **Late-entrant scale disadvantage:** StanChart enters a market where Coinbase (~$300bn) and BitGo (~$65bn) already hold the ETF/institutional flow; custody economics are thin-margin and scale-driven, so a sub-scale AUC risks being a loss-leader feeding its trading/GSR franchise rather than a standalone profit centre.
3. **Integration/concentration risk:** the Singapore push rides on the just-absorbed Zodia book — integration slippage or client attrition from the Zodia consolidation could undercut the expansion; dependence on one acquired platform concentrates operational risk.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is the APAC leg of a broader **bank re-entry into crypto custody** — regulated incumbents (StanChart, Citi, BNY) displacing crypto-native custodians on the institutional mandate where counterparty trust and a banking licence matter most (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if MAS licensing clears and StanChart converts its existing asset-servicing clients, bank-channel custodians take measurable share of tokenised-RWA/institutional AUC in Singapore within 12–18 months; trigger to watch — a confirmed MAS go-live and the first disclosed Singapore AUC/client numbers. What breaks it: MAS delay, or crypto-native custodians (Coinbase/BitGo, now bank-chartered themselves) defending the ETF/institutional flow on price and asset breadth.
