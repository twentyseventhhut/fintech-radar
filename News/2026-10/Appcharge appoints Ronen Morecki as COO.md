Appcharge appoints Ronen Morecki as Chief Operating Officer
 
Appcharge has appointed Ronen Morecki as Chief Operating Officer. He joins from monday.com.
The hire comes as app developers and publishers seek more direct control over payments and customer relationships outside traditional app-store billing channels.
Morecki was previously Vice President of Payments at monday.com. Earlier in his career, he co-founded payments technology company ZooZ and later became Chief Technology Officer at PayU after it acquired the business.
At Appcharge, he will oversee payments strategy and operations as the company expands its Merchant of Record platform for app developers, starting with mobile gaming and moving into other parts of the app economy.
Game publishers were the first to adopt direct-to-consumer payment models at scale, according to Appcharge. The company says changes in court rulings and regulation in the US and EU have widened the opening for apps selling digital goods or subscriptions through Apple and Google to use alternative payment routes.
Appcharge expects subscription, fitness, education and AI apps to follow, as more app businesses weigh the trade-off between app-store distribution and direct customer ownership.
Morecki brings experience from both sides of the payments market. On the provider side, he helped build ZooZ before its sale to PayU. On the merchant side, he led work at monday.com spanning payments, pricing and packaging, billing and the subscription lifecycle.
Appcharge also highlighted the scale of the market it is targeting, citing Grand View Research forecasts that the global in-app purchase market across consumer apps will reach USD $450 billion by 2030. The company sees that as creating a direct-to-consumer opportunity worth tens of billions of dollars across the wider app economy.
Its Merchant of Record model is designed to handle functions beyond payment acceptance alone, including tax, fraud management, chargebacks, compliance obligations and the management of multiple acquirers across regions.
 Payments shift 
For mobile game publishers, those issues are especially complex because of high transaction volumes and the need to support a wide range of local payment methods in different markets. That has made gaming an early test case for companies trying to move more sales beyond app-store billing systems.
Morecki's remit will include strengthening the platform, broadening payment coverage and helping build the next phase of the company's operations as it targets a wider range of app categories.
Maor Sason, Chief Executive Officer and Co-Founder of Appcharge, set out the company's view of the market. "Games are the hardest payments problem in the app economy, with huge transaction volumes and dozens of local payment methods in every market," said Sason.
"Ronen has built and run complex global payment organisations, which is exactly what we need as we take DTC further and deliver value for publishers in the wider app economy," he added.
Morecki described Merchant of Record as the next stage after payment orchestration tools, which have focused mainly on routing transactions. His comments reflect a broader debate in digital commerce over which parts of the payment stack merchants should control directly and which they should outsource to specialist providers.
"Every business wants a payments partner that takes the complexity out without taking away control," said Ronen Morecki, Chief Operating Officer at Appcharge. "Orchestration platforms solved technical routing. Merchant of Record is the next step. It absorbs the financial complexity too, from tax, fraud and chargebacks to compliance and managing multiple acquirers across every region, while staying optimised for each merchant. Game publishers have already proved Appcharge's model at more than a billion dollars a year. My focus is on reinforcing Appcharge's payments infrastructure as the strongest in gaming, and ready for any app that follows."

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** Appcharge, an Israeli direct-to-consumer (DTC) payments platform for mobile-game publishers, named Ronen Morecki as its Chief Operating Officer — reportedly the company's first COO. Morecki joins from monday.com, where he was VP of Payments; earlier he co-founded payments-orchestration startup ZooZ (2010) and became CTO of PayU after PayU acquired ZooZ in 2018. At Appcharge he will own payments strategy and operations for the company's Merchant-of-Record (MoR) platform, starting in gaming and expanding into the wider app economy (subscriptions, fitness, education, AI apps). ([itbrief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/appcharge-appoints-ronen-morecki-as-chief-operating-officer), [finextra](https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/111384/appcharge-names-chief-operating-officer), [gamesbeat](https://gamesbeat.com/appcharge-appoints-first-coo-as-gaming-dtc-platform-eyes-broader-app-economy-exclusive/))

**[1] Who Appcharge is.** Appcharge builds infrastructure that lets mobile-game publishers sell directly to players — branded web stores, SDKs, global payment acceptance, and an MoR layer that absorbs tax, fraud, chargebacks, compliance and multi-acquirer routing — so publishers bypass the ~30% Apple/Google app-store commission. It raised a $58M Series B led by IVP in Aug 2025 (with Playrix, Creandum, Play Ventures, Glilot, Smilegate, Moneta, BITKRAFT, Corundum), on top of a $26M Series A (Nov 2024), for ~$89M total raised. See prior note [[Appcharge raises $58 million Series B for game commerce]]. The company reports it has crossed ~$1B in annualized DTC transaction volume, roughly doubling in the six months after the Series B. ([crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/appcharge), [ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/business/article/hya6qpfjzx))

**[2] Why the hire matters.** It is a senior operator hire, not a capital/structural event. The signal: Appcharge is professionalizing its payments org and positioning to expand beyond gaming. Morecki's profile is unusually on-point — he has sat on both the provider side (ZooZ/PayU orchestration) and the merchant side (monday.com billing, pricing/packaging, subscription lifecycle), which maps directly to Appcharge's pitch that MoR is "the next step after orchestration." CEO Maor Sason framed games as "the hardest payments problem in the app economy," using the hire to credential the platform for other app categories. ([mobidictum](https://mobidictum.com/appcharge-monday-com-ronen-morecki-coo/))

**[3] Market / competitive context.** Appcharge sits in the app-store-disintermediation wave accelerated by the Epic v. Apple rulings and EU DMA, which force Apple/Google to permit external payment links/alternative billing for digital goods. Appcharge cites Grand View Research's ~$450B global in-app-purchase market by 2030 and its own research sizing mobile-gaming DTC at ~$17Bn already. Competitors/adjacencies in the news DB include payment orchestration ([[Payment orchestrator Gr4vy partners with Mastercard on integration]]) and subscription-commerce tooling ([[Solidgate launches Billing Dashboard for subscription KPIs]]); broader MoR peers (not in DB) include Xsolla, FastSpring, Paddle and Lemon Squeezy. ([businesswire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260624293221/en/New-Appcharge-Research-Direct-to-Consumer-is-Already-a-$17Bn-Market-in-Mobile-Gaming-and-the-Rest-of-the-App-Economy-is-Set-to-Follow))

**[4] So what / watch-items.** Low-materiality event in isolation (one exec hire at a Series-B private company), but a useful read-through on where DTC payments infrastructure is heading post-DMA/Epic. Watch: (a) whether Appcharge actually ships beyond gaming into subscription/AI apps under Morecki; (b) a possible Series C given $1B annualized volume and 14x YoY growth cited at Series B; (c) Apple/Google's response to external-billing routes, which is the single biggest regulatory dependency for the whole model.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Direct-to-consumer (D2C) monetization is already a ~$17bn segment of mobile gaming, ~15% of the global mobile in-app-purchase (IAP) market (per Appcharge research, via BusinessWire/MobileMarketingReads, 24 Jun 2026). Appcharge cites Grand View Research that the broader consumer-app IAP market reaches ~$450bn by 2030 (note text; secondary citation — treat as vendor-sourced TAM, not independently verified). Structure: the "move sales off app-store rails" layer is young and fragmenting — orchestration + merchant-of-record (MoR) + web-shop builders overlap, with no dominant standard. Barriers are more regulatory/operational (acquiring licenses across geos, tax/VAT remittance as legal seller, fraud/chargeback liability) than capital. Why now: the Apr-2025 Epic v. Apple ruling forced Apple to permit external-checkout links with no off-app commission; the Dec-2025 appeals court let Apple reinstate a "reasonable, non-prohibitive" commission on external purchases (remanded to set the rate) (median.co, macrumors.com, Dec 2025). Net: the D2C door is open but the economics of the Apple cut are now unsettled — the tailwind is real but partly re-narrowed vs the April high-water mark.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: processed TPV/transaction volume (annualized), take rate on D2C sales, payment-method coverage (geos/local methods), fraud/chargeback rate. Players: Xsolla (incumbent games-commerce MoR, 200+ geos, 1,000+ local methods; Vietnam MoR license Mar-2026, Skich partnership May-2026 — BusinessWire); Coda Payments (Singapore, games/digital-content MoR, alternative app stores); Appcharge, Aghanim, FastSpring, Stash, Tebex, Paddle (SaaS-leaning MoR) as D2C/web-shop peers (fastspring.com, thegamescientist.com, 2026). Basis of competition: payment-method breadth, store/UX quality (no-code web shops), and how much financial complexity (tax/fraud/compliance) the MoR absorbs vs plain orchestration. Appcharge's position: fast-scaling niche challenger, gaming-first — annualized D2C transaction volume ~$500m (Jul-2025) → ~$700m (Jan-2026) → ~$1bn (Mar-2026), more than doubling in six months (ynetnews, Appcharge). Moat `(analysis)`: switching costs once a publisher embeds the web-shop + acquirer stack, plus gaming-specific fraud/payment tuning; thin vs Xsolla's geo/licensing depth. The Morecki hire (ex-monday.com VP Payments; ZooZ co-founder → PayU CTO) signals a push to harden payments ops and extend MoR beyond gaming into subscription/fitness/education/AI apps.

**Comps & multiples.** Private peers, round valuations (not market caps):
- Xsolla — up to ~$3bn indicative valuation (Goldman/BofA cited, thebell.io); revenue estimates vary widely ($52.7m–$104.5m across trackers) — unreliable, so EV/Rev = no data / `[UNSOURCED]`.
- Coda Payments — $2.5bn valuation on a $690m raise (venturebeat/80.lv).
- Paddle — $1.4bn (Series D, ~$200m, KKR); ~$90.9m 2024 revenue (getlatka) → implied P/S ≈ `$1.4bn / $0.091bn ≈ 15x` (analysis; mixed-vintage revenue vs valuation, treat as rough).
- Appcharge — $89m total funding ($26m Series A Nov-2024 + $58m Series B Aug-2025, led by IVP); post-money not disclosed → valuation multiple = no data. Internal: [[Appcharge raises $58 million Series B for game commerce]]; peer MoR launch [[Payrails launches Merchant of Record product]].
Distribution not computed (only 1 clean P/S, mixed vintage); qualitative read: MoR/game-commerce names carry rich P/S (~15x Paddle) typical of high-growth private infra — justified only if Appcharge's volume doubling sustains. Flag: in-line-to-rich if Appcharge raises next at a Paddle-like multiple on ~$1bn TPV (not revenue), but take-rate on that $1bn is undisclosed, so revenue — and any true multiple — is unknowable here.

**Risk flags.**
1. Platform-economics risk — the Dec-2025 appeals ruling lets Apple charge a "reasonable" commission on external links; if the remanded rate lands high, the D2C cost advantage (and Appcharge's core pitch) narrows, directly compressing the tailwind the whole model rides on.
2. Concentration / disclosure gap — $1bn is annualized *transaction volume*, not revenue; take rate, net margin and customer concentration are all silent. A few large publishers (e.g. Playrix, an investor) could drive much of that TPV, making the metric fragile.
3. Competitive disintermediation — Xsolla (geo/licensing depth, 200+ markets) and Coda ($2.5bn) are larger and incumbent; orchestration players (Payrails, Primer) are adding MoR, and the category has no moat standard — Appcharge must win on speed/UX before scaled peers close the web-shop gap.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The hire reframes Appcharge from a games-D2C point tool toward a horizontal MoR play (subscriptions/AI/fitness apps) — a land-grab thesis riding the post-Epic/DMA opening. Falsifiable: watch (a) the remanded Apple commission rate (a high rate is the thesis-breaker), and (b) whether Appcharge discloses take-rate/revenue or a priced round above the ~$89m funding base; a down/flat round or expansion stalling outside gaming would invalidate the horizontal story. Trigger to watch: first non-gaming MoR customer logos and any 2027 raise.
