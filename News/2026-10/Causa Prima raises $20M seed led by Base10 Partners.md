# Causa Prima raises $20M seed led by Base10 Partners

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Connecting the Dots in Fintech] 🇺🇸 Causa Prima has raised $20 million in a seed round, led by Base10 Partners. Its agent to agent platform uses AI agents to resolve billing disputes and speed up payment times between companies and their suppliers. The round brings total funding to $30 million and will support a new office in New York.

### fortune.com
<https://fortune.com/2026/10/13/startup-causa-prima-20-million-billing-disputes-ai-agents>
*621 слов · direct*

Artificial intelligence is becoming a fixture in the workplace. Soon, many firms will have agents that don’t just operate within a business—but also interact with agents at other companies. One startup is trying to jump-start that future with software that lets AI agents act on companies’ behalf, working together to sort out payments between businesses.
On Tuesday, Delaware-based Causa Prima announced a $20 million seed round that closed in September to expand its agent-to-agent platform, which resolves billing disputes and speeds up payment times between companies and their suppliers. Cofounded by German entrepreneurs Maex Ament, Henrik Gebbing, and Philip Stanislaus, the company previously raised $10 million in a pre-seed round, bringing its total funding to $30 million.
“The status quo today is that a lot of things are still somewhat manual,” Ament told Fortune . “What we need is a network of agents, and … the area that felt natural to us was … accounts payable of one company and accounts receivable of another.”
Base10 Partners led the seed round, with participation from QED Investors, FJ Labs, and existing investors Creandum, Kfund, and Borusan Ventures. Causa Prima declined to disclose its valuation following the round.
“Every dollar going into [accounts payable and accounts receivable] automation today assumes the bottleneck sits inside one company. Causa Prima is building for the handoff between companies,” Base10 general partner Rexhi Dollaku told Fortune in a written statement. 
Causa Prima has 25 employees across offices in Madrid and Munich and plans to use the funding to open a New York office as it expands into the United States.
Payments between businesses total roughly $98 trillion a year, according to market research firm Fortune Business Insights, while Mastercard estimates the broader market opportunity at $125 trillion. Manual approvals can delay those payments. Nearly half of executives working in accounts payable name slow invoice approvals as their biggest challenge, with processing taking more than eight days on average, according to a 2025 report by research firm Ardent Partners.
Causa Prima monetizes that gap by taking a cut whenever its agents successfully negotiate early payments between buyers and suppliers. 
 Friends to founders 
Ament isn’t new to the payments industry. Born and raised in Germany, he began his career as a software engineer at SAP a German multinational whose software helps businesses manage finances and supply chains. He later worked at fintech companies focused on accounts payable and receivable before founding Taulia in 2009, a company specializing in supply-chain finance and electronic invoicing. In 2017, Ament shifted his focus from traditional fintech to blockchain and founded Centrifuge, a decentralized finance company that tokenizes and trades real-world assets .
Gebbing left the corporate world in 2018 after three years as a senior associate at consulting firm McKinsey & Co. to cofound Finoa, a fintech company that helps bring businesses on-chain.
The two met in Madrid nearly a decade ago while pitching their earlier startups to local venture capitalists, who passed on investing but introduced them after noticing both were German founders married to Spanish women. 
Years of friendship turned into a business partnership last year when Gebbing moved into a board role at Finoa to take some time off. Instead of relaxing, he and Ament began sketching out ideas around the emerging wave of agentic AI. They brought in Stanislaus, who had previously led engineering under Ament at Centrifuge, as chief technology officer. 
While brainstorming, the three realized that internal corporate AI agents were still getting stuck waiting days for humans to answer emails, prompting them to found Causa Prima early this year. They drew the company’s name from the Latin term for “first cause,” a nod to Ament’s philosophy that the root cause of all business is simply getting paid.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- Delaware-incorporated Causa Prima announced (2026-10-13, Fortune) a **$20M seed that actually closed in September 2026**. Lead: **Base10 Partners**. Participants: **QED Investors, FJ Labs**, and existing investors **Creandum, Kfund, Borusan Ventures**.
- **Total funding now $30M** = this $20M seed + a prior **$10M pre-seed** (Fortune's own framing; note external outlets/Crunchbase loosely call the earlier round a "$10M seed led by Creandum" — the primary source says pre-seed, which I follow). The June corpus note describing the Creandum $10M is that earlier round.
- Product: an **agent-to-agent platform** — AI agents sit on the buyer's accounts-payable side and the supplier's accounts-receivable side and negotiate directly to **resolve billing disputes and accelerate payment**. 
- **Monetization (the one hard number on economics):** Causa Prima **takes a cut whenever its agents successfully negotiate an early payment** between buyer and supplier. This is a dynamic-discounting / supply-chain-finance take-rate dressed in agentic framing (see [2] — founder invented dynamic discounting at Taulia).
- Team: 25 employees, offices in Madrid + Munich; proceeds fund a **New York office / US expansion**.
- **Valuation: undisclosed** (explicitly declined). 
- **+ Why framed this way:** the PR leans on two TAM numbers — ~$98T/yr B2B payments (Fortune Business Insights) and Mastercard's $125T "opportunity" — plus an Ardent Partners stat (invoice approval >8 days; ~half of AP execs cite slow approvals as top pain). That's a classic "giant-denominator" seed pitch: the live, de-PR'd substance is a pre-revenue-grade network with an experienced founder, not disclosed GMV/clients. No customer count, no volume, no conversion, no valuation → traction is unproven (announced, not demonstrated).

## [1] Competitors / peers
The AP/AR-automation + agentic-commerce field is crowded, but Causa Prima's specific wedge — the **cross-company handoff** (an agent on each side) — is less contested than single-sided automation:
- **Single-company AP/AR incumbents & agents:** HighRadius, Billtrust, Quadient AR (ex-YayPay), Tabs, Chargebee Receivables, Automation Anywhere — all automate inside ONE company's four walls. Base10's own thesis (Dollaku) explicitly positions Causa Prima against these: "every dollar going into AP/AR automation today assumes the bottleneck sits inside one company."
- **Direct agentic-AR/AP seed peers in corpus:** [[Paraglide raises $5M Seed to reinvent accounts receivable with Agentic AI]] (Jan 2026, $5M seed, agentic AR). Causa Prima's $20M dwarfs it — Causa Prima is a top-~1% German-fintech seed by size (Dealroom benchmark across 155 comparable rounds).
- **Agentic-commerce rails / standards layer (corpus, Jan 2026):** [[Mastercard unveils Agent Suite for agentic AI]], [[Visa works with over 100 partners on agentic shopping solutions]], [[PayPal acquires Cymbio to accelerate agentic commerce]], protocol efforts (Google AP2, x402). These are mostly **consumer/checkout agentic commerce**; Causa Prima is B2B invoice-settlement — adjacent, not head-to-head.
- **Enterprise-treasury agents:** Airwallex (Agentic Business Accounts), Ant/WorldFirst agent accounts, Sunrate.AI, LianLian+Visa LoopXPay — these move money for agents but don't arbitrate disputes between two firms' back-offices.
- **+ Why the landscape is this way / second-order:** the hard part isn't the AI, it's getting BOTH counterparties onto one network (two-sided cold-start). Incumbents (HighRadius/Billtrust) already sit inside thousands of AP/AR departments and could bolt on a "handoff" agent, so Causa Prima's moat is being the neutral inter-company layer before an incumbent extends. Whoever owns the rails (Visa/Mastercard/bank payment networks) can also capture the settlement margin — Causa Prima must stay the negotiation/dispute layer and avoid being commoditized into a feature.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Founders fit is the strongest asset here.** CEO **Maex (Markus) Ament**: ex-SAP engineer; co-founded **Ebydos** (invoice automation, sold to ReadSoft); co-founded **Taulia** in 2009 where he **invented dynamic discounting** in supply-chain finance (SAP acquired Taulia in 2022); then co-founded **Centrifuge** (tokenizing real-world assets / on-chain credit). Co-founder **Henrik Gebbing** (ex-McKinsey; co-founded **Finoa**, crypto custody). CTO **Philip Stanislaus** led engineering under Ament at Centrifuge.
- Causa Prima founded early 2026; name = Latin "first cause," from Ament's view that the root of all business is "getting paid."
- **+ Why they act this way / structural logic:** this is Ament re-platforming his career-long thesis — dynamic discounting (a take-rate on early payment) — onto agentic AI. The monetization in [0] is literally dynamic discounting with AI agents doing the negotiation. So the "novelty" is a known SCF economic model with a new automation layer, led by the person who pioneered the model. That's why a seed is a top-1%-size $20M: investors are paying up for a proven founder + a 25-year network, not for demonstrated product traction.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely newer wedge:** inter-company agent-to-agent negotiation (handoff between two firms) vs the single-company automation that dominates the market — that framing is credibly differentiated (Base10's explicit thesis).
- **But the underlying economics are old:** take-a-cut-on-early-payment = dynamic discounting / SCF, which Ament himself commercialized a decade ago at Taulia. The AI agents are the delta, not the business model.
- **Traction = essentially unproven.** No disclosed revenue, GMV, number of merchants/suppliers on the network, or dispute-resolution volume. 25 staff, pre-US. This is announced capability, not adoption.
- **+ Who captures the margin (why value-add may or may not hold):** value accrues only if Causa Prima becomes the **neutral two-sided network** both buyer and supplier trust to negotiate and settle. Risks: (a) two-sided cold-start — needs density on both AP and AR simultaneously; (b) incumbents (HighRadius/Billtrust) or payment networks (Visa/Mastercard agent suites) extend into the handoff and relegate Causa Prima to a feature; (c) the take-rate is only defensible while Causa Prima controls the negotiation — if settlement rails disintermediate it, the margin migrates to whoever actually moves the money. Durable value is network ownership, not the AI itself (analysis).

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Near-term: open NY office, enter US, scale the agent network; monetize via early-payment take-rate.
- Sentiment is hot — multiple outlets covered it as a flagship agentic-B2B raise; investors cite 90% of B2B buying AI-agent-intermediated by 2028 / >$15T B2B spend via agent exchanges (Forrester-type forecasts) as the tailwind.
- **Regulatory/trust backdrop:** agentic commerce raises consent, authority, and fraud-liability questions already flagged in corpus ([[Mastercard agentic commerce raises new consent and trust questions]]) — in B2B, "which agent had authority to settle/discount, and who eats a bad negotiation" is unresolved and Causa Prima is silent on liability.
- **+ Why / counterintuitive second-order:** the TAM is real but the bottleneck is TRUST and two-sided adoption, not AI capability. A big seed from a top-tier syndicate is a signal, not a moat — and a high entry price plus undisclosed valuation means future rounds must show real inter-company network density, or the step-up stalls. Capital concentration at seed makes the next milestone (live GMV through the network) the make-or-break, not the tech demo.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Subvertical: agentic-commerce applied to B2B payments — specifically the AP/AR "handoff" between two companies (one firm's accounts payable talks to the counterparty's accounts receivable via AI agents). Market size for the underlying rail is large but the figures vary by source and definition: B2B payments ≈ $98tn/yr per Fortune Business Insights and ≈ $125tn "broader opportunity" per Mastercard (both via Fortune, 2026-10-13); a separate estimate puts 2024 global B2B payment transactions at ≈ $35.8tn (per PYMNTS/fxcintel, 2026). These are rail-volume TAMs, not Causa Prima's addressable revenue — the company monetizes only a cut of successfully negotiated early payments, so the serviceable base is a small fraction of these headline numbers (analysis). Structure: the AP/AR software layer is fragmented and incumbent-heavy (ERP-native modules, AP-automation vendors), with value historically captured *inside* one company's four walls. "Why now": two secular drivers — (1) agentic AI maturing enough to act (not just read) on both sides of a transaction; (2) persistent manual bottleneck — nearly half of AP executives name slow invoice approvals as their top pain, approvals averaging >8 days (Ardent Partners 2025, via Fortune). Causa Prima's bet is the *inter-company* handoff, explicitly contrasted by Base10 with AP/AR automation that "assumes the bottleneck sits inside one company."

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for a take-rate network — TPV/payment volume negotiated, take rate on early-payment discounts, number of agents/counterparties onboarded, time-to-payment reduction. None are disclosed by Causa Prima (pre-revenue-disclosure seed) → `[UNSOURCED]`. Players split three ways: (a) incumbent AP/AR & supply-chain-finance platforms (Taulia — ironically founded by the same Maex Ament, acquired by SAP; Basware, which just closed its Trustpair acquisition 2026-10-05 to cover invoice-to-payment integrity); (b) AI-invoice-verification new entrants ([[Iridium Credit raises $3M to verify every invoice]], 2026-10-08; [[Antidote completes $5M seed round for billing compliance automation]], 2026-01); (c) the agentic-commerce rails being built by networks (Mastercard Agent Suite, PayPal/Cymbio, AP2-compatible wallets) which mostly target consumer/retail checkout, not inter-company AP/AR. Basis of competition: network density + trust/liability at the agent handoff, not price. Causa Prima's position: niche-but-credible new entrant — its moat is primarily founder/intangibles (Ament built Taulia's dynamic-discounting playbook; team ex-Centrifuge/Finoa) plus a would-be two-sided network effect that does not yet exist at scale (analysis). 25 employees, offices Madrid/Munich, opening NYC (Fortune, 2026-10-13).

**Comps & multiples.** Valuation NOT disclosed ("Causa Prima declined to disclose its valuation" — Fortune) → EV/Revenue, post-money multiple = **no data**; the only hard figures are round size ($20M seed, closed Sept 2026) and cumulative funding ($30M incl. a $10M pre-seed). No public revenue → all revenue/EV multiples `[UNSOURCED]`. Internal seed-round comps in the AP/AR-automation + agentic-payments cluster (round size as a crude proxy, not valuation):
- [[Iridium Credit raises $3M to verify every invoice]] — $3M, AI invoice verification (2026-10)

Read: at $20M, Causa Prima's seed is 4–7x the typical AP/AR-adjacent seed in-base (Iridium/Antidote/Revena cluster at $3–8M) — **rich vs the sub-vertical**, consistent with a repeat payments founder (Taulia) and a two-sided-network thesis rather than disclosed traction. With no valuation and no revenue, the premium can't be checked against a multiple — distribution not computed, qualitative only. Note a minor source discrepancy worth flagging: the note's text implies the $10M pre-seed predates this round, while secondary coverage (fxcintel) dates the pre-seed to June 2026 — treat the exact pre-seed timing as unconfirmed.

**Risk flags.**
1. **No network yet = chicken-and-egg.** The thesis requires agents on *both* sides; a two-sided AP/AR network has no value until both a buyer and its supplier are onboarded, so early TPV/take-rate will be thin — execution/adoption risk is the dominant one, not technology.
2. **Valuation opacity + rich seed vs comps.** Undisclosed valuation on a $20M seed that is 4–7x in-base peers; if it was priced on founder pedigree rather than traction, a flat/down Series A is a live possibility if the network doesn't compound (analysis).
3. **Disintermediation by the stack above.** The value could be captured by ERPs (SAP/Taulia) or networks (Mastercard Agent Suite, AP2) embedding agent-to-agent settlement natively — Causa Prima sits on someone else's payment rails and could be reduced to a thin negotiation layer.
4. **Agent-handoff liability/fraud (who's silent).** PR is silent on who bears liability when two autonomous agents mis-settle a disputed invoice, and on fraud controls at the handoff — the hardest part of inter-company agentic payments and the one least addressed in the announcement.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Signals that agentic-commerce funding is widening from consumer checkout (PayPal/Mastercard/Stripe) into the far larger but harder B2B AP/AR handoff — a *new-entry* story, not consolidation (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if inter-company agent-to-agent settlement is real, expect (a) a named marquee buyer–supplier pair live on the network and (b) incumbents (SAP/Taulia, Basware) shipping a competing "agent handoff" feature within 12–18 months. Catalyst to watch: Series A terms + first disclosed TPV. Thesis breaks if the next round is flat/absent or if an ERP ships native agent-to-agent AP/AR first, collapsing the standalone-network case.
