PawaPay secures Payment Service Provider licence in Mozambique
PawaPay has secured a Payment Service Provider (PSP) licence from the Bank of Mozambique through its local subsidiary, Quidexplus Mozambique, marking an important step in our operations in the country.
As one of the first companies licensed to operate as a PSP in Mozambique, we can now onboard merchants directly and provide regulated payment services, connecting businesses to the mobile money services their customers use every day.
Mobile money payments in Mozambique
Mozambique has more than 12 million active mobile money accounts across three operators: M-Pesa (Vodacom), E-Mola (Movitel) and mKesh (Tmcel).
“Mozambique has been one of the most active mobile money markets on the continent for years, but merchants have had no reliable way to operate here at scale,” said Ilídio Matchebe, Mozambique Country Director at PawaPay.
“Becoming one of the first PSPs licensed by the Bank of Mozambique changes that. We can now onboard merchants directly and connect them to the operators their customers already use day to day.”
PawaPay across Africa
PawaPay operates across 23 African markets , processing around 7 million transactions a day for customers in sectors including ride-hailing, remittance, retail and NGOs.
Founded in 2020, PawaPay has now successfully processed more than 3.5 billion transactions .

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened (one line).** PawaPay secured a Payment Service Provider (PSP) licence from the Bank of Mozambique via its local subsidiary Quidexplus Mozambique, becoming one of the first firms licensed to onboard merchants directly and provide regulated payments linking businesses to the country's mobile-money rails (announced ~2026-10-08).

**[1] Dated timeline.**
- 2020 — PawaPay founded as a mobile-money payments aggregator for Africa; later raised a ~$9M seed (MSA Capital, 88mph, Vunani, Kepple, Zagadat).
- 2026-06-16/17 — PawaPay crosses 3 billion lifetime transactions, doubling daily volumes (see [[PawaPay hits three billion transactions, doubling daily volumes]]).
- 2026-09-16 — Quidexplus Kenya Ltd (PawaPay group) authorised as a PSP by the Central Bank of Kenya (directory shows initial licensing 2026-06-17; publicised in Sept).
- 2026-10-08 — Bank of Mozambique PSP licence announced via subsidiary Quidexplus Mozambique; described as PawaPay's "second [PSP] licence in a month," expanding its regulated footprint from 23 to 24 African markets.

**[2] Why it matters (significance).** This is a shift from being an API aggregator riding on operator rails to holding its own regulated merchant-acquiring entitlement in-market. Mozambique is a deep mobile-money market — ~12M active accounts (Bank of Mozambique counted ~24.6M accounts end-Nov 2025, ~4x the ~6.7M bank accounts), across M-Pesa (Vodacom), e-Mola (Movitel) and mKesh (Tmcel), which are now interconnected. Being one of the first PSPs lets PawaPay onboard merchants directly rather than via partners, a higher-margin, stickier position. It also signals a deliberate "licence-in-every-market" strategy (Kenya then Mozambique within weeks).

**[3] Key numbers / entities.** 23→24 African markets; ~7M transactions/day; 3.5B+ transactions processed (per the note's source; company elsewhere cites 3B+ as of June 2026); 2023 ~$24M processed → 2024 ~$207M; subsidiary Quidexplus Mozambique / Quidexplus Kenya; Mozambique: ~12M active mobile-money accounts, three operators; regulatory basis Decree №99/2019 + capital via Aviso №3/GBM/2020. Country Director: Ilídio Matchebe.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**
1. Is a "PSP licence" here merchant-acquiring authorisation, or a narrower payment-gateway/aggregator permit? The scope (settlement, holding funds, direct merchant contracts) materially changes how big a deal this is.
2. "One of the first companies licensed as a PSP" — how many PSPs has the Bank of Mozambique actually licensed, and who are the others? First-mover claim needs a denominator.
3. The source says "3.5 billion transactions" but June 2026 coverage said 3 billion — which is current, and is the gap real growth or marketing rounding?
4. Does the licence let PawaPay hold/settle funds locally, or must it still clear through the three operators (M-Pesa, e-Mola, mKesh)? If it still depends on operator rails, the "direct" framing is softer than it sounds.
5. The three Mozambican wallets are already interconnected — does that reduce the value of a PSP acting as an aggregator, since merchants could in principle integrate operators directly?
6. What is the revenue model and take rate in Mozambique, and is merchant demand actually constrained by licensing, or by MDR economics / FX / limited card-less e-commerce?
7. Kenya (Sept) then Mozambique (Oct): is this a coherent strategy or opportunistic PR cadence? Are these licences won organically or via acquiring existing shell entities (note "Quidexplus" subsidiaries)?
8. Competitive exposure: Flutterwave and Onafriq already cover Mozambique at the network level — does a local PSP licence give PawaPay a durable moat or just parity?
9. Political/macro risk: Mozambique has had post-election unrest and currency/liquidity stress — how exposed is near-term merchant growth to that?
10. Regulatory burden: PSP status brings AML/KYC, capital (Aviso №3/GBM/2020), and reporting obligations — is the compliance cost justified by Mozambique's standalone volume?
11. Is "24 markets" licensed presence or merely operational connectivity? The note conflates "operates across 23/24 markets" with "regulated" — which markets does it actually hold licences in?
12. FX/settlement: how does PawaPay repatriate or convert metical balances, and does that erode margins?
13. Source concentration: much of the detail comes from PawaPay's own blog and syndicated tech-press rewrites — any independent confirmation from the Bank of Mozambique itself?
14. Timing vs. stablecoins: PawaPay has a crypto/Head of Crypto hire ([[Pawapay appoints Jessica Gubert as Head of Crypto]]) — does the PSP licence complement or compete with a stablecoin-rails strategy in Africa?
15. What measurable milestone (merchants onboarded, local volume) would prove this licence delivered within 6-12 months?

**Importance: 3/5 — rationale.** Concrete regulatory milestone in a large, underpenetrated mobile-money market for a credible pan-African aggregator, and part of a visible licence-acquisition strategy (Kenya+Mozambique in weeks). But it is a single-country permit (not a funding round, M&A, or category-defining event), scope/exclusivity is unverified, and the market is small relative to PawaPay's overall footprint. Solidly above routine, below strategically pivotal.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** African mobile money is the structural backdrop. Sub-Saharan + North Africa held ~1.2bn registered mobile-money accounts in 2025 (>half the global 2.3bn) and ~347m active 30-day accounts (~60% of the global 593m); the continent processed ~$1.43tn in transaction value in 2025, +27% YoY, ~66% of the $2.09tn global total (per GSMA, via Connecting Africa / CIO Africa, 2025). It took 20 years to pass $1tn and only ~4 more to double it (2021→2025) — the volume base is secular, not cyclical. Structure: highly fragmented at the operator level (each market has 2-4 telco wallets on incompatible rails), which is exactly the gap aggregators monetize — value accrues to whoever abstracts the fragmentation into one API. "Why now": the binding constraint is shifting from consumer adoption (largely done) to merchant acceptance and regulation — Mozambique's >12m active accounts across M-Pesa (Vodacom), E-Mola (Movitel), mKesh (Tmcel) had, per PawaPay, "no reliable way [for merchants] to operate at scale," and a Bank of Mozambique PSP licence is the key that unlocks direct merchant onboarding (analysis).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV, transactions/day, number of operators/wallets connected, number of markets, take rate (none of the economics are disclosed here). Players & basis of competition: the aggregator layer competes on breadth of operator coverage, reliability/success-rate and regulatory licences — not price alone. Onafriq (ex-MFS Africa) runs the widest hub (claims ~500m wallets across ~40 countries) and in Sep-2025 partnered Flutterwave on cross-border; Flutterwave itself is the largest African payments company (>1bn transactions, >$40bn TPV cumulatively, per company) and is primarily a merchant-acquiring/gateway play. PawaPay's position: a focused collections/payouts aggregator — claims 23 markets and ~7m transactions/day, >3.5bn processed cumulatively (company PR, not independently verified). It is niche-but-deep on mobile-money rails rather than a full-stack acquirer (analysis); the Mozambique licence makes it "one of the first" licensed PSPs there — a regulatory first-mover edge in one market, not a continental moat. Moat basis = operator integrations + accumulating licences (switching costs for merchants once wired in); thin on network effects vs Onafriq's larger hub (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round attaches to this news, so no multiple can be computed for PawaPay. Last disclosed PawaPay financing is a **2021 $9m seed** (MSA + 88mph, per Electronic Payments Intl / Techpoint); no Series A/B publicly reported since — current valuation = no data, and the "23 markets / 7m tx/day" scale claims are unverified against any funded milestone. External peer reference points (round valuations, not market caps): Onafriq ~$300-500m (per TechCabal Africa valuation table, Dec-2025); Flutterwave ~$3bn off its Feb-2022 Series D, a now-stale mark the company has not refreshed in 3+ years (per TechCrunch/TechCabal, 2025). Distribution not computed (no comparable disclosed metric across the three private names) — qualitative only. Internal comps from the base (mobile-money / African payments licensing & aggregation): [[Flutterwave selects Polygon as its default blockchain for cross-border payments]], [[Thunes and Ecobank to power instant payments in Africa]], [[Cellulant and Pesalink enable instant bank-to-merchant payments in Kenya]], [[Senegal launches interoperable instant payment platform PI-SPI]], [[Morocco's Chari raises $12 million Series A, wins licence]], [[Central Bank of Kenya revokes PayU Kenya license]].

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory concentration & reversibility.** A PSP licence is country-by-country and revocable — see [[Central Bank of Kenya revokes PayU Kenya license]]; one licence in one market (Mozambique) is a narrow, repeatable-but-slow unlock, and the moat is only as durable as each regulator's forbearance. Second-order: scaling pan-African coverage is a grind of ~23 separate regulatory relationships, each a single point of failure.
2. **Disintermediation / rail dependence.** PawaPay sits on top of telco rails (M-Pesa/Vodacom etc.) that it does not own; the operators can tighten API terms, raise interchange, or build merchant acquiring themselves, compressing the aggregator's take rate. The economics (take rate, per-txn margin) are undisclosed — classic "who's silent about what."
3. **Stale capital / undisclosed valuation.** No funded round since 2021 $9m seed is publicly visible despite claimed 10x-plus scale; if true the company is either self-funding off revenue or raised quietly — either way, external parties cannot size unit economics, and a future raise could reveal a flat/down mark (cf. African fintech valuation reset, 2025).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Incremental, not re-rating: the news signals the sector's center of gravity moving from consumer wallets to *regulated merchant acceptance*, where licences — not apps — are the scarce asset (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if PawaPay converts the Mozambique licence into visible merchant-acceptance share within ~12 months and stacks similar PSP licences in its other claimed markets, it becomes a credible #2 to Onafriq in mobile-money aggregation; trigger to watch = a named funding round (which would finally price the scale claims) or a second country PSP licence. What breaks it = operators building direct merchant acquiring, or a licence being withheld/revoked in a core market.
