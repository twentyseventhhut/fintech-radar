## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
[0] **TL;DR** — On 2026-10-14, alternative-investment firm **Saluda Grade** led (as sole investor) a **$20M Series C** into **Zippy**, a Dallas-based fintech lender/servicer focused purely on **manufactured-housing finance**. Beyond cash, Saluda Grade commits to support Zippy on credit, capital markets, balance-sheet management and product development — a classic credit-firm-backing-its-origination-pipeline deal. Zippy says it has secured **>$600M in capital commitments in 2026** and operates in **27 states**.

[1] **What Zippy does** — Founded 2021 by ex-JPMorgan Chase bankers **Ben Halliday** (CEO) and **Jordan Bucy**. Digital, tech-driven platform for **chattel (personal-property) loans** on manufactured homes — borrowers apply, close (claimed in as little as 5 days) and service entirely online. Distributes via community, retail and wholesale channels. Prior backers include Brand Foundry and **FirstBank** (strategic bank investor). Addresses a historically underserved, high-friction niche where personal-property loans are denied ~65.6% of the time vs ~8.8% for site-built homes, and carry ~9.24% rates vs ~6.63% conventional.

[2] **The round / counterparty** — **Saluda Grade** (founded 2019, ~**$4.4B AUM** as of 2026-01-01) is an asset-based-finance specialist in residential real estate: >40 alternative-mortgage securitizations (~$10B), e.g. a $300M RTL securitization closed 2026-03-05 and HELOC-backed $366.9M deals. Its **growth-equity strategy** is deliberately tied to its credit book — it funds non-bank lending platforms it can also finance/securitize. So the $20M equity is really a gateway to funding/offtaking Zippy's loan production (explains the >$600M commitments narrative). Patrick Lo named Co-CIO (2026-02) to deepen asset-based-finance push.

[3] **Market context** — Broader manufactured-housing market ~**$28.5B (2026) → ~$54.6B by 2035, ~7.5% CAGR**. Chattel loans = ~42–70% of MH purchases depending on source. Incumbent "Big Three" chattel lenders: **21st Mortgage** and **Vanderbilt Mortgage** (both Berkshire Hathaway), plus **Triad Financial** and **Cascade**. GSE (Fannie/Freddie) Duty-to-Serve activity has compressed chattel spreads ~80bps since 2022 and widened the qualifying-buyer pool; FHA Title I participation remains marginal — leaving room for a digital-native challenger. Zippy's thesis: affordable-housing demand + fragmented, analog incumbents = openings for a tech origination + securitization flywheel.

[4] **Internal links & why it matters** — Fits the vault's specialty-lending / asset-based-finance thread: [[Demand surges for private-credit secondaries]] (same private-credit/securitization capital cycle), [[MQube tokenises £1.3 billion mortgage debt]] (mortgage-debt financing innovation, UK analog), [[Rocket closes $14.2 billion acquisition of Mr. Cooper]] (US mortgage consolidation backdrop). Signal: private-credit firms increasingly buy equity stakes in the non-bank originators that feed their securitization pipelines, concentrating niche-lending verticals (here, affordable manufactured housing).

4. Is the >$600M "secured in 2026" committed capital or drawn/deployed? Capital commitments ≠ originations; verify funded volume.
5. Can a digital challenger actually dent Berkshire's 21st Mortgage + Vanderbilt dominance, or is it carving a thin tech-forward slice?
6. Chattel loans carry ~9%+ rates and ~66% denial rates — is Zippy's "5-day close" solving distribution, or just repackaging the same expensive credit?
7. Rate environment risk: if GSE Duty-to-Serve expands chattel further, do GSE-backed rates undercut Zippy's private-credit-funded economics?

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Manufactured-housing (MH) finance — a niche of US specialty/mortgage lending. Sector size: the broader manufactured-housing market is put at ~$28.5bn in 2026 growing to ~$54.6bn by 2035 (~7.5% CAGR) per Market Research Future — but that measures home production, not the loan pool, so treat it as a demand proxy, not a lending TAM ("no data" on a clean MH-loan-origination TAM from a free source). Structure: the subvertical is small, fragmented and historically underserved by banks — ~76% of new MH units are titled as personal property and financed by **chattel loans** (not real-estate mortgages), ~40% of the MH loan market (per CFPB/BIS research, via Market Research Future summary). Chattel is where the dislocation lives: rates run ~200–300bps above conventional mortgages (recent data ~9.24% vs ~6.63%), and personal-property loans are denied at ~65.6% vs ~8.8% for site-built (CFPB HMDA). Drivers / "why now": (1) **affordability squeeze** — as site-built homes stay out of reach, MH is the cheapest path to ownership, pulling demand into exactly the segment banks avoid; (2) **GSE duty-to-serve** — Fannie/Freddie mandates have opened secondary-market financing for chattel and compressed spreads ~80bps since 2022, which is what makes institutional capital (and securitization) viable here. Second-order effect: a structurally high-rate, high-denial, bank-light pocket is precisely the kind of orphaned asset class a securitization-led specialist can underwrite and term out.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: origination volume, cost of funds / warehouse + forward-flow capacity, servicing book (UPB), and credit performance (delinquency/loss) on chattel paper. Zippy's position: a pure-play MH originator-and-servicer (founded 2021 by ex-JPMorgan bankers), live in 27 states, >$600m capital commitments secured in 2026, now adding retail/wholesale/community channels (per HousingWire / fintech.global / citybiz). Basis of competition here is **capital access and credit underwriting**, not consumer brand — whoever can fund and securitize chattel cheapest wins the spread. Saluda Grade is not a generic VC: it is an alternative-investment firm whose model is to partner with non-bank lenders for loan flow and term it out via credit funds and **securitization** (it files Form ABS-15G as "Saluda Grade Mortgage Funding LLC", SEC EDGAR — confirms an active ABS shelf). A Forum Markets SEC filing notes a subsidiary agreed (Mar 23) to buy up to $150m of Zippy chattel loans over five years. The protagonist's position: niche leader-by-focus in a thin field; moat is the **capital-markets relationship + servicing data**, not scale — switching costs are low for borrowers but high for the funding stack once a forward-flow/securitization pipe is wired. `(analysis)`

**Comps & multiples.** This is a $20m equity cheque into a private, pre-scale lender with no disclosed revenue/valuation → **no post-money, no multiple computable** ("no data" / `[UNSOURCED]`). Internal comps (US specialty/mortgage credit, Oct 2026):
- [[Valon raises $150M Series D at $2.3B valuation]] — AI-native **mortgage servicing** platform, $150m Series D, $2.3B valuation. Far larger and later-stage; relevant as the servicing-tech analogue, not a direct comp (Valon is prime/conventional servicing; Zippy is chattel origination).
- [[Octane closes $1.4B forward flow deal with institutional investors]] — the structural twin: a non-bank specialty lender (powersports/outdoor) funding fixed-rate chattel-like installment paper via a **$1.4bn forward-flow** with insurers (NY Life, MetLife, Equitable, Pacific Life, Victory Park). Shows the exact capital-markets playbook Zippy/Saluda are running, one order of magnitude up.
- [[Fundbox secures $175M warehouse facility from MA Financial]] — comparable debt-side plumbing (warehouse financing for a non-bank lender).

Distribution not computed — only one equity round here and no comparable valuations; qualitative comparison only. The telling number is the funding mismatch: $20m of equity against >$600m of 2026 capital commitments — i.e. the business is **balance-sheet/securitization-led, equity-light**, which is normal for this model but concentrates the real risk in the funding stack, not the cap table.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Credit / vintage risk on chattel.** Zippy underwrites the highest-rate, highest-denial consumer segment; if affordability-driven demand is also credit-stressed demand, loss curves on 2026 chattel vintages could surprise — and the $600m of committed capital means losses are levered, not sandboxed.
2. **Single-source capital concentration / rate dependence.** Saluda Grade is the sole Series C investor AND the securitization counterparty (ABS-15G shelf + a $150m forward-flow). That tightly couples Zippy's funding to one firm's appetite and to a securitization market that only works while GSE duty-to-serve spreads and ABS demand hold — a widening of chattel spreads or a cooling ABS bid would hit origination economics directly.
3. **Regulatory / policy dependence.** The entire "why now" rests on Fannie/Freddie duty-to-serve keeping chattel financing viable; any rollback of GSE MH mandates removes the secondary-market tailwind that makes the spread bankable.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is less a VC bet than the **equity layer of a securitization machine**: Saluda is buying origination flow it can term out, and the $20m is strategic, not growth capital. Watch for the first **Zippy-collateralized MH/chattel ABS deal** out of Saluda Grade Mortgage Funding — that, not the next equity round, is the real catalyst and the falsifiable test: if a Zippy-backed securitization prices at competitive spreads in the next 6–12 months, the thesis (bank-light chattel is a fundable asset class) is confirmed; if forward-flow stays private and no ABS prints, the model is stuck at warehouse scale.
