## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** Russian mortgage issuance hit a 2026 high in September, but the record was driven by front-loading ahead of the family-mortgage (семейная ипотека) tightening that took effect 1 October 2026 — not by improving affordability. Borrowers rushed to close deals under the old rules. Per the United Credit Bureau (ОКБ / OKB), September issuance rose 59% by volume and 45% by count year-over-year; banks extended 133,600 loans worth 641.5bn rubles — the most since the start of the year (month-on-month ~+61% volume / +41% count). About 69% of the ruble growth came from new-build (новостройка) lending, and per ДОМ.РФ the preferential-program share reached 72% of market volume. The average new-build loan term jumped nearly six years in a month to 26 years 5 months — a sign of stretched affordability, not improved access ([vedomosti.ru](https://www.vedomosti.ru/finance/news/2026/10/26/1240064-ipotechnih-kreditov), [kommersant.ru](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/9007682)).

**[1] What changed 1 October (the trigger).** The family-mortgage program stopped being universal: the flat 6% rate for all was abolished and replaced by an addressed (адресная) model where rate and limit depend on the number of children and region. In Moscow / St. Petersburg and their oblasts: ~12% for one child, 10% for two, 8% for three, 6% for four, 4% for five+. In other regions: ~10% / 8% / 6% by one / two / three children, with limits of 6/8/10m rubles. Max subsidized term cut to 15 years; longer tenors tie to the key rate. This is the long-trailed reform first reported in mid-2026 — see [[Finтехно Russia weighs major reform of family mortgage program]] (Jun 24, which already laid out the exact per-child Moscow rate grid) and [[Russia keeps family mortgage terms unchanged until October 2026]] (Jun 29, Minfin pinning the start to 1 Oct) ([meduza.io](https://meduza.io/en/news/2026/09/24/russia-ties-family-mortgage-rates-to-the-number-of-children-in-moscow-the-rate-for-one-child-families-doubles-to-12-on-october-1), [rbc.ru](https://amp.rbc.ru/rbcnews/finances/01/10/2026/6abccb779a794713bc538f0e)).

**[2] Scale and concentration.** Sber alone issued a record 445.5bn rubles of mortgages in September (+65.8% m/m, +62.2% y/y), i.e. roughly two-thirds of the market's 641.5bn — underscoring how concentrated the surge was in the dominant lender ([kommersant.ru](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/9007978)). The 72% preferential share is a February high; ДОМ.РФ expects the share to fall by roughly a quarter — to ~58% from ~79% in 2025 — once the new, stricter terms bite ([realty.ria.ru](https://realty.ria.ru/20261001/rossija-2121406236.html)). Домклик put the September preferential share even higher at 74%.

**[3] The CBR concern — "preferential crowding-out."** The note frames this near the CBR's feared scenario: even if subsidized programs become targeted, if they still cover ~90% of mortgages, market-rate lending becomes effectively unavailable. The Bank of Russia has consistently argued mass subsidized mortgages distort the market, inflate new-build prices, and sideline market lending — the same logic behind the move to an addressed model and behind parallel moves to rein in developer-financed installments ([[Russia reworks bill to regulate developer-financed home installments]], Oct 22; 1.5tn rubles of partially-unpaid DDU contracts in Q2 2026) and Q3 debt-load / installment rule changes ([[Финтехно Q3 brings debt-load and installment-market rule changes]]).

**[4] Why it matters / what to watch.** A pull-forward of demand means Q4 2026 issuance will likely slump as September cannibalized future months — classic front-loading around a subsidy cliff. Watch: (a) the October–December volume drop and how much was truly net-new vs. borrowed from later; (b) whether the preferential share actually falls to ДОМ.РФ's ~58%; (c) new-build price softening as subsidized demand cools; (d) rising loan terms (26y5m) as a distress-affordability signal rather than genuine access. The lengthening tenor plus subsidy withdrawal raises debt-service and refinancing-cliff risk for marginal borrowers once rates reset to key-linked levels.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Q1. Are the headline figures corroborated?** Yes. ОКБ's 133,600 loans / 641.5bn rubles / +59% volume / +45% count y-o-y, and the "max since start of year" framing, are confirmed by Vedomosti (26 Oct 2026) and Kommersant. Verified.

**Q2. Is the "record" a true all-time record or only a 2026 high?** Only a 2026 high ("максимум с начала года"), not an all-time record. 2025 was a down year (-10% issuance). The note is correctly worded; do not overstate. Answered.

**Q3. Was September really front-loading rather than genuine demand?** Strongly supported: the 1 Oct family-mortgage tightening was pre-announced and the surge concentrated in subsidized/new-build lending with a Sep 30 deadline. Sources explicitly attribute the jump to closing deals "по старым правилам." Verified.

**Q4. Does the 72% preferential share hold up?** Yes per ДОМ.РФ / Kommersant (72%, a February high); Домклик reports 74%. Range 72–74% — the note's 72% is the conservative/ДОМ.РФ figure. Verified.

**Q5. Is the "69% of ruble growth from new-builds" verifiable?** Directional/plausible and consistent with subsidized programs (which overwhelmingly fund new-builds) driving the surge; exact 69% appears sourced to the ОКБ/Финтехно read. Not independently re-verified but internally consistent. Partially open.

**Q6. Is the 26y5m average term and "+6 years in a month" real or a composition artifact?** Likely largely a composition effect: a flood of long-tenor subsidized new-build loans (now capped at 15y going forward, but closed under old rules) shifts the average. The note already reads it as stretched affordability, not improved access — correct framing. Partially open (composition vs. genuine term lengthening).

**Q7. The "CBR 90% scenario" — is this an actual CBR statement or a journalistic framing?** The specific "90% of mortgages → market lending unavailable" line was NOT found verbatim in CBR materials via search. It reflects the CBR's well-documented worry about preferential crowding-out but should be treated as the channel's illustrative scenario, not a quoted CBR forecast. Open — flag as framing.

**Q8. Could the surge reflect rate-cut expectations rather than the subsidy cliff?** Unlikely to be the primary driver — the concentration in subsidized/new-build and the explicit deadline timing point to the cliff. Market-rate mortgages stayed expensive (key rate ~ mid-teens). Answered.

**Q9. How concentrated is the record?** Highly: Sber's 445.5bn is ~69% of the 641.5bn total. The "market record" is substantially a Sber record, which matters for interpreting breadth. Verified.

**Q10. What's the downside/Q4 risk the note implies?** Demand pull-forward → expected Q4 slump; ДОМ.РФ projects preferential share falling to ~58% (from ~79% in 2025). Verified directionally.

**Q11. Does this duplicate a prior note?** No. Prior notes cover the reform proposal (Jun 24) and the Oct-1 start date confirmation (Jun 29); this note is the first to report the actual September outcome data. Fresh.

**Q12. Any entity-name traps?** ОКБ = Объединённое кредитное бюро (United Credit Bureau), a credit bureau — not to be confused with other "OKB" acronyms. ДОМ.РФ and Домклик (Sber's platform) are distinct sources with slightly different shares. Resolved.

**Q13. Is affordability actually worsening?** Yes — longer tenors + subsidy withdrawal + high key rate all point to deteriorating affordability; the record volume masks this. Answered.

**Q14. Second-order effect to watch?** New-build price dynamics once subsidized demand cools, and debt-service/refinance-cliff risk when key-rate-linked resets hit longer-tenor loans. Open — monitor Q4–Q1.

**Q15. Reliability of the single source?** Note is single-channel (Финтехно) but all core figures independently confirmed by Vedomosti, Kommersant, ДОМ.РФ/RIA. High confidence on numbers; the "90% CBR scenario" is the one soft spot.

**Importance: 4/5** — A clean, well-corroborated read on a macro-significant Russian housing-finance inflection (end of universal 6% family mortgage, pivot to an addressed model, record front-loading into a subsidy cliff). Directly material to mortgage lenders, developers, and CBR policy narrative, and the figures check out across multiple outlets. Not a 5 because it is a single-market research/commentary item with no new deal/entity event and one unverifiable framing claim.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russian residential mortgage / subsidized housing-finance. September 2026 marked a record-since-year-start month: RUB 641.5bn across 133.6k loans (+59% volume, +45% count YoY, per OKB via Финтехно); DOM.RF put preliminary September issuance near RUB 614bn, +54% vs August and the strongest month YTD. The structure is heavily state-dependent: per DOM.RF, subsidized programs reached ~72% of market volume in September, and new-builds drove ~69% of the ruble growth — the market runs on budget subsidy, not market-rate credit, which is effectively frozen out at a ~14–16%+ key-rate environment. "Why now": a demand pull-forward ahead of the 1-Oct-2026 Family Mortgage reform, which replaced the flat 6% rate with a child-count/region-differentiated scale (2–10% outside the capitals, 4–12% in Moscow/SPb; one-child Moscow rate doubles to 12%), capped the subsidy period at 15 years, and tightened loan-size limits (per Meduza / iz.ru, as of Sep–Oct 2026). Buyers rushed to close under old rules — so September's "record" is a borrowed-from-the-future spike, not improving affordability.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: issuance volume/count, subsidized-vs-market mix, preferential-share-of-volume, average loan tenor. The note flags average new-build tenor jumping ~6 years in a month to 26y5m — a de-PR read: longer tenors mask deteriorating affordability (lower monthly payment, same unaffordable price), not a product improvement. Basis of competition among lenders is largely distribution + subsidy-channel access, not price (the state sets the subsidized rate). Recent moves: VTB said market mortgages will overtake preferential ones by end-2026 (per iz.ru, 15-Oct-2026); forecasts put market vs preferential at ~52/48 by transaction count but state support still leading ~56/44 by volume (iz.ru). Bank DOM.RF extended Family Mortgage to secondary housing (akm.ru). Position: this is a macro/sector story, no single protagonist — the "incumbent" is the state subsidy program itself; private lenders are rail-dependent intermediaries.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/deal in this macro story — trading multiples N/A. Internal comps (policy trajectory, same program, Финтехно source):
- [[Finтехно Russia weighs major reform of family mortgage program]] (2026-06-24) — pre-announced the exact reform: 15y compensation cap, "key+2 / key+2.5 p.p." bank compensation, child/geography differentiation.
- [[Russia keeps family mortgage terms unchanged until October 2026]] (2026-06-29) — Minfin delayed the change from 1-Jul to 1-Oct, the window that created the September pull-forward.
- [[Bank of Russia signals no rate cut just to ease business]] (2026-07-01) — CBR's logic: broad subsidized programs force tighter policy; high key rate is a symptom of overheated subsidized demand. Directly frames the 90%-subsidized scenario the note cites.
Budget cost comp: preferential programs cost ~RUB 2tn in 2025, with ~RUB 1.8tn budgeted for 2026–2028 and actuals already running ahead of plan (per iz.ru/Minfin, 2026); Minfin's stated goal is to cut the state share to 25–30% by 2030.

**Risk flags.**
1. Demand pull-forward → an air pocket in Q4 2026+. September issuance borrowed from later months; the note itself flags this. Watch for a sharp sequential drop in October–December volumes and new-build sales (DOM.RF already guides new-build sales lower in 2026).
2. Subsidy-share concentration (~72% of volume). The CBR-feared scenario — even "targeted" programs covering ~90% of mortgages leaves market-rate credit practically dead. Second-order: lenders become subsidy conduits, book duration/basis risk concentrated on the budget, and a budget squeeze (actuals already over plan) could force abrupt tightening.
3. Rate/budget fragility. The 15-year subsidy cap means post-year-15 rates reset to a key-rate-linked level; at today's ~14%+ key rate, long-tenor (26y+) borrowers face a large embedded rate step-up — a latent affordability/credit-quality risk if the key rate stays high.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is the inflection from "cheap-money era" to targeted, budget-capped subsidy — the 1-Oct reform structurally shrinks the subsidized channel that is 72% of the market (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: Q4 2026 issuance drops materially (double-digit % MoM) as the pull-forward unwinds and higher differentiated rates bite. Trigger/catalyst: October–November OKB/DOM.RF issuance prints and new-build sales. What would break the thesis: a key-rate cut or a further subsidy top-up reflating demand. Watch the subsidized-vs-market mix crossover VTB forecasts for end-2026.
