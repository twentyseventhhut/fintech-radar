Financial Information Technologies (Fintech) Acquires Ground Signal, Expanding Industry-Leading Market Intelligence Data and AI Capabilities Across the Beverage Alcohol Ecosystem
 October 13, 2026 
 TAMPA, Fla. – Financial Information Technologies, LLC (Fintech), the leading provider of business solutions for the beverage alcohol industry, today announced the acquisition of Ground Signal, a leading provider of on-premise market intelligence and analytics.
The acquisition represents a significant step in Fintech’s strategy to build the beverage alcohol industry’s most comprehensive technology, data, payments, and insights platform. Together, the companies will provide suppliers, distributors, and retailers with greater visibility into consumer behavior, market trends, product performance, and growth opportunities across the three-tier system.
Ground Signal has built a unique market intelligence platform powered by proprietary consumer, menu, sales, and location datasets developed over more than a decade. Its technology helps beverage alcohol companies understand where consumers are spending their time, how their brands are performing on menu by market and channel, what products are gaining momentum, and how shifting consumer and market dynamics create opportunities for growth. Ground Signal works with many of the beverage alcohol industry’s leading suppliers and distributors.
By combining Ground Signal’s capabilities with Fintech’s transaction and product data insights across its extensive network of suppliers, distributors, and retailers, the company plans to deliver a new generation of analytics and decision-support solutions designed specifically for the beverage alcohol industry.
“Fintech has spent more than 35 years connecting the beverage alcohol industry through technology and trusted business solutions,” said Tad Phelps, Chief Executive Officer of Fintech. “Ground Signal accelerates our ability to deliver something our customers increasingly need: actionable intelligence. Combining Fintech’s scale and industry reach with Ground Signal’s unique market visibility creates a powerful foundation for the future. Together, we can help suppliers, distributors, and retailers better understand market trends and make more informed business decisions.”
The acquisition also strengthens Fintech’s long-term artificial intelligence strategy. As the industry continues to generate increasing volumes of data, the greatest opportunity lies in transforming information into insights that improve commercial performance, identify emerging trends, and help customers act faster and more effectively. Ground Signal’s combination of proprietary data assets and industry-specific AI models will play an important role in helping Fintech deliver on that vision.
“Ground Signal was built on the belief that the best decisions come from connecting data that traditionally exists in silos,” said Melki Ko, Founder of Ground Signal. “Joining Fintech dramatically expands what is possible. Together, we can connect insights across suppliers, distributors, and retailers in ways that help the industry better understand consumers, anticipate market shifts, and uncover new growth opportunities. We’re excited to help build the next generation of data products for the beverage alcohol market.”
The combination creates a powerful opportunity to leverage data across all three tiers of the industry. Fintech’s position at the center of the beverage alcohol ecosystem, combined with Ground Signal’s market insight capabilities, will accelerate the development of new products and services that help customers improve execution, strengthen planning, and make more informed decisions.
The Ground Signal team, including Melki Ko, Dan Adams, and the company’s leadership group, will join Fintech and help lead the expansion of the combined organization’s Data & Insights offerings.

## [0] What happened

On **2026-10-13** (Tampa, FL), **Financial Information Technologies, LLC** — which brands itself "Fintech" and is **not a generic fintech company but a 35-year-old B2B business-solutions provider for the beverage-alcohol industry** — announced the acquisition of **Ground Signal**, an on-premise market-intelligence and analytics firm. **Terms were undisclosed** (no price, no structure, no multiple disclosed by either party or the trade press). Ground Signal's team — founder/CEO **Melki Ko**, **Dan Adams**, and the leadership group — joins Fintech to help lead a combined **Data & Insights** unit. The stated rationale: pair Ground Signal's proprietary consumer/menu/sales/location datasets with Fintech's transaction and product data to build "the beverage-alcohol industry's most comprehensive technology, data, payments and insights platform" across the US three-tier system (suppliers → distributors → retailers), and to feed Fintech's "long-term AI strategy."

Source: [fintech.com press release](https://fintech.com/news/fintech-aquires-ground-signal-expanding-data-ai-capabilities-across-bev-alc-industry) · [Dealroom note](https://dealroom.co/news/162149-fintech-buys-ground-signal-to-add-ai-analytics-to-its-beverage-alcohol-p/) · [Yahoo Finance UK (BusinessWire syndication)](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/financial-information-technologies-fintech-acquires-130000439.html)

## [1] Who are the players

- **Fintech (Financial Information Technologies, LLC)** — Tampa-based; founded ~1991 (35+ years). Flagship product **PaymentSource®** automates COD/term alcohol-invoice payments and compliance across the US three-tier system. Claims 1.3M+ B2B relationships, ~320,000 businesses served (up from ~285,000 at the Aug-2023 GA investment). CEO **Tad Phelps**. PE-backed by **TA Associates** (long-time owner) and **General Atlantic** ([strategic investment Aug 1, 2023](https://www.generalatlantic.com/media-article/financial-information-technologies-announces-strategic-investment-from-general-atlantic/)) — capital explicitly earmarked for "product innovation and M&A."
- **Ground Signal** — Boston, MA (36 Bromfield St). Founded ~2011–2015 as a **location-based audience/geosocial intelligence** platform; CEO/founder **Melki Ko** (ex-Morgan Stanley), co-founder **Dan Adams**. Per PitchBook, last financing was a **Series A3 of ~$5.34M on 2021-09-15**, backed by **Morningside**. Over the last decade it repositioned into on-premise beverage-alcohol market intelligence (consumer, menu, sales, location datasets; "industry-specific AI models"). See [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/ground-signal) · [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/58638-79).

## [2] Why it matters / strategic context

This is a **tuck-in data/AI acquisition** by a PE-backed vertical-software roll-up, not a transformative deal. Fintech sits at the payments/compliance chokepoint of US alcohol distribution; its moat is regulatory (three-tier) and network (1.3M connections). It is systematically converting that transaction network into a **data-and-insights** business — the classic "payments → data → AI" vertical-SaaS playbook. Ground Signal supplies the demand-side layer (what consumers order on-premise, menu placement, brand momentum) that Fintech's supply-side transaction data lacks; combined, it promises cross-three-tier analytics no single competitor holds. For suppliers/distributors this could reduce reliance on standalone bev-alc intelligence vendors.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits at the intersection of beverage-alcohol industry software/payments and vertical market-intelligence/alternative data — not mainstream fintech. Market backdrop: the US alcoholic-drinks market is large and slow-growing — roughly $329bn in 2026 projected to ~$441bn by 2032, ~5% CAGR (per Straits Research / secondary market-research citations, as of 2026). Structure: the US operates a legally mandated, state-fragmented **three-tier system** (suppliers → distributors → retailers), which is the core structural driver here — it forces data to sit in silos across tiers and across 50 differently-regulated state markets, creating a defensible niche for anyone who can stitch cross-tier data together. Entry barriers are less about capital than about network position (being plugged into invoice/payment flows across all three tiers) and decade-scale proprietary datasets. "Why now": (1) the industry is generating rising transaction/POS volumes while on-premise consumption patterns shift post-pandemic, raising the value of turning raw data into decision support; (2) AI makes vertical, proprietary-data models commercially useful — the stated rationale for folding Ground Signal's "industry-specific AI models" into FIT's platform. Headwind: flat-to-soft US alcohol volumes (moderation/GLP-1 narratives) cap organic growth, pushing incumbents toward data/software monetization and M&A rather than volume.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for this vertical-data/payments model: network reach (FIT cites 1.3M B2B relationships, 320,000 businesses), cross-tier data coverage, and recurring software/data revenue (ARR) — none of the last disclosed. Basis of competition is distribution/network position + proprietary data, not price. Key players: **Provi + SevenFifty** (Provi acquired SevenFifty in 2022; integrated marketplace spanning 1,200+ US distributor portfolios) are the most direct adjacent incumbent on the workflow/marketplace side; distribution giants like Republic National Distributing and Southern Glazer's sit upstream as the data-generating distributors (potential customers and potential disintermediators). FIT's position: **ahead/entrenched** on the payments-and-invoice rail (PaymentSource, ~35 years, backed by TA Associates since 2018 and General Atlantic since Aug 2023), now bolting on the intelligence layer via Ground Signal. Moat = network effects + switching costs around embedded payment/invoice rails across all 50 states (analysis). Recent FIT moves confirm a data/scale roll-up pattern: Nexxus Group asset acquisition (closed May 2024, B2B payments), iControl (retail/bev-alc), and 2024 expansion of PaymentSource beyond alcohol — Ground Signal continues that playbook. Ground Signal (founded 2015, NY; founders Melki Ko, Dan Adams) is a niche sub-scale specialist — ~$8M total funding raised (Dealroom/Tracxn), decade of on-premise/menu/location data.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal terms were **undisclosed** — no price, no revenue for Ground Signal → EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E all **"no data"**; no multiple can be computed, and FIT is private (last disclosed total raised ~$40.9M per PitchBook; post-money valuation not public → `[UNSOURCED]`). Qualitative anchor only: Ground Signal raised ~$8M over its life, so any outcome is a small tuck-in, not a franchise deal. Internal comps (vertical data/intelligence M&A in the base):
- [[S&P Global to acquire With Intelligence for $1.8 billion]] — private-markets intelligence; $1.8bn price is disclosed there, but it is a far larger, data-subscription franchise, not scale-comparable to an ~$8M-funded niche target.
- [[BlueMatrix to acquire AI research platform Aiera]] — AI research/analytics bolt-on, closer in spirit (small AI-data tuck-in into an incumbent workflow platform), also undisclosed terms.
Distribution not computed (fewer than 3 comparable priced figures, and the one priced comp is not scale-comparable) → qualitative comparison only. No outlier flag possible without a price.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Undisclosed, sub-scale target = limited standalone value; execution/integration risk.** Ground Signal raised only ~$8M; value depends entirely on FIT successfully fusing its siloed datasets with FIT's transaction data — a data-engineering and go-to-market integration bet, not a proven combined product. Second-order: if integration stalls, the "new generation of analytics" remains PR, not revenue.
2. **Data-source dependence / disintermediation.** FIT's and Ground Signal's intelligence is only as good as access to distributor/retailer/menu data; the large distributors (RNDC, Southern Glazer's) and marketplaces (Provi/SevenFifty) own their own flows and could build or withhold, capturing the data-margin themselves. Second-order: the moat is positional, not contractual, and could erode if a tier verticalizes.
3. **Soft end-market.** Flat US alcohol volumes cap the total pool these data products monetize; a structural demand headwind means FIT is monetizing a shrinking-share-of-wallet category, raising the bar for data ARR to offset volume softness.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is **consolidation of a niche vertical-data stack**, not a re-rating event — a PE-backed incumbent (TA/General Atlantic) assembling the "payments + data + insights" bundle for US bev-alc via serial tuck-ins (Nexxus, iControl, now Ground Signal) (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: FIT is positioning for a larger exit/recap where the data/AI layer justifies a higher software-style multiple than a pure payments processor. Trigger to watch: a disclosed-value deal, a named data-ARR metric, or a rival (Provi/SevenFifty) making a countering data-intelligence acquisition. What breaks the thesis: a major distributor pulling its data or launching a competing in-house analytics product, which would expose the positional moat. (analysis)
