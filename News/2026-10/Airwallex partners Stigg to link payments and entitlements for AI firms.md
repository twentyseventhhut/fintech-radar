Partnering with Stigg to connect global payments and entitlement management for AI companies
 By Dan Kim, VP of Product Partnerships, and Curtis Shoung, Associate Director of GTM Partnerships – Airwallex 
AI companies are global from the start. A customer can sign up from Singapore, top up from Germany, and expand usage in Brazil. The company needs to know, at any moment, what a customer used, what they owe for it, whether the payment cleared, and what they’re still allowed to access.
Addressing gaps in that data would be manageable if prices rarely changed and purchases were infrequent, but that’s not how AI companies operate. Customer usage is continuous, pricing is fluid, and their products need to know in milliseconds whether to support or refuse the next request.
That’s why we’re partnering with Stigg . Stigg tracks what each AI customer is allowed to use, and keeps that decision updated as soon as a customer buys more, consumes more, or misses a payment. Together with Airwallex’s global financial infrastructure, AI companies get rapid payment acceptance and outcomes synced with Stigg in real time.
 Connecting the payment and entitlement layers 
AI companies often monetise through a mix of subscriptions, usage, credits, add-ons, and plan-based access. The business model can change quickly as products evolve, new capabilities launch, and customers scale their usage.
Airwallex provides a unified payments platform for global businesses, with support for 160+ local payment methods, like-for-like settlement, and AI-powered payment optimization to increase conversions and reduce fraud. Stigg’s integration with AIrwallex connects payment and entitlement workflows, so an AI company can:
 Accept payments through Airwallex across global markets. 
 Sync payment outcomes back to Stigg. 
 Update customer access in Stigg based on successful payments, failed payments, upgrades, top-ups, or plan changes. 
 Reduce custom work to keep payment status and product access aligned. 
AI companies record payment outcomes, and entitlement decisions reflect those outcomes, in the systems their finance and product teams want to use.
 Built for global AI businesses 
AI companies rarely scale to new markets in a linear way. Their product reaches customers anywhere an API key, app, or browser session can find them. That means their payments infrastructure needs to support international buyers, currencies, and payment expectations from the beginning.
With Airwallex, companies can accept payments globally in 130+ currencies across 180+ countries, hold funds in multiple currencies, convert money, pay suppliers and contractors, issue cards, and manage spend through one platform.
Instead of adding separate payment integrations as they expand, Stigg customers can use Airwallex as the payments layer and Stigg as the entitlement layer, with payment outcomes flowing between them. Stigg’s team recorded the whole flow so you can see how it works end to end.
 Getting started 
If you already use Stigg, connect Airwallex Payments in Stigg’s integration settings. The integration is in public beta for Stigg customers, and Stigg covers the setup in its docs .

# Context-enrichment: Airwallex partners Stigg to link payments and entitlements for AI firms
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

**FRESHNESS: fresh.** No prior note covers the Airwallex–Stigg tie-up (Stigg appears only in this note; `grep -rl "company/stigg" News/` = this file only). It is NOT the same as Airwallex's own [[Airwallex launches modular Airwallex Billing platform for global businesses]] (May 2026, Airwallex-owned invoicing/subscription/usage product) — this is an integration where a THIRD-party entitlement vendor (Stigg) plugs Airwallex in as its payments rail. Genuinely new, but low-weight (integration announcement, public beta, no adoption numbers).

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- Airwallex and Stigg announced a product integration: AI companies can accept payments via Airwallex and have payment outcomes (success, failure, upgrade, top-up, plan change) **synced back to Stigg in real time**, which then updates the customer's entitlements (what they're allowed to access). Stigg is the **entitlement/access layer**, Airwallex is the **payments layer**.
- Status = **public beta for Stigg customers** (per Airwallex blog + Stigg's own release notes). "If you already use Stigg, connect Airwallex Payments in Stigg's integration settings." → This is an opt-in connector, NOT a live, GA, co-sold product with named customers. No merchant count, GMV, or conversion figures disclosed.
- The real "what was done": a webhook/connector so Stigg customers don't hand-build the payment-status → access-control plumbing. Marketing frame ("checkout to collection, zero custom code") overstates a fairly standard payments-webhook integration.
- Why structured this way: Stigg deliberately stays **billing-provider-agnostic** — it governs credits/quotas/access *before* usage is allowed and sits on top of whatever settlement layer the customer uses (Stripe, Zuora, Chargebee, NetSuite, Checkout.com, now Airwallex). Airwallex is just one of several payment rails Stigg connects. So this is Airwallex **joining an existing roster**, not an exclusive — reveals Airwallex is the one needing distribution into Stigg's AI-native customer base more than the reverse.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Stigg's layer (entitlements/usage runtime):** sits upstream of billing. Competes less with pure billing and more by *coexisting* with it. Key context: **Stripe acquired Metronome (Jan 2026)** and bundles a Meters API; Orb and Metronome handle billing *after* usage is recorded; Stigg enforces credits/quotas/access *in the request path, before* usage. Stigg raised a **$17.5M Series A** (Red Dot Capital, total ~$24M), founded 2021, Tel Aviv; customers include Miro, Webflow, AI21 Labs, PagerDuty. Stigg recently **acquired Received.ai** (contract management + invoicing into its runtime).
- **Airwallex's layer (global payments):** direct comps are Stripe, Checkout.com, Adyen, PayPal. Crucially Stripe is BOTH a payments rail AND (via Metronome/Stripe Billing/Entitlements) an entitlement+billing stack — i.e. Stripe can offer the whole flow natively, which is exactly what this Airwallex+Stigg pairing stitches together across two vendors.
- Position: Airwallex is **catching up on the "AI monetization" narrative** via partnership rather than owning the entitlement layer. It already has its own Airwallex Billing (May 2026), so partnering with Stigg is partly an admission that third-party entitlement runtimes (Stigg) win the request-path access-control job that Airwallex Billing doesn't do.
- Why the landscape is this way (second-order): the AI-SaaS stack is fragmenting into (metering) → (entitlement/credit governance) → (billing/invoicing) → (payments/settlement). Stripe is trying to own ALL of it; Stigg's bet is that the governance layer is best neutral and multi-rail. Airwallex's bet is to be a preferred rail *inside* that neutral layer rather than fight Stripe head-on for the whole stack.

## [2] Company history / fit
- Airwallex trajectory: [[Airwallex raises new funding at $12 billion valuation]] (May 2026), [[Connecting the Dots how Airwallex reached $1B ARR profitably]], $320M raise framed around "autonomous finance" / AI-native infrastructure, acquired OpenPay to build [[Airwallex launches modular Airwallex Billing platform for global businesses]]. Clear pattern: Airwallex is pushing up the stack from FX/cross-border payments into billing, treasury ([[Airwallex launches Yield treasury product in the US]]), and now AI-company GTM.
- Fit: logical. Airwallex's genuine edge is **global acceptance** — 160+ local payment methods, 130+ currencies, 180+ countries, like-for-like settlement. AI companies are global-from-day-0 (the blog's whole thesis). So Airwallex offering the *payments* leg to Stigg's AI-native customers plays to its real strength (cross-border acceptance) rather than its weaker new line (owning entitlement logic).
- Why Airwallex acts this way: cross-border payments is a margin-compressing, commoditizing take-rate business → Airwallex needs software/multiple and stickier AI-era revenue → partnering into the AI monetization stack is cheap distribution to high-growth AI startups without building the entitlement runtime itself.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- Novelty: **low-to-moderate.** The mechanism (payment webhook → entitlement update) is not new; what's modestly new is a packaged, bidirectional Airwallex⇄Stigg connector aimed specifically at global AI firms. Nothing here is technically differentiated vs a Stripe-native entitlements flow.
- Traction: **none disclosed.** Public beta, no named joint customers, no volume. "Announced," not proven adoption. (analysis) The value-add is real only for Stigg customers who (a) sell globally and (b) want Airwallex's cross-border acceptance over Stripe — a meaningful but narrow slice.
- Who captures the margin (second-order): Stigg owns the customer-facing control plane (the access decision), so it captures the stickier, higher-multiple position; Airwallex captures transaction take-rate on the payments flowing through — the lower-multiple, commoditizable piece. (analysis) This mirrors the Ramp dynamic: being the entitlement/decision layer is worth more than being the rail. Airwallex risks being "just the pipe" in AI monetization unless it converts these connector relationships into its own billing/entitlement attach.
- Risk to the value-add: Stripe can natively collapse payments+billing+entitlements for the same AI customer; if Stripe-native is "good enough," the two-vendor Airwallex+Stigg combo only wins where Airwallex's global acceptance materially beats Stripe's.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Next: watch for GA + named joint AI customers (would raise importance); Stigg's Received.ai acquisition suggests it's building contract→entitlement→invoice coverage, so expect the Airwallex rail to extend to invoicing/collection ("checkout to collection").
- Sentiment: broadly positive on "AI monetization infrastructure" as a 2026 theme (usage-based pricing for AI is exploding; many vendors — Metronome/Orb/Stigg/Amberflo — competing). Airwallex funding momentum is strong ([[Airwallex raises new funding at $12 billion valuation]]; separate reports of $320M at ~$11B).
- Why the market goes this way + counterintuitive effect: AI pricing is fluid (credits, usage, plan changes mid-cycle) → real-time entitlement enforcement in the request path becomes mandatory, which structurally favors neutral governance layers like Stigg over any single payments vendor owning the whole stack. Counterintuitive second-order: by partnering rather than building, Airwallex gets fast reach but **cements Stigg (and Stripe) as the ones who own the AI customer relationship**, leaving Airwallex competing on take-rate — the weakest position in the stack.
- Regulatory backdrop: minimal direct exposure; standard payments/PSP licensing, which Airwallex already holds broadly.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** This sits in AI/SaaS monetization infrastructure — the layer that meters usage, enforces entitlements (feature/plan access) and reconciles it with payments. No clean public TAM is verifiable, so TAM/CAGR = "no data"; the structural signal is clearer. Usage-based and hybrid pricing is now the default for AI products (subscriptions + usage + credits + add-ons), because inference cost is marginal per call and vendors must charge per-token/per-call to protect gross margin — this is the "why now." Second-order effect: billing stops being a back-office batch job and becomes a real-time control-plane decision ("support or refuse the next request in milliseconds," per the Airwallex blog, 2026-10-19), which is why the payment outcome and the entitlement decision have to be wired together. The segment is fragmenting into specialists: metering/billing engines (Metronome, Orb, Lago), entitlement layers (Stigg), and payment acceptance/FX rails (Airwallex, Stripe) — value accrues to whoever owns the real-time sync between money-in and access-granted.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for billing infra — dollars billed/processed on-platform, net revenue retention, number of end-customers metered; for Airwallex the money rail — TPV, take rate, ARR. Airwallex's position: ahead on cross-border rails (160+ local payment methods, 130+ currencies, 180+ countries), catching-up/partnering on the entitlement/metering layer where Stripe and Adyen are buying their way in. Moat (analysis): proprietary multi-currency licensing network (85+ licenses) and FX — switching costs + regulatory barriers, not the entitlement logic itself (which it is renting from Stigg).
