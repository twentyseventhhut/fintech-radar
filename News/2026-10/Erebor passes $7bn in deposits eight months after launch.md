# Context-enrichment: Erebor passes $7bn in deposits, eight months after launch
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 4/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Per the FT (reported ~6 Oct 2026), Erebor's deposits have reached **~$7bn**, up from the **$4.05bn** disclosed in July (see [[Erebor Bank seeks $8 billion valuation, nearly doubling prior round]]) — i.e. **+~$3bn and ~500 new customers in one quarter**, ~8 months after the bank opened (Feb 2026). "Eight months after opening" checks out (Feb→Oct 2026).
- **Data quality — the key de-PR'ing:** $7bn is an **FT/press-reported** figure, NOT a filing. The last *regulator-grade* number is the **Q2 2026 OCC/FDIC call report at ~$4.06bn**; the +$3bn jump is not yet in a call report. So the headline is a self-reported mark, not audited.
- **What the Q2 call report actually shows (primary, and the real tell):** gross loans **~$77.8M** — Erebor has lent out **~1% of deposits**; cash + interbank balances **~$4.17bn** (roughly 90%+ of assets parked at other banks); Tier-1 leverage **~38.8%** (vs the OCC-imposed 12% minimum for its first three years, per the FDIC order in [[FDIC approves Thiel-backed Erebor Bank for deposit insurance]]); **net loss ~$16.3M**, ROA ~−1.03%, NIM ~0.71%.
- **Why this framing matters (→ second order):** the "fastest deposit growth of any US bank" story is true on *gathering* but misleading on *banking*. Erebor is essentially a very-safe warehouse for tech/crypto/VC operating cash, earning spread on interbank balances while losing money on opex. The deposit figure flatters the narrative; the business underneath (deposit-funded lending, stablecoin rails) is still essentially pre-revenue. Management previously admitted (July) that **crypto-backed lending demand is lower than expected** — so the highest-margin novel leg is soft, and growth is coming from plain deposits.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Charter-owning comparables:** Column (~$291M ARR), Lead Bank (~$551M ARR, Stripe/Bridge stablecoin backbone), Grasshopper — true apples-to-apples (own-charter). On deposits Erebor is now faster; on **revenue it trails all of them** (it is loss-making and pre-revenue-at-scale).
- **Startup-banking overlap:** **Mercury** (~$20bn deposits, ~$650M ARR, GAAP-profitable, now with conditional OCC charter approval — see [[Mercury raises $200 million at $5.2 billion valuation]]) is ~3x Erebor's deposits with a real fee/software revenue engine. **Brex** now sits inside Capital One (~$5.15bn deal, Jan 2026 — [[Capital One to acquire Brex for $5.15 billion]]), giving it a Tier-1 balance sheet.
- **Crypto-charter precedent:** Anchorage has held an OCC charter since 2021; Erebor is first *de novo under Trump 2.0*, not first crypto-charter.
- **Position & why (→):** ahead on *deposit momentum and political-tailwind narrative*, but at **parity-to-behind on durable moat** — the "we own the charter/balance sheet" edge is closing (Mercury chartering, Brex→Capital One). The premium is a scarcity/momentum premium, not a defensible-earnings premium (analysis).

## [2] Company history / fit
Trajectory (all dated, from corpus): [[Erebor raises at $2.25B valuation, backed by Palmer Luckey]] (Jun 2025) → [[Palmer Luckey to launch crypto bank Erebor]] (Jul 2025) → [[Trump-backed crypto bank Erebor wins preliminary OCC approval]] (15 Oct 2025) → [[FDIC approves Thiel-backed Erebor Bank for deposit insurance]] (Dec 2025) → $4.35B/$350M Series B (Dec 2025) → [[Erebor Bank receives national bank charter under Trump]] (Feb 2026, first Trump 2.0 de novo, ~$635M capital, opened) → $4.05bn deposits + $8B valuation talks ([[Erebor Bank seeks $8 billion valuation, nearly doubling prior round]], Jul 2026) → **$7bn deposits (Oct 2026)**.
- **Why it moves this fast (→):** a politically-gated charter is a one-time scarce asset; founders (Luckey/Thiel/Lonsdale; Founders Fund, 8VC, Lux, Haun) are monetizing the scarcity + the AI/crypto/defense narrative while the OCC window is open. Gathering deposits fast is the cheapest way to justify the $8B mark before Mercury/others dilute the scarcity. Leadership note vs prior corpus: Owen Rapaport is now **sole CEO** (was co-CEO); Jacob Hirshman is CSO; Michael Hagedorn is President (the career banker).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Real & new:** a genuinely chartered, FDIC-insured national bank aimed at the SVB-successor niche, gathering deposits at record de novo speed — structurally rare.
- **Traction is real but thin/possibly concentrated:** $4bn→$7bn in a quarter is fast, but off a tiny base, among ~500–600 total customers (inherently concentrated, VC/crypto-correlated), and Luckey's earlier volunteered denial ("none of the growth came from my own companies") is itself a tell about related-party concern.
- **Who captures the margin (→ why it matters):** with ~1% lent out, no live stablecoin issuance (GENIUS-Act-gated), and a net loss, Erebor today earns a thin spread on parked cash. That is an ordinary — and currently loss-making — spread-bank economics that deserves ~1–2x book, not a venture multiple. The $8B ask assumes the stablecoin/crypto rails eventually capture platform-like margin; by the company's own July admission, that leg is under-delivering. So the multiple rests on deposit *growth* + narrative, not proven earnings.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Plans:** USD-stablecoin deposits/payments (gated on GENIUS Act final rules, proposed ~Feb 2026 — not live); profitability was guided "by year-end" (Q2 shows a loss, so unproven); a non-binding LOI with Banco de Venezuela for correspondent banking (sanctions/reputational landmine); Series C (~$1.5B @ ~$8B) in progress but **unclosed** — treat the valuation as a target.
- **Regulatory backdrop — the real risk:** Sen. Elizabeth Warren has "serious concerns" about how fast the charter was granted and whether political ties sped it. A charter obtained under a favourable OCC is exposed to reversal/review risk.
- **Second-order (analysis):** Erebor has so far **avoided SVB's duration mistake** (it sits in cash/interbank, not long HTM bonds) — the one genuine improvement. But it **inherits SVB's uninsured/concentrated-client run-risk DNA** (VC-herd depositors, large balances, tight network). The most important single number — the **insured vs uninsured deposit split** — is undisclosed. Counterintuitively, the faster the mark compounds on political + deposit momentum, the more fragile it is to a political reversal or a funding-winter deposit drain.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US venture/crypto/startup banking — the niche vacated by Silicon Valley Bank (deposits peaked ~$198bn Mar-2022, ~$173bn at its Mar-2023 failure; per S&P Global / Wikipedia). No reliable public TAM for the "innovation-economy deposits" slice — no data; proxy = SVB's former ~$170bn+ deposit book plus the crypto/stablecoin treasury layer opened by the GENIUS Act. Structure: regulatory-gated (OCC national charter is the moat and the barrier — Erebor won preliminary OCC approval Oct-2025, see [[Trump-backed crypto bank Erebor wins preliminary OCC approval]]) and politically positioned toward crypto/AI/defence. Why now: (1) SVB's collapse left tech/VC deposits looking for a dedicated home; (2) the post-Synapse retreat from the sponsor-bank model is pushing fintechs to seek their own charters (per Mercury CEO via CNBC, 2026-05); (3) stablecoin/GENIUS tailwind makes a "stablecoin-native" bank a live thesis. Erebor launched ~Feb-2026 and reported $1.1bn deposits (Mar-2026) → ~$4.06bn (June call) → >$7bn (Oct-2026), +$3bn and 500+ customers in the last quarter (per FT, via Simon Taylor / Crowdfund Insider / Cryptopolitan).

**Competitive landscape.** Bank KPIs that matter here: deposit growth, loan-to-deposit ratio (LDR), deposit concentration/granularity, net interest margin, tier-1 leverage. Players: regulated-charter challengers — Mercury (conditional OCC approval 2026, see [[Mercury wins conditional OCC approval for bank charter]]; $5.2bn valuation, 300k+ customers, $650m annualised revenue, four years profitable — [[Mercury raises $200 million at $5.2 billion valuation]]), Brex, Column (SVB-alumni–founded sponsor bank / infra, and a Mercury partner bank), and newer AI-bank entrants ([[Augustus gains OCC approval and raises $40 million for AI bank]]). Basis of competition: regulatory status + distribution into the tech/VC network, not price. Erebor's position: fastest deposit ramp of the cohort but on a tiny, concentrated base (~500+ accounts vs Mercury's 300k+) — niche, deposit-led, barely lending. Moat `(analysis)`: charter + politically-aligned founder network (Luckey/Thiel/Lonsdale; backers a16z, Founders Fund, Lux — per Bloomberg/TheBlock); switching costs are low this early, so the moat is mostly scarcity of the charter, not stickiness.

**Comps & multiples.** Erebor reportedly in talks to raise $1.5bn at an ~$8bn valuation (per Bloomberg via TheBlock / Tech Startups, Aug-2026) — a round (private) valuation, NOT market cap, and struck before the $7bn deposit print. P/deposits = `$8bn / $7bn = ~1.1x` on *deposits*; a bank is normally valued on equity/earnings, not deposits, so treat this as a venture multiple, not a bank multiple — rich vs a deposit-taker that lends almost nothing (interest income is effectively a cash/treasury carry, not a lending franchise). Internal private-bank comp: Mercury at `$5.2bn` on $650m annualised revenue = `~8x` revenue ([[Mercury raises $200 million at $5.2 billion valuation]]); Erebor discloses no comparable revenue/earnings → EV/Revenue and P/E = no data / `[UNSOURCED]`. Distribution not computed (only one clean private comp). Qualitative read: Erebor is priced on deposit-growth momentum and strategic positioning, not on a proven P&L.

**Risk flags.**
- *Deposit concentration / run risk.* >$7bn across only ~500+ large accounts in correlated sectors (crypto, AI, defence) — the exact granularity problem (few large, uninsured, herd-prone depositors) that detonated SVB in 36 hours. Second-order: one sector wobble or a few treasury teams pulling at once can trigger a self-reinforcing outflow faster than a retail book ever would.
- *Asset-side under-utilisation / unproven franchise.* $78m loans against ~$7bn deposits (LDR ~1.1%; reportedly capping LDR near ~50%, per insights4vc/airwallex). Conservative = safe on credit, but it means earnings ride almost entirely on the rate carry on cash/interbank balances — fragile to Fed cuts and offering no lending moat. Second-order: if rates fall, the "bank" has no demonstrated way to make money, undercutting the $8bn valuation.
- *Crypto/political concentration.* Crypto + defence tilt and overt political alignment is both the distribution edge and a tail risk — regulatory/administration turns, or a Silvergate/Signature-style crypto-deposit flight, hit this base disproportionately.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The SVB gap is being refilled not by one successor but by a charter-race cohort (Mercury, Erebor, Augustus); Erebor is the deposit-growth outlier but also the most concentrated and least proven as a lender. Falsifiable thesis: the $8bn valuation holds only if Erebor converts deposit momentum into a durable, diversified franchise (granular deposits + real lending/payments revenue) before a rate cut or a sector shock tests the concentrated book. Trigger to watch: next quarterly deposit/customer-count print (is growth broadening the base or just adding more whales?) and whether the $1.5bn raise closes at ~$8bn. What breaks it: a single large-depositor exodus or crypto-deposit flight that exposes the run-risk the SVB parallel implies.
