Vivid selects Wise Platform for international payments for SMEs 
 London / Berlin, October 14th, 2026 –  As demand for cross-border payments surges among its European small and medium-sized businesses (SMEs), Vivid has selected Wise Platform, Wise’s global payments infrastructure for banks and enterprises, to launch fast, transparent and seamless international payments for its business customers.
The partnership comes as Vivid sees rapidly growing demand for international payments among its business customers. Over the ten months to May 2026, the number of Vivid Business customers making an international payment in a given month grew more than fivefold 1 .
Growth has been particularly strong in major trading hubs outside Europe, including China, the UAE and Singapore. Comparing the latest six months with the six months prior, payments by Vivid Business customers to China more than quadrupled, while payments to the UAE increased approximately 250% and payments to Singapore rose by approximately 40% 2 .
Through its partnership with Wise Platform, Vivid Business customers will be able to make fast, transparent international payments into more than 40 currencies. CNY payouts are already available across 27 European countries and the next currencies are to be rolled out soon. 
 Alexander Emeshev, Co-Founder at Vivid, said: "The modern entrepreneur is cosmopolitan, so their business banking should be. Cross-border payments are therefore essential for today's business banking. The growth we've seen over the past six months in customers sending money to markets like China, the UAE, and Singapore is proof of that demand. Together with Wise, we're now meeting that demand: offering the same speed and transparency when moving money to Shenzhen as when moving it to Stockholm."
 Manuel Sandhofer, General Manager for EMEA at Wise Platform, said: “As European SMEs do more business around the world, their payments need to keep pace with their ambitions and work just as well across borders as they do at home. That means the same speed, transparency and reliability wherever they’re moving money. We’re excited to work with Vivid to bring the infrastructure Wise has built to make that possible for more businesses across Europe.”
Wise Platform is powered by Wise’s global payments network, more than 80 licenses and direct access to domestic payment systems in nine markets worldwide, enabling 77% of its payments to arrive instantly, in under 20 seconds. Vivid joins a growing number of leading institutions including Monzo, UniCredit, Raiffeisen Bank International and N26 using Wise Platform to deliver a seamless and reliable experience that meets the evolving expectations of their customers.
Vivid payment services are provided by Vivid Money S.A., an Electronic Money Institution licensed by the CSSF in Luxembourg.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened (dated).** On 14 October 2026 (London/Berlin), German SME neobank **Vivid** selected **Wise Platform** — Wise's global payments infrastructure for banks and enterprises — to power fast, transparent international payments for its business customers. Vivid Business customers can now pay out in **40+ currencies**; **CNY payouts are already live across 27 European countries**, with more currencies to follow. Payments are provided via **Vivid Money S.A., an EMI licensed by the CSSF in Luxembourg**.

**Why now — the demand signal.** Vivid cites its own transaction data: the number of Vivid Business customers making an international payment in a given month **grew >5x over the ten months to May 2026**. Asian corridors are the standout — vs. the prior six months, payments to **China >4x**, **UAE ~+250%**, **Singapore ~+40%**. The pivot context matters: SoftBank-backed Vivid **axed standalone retail banking in March 2025 to go all-in on SME/business banking**, and has since expanded beyond Germany into France, Spain, Luxembourg and the Netherlands. Public milestones: >50k business customers by mid-2025 (~16 months after the Jan-2024 Vivid Business launch), surpassing **100,000 SME customers** by early 2026 ([[Vivid surpasses 100,000 SME customers with multi-currency interest]]). Cross-border payouts are the natural next layer on top of its multi-currency Interest Accounts / Treasury stack.

**What Vivid actually gets.** Wise Platform rails give it instant-ish, transparent FX payouts without building its own global network: Wise runs on **80+ licences**, a single global tech stack, 24/7 ops, and **direct connections into domestic payment systems in nine markets**, reaching **160+ countries** with **77% of payments arriving in under 20 seconds**. Vivid doesn't disclose economics, so this is a classic build-vs-buy / speed-to-market call rather than a margin play for Vivid.

**For Wise — this is the infrastructure flywheel, not a one-off.** Wise Platform is Steve Naudé's growth engine: it sits at **~6% of group cross-border volume today, with a medium-term target of 10% and a stated long-term ambition of >50%** ([[Wise partners OakNorth to expand infrastructure division]], 2026-10-07). Vivid is the second European SME-infra win in October 2026 alone (OakNorth being the UK equivalent). The client roster Wise now advertises — **Morgan Stanley, Standard Chartered, Nubank, Monzo, N26, UniCredit, Raiffeisen Bank International, Bank Mandiri** — shows the division spanning global banks to neobanks. Prior pattern in-DB: [[MBSB Bank becomes first Malaysian bank to partner Wise]], [[GoTyme Bank and Wise Platform enable Filipino remittances]], [[Lunar expands in Nordics via Wise and Pismo partnerships]]. Group scale backdrop: [[Wise reports FY2026 net revenue of $2.5 billion, up 19%]] (cross-border volume +31% to $243bn, 19m active customers).

**So what.** A fast-growing European SME platform outsourcing its cross-border layer to Wise, with China/UAE/Singapore as the demand drivers, is a concrete datapoint that (a) European SME banking is going global-by-default and (b) Wise's B2B2X infrastructure bet is compounding across both incumbents and challengers — the strategically interesting side of this story is Wise, not the individual deal.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is this a signed, live integration or an announced intent? The release says CNY payouts are "already available" across 27 countries — but is full 40-currency coverage live or roadmap ("next currencies to be rolled out soon")?
2. What are the actual economics? Vivid discloses no fees/revenue-share; is Vivid marking up Wise's mid-market rate to its SMEs, or passing it through? "Transparent" is Wise's brand claim, not a verified Vivid price.
3. The ">5x growth in monthly international-paying customers" is off a self-reported, undisclosed base. 5x of a tiny number is still small — how many customers actually pay cross-border?
4. China/UAE/Singapore growth (>4x / ~250% / ~40%) is share-of-volume framing from Vivid's internal data, unaudited. How representative of total flows?
5. Does Wise Platform actually clear CNY natively, or route via correspondents? "Direct access in nine markets" — is China one of them, or is CNY payout still intermediated?
6. Concentration/dependency risk: Vivid is now reliant on a single external rail for a core SME feature. What's the switching cost / fallback if Wise re-prices or de-prioritises the EMEA SME segment?
7. Competitive read: Vivid already uses Adyen for payouts ([[Adyen signs Vivid for payouts solution]]). Is Wise additive (international) or does it signal multi-vendor fragmentation in Vivid's stack?
8. Is Vivid a meaningful volume contributor to Wise Platform's 6%→10% goal, or a logo/PR win? OakNorth same month looks larger (UK SME lender w/ 60% challenger-lending share).
9. Regulatory surface: Vivid operates as a Luxembourg EMI (CSSF), not a bank. Does using Wise's licence network create any passporting/AML responsibility-split ambiguity for high-risk corridors (UAE, China)?
10. "77% under 20 seconds" and "160+ countries" are Wise group stats — do they hold for the specific corridors Vivid's SMEs actually use, or are the fast ones the common EUR/USD routes?
11. Timing coincidence with OakNorth (both Oct 2026): is Wise front-loading SME-infra announcements ahead of a results/investor narrative (post-US listing)?
12. Durability: SME demand to China/UAE could be trade-cycle-driven; is the 5x structural or a 2025–26 trade-flow spike?
13. Does this cannibalise or complement Vivid's multi-currency Interest Accounts (hold USD/GBP) — i.e., are SMEs converting/paying out vs. holding?
14. Any exclusivity? Nothing stated — Vivid could add other FX rails later, limiting Wise's lock-in.

**Importance: 3/5 — rationale.** A clean, dated, real infrastructure win with named counterparties and credible demand data. It matters mostly as a second October-2026 proof point for Wise Platform's B2B2X flywheel (OakNorth being the first), reinforcing Wise's 6%→10%→>50% infra thesis, and as a marker of European SME banking going global-by-default. But as a standalone item it's incremental: no disclosed economics, Vivid is a mid-size neobank, and the capability (outsourced cross-border rails) is now table stakes. Not a 4 (no market-moving scale, no new mechanism); clearly above a 2 (concrete, strategic, fits a tracked trend). Fresh — not previously covered in-DB.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits in the cross-border SME-payments *infrastructure* layer — the "embedded FX rail" that a neobank/EMI buys rather than builds. The underlying flow pool is very large: cross-border B2B transaction value is put at ~$198trn in 2026 growing ~6% CAGR to ~$266trn by 2031, with the SME sub-segment growing faster (~8.7% CAGR) as fintech tooling lowers the cost of international payables (per Mordor Intelligence, via web, as of 2026). Structure: the retail/SME transfer market is fragmenting at the front end (dozens of neobanks/EMIs like Vivid) while *consolidating* at the rail layer — a handful of licensed FX-infrastructure providers (Wise Platform, Visa-owned Currencycloud, Nium, Airwallex) wholesale the licences, local clearing and FX to everyone else. Entry barrier is regulatory/capital (Wise Platform runs on 80+ licences and direct access to domestic payment systems in nine markets; 77% of payments arrive in <20s — Vivid/Wise PR, 2026-10-14). **Why now:** SME demand is spiking — Vivid says monthly business customers making an international payment grew *more than 5x* over the ten months to May 2026, with China payments >4x, UAE ~+250% and Singapore ~+40% (six-months-on-six-months; Vivid internal data, PR 2026-10-14). The build-vs-buy calculus has flipped: a sub-scale EMI cannot replicate 80+ licences, so it rents the rail and competes on UX. `(analysis)`

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for the rail layer: cross-border volume (TPV), take rate (bps), number of institutional clients, and currency/corridor coverage. Key rail providers and the basis of competition (coverage + price + speed + integration):
- **Wise Platform** — Wise's infrastructure arm; 160+ countries, 40+ currencies; clients include Monzo, UniCredit, Raiffeisen, N26, Morgan Stanley, Standard Chartered, Nubank, Bank Mandiri (Vivid/Wise PR, 2026-10-14). Carried on Wise's group cross-border volume of **$243.5bn (+31% YoY)** at a group take rate of **~0.52%** (Wise FY2026 audited results, 2026-06-25; see internal [[Wise reports FY2026 net revenue of $2.5 billion, up 19%]]).
- **Currencycloud** — Visa-owned since Dec-2021; the incumbent white-label FX-API, bundled with Visa's distribution.
- **Nium** — ~$25bn annual volume, >$250m raised (Visa, Riverwood among backers), publicly targeting a US IPO by end-2026 (via web, 2026).
- **Airwallex** — ~$1.5bn ARR (May-2026), ~$300bn annualized volume, ~$11bn valuation (May-2026 round led by Addition); 200k+ businesses — but increasingly a *direct* SME competitor to Vivid, not just a rail (Forbes/Sacra, via web, 2026). See internal [[Mellow launches Airwallex-powered global contractor management platform]], [[OPTRA Labs and Airwallex partner to support cross-border SME growth]].
**Protagonist position (Vivid):** niche front-end EMI (Vivid Money S.A., CSSF-licensed Luxembourg; ~50k business customers as of Jun-2025 after its 2025 retail-exit SME pivot — via web). Vivid is the *customer* here, not the rail; its moat is the SME relationship/UX, not FX infrastructure. **Wise is the real winner** — this is another B2B2X win feeding its infrastructure flywheel. `(analysis)`

**Comps & multiples.** This is a commercial **partnership, not a round or M&A** — there is no valuation for Vivid and no deal economics disclosed, so a trading-comp table is **not applicable**. Qualitative rail-layer comparison instead: Airwallex ~$11bn private valuation on ~$1.5bn ARR ≈ **~7x ARR** (round valuation, not market cap; via web May-2026) frames the premium the market pays for cross-border-SME rails; Nium (~$25bn volume) is the closest IPO-stage read; Currencycloud has no standalone mark (absorbed into Visa). Wise itself (public) trades at ~3.4–4.7x revenue on FY2026 net revenue of $2.5bn (see internal [[Wise reports FY2026 net revenue of $2.5 billion, up 19%]]). **Wise Platform's own revenue is NOT separately disclosed** — only its *volume share* is quantified: it is **~6% of group cross-border volume today, targeting 10% medium-term and >50% long-term** (Steve Naudé, head of Wise Platform — see internal [[Wise partners OakNorth to expand infrastructure division]]; web corroborates ~5–6%). Put against $243.5bn group volume, ~6% ⇒ **~$14–15bn of Platform volume today** `(analysis)`; a path to 10% ⇒ ~$24bn. Any per-deal revenue from Vivid = `[UNSOURCED]`.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Disintermediation of the front end (for Vivid).** By renting Wise's rail, Vivid outsources the hardest, highest-moat part of cross-border; it keeps only the UX/relationship layer, which is the most contestable. Second-order: Wise Platform's own stated ambition is for infrastructure to become >50% of group volume — the rail provider is structurally incentivised to capture ever more of the value chain, and nothing stops Wise from also courting Vivid's SME segment directly.
2. **Rail-provider concentration / dependence on someone else's licences.** Vivid's cross-border proposition now depends on Wise's 80+ licences and nine direct connections; pricing, corridor roll-out ("next currencies to be rolled out soon") and fraud/settlement liability sit with Wise. A single-rail dependency is a concentration risk if terms change.
3. **"Announced vs live" gap + thin economics.** De-PR: CNY payouts are live across 27 European countries, but the 40+ currency promise is forward ("rolled out soon"). Neither party disclosed volume committed, revenue, take rate or fraud-liability split — the economics that would let anyone judge whether this is material to Wise Platform's 6%→10% path. For Wise it is one more logo (alongside Monzo, N26, UniCredit); for Vivid it is a product catch-up, not differentiation — rivals Revolut Business, bunq and Airwallex already offer multi-currency SME payments.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Reinforces the thesis that cross-border value is migrating from the *front end* (neobanks/EMIs) to the *rail layer* (Wise Platform, Currencycloud, Nium, Airwallex) — the SME-facing brands increasingly compete on UX while a shrinking set of licensed infrastructure providers monetise the flow. `(analysis)` Falsifiable thesis: if Wise Platform's share of group cross-border volume keeps climbing toward its 10% medium-term target, wins like Vivid are the mechanism and the infrastructure re-rating is real; **trigger to watch** — Wise's next disclosure of Platform volume share (6% → trending up?) and whether it ever breaks out Platform *revenue*. Thesis breaks if Platform share stalls at ~6% despite logo wins, which would mean these partnerships are PR-heavy and volume-light.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Verdict (headline read).** The Vivid win is not an earnings event — it's a Wise Platform B2B2X partnership. But it maps directly onto Wise's IR-reported infrastructure momentum. Latest reported print: Q1 FY27 trading update (3 months to 30 Jun 2026, released 17 Jul 2026): net revenue $714.0m (+25% YoY), transaction revenue $540.9m (+27%), cross-border volume $69.3bn (+26%); full-year FY26 (ended 31 Mar 2026): net revenue $2,502.8m (+19%), cross-border volume $243.5bn (+31%), reported income before tax $660.4m (26% margin). Read on this deal: IN-LINE confirmation of the Wise Platform flywheel, not a needle-mover for Wise group numbers.

**Key figures (with growth).**
- **FY26 (y/e 31 Mar 2026, earnings release 25 Jun 2026):** Net revenue $2,502.8m, +19% YoY. Reported income before tax $660.4m, 26% margin. Net income $498.7m. Cross-border volume $243.5bn, +31% YoY. Cross-border take rate 0.52%, -6bps YoY (deliberate price cuts + mix). Active customers 18.9m, +21% YoY. Card spend $43.6bn, +37%. Customer holdings $39.0bn, +40%. Wise guided underlying PBT margin for FY26 towards the top of its 13–16% range (reflecting reinvestment into pricing, product and infrastructure) — note the headline 26% income-before-tax margin is boosted by interest on customer holdings; the underlying (ex-interest) margin is the lower 13–16% figure. Adjusted EBITDA not separately disclosed in the release [no data].
- **Q1 FY27 (3m to 30 Jun 2026, latest reported):** Net revenue $714.0m, +25% YoY. Transaction revenue $540.9m, +27%. Cross-border volume $69.3bn, +26%. Take rate 0.50%, down from 0.52% (further price cuts). Active customers 11.9m (personal), +21%. Customer holdings $41.2bn, +31%. Instant transfers 77% of payments, up 7pts YoY — the same "77% arrive instantly, under 20 seconds" infrastructure stat Wise and Vivid cite in this PR.

**By segment / driver (Wise Platform relevance).** Wise Platform is Wise's B2B2X infrastructure arm and sits at **~5% of group cross-border volume** (per FY26 results) — small but a strategic growth vector. FY26 explicitly flagged new Platform partners **UniCredit, Raiffeisen Bank, MBSB Bank and (Apr 2026) Capitec**, plus Upwork and Lunar in earlier updates, "with both new and existing partnerships contributing to growth in cross-border volume from Wise Platform." Vivid (a CSSF-licensed Luxembourg EMI, business-banking) extends that roster into European SME business banking, joining the Monzo/N26/UniCredit/Raiffeisen/Nubank/Standard Chartered/Morgan Stanley cohort the PR names. The win rides a real demand signal — Vivid's monthly business customers making international payments grew >5x over the ten months to May 2026 — but a single mid-size EMI adds incremental, not step-change, volume to a $243.5bn/year base.

**vs expectations / prior period.** Q1 FY27 +25% net-revenue / +26% cross-border volume **accelerated** vs FY26's +19% / +31% full-year pace on revenue (volume comparably strong), and is tracking around the middle of the 15–20% medium-term range. Take rate continues its managed glide down (0.52% → 0.50%), the intentional price-cut strategy — volume growth (+26%) more than offsets it. No public consensus pulled for this trading update [UNSOURCED on Street estimate]; vs prior year the trajectory is clearly up. Internal corpus trend: cross-border volume path — [[Wise serves 11 million customers as volumes hit £47 billion]] (FY end) and [[Wise income rises 11% in Q1 FY26]] (prior-year Q1, slower +11% income) → marked acceleration into Q1 FY27 (+25%).

**Guidance / forward.** FY27 guidance **reaffirmed** at Q1: net revenue growth around the middle of the 15–20% medium-term range (constant currency), income-before-tax margin around the top of the 20–25% range (assumes no material change in customer interest paid / central-bank rates). Underlying (ex-interest-income) medium-term targets: 15–20% net-revenue CAGR off an FY24 base, 13–16% underlying PBT margin — kept at the reinvestment end as Wise spends on pricing cuts, product and payment-system infrastructure (new direct connections to Brazil and Japan domestic rails in FY26; 9 direct connections, 80+ licences — the engine Vivid is buying into). Management tone: confident on volume/customer growth, deliberately silent on near-term underlying-margin expansion (reinvestment priority); interest-rate sensitivity is the unspoken swing factor on the headline margin.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. **B2B2X flywheel is live, not just announced.** Fact: FY26 named Platform partners contributed to Platform cross-border volume growth; Vivid adds a European business-banking EMI. Why it matters: validates the "infrastructure for banks/enterprises" leg as a genuine distribution channel (80+ licences, 9 direct rails, 77% instant) rather than a slideware segment. Second-order: each incumbent/neobank that rents Wise rails deepens Wise's cost/speed moat via scale, widening the gap vs standalone correspondent-banking competitors — but also quietly makes those partners dependent on a firm that itself competes for the same SME customer.
2. **Platform is still only ~5% of volume** — Vivid is accretive to the growth story, immaterial to FY27 numbers. Watch whether Wise starts disclosing Platform volume/revenue separately; absence of a standalone Platform P&L line is the key "what they're silent on."
3. **Take-rate compression is strategy, not weakness** (0.52%→0.50%), but it caps revenue leverage — the thesis depends on volume (+26%) and holdings/interest income outrunning price cuts; a rate-cut cycle would pressure the headline 20–25% IBT margin.
4. **Vivid-specific signal:** >5x growth in Vivid business customers paying cross-border (10m to May 2026), with China 4x, UAE ~+250%, Singapore ~+40% — evidence SME cross-border demand (the TAM Wise Platform monetises) is structurally expanding, supporting the group volume thesis.
