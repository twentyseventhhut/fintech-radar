## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened.** On 2026-10-23 Solidgate announced it was authorized as a Crypto-Asset Service Provider (CASP) under the EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA) by the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC). The licence is held via a Cypriot entity and **passported across the European Economic Area** (30 countries), giving Solidgate its own regulated base for stablecoin services alongside its existing fiat payment rails. Permitted activities: **custody** of stablecoins on behalf of clients, **exchange** between fiat and stablecoins, **payments** (stablecoin payouts and cross-border), and **settlement** of processing revenue in stablecoins. The company frames this as owning its own licence ("own controls, own pace") rather than renting access through a partner.

**Who Solidgate is.** A payment orchestration platform / PSP founded in Ukraine in 2016 (Yuri Alekseev), spun out of Genesis (Ukraine's largest IT group); now HQ'd in Nicosia, Cyprus with offices in Kyiv and Warsaw. Bootstrapped, no external VC funding. ~329 staff (Aug 2026), ~$33.6M annual revenue (2026). It connects merchants to 100+ acquirers/PSPs/APMs through a single integration with smart routing, billing, chargeback management; strong in e-commerce and subscription/iGaming verticals. The MiCA move sits atop a 2025 run of infrastructure plays — Treasury launch, SWIFT/SEPA (LHV) connectivity, J.P. Morgan Payments relationship, Klarna BNPL partnership (Sep 2026) — i.e. Solidgate building its own money-movement stack rather than reselling.

**Why it matters / competitive context.** MiCA's CASP regime became the gating licence for regulated crypto/stablecoin services in the EU, with the transitional window closing 1 July 2026 ([[MiCA transitional window for crypto service providers closes 1 July]]); 102 CASPs were approved in the regime's first year ([[EU's MiCA marks first year with 102 approved CASPs]]). Solidgate joins a growing cluster of **PSPs/orchestrators adding CASP authorization to bolt stablecoins onto fiat rails** — most directly comparable to [[Nuvei secures EU CASP licence under MiCA]] (Dec 2025, which paired CASP with a PI licence). Cyprus/CySEC has become a favoured passporting hub for crypto licences — the same route used by [[Revolut secures MiCA license in Cyprus for EEA crypto]] (Oct 2025). Pure-play / stablecoin-native peers that took the CASP route include [[Triple-A secures MiCA CASP licence from France's AMF]] and [[Stablecoin player Fipto achieves dual authorisation status]] (dual MiCA CASP + PI). Note a structural caveat: from March 2026, firms that custody and transfer **e-money tokens (EMTs/stablecoins)** may need BOTH a MiCA CASP authorisation and a separate PSD2 Payment Institution licence — Solidgate's release cites only CASP, so its PI/EMI coverage for EMT flows is not stated here.

**Net:** a mid-sized, profitable, bootstrapped orchestrator securing its own EEA-wide CASP licence — a credible, infrastructure-first step positioning stablecoins as "the next global rail" for its merchant base, but one authorization among many in a crowded 2026 field, and its practical impact depends on execution and any additional PI/EMI permissions.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
1. **Is "CASP from CySEC" the whole permission set?** From March 2026, custodying/transferring EMTs (stablecoins) can require BOTH a MiCA CASP authorisation AND a separate PSD2 Payment Institution/EMI licence. The release cites only CASP — does Solidgate actually have the PI/EMI leg needed for stablecoin payment flows, or is the "payments/settlements" claim partly aspirational?

2. **Does passporting to 30 EEA markets equal live service in 30 markets?** CASP passporting is a regulatory right, not an operational rollout. Is any stablecoin product actually live for clients today, or is this authorization-only with product TBD?

3. **How differentiated is this?** Nuvei (Dec 2025), Triple-A, Fipto, Crossmint, Ripple, OKX and 100+ CASPs already hold EU crypto authorizations. What does Solidgate offer that an existing CASP-licensed orchestrator doesn't — is "own licence vs rented" a real moat or marketing?

4. **Scale check.** At ~$33.6M revenue and ~329 staff, is Solidgate large enough for its stablecoin rails to matter to large merchants, versus Adyen/Checkout.com/Stripe-scale players or crypto-native exchanges?

5. **Which stablecoins, and MiCA-compliant?** Custody/exchange of which tokens? Only MiCA-authorized EMTs (e.g. EURC, compliant USD coins) or broader? Non-compliant stablecoins face EU restrictions — does the permission actually monetize the dominant USD stablecoins?

6. **Source quality.** This is a company press release via GlobeNewswire (self-announced), not a regulator statement. Is the CASP entry verifiable on the CySEC/ESMA CASP register? No third-party confirmation in the note.

7. **Demand question.** Merchant appetite for stablecoin payouts/settlement in Solidgate's e-commerce/subscription/iGaming base — is there evidenced demand, or is this supply-led betting on a thesis ("next global rail")?

8. **Economics.** Does owning the licence improve unit economics vs partnering, net of compliance cost (capital, custody, AML, operational resilience the release says took "months")? Ongoing cost of running a regulated CASP is nontrivial for a company this size.

9. **Entity/regulatory geography.** Licence sits in a Cypriot entity; company is Ukraine-founded, Warsaw/Kyiv-operated. Any governance, substance, or geopolitical (Ukraine exposure) considerations for a crypto-custody licence?

10. **Timing vs the regime.** MiCA transitional window closed 1 July 2026 — is Solidgate late to a maturing field (first-year 102 CASPs already), reducing first-mover value?

11. **Custody risk.** "Enterprise-grade safekeeping of stablecoins on behalf of clients" — who is the actual custodian/tech, and what's the insurance/segregation model? Custody is where crypto blows up.

12. **Does this move the needle on the company's core business?** Solidgate's bread-and-butter is fiat orchestration; stablecoins are additive. Is there any disclosed revenue/volume target, or is this reputational/strategic positioning only?

Importance: 3/5 — A credible, profitable, bootstrapped orchestrator securing its own EEA-wide MiCA CASP licence is a real, infrastructure-first step and a clean datapoint in the PSP-adds-stablecoins trend (peer to Nuvei/Triple-A/Fipto). But it is one of 100+ CASP authorizations in a crowded 2026 field, self-announced via press release, product-not-yet-shipped, at modest scale ($33.6M revenue), with the PSD2 PI/EMI leg for stablecoin payments unconfirmed. Notable for Solidgate and the thesis, not a market-moving event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Solidgate sits at the intersection of payment orchestration/PSP and the EU MiCA crypto-licensing race. Stablecoin real-economy payments reached ~$401–527bn Jan–Aug 2026, up ~42–63% YoY (per FXC Intelligence / BCG estimates, via axis-intelligence & bitbase, as of 2026) — note the gap between headline on-chain "trillions" (bot/trading inflated) and actual payment flows. Structure of the EU CASP market is a licensing land-grab racing the MiCA transitional deadline of 1 July 2026, after which grandfathered national regimes expire (see [[MiCA transitional window for crypto service providers closes 1 July]]). As of ~19–22 Oct 2026 ESMA's register held ~377–384 authorised CASPs; 154 (~41%) are TradFi firms, and Cyprus/CySEC has licensed ~25 (per outrun.at / carci.org, as of Oct 2026). Why now: the transitional window has closed, so a CASP licence is now the entry ticket to offer EU-wide stablecoin custody/exchange/payments; the single licence + EEA passport ("one licence, 30 countries") is the barrier-to-entry drivers — months of CySEC scrutiny on capital, custody, AML and operational resilience (per note source). Barrier = regulatory capital + compliance build, not technology.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for a PSP/orchestrator — TPV, take rate, acquirer/APM coverage; for a CASP rail — custody AUC, fiat↔stablecoin FX spread, settlement speed (Solidgate discloses none of these). Basis of competition: not price but which licence + which rails a provider owns end-to-end. Two camps are converging on the same CASP licence: (1) crypto-natives adding payments (OKX Malta PI licence, Feb 2026 — see [[OKX secures European payments licence for crypto expansion]]); and (2) PSPs/acquirers adding crypto rails — Nuvei MiCAR CASP (Dec 2025, [[Nuvei receives EU MiCAR crypto-asset services license]]), Worldpay/Checkout.com/Fiserv building stablecoin settlement, and Stripe via the $1.1bn Bridge deal (closed Feb 2025) (per spark.money/coindesk, 2026). Pure stablecoin-payment specialists already dual-licensed: Fipto (PI + CASP via AMF, Jan 2026 — [[Stablecoin player Fipto achieves dual authorisation status]]) and Triple-A (CASP via AMF, Jun 2026 — [[Triple-A secures MiCA CASP licence from France's AMF]]). Solidgate's position: catching up, not first-mover — it connects 100+ acquirers/PSPs/APMs (per note) but is a late CASP entrant versus Nuvei and specialists; moat is the orchestration distribution/merchant base it can now cross-sell stablecoin rails into (switching-cost + distribution moat) `(analysis)`, not the licence itself, which is now held by ~380 firms.

**Comps & multiples.** Solidgate is private and discloses no TPV, take rate or valuation; Tracxn reports "no funding raised" and revenue banded at $10–50M (unverified aggregator, treat as `[UNSOURCED]`), with a stated ambition to reach $100bn annual processing within 5y via the Silverflow partnership (forward target, not actual TPV — per ffnews, 2026). No valuation or round → EV/Revenue, P/S, price-per-merchant all = **no data**; distribution not computed (no comparable disclosed figures). Qualitative internal comps from the base (all CASP/stablecoin-rail precedents, none with public multiples): [[Nuvei receives EU MiCAR crypto-asset services license]], [[OKX secures European payments licence for crypto expansion]], [[Triple-A secures MiCA CASP licence from France's AMF]], [[Stablecoin player Fipto achieves dual authorisation status]], [[Alipay bets on EU stablecoin via Luxembourg license]]. In-line vs peers on licensing status; no valuation outlier can be assessed.

**Risk flags.**
1. *Licence ≠ live revenue* — this is an "announced/authorised" event; the PR lists custody/exchange/payouts/settlements as "enabled," with no live volume, merchant adoption or stablecoin TPV disclosed. Second-order: re-rating on the announcement alone is unwarranted until flows appear.
2. *Commoditisation of the CASP licence* — with ~380 CASPs authorised and Nuvei (a far larger acquirer) already holding one since Dec 2025, the licence is table-stakes, not a moat; the margin accrues to whoever owns distribution + end-merchant relationships, and Solidgate competes here against Stripe/Bridge scale.
3. *Disintermediation / rails dependency* — Solidgate's orchestration value can be captured by upstream acquirers or stablecoin issuers (USDC/USDT) adding native EU rails; owning its own CASP mitigates but does not remove dependence on issuer and network economics it doesn't control.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Signals the EU stablecoin-payment layer is consolidating into licensed orchestrators — the thesis is that PSPs with existing merchant distribution (Solidgate, Nuvei) out-monetise crypto-native CASPs because distribution, not the licence, is scarce. Falsification/trigger to watch: Solidgate disclosing actual stablecoin settlement volume or a named enterprise client within 2–3 quarters; absence of any live-volume milestone by mid-2026 would confirm the licence is a defensive checkbox, not a growth driver.
