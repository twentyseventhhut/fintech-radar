UK fintech Wise is set to reveal a partnership with small business lender Oaknorth as the group looks to make its infrastructure arm a driving force. 
The cross-border finance firm’s tie-up with Oaknorth will see the latter adopt Wise’s international payments engine directly into its own banking platform for small businesses.
Fintechs and challengers have dominated the small business lending market after high street giants retreated. Research from the British Business Bank puts challenger banks on around 60 per cent of gross lending to mid-sized firms, compared to nearly two decades ago, when the four largest banks made up 90 per cent.
Steve Naudé, head of Wise Platform, said the latest partnership, which is set to go live this quarter, tees the division up for its medium-term target of making up 10 per cent of the broader group’s cross-border volumes. It currently sits at six per cent.
“Longer term, we want this to be more than 50 per cent of the group… if you take the size of the market, the size of that cross-border volume and look at what we process today, there is still a significant opportunity out there,” he added.
The focus on the infrastructure division marks a pivotal shift for Wise, which began as a disruptor to the banking sector, seeking to reduce international transfer fees.
Wise infrastructure division becoming ‘core’ to group
“To really solve our mission to make moving money globally as easy as possible… we will have to do it by powering those customers in the tool they’re already using: their bank, their fintech, their payroll provider, their bill pay platform, whatever their use case is,” Naude said.
“That’s why the platform strategy becomes core to the group.”
Oaknorth, which primarily caters to growing mid-sized businesses and entrepreneurs, recorded $1.4bn in total volume of new loans in the last year, more than trebling 2024’s level. 
The new agreement comes as the bank expands into the US, which made up around 40 per cent of new lending in the last year.
The firm joins Monzo, UniCredit, Morgan Stanley and Standard Chartered in using Wise’s infrastructure.
“Small business banking is one of those spaces that has been quite underserved,” Naudé said.
“Banks have strong retail offerings and strong corporate offerings, and then small businesses can get squeezed between the two.”

# Context-enrichment: Wise partners OakNorth to expand infrastructure division
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**FRESHNESS VERDICT: FRESH.** This is a newly-announced, dated (c. 7 Oct 2026) Wise Platform win — OakNorth adopting Wise's international-payments engine into its SME banking platform, "set to go live this quarter." It is NOT a re-report. The closest prior OakNorth corpus items are about different events: [[OakNorth acquires embedded finance provider Monite]] (Mar-2026, AR/AP automation), [[OakNorth hires in the US ahead of bank acquisition]], [[Federal Reserve orders OakNorth to set up UK holding company]] (Mar-2026) — none covers this Wise tie-up. The closest Wise item, [[Wise rolls out overseas QR payments and eSIM plans]] (same 2026-10-07 date), is a consumer product, a different event. No duplicate. (That said, this IS the Nth entry in a long, near-identical series of "Bank X adopts Wise Platform" partnership notes — see [1] — so its *marginal* news value is modest, which caps importance.)

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- OakNorth (UK digital business/SME lender) will embed **Wise Platform's cross-border payments engine** directly into its SME banking app. OakNorth's target customers are scaling businesses with £1m–£100m turnover. Capability: international payments in **20+ currencies** (EUR, USD, GBP, INR, HKD, CNY…) at the **mid-market rate**, with ~**77% delivered in under 20 seconds** (per fintechfutures/financialit). Source: cityam, fintechfutures, financialit, Oct-2026.
- Status: **"set to go live this quarter"** — i.e. announced, NOT yet live. Treat as a signed/integration-in-progress deal, not proven adoption.
- The PR's strategic anchor is a **Wise metric, not an OakNorth one**: Wise Platform head **Steve Naudé** frames the deal as advancing Wise Platform's medium-term target of **10% of group cross-border volume** (currently **6%**), with a long-term ambition of **>50%**. OakNorth's own usage numbers (expected volumes, pricing to its SMEs) are not disclosed.
- **Why framed this way / what it reveals.** The headline is written from Wise's side ("Wise beefs up its infrastructure division"), because the real story for the market is Wise's *platform re-rating*, not OakNorth's feature add. One level deeper: Wise is a formerly-consumer disruptor converting itself into B2B "plumbing"; naming a single SME-lender win is a proof point in a volume-share narrative (6%→10%→>50%), not a transformational contract. The vagueness that matters: no economics (take-rate OakNorth pays, revenue to Wise, exclusivity, volume commitment) and no live date beyond "this quarter."

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Wise Platform's own prior wins (the real comp set — same product, repeated):** Monzo ("powered by Wise", longest/largest UK partner), UniCredit ([[Wise signs UniCredit for cross-border retail payments]], live Jul-2025), Standard Chartered (SC Remit), Morgan Stanley (corporate settlements), Jenius/SMBC Indonesia ([[Jenius chooses Wise Platform for cross-border payments]]), Wealthsimple ([[Wise integrates with Wealthsimple for cross-border payments]]), GoTyme ([[GoTyme Bank and Wise Platform enable Filipino remittances]]), Banco XP, MBSB, Upwork. Wise Platform now claims **100+ institutions**. → OakNorth is **parity / incremental**: another name on a long list, not a first-of-kind.
- **Cross-border infra / BaaS rivals for the same SME-embedding use case:** Nium (190+ countries, 100 currencies), Airwallex (150+ countries, ~50 direct rails, strong APAC), Thunes (payout orchestration, 130+ countries; Juniper top-3 with Swift/Visa), Banking Circle, Currencycloud (Visa-owned), Ebury, Payoneer; plus Swift itself (which has separately partnered Wise). Stablecoin-native entrants (BVNK, Bridge, Thunes stablecoin corridors) attack the same sub-1% corridors.
- **Position & second-order.** Wise's edge over Nium/Airwallex/Thunes for a *bank* partner is its owned, licensed local-rail network (80+ licenses, ~8–9 direct domestic system connections, mid-market rate) — it sells banks the one thing they can't cheaply build: transparent, near-instant retail/SME FX. **Why the landscape is this way:** banks retreated from unglamorous SME cross-border; the margin on correspondent banking is being arbitraged away, so banks would rather *rent* a cheaper rail than defend a commoditizing one. The second-order question is whether Wise, as it becomes the rail for its own distributors, eventually disintermediates them — or whether stablecoin rails disintermediate Wise.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Wise trajectory toward infrastructure:** consumer FX disruptor → 80+ licenses, 8 direct domestic connections, ~52bps take rate, 4.7m txns/day ([[Fintech Wrap Up inside Wise's products, licenses, rails, and tech stack]]); Nasdaq dual-listing May-2026 explicitly to re-brand as "financial infrastructure" ([[Wise moves primary listing to United States]]); FY2026 net revenue $2.5bn, cross-border volume $243bn ([[Wise reports FY2026 net revenue of $2.5 billion, up 19%]]). Wise Platform is the B2B leg of this pivot.
- **OakNorth trajectory:** SME lender, >$20bn cumulative lending UK+US; **$1.4bn new loans last year (>3x 2024)**; aggressive **US expansion** (~40% of new lending; US unit on track to overtake UK per CEO Khosla); [[OakNorth acquires embedded finance provider Monite]] (AR/AP automation) and [[OakNorth hires in the US ahead of bank acquisition]]; [[Federal Reserve orders OakNorth to set up UK holding company]] (regulatory cost of US push). It has been building a *software-rich* SME banking stack around its lending core.
- **Why each acts this way.** Wise: a commodity FX take-rate (67→52bps) forces it to chase a software multiple → B2B platform volume is the growth lever that justifies the US re-rating. OakNorth: it is bolting non-lending SME features (payments, AR/AP) onto a lending balance sheet to deepen relationships and cross-sell — and it would rather buy/rent payments infra (Wise) than build it, just as it bought Monite for AR/AP. The fit is logical: OakNorth's £1m–£100m SMEs genuinely transact cross-border (esp. with the US push), a segment Wise says is "squeezed between" retail and corporate bank offerings.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low-to-moderate.** The *capability* (Wise Platform embedded cross-border) is years old and deployed at dozens of banks; OakNorth is one more distributor. What's incrementally new: Wise adding a **UK SME-lender** channel and OakNorth getting instant 20+-currency FX it couldn't economically build.
- **Traction: announced, not yet live** ("this quarter"). The only quantified traction is Wise's platform-share figure (**6% of group cross-border volume today**, targeting 10% medium-term) — a real, disclosed operating metric, but OakNorth's contribution to it is unquantified and likely small initially.
- **Who captures the margin (1–2 levels deeper).** Wise captures the FX/payment margin on OakNorth's SME flows; OakNorth captures stickier relationships and possibly a referral/markup. The durable value for Wise is **volume feeding its owned rails** (scale → lower unit cost → more price cuts → more volume — the flywheel). The fragility: Wise Platform revenue is **not separately disclosed** (open question in the FY26 note too), so the "infrastructure division becoming core" thesis is still largely a narrative; and as Wise powers more banks it risks the classic platform tension — is it the *rail* (margin capture) or just a *vendor* banks can swap for Nium/stablecoins when cheaper?

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Next:** go-live "this quarter" (Q4-2026); watch whether Wise discloses Platform volume/revenue separately in future results (it currently buries it). Naudé's 6%→10%→>50% ladder is the KPI to track. OakNorth's US expansion means cross-border US corridors are the likely growth vector for the embedded service.
- **Sentiment / drivers.** Structural tailwind: high-street banks retreated from SME lending (challengers now ~60% of gross mid-market lending per British Business Bank) and from transparent SME cross-border — leaving a gap Wise+challenger-bank pairings fill. **Counterintuitive second-order:** every bank Wise onboards both grows its volume *and* trains the market that cross-border FX is a cheap, rentable commodity — accelerating the margin compression that pushed Wise to chase a software multiple in the first place. And the more banks depend on Wise's rail, the more systemically exposed Wise's "infrastructure" framing becomes to stablecoin disintermediation of exactly those corridors.
- **Risks:** deal is pre-live (could slip/underdeliver); no disclosed economics; OakNorth is small relative to Wise's $243bn volume, so market impact on Wise is marginal; competitive pressure from Nium/Airwallex/Thunes and stablecoin rails.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Cross-border payments is a large, structurally growing pool: estimates cluster around ~$238bn in 2026 revenue at a ~7% CAGR to the early 2030s (per Straits Research / Fortune Business Insights, via web, as of 2026), with the B2B slice the largest (~52% share in 2026). The "why now": incumbents are retreating from SME lending and underserving the embedded-payments layer — UK challenger banks now hold ~60% of gross lending to mid-sized firms vs the big-four's historic ~90% (per British Business Bank, cited in the City AM source). That same vacuum is what Wise's infrastructure arm (Wise Platform) is targeting: rather than own the customer, Wise rents its FX/settlement rails to banks and fintechs. Structure — the rails layer is consolidating around a few scaled networks (Wise, dLocal, Thunes, Nium) because the barriers are high: licences in dozens of jurisdictions, direct central-bank scheme access (Wise is in BoE RTGS, Zengin, etc.), and capital. Second-order effect: whoever owns the rails captures volume economics regardless of which app the end-user touches — the thesis Naudé states explicitly ("power the tool they're already using").

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: cross-border volume (CBV), take rate (bps), and for the Platform specifically the share of group CBV routed through it. Wise FY26 (year to 31 Mar 2026, filed results): CBV $243bn (+31% YoY), net revenue $2.5bn (+19%), underlying income £1,609.2m (+18%), average take rate 52bps (down from 58bps in FY25), 19m active customers (+21%), PBT $660.4m at a 26% margin (SEC 6-K/owners.wise.com). Wise Platform is the protagonist here: currently ~6% of group CBV, medium-term target 10%, aspiration >50% (Naudé, City AM). On $243bn group CBV, 6%→10% implies routing growing from ~$14.6bn to ~$24.3bn (analysis). Basis of competition is distribution + breadth of licences, not price at the infra layer. Named Platform clients now span Monzo, UniCredit, Raiffeisen, MBSB, Capitec, Morgan Stanley and Standard Chartered — OakNorth joins as an SME-lending-led adopter. Position: Wise is ahead among non-bank rails on direct scheme access and disclosed economics; moat = regulatory/licence intangibles + scale network effects (more corridors → cheaper FX → more volume). OakNorth's own momentum is real: FY2025 PBT £223m, total facilities +18% to £7.2bn ($9.3bn), gross originations +33% to £2.8bn, with the US now ~40% of new lending ($1.4bn facilities) (OakNorth FY2025 results). The US push is precisely why it needs third-party cross-border rails rather than building its own.

**Comps & multiples.**
- **Wise** — mkt cap ~$11.95bn (WSE/NYSE, 5 Oct 2026; LSE £8.79bn, 1 Oct 2026, stockanalysis.com) on $2.5bn net revenue → P/S ≈ `$11.95bn / $2.5bn = 4.8x`. In-line-to-rich for a +19% grower with a 26% PBT margin; justified by profitability (not a cash-burning rails play). Mkt cap is down ~17% YoY.
- **dLocal (DLO)** — mkt cap ~$4.33bn (web); Q2'26 TPV $17.69bn (+92% YoY). Pure cross-border/emerging-markets rails; growing far faster but lower-margin, merchant-concentrated. Revenue multiple not computed (full-year revenue not sourced here) → "no data".
- **Remitly (RELY)** — FY25 revenue $1.6bn (+29%), send volume ~$75bn, take rate ~2.13%. NOT a clean comp: ~4x Wise's take rate because it is a consumer remittance front-end, not an infra/rails provider — a reminder that Wise's thin 52bps is a feature of the wholesale model, not weakness.
- Internal comps (grep): [[Wise reports FY2026 net revenue of $2.5 billion, up 19%]], [[Wise FY26 11.3 million customers and £29.4 billion in holdings]], [[Wise serves 11 million customers as volumes hit £47 billion]], [[Mambu teams with Mastercard on cross-border payment integration]], [[Aljazira Bank adopts Thunes for cross-border transfers]].
- Distribution not computed (only one clean public revenue multiple); qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. *Announced vs live / thin disclosure.* The deal "is set to go live this quarter" — not yet adopted; no revenue, ticket size, FX-margin split, or exclusivity disclosed. One partnership moves Platform share from 6% toward 10% only cumulatively; a single SME-lender integration is a small contributor, so the >50% aspiration is far out and unquantified (de-PR: treat as a pipeline signal, not a step-change).
2. *Take-rate compression.* Wise is deliberately cutting price (58→52bps) to drive volume; the Platform/wholesale mix is structurally lower-margin still. If infra becomes "core," blended take rate faces continued downward pressure — volume must outrun price to protect the 26% margin (second-order: multiple de-rates if growth decelerates while take rate keeps falling; the stock is already -17% YoY).
3. *Client concentration in a cyclical adopter.* OakNorth's growth leans ~40% on US mid-market lending entered only in 2023; a US credit-cycle wobble would cut OakNorth's cross-border volume and thus Wise's Platform throughput from this account (disintermediation of Wise's own volume by its client's credit cycle).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a positioning move, not a re-rating: Wise is pivoting from consumer disruptor to the "Intel inside" of cross-border, and banks/lenders increasingly rent rather than build rails (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Platform's share of group CBV is still ~6% at the next 1-2 trading updates despite marquee logos (OakNorth, UniCredit, Capitec), the "core to the group" narrative is ahead of the economics. Trigger to watch: Wise Platform CBV-share disclosure in the next quarterly update, and whether the OakNorth integration actually goes live this quarter as stated.

**Thesis-flags.**
