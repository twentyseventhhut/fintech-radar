**CFO Outlook Commentary**

We are raising our expense outlook to incorporate the $9.9 billion charge related to legal proceedings recognized in the third quarter. We now expect full year 2026 total expenses to be in the range of $175-179 billion.

Excluding the third quarter legal charge, we continue to expect 2026 operating income to be above 2025 operating income.

At a segment level, we now expect Reality Labs losses to decline compared to 2025 to a range of $18-19 billion.

We anticipate 2026 capital expenditures, including principal payments on finance leases, to be in the range of $135-145 billion, narrowed from our prior outlook of $130-145 billion.

**Establishment of New Class C Common Stock**

The Board of Directors has approved the establishment of a new class of publicly listed, non-voting Class C common stock, with an effective date in January 2027.

The approval by Meta's Board follows the unanimous recommendation of a Special Committee of disinterested and independent directors, which was established by the Board to evaluate the company's capital structure. The Special Committee and the Board determined that creating this new class of stock is advisable, fair, and in the best interests of Meta and its shareholders for a number of reasons, including:

*   **Creating a New Class of Shares for Capital Raising, Employee Compensation, Potential Acquisitions, and Other General Corporate Purposes****–** The scale of investment required to compete, sustain growth, expand infrastructure, and pursue new strategic initiatives amidst the AI transformation taking place in the industry is significant. The Special Committee determined this new class of common stock will provide Meta the ability to finance its growth and help the company remain a leader among fierce competition both domestic and international.
*   **Continuing to Maximize Long-Term Value for All Shareholders****–** Since Meta's formation, its management team has prioritized long-term goals and value creation, and its stock price has increased by more than 1,700% since its IPO. By contrast, over the same period, the S&P 500 has increased approximately 495%. The Special Committee wants Meta to continue innovating with an emphasis on long-term growth, which the creation of the Class C stock is intended to help facilitate.
*   **Ensuring Founder and CEO Mark Zuckerberg Continues to Lead Meta into the Next Era –** The Special Committee concluded that Mr. Zuckerberg is best suited to lead Meta into its next era and beyond. They recognized that Meta's track record of significant value creation for its shareholders is in large part due to his strong leadership, unique and creative vision, and exceptional management abilities. The creation of the Class C common stock will continue the founder-led model that has driven the company's success since its inception.

Meta intends to file an amended and restated certificate of incorporation in Delaware to establish the Class C common stock, with an effective date of January 19, 2027. The Board has also declared a one-time stock dividend of one share of Class C common stock for each outstanding share of Class A common stock and Class B common stock, contingent upon, and to be paid after, the effectiveness of the amended and restated certificate of incorporation.

The record date and payment date of the stock dividend will be determined by the Board at a later date. After the payment date of the stock dividend, we expect the market price for our Class A common stock to reflect the effect of a two-for-one stock split. The stock dividend will not change the voting power of any shareholder.

The amended and restated certificate of incorporation has been approved by shareholder written consent. We have also entered into a Founder Alignment Agreement with Mr. Zuckerberg, which has terms related to his sales of Class C common stock received in the stock dividend, his service at the company, and conditions for potential future stock dividends of Class C common stock.

Meta's Class A common stock will continue to trade on Nasdaq, and we intend to qualify the Class C common stock for listing on Nasdaq upon its issuance as a result of the stock dividend. More information will be available in our Report on Form 8-K and information statement to be filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission.

[0] What happened. Meta Platforms (Nasdaq: META) reported Q3 2026 (quarter ended Sept 30, 2026) on Oct 21, 2026: revenue +26% YoY to a record $64.60B (vs ~$63.3B consensus), of which advertising delivered ~$63B (+26%). Ad impressions rose +10% YoY and average price per ad +14% YoY; Family daily active people reached 3.64B (+3%). Reported operating income fell 37% YoY to $12.99B and operating margin compressed from 40% to 20% — but that is almost entirely driven by a one-off $9.89B litigation charge and a surge in AI/infrastructure costs (total costs +68% to $51.6B). Net income was $9.86B (+264% YoY off a depressed Q3 2025 base that carried an unusually high 87% tax rate); diluted EPS $3.83.

[1] The real earnings story (strip the noise). The headline "net income 3.6x" and "margin halved" are both distorted by non-operating items. Excluding the $9.89B legal charge, operating income would have been $22.88B and EPS $7.40 — crushing the ~$5.89 Street estimate. The $9.89B charge is this-quarter's portion of an August 2026 settlement with 52 state attorneys general over teen mental-health / social-media harms, with total payments of up to ~$18.59B still to come. The Q3 2025 base year had a one-time 87% effective tax rate, which is why YoY net income growth looks enormous; the underlying operating business is the signal, not the +264%. Reality Labs revenue was just $0.44B with an operating loss of ~$(4.13)B; full-year RL loss guidance was set at $18-19B (narrowing toward ~$15B in 2027 as spend reallocates to AI).

[2] The scare: AI cash burn. Capex (incl. finance-lease principal) was $34.91B in the quarter; FY2026 capex guidance was narrowed and raised at the low end to $135-145B (from $130-145B). Crucially, Q3 free cash flow turned NEGATIVE at ~$(2.12)B — the market's focus. Despite the revenue beat, META fell ~7% after the print (from ~$697 to ~$649) as investors digested negative FCF plus the legal charge. Q4 revenue guided to $73-76B; FY2026 total expenses raised to $175-179B to absorb the legal charge. Separately, the board announced a new non-voting Class C common stock (effective Jan 2027) via a 1-for-1 stock dividend functioning as a 2-for-1 split, explicitly to finance AI investment while preserving Zuckerberg's founder-led control.

[3] Fintech angle — this is where Meta matters to payments. On the call Meta framed AI agents as a commerce/payments engine: >1M businesses now use Meta's AI agents weekly on WhatsApp and Messenger; the biggest Muse use case is shopping (>25% of Muse users, avg purchase >$200), with Meta monetizing via transaction fees paid by businesses, not consumers. Meta is co-developing a Personal Agent Protocol for secure, permissioned agentic transactions with Walmart, Stripe and Shopify — tying directly to its separate "Meta and Sierra introduce Personal Agent Protocol standard" note and the "Meta's Muse hits 10M US downloads in 40 days" note. "Family of Apps other revenue" hit $1.1B (+59%), driven by WhatsApp paid messaging/subscriptions — the clearest line item showing WhatsApp becoming a revenue (and commerce) rail. This sits atop Meta's broader fintech push: stablecoin comeback [[Meta plans stablecoin comeback in second half of year]], USDC creator payouts via Stripe [[Meta begins paying creators in USDC via Stripe]], Stripe checkout in Facebook ads [[Stripe brings new checkout experience to Facebook ads]], and the India payments play [[Meta invests $900M in India payments, taps CRED founder Kunal Shah]].

[4] Why it matters / what to watch. Meta is the single largest corporate AI-capex spender outside the hyperscalers' cloud units, and its willingness to run FCF negative sets the tone for the whole AI capex super-cycle — a read-through for chips, power and data centers. For fintech specifically: agentic commerce (Muse + Personal Agent Protocol + Stripe/Shopify/Walmart) is Meta's path to diversify beyond ads and insert itself into the payments stack via transaction fees — a potential threat/partner to card networks and checkout players. Watch: (a) whether WhatsApp paid-messaging/commerce "other revenue" keeps compounding ~60%; (b) agent-commerce take rate and whether Stripe/Shopify remain partners vs. competitors; (c) the still-outstanding ~$8.7B remaining of the AG settlement and pending youth-related trials; (d) FCF trajectory into 2027 given uncapped AI spend. **Market read (fintech angle: ad economics, AI-capex arms race, WhatsApp/agentic commerce).**

**IR-grounded Q3 2026 print (primary; [Meta IR press release, 2026-10-21](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)).** Revenue $64.60B (+26% YoY, reported and constant-currency); ad revenue ~$63B (+26%). The ad engine is the story that matters for fintech: impressions +10% and average price per ad +14% — i.e. pricing, not volume, drove most of the gain, which Meta explicitly attributes to AI-driven ranking/targeting. Family DAP 3.64B (+3%). Reported operating income fell to $12.99B with operating margin collapsing from 40% to 20%, but that is almost entirely a one-off: a **$9.89B charge** from the August 2026 settlement with 52 state AGs over teen mental-health litigation (total exposure up to ~$18.59B). Ex-charge, operating income would have been $22.88B (margin ~35%) and diluted EPS $7.40 vs the reported $3.83. Net income $9.86B (+264%) is flattered by a prior-year tax-base distortion (FY25 effective tax rate 87% → 24% this quarter), so the headline "profit up 3.6x" is a comp artifact, not operating momentum. Headcount 66,775, down 15% YoY.

**The actual market verdict was negative, not the +26% headline.** Despite beating on revenue and EPS, the stock fell ~7% the day after and was down ~14.5% from its Oct-13 peak of ~$762 to ~$643, dragging the Nasdaq to its worst session since June ([CNBC, 2026-10-22](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/22/meta-stock-q3-earnings-free-cash-flow.html); [Investor's Business Daily via CNBC coverage]). The trigger: **capex $34.91B in the quarter now exceeds operating cash flow of $32.79B, pushing free cash flow to negative $2.12B — Meta's first negative FCF since its 2012 IPO** (IR release, confirmed by [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/22/meta-stock-q3-earnings-free-cash-flow.html)). FY26 capex guidance was *narrowed up* to $135–145B (from $130–145B), and Meta signalled it will "continue investing aggressively" in 2027 without a revenue outlook — the market read this as spend accelerating with no committed return date.

**AI-capex arms race — context from in-base + web.** Meta's $135–145B sits inside a ~$735B 2026 hyperscaler capex pool (Amazon ~$220B, Google ~$195–205B, Microsoft ~$175B), up ~79% from ~$410B in 2025, with 2027 projections pushing toward $1.4T ([Yahoo Finance / UBS](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/hyperscalers-hit-700-billion-2026-111243744.html)). Base skepticism on this is already on record: [[News/2026-06/JPMorgan warns AI trade echoes dot-com-era bubble]] and [[News/2026-06/Linas Newsletter Coatue's $12 trillion AI bet report]]. Meta is uniquely exposed because — unlike AWS/Azure/GCP — it has **no external cloud revenue to recycle capex into**; its monetization is entirely downstream in ads. The bull case (and Meta's own framing) is that AI already pays for itself via the +14% ad pricing; the bear case is that a social-ads-only balance sheet cannot indefinitely absorb data-center capex that now outruns operating cash flow. Note the simultaneous creation of non-voting Class C stock "for capital raising" — a structural tell that Meta is pre-positioning to fund this build with equity/debt beyond internal cash.

**Fintech/payments optionality — thin today, strategically real.** Ads remain ~98% of revenue, so the payments/agentic-commerce angle is optionality, not a current line item. But the Q3 call frames "new revenue opportunities" from AI products, and the in-base trail shows a coherent commerce stack forming: [[News/2026-10/Meta and Sierra introduce Personal Agent Protocol standard]] (open agent-commerce standard co-developed with **Stripe, Shopify, Walmart, Genesys** — the payments/checkout rails for agentic buying), [[News/2026-10/Meta's Muse hits 10M US downloads in 40 days]] (consumer AI-agent distribution), WhatsApp Business Agent token-billing at $2/1M tokens (~4–5c/interaction, [CNBC, 2026-06-03](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/03/meta-business-agent-is-zuckerberg-latest-effort-to-diversify-from-ads.html)), and the [[News/2026-06/Meta invests $900M in India payments, taps CRED founder Kunal Shah]] / WhatsApp Pay push. If agentic commerce on WhatsApp/Instagram converts, Meta captures ad + lead-gen + messaging-fee + eventual payment-flow economics — but every one of those lines is pre-revenue-scale today and depends on the same capex it is being punished for.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Negative FCF regime.** Capex ($34.9B/qtr) now exceeds operating cash flow; first negative FCF since IPO. If 2027 capex ramps as guided while ad growth normalizes off the +26% comp, the cash gap widens — ad-pricing gains (+14%) are cyclical/finite and cannot compound forever.
2. **Ads-only funding model vs cloud-backed peers.** No external cloud revenue to amortize AI infrastructure; monetization is 100% indirect. Highest-risk balance-sheet profile among the hyperscalers if AI-ROI thesis slips.
3. **Litigation overhang is structural, not one-off.** The $9.89B teen-safety charge is the recognized piece of up-to-$18.59B total; CFO flagged *further* youth-related trials scheduled in coming months in the U.S. Future charges are plausible and recurring, not settled.
4. **Governance/dilution signal.** New non-voting Class C stock + Founder Alignment Agreement entrenches Zuckerberg control while explicitly enabling capital raising — markets may read persistent equity issuance to fund capex as dilutive and a sign internal cash is insufficient.
5. **Payments/agentic-commerce is optionality, not proof.** ~98% ad dependence persists; WhatsApp/Business-Agent/PAP revenue is sub-scale and itself capex-dependent. Diversification narrative is unproven and years from materiality.
6. **Headline-figure traps.** "+26% revenue / profit +3.6x / record $64.6B" overstates health: the margin reads 20% only because of the charge, and the net-income jump is a tax-base comp artifact — not evidence of improving unit economics.

**Verdict (headline read).** BEAT · revenue $64.60B (+26% YoY; vs public consensus ~$63.3B, +$1.3B / ~+2%) · GAAP EPS $3.83 (+265% YoY) but distorted by a one-time $9.89B legal charge — ex-charge EPS would have been $7.40 (above ~$6.5B-implied/$6.54 consensus) · ad engine still accelerating on AI, but capex guidance raised and free cash flow turned negative. Top-line and ex-charge profitability beat; the market reaction was negative-to-mixed (shares -5% to -7% on Oct 22 after an initial +2.5% pop) as rising AI capex and negative FCF overshadowed the beat.

**Key figures (with growth).**
- Revenue $64.601B, +26% YoY (vs $51.242B in Q3 2025), +26% on both reported and constant-currency basis — ad revenue $63B, +26%.
- Costs and expenses $51.613B, +68% YoY (vs $30.707B) — inflated by the $9.89B litigation charge.
- Income from operations $12.988B, -37% YoY (vs $20.535B); operating margin 20% vs 40% a year ago. Excluding the legal charge, operating income would have been $22.88B (+11% YoY) and the margin would have held near 2025 levels.
- Net income $9.860B, +264% YoY (vs $2.709B — note the Q3 2025 base was itself depressed by an 87% effective tax rate / $18.954B tax provision tied to a one-time item). GAAP diluted EPS $3.83 (+265%); ex-charge EPS $7.40.
- Provision for income taxes $3.141B; effective tax rate 24% (vs 87% in Q3 2025). Ex-charge rate would have been ~17%.

**By segment / driver.** Growth is entirely ad-engine-driven: ad impressions +10% YoY and average price per ad +14% YoY — i.e. roughly half the revenue growth from volume, half from pricing, with pricing the larger contributor. Management attributes the pricing/engagement lift to AI recommendation and targeting improvements. Family daily active people (DAP) reached 3.64B for September 2026, +3% YoY — modest user growth, so monetization (ARPU/pricing), not audience expansion, is carrying the quarter. Reality Labs continues to run large losses (full-year 2026 guided to $18-19B, now expected to decline vs 2025). Capex was $34.91B in the quarter, exceeding $32.79B operating cash flow and driving free cash flow to -$2.12B.

**vs expectations / prior period.** Revenue beat public consensus of ~$63.3B (MarketBeat/aggregators) by ~$1.3B (~+2%). GAAP EPS $3.83 vs ~$3.57 consensus (+7%); ex-charge $7.40 vs ~$6.54 implied. YoY momentum is strong and roughly in line with the prior-quarter trajectory (constant-currency +26%). No prior Meta Platforms earnings note exists in the corpus for a direct [[wikilink]] trend comparison; prior-period figures are taken from the release's own Q3 2025 YoY column.

**Guidance / forward.** Q4 2026 revenue guided to $73-76B (FX a ~1% YoY headwind). Full-year 2026 total expenses raised to $175-179B to absorb the $9.9B legal charge. Full-year 2026 capex narrowed to $135-145B (from $130-145B) — a higher floor, confirming AI infrastructure spend keeps climbing. Management tone (Zuckerberg/CFO): confident on core ad business and AI ROI ("AI investments already delivering substantial incremental advertising revenue"), but explicitly flags longer payback on MSL/superintelligence investments and continued aggressive spend into 2027 — a soft pre-warning on 2027 margins. Capital-structure action: new non-voting Class C common stock approved (effective Jan 2027, functions as a ~2-for-1 split), extending founder control and funding flexibility for AI capex.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. AI-spend ROI vs cash drain. Fact: capex $34.91B > $32.79B operating cash flow → FCF -$2.12B, with FY capex floor raised to $135B. Why: Meta is now spending more than it earns in cash on AI data centers. Why it matters: the bull case rests on AI monetization (the +14% ad-price lift is the first tangible proof), but the market punished the stock (-5% to -7%) because returns lag spend and 2027 capex is signaled higher still. Second-order: negative FCF plus a new share class raises the odds of external financing / debt issuance to fund the buildout.
2. Margin optics vs underlying. Fact: GAAP operating margin collapsed to 20% from 40%, but ex the $9.89B one-off it held ~35%+. Why it matters: the headline margin crash is a one-time legal charge, not operating deterioration — de-PR cuts both ways here, the real erosion is from the +68% cost growth that persists even ex-charge. Second-order: with FY expenses guided $175-179B and capex rising, depreciation and compute costs will keep pressuring margins into 2027 even without legal items.
3. Monetization is pricing-led, not user-led. Fact: DAP only +3%, but price/ad +14%. Why it matters: a maturing user base means growth depends on continued AI-driven pricing/engagement gains; any plateau in ad-price elasticity would decelerate revenue fast. Second-order: regulatory/youth-safety overhang (the $18.59B AG settlement, more youth trials scheduled) is a live tail risk to the engagement model that funds everything.
4. Governance. Fact: non-voting Class C stock entrenches Zuckerberg control while enabling share-based funding for AI. Why it matters: reduces minority-shareholder influence precisely as capital intensity and spend discipline become the key debate.
