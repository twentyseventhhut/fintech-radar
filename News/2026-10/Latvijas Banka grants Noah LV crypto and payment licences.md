Latvijas Banka grants Noah LV SIA crypto-asset and payment licences
Stefana Apetrei
06 Oct 2026 / 5 Min Read
Latvijas Banka has granted Noah LV SIA two authorisations: a MiCA crypto-asset licence and a payment institution licence.
The permissions bring digital asset and payment services under one licensed entity. Each falls under its own regulatory regime, and the company now holds approval under both.
Licences scope
The crypto-asset licence was issued under the requirements of the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA). It covers four activities: Noah LV SIA may hold digital assets in custody and administer them for clients, exchange them for funds, execute orders on customers’ behalf, and provide transfer services.
These approvals cover the main stages of a client's asset activity: safekeeping, conversion into funds, order execution, and movement of assets. That range lets the company act as a custodian and as an intermediary for its customers.
The second permission allows Noah LV SIA to operate as a payment institution, covering the execution of payment transactions, credit transfers, and standing orders. With both licences, the company can manage crypto-assets alongside the execution of fund-based payments.
MiCA passporting and Latvia's licensed market
According to Latvijas Banka, it has issued permissions to 12 companies under the requirements of MiCA. Separately, 13 companies in Latvia hold a payment institution licence.
MiCA's cross-border framework gives the Latvian authorisation reach beyond the domestic market. A crypto-asset service provider certified in one EU member state under MiCA can operate throughout the EU using the cross-border notification mechanism. A licence granted by Latvijas Banka can therefore serve as the basis for Noah LV SIA to offer its authorised crypto-asset activities in other EU markets once that notification process is complete.
Alongside the announcement, Latvijas Banka pointed applicants to its licensing guides . The guides set out the licensing process and requirements, including the documents applicants must prepare and submit, and the deadlines for reviewing applications.
Furthermore, the central bank also offers pre-licensing consultations. In these sessions, Latvijas Banka's experts explain the applicable legal framework and make an initial assessment of a company's compliance with regulatory requirements. According to the regulator, working together at this stage helps applicants submit better-prepared applications and helps the process run without disruption.

# Context-enrichment: Latvijas Banka grants Noah LV crypto and payment licences
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On **30 Sep 2026** the Supervision Committee of **Latvijas Banka** (the Bank of Latvia) decided to issue **two** authorisations to **Noah LV SIA** — the Latvian subsidiary of UK-based **Noah HQ Ltd** (stablecoin cross-border payments infra, founder/CEO Shah Ramezani, president Thijn Lamers ex-Adyen). Publicly reported ~1–6 Oct 2026 (thepaypers 06 Oct; LSM 01 Oct).
- **(1) MiCA crypto-asset service licence** — four CASP activities: (a) **custody & administration** of crypto-assets for clients, (b) **exchange** of crypto-assets for funds (fiat), (c) **execution of orders** on clients' behalf, (d) **transfer services** for crypto-assets. This is the standard custody+exchange+transfer bundle — NOT a stablecoin-issuer (ART/EMT) authorisation; Noah is a service provider/orchestrator, not a token issuer.
- **(2) Payment institution (PI) licence** — execution of **payment transactions, credit transfers, standing orders** (i.e. PSD2-style fiat payment services).
- **Why this dual structure matters:** Noah's whole product is fiat-in → convert to stablecoin → move → fiat-out across local rails. That flow legally requires BOTH a crypto-asset authorisation (to custody/exchange/transfer the stablecoin leg) AND a payment-services authorisation (to touch the fiat leg). Holding both **under one entity in one member state** is the point — it lets Noah run the full fiat↔stablecoin↔fiat loop compliantly end-to-end rather than stitching third-party licence-holders into the chain. **Why structured this way:** this is a licence-acquisition/footprint event, not a product launch — the deliverable is a *legal passport*, the substance is paperwork, and there is zero traction (GMV/clients/volume) disclosed because none is implied by a licence grant.
- **Why Latvia:** Latvijas Banka has positioned itself as a fast, consultative MiCA gateway (pre-licensing consultations, published guides). Noah is the **12th MiCA CASP** licensed in Latvia; separately **13 firms** hold a Latvian PI licence. Latvia is a deliberate "licence-shopping" jurisdiction for the passport — cf. BVNK via Malta, Circle via France, Nuvei's EU CASP. (The thepaypers framing — "brings both services under one licensed entity" — is accurate but is a structural fact, not a competitive scoop.)

## [1] Competitors / peers
Direct comps are other stablecoin-payments firms racing for a MiCA passport:
- **BVNK** — MiCA CASP from **Malta (MFSA), Feb 2026**, stacked on a Maltese **EMI** licence + SEPA access via Bank of Lithuania CENTROlink; now Mastercard-owned (deal closed Aug 2026). The scaled benchmark — same "EMI/PI + CASP in one regulated stack" play Noah is now assembling, but at far greater scale/backing. See [[PayDo partners with BVNK to add stablecoin payment capabilities]].
- **Nuvei** — EU **CASP + payment-institution** licences under MiCA, **Jan 2026** — the near-identical dual-licence structure to Noah's. See [[Nuvei secures EU CASP licence under MiCA]].
- **Circle France** — AMF MiCA approval, **May 2026**, for custody/transfer of its own USDC/EURC. Issuer-side, passportable across the EEA — a **disintermediation peer**: if issuers hold their own CASP + orchestration, they can bypass middle-layer firms like Noah. See [[Circle France gets MiCA approval for crypto-asset services]].
- **Conduit** — $36M Series A (May 2025, Dragonfly/Altos); Noah's closest EM-corridor rival (LATAM/Asia/Africa payout rails); regulatory-footprint race in parallel.
- Other MiCA CASPs (context, not direct): **Relai** (Oct 2025), **Safello** (Sweden, Oct 2025), **Paybis Europe** / **MOIN Payments** (fellow Latvia grantees, Oct 2026). See [[Relai secures MiCA license to expand crypto access in Europe]], [[Safello granted MiCA authorization in Sweden]].
**Position:** Noah is **catching up** on the licence race — Nuvei and BVNK got the same/stronger stacks 6–8 months earlier; BVNK already has an EMI (stronger than a PI) + SEPA. **Why the field looks like this / second-order:** MiCA's July-2026 grandfathering cutoff forced every serious EU crypto-payments player to grab a CASP in H1-H2 2026 → a licence is now **table-stakes, not a moat**. 102 CASPs existed by Jan 2026; Noah is the 12th *in Latvia alone*. The differentiator is therefore NOT "has a MiCA licence" but *which fiat rails + which EM corridors* sit behind it — and whether an EMI (card/e-money issuance) eventually replaces the thinner PI licence.

## [2] Company history / fit
Noah's trajectory: **$22M seed (Jun 2025, LocalGlobe-led)** → 2025–26 EM-corridor partnership flywheel (Nala, Picnic/Brazil, VelaFi/LATAM, Nafolo/Hurupay/Payd/Africa, PEXX/Asia, Cenoa/Pakistan, Opera MiniPay; chains Gnosis/Algorand/Hyperbeat; compliance Sumsub) → **MiCA + PI licence (30 Sep/06 Oct 2026)** → **$38M seed close via +$16M extension (07 Oct 2026)**, with a NY office / US push. See [[Noah raises $22M to build global stablecoin payments network]], [[Noah and PEXX unlock borderless USD and EUR accounts in Asia]], [[Noah and Nafolo partner to cut Africa remittance fees]], [[Noah closes $38M seed after extra $16M raise]].
**Why now / why structured this way:** the June-2025 seed explicitly earmarked capital for "international licensing across US, Europe, Asia." The Latvia licence is the **Europe leg of that plan executing on schedule** — and it lands **one day before** the funding close, which is not coincidental: an EU passport de-risks the fundraising narrative and the $38M (half of it) is explicitly earmarked to "expand regulatory footprint." Structural logic: in stablecoin cross-border, **the binding constraint is regulatory coverage, not tech** — so the company spends capital on licences and rail integrations, and events cluster (licence → raise → US office) because they are one strategy, not three.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: low.** A MiCA CASP + PI grant is a compliance deliverable, not a product or a technical first — Nuvei (Jan) and BVNK (Feb) did the identical/stronger thing earlier in 2026, and Latvia had already licensed 11 CASPs before Noah. **Real value-add:** (a) an **EU-passportable** crypto-asset + payment stack in one entity → Noah can offer its authorised activities across the EU/EEA via the cross-border notification mechanism without 27 separate approvals ("a Riga licence is an EU licence"); (b) removing a legal dependency on third-party licence-holders in the EU fiat leg. **Traction: none disclosed** — a licence is capability, not usage; zero EU GMV/clients/volume is implied or claimed. **Who captures the margin / second-order:** the licence lets Noah *keep* margin that would otherwise leak to a licensed partner in the EU chain — a cost/control win, not a revenue event. BUT the PI licence is **narrower than an EMI** (no e-money issuance / stored-value / card rails); BVNK's EMI+SEPA stack is strictly stronger, so Noah's EU regulatory position is **functional but not best-in-class**. The durable asset remains the EM-corridor rails; the MiCA passport just makes the European node compliant (analysis).

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Next: activate EU passporting via cross-border notification; pair the licence with the $38M capital to deepen EU local-rail connections; parallel **US push (NY office)** into a far more fragmented, contested regime. Sentiment: **positive but crowded** — MiCA passporting is the single most commercially significant EU-crypto feature and 2026 is the year everyone banked it; a licence is now expected, not celebrated. **Why the market goes this way / counterintuitive second-order:** MiCA's grandfathering cutoff (1 Jul 2026) + ESMA's push to centralise CASP supervision (and scrutiny of light-touch venues like Malta) means **the licence itself commoditises and the competitive edge migrates to rail depth, EMI-grade licences, and scale/distribution** — exactly where Mastercard-owned BVNK and issuer-side Circle lead. So the Latvia grant **neutralises a disadvantage (table-stakes) rather than creating an advantage**; a small, extension-funded firm acquiring a passport in a consolidation phase (BVNK→Mastercard sets the exit template) is **closing a gap, not opening a lead** (analysis). Key risks: ESMA jurisdiction-shopping scrutiny; the PI (vs EMI) ceiling; issuer disintermediation; and that EU go-live/volume may lag the licence by quarters.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** EU MiCA CASP licensing layer for stablecoin cross-border payments. Structure, not size, is the story here: MiCA's crypto-asset regime went fully live when the transition window closed on 2026-07-01 (per ESMA, via industry trackers), and a single CASP authorisation passports across all 30 EEA states via a notification mechanism — the note itself describes Noah's Latvian licence as "the basis to offer its authorised crypto-asset activities in other EU markets once that notification process is complete." Adoption of the regime is scaling fast: ~102 approved CASPs at MiCA's first anniversary (Jan-2026, per BBVA, see [[EU's MiCA marks first year with 102 approved CASPs]]) → ~204 by 18-Jun-2026 → ~280 by 3-Jul-2026 (per ESMA interim register, via web). Latvijas Banka has granted MiCA permissions to 12 firms and PI licences to 13 (per the note). TAM context for the underlying flow (from the sibling note's PR, see [[Noah closes $38M seed after extra $16M raise]]): ~$208tn cross-border flow in 2025, B2B stablecoin ~$226bn annualised (+733% YoY) but <1% of the ~$34.8tn B2B cross-border total. Structure: the CASP/PI licence is a regulatory moat input, not a market — fragmented across 27+ national regulators, where barriers are compliance capital + regulator relationship, not technology. **Why now:** the 2026-07 MiCA deadline forced the whole EU stablecoin field to pick a licensing jurisdiction in 2025–26; Latvia is explicitly marketing itself as a fast-track gateway (free pre-licensing consultations, ~48h response, licences reportedly down to ~3 months, supervisory fee capped at 0.6% of gross revenue — per Latvijas Banka/Finextra/BalticTimes via web). Second-order: MiCA converts "which countries can I serve" from a 27-way licensing slog into a single authorisation + notification — collapsing the regulatory-coverage moat that mid-market rails players (incl. Noah) had been building corridor-by-corridor, and inviting larger entrants to passport in.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for the licence layer: jurisdictions/EEA-passport coverage, CASP activity scope (custody / exchange / order-execution / transfer), whether a Payment-Institution (PI/EMI) licence sits alongside the CASP (the dual-licence combo that lets a firm move both stablecoins and fiat under one roof), and direct SEPA access. Basis of competition = regulatory breadth + fiat-rail integration + institutional trust, not price. Dual-licence peers already ahead of Noah on this exact axis:
- **BVNK** — CASP licence in Malta (Feb-2026), already an e-money institution there with direct SEPA access via Bank of Lithuania CENTROlink; markets the MiCA + EUR-payments + SEPA triple as its edge (see [[BVNK secures MiCA licence in Malta]]). Now Mastercard-owned (closed Aug-2026).
- **Triple-A** — CASP from France's AMF (Jun-2026) across all 30 EEA states PLUS a PI licence — one of only two of 19 AMF CASP holders with both (see [[Triple-A secures MiCA CASP licence from France's AMF]]). AMF is a more demanding "institutional-grade" badge than Latvia.
- **Safello** (Sweden, Oct-2025, see [[Safello granted MiCA authorization in Sweden]]), **Relai** (Oct-2025, see [[Relai secures MiCA license to expand crypto access in Europe]]), **Nuvei**, **Crossmint**, **Fipto** (dual authorisation), **Banking Circle** (CASP + stablecoin settlement) — a crowded EEA CASP field.
Scaled-rail incumbents that can passport the same corridors: **Bridge** (Stripe, ~$1.1bn acq.) now rolling Visa stablecoin cards across 100+ countries; **BVNK** ~$30bn annualised volume; **Conduit** >$10bn annualised, 100+ payout countries (per web). **Noah's position:** a credible but late/small entrant on the licence axis — it gets the dual CASP + PI combo (the "good" structure BVNK/Triple-A already hold), but as Latvia's 12th CASP, after the field, and via the fast-track jurisdiction rather than a prestige regulator. Moat (analysis): the licence is table-stakes catch-up, not differentiation — the durable edge, if any, remains Noah's emerging-market corridor/local-rail density (claimed 150+ markets / 60+ currencies), which MiCA does NOT help with (MiCA only covers the EEA).

**Risk flags.**
1. **MiCA erodes, not builds, the moat.** Noah's whole pitch is corridor-by-corridor regulatory coverage; MiCA's single-passport design lets any CASP (incl. deep-pocketed Bridge/Stripe, Mastercard-BVNK) serve all 30 EEA states off one licence — so the EU regulatory "edge" is commoditised the moment competitors passport in. Second-order: the licence defends EU *access* but does not create EU *differentiation*.
