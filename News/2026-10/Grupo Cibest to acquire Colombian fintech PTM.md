## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Grupo Cibest (the holding over Bancolombia, Nequi, Wenia, Wompi and the Central American subsidiaries) filed with Colombia's antitrust/consumer regulator SIC a plan to buy **100%** of **Comercial Card S.A.S.** (brand **PTM**, Medellín, led by Sebastián Tobón Palacio, family-held) through its vehicle **Estrategias Cibest**. PTM is a **banking-correspondent AGGREGATOR**: it manages networks of ~**20,000 merchants** across **900+ municipalities / 32 departments**, turning corner shops into cash-in/cash-out/bill-pay points. Crucially it is **multi-bank** — traditional correspondent for Bancolombia, digital correspondent for Nequi, Grupo Aval, Davivienda/DaviPlata, BBVA, Banco Agrario.
- Status: **announcement + SIC filing only.** NOT closed, NOT approved. **Price undisclosed** (open). Earliest dated reporting is **2026-09-17** (Halcones y Palomas), broad wave **2026-09-30** (Valora, LatamFintech); the 2026-10-07 note is a re-report of the same event (see freshness note below).
- **Why the PR anchors to the SIC filing's figures:** the coverage leads with Bancolombia's Dec-2025 dominance of the correspondent channel — **62.56M operations (74.98% of channel), COP 34.12T (85.44% of value)**, transfers 99.72%/99.67%, cash deposits 88.25%/91.85%, withdrawals 92.90%/94.51%. These come straight from the merger filing and frame Bancolombia as already-dominant. **Skeptical flag:** these are shares of *that specific correspondent sub-channel as measured in the filing*, not Bancolombia's share of all physical correspondent points (one source separately puts Bancolombia near ~10% of a "highly atomized" broader correspondent market). Do not conflate the two.
- **Skeptical flag on PTM size:** one source cites 2025 "facturó COP 2.11T" with net profit only ~COP 20.7B. COP 2.11T is almost certainly **gross transaction volume through the platform, not revenue**; true aggregator fee revenue is unstated (open).

## [1] Competitors / peers
Correspondent-network aggregators in Colombia: **Supergiros** (~20k+ points, allied to Banco de Occidente), **Puntored** (35k+ convenios; 10k-point BBVA alliance), **Efecty**, **Reval, Gana, Moviired, SuRed, Vía Baloto, Movii**. Colombia's correspondent points grew ~16% (473,701→550,482) in 2022→2023; Bancolombia alone reported ~29,000 correspondents end-2023. PTM is a **mid-sized niche player**, not a leader by node count.
- **Why the deal type isn't novel but this instance is:** banks routinely ally into agent networks (BBVA–Puntored, Occidente–Supergiros). What is distinctive is **ownership (not alliance) of a MULTI-BANK aggregator by the channel's dominant bank**. → Second-order: the competition question isn't "is M&A in agent networks normal" (it is) but "can the dominant bank own the neutral rail that also carries its rivals' traffic." That is the real test.

## [2] Company history / fit
PTM/Comercial Card: incorporated ~**2009** in Medellín (family-venture roots ~2004), **100% Tobón family**, no identified outside/VC funding (appears bootstrapped); began operating Bancolombia correspondents ~2015. Cibest side: this is Cibest's **second fintech bolt-on of 2026** — it closed 100% of **Avista Colombia** (libranza lending) on ~2026-08-03/04, also undisclosed price.
- **Why Cibest acts this way:** a clear 2026 vertical-integration pattern — Avista for lending origination, PTM for distribution. Structural logic: as **Nequi** scales toward independent-financing-company status (27M+ users, formal separation expected H2 2026), it needs owned **cash on/off-ramps**; renting PTM's rails is a strategic dependency, so Cibest moves to own them.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Real substance = **owning distribution rails** rather than renting them, in 900+ underbanked municipalities. Traction is genuine (20k active merchants, live Nequi correspondent service) — this is operating infrastructure, not an announcement-only pilot.
- **Who captures the margin / what breaks it:** correspondent aggregation is a thin-fee, high-volume commodity business; the value for Cibest is less in PTM's standalone fee take-rate than in (a) securing Nequi's cash rails and (b) controlling a node that competitors also depend on. **The multiple-breaker:** if SIC forces behavioral remedies (open access, no self-preferencing, firewall of rival banks' data), Cibest keeps the distribution but loses the strategic exclusivity → the deal degrades to a modest, commoditized rail purchase. Conversely, if rivals Davivienda/BBVA/Aval decide a Bancolombia-owned PTM is a conflict, they may migrate off, shrinking PTM's multi-bank volume post-close.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Next gate: **SIC** competition review (plus likely **SFC** financial oversight per Halcones y Palomas); Cibest targets close **before end-2026**. No regulatory decision, and **no public competitor objection**, yet (both open).
- **Why the structural tension matters (analysis):** a dominant bank buying a neutral multi-bank aggregator is a classic **vertical-foreclosure / conflict-of-interest** fact pattern. Counterintuitive second-order effect: the very dominance figures Cibest filed (75%/85%) to describe the channel could become the **ammunition SIC uses to condition or block** the deal. Risk register: antitrust remedies, rival-bank migration off PTM, undisclosed price masking valuation risk.

## Top challenge / red-team questions
1. **What is PTM's actual fee REVENUE** (not the COP 2.11T gross volume)? Net profit ~COP 20.7B implies thin margins — what multiple is Cibest paying? — **open (price & revenue undisclosed).**
2. **Is COP 2.11T volume vs ~COP 20.7B profit** the tell that this is a commodity thin-fee rail, not a software-multiple asset? — Analysis: yes, likely ~1% net margin on throughput → valuation should be modest.
3. **Can a bank with 75%/85% of the correspondent channel own the neutral aggregator** without SIC remedies? — **open**; this is the core merger-control question.
4. **Do the 75%/85% figures describe the whole correspondent market or a sub-channel?** — Sub-channel/filing basis; one source puts Bancolombia ~10% of the broader atomized market. Don't overstate dominance.
5. **Will rivals (Davivienda, BBVA, Grupo Aval, Banco Agrario) migrate off PTM** once it's Bancolombia-owned? — **open**; a credible post-close risk that shrinks PTM's multi-bank volume.
6. **Is there a data-firewall concern** — does Bancolombia gain visibility into rivals' correspondent traffic via PTM? — Analysis: classic self-preferencing/data risk; natural basis for behavioral remedies.
7. **Has any competitor or regulator publicly objected?** — **No, not as of 2026-10-08.** Filing/review stage only.
8. **Is this genuinely new or activation of an existing tie?** — PTM already a Bancolombia/Nequi correspondent since ~2015; the ACQUISITION (ownership) is new, the operational relationship is not.
9. **Why own vs keep renting the rails?** — Nequi's march to independence (H2 2026 separation) makes owned cash on/off-ramps strategic rather than a rentable dependency.
10. **How does PTM rank vs peers by scale?** — Mid-tier: ~20k points vs Puntored 35k+, Supergiros ~20k+; not the market leader.
11. **What's the regulatory path and timeline?** — SIC (competition) + likely SFC (financial); target close before end-2026. Decision date **open**.
12. **Is the undisclosed price hiding valuation risk** or just Colombian private-deal norm? — Likely norm (Avista also undisclosed), but prevents assessing whether Cibest overpaid.
13. **Does the deal advance financial inclusion** materially or just consolidate an incumbent's grip? — Both framings valid; 900+ municipalities reach is real, but concentration is the counter-narrative.
14. **What breaks the thesis?** — SIC conditions (open access/no self-preferencing), rival-bank migration, or a price that proves aggregation isn't worth a strategic premium.
15. **Second-order:** could Cibest's own filing (disclosing 75%/85% dominance) become SIC's ammunition to condition/block? — Plausible; the dominance narrative cuts both ways.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Colombian banking-correspondent (corresponsalía bancaria) channel — low-income/cash-in-cash-out agent networks that turn neighborhood shops into bank touchpoints. Size: ~35,570 correspondent points across 984 municipalities / 32 departments; >900mn transactions processed through the channel in 2025, with >410mn in early 2026 (per Banrep / sector press, via infobae & portafolio, as of 2024-2026). On the aggregate figure cited to the SIC in the note: the channel moved 83.43mn operations worth COP 39.94tn in the Dec-2025 cut (note's own SIC data). Structure: **highly consolidated at the acquirer end** — Bancolombia alone = 74.98% of operations and 85.44% of value in that SIC cut (99.72% of fund transfers; ~88-93% of cash deposits/withdrawals). Barriers: regulatory licensing of credit entities, physical distribution density, and incumbency. "Why now": agent networks are the cheapest rail for financial inclusion in a cash-heavy market, and buying the aggregator vertically integrates the merchant layer Bancolombia already dominates in volume.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: active correspondent/merchant points, transactions per point, transaction value, and take/commission per transaction (PTM/Comercial Card is an *aggregator* — its economics are fees on managed flow, not float). Key players: aggregators managing agent networks (PTM/Comercial Card ~20,000 merchants, 900+ municipalities) sitting between credit institutions (Bancolombia, Grupo Aval, Davivienda/DaviPlata, BBVA, Banco Agrario) and wallets (Nequi). Basis of competition: distribution reach, number of banks served, and uptime/platform reliability — not price to end-users. Recent moves: Bancolombia's Wompi integrated into the correspondent model (2025-06, [[Bancolombia's Wompi integrates into national payments network]]); Banco Serfinanza bought 30% of Tpaga (2026-03, [[Banco Serfinanza buys 30% stake in Colombia's Tpaga]]); Getnet/Santander launched acquiring in Colombia (2026-03). Protagonist's position: Cibest is the dominant buyer of correspondent *flow* acquiring a key *aggregator* — vertical integration from a position of strength (analysis). Moat: distribution scale + switching costs for 20,000 merchants; low intangible/tech moat (the platform is replicable).

**Comps & multiples.** PTM/Comercial Card (founded 2009, Tobón family): last-year revenues "COP 2.11tn" and net profit COP 20.725bn (per halconesypalomas/valoraanalitik, as of 2026-09). **Caution — the COP 2.11tn figure is almost certainly gross managed flow / recaudos, not net revenue** (a ~1% net margin on COP 2.11tn of true revenue would be anomalous for an asset-light aggregator; COP 20.725bn net on fee income is far more plausible) — treat as `[UNSOURCED]` for multiple purposes. **Deal price: not disclosed** to the SIC filing surfaced → EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E all = no data; no acquisition multiple computable. Internal comps (in-base): [[Banco Serfinanza buys 30% stake in Colombia's Tpaga]] (2026-03, 30% stake, price undisclosed), [[Topaz acquires Valid's payments business in Brazil]] (2025-06), [[Rapyd accelerates Colombia expansion after PayU acquisition]] (2025-06) — none carry disclosed multiples, so distribution not computed; qualitative only. Acquirer context: Grupo Cibest FY2025 net profit COP 3.8tn (-38% YoY on Banistmo sale + goodwill impairment; +16.09% ex-Banistmo), ROE 9.09% (17.21% ex-Banistmo) (per elcolombiano/larepublica, 2025). Against that, a bolt-on aggregator is immaterial to group P&L — this is strategic, not financial.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory / antitrust (primary).** Deal is pending Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio review. The acquirer already holds ~75% of operations and ~85% of value in the correspondent channel per the SIC's own data; acquiring an aggregator that also serves rival banks (Grupo Aval, Davivienda, BBVA) concentrates further and invites remedies or a block. Second-order: approval conditions could force PTM to keep serving competitors on arm's-length terms, capping the strategic value.
2. **Channel conflict / customer attrition.** PTM's multi-bank merchant relationships (Davivienda, BBVA, Banco Agrario) sit uneasily under Bancolombia ownership; rival banks may pull volume to neutral aggregators, eroding the acquired network. Dependence on rivals' continued participation is a structural fragility.
3. **Thin disclosure / valuation opacity.** No price, no clean revenue/EBITDA, ambiguous COP 2.11tn figure — impossible to judge whether Cibest is overpaying; execution/integration risk unquantifiable from public data.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Consolidation play: a dominant bank absorbing the merchant-aggregation layer it already feeds — classic vertical integration to lock cash-in/cash-out distribution ahead of Nequi's separation and LatAm expansion (analysis). Watch next: the SIC decision (approve / block / conditions) is the binary catalyst; a conditional approval forcing continued rival-bank service would be the tell that regulators see concentration risk — and would falsify the "clean distribution lock-in" thesis. Trigger: SIC ruling expected before end-2026.
