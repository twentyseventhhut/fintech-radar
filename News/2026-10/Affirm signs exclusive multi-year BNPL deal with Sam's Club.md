## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-10-22 Affirm (NASDAQ: AFRM) announced a multi-year deal to become the *exclusive* buy now, pay later (pay-over-time) provider for Sam's Club, Walmart's warehouse-club chain. Affirm goes live on SamsClub.com "in the coming weeks" (ahead of the holidays) and expands into physical Sam's Club locations in 2027. At checkout shoppers get a real-time, purchase-specific eligibility decision, then pick a monthly plan with the payoff date shown upfront; Affirm reiterates no late fees and no compounding interest. Reporting notes down payments may be required on purchases of $500+. Financial terms were not disclosed.

**[1] Why it matters — the Walmart redemption arc.** This is the headline subtext: in March 2025 Walmart *dropped* Affirm and named Klarna its exclusive U.S. BNPL provider, delivered through OnePay (the Walmart/Ribbit fintech, majority-owned by Walmart). Affirm had disclosed the Walmart relationship was ~5% of GMV and ~2% of adjusted operating income in H2 of the prior year, so losing it was a real dent and a reputational blow. Winning Sam's Club — still inside the Walmart ecosystem but a separate banner — lets Affirm reclaim a Walmart-family relationship and frame rivals' BNPL entry as validation. CEO Max Levchin's public line has been "rivals entering BNPL is proof it already won."

**[2] The demographic logic.** Analysts frame Sam's Club as a *better fit* for Affirm than mass-market Walmart was. Don Apgar (Javelin Strategy & Research): "Affirm was a better fit for Sam's Club based on the more affluent demographic of the Sam's shopper versus a Walmart customer... Affirm's portfolio is more closely aligned with the demographic of the Sam's customer versus a Klarna one." Sam's Club carries an estimated ~60–65M U.S. paid members (Walmart does not disclose exact club membership), and bigger-ticket warehouse purchases (electronics, appliances, tires, bulk) suit Affirm's longer, interest-bearing installment plans better than small-basket Klarna "pay-in-4."

**[3] Competitive / market context.** BNPL is now a land-grab with heavy user overlap: survey data cited this year had Affirm reaching ~50% of U.S. BNPL users, narrowly ahead of Afterpay (~48%), Klarna (~47%) and PayPal Pay Later (~41%), with ~74% of users using two or more providers. Exclusivity deals are therefore how issuers lock distribution. Affirm has stacked several exclusive/enterprise wins recently — [[Expedia and Affirm deepen partnership with US exclusivity]] (US BNPL exclusivity for Expedia/Hotels.com/Vrbo lodging, Jan 2026), [[Fiserv and Affirm join forcesto bring pay-over-time capabilities to debit card programs]] (turnkey debit BNPL to banks), [[Affirm expands Worldpay partnership with embedded payments]], and AI-agent commerce via [[Affirm to support Google's Agent Payments Protocol]] and [[Google adds Affirm and Klarna BNPL to AI shopping]]. See also [[Affirm launches BNPL on Stripe Terminal in US and Canada]] and [[Affirm offers BNPL for rent via Esusu partnership]].

**[4] Watch-items / numbers.** Affirm's fiscal Q (FY2026) report lands around Nov 5 — this deal's framing will be a talking point but contributes ~zero near-term GMV (online launch only in "coming weeks," clubs not until 2027). Markets were muted on announcement: AFRM closed down ~1.5% at ~$72.40 on the deal day, reflecting no disclosed economics and a slow ramp. Key unknowns: the exclusivity's scope/duration, Affirm's take-rate/credit terms with a warehouse-club basket mix, and whether Sam's Club volume can meaningfully offset the Walmart GMV lost to Klarna in 2025.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. "Exclusive, multi-year" — but for how many years, and does exclusivity cover in-club, online, and the Sam's Club app, or only SamsClub.com? The press material is silent on scope and duration.
2. No deal economics were disclosed. Who sets the take rate / merchant subsidy, and does Affirm earn on interest-bearing plans only, or also on 0% APR promos that Sam's Club may subsidize?
3. Timing gap: online "in the coming weeks," clubs not until 2027. How much GMV realistically lands in FY2026 vs. this being a 2027-weighted story?
4. Is this truly incremental, or does it mostly offset the Walmart GMV (~5% of Affirm GMV) lost to Klarna in 2025? Net GMV effect is the real question, not the headline win.
5. Sam's Club member count (~60–65M) is an *estimate* — Walmart doesn't disclose it. How many are active online buyers of Affirm-eligible big-ticket items?
6. Does Walmart's OnePay (which controls Walmart-banner BNPL via Klarna) have any claim or future option over Sam's Club checkout that could dilute Affirm's exclusivity later?
7. Warehouse-club baskets skew to bulk/consumables that don't finance well; the financeable slice is electronics/appliances/tires. How large is that addressable basket really?
8. Credit-quality angle: more affluent Sam's demographic may mean lower delinquency but also lower propensity to finance. Could this be high-prestige, low-volume?
9. Why did the stock close *down* ~1.5% on a "win"? Is the market pricing in no near-term economics, or skepticism about the ramp?
10. Competitive durability: with ~74% of BNPL users multi-homing, does an exclusive checkout slot actually win share, or do shoppers just use Klarna/PayPal elsewhere?
11. Regulatory overhang: BNPL affordability/disclosure scrutiny and the $500+ down-payment requirement — any friction that caps attach rates?
12. Single-source risk: the ingested note cites only retailtechinnovationhub.com (which also pads with award-ceremony filler). Primary = Affirm/Walmart press + Bloomberg/PYMNTS; all corroborate, so the core facts are solid.
13. Does "exclusive BNPL" exclude card-linked installment (e.g., a OnePay or issuer debit-BNPL product), or only third-party BNPL buttons at checkout?

**Importance: 3/5** — A genuine, strategically resonant win (reclaims a Walmart-family banner after the 2025 Klarna loss, good demographic fit, locks a large retailer's BNPL slot). But undisclosed terms, a slow ramp (online soon, clubs 2027), muted stock reaction, and the fact that it partly backfills lost Walmart GMV cap it below a top-tier event. Clearly above routine partnership noise.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Deal in one line.** Affirm becomes the *exclusive* pay-over-time (BNPL) provider for Sam's Club under a multi-year agreement, launching on SamsClub.com "in the coming weeks" and expanding to physical clubs in 2027 (announced 2026-10-22/23). No contract length, economics, or minimum-volume terms were disclosed by either party.

**Why this is strategically larger than a routine merchant win.** This is a direct reversal of a 2025 loss. Affirm had been Walmart's BNPL partner since 2019 but lost that relationship in March 2025, when Klarna won exclusive rights to power installment loans through Walmart-owned OnePay across walmart.com and stores. Winning Sam's Club — Walmart's warehouse-club subsidiary — back as *exclusive* provider claws back a foothold in the same corporate family and into warehouse retail (big-ticket electronics, appliances, bulk household), a high-AOV category well suited to pay-over-time. Sources: [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/bnpl/2026/affirm-inks-exclusive-deal-with-sams-club-as-competition-for-bnpl-shoppers-intensifies/), [FinTech Futures (Klarna/Walmart 2025)](https://www.fintechfutures.com/bnpl-payments/klarna-to-become-walmart-s-exclusive-bnpl-provider-through-onepay-partnership).

**IR-grounded scale (PRIMARY evidence).** Affirm's reported fundamentals frame how material this merchant could be. From the FQ4'26 report (fiscal year ended 2026-06-30, reported 2026-08-27 — this supersedes the stale FY-Q3 figure in our ir_latest.json, confirmed via web):
- FY'26 GMV **$50.2B**, +~37% YoY; FY'26 revenue **$4.26B**; revenue-as-%-of-GMV (blended take rate) **~8.5%**.
- FQ4'26 GMV **$14.1B**, +36% YoY; revenue **$1.17B**, +33% YoY; transactions **53M**, +41%.
- Active consumers **27.8M**, +21%; transactions per active consumer **7.0**, +20%.
- Active merchants **571,000**, +51% YoY.
- Affirm Card GMV **$2.8B**, +124% YoY; active card users **5.2M**, +125%.
Sources: [Investing.com Q4 FY'26 slides](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/affirm-q4-fy26-slides-record-profit-36-gmv-growth-strong-outlook-93CH-4880314), [Affirm Q4 FY'26 earnings supplement](https://investors.affirm.com/static-files/c160ce8f-a9b5-4896-b8ed-6544ac430809), [EDGAR FY'26 ARS](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001820953/000162828026067039/afrm-arsfiling2026.pdf). Context cross-check from in-base earnings note [[News/2025-09/This Week in Fintech Affirm's Q4 FY2025 shareholder letter]] (FY'25 Q4 GMV +43% to $10.4B/quarter; top-5 merchants grew 41% — i.e. large partners already drive concentration).

**Sector context + in-base comps.** Affirm and Klarna are the two pure-play BNPL leaders by US mindshare and marketshare; YoY revenue growth across the sector has run mid-20s to high-70s% ([[News/2025-06/This Week in Fintech is Klarna fintech's main character]]). The distribution land-grab is intensifying across channels: Affirm went live on [[News/2025-08/Affirm launches BNPL on Stripe Terminal in US and Canada|Stripe Terminal]] and into [[News/2026-05/Google adds Affirm and Klarna BNPL to AI shopping|Google AI shopping/agentic commerce]], while Klarna pushed through Fiserv/Clover to ~100,000 US locations. Big-box/warehouse exclusives (Walmart→Klarna, now Sam's Club→Affirm) are becoming the key battleground because PYMNTS notes ~74% of BNPL users carry multiple providers, so merchant *exclusivity* is one of the few ways to capture non-overlapping volume.

**Risk flags (skeptical read):**
1. **No disclosed economics or volume floor.** "Exclusive, multi-year" with no length, no take-rate, no minimum GMV, and no 0% APR commitment disclosed. Exclusivity in BNPL is routinely time-boxed and contestable — Affirm itself just demonstrated this by being displaced at Walmart. Treat as a distribution option, not locked annuity revenue.
2. **Phased / back-loaded ramp.** Online launch is only "in the coming weeks" and in-club not until 2027. Near-term GMV contribution is small relative to a $50B/yr base; material impact is a FY'27–'28 story, exposed to renegotiation/churn risk before it scales.
3. **Merchant-concentration & margin dilution.** Affirm's top merchants already drive outsized GMV; a large exclusive warehouse partner likely carries aggressive merchant pricing (low merchant-discount / 0%-APR-style economics) that can dilute the ~8.5% blended take rate even as GMV grows — watch revenue-less-transaction-costs, not headline GMV.
4. **Credit cyclicality in big-ticket warehouse spend.** Warehouse/big-ticket financing skews to larger balances and longer terms; a consumer-credit downturn would hit delinquencies/charge-offs on exactly this cohort, and Affirm funds loans on balance sheet/via capital markets — funding cost and loss-rate sensitivity are the real constraint.
5. **Walmart-family counterparty ambiguity.** Sam's Club is Walmart-owned, yet Walmart's own OnePay runs Klarna. Overlapping corporate relationships create channel-conflict / renewal risk — Sam's Club exclusivity does not guarantee any Walmart-banner halo and could itself be revisited.
6. **"No late fees / no compounding interest" is marketing, not moat.** Affirm's transparency framing is a consumer pitch, not a defensible competitive advantage; competitors offer comparable messaging. It does not de-risk the economics or the renewal.

**Net.** Credible, reputationally strong win that reverses the 2025 Walmart loss and plants Affirm in warehouse retail — but disclosure is thin, the ramp is 2026–27 phased, and the durability/economics are unverified. Upgrade conviction only on (a) disclosed take-rate/volume or (b) observed in-club rollout in 2027.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
**Financial backdrop (Affirm's own filings).** The Sam's Club tie-up is a merchant/distribution deal, not an earnings event, so it is read here against Affirm's latest reported results. Affirm's most recent filed quarter is **FQ4'26 (ended June 30, 2026)**, reported August 2026 (FY2026 10-K filed, afrm-20260630.htm). Note: `ir_latest.json` still points to the older FQ3'26 10-Q — the figures below are cross-checked against the FY2026 filing.

**Latest reported quarter — FQ4'26 (beat):**
- **GMV: $14.1B, +36% YoY** — beat consensus (~$13.39B). Eleventh straight quarter of >30% GMV growth.
- **Revenue: $1.166B, +33% YoY** — beat consensus (~$1.11B); revenue was 8.3% of GMV.
- **Revenue less transaction costs (RLTC): $589M, +39% YoY** — 4.2% of GMV.
- **GAAP operating income: $147M (12.6% margin)**, up ~6pp YoY — Affirm's profitability inflection holding.
- **Adjusted operating income: $353M, 30% margin.**
- **GAAP net income: $1.617B** — flattered by a one-time **~$1.448B deferred-tax-asset benefit**; underlying operating profit is the $147M figure, not the headline net income.
- **Active consumers: 27.8M, +21% YoY**; transactions/active consumer **7.0, +20% YoY**; total transactions 52.9M, +41%.
- **Affirm Card: 5.2M active (+125% YoY), ~$2.8B GMV (+124% YoY)** — the fastest-growing surface and the strategic relevance point for an in-store Sam's Club rollout (2027).
- 30+ day delinquency on monthly installment loans ~2.5%, improved from ~2.7–2.8% in prior quarters.

**FY2026 full year:** GMV **$50.2B** (vs $36.7B FY25), revenue **~$4.26B**; active merchants ~571k (+51%).

**Guidance (management, as reported):** FQ1'27 GMV **$13.70–14.00B**, revenue **$1.190–1.220B**, adjusted operating margin 28–30%. **FY2027: GMV to exceed $64B**, revenue ~8.5% of GMV, adjusted operating margin >30.5%, GAAP operating margin >14.5%.

**Thesis flags — concentration and take rate:**
- **GMV concentration is the live risk the Sam's Club deal touches.** Per the FY2026 10-K, **top-5 partner GMV concentration was 44%** (down from 47% in FY25/FY24) and **Amazon alone is ~22% of GMV**; no single merchant exceeds 10% of revenue. A multi-year exclusive with Sam's Club (Walmart-ecosystem enterprise retail) is additive volume that diversifies away from the Amazon/Shopify core — modestly constructive for concentration, though it concentrates Affirm deeper into a few mega-retailer relationships.
- **Take-rate watch.** Enterprise/big-box integrations like Sam's Club typically carry lower merchant fees than SMB/Shopify volume; large incremental GMV at thin merchant economics can dilute revenue-as-%-of-GMV (8.3% in FQ4'26) and RLTC margin (4.2% of GMV). The in-store expansion (2027) and 0%-APR/pay-over-time mix will determine whether the deal is RLTC-accretive or GMV-only.
- **Positive signal for the Affirm Card / omnichannel thesis:** physical Sam's Club locations in 2027 extend Affirm beyond e-commerce checkout, reinforcing the +125% Card-active trajectory.

Verdict vs the latest print: Affirm's most recent reported quarter was a **beat** on both GMV and revenue with expanding GAAP operating margin; the Sam's Club exclusive is incremental, diversifying GMV whose margin impact depends on the enterprise take-rate.
