 Both fraud sessions and bot activity decline 
 NEW YORK (Oct. 21, 2026) — Between the second half of 2025 and the first half of 2026, U.S. community banks and credit unions saw a 25% increase in the number of digital banking sessions during which the user attempted to launder money. The average value of those mule transfers increased by 27%. 
 “More than half of mule sessions among our mid-market customers now originate from new devices,” BioCatch Director of Global Fraud Intelligence Thomas Peacock said. “Mules often switch devices to obscure their identity and past transaction history, splitting up incoming sums into a series of smaller outbound transfers to make it difficult for investigators to follow the money. On average, mid-market mules transferred just 38% of the total balance in their accounts per payment.” 
 While mule sessions at community banks and credit unions increased in 2026, both the number of fraud sessions (-11%) and bot activity (-15%) declined. 
 “We continue to see evolving fraud trends, including increased suspected mule account activity and the suspected misuse of legitimate technologies in connection with unauthorized access attempts and payment fraud activity,” St. Mary’s Bank Fraud Manager Lisa Allard said. “By leveraging advanced behavioral intelligence, we are strengthening our ability to identify hidden account relationships, detect emerging suspicious activity, and proactively mitigate fraud risks as these tactics continue to evolve.” 
 More than 250 financial institutions contributed to this dataset. All sampled institutions work with BioCatch , which prevents fraud and scams by recognizing patterns in user behavior. The report also includes a case study following a single U.S. credit union retail account over a 40-day period, showcasing how behavioral intelligence helped to identify that account as a money mule.

## Контекст

### [0] What happened
On Oct. 21, 2026, BioCatch (Israel-based behavioral-biometrics fraud-prevention vendor) released its **2026 Digital Banking Fraud Trends in the U.S. Mid-Market** report, comparing H2 2025 vs H1 2026 across its U.S. community-bank and credit-union customers. Headline: digital banking sessions in which a user attempted to launder money (mule sessions) rose **25%**, and the average value of those mule transfers rose **27%**. Counter-intuitively, over the same window overall **fraud sessions fell 11%** and **bot activity fell 15%** — i.e., the mule/money-laundering layer is growing even as the raw-attack and automated-abuse layers contracted. A paired companion report on Spain (separate source in this note) showed social engineering at 43% of fraud cases / 60% of losses and a 150% jump in stolen-device sessions.
Sources: <https://www.biocatch.com/press-release/us-community-banks-and-credit-unions-report-increase-in-money-laundering-sessions> · report page <https://www.biocatch.com/report-2026-digital-banking-fraud-trends-in-the-usa> · <https://www.biometricupdate.com/202610/biocatch-reports-rising-mule-activity-in-us-social-engineering-fraud-in-spain>

### [1] Why it matters
The mule economy is the cash-out rail for scams (APP/first-party fraud, investment/romance, pig-butchering). Growth in mule sessions at smaller institutions signals that fraud rings are routing laundered funds through community banks and credit unions — thinner fraud teams, less sophisticated monitoring — rather than money-center banks. BioCatch's behavioral-tradecraft detail is the valuable part: **>half of mule sessions now originate from a new device** (fraudsters switching devices to shed history), and mules move only **~38% of an account balance per payment**, deliberately fragmenting outflows to defeat transaction-threshold and velocity rules. That "structuring-lite" behavior is precisely what static rules miss and what behavioral/session intelligence is positioned to catch. This maps to the broader US fraud-mix shift already logged in the vault: first-party fraud now >40% of bank losses ([[This Week in Fintech first-party fraud tops 40% of bank losses]]) and Cornerstone's banker survey putting fraud top-of-mind for 2026 ([[Cornerstone study finds rising fraud at banks and credit unions]]).

### [2] Key metrics / data
- Mule / money-laundering sessions: **+25%** (H2 2025 → H1 2026)
- Average mule transfer value: **+27%**
- Fraud sessions: **−11%**; bot activity: **−15%**
- Share of mule sessions from a **new device: >50%**
- Average balance moved per mule payment: **~38%** (fragmented cash-out)
- Dataset: **>250 U.S. mid-market FIs**, all BioCatch customers; includes a 40-day case study tracking one credit-union retail account flagged as a mule
- BioCatch network scale (end of H1 2026): **>370 FIs, 19B sessions/month, >760M users, 1.8B devices, 3,000+ behavioral data points**

### [4] Open questions / what to watch
- **Vendor marketing caveat:** BioCatch is a seller of behavioral intelligence; "mule sessions up 25%, fraud down 15%" doubles as a sales argument for its product. Treat the directional story as credible, the framing as self-interested.
- **Visa overhang:** Visa agreed in Aug 2026 to acquire BioCatch for **$2.4B** (all-cash, expected close ~Q2 2027). This report is BioCatch's thought-leadership under a pending Visa umbrella — watch whether the dataset/network becomes a Visa value-added-services asset. ## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
### Red-team questions
1. Is the "+25% mule sessions" a count of flagged sessions or of confirmed mule accounts? BioCatch reports *attempted/suspected* sessions — detection volume, not confirmed laundering. Could rise simply because detection improved.
2. Does "fraud sessions −11%" measure real fraud decline, or BioCatch customers deploying more coverage and pushing fraudsters to non-BioCatch FIs? No industry-wide baseline given.
3. Denominator transparency: are the %s like-for-like across H2 2025 and H1 2026, or does the "250+ FI" panel composition change between periods (new customers added inflating/deflating deltas)?
4. "Mid-market" vs the note's "community banks and credit unions" — are these identical cohorts, or does mid-market include larger FIs? The press release and report page use "mid-market."
5. The 38%-of-balance figure: is it a mean, median, or per-institution average? Means are skewed by a few large mule accounts; no dispersion reported.
6. Seasonality: H2 vs H1 comparison mixes different halves of the year (holiday scam season vs post-holiday). Is the 25% a seasonal artifact rather than a trend?
7. No absolute dollar-loss figure for the US cohort is given — only growth rates and "average transfer value +27%." Hard to size the actual harm.
8. Vendor incentive: BioCatch sells behavioral intelligence; "mules rising, catch them with us" is a sales thesis. Is any of this peer-reviewed or externally validated? No.
9. The Spain stats (43% SE / 60% losses / 150% stolen-device) are from a *separate* report bundled into this note — do not conflate Spanish and US figures.
10. Pending Visa $2.4B acquisition: does Visa's ownership bias future BioCatch "network" claims toward Visa's rails/narrative? Report credibility going forward.
11. "More than half of mule sessions from new devices" — is "new device" defined relative to the BioCatch profile only? A legitimate user on a new phone would also be "new device"; false-positive rate not disclosed.
12. The single 40-day credit-union case study is an anecdote, not evidence of prevalence — flagged as "showcasing" the product.
13. Is +25%/+27% statistically significant given the panel size, or within noise? No confidence intervals.

### Importance: 3/5 — Credible, well-quantified read on a real and under-covered threat (mule/laundering migration into US community banks and credit unions), with specific behavioral tradecraft (new-device switching, 38% fragmented cash-out) that is genuinely actionable. Marked down because it is vendor-sourced marketing data with no external validation, no absolute loss figures, an unclear/possibly-shifting panel, and seasonal-comparison risk. Elevated slightly by the Visa $2.4B acquisition context, which makes BioCatch's dataset strategically significant.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Behavioral biometrics for fraud/fincrime is a small but fast-growing niche: ~$3.45bn in 2026 growing at ~27% CAGR to ~$11.4bn by 2031 (per Mordor Intelligence, as of 2026). It sits inside the broader bank fraud/AML-detection stack. Structure is moderately consolidated and tilting toward platform roll-ups: the top five behavioral-intelligence/fraud-orchestration players (BioCatch, LexisNexis Risk Solutions, Mastercard, TransUnion, Thales) reportedly control ~52% of activity (per secondary market research, 2026) — this is a vendor-market claim, treat the exact share as indicative, not audited. Entry barriers are real: scale of labeled session data (BioCatch cites 19bn sessions/month across 370+ FIs as of H1 2026, per its own PR) plus ML models trained on cross-institution network effects. **Why now:** mule/laundering activity is rising even as raw fraud-session counts fall (-11% US mid-market, per note) — human-operated money movement evades legacy rules-based controls, pushing banks toward behavioral detection. Regulatory tailwind from scam-reimbursement liability shifting onto banks (UK APP model, spreading) also drives demand.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: ARR + ARR growth, sessions analyzed/month, FIs deployed, detection precision (false-positive rate). Key players / basis of competition — product depth (passive behavioral signals vs device/location) and distribution:
- **BioCatch** — behavioral-biometrics pure-play; >$100M ARR at end-2023 with 49% ARR growth and EBITDA-profitable (per Permira/press, 2024); 370+ FIs, 19bn sessions/mo (H1 2026 PR).
- **Feedzai** — AI fraud platform; ~$2bn post-money at Oct-2025 Series E (per fintech.global); protects ~$70bn annualized volume.
- **Featurespace** — acquired by **Visa** for $946M, closed Dec-2024 (per Visa 10-Q / press) — folded into a network, no longer independent.
- Others: Mastercard/NuData, LexisNexis ThreatMetrix, Sardine, NeuroID, DataVisor, Cleafy, Outseer.
Recent peer moves: Visa/Featurespace close (Dec-2024); Feedzai $2bn raise (Oct-2025); BioCatch regional network launches (Argentina Jun-2025 [[BioCatch launches fraud intelligence network in Argentina]], LatAm +155% scam attempts Oct-2026 PR). **Protagonist position:** ahead/leader in the behavioral-biometrics subvertical (analysis) — moat is intangibles + network effects from cross-FI data; but it is a point solution facing bundling by networks (Visa) and broad identity/orchestration stacks.

**Comps & multiples.** Private names, so round valuations not market caps. Revenue multiples where numerator and denominator are both public:
- **BioCatch:** $1.3bn valuation at Permira's Sep-2024 majority deal ($750M for 60%); on end-2023 ARR of ~$100M+ → implied EV/ARR ≈ $1.3bn / $0.1bn ≈ **~13x** (ARR grown since, so trailing multiple is likely lower today — directional only).
- **Feedzai:** ~$2.0bn post-money (Oct-2025); revenue not disclosed → EV/Rev = no data.
- **Featurespace:** $946M acquisition (Visa, Dec-2024); revenue not disclosed → multiple = no data.
Internal comps (in-base): [[BioCatch launches fraud intelligence network in Argentina]], [[Incognia report only 16% of firms catch mule-account fraud early]], [[LexisNexis reports eight-fold rise in synthetic identity fraud]], [[This Week in Fintech ACI Worldwide on AI fraud prevention]].
Distribution not computed (only one verifiable revenue multiple; qualitative comparison). BioCatch's ~13x EV/ARR is rich vs mature software but in-line for a 49%-growth, EBITDA-positive fincrime-SaaS name — justified by growth, not an automatic flag.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Disintermediation / bundling by networks** — Visa owning Featurespace and Mastercard owning NuData means fraud detection is being absorbed into card-network rails; a standalone behavioral-biometrics vendor risks margin compression or being designed out of the stack (second-order: banks may default to network-bundled tools).
2. **Data from BioCatch's own PR is the primary source** — the +25% mule / +27% transfer figures are drawn exclusively from BioCatch customers and marketed to sell its product; they are directional on a self-selected base, not an independent industry measure (de-PR: "who's silent" — no false-positive rate or dollar loss total disclosed).
3. **Private, PE-controlled (Permira 60%)** — valuation is set by a single sponsor at $1.3bn, not a liquid market; exit timing/re-rating risk and limited public financials make comp work thin.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** The signal — mule/laundering sessions UP while total fraud sessions DOWN — reframes the fight from "stop the fraudster" to "catch the receiving account," favoring behavioral/network vendors that see cross-institution money flow over point device-checks (analysis). Watch for: consolidation continuing (a network or bureau acquiring a remaining independent like Feedzai or BioCatch at a Permira exit) as the trigger; thesis breaks if banks find in-house GenAI mule-detection adequate and churn off third-party behavioral vendors.
