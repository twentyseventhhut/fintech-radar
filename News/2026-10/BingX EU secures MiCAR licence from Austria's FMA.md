VIENNA , Oct. 14, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- BingX EU (MEGE zweite GmbH) has secured its Markets in Crypto -Assets Regulation (MiCA or MiCAR) license from Austria's Financial Market Authority (FMA), that will enable the platform to offer regulated crypto services across 29 countries of the  European Economic Area (EEA), subject to ongoing passporting arrangements. The authorization marks a significant milestone in BingX's European expansion, providing EEA users with access to digital asset services under a unified regulatory framework built on transparency, security, and investor protection.

 The authorization reflects BingX EU's long-term commitment to responsible growth in Europe. As digital assets become increasingly integrated into the financial system, regulatory clarity plays an essential role in strengthening user confidence, supporting institutional participation, and enabling sustainable industry development. Through its regulated European entity, BingX EU will provide compliant crypto services designed to meet the evolving needs of both retail and institutional users across the EEA.
"Our priority is to provide European users with trusted and locally regulated trading experience," said Daniel Lai, Chief Executive Officer of BingX EU. "Operating under MiCAR will allow us to deliver our services under a clear regulatory framework that gives users confidence while supporting the long-term development of Europe's digital ecosystem. It also enables us to combine innovation with the high standards of security, transparency and accountability that European users and institutions expect. We believe this foundation can help create a more accessible and sustainable environment for the continued adoption of digital assets across the region."
Looking forward, BingX EU will continue working closely with Austria's FMA and other European regulators as it prepares for the launch of its regulated services across the European Economic Area. BingX EU remains committed to operating within Europe's regulatory framework while providing users with secure, transparent and compliant access to digital asset services.
 About BingX EU 
MEGE zweite GmbH (" BingX EU ") is a MiCAR-regulated crypto -asset platform, headquartered in Vienna, licensed as a Crypto -Asset Service Provider (CASP) under the Markets in Crypto -Assets Regulation (MiCAR). Dedicated to serving clients across 29 countries in the European Economic Area, BingX EU delivers fully regulated services in alignment with European standards for investor protection and market integrity. Under its MiCAR authorization, BingX EU provides a platform that offers regulated broker services, such as crypto custody, exchange, and other  crypto services.
 About BingX 
Founded in 2018, BingX is one of the world's leading multi-asset trading platform, serving more than 40 million users worldwide. The company has been the Principal Partner of Chelsea FC since 2024 and became the Official Team Partner of Scuderia Ferrari HP in 2026.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened (dated).** On **14 Oct 2026** BingX EU — the Vienna-based entity **MEGE zweite GmbH** — announced it had secured a **Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCAR) CASP licence from Austria's Financial Market Authority (FMA)**. The authorisation lets BingX EU offer regulated crypto services (press framing: **crypto custody, exchange, and broker/brokerage services**) across the **29 EEA countries**, "subject to ongoing passporting arrangements." CEO of BingX EU is **Daniel Lai**. Notably, **no go-live date was announced** — BingX said it "will continue working… as it prepares for the launch," so this is an authorisation milestone, not a live-service launch (per [cryptobriefing.com](https://cryptobriefing.com/bingx-eu-austria-mica-casp-license/): "BingX EU has not said when services will actually go live"). Global BingX (founded 2018) claims **40M+ users**, is Chelsea FC Principal Partner (since 2024) and Scuderia Ferrari HP Official Team Partner (2026).

**[1] Why it matters.** MiCAR replaced the national CASP patchwork with a single passportable EU licence; the transitional window closed **1 July 2026** (see [[MiCA transitional window for crypto service providers closes 1 July]]). A single national authorisation (here, Austria) passports into the whole EEA, so securing one is now the price of admission for any global exchange that wants compliant EEA retail/institutional access. BingX joins the post-deadline wave of licensees; peers that *failed* to get one (Binance) have been losing EU access and users (see [[Binance struggles after missing MiCA licence, loses users]], [[Tether exits EU market as MiCA blocks USDT from exchanges]]).

**[2] Austria's FMA as the de-facto exchange hub.** BingX EU's choice of Austria is not an outlier — it is the emerging pattern. Prior notes show the FMA has become the preferred domicile for Asia-origin exchanges: **KuCoin EU** (Vienna, FMA-supervised CASP; platform launched 28 Jan 2026 — [[KuCoin EU launches MiCAR-compliant platform across EEA]], with an AML-staffing follow-up [[KuCoin EU hires AML talent to satisfy Austrian regulator]]), **WhiteBIT EU** (FMA MiCA licence, Jun 2026 — [[WhiteBIT EU secures MiCA license from Austria]]), **Bybit EU** (FMA-authorised, bybit.eu — [[Bybit EU works with PayPal to simplify crypto funding]]), and **Bitget**, which hired ex-Bitpanda CLO Oliver Stauber to build a Vienna MiCA hub ([[Bitget taps ex-Bitpanda Legal Chief Oliver Stauber to build Vienna MiCA hub]]). BingX is the latest to pick the same Vienna/FMA route.

**[3] Competitive benchmark (where the big exchanges domiciled).** Different majors chose different home regulators, all passporting EEA-wide:
- **OKX** — Malta (MFSA), authorised Jan 2025 ([[OKX secures European payments licence for crypto expansion]], [[Coinbase and OKX lure EU users as Binance restricts services]]).
- **Coinbase** — Luxembourg (CSSF), Jun 2025 ([[Coinbase secures MiCA licence to serve EU crypto customers]]).
- **Kraken / Payward** — Ireland (CBI), Jun 2025; separately pursuing a Lithuanian banking licence ([[Kraken to pursue European banking licence via Lithuania]], [[Kraken promotes Stavros Vassiliades to COO of EU business]]).
- **Bybit** — Austria (FMA), 2025 ([[Bybit limits EEA access as MiCA deadline nears]]).
- **Ripple** — Luxembourg CASP ([[Ripple secures full MiCA CASP authorisation in Europe]]).
So BingX is a **late entrant** relative to Tier-1 exchanges that were licensed 12–18 months earlier; it is catching up, not leading.

**[4] Base rate / market scale.** MiCA passed its first year with **102 approved CASPs** ([[EU's MiCA marks first year with 102 approved CASPs]]), and the count has grown materially since (e.g. [[YouHodler Italy authorised as CASP under MiCA]], [[BNY expands digital asset custody in EU under MiCA]] in Oct 2026). A CASP authorisation is therefore now a **commoditised, expected credential**, not a differentiator — BingX is one of well over a hundred. Supervisory architecture is also shifting: the EU Council has agreed to route only the **largest** crypto firms to direct ESMA supervision ([[EU Council agrees to send only largest crypto firms to ESMA]]), so mid-tier CASPs like BingX EU remain under national (FMA) oversight for now.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** EU crypto-licensing under MiCAR is now a scale race for jurisdictional access, not a product launch: one national CASP authorization passports into 29 EEA markets. The register has grown fast — 102 approved CASPs at MiCA's one-year mark (per This Week in Fintech, 2026-01-05, [[EU's MiCA marks first year with 102 approved CASPs]]), ~204 full CASPs by 2026-06-18 and ~362 by 2026-09-30 (per outrun.at/casptracker MiCA trackers). "Why now": the PSD2/MiCA transition period ended (supervisory actions from 2026-03, [[EBA sets supervisory actions as MiCA PSD2 transition ends]]), so operating EEA-wide without a CASP is closing off — hence the licence rush. Structure: fragmented at the entity level but converging on a few favored home regulators (Austria FMA, Malta MFSA, Ireland, Luxembourg CSSF); entry barrier is regulatory (capital, governance, ring-fenced EU entity), not technological. Vienna/FMA has become a concentration point — 9 CASPs authorized by the Austria FMA by ~May 2026, plus ~136 passporting in (per tangem.com/FMA data).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: spot/derivatives trading volume, registered EEA users, assets listed, and — increasingly — whether services are actually *live* vs merely licensed. Key players and the FMA cohort BingX now joins: **KuCoin EU** (FMA CASP, launched live across 29 EEA markets 2026-01-28, [[KuCoin EU launches MiCAR-compliant platform across EEA]]), **Bybit EU** (FMA-authorized 2025-05-28; [[Bybit Pay expands into Europe]]), **Bitpanda** (FMA + BaFin + MFSA entries), with **Bitget** still only an applicant (FMA filing 2026-07-02, not yet in ESMA register as of 2026-10-16). Broader licensed set: Coinbase (Luxembourg), Kraken (Ireland), OKX (Malta — which also layered a Maltese PI licence for stablecoin payments, [[OKX secures European payments licence for crypto expansion]]). Basis of competition: regulatory legitimacy + brand/distribution (BingX leans on Chelsea FC and Scuderia Ferrari HP sponsorships) + fee/product depth. BingX's position: **catching up / late-mover** — it is a large global venue (40M+ users claimed; ~$5bn 24h volume, coingecko/coinmarketcap late-Sep 2026) but a laggard on EU authorization vs KuCoin/Bybit, which are already live. Moat is thin: the MiCAR licence is table-stakes parity, not differentiation — at least a dozen peers hold equivalent passports (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/deal in this news (a licence, not a raise) and BingX is private — EV/Revenue, P/E, EV/EBITDA all **no data**. Price-per-user etc. not computable (no disclosed EU financials). Internal comps are structural precedents, not multiples: [[KuCoin EU launches MiCAR-compliant platform across EEA]] (same FMA home, same 29-market passport — the closest template), [[Bybit Pay expands into Europe]], [[OKX secures European payments licence for crypto expansion]], [[Nuvei secures EU CASP licence under MiCA]], [[BVNK secures MiCA licence in Malta]]. Distribution not computed — qualitative only: BingX is *in-line* with the licensed cohort on regulatory status and simply joining a crowded field. [UNSOURCED]: BingX EU revenue, EEA user count, planned fee schedule.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Announced vs live gap.** The PR says the licence "will enable" services and BingX is still "preparing for launch" — no live EEA product yet, unlike KuCoin EU (live since Jan 2026). Second-order: first-mover peers are already acquiring EU users while BingX ramps, so the licence does not by itself close the distribution gap.
2. **Regulatory/supervisory risk now that it is in-scope.** The FMA is an active enforcer — it fined Bitpanda ~€70–81k in the first published MiCA penalty (2026-08-17, theblock/cryptotimes). Being licensed means being supervised; compliance cost and penalty exposure are real, and marketing-communication rules bite (the PR itself carries a MiCAR marketing disclaimer).
3. **Commoditized moat / crowded passport.** With 200+ CASPs and ~9 just at the FMA, the licence confers parity, not advantage; differentiation must come from fees/product/liquidity, pressuring take rate (analysis).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Incremental consolidation of the "licensed EEA exchange" club rather than a re-rating — the marginal CASP approval is now near-routine. Falsifiable thesis: BingX's EU entity is a defensive access play, not a growth catalyst; it *fails* if, within ~6–12 months, BingX discloses live EEA volumes/users materially above the KuCoin/Bybit baseline. Watch: actual go-live date, product scope (spot-only vs derivatives/stablecoin payments like OKX), and any FMA supervisory action. (analysis)
