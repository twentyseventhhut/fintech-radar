Second asset-backed securitization supports continued growth, bringing DailyPay's total funding backed by On-Demand Pay receivables to approximately $1.4 billion 
 NEW YORK , Oct. 8, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- DailyPay , the leader in On-Demand Pay, announced a $200 million asset-backed securitization (ABS) of its On-Demand Pay receivables. The transaction marks DailyPay's second ABS issuance, following its inaugural $200 million securitization in June 2025 . DailyPay is the only On-Demand Pay provider to access the ABS market.
DailyPay enables employers to offer On-Demand Pay as an employee benefit, giving employees access to pay they have already earned before payday, while DailyPay funds the transfers with no impact to employer cash flow or existing payroll processes. This purpose-raised capital supports DailyPay's platform at scale, which serves more than 2,000 employers and over 6 million employees.
"Employers are increasingly embracing On-Demand Pay as a meaningful employee benefit, driving our need for funding capacity to support our growth," said Deepa Subramanian, Chief Financial Officer, DailyPay. "The strong performance of our inaugural ABS supported another oversubscribed transaction, demonstrating continued investor demand for an asset class we pioneered helping reduce our cost of capital and broadening our funding base."
The offering included four classes of notes: Class A, Class B, Class C, and Class D. Morningstar DBRS rated all classes of notes, assigning ratings ranging from AA (sf) to BB (sf), respectively. Citi acted as lead bookrunner and structuring agent, with Bank of America and Barclays serving as joint bookrunners, and KeyBanc, Scotiabank, and Wells Fargo serving as co-managers. Latham & Watkins LLP advised DailyPay, and Mayer Brown LLP advised the bookrunners.
With the latest securitization, DailyPay has secured approximately $1.4 billion in debt financing backed by its On-Demand Pay receivables, including its $960 million secured credit facility, its inaugural $200 million ABS issuance completed in June 2025, and its second $200 million ABS issuance completed in October 2026.
 About DailyPay DailyPay is the leader in On-Demand Pay, helping employers modernize how people get their pay. DailyPay serves more than 2,000 employers and over 6 million employees, including many of the world's most recognized brands. Through access to earned wages and financial wellness tools, DailyPay empowers workers to manage their finances and helps employers attract and retain talent. DailyPay is helping define the future of pay, where money moves at the speed of work. Learn more at www.dailypay.com/press .

# Context-enrichment: DailyPay completes $200M asset-backed securitization
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-08 DailyPay closed its **second** $200M asset-backed securitization (ABS) of On-Demand Pay receivables — "DailyPay Securitization Trust 2026-1". Structure: four tranches (Class A/B/C/D) rated by Morningstar DBRS from AA (sf) down to BB (sf) — i.e. the same credit-enhancement waterfall as the inaugural 2025-1 deal. Citi was lead bookrunner + structuring agent; BofA and Barclays joint bookrunners; KeyBanc, Scotiabank, Wells Fargo co-managers. Latham & Watkins for DailyPay, Mayer Brown for the banks.
- **This is debt, not equity.** No new valuation, no equity raise. It expands *funding capacity* to advance earned wages, nothing about the company's enterprise value. The PR headline "expanding funding capacity" is accurate but the word "raise" should not be read as a venture round.
- **The $1.4B "total funding" figure is a stacking of facilities, de-PR'd:** $960M secured revolving credit facility + inaugural $200M ABS (June 2025) + this $200M ABS (Oct 2026) = ~$1.36B, rounded to ~$1.4B. **Why anchor to $1.4B:** it bundles committed revolver capacity with two term ABS issuances to present a bigger balance-sheet-funding story than any single transaction. Note there is likely partial overlap/refinancing in how receivables are allocated across facilities — the PR does not break out net new fundable capacity, so "$1.4B backing receivables" overstates simultaneously deployable dry powder.
- **Why an ABS rather than just drawing the revolver:** term ABS (a) terms out short-dated receivables into rated notes sold to a broad investor base, diversifying away from a handful of bank lenders, and (b) a rated, oversubscribed deal *lowers cost of capital* vs a bilateral facility. DailyPay explicitly claims the asset class is one "we pioneered" and that it is "the only On-Demand Pay provider to access the ABS market" — a real, verifiable differentiator (competitors fund off balance sheet or bank facilities).
- Operating scale cited: >2,000 employers, >6M employees. These are the same figures repeated across 2026 releases — flag as possibly stale/unchanged headline metrics, not fresh growth proof.

## [1] Competitors / peers
EWA market ~USD 9bn in 2026, ~16% CAGR, moderately fragmented (no vendor >15% share). Named leaders: **Payactiv, DailyPay, Earnin, Wagestream, Instant Financial, Branch**.
- **Funding-structure positioning is the real story.** Payactiv raised >$120M VC by 2020 and funds advances off balance sheet / facilities. Earnin and Branch are D2C / app-led, bank-account-linked, gig-cohort focused. None has tapped the term ABS market. DailyPay's repeat, rated, oversubscribed securitization is a genuine capital-markets lead — it turns EWA receivables into an institutional asset class.
- **Why it matters (2nd order):** EWA is capital-intensive — every advance is a receivable that must be funded until payday. Whoever funds advances most cheaply can price lowest / subsidize the "free to employee" model longest. A ~AA-to-BB rated structure materially cheapens DailyPay's marginal funding vs a competitor stuck on bank revolvers → structural unit-economics edge in a commoditizing, fee-compressed market. The competitive question shifts from "who has the best app" to "who has the cheapest, most durable funding engine".
- Internal corpus: regional EWA peers appearing in the DB — [[Kenya's Jahazii raises $400K for earned-wage access]], [[ABHI partners Saudi Arabia's TRAY to launch earned wage access]], [[Stream raises $90M Series D led by Sofina for workplace finance]], [[Paymenow and PayCurve merge to boost employee financial wellness]] — all far earlier-stage / equity-funded, underscoring how far ahead DailyPay is on funding maturity.

## [2] Company history / fit
Trajectory: Series A ~$5M (2016) → ~$950M total equity+debt across rounds → $1.8B valuation at a $175M round (Jan 2024: $75M equity + $100M facility increase from Citi). Then a clear **debt-funding cadence**:
- June 2025 — inaugural $200M ABS (Trust 2025-1).
- Dec 2025 / Jan 2026 — $195M senior secured revolving credit facility ([[DailyPay announces $195m senior secured revolving credit facility]]).
- Feb 2026 — $200M upsize of secured facility to **$960M total** committed (added TD Bank Group, RBC to Barclays/Citi/TPG Credit).
- Oct 2026 — second $200M ABS (this note).
- **Why this cadence:** EWA economics force a treasury-first posture — growth = more receivables = more funding needed, with no impact to employer cash flow (DailyPay fronts the money). Leadership also turned over amid the funding build-out: [[DailyPay names chairman Nelson Chai as CEO]] (Oct 2025, ex-Uber/Merrill/NYSE CFO profile) and [[DailyPay hires Andrew Brandman as COO]] — a capital-markets-heavy bench, consistent with an IPO-track, balance-sheet-scaling company. Distribution expansion also fits: [[Workday and DailyPay partner on on-demand pay for workers]], [[DailyPay partners Wurk to offer on-demand pay in cannabis sector]].

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new vs prior art:** this is a *repeat* of a capability DailyPay first demonstrated in June 2025, now proven to be programmatic (a "shelf"-like Trust 2025-1 → 2026-1 cadence). Novelty is not "first-ever ABS" (that was 2025) but **"the ABS funnel is now a recurring, oversubscribed program"** — real traction evidence, not an announcement.
- **Traction that is actually verifiable:** oversubscription + DBRS maintaining the same AA-to-BB rating band on the second deal = capital markets validating the receivables' credit performance after a year. That is harder evidence than user-count PR.
- **Where value is captured (2nd order):** the durable moat here is *funding cost*, not the consumer app (easily commoditized). But the margin is thin and shared — banks (Citi/BofA/Barclays) and note investors take the spread; DailyPay captures the difference between employer/employee fees and its blended cost of capital. If EWA fees compress (regulatory or competitive), the ABS edge is what keeps the model solvent. **Risk:** receivables quality is tied to employer payroll reliability and employee churn; a recession raising "employer-facilitated deduction insufficient" events would hit the pool's performance and widen note spreads — eroding the cost-of-capital advantage exactly when it's most needed.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Regulatory tailwind is the under-appreciated driver.** In Dec 2025/Jan 2026 the CFPB issued an interpretive rule that **"Covered EWA" (employer-partnered, no-recourse, free, no credit-reporting) is NOT credit under TILA/Reg Z**, withdrawing the 2024 proposal that would have applied lending rules. **Why this matters for the ABS:** regulatory clarity that advances are not "loans" de-risks the receivables as an asset class → supports ratings, investor appetite, and lower spreads. The repeat oversubscription lands *after* this clarity — plausibly not coincidental (analysis).
- **Second-order / counterintuitive:** the CFPB "not credit" treatment is a double-edged sword. It legitimizes the asset class *but* keeps DailyPay's product fee-capped/"free to employee" to stay "Covered" — constraining the very revenue that must cover funding costs. The moat therefore leans even harder on cheap capital (the ABS), reinforcing why this deal matters more than it looks.
- Outlook: expect continued ABS cadence (Trust 2026-2, etc.), possible IPO track given the CFO/COO hires. Main risks: credit cycle hitting receivable performance, fee compression, and a future CFPB/administration reversal re-opening the TILA question.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Earned Wage Access / On-Demand Pay. Market sizing varies widely by segment and source (all secondary market-research firms, treat as directional): per Research and Markets, the Earned Wage Access Providers market is ~$12.6bn in 2026 → ~$36.3bn by 2030 (~30% CAGR); the direct-to-consumer EWA slice ~$5.3bn (2025) → ~$7.2bn (2026), ~34.8% CAGR (via researchandmarkets.com, as of 2026). Order of magnitude is consistent (high-$bn, ~25–35% CAGR) but no single authoritative TAM — flag as estimate. Structure: fragmented, two models — employer-integrated EWA (DailyPay, Payactiv) vs direct-to-consumer (Earnin); the real barrier is not product but **funding capacity** — EWA providers advance cash against unpaid wages, so the business is balance-sheet/receivables-intensive and gated by access to cheap, scalable debt. "Why now": (1) the CFPB advisory opinion of 23 Dec 2025 reaffirmed that certain employer-integrated EWA is NOT "credit" under TILA/Regulation Z (withdrawing the 2024 proposed rule) — regulatory tailwind for the employer-integrated model specifically (via goodwinlaw.com / ABA banking journal, Jan 2026); (2) employer adoption as a retention benefit drives funding need, exactly the rationale DailyPay's CFO cites in the PR.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: # employers / # employees on platform, total receivables funded, cost of capital / funding spread, transfer volume. Key players: DailyPay and Payactiv (employer-integrated incumbents), Earnin (D2C), plus a long tail (Instant, Payfare, DoubleNet) and global EWA peers. Basis of competition: increasingly **funding cost and scale**, not just UX — the cheaper you fund wage advances, the more you can price/scale. Recent moves (DailyPay, IR-grounded): inaugural $200M ABS Jun 2025; $195M senior secured revolving facility Jan 2026 [[DailyPay announces $195m senior secured revolving credit facility]]; secured credit facility upsized to $960M Feb 2026; second $200M ABS Oct 2026 → ~$1.4bn total receivables-backed debt. DailyPay's position: **ahead on the funding axis** — per the PR and secondary press it is "the only On-Demand Pay provider to access the ABS market," turning EWA receivables into a rated asset class (Morningstar DBRS, AA(sf) down to BB(sf) across four tranches; oversubscribed) (via asreport.americanbanker.com, prnewswire, Oct 2026). Moat (analysis): capital-markets access + a track record that lets it repeat issuance and compress cost of capital — a scale/financing moat peers haven't replicated, reinforcing distribution to 2,000+ employers / 6M+ employees.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Funding-model concentration / receivables credit risk.** The whole model rests on wage-receivable performance; a labor-market downturn (rising unemployment, employer churn) would hit repayment via payroll deduction and could re-price future ABS tranches (BB(sf) junior notes already signal residual risk). Second-order: cost of capital — DailyPay's core competitive edge — is cyclical, not permanent.
2. **Regulatory reversal risk.** The favorable CFPB "not credit" stance (Dec 2025) is an advisory opinion, not statute; pending federal legislation (H.R. 9330, advanced by HFSC Jun 2026) and a patchwork of state EWA laws (fee caps, licensing) could reclassify or cap fees — directly compressing unit economics and the receivables that back the ABS.
3. **Economics undisclosed (de-PR).** The PR trumpets scale and "oversubscribed / lower cost of capital" but is silent on the actual coupon/spread, net advance losses, and take rate per transfer — the numbers that would prove the funding edge is real. Treat "reduced cost of capital" as a claim, not a measured result.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) DailyPay is institutionalizing EWA receivables as a repeatable, rated asset class — the second oversubscribed ABS is evidence the funding channel is durable, not a one-off, and widens its cost-of-capital gap over Payactiv/Earnin. Falsifiable thesis: if DailyPay can keep issuing (watch for ABS #3 / larger size and any IPO filing with disclosed funding spreads), it compounds a financing moat; the thesis breaks if (a) a peer accesses the ABS market on comparable terms, or (b) CFPB/legislative reclassification forces EWA toward "credit" treatment, raising compliance cost and shrinking the eligible receivables pool.
