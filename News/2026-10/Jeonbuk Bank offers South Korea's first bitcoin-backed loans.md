Jeonbuk Bank has started offering bitcoin-backed loans to corporate clients, becoming the country's first bank to let firms borrow cash against their crypto holdings.
The South Korean bank, a unit of JB Financial Group, will issue loans in the country’s fiat currency , the Korean won, with proceeds limited to business operating costs, according to local news outlet Edaily.
A Jeonbuk Bank official confirmed to local outlet Jaekyung Ilbo that a loan has already been executed, but declined to name the borrower.
Only bitcoin BTC $82,292.69 can be used as collateral for these loans for the time being. Jeonbuk Bank will not hold the BTC itself and instead, a registered crypto service provider will keep the assets in custody while the bank issues the loan.
If a borrower fails to repay, the bank would move to recover its money through a court-ordered sale of the bitcoin.
The process draws on revised Civil Enforcement Rules that took effect Oct. 1, per the local news outlet. These changes set out legal steps for seizing and selling rights tied to digital assets.
The bank ran its own stress tests to assess how bitcoin would hold up during sharp price drops and chose BTC based on its deep liquidity, allowing the funds to be liquidated when needed.
The service brings a South Korean bank’s offering in line with a wider trend bitcoin-backed lending evolving into a more mainstream source of credit.
The report did not disclose loan rates, borrowing limits or collateral requirements, though Jaekyung Ilbo reported that the bank reviewed loan-to-value ratios and margin-call thresholds for major tokens, including bitcoin and ether, while designing the product.
Jeonbuk Bank could expand the service based on market demand and changes to crypto regulations. The new offering comes after the bank’s CEO Park Chun-won said earlier this year the lender would roll out the country's first crypto-backed loan .

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] TL;DR.** Jeonbuk Bank (a unit of JB Financial Group) became **South Korea's first traditional bank to issue won-denominated loans collateralized by bitcoin**, launched for corporate clients on 2026-10-12. The bank does **not** take custody of the BTC — a registered crypto service provider holds it — and on default the bank recovers via a **court-ordered sale** under revised Civil Enforcement Rules effective 2026-10-01. At least one loan has already been executed (borrower undisclosed). It is the first concrete step in CEO Park Chun-won's "Crypto Bank" vision.

**[1] Timeline.**
- **2026-04-08** — South Korea's ruling Democratic Party proposes a comprehensive **Digital Asset Basic Act** (issuance/trading/custody rules, stablecoin framework, path to spot BTC ETFs).
- **2026-08** — Korean banks reported racing to secure crypto-custody stakes ahead of institutional demand / possible BTC ETF (Seoul Economic Daily).
- **Earlier in 2026** — Jeonbuk CEO Park Chun-won signals the lender will roll out the country's first crypto-backed loan.
- **2026-10-01** — Revised **Civil Enforcement Rules** take effect, setting legal steps for seizing/selling rights tied to digital assets (the legal enabler).
- **2026-10-12** — Jeonbuk Bank launches the bitcoin-backed corporate loan; a Jeonbuk official confirms to Jaekyung Ilbo one loan already executed. Reported by Edaily, Jaekyung Ilbo, CoinDesk.

**[2] Mechanics & specifics (reported).**
- **Collateral:** BTC only "for the time being." Chosen for deep liquidity / ability to liquidate under stress (bank ran internal stress tests for sharp drawdowns).
- **Custody split:** Bank issues won; a **registered custodian** (ISMS-certified, compliant with the Virtual Asset User Protection Act) holds the BTC. Lending is separated from custody — the bank never owns the pledged asset.
- **Valuation:** Collateral marked using an **index = weighted average of prices across multiple global exchanges** (to blunt single-venue price swings).
- **Use of proceeds:** Restricted to **business operating costs** (payroll, suppliers, etc.); corporate clients only.
- **Default:** No direct seizure — recovery via **court-ordered sale** of the BTC under the new rules.
- **Not disclosed:** loan rates, borrowing limits, LTV, margin-call thresholds — though the bank reviewed LTV and margin-call parameters for BTC and ETH during design (ETH not yet accepted).

**[3] Why it matters.** This brings a **regulated incumbent bank** (not a crypto-native firm) into BTC-collateral lending, within an explicit legal framework — a template other Korean banks can copy under existing law. It validates "bitcoin-as-collateral" moving from crypto-native/DeFi toward mainstream bank credit, and signals Korean banks positioning for a broader crypto build-out (custody stakes, possible ETF). The custody-outsourcing + court-enforcement model is a notably conservative, bank-friendly design versus self-custodial or on-platform crypto lenders.

**[4] Internal comps / related notes.**
- [[Swiss bank Sygnum launches bitcoin-backed loan platform]] — closest "regulated bank" precedent (Switzerland); Sygnum/Debifi multisig model lets clients retain collateral control (contrast with Jeonbuk's third-party custody).
- [[Coinbase's Bitcoin-backed loans surpass $1 billion]] — scale benchmark for BTC-collateral lending (crypto-native, up to ~$5M/borrower, no fixed term).
- [[Ledger launches Bitcoin-backed Crypto Loan at TOKEN2049]] — **same month, different model**: self-custodial DeFi (Morpho/Yield.xyz), wrapped BTC → stablecoins; not a bank. Not a duplicate.
- [[Kraken launches Flexline crypto-backed loans at 10-25% APR]] — exchange-side crypto-backed lending for rate/LTV context.
- Related Korea-bank fintech: [[Toss and Gwangju Bank complete stablecoin QR payment PoC]] (stablecoins, not lending — distinct).

External comps for LTV/rates (none disclosed by Jeonbuk): Xapo Bank (USD loans up to $1M, 20-40% LTV, collateral never rehypothecated); Coinbase (~5% rates, higher LTV); Sygnum MultiSYG (multisig).

3. The "one loan already executed" is a single anonymous data point — is this a real product at scale or a one-off pilot/marketing launch?
4. No disclosed LTV, rate, or borrowing limits. Without these, can we assess whether the product is actually competitive or commercially meaningful?
5. Who is the "registered crypto service provider" holding custody? Unnamed — concentration/counterparty risk is unquantified.
6. Court-ordered sale on default: untested in practice. How fast can a Korean court liquidate seized BTC during a crash, and does that defeat the liquidity rationale?
7. Does the Oct-1 Civil Enforcement Rule actually cover BTC collateral cleanly, or is there legal ambiguity the first default will expose?
8. BTC was ~$82-83K and visibly volatile (decoupling from Korean stocks per same-day headlines). Launching BTC-collateral lending into volatility — prudent or mistimed?
9. Corporate-only, operating-costs-only: how large is the addressable demand? Korean firms holding material BTC on balance sheet may be a thin niche.
10. Margin-call mechanics undisclosed. In a fast drawdown, with a third-party custodian and court process for seizure, can the bank actually top-up or liquidate in time?
11. Regulatory reversibility: is this permitted under existing law durably, or could the FSC/FSS tighten and curtail it (cf. Korea's history of crypto crackdowns)?
12. Is Jeonbuk (a regional bank) a credible bellwether for the big Korean banks, or an outlier taking niche risk for PR?
13. Differentiation vs. same-month [[Ledger launches Bitcoin-backed Crypto Loan at TOKEN2049]] and Sygnum/Coinbase — is the "bank + court enforcement + outsourced custody" angle genuinely new or incremental?
14. ETH was reviewed but excluded. Is BTC-only a liquidity call or a hint the product is deliberately minimal/experimental?
15. Does this materially change the Korean crypto-credit landscape, or is it a symbolic "first" with limited near-term volume?

**Importance: 3/5 — rationale.** A genuine regulatory/market "first" for a traditional Korean bank doing BTC-collateral lending, with a clean legal enabler (Oct-1 rules) and a copyable template — meaningful signal for Korea and bank crypto-credit adoption. Capped at 3 (not higher) because: it's a *regional* bank, undisclosed economics (rate/LTV/limits), a single anonymous loan executed so far, corporate-only niche scope, and it fits an already-well-covered global trend (Sygnum, Coinbase, Ledger, Xapo). Not a 2 because the incumbent-bank + statutory-enforcement angle is novel; not a 4+ because scale and durability are unproven.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Crypto-collateralized lending — a borrower pledges BTC, gets fiat/stablecoin liquidity without selling. Market size estimates vary widely by scope: per a Ledn May-2026 research note, the *retail* bitcoin-backed loan market is ~$3bn today and modeled to reach $1 trillion in outstanding loans within ~10 years (via cryptonews.net, 2026); separately the broader "crypto lending platform" market is put at ~$12.7bn in 2026 (18.8% CAGR, Research and Markets, 2026). Treat the trillion-dollar figures as vendor/analyst projections, not realized volume. Structure: still fragmented and bifurcated — DeFi protocols (Morpho, Aave) carry the majority of outstanding loans vs CeFi/bank platforms, and the segment is split between self-custodial retail (Ledger, Coinbase-via-Morpho) and bank-grade custodial lending (Sygnum, Figure). "Why now" for a *traditional Korean bank*: a concrete legal rail — revised Civil Execution Rules effective Oct 1, 2026 set out court-ordered seizure/sale of digital-asset rights, landing <2 weeks before launch, plus a broader 2026 Korean policy shift (FSC lifted the corporate crypto-trading ban in Jan 2026; National Asset Basic Act recognized crypto in Jul 2026) that moved crypto from a retail-only risk toward core financial strategy (per Coinpedia/Chambers, 2026).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: initial LTV, margin-call/liquidation thresholds, custody model (custodial vs self-custodial), collateral asset set. Industry LTVs in 2026 run ~20–75%, most commonly ~50%; margin calls ~65–85%, liquidation ~80–91% (per bitcoinlendingintel/KuCoin, 2026). Jeonbuk did NOT disclose its rates, limits, or LTV — only that it reviewed LTV and margin-call thresholds for BTC/ETH and currently accepts BTC only; one loan reportedly already executed (borrower unnamed) `[UNSOURCED on terms]`. Players: banks/regulated — Sygnum (non-custodial MultiSYG w/ Debifi, HNW/institutions), Figure ($250m facility w/ Cross River); exchange/wallet — Coinbase (>$1bn originated via Morpho, retail US), Kraken (Flexline 10–25% APR), Ledger (self-custodial, TOKEN2049 2026), plus JPMorgan entering. Basis of competition: custody model + legal enforceability + collateral breadth, not price. Jeonbuk's position: *first-mover among traditional Korean banks* and corporate-only — a niche/geographic play, not a global scale player; its moat is regulatory timing (local legal rail) and incumbent corporate relationships, not technology `(analysis)`. Notably it outsources custody to a registered VASP — so the bank captures the credit spread while a third party holds the collateral-handling/fraud risk.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Collateral volatility / liquidation.** BTC is a single volatile asset (~$82k at launch, having swung to/below $83k same week per CoinDesk). A sharp drop between margin-call and court-ordered sale can leave the bank undercollateralized — and recovery via *court auction* is slow vs automated DeFi liquidation, widening the gap between the liquidation trigger and realized proceeds. Jeonbuk's own stress tests and BTC-only choice (deep liquidity) mitigate but don't remove this.
2. **Untested legal/enforcement rail.** The entire default pathway rests on Civil Execution Rules that took effect only Oct 1, 2026 — no case law on seizing/auctioning digital-asset rights yet; enforceability and timeline are unproven in a real default.
3. **Custody outsourcing / counterparty dependence.** The bank does not hold the BTC; a registered VASP does. The product's integrity depends on that custodian's security and solvency — a single-point-of-failure the bank doesn't control, and prior-cycle failures (BlockFi, Celsius) stemmed from exactly this layer.
4. **Regulatory reversibility.** The enabling 2026 Korean policy is still in flux (stablecoin-issuance turf war BOK vs FSC; rules evolving) — expansion ("based on regulatory changes") could be constrained if the stance tightens.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is the first *traditional* (non-exchange, non-digital-asset-specialist) Korean bank to underwrite BTC collateral — a template other Korean commercial banks can copy now that a legal enforcement path exists, so watch for fast-follower launches (KB, Shinhan, Hana) as the signal that crypto-collateral is normalizing in Korean banking. Falsifiable thesis: if a second mainstream Korean bank announces a comparable product within ~6 months, the Oct-1 rule change was the real unlock; if none follow and Jeonbuk keeps it corporate-only/low-volume, it stays a niche experiment. Trigger to watch: first disclosed default running through the new court-auction process — that's the stress test for the whole model.
