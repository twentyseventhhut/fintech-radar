# Context-enrichment: Revolut introduces Revolut Register POS with detachable tablet
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**Freshness verdict: FRESH.** No prior corpus note covers Revolut Register or "Pay with Smile" (grep over `News/` returns only this note for both terms). This is a distinct hardware/GA event: Revolut's own-brand modular POS terminal ("Register") with published pricing. It builds on the Sept 2026 UK "Pay with Smile" facial-recognition pilot (external only — never noted in corpus), so it is not a re-run of an existing note. It fits Revolut's broader Oct-2026 push (see [[Revolut aims to become global tech company beyond finance]], [[Revolut plans broader technology product suite beyond finance]], [[Revolut CEO targets JPMorgan and Amex in US push]]).

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Revolut launched **Revolut Register**, an own-brand, modular two-screen POS: a main touchscreen checkout unit plus a **detachable tablet** so staff can take payments away from the counter (table-side / queue-busting — classic hospitality pattern). It bundles a camera for **Pay with Smile** facial-recognition payments. Merchants can mix as many screens and tablets as they need.
- **Pricing (the real news vs the Sept pilot):** hardware ~**£699 + VAT RRP**, discounted **50% to £349.50 + VAT on the first terminal until 31 Dec 2026**; **no activation fee, no monthly device charge** (per external coverage, cityam/financemagnates).
- **"Zero processing fees" is narrow:** 0% applies **only to Revolut Pay with Smile** transactions, and only because Revolut is currently choosing to charge nothing for that rail. Standard card acquiring still carries fees (Revolut Business card-present is ~0.8% + 2p on the Grow plan per external comparison). The PR headline "zero processing fees" is a **steering lever**, not a general price cut.
- **Why structured this way:** Smile runs on Revolut's own closed loop (Revolut customer → Revolut merchant), so Revolut pays **no scheme interchange** on it → it can give 0% as a loss-leader to pull both sides onto its rails and off Visa/Mastercard. The hardware is sold near cost (one-off, no SaaS fee) to maximise terminal placement; monetisation is the payment flow and the attached business account, not the box.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Direct hardware/acquiring:** Square (Block), PayPal **Zettle**, **SumUp**, Dojo, myPOS, Viva.com, Flatpay. SumUp and Square each cite ~4M merchants; Revolut cites 65k+ merchants and in-person volumes "quadrupling" (external, no absolute GMV). Revolut is **catching up** on scale but **undercutting on headline rate** (Square/Zettle 1.75%, SumUp 1.69% vs Revolut ~0.8%+2p).
- **Face-payment prior art:** this is NOT first-mover. [[Toss to expand pay-by-face to 1 million stores by 2026]] and [[Facial recognition payments are growing in South Korea with local fintech Toss]] show pay-by-face at real scale in Korea; [[Stripe and NEC launch face recognition payments in Japan]]; [[Tencent Cloud and Treeal launch palm payments in Brazil]]; [[UAE Central Bank pilots palm and face biometric payments]]. Revolut's Smile is a **UK-first**, not a world-first.
- **Why the landscape is this way:** incumbents (Square/Zettle) monetise the terminal + a flat take-rate; Revolut's edge is the **closed loop + banking bundle**, letting it zero-rate the on-us Smile flow — something a pure acquirer can't match without the consumer side. Second-order: the differentiator is distribution of the Revolut consumer base, not the hardware.

## [2] Company history / fit
Revolut has been building merchant acquiring for years — now live in ~19 countries, launched acquiring/merchant suites in Australia and Singapore in 2025–26 (external). Register is the **hardware layer** on top of that software acquiring stack, plus a **hospitality OS** ambition (POS + banking + ops in one). Fits the Oct-2026 narrative of becoming a "global tech company beyond finance" ([[Revolut aims to become global tech company beyond finance]]) and widening the product suite. **Why:** card acquiring is a commodity take-rate business; owning the terminal + hospitality workflow + the consumer wallet lets Revolut capture more of the merchant relationship (and justify a software, not payments, multiple ahead of a possible US IPO — [[Revolut favors US primary listing for potential IPO]]).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new (for Revolut/UK):** own-brand modular terminal with detachable tablet + embedded face-pay at this price, 0% on the on-us rail.
- **Not new (globally):** detachable-tablet POS (Square, Zettle already do it), face payments (Korea/Japan/China at scale).
- **Traction = thin so far:** Smile was a **3-day pilot** (24–26 Sep 2026) at three Kiss the Hippo Coffee sites in London. Register is a **launch with a promo price**, not reported adoption. No GMV, no merchant count on Register, no Smile enrolment numbers → announcement, not proven traction.
- **Who captures the margin:** on the Smile loop, Revolut captures everything (no interchange leakage) but currently earns **0%** by choice — so near-term it's customer-acquisition spend. Real value depends on (a) converting Smile users to the full card book later, and (b) biometric consent/adoption at scale, where UK consumer appetite for face-pay is unproven.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect UK-first rollout, promo through end-2026 to seed terminals, then wider geography following the acquiring footprint. **Risks:** (1) biometric payments face UK/EU privacy and consent scrutiny (GDPR, ICO) — regulatory friction is the main brake, not tech; (2) 0% is unsustainable long-term → pricing will rise once enrolled, a classic funnel; (3) hardware near-cost means Revolut eats CAC upfront with payback only via downstream flow. **Counterintuitive second-order:** the loud "0% + facial recognition" framing is marketing to win attention in a crowded terminal market where Revolut is still small vs Square/SumUp; the durable moat is the banking bundle, not the smile.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** SMB POS/acquiring is a large, still-expanding market. Merchant acquiring is ~$28.2bn (2025) → ~$31.2bn (2026) at ~10.8% CAGR, and the broader POS market ~$43bn (2026) growing ~13% CAGR (per researchandmarkets / fortunebusinessinsights, via WebSearch, as of Oct 2026 — secondary analyst figures, treat as directional). Structure: fragmented at the long tail and increasingly bifurcated — commodity card-reader players (SumUp, Zettle) at one end, vertical software-led all-in-one platforms (Toast in restaurants, Square across retail/food, Clover/Fiserv) at the other; hardware itself has largely commoditised on Android, so value accrues to software + embedded financial services, not the terminal. Entry barriers are regulation (acquiring/e-money licences), distribution, and network effects from an installed base. Why now: (1) the land-grab has shifted from "take a card payment" to "run the whole floor" (orders, staff, banking) — Revolut Register is explicitly a modular two-screen "unify payments + banking + floor operations" play `(analysis)`; (2) biometric/"Pay with Smile" and 0% processing on Revolut Pay are the differentiating wedge, piggybacking Revolut's own 68m+ consumer base to bypass interchange on on-us transactions.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: GPV/TPV, take rate (payments gross profit / GPV), hardware attach, and software ARR/NRR for the platform names. Players & basis of competition: Square/Block competes on breadth + ecosystem (Q1'26 Square GPV $61.2bn, +13%; Block group gross profit $2.9bn, +27%); Toast on restaurant verticalisation (Q1'26 GPV $51.3bn +22%, ARR $2.2bn +26%, net income $126m); SumUp/Zettle on cheap, simple card acceptance for micro-merchants (SumUp ~4m merchants, 36 countries); Fiserv's Clover on scale distribution (5m+ devices shipped — see internal comp). Recent moves: Square launched a $1,999 AI voice-ordering restaurant kiosk (Oct 2026); Fiserv crossed 5m Clover devices (Oct 2026); SumUp exploring a ~$10–15bn European IPO. Revolut's position: a **new entrant catching up** on hardware/floor software (Square/Toast have multi-year installed bases) but with an unusual moat — it already banks the merchant AND tens of millions of consumers, so Revolut Register + Pay with Smile can offer 0% processing on on-us flows that acquirers reliant on card rails cannot match `(analysis)`. Earlier in 2026 Revolut pushed Pay with Smile / Terminal (Sept 2026) — Register is the next hardware step in the Business land-grab.

**Risk flags.**
1. **0% processing is a loss-leader, not a free lunch** — it only holds for on-us Revolut Pay / Pay with Smile; the economics on standard card transactions (interchange, scheme fees, fraud liability) are undisclosed. Watch for who's silent: Revolut hasn't published blended take rate or hardware margin.
2. **Biometric / facial-recognition regulatory exposure** — Pay with Smile collects biometric data; GDPR/UK-GDPR special-category-data rules and emerging biometric regs raise compliance and consent-friction risk, and any breach/misuse is reputationally acute. Revolut already tops UK bank fraud complaints (internal corpus), which compounds trust risk.
3. **Execution vs entrenched installed bases** — Square, Toast and Clover have years of merchant lock-in and vertical software depth; hardware is commoditised, so Revolut must win on software + bundled banking, where it is unproven at merchant scale. Second-order: if adoption lags, Register becomes a costly hardware subsidy (50% intro discount to £349.50) without the recurring software attach that makes peers' unit economics work.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Revolut Register signals Revolut weaponising its dual consumer+merchant balance sheet to attack acquiring from the inside — pricing to zero on on-us volume to pull merchants onto its stack, then monetising via banking/FX/lending rather than the terminal. Falsifiable thesis: if Revolut discloses meaningful Business GPV or merchant-count traction (e.g. in FY2026 results) alongside Pay with Smile adoption, it re-rates as a credible fourth force in European SMB acquiring; trigger to watch = Business segment metrics in the next annual report. What breaks it: low biometric opt-in + regulatory pushback on facial recognition leaving Register as a niche hardware line.

Figures below are an IR-grounded financial backdrop for the acquiring push, from Revolut Group Holdings Ltd's **filed FY2025 consolidated accounts** (year ended 31 Dec 2025, published 2026-04-29) — not product-specific numbers (none disclosed).

**Backdrop (FY2025, filed accounts).** Group revenue **$6.0bn (£4.5bn), +46% YoY** (FY2024 $4.0bn/£3.1bn). **Profit before tax $2.3bn (£1.7bn), +57% YoY**, PBT margin **38%** (up from 35% in FY2024). **Net profit (profit for the year) $1.7bn, up from ~$1.0bn in FY2024 (~+70%)** — fifth consecutive year of net profitability. 11 product lines each cleared c.$135m (£100m) revenue, i.e. revenue diversification beyond interchange/FX.

**Customers / scale.** Retail base **68.3m, +30% YoY**; **business customers 767k, +33% YoY** — the segment Revolut Register targets. Customer balances **$67.5bn (£50.2bn), +66% YoY**.

**Why it matters for Register (acquiring).** Register extends Revolut Business from online/soft-POS into physical card-present acquiring with hardware, and uses "zero processing fees on Revolut Pay with Smile" to pull the ~767k business customers (and their retail counterparts) onto a closed-loop, interchange-light rail — defending take-rate vs incumbent acquirers (SumUp, Square, Adyen, Stripe Terminal). The +33% business-customer growth and 11-product diversification are the thesis context: acquiring is a wedge to deepen merchant monetisation, not yet a reported revenue line. No standalone Business/acquiring revenue is broken out in the public figures — **[UNSOURCED]** at product level.

**Valuation context.** ~$75bn in the Nov-2025 secondary share sale (vs $45bn Aug-2024, +67%); a 2H2026 secondary reportedly targeting >$100bn, IPO ambitions $150–200bn (analysis — press, not filings).

**Verdict:** no results print — **backdrop only** (strong FY2025: revenue +46%, net profit ~+70%); Register is strategic optionality on the already-growing business segment.
