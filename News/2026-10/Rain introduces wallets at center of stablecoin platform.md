 NEW YORK , Oct. 13, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Rain, the global stablecoin payments platform, today announced Rain wallets , embedded stablecoin wallets that partners can offer their users inside their own apps, with the ability to earn on balances to follow by end of year. The wallet is the account at the center of Rain's platform. It is accessible through the same integration that issues the card, powers rewards, and supports money movement, so a user can get paid, save, spend, and send from one balance.
Companies bringing stablecoins to their users want them to work the way any other money does. For that to happen, a stablecoin balance needs to connect to everything a user wants to do with it. Rain wallets put that balance at the center of Rain's platform, so the full life cycle of money runs in one place, from the moment it arrives to the moment it is spent or sent.
Consider a freelancer in Mexico paid by a U.S. client. Her pay is sent in dollars to her virtual account and lands in her Rain wallet as stablecoins, with no manual conversion. She can spend that afternoon at any of the 175 million merchant locations where her Rain-issued card is accepted, or she can access additional ways to earn on balances directly in her app. When she needs pesos, she can send them straight to her local bank account using Rain's global payouts. The entire experience lives inside one app, and it's all powered by Rain's infrastructure.
"When a company builds with stablecoins, the first thing its users need is a place to hold them," said Pooja Shah, Head of Product at Rain. "Rain wallets are that place. Because they are integrated into the broader Rain stack, every balance is ready to use the moment it arrives. Partners can get all of it from Rain, or keep the wallet they have and plug it into everything else we run."
 How Rain wallets fit into Rain's full-stack platform 
The wallet is the account at the center of Rain's full-stack platform, the place where a partner's users hold their stablecoins and the balance every other Rain product draws on. Rain started by making stablecoins spendable through cards and is now powering all onchain experiences through the wallet. A balance can arrive through a virtual account or onramp, fund a card, be put to work, and be sent to a bank account in local currency without ever leaving the partner's app. For enterprises, that means one vendor integrating the full flow of funds, and every product a partner adds works with the ones they already run.
While Rain offers the convenience of an end-to-end platform, its infrastructure is also built to be open and interoperable. Partners that already work with a wallet provider can connect it to Rain's cards, rewards, onramps, and payouts. Rain wallets support multiple stablecoins and other crypto assets across major blockchains, giving partners the flexibility to choose which assets or networks best fit their business.

# Context-enrichment: Rain introduces wallets at center of stablecoin platform
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On Oct 13, 2026 Rain — the Visa & Mastercard Principal-Member stablecoin payments platform — launched **Rain wallets**: embedded stablecoin wallets that its *partners* (enterprises, neobanks, platforms, AI agents) embed inside *their own* apps. This is a B2B2C infrastructure product, not a consumer wallet from Rain.

De-PR'd facts:
- **Live today:** only the **non-custodial** variant (users hold their own keys), available "to Rain partners today." Onboarding via passkey or email/phone code (no seed phrase).
- **Announced, NOT live:** custodial wallets AND the headline **"earn on your balance"** feature — both "before the end of the year." The yield/earn capability is explicitly future and provided by *third parties* ("not guaranteed") — i.e. the most commercially interesting piece is vaporware for now.
- Supports **12 blockchains** (Arbitrum, Avalanche, Base, BNB, Celo, Ethereum, Monad, Plasma, Optimism, Polygon, Solana, ZKsync) and USDC/USDT + other assets.
- Wallet balances natively fund **Rain-issued cards** at 175M merchant locations across 220+ countries.
- Interoperable: partners can keep a third-party wallet and still plug into Rain's cards/rewards/on-offramps/payouts.

**→ Why structured this way:** Rain is repositioning the *account/wallet*, not the card, as the center of gravity. Previously the card was the hook ("Rain started by making stablecoins spendable through cards"); now it wants to own the balance the card draws on. Controlling the wallet = controlling where the money sits = owning the full lifecycle (receive → hold → earn → spend → send) inside one integration. **→ What it reveals:** the land-grab is for the *account relationship*, because whoever holds the balance captures float/yield economics and the integration lock-in, not just per-swipe interchange. The PR leads with "wallets at the center" precisely because card-only infra is becoming commoditized (see [1]).
**→ Vagueness flags:** "175M merchant locations" and "twelve blockchains" are supply-side breadth metrics; zero demand-side numbers (wallets provisioned, balances held, partners live on wallets). No named launch partner for wallets specifically — contrast with prior releases that named Western Union/Nuvei/KAST.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Stablecoin card/wallet infrastructure is now crowded:
- **Bridge (Stripe)** — the sharpest comp. Acquired by Stripe for $1.1B (closed Feb 2025), it offers virtual accounts, orchestration, card programs AND embedded wallets spanning custodial Bridge wallets + non-custodial **Privy** wallets + Stripe Financial Accounts ([[This Week in Fintech Bridge powers Shopify, MetaMask, Remitly]], [[Visa, Bridge, Pine Labs and Wirex expand stablecoin card initiatives]]). Stripe/Bridge is the gravity well: distribution + Privy (acq. June 2025) + Visa ties.
- **Pure embedded-wallet-as-a-service:** Privy (Stripe), **Turnkey** (AWS Nitro enclaves, passkey-first), **Dynamic** (acquired by Fireblocks Oct 2025), Magic, Crossmint, Openfort. These sell the wallet SDK layer Rain now bundles.
- **Card-issuing stablecoin peers:** Reap (being acquired by Kraken/Payward for up to $600M — [[Kraken parent Payward to acquire Reap for up to $600 million]]), KAST, Wirex, WasabiCard ([[WasabiCard closes Series Pre-A for stablecoin cards]]), Immersve, MiniPay+Visa.
- **Networks moving in directly:** Stripe/Visa/Mastercard joint stablecoin platform ([[Stripe, Visa and Mastercard near joint stablecoin platform]]); Mastercard/Visa stablecoin settlement ([[Mastercard and Visa extend support for stablecoin settlement]]).

**Position:** Rain is at **parity/slightly behind Bridge** on wallets — Bridge already ships custodial + non-custodial wallets; Rain is launching non-custodial first with custodial "by year end." Rain's differentiator vs pure WaaS vendors is that it owns the *Visa/Mastercard Principal Membership* + card settlement rail, so the wallet comes pre-wired to spend. Pure WaaS players (Privy/Turnkey) don't issue cards; Rain bundles both.
**→ Second-order:** The real squeeze is from above (Stripe/Bridge with distribution) and below (WaaS SDKs commoditizing the wallet primitive). Rain's defensibility is the *middle*: direct network membership + full flow-of-funds in one integration. That's genuine but narrowing as Bridge replicates it with far more distribution.

## [2] Company history / fit
Rain trajectory (dated): Series A (~Mar 2025) → Series B (Aug/Sep 2025) → **$250M Series C led by ICONIQ at $1.95B, Jan 9 2026** (~17x in 10 months, total funding >$338M; active card base +30x, payment volume +38x YoY, >$3B annualized, 200+ partners) ([[Rain raises $250M Series C at $1.95B valuation]], [[Rain builds full stack for enterprise stablecoin cards]]) → **Visa membership into APAC, Mar 24 2026** ([[Rain expands Visa membership into Asia-Pacific for stablecoin cards]]) → **integrated loyalty/Rewards, Jun 2026** ([[Rain brings integrated loyalty to stablecoin card programmes]]) → **wallets, Oct 13 2026** (now 100+ orgs, Visa *and* Mastercard Principal Member, 175M merchants / 220+ countries).

**→ Fit:** Logical and consistent. Rain's Series C PR explicitly promised "invest ahead of the curve in new products" and listed "power secure wallets" in its stack. Wallets is the deepening of the full-stack thesis, not a pivot.
**→ Why Rain acts this way:** card issuing/settlement is a thin-margin, network-dependent commodity take-rate business. To justify a $1.95B valuation (and the next round) Rain needs a *software/platform* multiple and recurring, sticky relationships. Owning the wallet/account = the stickiest layer (balances, yield, data). Structural pressure: commoditization of the card rail → push upstream to the account to capture durable margin and lock-in.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**What's actually new:** a single-integration bundle where one vendor supplies wallet + card + virtual account + on/offramp + payouts + rewards across 12 chains. The *bundling under one Visa/MC-member roof* is the novelty, not any single primitive.
**What's NOT new:** embedded non-custodial wallets, passkey onboarding, multi-chain support — all standard (Privy, Turnkey, Dynamic, Bridge). Rain itself already listed "wallets" in its stack back in Jan; this launch productizes/names it.
**Traction:** essentially **zero wallet-specific traction disclosed.** No wallets-live count, no partner names on the wallet product, no balances. The only hard numbers (175M merchants, 100+ orgs, 12 chains) are pre-existing platform/supply metrics. This is an **announcement, not adoption.**
**→ Where value-add is real / who captures the margin:** real value-add exists IF partners consolidate onto Rain (fewer vendors, pre-wired spend). But the margin question is sharp: the headline "earn on balances" — the thing that would let Rain monetize float/yield — is (a) future and (b) delivered by *third parties*, so Rain may NOT capture that yield spread. The card interchange is split with Visa/MC. So today Rain captures integration/SaaS fees + card economics; the juicy balance/yield layer is unconfirmed and possibly disintermediated to partners. **The central question shifts from "did Rain ship a wallet" (yes, a table-stakes one) to "will Rain capture the balance economics, or just host balances that monetize elsewhere?"**

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Near-term: custodial wallets + third-party-powered yield "by end of 2026." Custodial is what most neobank/fintech partners actually want (simpler UX), so the *year-end* release is arguably the more important shoe to drop.
- Macro tailwind: stablecoin card/wallet infra is hot — GENIUS Act regulatory clarity (US), stablecoin volumes +91% to $10.9T ([[Stablecoin volumes grew 91% to $10.9 trillion last year]]), networks and Stripe all racing in. Capital is abundant (Rain's $1.95B, Reap $600M exit).
- Risks: (1) Stripe/Bridge out-distributing Rain; (2) Visa/Mastercard building the wallet+card stack themselves and relegating Rain to a reseller; (3) commoditization of WaaS compressing any wallet premium; (4) regulatory/custody exposure once custodial wallets launch (Rain is "not a bank"; banking via partner banks).
**→ Why the market goes this way / counterintuitive:** The flood of capital and me-too launches makes individual names *more* fragile, not safer — every infra player is converging on the same "full-stack wallet+card+payout" bundle, so differentiation erodes and the winners will be decided by distribution (favoring Stripe/Bridge) and by who owns the end-customer, not by feature parity. Rain shipping a non-custodial wallet is necessary defense, not a moat.

## Top challenge / red-team questions

1. **What is actually live vs announced?** Answer: only non-custodial wallets are live "to partners today"; custodial wallets AND the "earn on balance" feature are future ("by end of year"). The marquee monetization feature is not shipped.

2. **Does Rain capture the yield on held balances?** Open/skeptical. The earn feature is explicitly provided by **third parties** and "not guaranteed" — Rain may host balances without capturing the float/yield spread. This is the key margin question.

3. **Any wallet-specific adoption numbers?** No. Zero wallets-provisioned, balances-held, or named wallet launch partners disclosed. All hard numbers (175M merchants, 100+ orgs, 12 chains) are pre-existing platform metrics. Announcement ≠ traction.

4. **Is the embedded non-custodial wallet itself novel?** No — Privy, Turnkey, Dynamic, Bridge already ship these; passkey onboarding and multi-chain are standard. Novelty is only the *bundling* under one Visa/MC-member roof.

5. **How does this compare to Bridge/Stripe?** Bridge already offers custodial + non-custodial (Privy) + Stripe Financial Account wallets, with vastly more distribution. Rain is at parity-to-behind on the wallet; ahead only via owning its own Visa/MC Principal Membership + card settlement.

6. **Did Rain build the wallet or white-label it?** Open. PR doesn't name an underlying wallet infra provider; unclear if Rain's non-custodial wallet is in-house or on a Privy/Turnkey-style SDK. Materially affects the defensibility claim.

7. **Who holds keys / custody & regulatory exposure?** Non-custodial = users hold keys (lower Rain liability). Custodial (year-end) raises custody/licensing questions; Rain is "not a bank," banking via partner bank(s). Regulatory risk shifts once custodial ships.

8. **Is this a duplicate of a prior Rain note?** No. Prior notes cover funding (Jan), APAC Visa expansion (Mar), loyalty (Jun) — distinct events. This is the first wallet-product launch. FRESH.

9. **Why reposition from card-first to wallet/account-center now?** Card issuing is a thin, network-dependent commodity; to defend a $1.95B valuation Rain must own the stickier account layer (balances, data, yield) for a software multiple.

10. **What's the second-order risk of the capital flood?** Me-too convergence (everyone shipping wallet+card+payout bundles) erodes differentiation; winners decided by distribution → favors Stripe/Bridge. Abundant capital makes individual names more fragile, not safer.

11. **Can Visa/Mastercard disintermediate Rain?** Real risk — networks are building joint stablecoin platforms ([[Stripe, Visa and Mastercard near joint stablecoin platform]]); Rain could be relegated to reseller of rails it doesn't own long-term.

12. **Does interoperability ("keep your own wallet") help or hurt Rain?** Double-edged: lowers switching friction to adopt Rain cards, but also lets partners NOT adopt Rain's wallet, undercutting the account-ownership land-grab.

13. **Is "12 blockchains / 175M merchants" meaningful?** Supply-side breadth, not demand. Chain count is a checkbox; merchant count is inherited from Visa/MC acceptance, not Rain-specific.

14. **What does the end-of-year roadmap signal?** Custodial wallet is what most fintech/neobank partners actually want for UX; the year-end release may be the more commercially important event than today's non-custodial launch.

15. **Who is silent on what?** No disclosure on wallet take-rate, yield economics, a single named wallet partner, or fraud/custody liability for the forthcoming custodial product.

Importance: 3/5 — A logical, well-timed deepening of Rain's full-stack thesis by a credible, well-funded ($1.95B) Visa+MC Principal Member, in a hot category. But down-weighted because: the shipped piece (non-custodial embedded wallet) is table-stakes and widely available; the differentiating/monetizing piece (custodial + third-party yield) is future and possibly disintermediated; and there is zero wallet-specific adoption. A real product step for a notable company, not a market-moving event.

## Market Research

**Sector & drivers.** Rain sits in stablecoin payment orchestration — the thin "platform" layer that turns a stablecoin balance into spendable, sendable money via card issuing (Visa/Mastercard), virtual accounts, on/offramps and payouts. Market sizing is estimate-heavy and PR-sourced: Bloomberg Intelligence projects stablecoin payment volume rising from ~$2.9tn (2025) to ~$56.6tn by 2030 (per Bloomberg Intelligence, via multiple 2026 trade write-ups — treat as directional, not audited). Third-party "market size" reports ($409bn 2026 → $1,045bn 2030 at ~26% CAGR) are vendor press-releases and should be read as [UNSOURCED] marketing, not verified TAM. Structure: fragmented and rapidly consolidating — the defensible value is in regulated card-issuing rails + compliant reserve/custody, not the wallet UI. "Why now": the GENIUS Act (P.L. 119-27) created a federal framework for Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuers; OCC issued implementing rules on 2026-03-02 (per OCC/Federal Register). Regulation is the moat-maker here, and Rain is explicitly chasing it — it filed for a U.S. national trust bank charter on 2026-10-07 to custody assets and issue dollar stablecoins (per [[Rain seeks US national trust bank charter]]).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: active cards / payment volume (TPV), partner count, merchant acceptance reach, and (undisclosed) take rate. Rain's disclosed metrics from its Series C: ~30x active cards, ~38x payment volume to ~$3bn annualized, 200+ partners (per PYMNTS/Morningstar, Jan 2026); PR here claims "100+ organizations" and Visa+Mastercard Principal Member status with 175mn merchant locations across 220+ countries. Basis of competition = regulatory posture + breadth of integration (card + wallet + virtual accounts + payouts in one API), not price. Key players: Bridge (Stripe-owned, acquired Feb 2025 for $1.1bn — now bundled into Stripe's cross-border stack); BVNK (being acquired by Mastercard, ~$1.8bn agreed Mar 2026, closed Aug 2026 per trade press; partnered with Marqeta for stablecoin-backed cards); plus Immersve (card-issuing partner behind KuCoin/Bitget crypto cards, see [[KuCoin launches KuCard in Australia with Mastercard and Immersve]]). Rain's position: credible independent challenger / catching up — it is the main still-independent full-stack stablecoin card platform after Bridge and BVNK were absorbed by Stripe and Mastercard respectively (analysis). Moat = network-member card rails + (pending) trust-bank charter; switching costs from single-integration lock-in. Take rate, CAC, card economics → [UNSOURCED].

**Comps & multiples.** Private names; use round valuations, NOT market caps.
- Rain: $1.95bn post-money on $250m Series C (ICONIQ-led), total funding >$338mn (per Bloomberg/PYMNTS, Jan 2026). Revenue undisclosed → EV/Rev = no data. **Risk flags.**
1. **Charter/regulatory execution.** Rain's $1.95bn thesis partly rests on winning a national trust-bank charter; that filing sits amid an ICBA lawsuit against the OCC's trust-charter framework (per [[Rain seeks US national trust bank charter]]). If denied/delayed, Rain stays dependent on partner banks and third-party issuers — weakening the "full-stack" story. Second-order: GENIUS Act pushes issuance toward bank subsidiaries, so a non-bank Rain is structurally disadvantaged vs issuers who get bank status.
2. **Disintermediation / roll-up pressure.** With Bridge→Stripe and BVNK→Mastercard, the two largest card/network owners now own in-house stablecoin issuing. Rain depends on Visa/Mastercard rails it does not control; those networks can favor their own captive stacks, squeezing Rain's economics or access. Second-order: Rain becomes an acquisition target more than a standalone compounder.
3. **Early-stage concentration / undisclosed economics.** Huge growth multiples sit on a ~$3bn-TPV, 200-partner base; PR is silent on take rate, net revenue, fraud/chargeback liability, and custody risk on non-custodial wallets across 12 chains. "Earn on balances" via third parties (explicitly "not guaranteed") adds counterparty/yield risk. Second-order: a single large partner churn or a reserve/yield-partner failure would dent both growth and trust.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** The wallet launch reframes Rain from a card-issuer into a would-be full-stack "stablecoin account" platform — a bid to own the balance (and the user relationship) rather than just the spend rail (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: Rain is positioning to be the last independent stablecoin card platform and will either win the trust charter and re-rate, or get acquired by a network/neobank within ~12-18 months. Watch triggers: (a) OCC decision on the trust charter and resolution of the ICBA suit; (b) custodial-wallet + yield features shipping by year-end 2026 as promised (announced ≠ live); (c) any disclosed net-revenue/take-rate figure that would let the $1.95bn be tested against economics rather than TPV growth. Thesis breaks if a network cuts Rain off or Stripe/Mastercard undercut it on price with their captive stacks.
