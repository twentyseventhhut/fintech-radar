 Aspace интересуется покупкой сети гибких офисов Multispace 
О том, что оператор сервисных офисов Aspace заинтересовался покупкой сети гибких офисов Multispace, сообщили три источника “ Коммерсанта ”. В Aspace сообщили, что сейчас компания не является ни прямым, ни косвенным владельцем Multispace, однако рассматривает разные варианты для расширения своего бизнеса, в том числе за счет приобретения активов Multispace.
По данным собеседников издания, в сделку могут войти все четыре коворкинга в Москве — Multispace Dinamo, Multispace Pravda, Multispace Tverskaya и Multispace Dubinin’Sky, общая площадь которых составляет около 18,2 тыс. кв. м. Стоимость операционного бизнеса этих площадок гендиректор «Infoline-Аналитики» Михаил Бурмистров оценивает в 800–950 млн руб. По его словам, при заполнении арендаторами такого объема площадей на 90% выручка собственника может достигать 1,4 млрд руб. в год.
Сеть гибких офисов Multispace была создана Pridex, которая в августе 2026 года продала актив «группе профессиональных частных инвесторов», говорилось тогда в сообщении компании. Сейчас, по данным СПАРК, 100% долей в ООО «Мультиспейс Скайлайн», ООО «Мультиспейс Правда», ООО «Мультиспейс Тверская» и ООО «Мультиспейс Динамо» принадлежат ООО «Флекс Капитал». Бенефициаром и гендиректором этой организации выступает Ксения Болотова, которой принадлежат 90,1% долей в компании. Остальные 9,9% контролирует Владислав Бондаренко.
Под управлением Aspace находится 43,7 тыс. кв. м сервисных офисов. По объему управляемых коворкингов компания находится на втором месте по объему портфеля, уступая Space1, по данным CMWP. Основным владельцем, контролирующим 75% долей в компании, является АО «Мера недвижимости», 15% долей напрямую принадлежат Сергею Седову, остальные 12,75% — Ивану Гуськову. Выручка АО «А Спэйс», управляющей компании, увеличилась почти вдвое год к году, до 1,5 млрд руб., чистая выручка — на 52% год к году, до 1,1 млрд руб., следует из данных СПАРК.
Pridex решила продать актив, чтобы сосредоточиться на профильном бизнесе по проектированию и строительству офисов, считает руководитель офисного департамента «Инвест7» Ксения Харкевич. Для Aspace сделка может стать возможностью расширить свой портфель готовыми коворкингами, часть из которых уже сдана в аренду, добавляет она. При этом партнер Ricci Дмитрий Жидков называет площадки Multispace наиболее капиталоемкими с точки зрения инвестиций по сравнению с другими операторами сервисных офисов. Впрочем, Aspace имеет устойчивую коммерческую модель и доступ к долговому финансированию, что позволяет привлекать средства для подобных сделок, отмечает управляющий партнер Refolio (оператор сервисных офисов) Андрей Новоселов.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Per Kommersant (12 Oct 2026, via Mergers.ru), serviced-office operator **Aspace** (АО «А Спэйс») is *exploring* buying the four Moscow **Multispace** coworkings (Dinamo, Pravda, Tverskaya, Dubinin'Sky; ~18,200 sqm). This is **interest, not a deal**: Aspace's on-record line is a non-denial — "we are neither direct nor indirect owner, but consider various expansion options." No price, no term sheet, no timeline. The source itself is "three Kommersant sources," i.e. rumour-grade.
**Why framed this way →** The only hard number in the story is an analyst's *estimate*, not a transaction price: Infoline-Analytika's Mikhail Burmistrov values the operating business at **800–950m RUB** and sees up-to **1.4bn RUB/yr** revenue at 90% occupancy. Anchoring to a third-party valuation (rather than a disclosed price) is the tell that nothing is signed. The seller is **ООО «Флекс Капитал»** (Ksenia Bolotova 90.1%, Vladislav Bondarenko 9.9%), who only bought Multispace from **Pridex in Aug 2026** — so a ~2-month-old flip is being shopped again, which reads opportunistic, not strategic.
**Note:** no fintech/proptech-*tech* angle — this is commercial real-estate operator consolidation. The only finance threads are incidental (Aspace is a bond/DFA issuer; shareholder Sergey Sedov owns MFO Zaymer, unrelated to the deal).

## [1] Competitors / peers
Russian flex-office operators: **Space1** (#1, >50k sqm, 14 Moscow sites + Dubai — bought WeWork Russia in Apr 2024), **Aspace** (#2, 43.7k sqm per CMWP), **SOK**, **Workki**, **Business Club** (Iskra-Park), legacy **Regus/IWG**. The cleanest comparable is **Space1 ↔ WeWork Russia (10–16 Apr 2024)**: ~18,000 sqm / 4 Moscow sites / ~3,000 workplaces, rebranded Space1 Jet, price undisclosed — almost identical scale to the Multispace target.
**Why this matters →** Aspace is the #2 consolidator mirroring the #1's playbook (bolt-on a distressed/exited ~18k-sqm 4-site chain). But two data points (Space1/WeWork, Aspace/Multispace interest) are **not yet a "consolidation wave"** — call it directional, not a trend.

## [2] Company history / fit
Aspace **doubled its portfolio in 2025** to ~40,170 sqm / 10 objects / 5,605 workplaces (added ~19.7k sqm, ~51% of market growth). The deal article cites UC revenue ~1.5bn RUB (≈2x YoY), net ~1.1bn. Multispace is a **Pridex side-venture**; Pridex exited to refocus on its core office design-and-build business (per Invest7's Ksenia Kharkevich).
**Why Aspace acts this way →** In a supply-starved market (below), the fastest way to defend the #2 slot is to buy **ready, partly-leased** space rather than build. Fit is logical — but Ricci's Dmitry Zhidkov flags Multispace sites as **the most capital-intensive** among peers, a real valuation/diligence risk, and a ~2-month-old asset being re-sold invites a "why so soon?" question.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Nothing novel in product terms — this is a portfolio bolt-on, not a new capability. "Traction" here = whether a deal materializes at all, and currently it is **only interest**. The asset's value-add depends entirely on occupancy: Burmistrov's 1.4bn revenue is gated on 90% fill, into a market where coworking demand fell **>50% YoY** and vacancy is in the **mid-to-high teens and rising**. Capital-intensity (Zhidkov) compresses the buyer's return.
**Who captures margin →** In serviced offices the operator sits between landlord and tenant on a spread; in a rising-vacancy, rising-rent market that spread is squeezed from both ends. Buying capex-heavy sites at a cooling-demand trough is only accretive if Aspace's funding (bonds/debt) is cheap enough to outlast the dip — Refolio's Andrey Novoselov notes Aspace does have a stable model and debt access.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Russian flex-office is **cooling on demand while rents spike**: 2025 new supply −25% YoY, 2026 forecast a further ~−44% to ~20k sqm; May-2026 demand −50%+ YoY; rent ~63.3k RUB/workplace/mo (+42% YoY); vacancy ~16% (May 2026) / 18.7% Jan–Sep 2026, both rising.
**Why the market goes this way →** Supply contraction + rent inflation + rising vacancy is a stressed, bifurcating market — good for opportunistic buyers of distressed/exited assets, bad for occupancy assumptions. Second-order: the "flip in <2 months" by Flex Capital suggests the seller side is more motivated than the buyer, so if a deal closes, pricing likely lands at or below Burmistrov's 800–950m floor. Base case: talks may stall or close quietly; low market-moving significance.

## Top challenge/extra questions
1. Is this a deal or just interest? **Interest only** — Aspace confirms no ownership, only "evaluating options."
2. Price disclosed? **No.** Only an analyst estimate (800–950m RUB). Open.
3. Why is a 2-month-old asset (Pridex→Flex Capital, Aug 2026) already being shopped? **Open** — reads opportunistic/distressed, not strategic.
4. Does the 1.4bn revenue hold? **Only at 90% occupancy** — heroic given >50% YoY demand drop and mid-teens-and-rising vacancy.
5. Capital-intensity risk? **Yes** — Ricci calls Multispace the most capex-heavy peer; compresses buyer returns.
6. Aspace ownership splits? **Inconsistent in source** (75%+15%+12.75% = 102.75%). Open.
7. Revenue 1.5bn vs portfolio >2.5bn? **Different metrics** (operating-co revenue vs portfolio value) — don't conflate.
8. Geography: "four Moscow" vs Aug-sale "Moscow + St. Petersburg"? **Open** minor inconsistency.
9. Prior art? **Space1/WeWork Russia (Apr 2024)** — near-identical scale, clean comparable.
10. Consolidation wave? **Not yet** — only two data points; directional at most.
11. Fintech relevance? **None** — pure real-estate M&A; weak proptech tag.
12. Who needs whom more? **Seller (Flex Capital)** appears more motivated → pricing likely ≤ estimate floor.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Moscow flexible-office (coworking / serviced-office) stock reached ~426k sqm by H1 2026 and ~452k sqm by March 2026, having grown roughly 10x over a decade (per Kommersant / Moskovskaya Perspektiva, via web, as of 2026). The market is now in a supply-and-demand down-cycle: 2026 new-build delivery is guided to ~20k sqm, down ~44% YoY (Kommersant, via smart-lab, 2026), and H1 2026 take-up/absorption fell ~48.5% to ~20k sqm. "Why now": with new supply drying up and rents having risen ~31% in 2025, scaling via organic build-out is slow and capital-heavy, so established operators grow by buying ready, partly-leased stock — exactly the Multispace setup. Structure: fragmented and consolidating, dominated by a handful of Moscow operators; entry barriers are capital intensity (fit-out) and prime central locations, not regulation (analysis).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: managed area (sqm), occupancy, revenue/sqm, operator revenue. Key players — Space1 (market leader, >50k sqm, 14 Moscow locations; bought WeWork Russia in 2024), Aspace (#2 by managed coworking portfolio at 43.7k sqm per CMWP), plus Business Club, Refolio and others; basis of competition is location quality, fit-out and distribution to corporate tenants, not price. Recent moves: Pridex sold Multispace (4 sites, 18.2k sqm) to a group of private investors (now ООО «Флекс Капитал», Ksenia Bolotova) in Aug 2026 to refocus on its core design-and-build business; Aspace is now (Oct 2026) in talks to buy those same assets — a sub-two-month flip. Aspace's position: solid #2 catching up; its moat is scale + a stable commercial model and access to debt financing (per Refolio's Novoselov, via mergers.ru) — it is the only Russian flex-office operator with a credit rating and bond issuance (web, analysis). Aspace UC revenue ~1.5bn RUB (nearly 2x YoY), net revenue 1.1bn RUB (+52% YoY), per SPARK.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal/asset value of the Multispace operating business: 800–950m RUB for 18.2k sqm → implied ~44–52k RUB/sqm (`800m / 18,200 = 43,956` to `950m / 18,200 = 52,198` RUB/sqm) (analysis). Against potential revenue "at 90% occupancy" of up to 1.4bn RUB/yr, that is ~0.6–0.7x revenue (`800–950m / 1,400m`) — but note 1.4bn RUB is a conditional, not-yet-earned figure (Infoline-Analytics' Burmistrov, via mergers.ru), so treat the multiple as illustrative, not realized. No disclosed EBITDA → EV/EBITDA = no data. Internal comps: no comparable Russian flex-office deal exists in the base (the Jan–Oct 2026 "proptech/real-estate" hits are foreign — Habi, Yakeey, Property Finder, Terrenta — different model/geo, excluded by the comparability test); the only true precedent is the Aug 2026 Pridex→private-investors sale of the same assets (price undisclosed). [UNSOURCED]: Multispace standalone revenue, current occupancy, acquisition price for the Aspace deal.

**Risk flags.**
1. Demand down-cycle: H1 2026 take-up −48.5% and new supply −44% YoY signal a cooling market — buying capacity now risks paying for occupancy that may not materialize, and the 1.4bn RUB revenue case assumes 90% fill that is not yet real.
2. Capital intensity / asset quality: Ricci's Zhidkov calls Multispace sites the most capex-heavy among serviced-office operators — second-order risk that fit-out/maintenance spend compresses the return versus the 800–950m RUB price.
3. Rapid ownership churn + undisclosed economics: assets flipped from Pridex to private investors (Aug) to a potential Aspace sale (Oct) within ~2 months, with price undisclosed and only a single-source ("three sources of Kommersant") sourcing — deal, valuation and whether it even closes are all unconfirmed.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** If it closes, this is consolidation, not re-rating: Aspace adds 18.2k sqm (~42% of its current 43.7k) to narrow the gap with leader Space1, confirming that in a supply-starved Moscow market growth comes via M&A of ready stock rather than organic build (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: Aspace completes the buy within ~2 quarters near the 800–950m RUB range. What breaks it / trigger to watch: a disclosed price far above ~950m RUB (over-paying into a cooling market), or the talks lapsing — Aspace itself says it is "not an owner" and only "considering options". (analysis)
