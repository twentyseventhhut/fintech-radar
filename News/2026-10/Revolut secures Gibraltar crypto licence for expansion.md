# Revolut secures Gibraltar crypto licence to support international expansion plans

Revolut Global Digital Assets Limited will support the fintech's international crypto services alongside its existing UK and European operations.

By Brian Danga • Edited by James Hunt

Regulation•October 19, 2026, 6:42AM EDT

#### Quick Take

*   Revolut plans to announce specific regions and services supported by the newly licensed entity in due course.

Global fintech Revolut has secured a Distributed Ledger Technology provider licence from the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission for a newly formed entity, the company said in a statement shared with The Block on Monday.

The new entity, Revolut Global Digital Assets Limited, will support the company's international crypto operations, it said.

“Securing our DLT authorization from the GFSC reflects our commitment to operating at the highest standards of regulatory compliance,” Emil Urmashin, Revolut’s general manager for crypto and new bets, said in the statement.

Revolut already offers crypto services in the UK through Revolut Ltd and across the European Economic Area through Revolut Digital Assets Europe Ltd, which operates under the EU’s MiCA rules.

Advertisement

The company has more than 80 million retail customers worldwide and over 16 million crypto users, per the statement.

Revolut has doubled down on expansion this year. In March, the company received approval to launch Revolut Bank UK after British regulators lifted restrictions on its banking license. Last month, it secured conditional approval from the U.S. Office of the Comptroller of the Currency as it pursued a U.S. banking charter.

The company also began rolling out its euro-pegged EURR stablecoin to selected customers in Denmark, Poland, and Portugal in August, with broader availability across the EEA planned later this year.

Revolut did not say which markets its Gibraltar entity will serve or when. It said details on target regions and rollouts would come in due course.

# Context-enrichment: Revolut secures Gibraltar crypto licence for expansion
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-19 Revolut said the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) granted a Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT) provider licence (under Part 7 of Gibraltar's Financial Services Act) to a **newly formed** entity, Revolut Global Digital Assets Limited (RGDAL). Scope per press reports: transmission/execution of virtual-asset orders, virtual-asset custody, and staking. Stated purpose: support Revolut's **international** crypto operations, on top of existing UK (Revolut Ltd) and EEA (Revolut Digital Assets Europe Ltd, under MiCA) crypto permissions.

**+ Why structured this way / what it reveals.** This is a *licence grant for a new legal vehicle*, not a product launch. The PR is deliberately vague: Revolut "did not say which markets its Gibraltar entity will serve or when" — target regions/services come "in due course". That is the anti-PR tell: no live markets, no rollout dates, no incremental user/revenue figures tied to Gibraltar. The reused headline figures (80M+ retail, 16M+ crypto users) are *group* numbers, not Gibraltar-attributable. Gibraltar is a well-trodden regulatory wrapper (DLT regime live since 2018; ~13 licensees incl. Xapo, Bullish, eToro X, LMAX Digital, Tap Global) — Revolut is buying an additional passporting-light base for non-UK/non-EEA jurisdictions, likely as a lighter path than chasing MiCA-equivalent regimes one by one. One secondary source (Crypto Times) frames it as enabling resumed customer onboarding — treat as unconfirmed by the primary statement (analysis).

## [1] Competitors / peers
Gibraltar DLT-licensed peers: Xapo VASP, Bullish, eToro X, LMAX Digital, Tap Global (~13 total). Broader crypto-brokerage competition: [[Deutsche Bank signs crypto exchange Bullish as customer]], Coinbase, Binance, Crypto.com, Kraken. Among neobanks, Revolut is the most crypto-forward (Revolut X exchange since May 2024; EURR euro stablecoin via Bridge/Stripe rolling out from Aug 2026 — [[Revolut begins phased EURR stablecoin rollout]] / corpus [[Revolut launches fee-free 1 1 stablecoin conversions]]).

**+ Why the lay of the land is this way.** Gibraltar competes with the EU's MiCA, UK FCA registration and the UAE (VARA) as a "credible but faster" crypto licensing venue. For Revolut the real comps are *global exchanges* (Coinbase, Binance), not the small Gibraltar cohort: Revolut's edge is distribution (80M+ app users) rather than crypto-native depth, so a Gibraltar base matters only insofar as it unlocks *new geographies* its UK/EEA licences don't cover. Second-order: a pure licence grant doesn't move Revolut's competitive position until markets actually go live.

## [2] Company history / fit
Fits a dense 2026 licence-accumulation pattern: UK bank restrictions lifted (Mar 2026), US OCC conditional approval (Sep 2026 — [[Revolut takes important step toward becoming a US bank with conditional OCC approval]]), MiCA via Cyprus ([[Revolut secures MiCA license from Cyprus regulator]]), EURR stablecoin, plus banking/e-money pushes in Mexico, Colombia, Philippines, Singapore. On the IPO/valuation backdrop: [[Revolut hits $121.2B valuation as IPO talk intensifies]], [[Revolut favors US primary listing for potential IPO]].

**+ Why the company acts this way.** Revolut's strategy is "collect every regulatory wrapper that unlocks a geography/product", because its whole thesis is cross-selling more products to the same app base (11 product lines >£100M revenue in FY2025). Crypto is a high-margin, low-capital attach product; a cheap Gibraltar DLT wrapper is a logical marginal add ahead of a potential IPO where "global, fully-licensed" is part of the equity story.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Low novelty. Revolut already runs crypto in the UK and EEA; Gibraltar is a *third wrapper* for other regions, not a new capability. **Zero disclosed traction** tied to Gibraltar — no markets, no dates, no users. Genuinely new only as "new legal entity + new jurisdiction", not "new product live".

**+ Why value-add is (not yet) real.** The margin in crypto-brokerage sits with whoever holds the custody + order-flow relationship; Revolut captures that via its own app, so a Gibraltar licence is accretive *if* it opens sizeable new markets. Until regions are named, this is optionality, not value — the licence is necessary but not sufficient. Watch for the "in due course" market announcement as the real catalyst.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Revolut will "announce specific regions and services in due course". Sentiment is incremental-positive (crypto/regulatory momentum reinforces the IPO narrative) but this specific item is minor. Regulatory backdrop: MiCA (EEA), UK FCA, Gibraltar DLT, UAE/VARA — Revolut is assembling a multi-jurisdiction map. Risks: market-selection execution, GFSC conditions, crypto-cycle sensitivity, and reputational overhang (Sep 2026 KYC/Bitcoin data exposure incident — [[Revolut to cover costs for 680 customers hit by data hack]]).

**+ Why the market goes this way.** Jurisdiction-shopping for crypto wrappers is the sector norm post-MiCA; the counterintuitive risk is that accumulating licences faster than live products creates a "licence inventory" that invites regulator and investor scrutiny on *actual* AML/custody controls rather than headline breadth.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Crypto-asset services within a neobank. Gibraltar's DLT regime has been live since 2018 (one of the earliest purpose-built blockchain regimes; principles/outcomes-based under Part 7 of the Financial Services Act), with ~13 DLT licensees as of 2026 (per company.gi / CMS, as of 2026). Structure: the *licensing* layer is fragmented across venues (MiCA for EEA, UK FCA, Gibraltar DLT, UAE/VARA), while *value* in crypto-brokerage concentrates with whoever holds custody + order-flow. "Why now": post-MiCA, incumbents and neobanks are jurisdiction-shopping for the fastest credible wrapper to unlock new geographies; Gibraltar markets itself as a faster, credible alternative to MiCA for non-EEA reach (analysis). Drivers: regulatory clarity (tailwind) vs crypto-cycle sensitivity of volumes (headwind).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: crypto MAU/active users, trading volume, take-rate on trades, custody AUC. Key players: global exchanges (Coinbase, Binance, Kraken, Crypto.com) compete on depth/liquidity; neobanks (Revolut, and to a lesser extent others) compete on distribution. Gibraltar DLT cohort peers: Xapo VASP, Bullish, eToro X, LMAX Digital, Tap Global; Bullish won GFSC approval for tokenized-securities trading in 2026 ([[Bullish wins Gibraltar approval for tokenized securities trading]]). Recent moves: Revolut X exchange (May 2024), EURR euro stablecoin via Bridge/Stripe (phased from Aug 2026). Protagonist position: **ahead among neobanks on crypto breadth**, but a distribution-led challenger (not a crypto-native) vs the global exchanges — moat is its 80M+ app base and cross-sell, not liquidity depth (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** Revolut is private; no public market cap. Reference points: last primary/secondary valuation ~$121.2B ([[Revolut hits $121.2B valuation as IPO talk intensifies]]); prior $75B round ([[Revolut raises $3B at $75 billion valuation]]). Against FY2025 revenue of £4.5bn (~$6.0bn), implied P/S ≈ $121.2bn / $6.0bn ≈ **20.2x** (analysis; mixing a secondary-market valuation with reported revenue) — rich vs scaled fintech peers but consistent with Revolut's ~46% revenue growth and 38% pre-tax margin. No Gibraltar-specific revenue to multiple — "no data". Public exchange comp Coinbase trades on revenue/EBITDA multiples far below 20x [UNSOURCED exact figure], i.e. Revolut's premium is a neobank-growth premium, not a crypto-exchange one.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Execution / optionality risk** — a licence with no named markets or dates is optionality, not revenue; value realizes only if sizeable geographies go live (second-order: delay turns "licence inventory" into scrutiny rather than growth).
2. **AML/custody + reputation risk** — crypto custody/order-flow invites regulator focus; the Sep 2026 KYC/Bitcoin-data exposure ([[Revolut to cover costs for 680 customers hit by data hack]]) is an overhang on trust just as Revolut scales crypto.
3. **Crypto-cycle sensitivity** — crypto revenue is volume-linked and pro-cyclical; a downturn compresses this attach product's contribution.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector: reinforces that neobanks will assemble multi-jurisdiction crypto wrappers rather than build crypto-native depth — Gibraltar becomes a standard "third wrapper" for global reach (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Revolut names large new crypto markets (APAC/LatAm) with disclosed volumes within ~2 quarters, this re-rates from optionality to traction; if the "in due course" announcement keeps slipping, it stays a non-event. Trigger to watch: the specific regions/services announcement.

## Earnings Review

**No full earnings report in the news** — this is a licence grant, not a results print. Revolut reports annually; its latest filing is the FY2025 Annual Report (year ended 31 Dec 2025), published 2026-04-29 (IR-covered). Related latest-results context below for grounding only.

- Revenue: **£4.5bn, +46% YoY** (≈$6.0bn group revenue per Revolut's own release).
- Pre-tax profit: **£1.7bn, +57% YoY**; pre-tax margin **38%** (up from 35% FY2024) — fifth consecutive profitable year.
- Scale: retail customers **68.3m (+30%)**; business customers **767k (+33%)**; total customer balances **$67.5bn / £50.2bn (+66%)**; loan book **£2.2bn (+120%)**.
- 11 product lines each exceeded **£100m** revenue (breadth, not single-line dependence).
