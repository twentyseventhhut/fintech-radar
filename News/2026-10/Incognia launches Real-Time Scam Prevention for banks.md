Incognia Launches Real-time Scam Prevention Powered by Patented Location Intelligence 
 New solution combines real-world relationship intelligence with multiple risk signals to detect scams before money moves 
Subscribe to Incognia’s content

 SAN JOSE, Calif. — October 21, 2026 — Incognia , the leader in cross-device risk intelligence, today announced the launch of Real-Time Scam Prevention, an AI-powered solution that helps financial institutions detect and prevent payment scams, including authorized-payment fraud and social-engineering schemes, by combining Physical Social Graph intelligence with additional risk signals. 
 Authorized-payment scams present a growing challenge for financial institutions because the customer making the payment may be using a legitimate account, device and credentials. According to the FTC, consumers reported losing $3.5 billion to impersonation scams in 2025, nearly triple the amount reported in 2020. While traditional fraud controls focus on verifying the person initiating a transaction, they may miss signs that a legitimate customer is being manipulated into sending money to a scammer. 
 Incognia addresses this gap with its Physical Social Graph, powered by patented Trusted Locations technology. Using persistent, apartment-level indoor location intelligence, it reveals real-world connections between payment senders and recipients that conventional transaction data may miss, helping identify suspicious relationships and coordinated fraud activity. 
 In an analysis of transactions from a major financial institution, Incognia found that its Physical social graph technology alone identified approximately 70% of scam transactions in the analyzed dataset. The findings demonstrated how historical real-world relationships can provide valuable evidence of scam risk, even when conventional fraud signals appear legitimate. 
 “Banks have gotten very good at determining whether the person making a payment is legitimate, but with a scam, the legitimate customer is often the one making the payment,” said André Ferraz, co-founder and CEO of Incognia. “The question becomes - who are they sending money to, and does that relationship make sense? That is the context Incognia is bringing into scam detection.” 
 Incognia's patented Trusted Locations technology establishes persistent location patterns based on where users regularly spend time. Rather than relying on a snapshot of where a customer is when a transaction occurs, Real-time Scam Detection compares historical location patterns between the sender and recipient to create Incognia’s Physical Social Graph that may indicate additional risk. 
 Incognia's AI model combines Physical Social Graph intelligence with mule-account detection, active-call detection, remote-access-tool detection, device integrity and location-spoofing detection to assess payment risk in real time. Banks use these assessments alongside their own data and policies to determine the appropriate action. 
 The solution also connects payment activity and real-world relationships across participating financial institutions, helping reveal suspicious networks and coordinated activity that individual banks may not be able to identify on their own. 
 “Two people can live in the same building and have no real-world relationship, while two people living in completely different cities may know each other well,” said Alan Gomes, CTO of Incognia. “That's why distance alone isn't enough. Our apartment-level indoor location intelligence identifies patterns and connections over time, giving banks context that IP addresses or GPS coordinates alone cannot provide.” 
 For more information, visit Incognia.com . 
 About Incognia 
 Incognia provides cross-device risk intelligence for companies that rely on trusted digital interactions. Its technology blends location behavior, device integrity and behavioral analysis to create a risk signal that cannot be faked at scale and shows whether a user is trusted or risky. With real-time assessments and actionable data, Incognia helps organizations stop repeat and large-scale fraud while reducing friction for legitimate users.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What launched (2026-10-21).** Incognia (San Jose/Palo Alto CA; founded by André Ferraz, co-founder/CEO) announced **Real-Time Scam Prevention**, an AI product that helps banks detect authorized-payment (APP) scams and social-engineering fraud *before money moves*. The core novelty is its **Physical Social Graph**, built on patented **Trusted Locations** tech: persistent, apartment-level indoor location patterns that infer real-world relationships between a payment *sender* and *recipient*. The claim: historical sender↔recipient location overlap reveals whether a transfer relationship "makes sense," catching scams where the legitimate account holder is the one authorizing the payment. Incognia says the Physical Social Graph *alone* flagged ~70% of scam transactions in an analyzed dataset from one major FI, and the full model layers in mule-account detection, active-call detection, remote-access-tool (RAT) detection, device integrity, and location-spoofing detection. It also claims cross-institution network effects (linking payment activity across participating banks to surface coordinated mule networks).

**Why the APP/scam gap matters.** Traditional fraud controls verify *who is initiating* a payment — exactly the wrong lens for APP scams, where a genuine customer, on a trusted device with valid credentials, is socially engineered into paying a fraudster. This is the structural blind spot Incognia is selling against. US framing: FTC reports consumers lost **$3.5B to impersonation scams in 2025**, ~3x the 2020 level (bank-impersonation and government-impersonation among the most costly; social media the top attack vector). Real-time rails compress the fraud-decision window to seconds and settle irrevocably, raising the stakes — FedNow (live since 2023) and RTP volumes are in a high-growth phase (US RTP volume projected ~8B transactions in 2026), so "stop it before it sends" products have a widening TAM.

**Regulatory tailwind (mostly offshore, for now).** The sharpest reimbursement pressure is in the **UK**: the PSR's **mandatory APP reimbursement regime went live 2024-10-07** (up to £85k, cost split 50/50 between sending and receiving PSPs). One-year data: APP fraud down ~21% (~£73m), reimbursement rates up 54%→65%, ~£173m returned to victims. That regime makes *receiving*-bank liability real and rewards exactly the sender↔recipient-relationship signals Incognia sells. The US has **no equivalent federal reimbursement mandate**, so the US pitch is loss-avoidance/ROI rather than compliance — a softer commercial trigger than the UK.

**Company/funding posture.** Incognia raised a **$31M Series B (Jan 2024, led by Bessemer**; Point72 Ventures, Prosus, FJ Labs, Valor), ~$46.5M total; offices in San Jose, NY, São Paulo, Recife. It positions as "cross-device risk intelligence" — location + device integrity + behavior. This launch extends a steady 2026 cadence of scam/fraud product and thought-leadership output: see [[Incognia launches AI Rule Builder for fraud detection]] (2026-03), [[Incognia report only 16% of firms catch mule-account fraud early]] (2026-05), [[Incognia the state of mule account handovers in 2026]], and [[Incognia's Andre Ferraz on the AI-enabled fraud landscape]] (2026-05).

**Competitive context.** The APP/scam-detection field is crowded and consolidating. Behavioral-biometrics leader **BioCatch** (760M users, ~350 banks) is being **acquired by Visa for $2.4B (2026-08)** — a signal that networks want scam/ATO signal in-house. [[Feedzai raises $75M at $2B+ valuation for fraud platform]] (2025-10) ships prebuilt APP/scam/mule scenarios; **Featurespace** (now part of Visa) does adaptive behavioral analytics on velocity/device/beneficiary history. The **ECB picked [[ECB selects Feedzai for digital euro fraud detection]]**; the **Fed shipped [[Federal Reserve launches FedNow network intelligence API for fraud]]** (2026-05); India pushes RBI MuleHunter ([[India asks banks to adopt RBI's MuleHunter AI against mule accounts]]). Incognia's wedge vs. these behavioral/transaction players is its *location-derived relationship graph* — a signal competitors largely lack, but one that depends on mobile-SDK location consent at scale.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. The headline metric — "Physical Social Graph alone identified ~70% of scam transactions" — is from *one* major FI on an *analyzed dataset*. Is that in-production detection or a retrospective backtest? What was the false-positive rate / precision? 70% recall at an unstated FPR is marketing, not performance.
2. Does "identified 70%" mean the graph *flagged transactions that were scams* (recall), or that 70% of its flags were scams (precision)? The vendor phrasing conflates these. Without both numbers the claim is unfalsifiable.
3. Apartment-level indoor location between *sender and recipient* requires both parties to run an Incognia SDK with granular location consent. Scam recipients (mules, strangers) are usually NOT customers of the sender's bank. How does Incognia have location history on the recipient at all? This is the central credibility question.
4. The cross-institution "Physical Social Graph across participating FIs" only works if enough banks adopt AND share. What's actual bank adoption today? Network-effect claims pre-adoption are vaporware.
5. Privacy/regulatory exposure: persistent apartment-level location tracking of bank customers (and inferring their social relationships) invites scrutiny under state privacy laws, CFPB 1033 data-rights, and GDPR for any EU footprint. Is consent architecture litigation-proof?
6. "Trusted Locations" is "patented" — but patents don't equal accuracy. Has any independent party (not Incognia) validated the location-relationship inference?
7. The product targets APP/social-engineering scams, yet the strongest buying trigger (UK PSR reimbursement liability) is UK-only. In the US there's no reimbursement mandate — so what is the US bank's hard ROI to buy this vs. eating fraud losses?
8. Competitive moat: Visa now owns BOTH BioCatch ($2.4B) and Featurespace. A standalone ~$46M-funded vendor selling into banks that increasingly get fraud signal bundled from Visa/Mastercard faces distribution disadvantage. Can Incognia survive channel squeeze?
9. Scammers adapt: once "relationship-makes-sense" scoring is known, fraudsters can coach victims toward recipients with plausible location ties, or spoof/rotate mule accounts faster than the graph updates. What's the adversarial half-life of this signal?
10. Location-spoofing detection is listed as a feature — but the whole product rests on trusting location. A determined attacker who defeats spoofing detection collapses the graph. How robust is the anti-spoofing under real GPS/emulator/rooted-device attack?
11. "Detects scams before money moves" implies real-time inline scoring on instant rails (FedNow/RTP) where decisions happen in <seconds. What's the measured latency, and does it actually block or just score for a human review that's too slow for instant settlement?
12. Incognia's heritage and much of its proof (Webull Brazil onboarding, LatAm bank case studies, Cabify/Upwork marketplace trust) is consumer-internet and LatAm. Does any *US/UK retail bank* run this in production for APP scams, or is this a launch ahead of real banking references?
13. Single-source story: the only cited source is Incognia's own newsroom/PR (plus PRNewswire/GlobalFinTechSeries syndication). No bank named, no analyst, no customer quote. Treat all metrics as vendor-reported.

**Importance: 3/5** — A credible, differentiated product (location-relationship graph) from a well-funded, repeat player hitting the genuinely hard APP/social-engineering scam problem at the right moment (FTC $3.5B impostor losses, instant-rail growth, UK PSR liability). But it is an incremental product launch, not a funding/M&A/regulatory event; every performance number is vendor-reported from a single PR source; the recipient-side location-data premise is unproven at scale; and the competitive field is consolidating under Visa (BioCatch + Featurespace), capping the strategic weight of a standalone launch.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Fraud detection & prevention in BFSI ~$8.85bn in 2026, with the broader FDP market on a ~17.5% CAGR path toward ~$80bn as AI/ML, real-time payments and regulation drive demand (per GlobeNewswire/researcher citations, as of 2026-10-21) — treat the exact figures as third-party report estimates, not audited. The specific pocket Incognia targets — authorized-push-payment (APP) / social-engineering scams — is the fastest-growing fraud class: FTC says US consumers lost $3.5bn to impersonation scams in 2025, ~3x the 2020 level (per Incognia PR citing FTC); UK Finance reported £1.28bn of 2025 payment-fraud losses with APP fraud up 19% (per UK Finance, via web, 2026). **Why now:** regulation is converting scam losses into direct bank P&L. The UK PSR mandatory-reimbursement regime (live 7 Oct 2024) forces PSPs to refund APP-fraud victims within 5 business days, with liability split **50/50 between sending and receiving PSP** (cap lowered to £85k; £173m reimbursed in year one, ~88% of covered losses) — per Hogan Lovells/PSR, via web. The 50/50 receiving-side liability is what makes Incognia's "connect sender↔recipient relationships across participating banks" pitch commercially timed (analysis). Structure: fragmented and consolidating — many point-solution vendors (device, behavioral-biometric, graph, risk-orchestration) with payment networks now rolling them up. Barriers: data network effects (cross-institution signal), patents, and bank integration/switching costs.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: scam-detection rate / recall, false-positive (friction) rate, # of FIs and devices covered, net revenue retention. Named peers and basis of competition — product/data-signal differentiation, not price: **BioCatch** (behavioral biometrics — typing/swipe cadence; 350+ FIs, 21 countries, 760M users, 1.8bn devices), **Feedzai** (transaction-risk/AML platform + fraud intelligence network), **Featurespace** (adaptive behavioral analytics, acquired by Visa in 2024), **Sardine** (device intelligence + behavioral biometrics across full lifecycle). Recent moves: Visa agreed to buy BioCatch for **$2.4bn** (03 Aug 2026); Feedzai raised $75M at **$2bn+** (Oct 2025, [[Feedzai raises $75M at $2B+ valuation for fraud platform]]); Sardine raised $25M with National Bank of Canada (May 2026, [[Sardine raises $25 million as National Bank of Canada partners on fraud]]); ECB selected Feedzai for digital-euro fraud detection ([[ECB selects Feedzai for digital euro fraud detection]]). Incognia's position: **niche challenger** differentiated on apartment-level **location/Physical Social Graph** signal rather than behavioral biometrics or transaction graphs — a signal peers largely lack. Moat (analysis): data network effects — cross-institution location relationships that strengthen with each participating bank; hard to fake at scale. But it is Series-B scale vs incumbents with hundreds of FIs.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Network-effect cold-start / adoption risk** — the cross-bank Physical Social Graph only works if *multiple* FIs participate and share signal; a single-bank deployment captures far less value, and banks are reluctant to pool data. The headline "~70% of scam transactions identified" is **vendor PR on one bank's dataset** (not peer-reviewed, no false-positive rate disclosed) — "announced" capability, not independently validated live performance.
2. **Crowded, consolidating category with deeper-pocketed incumbents** — BioCatch (now Visa), Featurespace (Visa), Feedzai, Sardine all chase the same scam/APP pocket; a network-owned rival bundled into Visa's stack can out-distribute a Series-B vendor and commoditize the signal. Disintermediation/margin-capture risk if the payment networks own the fraud layer.
3. **Location-data regulatory & privacy exposure** — persistent apartment-level indoor location tracking is the core asset but also the core liability under GDPR/US state privacy law and app-store permission tightening; a consent/permission change could degrade the primary signal (second-order: moat erosion).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** APP-scam liability regimes (UK PSR 50/50, EU, and US pressure) are re-rating "scam prevention" from a nice-to-have into a mandated cost center — fueling both new entrants (Incognia's location angle) and consolidation (Visa's $2.4bn BioCatch take-out at 13x) (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: Incognia either signs enough FIs to make the cross-bank graph defensible and becomes an acquisition target itself, or stays a point-signal vendor sold *into* orchestration platforms. **Watch:** named multi-FI (not single-bank pilot) live deployments and independent/third-party validation of the 70% figure — absent those within ~12 months, the thesis is weak.
