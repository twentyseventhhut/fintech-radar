# Brim Financial partners Manulife Bank on credit cards

> [!info] 2026-10-26 · 1 упоминаний · 1 источника(ов) с текстом

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

Toronto-based Brim Financial has announced a partnership with Manulife Bank to transition its credit card portfolio and modernize its payments platform. 
The deployment brings card issuing, processing, servicing, loyalty, and customer engagement into an integrated platform embedded within Manulife Bank’s digital banking experience. Brim says the approach will help the bank introduce products faster and streamline operations.
“Brim’s modern, flexible technology strengthens our digital foundation and supports our transformation journey,” said Katy Boshart, President and CEO of Manulife Bank.
The platform also supports digital customer journeys for Manulife’s independent advisors, mortgage specialists, and customer-facing teams, with the goal of deepening relationships across the bank’s business.
Brim founder and CEO Rasha Katabi said financial institutions are reassessing technology architectures that depend on multiple vendors, which can increase costs and slow innovation. The company positions its integrated platform as a way for banks to manage cards and payments within their own digital environments.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** Toronto-based Brim Financial announced (2026-10-26) a partnership with **Manulife Bank** to migrate the bank's credit card portfolio onto Brim's platform and modernize its payments stack. Brim's deployment bundles card **issuing, processing, servicing, loyalty and customer engagement** into one integrated platform embedded inside Manulife Bank's digital banking experience. The platform also extends to digital journeys for Manulife's independent advisors, mortgage specialists and customer-facing teams. Quotes: Katy Boshart (President & CEO, Manulife Bank) on strengthening its "digital foundation"; Rasha Katabi (Brim founder & CEO) on banks moving off multi-vendor architectures. ([fintech.ca](https://www.fintech.ca/2026/10/26/brim-financial-partners-with-manulife-bank-to-modernize-credit-cards-and-payments/), [newswire.ca release](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/brim-financial-partners-with-manulife-bank-on-credit-cards-and-payments-modernization-889638619.html))

**[1] Who Brim is.** Brim is a Canadian **credit-card-as-a-service / platform-as-a-service (PaaS)** provider that is itself a licensed card issuer and bundles the full card value chain (issuing, processing, servicing, loyalty, omni-channel engagement) into a single modular stack — pitched as a replacement for the traditional 5-vendor issuer stack. Existing Canadian bank/credit-union clients cited publicly include **Laurentian Bank** (Visa cards launched 2023 — cut vendor count from five to one, removed ~90% of manual card work), **Canadian Western Bank** and **Affinity Credit Union**; brand partners include **Air France-KLM**. It also has a **Mastercard** partnership for North American growth. ([The Logic](https://thelogic.co/news/brim-financial-gears-up-for-north-american-growth-with-mastercard-partnership/))

**[2] Money / scale.** Brim raised an **US$85M Series C (April 2024)** led by **Export Development Canada (EDC)**, with Vistara Growth and returning investors (White Owl Group, Epic Ventures, Zions Bank), earmarked for **U.S. and global expansion**, product automation and open-banking integration. ([betakit](https://betakit.com/brim-financial-closes-85-million-series-c-led-by-edc-to-fund-us-expansion/), [pymnts](https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/2024/brim-financial-plans-international-expansion-after-85-million-series-c/)) More recently Brim arranged a **US$300M credit facility with i80 Group** (Dec 2025) to fund balance-sheet / funding-as-a-service capacity — see [[i80 Group secures $300 million credit facility with Brim Financial]].

**[3] Why it matters / where it fits.** This is a reference win for Brim: migrating an incumbent federally-regulated **bank's** entire credit card portfolio (not just a greenfield fintech card) validates the integrated-issuer thesis and deepens Brim's Canadian bank footprint alongside Laurentian/CWB. It lands amid a broader Canadian issuer-modernization wave — cf. [[Peoples Group teams with Fiserv to build Canada's next-generation payments platform.]] (incumbent-processor route) — and the global vertically-integrated issuer-processing trend profiled in [[Directory of UK and European card issuing and program platforms]] (Marqeta, Thredd, Pomelo, etc.). Brim has also been building out leadership/open-banking depth — see [[Canada's former open banking lead joins Brim Financial]].

**[4] Open items.** No disclosed financial terms, card volume, portfolio size, contract length, or go-live/migration timeline for the Manulife Bank deal. Unclear whether Brim acts purely as tech/PaaS or also as balance-sheet issuer for this portfolio, and which network (Visa/Mastercard) Manulife's cards run on. No independent confirmation beyond the company release and its syndication (fintech.ca / newswire.ca / CanadianSME).

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team Q&A**

1. **Is this an arm's-length story or a vendor press release?** Vendor-sourced. Originates from a Brim/Manulife joint announcement syndicated via newswire.ca and fintech.ca/CanadianSME. No independent reporting or third-party verification. Treat claims as company-framed.

2. **Are there hard numbers?** No. No deal value, no portfolio size (# of cardholders / $ outstandings), no fees, no timeline. Only the US$85M Series C and the i80 US$300M facility are externally quantified, and those are separate prior events.

3. **Is the migration actually live or just signed?** Open. Release says Manulife "will move" its portfolio — language implies forward-looking migration, not a completed cutover. Migrating a live bank card portfolio is a multi-quarter program with real execution risk.

4. **Does Brim issue the cards or just provide tech?** Open/ambiguous. Brim is a licensed issuer elsewhere, but the release frames this as an embedded platform "within Manulife Bank's digital banking experience," suggesting Manulife remains the issuer/balance-sheet holder and Brim is the PaaS layer. Not explicitly stated.

5. **Which card network?** Not stated. Brim has a Mastercard partnership and has powered Laurentian Visa cards — so it is network-agnostic; Manulife's network is unspecified here.

6. **Is this a new logo or an expansion?** New logo (Manulife Bank) — materially strengthens Brim's Canadian bank client roster beyond Laurentian/CWB/Affinity.

7. **How big is Manulife Bank's card book?** Manulife Bank is a relatively small Canadian bank (insurer-owned, strong in mortgages/deposits); its credit card portfolio is not a large national book. So the volume win may be modest in absolute terms even if strategically symbolic.

8. **Switching/concentration risk for the bank?** Real. Consolidating issuing/processing/servicing/loyalty into one vendor removes the 5-vendor pain but concentrates operational and resilience risk in a single ~Series-C-stage fintech. A Brim outage or failure would hit the entire card program.

9. **Is Brim financially durable enough to anchor a bank's cards?** Partially mitigating: US$85M Series C (2024, EDC-led) + US$300M i80 facility (2025) give runway and funding capacity, but it remains a private growth-stage company, not a Fiserv/TSYS-scale incumbent.

10. **Competitive read-across?** Contrasts with the incumbent-processor path (Peoples Group + Fiserv in Canada). Brim's pitch is the integrated-modern-stack alternative; this deal is a proof point but one bank does not settle the Canadian processor market.

11. **Any regulatory/outsourcing angle?** Open. OSFI third-party/technology-outsourcing (B-10) requirements apply when a federally regulated bank outsources core card operations; not addressed in the release.

12. **Does it move Brim's revenue needle?** Unknown — no disclosed economics. Strategic/reference value likely exceeds near-term revenue.

13. **Any conflicting prior coverage?** No contradictions found; prior vault notes (i80 facility, open-banking hire, card-platform directory) are consistent and distinct events — this is a genuinely new story (freshness: fresh).

14. **Reputational/affiliate exposure (Manulife side)?** Manulife is also in flux on partnerships broadly; no red flags specific to this card deal surfaced.

15. **What would raise importance?** Disclosed portfolio size/volume, confirmed go-live, or evidence this is a template for other Manulife/insurer-bank card modernizations.

**Importance: 2/5** — Credible, strategically meaningful reference win for a notable Canadian issuer-PaaS (new bank logo, validates integrated-stack thesis), but vendor-sourced, zero disclosed economics/scale/timeline, forward-looking migration, and Manulife Bank's card book is modest. Infrastructure-watch relevance, not market-moving.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Credit-card-issuing-as-a-service: platforms that hand banks/fintechs a single stack for card issuing + processing + servicing + loyalty, displacing the legacy multi-vendor model (issuer-processor + loyalty TSP + servicing). Global card-issuing-platform market ~$14.2bn (2025), projected to ~$38.7bn by 2034 at ~13.8% CAGR (per marketintelo/dataintelo secondary reports, as of 2026 — vendor-report figure, treat as indicative not audited). Value chain is fragmented between pure-play processors (Marqeta, Galileo/now SoFi Tech Solutions, Pine Labs) and integrated card-as-a-service suites (Brim). Entry barriers: scheme certifications (Visa/Mastercard), PCI/bank-grade compliance, and the switching cost of a live card portfolio — re-platforming an in-market book is a multi-year, high-friction migration, which is the real moat on the incumbent side. "Why now": banks are actively re-assessing architectures that depend on multiple vendors to cut cost and speed product cycles (stated by both parties here); Canadian mid-tier banks in particular are modernizing card tech rather than building in-house.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/volume on platform, take rate (bps on volume), number of live programs/issuers, revenue-per-program; for the vendor, migration count and gross retention of card books. Key players and basis of competition: Marqeta (API-first processor, scale), Galileo/SoFi Tech Solutions (processing + core + charter bundle), Pine Labs (end-to-end issuing, APAC), Fiserv/Elan (incumbent North American issuer-processing), vs Brim's pitch of one integrated stack embedded inside the bank's own digital experience. Basis of competition = breadth of integrated product + speed-to-launch + loyalty, not raw price. Recent peer moves: US Bank–Fiserv integrating Elan issuing into Credit Choice (2025-06, [[US Bank and Fiserv partner on digital card solutions]]); Nexi–Visa Managed Model for German banks (2026-04, [[Nexi and Visa launch managed card issuing for German banks]]); Pine Labs for Pan Asia Bank (2026-01, [[Pine Labs deploys card platform for Sri Lanka's Pan Asia Bank]]). Protagonist position: Brim is a niche-but-credible Canadian specialist — existing bank references (Laurentian Bank 2021/2023, Canadian Western Bank, Affinity Credit Union) plus Air France; Manulife Bank extends the bank-migration track record. Position = catching up on scale vs Marqeta, ahead on integrated card-as-a-service breadth in its home market (analysis). Moat = switching costs of a migrated live book + scheme/compliance certifications (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** Public anchor — Marqeta (MQ): net revenue $176m Q2 FY2026 (+17% YoY), gross profit $122m, TPV $120bn (+32%), take rate ~10bps, market cap ~$1.9bn (per investors.marqeta.com / stocktitan, as of 2026-08). Annualizing Q2 net revenue → ~$704m; P/S ≈ $1.9bn / $0.70bn = ~2.7x (approx, annualized run-rate not full-year actual — treat as rough). Brim (private): Series C C$85m closed Apr-2024 led by EDC Investments; valuation undisclosed but described as a "significant up round" vs a Series B that valued it near ~C$100m; ~C$102m raised total (per betakit/fintechfutures). No revenue disclosed → no public multiple computable for Brim ("no data"). Internal comps: [[i80 Group secures $300 million credit facility with Brim Financial]] (Dec-2025 debt facility, not an equity mark), [[US Bank and Fiserv partner on digital card solutions]], [[Nexi and Visa launch managed card issuing for German banks]]. EV/EBITDA, EV/Rev clean figures for the private names → [UNSOURCED]. Marqeta at ~2.7x P/S on ~17% net-revenue growth reads in-line-to-cheap for a profitable issuer-processor (analysis).

**Risk flags.**
- Announced vs live: release says Manulife Bank "will move" its portfolio to Brim — this is a signed migration, not yet a completed cutover; re-platforming a live credit-card book carries real execution/timeline risk, and no go-live date, portfolio size, or economics are disclosed (who's silent on what).
- Customer concentration / lumpy revenue: Brim's growth leans on a handful of named bank migrations (Laurentian, CWB, Affinity, now Manulife); each win is large but the base is thin, so one churned or delayed migration moves the needle materially.
- Disintermediation / scale gap vs Marqeta-class processors and scheme-led "managed issuing" (Nexi–Visa, US Bank–Fiserv): if schemes and incumbent processors bundle managed issuing for banks, a mid-scale specialist risks being squeezed on both price and distribution.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Another Canadian mid-tier bank choosing an integrated card-as-a-service suite over in-house or legacy multi-vendor stacks supports the thesis that bank card-tech re-platforming is a live, recurring deal flow — not a one-off. Falsifiable thesis: Brim converts bank references into a repeatable migration motion and US expansion (the stated use of Series C) within ~12–18 months. Watch/trigger: a disclosed US bank win or a stated live-date/portfolio size for Manulife would confirm traction; conversely, prolonged silence on go-live or a scheme-led managed-issuing land-grab in Canada would break it.
