FIS has launched the Agentic Insights Suite, an AI-driven platform designed to provide the office of the CFO with predictive intelligence on cash flow and risk. By unifying fragmented data across disconnected systems, the suite enables finance professionals to move beyond retrospective reporting toward active liquidity management in an era of elevated capital costs.

The FIS Agentic Insights Suite functions as a central intelligence hub for finance, credit, and treasury teams. It is built to address a significant adoption gap in the industry; while 46% of global finance leaders in a Deloitte survey identified working capital optimization as a primary use case for agentic AI, only 14% have successfully integrated such agents. The suite aims to close this divide by cleaning and connecting data from internal systems—including ERP, CRM, and treasury platforms—with external sources like public data and credit bureaus.

A key component of the launch is the multi-horizon cash flow forecasting module. Unlike traditional tools that provide a single estimate, this module utilizes machine learning to run multiple simulations, testing how variables such as faster outflows or delayed inflows impact overall liquidity. The suite also provides real-time account scoring and actionable recommendations, allowing teams to prioritize high-priority risks immediately rather than reconciling conflicting numbers by hand.

The platform is designed for rapid deployment, typically becoming operational within weeks through a self-serve setup. It serves as an intelligence layer that integrates directly with existing FIS workflows, specifically within Receivables products like GETPAID and BillerIQ, as well as Treasury products including Integrity and Quantum. Users interact with the system through a natural language interface and Power BI dashboards. Planned future releases for the suite will add payment anomaly detection and dispute forecasting capabilities.

> "Most finance teams can tell you what happened to their cash last quarter; very few can tell you what happens next month with confidence," said Andrés Choussy, President, Capital Markets at FIS. "FIS Agentic Insights Suite changes that: it does the work of pulling together credit, treasury and market signals and showing a CFO where the risk sits before it reaches the books. That is how FIS helps clients put their money to work, turning cash from something they report on into something they actively manage."

Andrés Choussy, President, Capital Markets at FIS.

Original Press Release

Finance teams are being asked to protect cash while the information they rely on sits in disconnected systems and most tools report on cash only after it has moved. To address this FIS® (NYSE: FIS), a global leader in financial services technology, today announced the launch of FIS® Agentic Insights Suite, an agentic AI solution for the office of the CFO. FIS Agentic Insights Suite turns fragmented internal and external data into forward-looking intelligence on cash and risk, and its new cash flow forecasting module projects cash across multiple horizons.

The pressure on working capital has intensified. With the cost of capital elevated and finance operations spread across more markets and systems, protecting cash has become a defining challenge for the office of the CFO, and finance teams are increasingly turning to agentic AI to meet it. Interest, though, is outpacing adoption: in Deloitte's Finance Trends 2026 survey, 46% of global finance leaders pointed to working capital optimization as a top area for agentic AI, while just 14% had fully integrated AI agents into their finance function. FIS Agentic Insights Suite is built to close that gap.

"Most finance teams can tell you what happened to their cash last quarter; very few can tell you what happens next month with confidence," said Andrés Choussy, President, Capital Markets at FIS. "FIS Agentic Insights Suite changes that: it does the work of pulling together credit, treasury and market signals and showing a CFO where the risk sits before it reaches the books. That is how FIS helps clients put their money to work, turning cash from something they report on into something they actively manage."

FIS Agentic Insights Suite works as a central hub that gathers data, runs the analysis and recommends action, rather than automating a single task. It cleans and connects data from many systems so finance, credit and treasury teams work from consistent, reliable data instead of reconciling conflicting numbers by hand.

FIS Agentic Insights Suite delivers:

*   **Real-time scoring and recommendations:** The suite provides account scoring on a real-time basis and recommends next steps, so teams can act on the highest-priority risks first.
*   **One trusted view:** The suite brings together internal systems such as ERP, treasury and CRM with external credit bureau and public data, giving teams one reconciled source for scoring and analysis.
*   **Multi-horizon cash flow forecasting:** The new module uses machine learning and forecasting simulations, running many possible outcomes rather than a single estimate, to project cash across configurable horizons and test how delayed inflows or faster outflows would affect liquidity.
*   **Plain-language interface and dashboards:** Users can ask questions in natural language and see results in Power BI dashboards, with standardized reporting across teams.
*   **Fast deployment and easy integration:** The suite starts with a self-serve setup, deploys in weeks rather than months, and connects as the intelligence layer, driving FIS® workflows across our Receivables products (GETPAID™ and BillerIQ™) as well as our Treasury products (Integrity and Quantum).

FIS Agentic Insights Suite is designed to help finance teams identify potential efficiency gains and better manage days sales outstanding (DSO), without necessarily adding headcount. Planned future releases will add payment anomaly detection and dispute forecasting.

**Red-team questions**

1. "Agentic" or rebranded analytics? How much of the Suite autonomously *acts* vs. merely forecasts, scores, and recommends for a human to approve? The marketing word may outrun the capability.
2. Where is a single named launch customer or design partner? A launch with zero references suggests pre-GA / announcement-driven timing.
3. No disclosed forecast-accuracy benchmark. HighRadius cites ~95% on 13-week horizons — why does FIS not quantify its ML forecasting module's accuracy?
4. Is "deploys in weeks, self-serve" credible for enterprises whose pain is precisely fragmented ERP/CRM/treasury data? Data cleaning/connecting is usually the months-long part.
5. Is the real addressable base just FIS's *existing* GETPAID/BillerIQ/Integrity/Quantum installed clients? If so, this is an upsell, not net-new TAM — how much greenfield is there?
6. The Deloitte 46%/14% stat is FIS-cited and self-serving. Does the primary Deloitte Finance Trends 2026 source actually support "working capital optimization = top use case"?
7. Pricing is undisclosed. Is this consumption-based, seat-based, or bundled — and does it meaningfully move FIS revenue, or is it a defensive retention play?
8. Against Oracle/SAP/Workday who own the ERP system of record, can FIS's "intelligence layer" win when the ERP incumbents are shipping their own native finance agents?
9. Data residency / model governance: whose LLMs power the NL interface, where does CFO-office financial data go, and how is it isolated? Unstated.

12. Roadmap risk: payment-anomaly detection and dispute forecasting are "planned." How much of the pitch depends on unshipped features?
13. Single-source, single-channel coverage in the DB (1 mention) — is this a materially adopted launch or a press-release blip amplified by trade media?
14. Fiserv's agentOS and FIS's own prior agentic products (financial crime, agentic commerce) show a pattern of rapid agentic announcements — is FIS shipping product or shipping press releases on a cadence?
15. Does "without necessarily adding headcount" / DSO-reduction framing have any quantified ROI, or is it aspirational?

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** FIS is launching into "agentic AI for the office of the CFO" — autonomous finance / working-capital intelligence (cash forecasting, collections, liquidity, credit-risk scoring). Market sizing for this exact niche is fuzzy and analyst-firm-dependent: the broader enterprise agentic-AI market was ~$2.6bn in 2024 and is put at ~$5.3bn in 2026 → ~$24.5bn by 2030 (CAGR ~46%, per Grand View Research); a narrower "AI-powered finance operations services" read is ~$3.2bn in 2026 (per Fact.MR). Treat all as directional, not a verified TAM for CFO-agentic specifically. "Why now": cost of capital remains elevated, so working-capital/DSO optimization is a board-level priority, and there is a visible adoption gap FIS is explicitly selling into — per Deloitte Finance Trends 2026 (cited in FIS PR), 46% of finance leaders flag working-capital optimization as a top agentic-AI use case while only 14% have fully integrated AI agents. Structure: fragmented — the CFO stack spans ERP (SAP/Oracle), TMS, O2C/AR, close/FP&A and banking rails, with value fragmented across those layers and switching costs high; FIS's angle is to sit as an "intelligence layer" on top of its own installed base (GETPAID, BillerIQ receivables; Integrity, Quantum treasury) rather than rip-and-replace.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs this plays on: DSO reduction, forecast accuracy, collections efficiency / working-capital freed, and (for FIS as a vendor) recurring-revenue attach onto the existing treasury/receivables base. Basis of competition = product depth + distribution into an installed base, not price. Key players: (1) autonomous-finance pure-plays — HighRadius (O2C + treasury, library of 186+ GA agentic agents per its Radiance 2026 update) is the most direct comp; (2) ERP incumbents SAP and Oracle embedding agents into their suites (interoperability/cross-platform reconciliation is their historical weak spot — which FIS's PR targets); (3) TMS/spend-adjacent names (GTreasury — see [[GTreasury acquires Solvexia for reconciliation and reporting]]; Modern Treasury moving up-stack; FloQast/close). FIS positioning: catching-up/fast-follower bundling agentic features onto a large captive treasury+receivables base — a genuine distribution moat (switching costs, embedded workflows) but no evidence of agentic-AI product leadership vs HighRadius. De-PR: this is "announced / self-serve deploy in weeks," not disclosed live-client traction — no named logos, no accuracy or adoption metrics given.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Launch ≠ adoption.** PR carries zero client logos, pricing, or forecast-accuracy metrics; FIS itself cites the 46%-vs-14% gap — the base rate for CFO-agentic going live is low, so execution/time-to-value risk is real (second-order: revenue impact is a 2027+ question, not 2026).
2. **Core-segment overhang.** The product lands inside Capital Markets/treasury just as FIS is reportedly trying to sell parts of that division after soft sales; a divested home could orphan or slow the suite's roadmap (why: strategic attention and R&D may follow the disposal, not the launch).
3. **Disintermediation by the ERP layer.** FIS's own thesis — that ERP vendors are weak at cross-platform reconciliation — cuts both ways: SAP/Oracle embedding native agents could capture the CFO-agent category at the data source, squeezing a bolt-on "intelligence layer" (margin/relevance risk).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is a defensive attach/retention play on FIS's treasury+receivables base more than a new market entry — the falsifiable thesis: if the suite is real traction, FIS should disclose named clients and Capital Markets recurring-revenue upticks within 2–3 quarters; absence of that, plus continued moves to sell the Capital Markets unit, would confirm it is a feature bundle, not a growth lever. Watch trigger: FIS Q4-2026/FY2026 commentary on Capital Markets recurring revenue and any agentic-suite client count.
