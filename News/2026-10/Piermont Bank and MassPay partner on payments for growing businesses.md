Partnership pairs Piermont's embedded banking capabilities with MassPay's global payout expertise to support evolving business payment needs at home and abroad
 NEW YORK , Oct. 22, 2026 /PRNewswire-PRWeb/ --  Piermont Bank , a digital-first commercial bank, today announced a partnership with MassPay , a fintech and global payout orchestration platform, to help growing businesses move money with speed and confidence, both domestically and across borders.
As businesses grow and expand across markets, they face increasingly complex payment demands—from paying suppliers and marketplace sellers to compensating contractors and distributed workforces through a growing range of payment methods. This partnership brings together Piermont's robust embedded banking capabilities, including payment rails and settlement services, with MassPay's payout orchestration platform, which supports payments across more than 180 countries. By combining their respective strengths, the two companies aim to advance payment solutions that keep pace with the evolving needs of growing businesses.
"Successful bank– fintech partnerships start with understanding what each partner does best and how those strengths can come together to solve real business challenges," said Wendy Cai-Lee, Founder and CEO of Piermont Bank. "At Piermont, we believe banks should provide the trusted financial infrastructure that allows fintech innovators to focus on what they do best—building solutions around their customers' needs. Our partnership with MassPay reflects that philosophy, combining the strength of regulated banking with specialized payment technology to support business growth."
MassPay has experienced significant growth in demand for its payout solutions, reporting a 510% year-over-year increase in payout volume in the third quarter of 2026, an indicator of the growing industry need for payout services. As businesses increasingly require flexible payment capabilities across domestic and international markets, the partnership reflects a shared commitment to supporting their evolving financial needs, and by doing so, the parties advance their shared vision to enable SMBs to scale.
"Going cross-border can grow a business significantly, but it often feels like a huge task, and even companies already working internationally end up expanding market by market because of onboarding and compliance hurdles," said Ran Grushkowsky, CEO of MassPay. "Our partnership with Piermont Bank closes that gap, giving growing businesses one path to pay people in 180 countries."
The collaboration underscores Piermont's commitment to advancing bank– fintech partnerships through its embedded banking platform, enabling fintech companies to build and scale specialized financial solutions while leveraging the bank's regulated infrastructure and banking expertise.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-10-22, **Piermont Bank** — a NY-based digital-first commercial bank and one of the US's earliest "embedded banking"/BaaS sponsor banks — announced a partnership with **MassPay**, a global payout-orchestration fintech, to serve growing businesses with domestic and cross-border payments. The deal pairs Piermont's regulated banking infrastructure (payment rails, settlement, sponsorship) with MassPay's payout rails across **180+ countries** (bank transfer, wallets, cards, cash pickup, stablecoins/crypto). MassPay cited **+510% YoY payout-volume growth in Q3 2026**. Source: [prweb press release](https://www.prweb.com/releases/piermont-bank-and-masspay-partner-to-advance-payments-for-growing-businesses-302916899.html), re-run via [Global Fintech Series](https://globalfintechseries.com/banking/piermont-bank-and-masspay-partner-to-advance-payments-for-growing-businesses/).

**[1] Who the players are.** Piermont Bank (founded 2019, CEO Wendy Cai-Lee) brands itself the "first cloud-native commercial bank in the US" and runs an embedded-banking platform for fintechs. MassPay (CEO Ran Grushkowsky) is a payout-orchestration platform with a single API connecting local rails, wallets, cards, cash and stablecoins; through 2026 it has reported a sustained growth run — [Q1 +317% YoY](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/masspay-enters-accelerated-growth-phase-opening-2026-with-3x-more-volume-than-previous-year-302751985.html), [Q2 +408% YoY](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/masspay-extends-record-run-q2-payout-volume-up-408-as-stablecoin-strategy-moves-from-vision-to-production-302820056.html), Q3 +510% YoY — plus stablecoin tie-ups with Circle and Coinbase and [single-FX-hop local-currency payouts](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/masspay-eliminates-the-double-conversion-businesses-can-now-pay-out-to-180-countries-in-local-currency-with-a-single-fx-hop-302895109.html).

**[2] Why it matters.** This is the recurring BaaS pattern — a regulated sponsor bank supplying the banking/compliance layer to a specialist payments fintech, letting SMBs "pay people in 180 countries" through one path without onboarding per-market. It extends Piermont's recent bank-fintech push; it earlier announced a similar [BlxckPay workforce-payments partnership](https://www.piermontbank.com/piermont-bank-and-blxckpay-announce-a-new-model-of-bank-fintech-partnership-connecting-business-banking-and-workforce-payments/). For MassPay, a US sponsor bank adds domestic-rail reach and regulated settlement to complement its cross-border payout orchestration.

**[3] Regulatory backdrop (material caveat).** Piermont is not a clean-slate sponsor bank: in Feb 2024 the FDIC issued it a **consent order (FDIC-23-0038b)** citing "unsafe and unsound" practices and inadequate internal controls / third-party (fintech) risk management, ordering enhanced board oversight and a look-back SAR review ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/piermont-sutton-bank-fdic-consent-orders-aml-bsa-baas-third-party-partners/711815/), [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/fdic-rebukes-sutton-bank-piermont-bank-over-fintech-partners)). Per the [FDIC sponsor-bank register](https://boldrails.com/resources/sponsor-bank-acceptance), that order was still **active as of Aug 2026 (no termination)**. The whole BaaS/sponsor-bank space (OCC/FDIC) remains under heightened third-party-risk scrutiny ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/digital-banking/2024/baas-faces-regulatory-reckoning-occ-and-fdic-actions-underscore-growing-scrutiny/)).

**[4] Related vault notes.** Same sponsor-bank/BaaS-plus-payments-partner template: [[nbkc bank adds Interchecks to its banking-as-a-service suite]], [[Payabli and Huntington Bank partner on digital banking payments]], [[Unit21 partners Helix by Q2 for AI-driven AML and BaaS oversight]] (directly on sponsor-bank oversight), and the global-payouts/embedded-finance theme in [[Forbes releases annual Fintech 50 list]] (Tipalti payouts) and [[Wio Bank partners Pine Labs for merchant acquiring]].

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. **Is this a signed, live integration or just an MOU/press release?** Open — the release is announcement-stage ("aim to advance," "closes that gap"); no launch date, no live customer, no volume committed. Treat as intent, not a shipped product.

2. **Is the source independent?** No — it is a Piermont-issued PRWeb/PRNewswire release (SOURCE: Piermont Bank), echoed verbatim by aggregators (Global Fintech Series, ADVFN). No independent reporting confirms scope.

3. **How material is the +510% YoY payout-volume figure?** Weak as evidence — it is a percentage off an undisclosed base, self-reported by MassPay, with no absolute dollar figure. High-growth % from a small base is common.

4. **Does Piermont's FDIC consent order undercut the story?** Materially, yes. The Feb-2024 order (FDIC-23-0038b) specifically targeted its fintech/third-party risk management and was still active as of Aug 2026 — so Piermont is onboarding a new fintech payments partner while under an open BaaS enforcement action. Not disclosed in the release.

5. **Is MassPay a "fintech partner" that falls under exactly the controls FDIC flagged?** Likely yes — a payout fintech moving money through Piermont's rails is precisely the third-party/BSA-AML exposure the consent order addressed.

6. **Is this exclusive?** No indication. MassPay already runs on multiple rails and has Circle/Coinbase stablecoin tie-ups; Piermont separately partners with BlxckPay. Neither side is single-sourcing.

7. **What does Piermont actually provide — sponsorship, or just settlement rails?** Open. Release says "payment rails and settlement services" + "embedded banking"; unclear whether Piermont sponsors MassPay programs/accounts or just provides domestic clearing.

8. **New domestic capability for MassPay, or marginal?** Plausibly incremental — MassPay's edge is the 180-country payout side; a US sponsor bank mainly strengthens its US on-ramp/settlement, not its core differentiator.

9. **Any numbers on the partnership itself?** None — no revenue, no customer count, no go-live. Entirely qualitative.

10. **Competitive context — is this differentiated?** No. Sponsor-bank + payments-fintech deals are a crowded template (nbkc/Interchecks, Payabli/Huntington, Wio/Pine Labs). Nothing structurally novel here.

11. **Reputational/execution risk for MassPay choosing Piermont?** Real — tying to a sponsor bank under an unterminated FDIC order carries continuity risk if remediation stalls or the bank restricts new programs.

12. **Is "first cloud-native US commercial bank" verifiable?** Marketing claim, unverified; not load-bearing for the story.

13. **Does it change competitive dynamics vs. Column, Lead, Thread, Evolve, etc.?** No evidence — no market-share or displacement claim.

14. **Would this move a reader's priors on either company?** Marginally. Confirms both are active in embedded/cross-border payments; adds no hard metrics or regulatory resolution.

**Importance: 2/5** — A press-release-stage, announcement-only bank–fintech payments partnership with no disclosed terms, metrics, or go-live, following a well-worn BaaS template. The one genuinely notable wrinkle (and a negative one) is that Piermont is still under an active, unterminated Feb-2024 FDIC consent order over exactly this kind of fintech-partner risk — context the release omits. Watch-item, not a mover.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Two overlapping sectors meet here: US Banking-as-a-Service / sponsor-bank infrastructure and cross-border B2B payouts. Cross-border payments is a large, growing pool — the broad market is sized at ~$201.6bn–$237.3bn for 2026 with a ~7.4% CAGR to ~$365bn by 2032 (per multiple research firms, via researchandmarkets / fortunebusinessinsights, as of 2026; figures diverge by methodology so treat as order-of-magnitude, not a precise TAM). The B2B segment alone is cited at ~$117.6bn for 2026 (Straits/Fortune, 2026). Structure: the payouts layer is fragmented (Payoneer, Tipalti, Nium, Thunes, dLocal, Rapyd, Wise Platform, Stripe/PayPal payouts all compete), while the US sponsor-bank layer is narrow and capital/charter-gated — only ~15 small banks run material BaaS programs (per sponsor-bank registers, 2026). Why now: post-Synapse (2024) collapse, US regulators (FDIC/OCC) sharply tightened BaaS oversight in 2022–2025, forcing a "fewer, better-governed partners" posture — sponsor banks now compete on compliance credibility, not just rails access. Piermont itself exemplifies both the demand (MassPay payout volume +510% YoY in Q3 2026, per note/PR) and the regulatory squeeze (see risk flags).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: payout volume / TPV, corridor coverage (countries, currencies, methods), take rate, and — on the bank side — program-level compliance/oversight capability. Players & basis of competition: on payouts MassPay (180 countries) competes on corridor breadth, embedded compliance/KYC and no-lock-in pricing against Tipalti (200+ countries, 120+ currencies, from ~$249/mo + fees), Nium (190+ countries, added USDC stablecoin payouts 2026-08-27), Payoneer (190+ countries), and Thunes (140+ countries, 720+ members, $150M Series D). On the bank side Piermont competes with other sponsor banks (nbkc, Sutton, Lead, Column) on embedded rails + ability to pass regulatory muster. Recent moves: Piermont has layered multiple BaaS middleware/partner deals — Treasury Prime (30+ fintechs signed), Unit (which it later moved to drop), and now MassPay (2026-10-22). Protagonist's position: MassPay is a niche-but-fast-scaling payout challenger (hyper-growth off a small base); Piermont is a niche cloud-native sponsor bank differentiating on being "disciplined" with fintech clients. Moat `(analysis)`: MassPay's is corridor coverage + embedded compliance (switching costs once integrated); Piermont's is its charter + remediated compliance posture — a regulatory moat only as long as the consent order doesn't constrain it.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round disclosed for either MassPay (private) or Piermont (private bank) → own multiples = no data. Public cross-border peers for scale reference:
- Payoneer (PAYO): market cap ~$2.42bn, TTM revenue ~$1.08bn (Q2 2026, +6.3% YoY) → P/S ≈ $2.42bn / $1.08bn = **~2.2x**; P/E (TTM) ~51x on thin EPS $0.14 (stockanalysis/companiesmarketcap, 2026).
- dLocal (DLO): market cap ~$3.5bn, EV ~$2.7bn, EV/EBITDA ~6.9x; Q2 2026 TPV $17.7bn (+92% YoY) (stockanalysis, 2026).
Read: public payout/cross-border names trade at modest revenue multiples (~2x P/S for Payoneer) despite high volume growth — the market prices these as thin-margin flow businesses, not SaaS. That is a useful anchor: MassPay's +510% YoY volume is impressive but, on this comp set, volume growth alone does not command a rich revenue multiple without margin. Internal comps (base): [[nbkc bank adds Interchecks to its banking-as-a-service suite]] (2025-10, near-identical sponsor-bank + payments-partner structure), [[This Week in Fintech BaaS growth amid shifting regulatory scrutiny]] (2025-12, enforcement-action context), [[Unit21 partners Helix by Q2 for AI-driven AML and BaaS oversight]] (2026-03, sponsor-bank oversight tooling). Distribution not computed (only 2 public comparables, different models) — qualitative comparison.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory overhang on the sponsor bank.** Piermont has been under an FDIC consent order (FDIC-23-0038b, dated 2024-02-26) for unsafe/unsound practices tied to BSA/AML and third-party relationships, and that order was still active with no termination date as of 2026-08 (bankingdive/americanbanker, 2026). Signing new fintech/payout partners while remediation is ongoing is the exact activity regulators scrutinized — second-order effect: growth could be capped or reversed if examiners view it as expanding risk before controls are proven.
2. **Protagonist-at-the-wrong-layer / disintermediation.** The economics live in the payout orchestration (MassPay) and the rails, not the deposit-holding bank; Piermont risks being a commoditized, replaceable compliance/settlement wrapper — larger or cleaner sponsor banks (or MassPay itself obtaining more licensing) could disintermediate it.
3. **Metric asymmetry / what's unsaid.** The only hard number disclosed is MassPay's +510% YoY payout volume — a percentage off an undisclosed (likely small) base, with no revenue, take rate, margin, or ticket size given. The PR is silent on the partnership's economics and on fraud/compliance liability allocation between bank and fintech — the precise points that sank prior BaaS arrangements.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is not a re-rating event; it is one incremental partner-signing in the post-Synapse BaaS consolidation, where banks rebuild program pipelines under stricter oversight. Falsifiable thesis: if Piermont can keep adding payout/fintech partners AND get its consent order terminated, it validates the "disciplined sponsor bank" model as a durable niche. Trigger/catalyst to watch: FDIC consent-order termination (bullish) vs. any new/expanded enforcement action or a partner offboarding (bearish, would confirm the overhang is binding). What would make the thesis wrong: the order persisting or tightening while partner count grows — a sign Piermont is again outrunning its controls.
