Plum has secured Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) authorisation to offer Targeted Support, a regulatory milestone allowing the fintech to provide automated, AI-driven investment suggestions to specific customer groups. This move addresses the UK’s "advice gap" by offering low-cost, proactive financial guidance to millions of savers currently holding excess cash in low-yield accounts.

The authorisation, granted through Plum’s investment entity Saveable Limited, permits the firm to deliver Targeted Support—a regulatory category designed by the FCA to sit between general guidance and full-scale personalised advice. Plum is among the first fintechs to receive this permission, which it intends to deploy via its existing AI assistant. The service will provide ready-made savings and investment suggestions to groups of consumers who share similar financial characteristics, helping them transition from cash savings to long-term wealth building.

The initial rollout, expected in the coming weeks, will focus on customers holding significant cash balances. According to the firm, there is approximately £600 billion in excess cash sitting in low-yield accounts across the UK that could potentially be invested. By using AI technology to facilitate these conversations, the company aims to reach a portion of the estimated 23 million consumers currently underserved by the traditional advice market. The AI assistant, which has already been utilised by more than 270,000 customers for goal-setting and financial planning, will now be able to offer more proactive, specific suggestions. This development follows the firm's stated intention to eventually secure a full advisory licence to provide personalised investment advice. The firm notes that currently, 91% of people in the UK do not access personalised financial advice.

This is a testament to our regulatory credibility and the strength of our governance."

Victor Trokoudes, CEO and founder of Plum

Plum, also known as Plum Fintech, is a "smart money" app designed to automate savings and investments for retail users. Founded by Victor Trokoudes, the company has positioned itself as a central hub for personal finance management, leveraging automation to help users set aside money and invest in diversified portfolios. The firm operates in a competitive UK fintech landscape, where it vies for market share against both traditional wealth managers and digital-first investment platforms.

The company’s regulatory framework includes its investment entity, Saveable Limited, which holds the necessary permissions to facilitate these new services. Plum has historically focused on democratising access to financial tools that were previously reserved for high-net-worth individuals, such as automated round-ups and AI-driven insights into spending habits. By integrating Targeted Support, the firm is moving deeper into the wealth management space, attempting to convert its large user base of savers into active investors. This strategy relies heavily on its proprietary AI technology to maintain a low-cost model while meeting the FCA’s requirements for consumer protection and governance.

FF News has closely followed Plum’s expansion into the UK wealth and savings market. In August 2026, the firm made a significant move to bolster its backend capabilities when Plum Selects Upvest to Power UK Investment Infrastructure in Major Expansion, a partnership aimed at scaling its investment offerings. The company has also been active in highlighting issues within the UK’s savings landscape; a recent Freedom of Information request by the firm revealed that 45,000 Lifetime ISA Savers Hit With Multiple Penalties in Single Year. Additionally, Plum’s data has provided insights into the broader investment climate, noting that ISA Millionaire Numbers Surge 52% to Record High as Top Pots Hit £12m, reflecting the growing concentration of wealth in tax-efficient wrappers.

This announcement signals a shift in how the FCA views the role of AI in closing the advice gap. By allowing a fintech to offer Targeted Support, the regulator is effectively greenlighting a middle-ground model that could put traditional wealth managers under pressure. If Plum can successfully convert cash-heavy users into investors without the overhead of human advisers, it proves the viability of the FCA’s new regulatory tier. However, it also raises questions about the boundary between "targeted suggestions" and "regulated advice." The industry will be watching to see how the FCA monitors AI-driven suggestions to ensure they do not inadvertently cross into personalised recommendations without the requisite advisory permissions.

Original Press Release

Smart money app Plum has been authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) to offer Targeted Support, through its investment entity Saveable Limited.

This approval means Plum will now be able to provide ready-made savings and investment suggestions to appropriate groups of consumers with shared characteristics, helping them consider whether investing could be right for them and take steps towards building wealth over the long term.

Plum is one of the earliest fintechs to secure this permission from the FCA, joining some of the UK’s most prominent financial institutions. The firm will also be among the first to deliver Targeted Support through AI technology, democratising access to financial knowledge and facilitating conversations with customers.

Targeted Support was introduced by the Financial Conduct Authority with the aim to bridge the gap between general guidance and personalised financial advice, helping people get the right level of support, at an affordable price when they need it most. It is estimated there is currently over £600 billion in excess cash sitting in low-yield accounts that could be invested.

The first live use cases of Plum’s Targeted Support service will be focused on helping customers who hold a relatively large amount of cash to get started with their investing journey so they can build their long-term wealth. It will be available in the coming weeks through a new iteration of Plum’s popular AI assistant, which has already been used by more than 270,000 customers to help with their finances. The product already enables users to set financial goals and receive plans to help achieve those goals.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On Friday 23 October 2026, UK "smart money" app Plum announced it had received Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) authorisation to offer **Targeted Support**, via its investment entity **Saveable Limited**. The permission lets Plum deliver "ready-made" savings and investment suggestions to groups of customers sharing similar characteristics (e.g. those holding large cash balances), delivered through a new iteration of its AI assistant. Plum positions itself as one of the earliest fintechs — and among the first to deliver Targeted Support via AI — with rollout "in coming weeks." ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/111428/plum-secures-fca-approval-to-deliver-targeted-support), [Professional Adviser](https://www.professionaladviser.com/news/4537830/plum-approved-targeted-support-eyes-advice-market), [Financial Planning Today](https://www.financialplanningtoday.co.uk/news/plum-to-offer-ai-based-targeted-support-via-app))

**[1] What Targeted Support is (the regulatory backdrop).** Targeted Support is a new regulatory category created under the FCA's **Advice Guidance Boundary Review** (AGBR) to sit between generic guidance and full personalised advice: firms may make recommendations designed for *groups* of consumers with similar circumstances, not individuals. The FCA published its **policy statement with near-final rules on 11 December 2025**, confirmed them final on **26 February 2026**, and the rules **came into effect 6 April 2026**. It applies to investments and pensions only; a firm may not provide it unless it has reasonable grounds to believe doing so leaves the client better off. Further FCA consultation on simplified advice / the advice-guidance boundary is planned through 2026. ([FCA AGBR page](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/advice-guidance-boundary-review), [Farrer & Co](https://www.farrer.co.uk/news-and-insights/the-advice-guidance-boundary-review-the-new-targeted-support-regime/), [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/risk-and-compliance/fca-unveils-near-final-rules-for-targeted-support-in-pensions-and-retail-investme-102lzft))

**[2] Plum's AI-assistant lineage — this is a regulatory unlock, not a new product.** Plum has been building toward this for over a year. In Sept 2025 it launched a [[Plum launches Gemini-powered AI money management tool|Gemini-powered AI money tool]], where CEO Victor Trokoudes explicitly flagged that the FCA's "targeted support" announcement had "massive potential." In June 2026 it shipped [[Plum launches AI-powered money guidance for UK consumers|AI-powered money guidance]] delivering tailored plans in minutes, stressing expert-monitored guidance vs "untested generic providers." The Oct 2026 approval converts that assistant (already used by 270,000+ customers for goal-setting) from general guidance into regulated group-level investment suggestions. Plum has also said it ultimately wants a full advisory licence for personalised advice.

**[3] The "advice gap" thesis and Plum's own positioning.** Plum's claimed prize: ~£600bn of excess cash in low-yield UK accounts and ~23m consumers underserved by the advice market (both FCA-cited figures), with 91% of UK adults not accessing personalised advice. Plum frames TS as a low-cost, AI-driven bridge to convert cash-heavy savers into investors. This dovetails with its wider UK investing push: in Aug 2026 it tapped Upvest to power its UK investment infrastructure, and it reached [[Smart money app Plum reaches profitability|positive EBITDA / profitability]] in Apr 2026 — a firm now scaling product-led rather than growth-at-any-cost. Other Plum vault context: [[Plum secures EMI license]], [[Plum's Cash ISA becomes flexible]], [[Plum launches new easy access savings account]].

**[4] Competitive landscape — Plum is NOT a true first-mover; it's the first fintech/AI angle.** The regime's early adopters are overwhelmingly large incumbents: **Aviva** got FCA TS approval in **June 2026** (phased rollout from summer, pensions-first), following **Quilter, Royal London and Vanguard**; **Legal & General** is also approved (first case: DC workplace pension members in cash); **Hargreaves Lansdown** is preparing TS from spring. Plum's differentiation is being an app-first fintech delivering TS conversationally through AI rather than through adviser/insurer channels — adjacent to the FCA's broader AI push ([[FCA begins live testing of AI applications in banking]], [[NatWest, Monzo, Santander among first in FCA AI live testing scheme]]). The open regulatory question remains policing the line between "group suggestions" and personalised recommendations that would require a full advisory permission. ([Aviva](https://www.aviva.com/newsroom/news-and-research-overview/news-releases/2026/06/aviva-receives-fca-approval-to-deliver-targeted-support/), [Money Marketing](https://www.moneymarketing.co.uk/news/aviva-secures-fca-approval-to-offer-targeted-support/))

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Q1. Is this actually a first-mover event?** Partly. Plum is an *early* fintech adopter and plausibly among the first to deliver TS *via AI*, but the broader "first wave" is dominated by incumbents — Aviva (Jun 2026), Quilter, Royal London, Vanguard, L&G, and HL (spring). "First fintech" is the defensible claim; "first" unqualified is not. (Open on exact ordering vs other fintechs.)

**Q2. Is the approval live revenue or just permission?** Permission only. Rollout is "in coming weeks" and restricted to initial use cases (large-cash-balance customers). No live product, uptake, conversion or revenue data exists yet. Monetisation path is unstated.

**Q3. How big is the "23m underserved / £600bn cash" prize to *Plum*?** These are FCA market-wide figures, not Plum's addressable base. Plum's relevant base is its ~270k AI-assistant users (subset of total customers). The headline TAM overstates near-term opportunity. Answered/skeptical.

**Q4. Does TS actually let Plum give investment "advice"?** No. TS is explicitly group-level, not personalised. Plum itself says it still needs a *separate full advisory licence* for personalised advice — a future, unsecured step. The note's framing should not conflate the two.

**Q5. Regulatory boundary risk?** Material and acknowledged even in the source: the line between AI-generated "group suggestions" and de-facto personalised recommendations is thin. An AI assistant tailoring suggestions per conversation could drift into regulated advice. FCA monitoring intensity is untested for AI-delivered TS.

**Q6. Suitability / consumer-duty exposure.** TS rules bar provision unless the firm has reasonable grounds it leaves the client better off. For AI-driven suggestions nudging cash-savers into investments, there is downside/market-risk and Consumer Duty liability if suggestions underperform or mis-segment. Open risk.

**Q7. Is the AI genuinely differentiated or marketing?** Open. Plum's prior assistant (Gemini-powered, Sept 2025; guidance tool, Jun 2026) is real, but "proprietary AI" delivering TS is as yet unproven at scale; competitors could add AI delivery quickly.

**Q8. Entity/permission detail — who actually holds the permission?** Saveable Limited (Plum's investment entity), not "Plum" the brand. Correctly captured; worth keeping precise for any regulatory claims.

**Q9. Dating integrity.** Source/press release dated Friday 23 Oct 2026; note dated 2026-10-26 (retrieval). Announcement predates note by 3 days — fine, flagged.

**Q10. Single-source risk.** Note body cites only tradetecheye.com (an FF News syndication of Plum's press release). Corroborated externally by Finextra, Professional Adviser, Financial Planning Today, financialit.net — but all trace to the same Plum PR. Treat quantitative claims as company-stated.

**Q11. Does TS cover savings or only investments/pensions?** FCA TS applies to investments and pensions only. Plum markets "savings and investment suggestions" — the "savings-suggestion" framing may exceed the strict regulatory scope of TS; worth scrutiny. Open.

**Q12. Competitive moat vs incumbents.** Incumbents (Aviva/L&G/HL) have far larger pension/customer bases to apply TS to. Plum's edge is UX/app engagement, not distribution scale. Durable advantage unproven.

**Q13. Dependency on Upvest.** Plum's investment execution leans on its Aug 2026 Upvest infrastructure deal; TS suggestions ultimately route into that stack — a third-party dependency for the end-to-end journey.

**Q14. Why does this matter beyond Plum?** It's an early test of whether the FCA's new TS tier works through a fintech/AI channel rather than incumbents — a signal for the AGBR's real-world viability and for how aggressively the FCA will supervise AI-delivered guidance.

**Q15. Any stale/duplicate concern?** No prior vault note covers *this* FCA TS approval. Closest priors (Gemini tool Sept 2025, AI guidance Jun 2026) are predecessor products, not the approval. Fresh.

**Importance: 3/5 — rationale.** A genuine regulatory milestone and a notable "fintech + AI" first within the FCA's new Targeted Support regime, strategically coherent with Plum's post-profitability wealth push. But it is permission-not-product (no live uptake/revenue), the TAM figures are market-wide not Plum-specific, incumbents already hold the regime's early-mover headlines, and full personalised-advice licensing remains a future unsecured step. Meaningful signal for the UK advice-gap/AGBR theme; not yet a market-moving outcome.
