### Visa Receives Approval to Begin Preparations for a Domestic Business in China

October 13, 2026

SAN FRANCISCO--(BUSINESS WIRE)--  Visa (NYSE:V), a global leader in digital payments, today announced that it has received approval of its Application to Prepare from the People’s Bank of China (PBOC), which allows the company to begin formal preparations to establish a domestic bankcard clearing institution in China. This marks an important milestone toward the company serving China’s domestic payments market.

“The approval of our Application to Prepare marks a significant step forward in Visa’s long-standing commitment to China. We greatly appreciate the important role of the leaders of the United States and China in pursuing a constructive and strategically stable relationship between the world’s largest economies,” said Ryan McInerney, Chief Executive Officer of Visa. “For more than 40 years, Visa has been an active contributor to China’s dynamic payments landscape. We look forward to taking the necessary steps in the process, pursuant to the PBOC’s regulations, to begin operating domestically in China.”

China is one of the world’s most advanced and dynamic digital payments markets, supported by a highly innovative ecosystem and continued commitment to further financial sector liberalization. Visa looks forward to bringing its world-class innovation, security and reliability to Chinese consumers and businesses, contributing to the continued development of China’s domestic payments landscape.

Visa will continue its efforts to complete the process and begin domestic bankcard clearing operations in China.

Our mission is to connect the world through the most innovative, convenient, reliable and secure payments network, enabling individuals, businesses and economies to thrive. We believe that economies that include everyone everywhere, uplift everyone everywhere and see access as foundational to the future of money movement. Learn more at Visa.com.

# Context-enrichment: Visa approved to prepare domestic business in China
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 4/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 13 Oct 2026 Visa (NYSE:V) said the People's Bank of China (PBOC) approved its **"Application to Prepare"** — permission to *begin formal preparations* to set up a domestic bankcard clearing institution in China. This is NOT a clearing license and NOT permission to process domestic transactions. Visa must still (a) complete preparatory work (typically within one year under PBOC rules), then (b) file a separate "Application to Open for Business," win that, and only then launch domestic RMB bankcard clearing.

**Why framed this way / what it reveals.** The press release leads with CEO Ryan McInerney thanking "the leaders of the United States and China … pursuing a constructive and strategically stable relationship" — an unusually explicit nod that the approval is geopolitical, timed around a US-China détente/leaders' meeting, not a routine regulatory tick. China's market-access process has two gates ("prepare" → "open for business"); Visa is only through gate one. The "40 years in China" line papers over the fact that Visa's *domestic* ambition was effectively frozen for ~6 years: it filed to run a clearing institution in May 2020 and got no decision until now (analysis: application dates per press/Reuters). So the real news is "the logjam broke," not "Visa is live." China's domestic card-clearing market is dominated by state-backed UnionPay (see [[UnionPay drives QR interoperability via China's CPG gateway]]).

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **American Express (via Express/LianTong JV with LianLian DigiTech):** first foreign network through the gate — got PBOC *preparation* approval in 2018, a full **bankcard clearing license in June 2020** (first Sino-foreign JV clearing institution), began processing later in 2020. Years ahead.
- **Mastercard (NUCC JV with NetsUnion):** *preparation* approval **Feb 2020**, formal **clearing license Nov 2023**, **operations began May 2024**. Its "prepare → license" leg alone took ~3.75 years.
- **Visa:** only now (Oct 2026) at the *preparation* stage Mastercard cleared in Feb 2020 and Amex in 2018 → Visa is roughly **6 years behind Amex and ~4+ years behind Mastercard** on this specific track.
- **UnionPay:** the incumbent domestic monopoly clearer; any foreign entrant competes against a state network with near-universal issuance/acceptance.

**Why the lay of the land is this way / second order.** The gap is policy-driven, not product-driven: China reopened the clearing market after a 2017 WTO ruling against it, but let applications sit. Amex/Mastercard got in during the 2018–2020 window; Visa's application stalled through the trade-war years. So the determinant of "who's ahead" is the timing of political approval, not technology. Second-order: being last means Visa enters a domestic market where UnionPay is entrenched AND two Western rivals are already building issuer relationships — latecomer economics.

## [2] Company history / fit
Visa has operated in China for 40+ years (cross-border acceptance, inbound/outbound travel rails) but was blocked from *domestic* RMB clearing. Recent China moves have been cross-border, not domestic: enabling China-issued Visa cards on Apple Pay for overseas use (Jan 2026, [[Visa enables Apple Pay for China-issued cards overseas]]), paralleled by Mastercard ([[Mastercard and Apple Pay add cross-border support for China cards]]). Domestic clearing is the one large pool Visa has never tapped.

**Why Visa pushes here.** China is one of the world's largest card/payments markets; Visa's global growth is maturing, so an entirely new domestic clearing pool (even a small slice of a trillion-RMB flow) is a rare TAM expansion. Structural pressure: card-network take-rates are a commodity under pressure from account-to-account and wallet rails (Alipay/WeChat Pay dominate Chinese retail payments), so Visa wants optionality and presence even if near-term domestic volume is modest.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty:** genuinely new for Visa — first-ever path to domestic RMB clearing. But in the broader market it is *catch-up*: Amex has cleared domestically since ~2020, Mastercard since 2024. **Traction: zero.** No clients, no RMB cards issued, no volume, no launch date disclosed. This is a regulatory milestone (gate one of two), not adoption.

**Why the value-add may be thinner than it looks (deeper).** Even once live, a foreign clearer faces: (1) UnionPay's entrenched domestic rails and state backing; (2) the reality that most Chinese consumer payment is QR/wallet (Alipay, WeChat Pay), where card clearing is not the value layer — so the clearing license captures a shrinking share of where consumer-payment margin actually sits; (3) Mastercard's own post-2024 domestic volumes are not large, a read-through for Visa's realistic ramp (analysis). The margin in Chinese payments is largely captured upstream by wallets, not by card clearers → the central question shifts from "did Visa get in" to "is domestic card clearing even where the value is in China."

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Next steps: finish preparation (≈1-year window), apply to open for business, win that, build issuer/acquirer relationships, then launch — on Mastercard's precedent this leg can take years. No timeline given. Sentiment: treated as a symbolic win for financial-sector opening and as a US-China goodwill signal; incrementally positive for Visa but not materially moving near-term revenue (analysis).

**Why the market reads it this way / counterintuitive second order.** The approval's value is as much diplomatic as commercial — a marker that US-China financial decoupling is pausing. Counterintuitively, that makes the asset *fragile*: an approval granted as a geopolitical gesture can be slow-walked, conditioned, or stalled at gate two if relations sour, exactly as Visa's 2020 application was. Regulatory risk here is bidirectional and political, not just procedural.

## Red-team / challenge questions (second-order)

1. **Is this a license or permission to prepare?** Permission to *prepare* only — gate one of two. Not a clearing license, no authority to process domestic transactions.

2. **Is it really new, or a re-report of an older event?** New: Visa's first-ever PBOC approval on the domestic-clearing track (13 Oct 2026). No prior corpus note covers it. The earlier Visa-China notes are cross-border Apple Pay, a different capability. → fresh.

3. **How far behind rivals is Visa?** ~6 years behind Amex (clearing license June 2020) and ~4+ behind Mastercard (prepare Feb 2020, license Nov 2023, live May 2024). Visa is only now at the stage rivals cleared years ago.

4. **Why did it take ~6 years from Visa's May 2020 application?** Political: applications were frozen through the US-China trade-war years; this approval is explicitly tied (per the CEO quote) to a US-China détente. → the timing is diplomatic, not procedural. (analysis)

5. **When can Visa actually process a domestic transaction?** Unknown — no timeline disclosed. On Mastercard's precedent, prepare→license→operations ran ~4+ years. Realistically years away. Open.

6. **How big is the realistic domestic opportunity vs UnionPay?** UnionPay is the entrenched state clearer; Alipay/WeChat Pay own consumer wallet volume. Card clearing is a shrinking slice of Chinese consumer-payment value. Likely modest near-term volume. (analysis)

7. **What is Mastercard NUCC's actual domestic volume post-2024?** Not disclosed in sources; believed small — a cautionary read-through for Visa's ramp. Open.

8. **Who captures the margin in Chinese payments?** Wallets (Alipay/WeChat Pay) upstream, not card clearers. A clearing license may capture little of where margin actually sits. (analysis)

9. **Could the approval be stalled or reversed at gate two?** Yes — if US-China relations deteriorate, gate-two ("open for business") approval can be slow-walked, exactly as the 2020 application was. Political risk is bidirectional. (analysis)

10. **Does Visa need a Chinese JV partner (as Amex/Mastercard did)?** Amex (LianLian) and Mastercard (NetsUnion) used local JVs. Visa's release doesn't name a partner/structure. Open — a key unknown for execution.

11. **What's silent in the PR?** No JV partner, no capital commitment, no timeline, no target issuers, no volume/market-share goal. Heavy on diplomacy, light on economics.

12. **Is "40 years in China" relevant to domestic clearing?** No — that's cross-border acceptance/travel rails. Domestic RMB clearing is a market Visa has never operated in. Framing device.

13. **Does this change Visa's revenue outlook near-term?** No material near-term impact; symbolic/optional value. Upside is multi-year and uncertain. (analysis)

14. **Why does Visa want this if volume is modest?** TAM optionality as global card growth matures and take-rates face A2A/wallet pressure; presence hedges against being locked out of a huge market. (analysis)

15. **Is the "financial opening" narrative durable?** Partly — China did reopen clearing (post-2017 WTO ruling) but gated access politically. Durability depends on US-China relations, not just China's liberalization rhetoric. Open.

Importance: 4/5 — Strategically significant (first-ever path for Visa into a massive previously-closed domestic market, and a notable US-China financial-opening signal), but it is a preparation approval not a live business: zero traction, no timeline, Visa is years behind Amex/Mastercard, and card clearing may capture only a thin slice of China's wallet-dominated payment value. Weight comes from strategic/geopolitical meaning, not near-term economics.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** China is the world's largest card market — c.10bn+ bankcards issued, and UnionPay alone processed RMB 66.04tn across 89.3bn transactions in Q4 2024 on its interbank clearing hub (per UnionPay/Baidu-cited stats, via coinlaw.io, 2026). Domestic bankcard clearing was a closed, state-adjacent monopoly until WTO/PBOC liberalisation forced access: the barrier here is regulatory and licensing, not capital — PBOC runs a two-stage gate ("Application to Prepare" → separate approval to actually clear). Foreign entry has been glacial: American Express (via GNEE/Lianlian JV) was first approved in 2020; Mastercard's NetsUnion JV got the prepare-approval in Feb 2020, formal clearing approval Nov 2023, and only switched its first live domestic transaction in **May 2024** (per Mastercard/scmp.com). "Why now": Visa's Oct 13 2026 approval is only the *prepare* stage — the same door Mastercard walked through four years before going live — framed by both sides as a US-China diplomatic goodwill gesture (Visa PR explicitly thanks "the leaders of the United States and China"). The real headwind isn't UnionPay cards but QR mobile wallets (Alipay, WeChat Pay) that already disintermediate the card rail at the point of sale.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: payments/clearing volume, processed transaction count, cross-border volume, take rate per transaction. Players by basis of competition: UnionPay — incumbent monopoly on network/regulatory scale (domestic value proxy ~42% of China card volume per Statista-cited data, though its global muscle is mostly its home turf); Alipay/WeChat Pay — the true POS disruptors via QR; foreign card networks (Amex live 2020, Mastercard NetsUnion live 2024 with 50+ card programs across ~20 Chinese banks, Visa now entering). Recent move: Mastercard's path is the direct template — prepare → formal approval → live, a ~4-year arc. Visa's position: **last of the three US networks to enter, at the earliest (prepare) stage** — strategically catching up, not leading. Moat is global cross-border acceptance and brand (intangibles + network effects on outbound/inbound Chinese travel/e-commerce), NOT domestic clearing where it has zero installed base `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Visa IS the protagonist; folding in its own filed results as PRIMARY evidence. Visa fiscal Q2 2026 (qtr ended 2026-03-31): net revenue **$11.2bn, +17%** y/y (+16% cc); payments volume +11% cc; total cross-border volume +12% cc; processed transactions **66.1bn, +9%** (Visa Q2-2026 Earnings Release, 2026-04-28). This is the scale a (still-notional) China domestic book would bolt onto — material absolute $ but years from contributing.
Trading multiples (co-equal public peers, same model/scale/geo):
- Visa (V): EV ≈ $738bn; EV/EBITDA (fwd) ≈ **22.5x**, P/E (TTM) ≈ 31.8x, fwd P/E ≈ 25.0x (gurufocus/marketscreener, as of ~2026-10-10).
- Mastercard (MA): EV/EBITDA ≈ **23.3–23.9x**, fwd P/E ≈ 24.9x, TTM P/E ≈ 31.6x (gurufocus, Sep–Oct 2026).
Flag: **in-line** — V and MA trade as a tight duopoly pair (EV/EBITDA within ~1x, fwd P/E within ~0.1x); both sit at the rich end of the EV/EBITDA sanity range (~8–25x), justified by ~mid-teens revenue growth and 60%+ margins. No re-rating implied by this single prepare-stage headline. UnionPay/Alipay/WeChat are private/state — no comparable public multiple, "no data".
Internal comps: [[UnionPay drives QR interoperability via China's CPG gateway]] · [[Geidea and UnionPay roll out dual-card routing in MEA]] · [[Barclays and Visa extend partnership]].

**Risk flags.**
1. **Timeline / "announced ≠ live" gap** — this is only the PBOC *prepare* approval, not a clearing licence. Mastercard's own precedent (prepare 2020 → live 2024) implies Visa is plausibly years from processing a single domestic transaction; near-term revenue impact is ~zero. Second-order: any stock move on this headline is sentiment, not cash flow.
2. **Rail disintermediation** — the Chinese POS is dominated by Alipay/WeChat QR wallets; even a fully-licensed Visa clears into a market where the card itself is secondary. The addressable domestic pie is smaller than the "10bn cards" headline suggests. Second-order: the economic prize may be outbound/co-brand cross-border, not domestic switching.
3. **Geopolitical dependency** — approval is explicitly tied to a "constructive" US-China relationship (Visa CEO quote). The licence is a diplomatic variable; a reversal in relations could stall the remaining stages. Second-order: Visa's China entry is hostage to tariff/tech-war cycles, not just execution.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector this is a *new-entry* story, not a re-rating: it widens the foreign-network field in China from two (Amex, Mastercard) to a prospective three, incrementally pressuring UnionPay's domestic monopoly at the margin `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: Visa will NOT clear a live domestic Chinese transaction before 2028 — watch for the second PBOC approval (formal clearing licence) and a named bank-partner/JV structure as the real trigger; absence of those by end-2027 confirms this is a multi-year option, not a 2026 catalyst `(hypothesis)`.

Management tone: confident on "resilient" consumer and business spending; de-PR flag — GAAP net income grew only +7% vs non-GAAP +8% and non-GAAP EPS +11%, the wedge driven by $563mn severance + $237mn interchange-MDL litigation provision that the headline non-GAAP framing strips out.

**Thesis-flags (relevant to the China domestic opportunity).**
1. *China is additive, not yet modeled.* The Q3 release contains no China revenue line and no mention of China — consistent with the news: PBOC approval is only to *begin preparing* a domestic clearing institution (one year to complete prep, then apply to operate). Fact → why → why it matters: today's $11.6bn/qtr run-rate includes ~zero mainland-domestic clearing revenue, so any China ramp is pure optionality on top of the current thesis, not priced into the print. Second-order: even a modest domestic share would diversify Visa away from the FX-sensitive international-transaction line (the +6% laggard this quarter).
2. *Scale of the prize vs time-to-revenue.* China bank cards processed ~963.6tn yuan in 2025 (press); but analysts (Daishin) flag revenue impact is "likely to take considerable time" and must clear further regulatory steps + UnionPay's entrenched domestic monopoly. Why it matters: the milestone de-risks the *path* but not the *timing* — no contribution to FY26/FY27 estimates; this is a multi-year call option.
3. *Core engine is intact while the option matures.* Payments volume accelerating to +10% cc and VAS +34% show the base business funding the China build-out from strength, not weakness. Second-order: Visa can absorb preparation/CAPEX for China (and the severance/litigation drag) without denting its double-digit EPS algorithm.
4. *Margin/one-off watch.* $563mn severance + $237mn litigation provision compressed GAAP vs non-GAAP this quarter; recurring litigation provisions (US interchange MDL) remain an open-ended liability that no China upside offsets. Watch whether severance signals a cost-reset or a one-off.
