# Context-enrichment: Sends and UnionPay International partner on card issuing and acquiring
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 2026-10-06 Sends (UK FCA-authorised EMI, CEO Alona Shevtsova) and UnionPay International (UPI) announced a "global cooperation" to work together on BOTH card **issuing** and **acquiring**. De-PR'd, the substance is thin and entirely forward-looking:
- Issuing: Sends **"plans to develop"** UPI card issuing so its customers can hold UPI-branded products usable on UPI's acceptance network.
- Acquiring: Sends is **"working to develop"** in-house UPI acquiring so merchants in its ecosystem can accept UPI cards, pitched at businesses serving Asian/Chinese travellers.
- No live date, no card volumes, no merchant count, no BIN, no geographic scope, no fee/economics. Pure "aims to / will develop" framing — a signed MoU-level cooperation, not a launched product.

**Why framed this way / what it reveals:** The PR sells "both sides of a transaction" (two-sided proposition), but critically this is NOT Sends' first UPI tie — it is an *escalation* of an existing relationship:
- 2025-10 (~27 Oct): "Alona Shevtsova Expands Sends' Global Reach with UnionPay International Integration" — Sends first connected to UPI.
- 2025-11-26: PAIO–Sends integration gave Sends UPI *acquiring* indirectly, where **PAIO** (with Decta) was the actual acquirer and Sends merely resold acceptance to its clients.
So the real 2026-10 delta is: (a) Sends moving from *reselling PAIO's acquiring* toward building its **own** direct UPI acquiring, and (b) adding **issuing** (brand-new capability for Sends). That is a genuine new scope, hence fresh — but the note's generic "two companies will work together" line hides that this is iteration #3 on the same UPI relationship, not a standing start.

## [1] Competitors / peers
UPI's partner-enablement playbook already has more advanced names:
- **Unlimit** — 2023-11-30 global *issuing* partnership integrating UPI cards into Unlimit's BaaS API (live product line, multi-geo). Clearly ahead of Sends on issuing.
- **OpenWay (Way4)** — 2026-03 expanded UPI cooperation: clients can issue/acquire/tokenize UPI cards on Way4; first Big Pay-for-UPI launch with six Central Asian banks ([[OpenWay and UnionPay International expand cooperation on Way4]]). OpenWay is the *processing platform* that enablers like Sends would sit on top of.
- **PVS (Argentina)** — 2026-10 UPI local card issuing, courting domestic users, wallet-linked ([[UnionPay to issue cards in Argentina with PVS]]).
- Acceptance/acquiring enablers: Getnet (Spain/Portugal), Geidea (MEA dual-routing), Amadeus Outpayce (airlines), NCR Atleos (UK ATMs), Worldpay (ExpressPay/3DS) — all UPI acceptance expansions through 2025–26.
- Network-level rivals: **Visa/Mastercard** issuer-processor/BaaS stacks are vastly deeper; the parallel in the corpus is [[LianLian Global partners UnionPay International on payments]] (2025-06, Money20/20).

**Position:** Sends is *catching up / parity-at-best* — a small EMI adding a second network rail that larger enablers (Unlimit, OpenWay) already operate at scale. **Why the landscape looks this way:** UPI's international growth is distribution-led — it needs local issuers/acquirers/BaaS partners in each market because it lacks the organic issuer base Visa/MA enjoy outside China. So "partner" announcements are UPI's standard go-to-market; the signal value of any single one is low unless it comes with live volume.

## [2] Company history / fit
Sends is a UK fintech / FCA EMI offering multi-currency accounts, SEPA/SWIFT/UK payments, card acquiring and payouts for SMEs and consumers across UK/EU; CEO Alona Shevtsova is a visible industry figure (award/speaker circuit). Trajectory on UPI specifically: integration (2025-10) → PAIO-brokered acquiring (2025-11) → direct issuing+acquiring cooperation (2026-10). The move fits logically: a cross-border EMI whose clients serve Asian-linked trade/travel wants UPI acceptance+issuance as a differentiator vs the Visa/MA-only crowd.
**Why the company acts this way:** a mid-size EMI competes on breadth of rails and corridors, not price. UPI (250M+ cards outside China, 183 countries acceptance) is the cheapest way to claim a "China/Asia corridor" story and win merchants exposed to Chinese customers — a marketing/positioning asset more than a near-term revenue driver.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Genuinely new vs Sends' own past: **issuing** is a net-new capability, and *direct* (vs PAIO-brokered) acquiring would be new. But value-add is unproven: everything is "plans to develop," zero traction metrics (no issued cards, no merchants onboarded, no go-live). Against the market it is **not novel at all** — Unlimit did BaaS UPI issuing in 2023; OpenWay industrialised issue/acquire/tokenize in 2026.
**Why the value-add is likely modest (1–2 levels deeper):** In the UPI enablement stack the margin accrues to (a) the network (UPI interchange/scheme fees) and (b) the processor/BaaS platform (OpenWay/Unlimit-type) that owns the certified rails. A small EMI like Sends is a *distributor* — it captures a sliver of acquiring/issuing spread on whatever volume it can route, and is itself dependent on an upstream processor's certification. So the deal broadens Sends' product shelf but does not change who captures the economics. The real question is not "does Sends have a UPI story" but "can Sends source enough China-linked merchant/cardholder volume to make a second network rail pay for its certification + ops cost" — unanswered.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch for: an actual issuing go-live date + BIN, named acquiring merchants, and whether Sends keeps PAIO in the loop or replaces it (tells you if this is real in-housing or just rebadged). Backdrop: UPI's 2026 global push (merchant acceptance, local issuance, cross-border QR; Fiserv tie for expansion; 15-partner CIFIT showcase) means more such announcements will flow — individually low-signal.
**Why the market goes this way / second-order:** UPI's geopolitical/regulatory baggage (US-China tensions, sanctions sensitivity — cf. [[China's UnionPay may reconnect select non-sanctioned Russian banks]]) makes it a *complement* rail for EU/UK EMIs, not a replacement for Visa/MA. Counterintuitively, adding UPI can raise Sends' compliance/KYC burden (China-corridor AML scrutiny) faster than it adds revenue — so the near-term effect may be cost, not upside.

## Red-team challenge questions

1. **Is this actually the first Sends–UPI tie-up?** No. Sends integrated UPI in 2025-10 and got UPI acquiring via PAIO (with Decta) in 2025-11. This 2026-10 note is iteration #3 — framed as a fresh "global cooperation." Fresh on *scope* (adds issuing + direct acquiring), not on *relationship*.
2. **Announced or launched?** Announced only. Entirely "plans to develop / working to develop." No go-live date, no issued cards, no onboarded merchants. Treat as MoU-level.
3. **What is the precise new capability vs the PAIO arrangement?** PAIO (not Sends) was the acquirer; Sends resold acceptance. The new claim is Sends building its *own* direct UPI acquiring + net-new *issuing*. Whether PAIO is being replaced is unstated — a key tell of substance. **Open.**
4. **Who captures the margin?** UPI (scheme fees) + the upstream processor/BaaS platform. Sends is a distributor taking a spread on whatever volume it routes. Deal does not shift economics in Sends' favour.
5. **Does Sends have the volume to justify a second network rail?** Unknown — no cardholder/merchant numbers disclosed. Certification + ops cost of UPI issuing is real; payback depends on China-corridor demand Sends has not quantified. **Open.**
6. **Who already did this, and better?** Unlimit (UPI BaaS issuing, 2023); OpenWay (issue/acquire/tokenize UPI on Way4 + first Big Pay-for-UPI, 2026). Sends is catching up, not leading.
7. **Which upstream processor will Sends issue/acquire on?** Not disclosed. Likely sits on a third-party issuer-processor (OpenWay-class) — meaning dependency risk. **Open.**
8. **Geographic scope?** Unstated. UK/EU issuing? Which acquiring markets? No answer — weakens the "global" label.
9. **Is "global cooperation" an exclusivity or a non-binding MoU?** No exclusivity or binding terms disclosed. Reads as standard UPI partner-enablement boilerplate.
10. **Fraud/chargeback liability on UPI acquiring?** Silent. Relevant given cross-border China-linked merchant risk.
11. **Compliance second-order cost?** Adding a China-corridor rail likely raises AML/KYC and sanctions-screening burden for a UK EMI — possible near-term net cost, not upside. (analysis)
12. **Geopolitical risk to the rail?** UPI carries US-China/sanctions sensitivity (cf. Russia-bank reconnection debate). A complement rail, not a Visa/MA substitute; exposure to policy shocks.
13. **Any numbers at all?** Only UPI's own network stats (250M+ cards outside China, 183 countries). Nothing Sends-specific. The PR leans on UPI's scale to inflate a small-EMI announcement.
14. **Duplicate check vs corpus?** No existing note covers *this* issuing+acquiring cooperation; the Sends–UPI priors are not in the news DB (only external). So not a corpus duplicate → fresh.
15. **What would make it a 4–5?** A dated issuing go-live with a BIN and real card volumes, named acquiring merchants, and confirmation Sends in-houses acquiring (drops PAIO). Until then it is positioning, not traction.

**Importance: 2/5 — rationale:** Genuine new scope for Sends (issuing + direct acquiring) keeps it fresh, but it is a small UK EMI's forward-looking "plans to develop" with zero traction, no numbers, no go-live, and it trails Unlimit/OpenWay on the exact same UPI capability. Low novelty + no adoption → 2/5. Would drop to 1 if it were merely a reprint of the PAIO deal; it is not.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Card issuing/acquiring infrastructure (processing + scheme connectivity) layered on card-network acceptance. The adjacent card-issuing-services market is sized at ~$14.8bn in 2026, forecast to ~$31.4bn by 2035 at ~8.7% CAGR (per MarkWide Research — a secondary forecaster, treat as indicative, not audited). The relevant network asset here is UnionPay's acceptance footprint: cards usable in 183 countries/regions, with >250m cards now issued outside mainland China across 83 countries, up from >150m across 67 countries (per UnionPay International / PRNewswire, as of 2026). Structure: value chain splits into scheme (UnionPay), issuer-processor/program manager (Sends), and acquirer — fragmented at the issuer/acquirer layer, highly consolidated at the scheme layer. Entry barriers are regulatory (EMI/e-money licensing) and network access (scheme certification), not capital-intensive per se. Why now: Chinese-network cross-border acceptance is expanding from a "serve Chinese tourists" posture toward courting local users in new markets (e.g. [[UnionPay to issue cards in Argentina with PVS]], 2026-10), and scheme diversification beyond Visa/Mastercard is a live theme for European/EMEA processors seeking Asia-facing flows.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/acquiring volume, cards issued, take rate per transaction, acceptance density — none disclosed by Sends `[UNSOURCED]`. Basis of competition: distribution (scheme partnerships + merchant reach) and licensing, not price. Recent peer moves on the SAME UnionPay rail cluster densely: Unlimit–UnionPay global issuing partnership; [[OpenWay and UnionPay International expand cooperation on Way4]] (full issue/acquire/tokenize support, 2026-03); [[Geidea and UnionPay roll out dual-card routing in MEA]] (2026-05); [[Getnet and UnionPay expand acceptance in Spain and Portugal]] (2025-11); [[LianLian Global partners UnionPay International on payments]] (2025-06); [[Amadeus Outpayce and UnionPay enable card acceptance for airlines]] (2026-03). Protagonist's position: niche / catching up (analysis). Sends (operated by Smartflow Payments Ltd, UK; per Yahoo Finance/Fortune) is a small EMI that already launched UnionPay acquiring in 2025; this deal extends it to issuing. Moat: thin — it resells access to someone else's network rather than owning rails or scale (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** No deal value, round, or valuation disclosed for Sends — so no multiple is computable for the protagonist (no data). External scale anchor: Marqeta (MQ), the listed card-issuing-platform comp, posted FY25 net revenue of $624.9m (+23% YoY) and Q2-2026 net revenue of $176m (+17% YoY) (per Marqeta IR/SEC). Market cap readings conflicted badly across sources ($7.46bn vs $1.66bn on ~the same date) so P/S is NOT computed here — `[UNSOURCED]` rather than a fabricated multiple. Internal comps above are partnership announcements, not valuations, so distribution not computed; qualitative comparison only. Takeaway: this is a capability partnership, not a priced event — multiples are not the right lens.

**Risk flags.**
1. Rail dependence / disintermediation — Sends sits atop UnionPay's network and an issuer-processor stack; the economics (take rate, fraud liability, exclusivity) are undisclosed, and the scheme captures the structural value. "Global cooperation" language is announce-stage, not live volume.
2. Crowding on the same rail — Unlimit, OpenWay, Geidea, Getnet, LianLian all offer UnionPay issuing/acquiring; Sends has no visible differentiation, raising the risk it is a commodity reseller with weak pricing power.
3. Geopolitical/compliance concentration — UnionPay is a Chinese state-linked network; EMEA issuers leaning into it carry sanctions/regulatory and reputational exposure if China-facing payment rails are restricted.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector, this is incremental scheme-diversification, not a re-rating: one more small European EMI bolting UnionPay onto its stack to chase Asia-facing flows (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: UnionPay is quietly assembling a wide bench of third-party issuer/acquirer partners in EMEA to grow usage by locals (not just Chinese tourists) — watch for Sends to disclose actual issued-card or TPV numbers within ~12 months. Thesis breaks if the partnership stays a press release with no launched product or volume.
