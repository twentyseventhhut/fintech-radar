# Context-enrichment: MNT-Halan plans $150M IPO on Egyptian Exchange
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 4/5._

**FRESHNESS: fresh.** This is the "talks → priced deal" conversion of [[Egypt's MNT-Halan prepares Cairo IPO of local business]] (25-Jun-2026), which was an explicitly *company-denied trial balloon* ("no decision made", no filing, no price, no float %). The Oct-2026 event is materially new: a **launched, priced offering** with firm terms — EGP 24.5/share, 320m shares (~20%), EGP 7.84bn/$150m target, a named cornerstone (CIB up to EGP 2bn), a dated trading start (20-Oct-2026), and — for the first time — **audited-style financials in a prospectus**. Not stale.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
MNT Tech Holding for Financial Investments — the **Egyptian arm only** of group MNT-Halan — set its EGX IPO at **EGP 24.5/share**, offering **320m shares (~20% of issued capital)** to raise **~EGP 7.84bn ($150m)**. Structure: **48m shares public** (offer runs to ~15-Oct), **up to 272m shares private placement (~85% of the deal, closing ~13-Oct)**; trading expected to **start 20-Oct-2026**. That prices the **Egypt unit at ~EGP 39.2bn (~$750m)** — Egypt's biggest IPO by dollar proceeds since 2021.
- **Cornerstones carry the book.** **CIB** (Egypt's biggest private-sector lender) committed **up to EGP 2bn (~$38m)**; with London's **Redwheel**, cornerstones pre-committed **>one-third of the base offering**. Why this matters → the deal is **anchor-led, not retail-pull**: only 48m of 320m shares (15%) are public; 85% is placed privately. That de-risks clearing the book but means the "IPO" is largely an institutional/related-party placement, and the public float / free-float liquidity is thin.
- **The valuation tell — resolved, and it confirms June's hypothesis.** The listed Egypt unit is valued at **~$750m**, i.e. the bankers' earlier **"up to $1bn"** pitch was **marked down to the low end**, and it sits **well below the $1.4bn group** post-money struck in Jun-2026 ([[MNT-Halan hits $1.4bn valuation in round led by Al Ahly Capital]]). So the carve-out of Türkiye/UAE/Pakistan does flatter the group: the listed cash-cow geography clears at ~$750m, implying rest-of-group ≈ $0.65bn to reconcile with $1.4bn — or that the private $1.4bn mark was rich (analysis).
- **Why structured this way → what it reveals.** Listing **only Egypt**, mostly via **private placement**, with **>half of proceeds reinvested in the Egyptian business** (per Nakhla): this is **permanent equity to keep levering a securitisation-funded book** plus **PE-exit liquidity** for 2021/2023-vintage backers — not a growth-capital necessity. The $150m raised is small vs the ~$947m gross loan book.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Direct EGX precedent — Valu (EGX: VALU):** Egypt's BNPL/consumer-finance player, public since 21-May-2025 via EFG in-kind distribution, ~$370m cap ([[Egypt BNPL firm ValU goes public on Egyptian Stock Exchange]]; [[Egypt's Valu reports 94% revenue growth in H1 2025]]; [[Amazon exercises option to acquire stake in ValU]]). MNT-Halan's Egypt unit at **~$750m is ~2x Valu** — but MNT-Halan now discloses **FY25 adjusted revenue ~$389.5m and net income ~$37.6m**, so unlike June the multiple IS computable: **~1.9x adjusted revenue, ~20x net income** on the $750m mark. That is a *reasonable* NBFI multiple, not obviously stretched — the richness fear from June eases once numbers exist.
- **Fawry (EGX: FWRY):** Egypt's first fintech IPO (Aug-2019); the listed payments reference ([[Fawry partners Erada Finance for instant microfinance in Egypt]]).
- **Private Egypt peers:** Paymob, Khazna, Telda (sub-scale); bank-launched [[QNB Group gets licenses for digital bank ezbank]]; diaspora [[Munify raises $3M seed for Egyptian diaspora neobank]].
- **Gulf anchors:** Tabby ($3.3bn), Tamara ($1bn) — poor proxies; MNT-Halan is a **lending-led NBFI** (NIM + cost of risk), not pure BNPL. Position: **clear domestic leader, only EGX-ready pure-play fintech**; now priced at a defensible ~2x Valu on real financials.

## [2] Company history / fit
Founded 2018 by **Mounir Nakhla** (CEO) & **Ahmed Mohsen** (CTO). ~$120m (2021) → **$400m/~$1bn** Chimera round (Jan-2023, Egypt's first fintech unicorn) → **$157.5m Series E + Tam Finans (Türkiye) acquisition** (Jul-2024) → 7th securitised bond (Oct-2025) → **$1.4bn** Al Ahly Capital round (Jun-2026) → **$82m securitised bonds** (Oct-2026, loan book nears $1bn, [[MNT-Halan closes $82M in securitised bond issuances]]) → **EGX IPO** (Oct-2026). Footprint: Egypt, Türkiye, UAE, Pakistan.
- **Why act now / why Egypt-only.** Funding is **off-balance-sheet securitisation**, not deposits — a securitisation-funded lender needs **permanent equity** to keep levering. Listing the **profitable, mature Egypt geography** (24.3% of Egypt's non-bank microfinance market by loan book; 14% of non-bank consumer finance by disbursement, end-2025) raises hard currency/local equity and a public mark, while keeping loss-making/earlier-stage international units unlisted (structural logic).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Real, now-disclosed traction (prospectus-grade):** FY25 **adjusted revenue ~$389.5m** (EGP 20.5bn, +44% CAGR 2023–25), **adjusted gross profit ~$231.8m**, **net income ~$37.6m**; **adjusted ROaE 32.2%** (2025). Loan book: **~$947m gross** at 30-Jun-2026 (loan book CAGR ~41%), **~$6.1bn cumulative disbursed since inception**, **~1.9m active customers**, **1,200+ distribution points across 25 governorates**, ~1.4m Halan cards issued since Oct-2023.
- **What's new vs June:** the *event* is the same strategic move (list Egypt), BUT the June note's central red flags are now **answered**: there ARE audited-style financials, the unit IS GAAP-profitable (~$37.6m net), and the valuation IS marked (~$750m, low end). The "no data → can't compute multiples" gate from June is lifted.
- **Why the value-add is real but capped (deeper):** ROaE 32.2% and ~20x P/E look attractive, BUT equity holders sit **junior to the securitisation funding stack** (bondholders first), and "**adjusted**" revenue/profit invites scrutiny on what's normalised out (securitisation gains, FX). The margin-capture question: as banks become partners (30+) AND shareholders (Al Ahly 2026, CIB cornerstone 2026), the moat shifts from "disrupt the bank" to "be the rails the banks fund" — durable but ceilings the re-rating vs an independent neobank.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Immediate:** public offer to ~15-Oct, private placement close ~13-Oct, **trading from 20-Oct-2026**, subject to FRA/EGX approvals and market conditions. Watch the **aftermarket**: with 85% placed privately and only ~15% public, early price discovery is thin.
- **Macro:** foreigners have been **net sellers of Egyptian stocks all year**; MNT-Halan is framed as **the best chance in years to pull foreign flows back to the EGX** — so the deal doubles as a **sentiment bellwether** for Egyptian equities, not just a fintech event.
- **Why the market may go this way → second-order:** a clean float re-rates Egyptian fintech and templates **bank-backed PE exits**. Counterintuitive risk: a largely **cornerstone/related-party-anchored** book (CIB + Al Ahly both lenders/partners AND holders) means the "$750m clearing price" is **not fully arm's-length**; a weak public aftermarket would signal the private marks (and the $1.4bn group) are soft. EGP devaluation remains the standing USD-translation risk on an EGP-earning book.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a non-bank lending / fintech-ecosystem IPO in an emerging frontier market (Egypt), so two sector layers matter: the EGX capital-market cycle and MENA/Africa fintech credit. (1) EGX is coming off a listing drought — MNT-Halan's ~$150M deal is billed as "Egypt's biggest IPO since 2021" (per Bloomberg/Arab News, as of 2026-10-06), and the exchange is targeting ~8 listings in 2026, its most active IPO year ever (per African Markets, 2026). (2) "Why now" is a currency/macro reset, not organic euphoria: two EGP devaluations + an IMF arrangement pushed the pound to a stabilized EGP 48–50 range and closed the parallel-market premium, so dollar investors who entered near the bottom captured positive USD returns (per PwC / Middle East Observer, 2026). The float is framed by local press as "one of the best chances in years to bring foreign investors back to the EGX," since foreigners have been net sellers of Egyptian equities all year (per EnterpriseAM, 2026-10-04). (3) Structurally the deal is unusual: MNT is listing its **Egyptian operating arm** (MNT Tech Holding), not the whole group — the Egyptian unit is valued at ~EGP 39.2bn (~$750M) (per Bloomberg, 2026-10-06). Non-bank lending in Egypt is capital-hungry (funded via repeated securitizations) and regulation-gated, which is the real entry barrier.

**Competitive landscape.** For a non-bank lender the KPIs are gross loan book, disbursement growth, net income, and market share of the microfinance/consumer-finance pools. MNT-Halan's disclosed position: gross loan book ~$947M (EGP 46.7bn) at end-June; ~$6.1bn disbursed since inception; ~1.9M borrowers; ~24.3% of Egypt's non-bank microfinance market by loan book and ~14% of non-bank consumer-finance by disbursements at end-2025 (per AGBI / Dealroom / Forbes ME, 2025–2026). Basis of competition = distribution + funding access rather than price; MNT's moat is a vertically integrated ecosystem (lending + payments + wallet + investments + e-commerce) plus Egypt's first fintech-unicorn scale and a bank license in Pakistan (analysis — the ecosystem lock-in is real but the numbers that would prove switching costs, e.g. cross-sell/retention, are `[UNSOURCED]`). Recent moves: $1.4bn group round led by Al Ahly Capital (NBE's arm) June 2026 [[MNT-Halan hits $1.4bn valuation in round led by Al Ahly Capital]]; repeated securitizations ($82M, $41M) to fund the loan book [[MNT-Halan secures $41m to fuel Egypt lending growth]]; CIB signed as cornerstone for up to EGP 2bn (~$38M) of this IPO (per EnterpriseAM, 2026-10-06). Position: regional category leader in Egyptian non-bank credit.

**Comps & multiples.**
- **Group valuation vs earnings (de-PR'd):** Group 2025 adjusted revenue ~$389.5M (EGP 20.5bn), up from $264.1M (2024); net income ~$37.6M; ~44%/41% revenue/loan-book CAGR 2023–2025 (per Forbes ME / AGBI, 2026). Against the June group valuation of $1.4bn: P/S = `$1.4bn / $389.5M = 3.6x`; P/E = `$1.4bn / $37.6M = 37.2x`. These are **group** figures, not the listed Egyptian unit.
- **Listed-unit multiples:** the EGX listing values only the Egyptian arm at ~$750M. Egyptian-unit-only revenue and net income are not separately disclosed, so unit P/S and P/E = **no data** — do not divide the group's $389.5M revenue by the $750M unit value (mismatched perimeters).
- **Internal comps (frontier/EM fintech IPOs in base):** [[OPay hires banks for US IPO at about $4 billion valuation]] (Nigeria payments/neobank, ~$4bn); [[GCash operator Mynt eyes $8 billion valuation in Philippine IPO]] (~$8bn); [[Razorpay files confidential IPO at around $5 billion valuation]] (India, ~$5bn); [[Lendbuzz files for IPO at around $1.5B valuation]] (US auto-lending, ~$1.5bn). Context: [[MENA records 14 IPOs raising $2.4B in Q1 2025]]. These frame MNT as small by deal size (~$150M raised) but the group $1.4bn valuation sits well below OPay/Mynt/Razorpay — consistent with Egypt's smaller, currency-discounted market. Peer market-cap P/E/P/S are not publicly computable here → distribution not computed, qualitative comparison only.
- **Flag:** group P/E ~37x and P/S ~3.6x are not cheap for a frontier-market lender, but net income grew off ~44% revenue CAGR, so the multiple is growth-justified rather than an automatic over-valuation flag. The genuine anomaly is the ~$750M unit value vs the $1.4bn June **group** round — explained by perimeter (subsidiary IPO), not a down-round, but worth watching.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Perimeter / minority-of-a-subsidiary risk.** Public buyers get 20% of the *Egyptian arm*, not the group that raised at $1.4bn; the parent (MNT Tech Holding) keeps control, cross-border assets (Türkiye, Pakistan bank, UAE) and the related-party plumbing. Minority holders of a controlled EM subsidiary face valuation leakage and governance risk — the IPO prospectus's related-party/transfer-pricing terms are the thing to read.
2. **FX / macro dependence.** The whole thesis rests on a stabilized EGP (48–50) after two devaluations; a renewed devaluation re-prices both the dollar value of the stake and the real value of an EGP loan book, and foreign demand (already net-selling all year) can reverse fast.
3. **Funding-model concentration.** The loan book is funded by serial securitizations and bank/DFI lines (Al Ahly/NBE, CIB cornerstone) — rate or liquidity tightening, or a single large-funder pullback, directly throttles disbursement growth, which is the entire growth story.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is less a company re-rating than a **market-reopening signal** for the EGX: if a fintech can clear a $150M book with CIB cornerstoning and pull foreign bids back, it unblocks the ~8-listing 2026 pipeline (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: the Oct-20 debut trades up and holds, with visible foreign participation, within ~1 month. What breaks it: a weak/undersubscribed private placement (closes Oct-13) or renewed EGP weakness — either would re-freeze the frontier-IPO window rather than reopen it.
