SBI Holdings, Startale join Open Standard


 
 Maria Nikolova 
 
 
 
 0 Comments 
 
 
 October 16, 2026 
 
 
SBI Holdings, Inc and Startale Group Pte. Ltd today announced they have joined Open Standard, the fast-growing alliance of more than 140 leading global companies, including Visa, Stripe, Mastercard, BlackRock, and Coinbase, driving the adoption of Open USD (OUSD), a U.S. dollar-denominated stablecoin built for global-scale settlement.
As part of this move, the two companies will explore interoperability between OUSD and JPYSC, the yen-denominated trust-type stablecoin issued by SBI Shinsei Trust Bank. The public launch of OUSD onchain provides a foundation for this partnership, with the companies working toward faster, more efficient settlement between the world’s two most-traded currencies.
The move comes as the global financial system undergoes a historic shift toward blockchain-based settlement. Landmark U.S. legislation, namely the GENIUS Act, has accelerated the build-out of regulated, enterprise-grade stablecoin infrastructure worldwide. In June 2026, this momentum culminated in the launch of Open Standard, uniting more than 140 companies spanning finance, payments, and technology behind a single mission: establishing OUSD as a common settlement layer for the onchain economy.
SBI and Startale have been at the forefront of this transformation in Asia. In July 2026, SBI Global Asset Management, DigiFT Tech Pte. Ltd., and Startale unveiled a proof-of-concept for settlement and distribution payments on tokenized securities, a project designed with JPYSC’s future utility in mind. Startale has continued to broaden JPYSC’s real-world use and to build institutional-grade trading infrastructure, extending onchain finance well beyond payments.
Against this backdrop, SBI and Startale see growing interoperability between JPYSC and OUSD as a powerful lever, one capable of streamlining yen-dollar settlement and unlocking new use cases for stablecoins across the onchain financial landscape. With OUSD now publicly launched, the companies will explore concrete opportunities to advance this vision.
Yoshitaka Kitao, Representative Director, Chairman, President & CEO, SBI Holdings, Inc, commented:
 “As onchain finance continues to evolve, cross-border transactions will become increasingly prevalent. SBI Group is working to establish the ‘SBI APAC Digital Economic Zone,’ and believes that enabling seamless transactions across borders and currencies lies at the heart of this vision. 
 In particular, ensuring seamless convertibility between yen-denominated and U.S. dollar-denominated stablecoins is essential. Our participation in Open Standard and exploration of a proof of concept for interoperability between OUSD and JPYSC represent an important step toward achieving this goal. SBI Group has already become the first in Japan to offer the U.S. dollar-denominated stablecoins USDC and RLUSD, and will continue to promote the broader adoption of digital assets through collaboration with leading partners in Japan and overseas.” 
Sota Watanabe, CEO, Startale Group Pte. Ltd, added:
 “Our participation in Open Standard reflects the shared conviction of SBI and Startale that the future of finance is onchain, borderless, and interoperable by design. We see the opportunity to pilot interoperability between JPYSC and OUSD as highly significant. The yen and the dollar are two of the world’s strongest and most trusted currencies and enabling them to be exchanged and utilized seamlessly onchain could unlock significant opportunities. By working closely with the SBI Group and Open Standard’s partner companies, we aim to connect Japanese assets and financial services with global onchain markets through the creation of concrete use cases and help build that future.” 
By joining Open Standard, SBI and Startale are positioning JPYSC for deeper integration with global stablecoin infrastructure, a move aimed at strengthening the international liquidity of yen-denominated assets. The companies will continue to deepen collaboration with Open Standard’s global partner network, working within applicable regulatory frameworks to build a safe, trusted, and truly interoperable stablecoin ecosystem for the onchain era.

# Context-enrichment: SBI Holdings and Startale join Open Standard OUSD stablecoin alliance
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

**FRESHNESS VERDICT: FRESH.** This is a new, distinct event (2026-10-16): SBI + Startale joining the Open Standard consortium and stating intent to pilot JPYSC↔OUSD interoperability. It is NOT a reprint of the JPYSC launch ([[SBI and Startale to launch Japan's first trust yen stablecoin]], Mar 2026) nor of [[Ripple and SBI launch RLUSD stablecoin in Japan]] (Jun 2026). Related same-month SBI stablecoin items are separate events: [[SBI tests stablecoin QR payments for Japan-Korea travel]], [[SBI, Mesh and Money Forward plan Japan crypto payments JV]], [[Japan's megabanks complete yen stablecoin cross-border pilot]].

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- SBI Holdings and Startale Group announced (2026-10-16) they **joined Open Standard**, the consortium behind the OUSD (Open USD) stablecoin, and will **"explore" a proof-of-concept** for interoperability between OUSD and JPYSC (SBI Shinsei Trust Bank's yen trust-type stablecoin).
- **Crucial de-PR:** this is explicitly exploratory. The underlying chainwire release states plainly "This announcement relates solely to the exploration of a pilot. It does not represent the launch of a new commercial service." So: no live yen-dollar settlement rail, no signed commercial integration — a membership + stated intent. [tradingview/chainwire]
- The PR frames "joining Open Standard" as a milestone, but Open Standard membership is a **governance/yield-share partner list** (140+ logos), not a hard commitment. SBI/Startale become 2 of 140+; **why framed this way** → joining a big-logo alliance manufactures credibility and global-reach optics cheaply, ahead of any real product.
- The claim "OUSD now publicly launched onchain provides a foundation" is accurate: OUSD went **live onchain 2026-09-30** (announced 2026-06-30) on Ethereum/Solana/Base/Tempo. [cryptobriefing, coinmarketcap]
- SBI's "first in Japan to offer USDC and RLUSD" is **confirmed**: USDC via SBI VC Trade launched 2025-03-26 (first JFSA-approved global USD stablecoin in Japan); RLUSD went live in Japan 2026-06-25. [theblock, coindesk]

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Open Standard vs the incumbent it attacks:** Open Standard/OUSD is a Bridge (Stripe-owned) issued coin, founding CEO Zach Abrams (Bridge co-founder), native on Stripe/Paradigm's **Tempo** L1. Its pitch undercuts **Circle/USDC** economics by rebating reserve yield to partners + zero mint/redeem fees. On the 2026-06-30 announcement Circle (CRCL) fell ~19%. [unchainedcrypto, crypto.news]
- **Closest consortium analog: Global Dollar Network (USDG) by Paxos** (launched 2024-11-01, 150+ members, same "distribution coalition + yield-share" model). Open Standard differs by routing issuance via Bridge/Stripe and launching with Tempo + payments-network logos (Visa/Stripe/Mastercard). [paxos, pymnts]
- **Yen-stablecoin peers to JPYSC:** JPYC (funds-transfer type, live 2025-10-27, capped at ~¥1M/transfer → retail/SME) vs JPYSC (trust type, no cap → institutional). The three megabanks (MUFG/SMBC/Mizuho) are piloting bank-issued yen stablecoins ([[Japan's megabanks complete yen stablecoin cross-border pilot]]). **Why the landscape is this way:** Japan's 2022/2023 amended PSA split stablecoins into trust-type (uncapped, institutional) and funds-transfer-type (capped, retail), so JPYSC's structure is its institutional edge.
- **SBI's position:** a fast-follower / bridge-builder, not a protocol owner. It is a *member* of OUSD, and the *distributor/issuer-side* of JPYSC — it captures Japan distribution, not the global settlement layer.

## [2] Company history / fit
- SBI's stablecoin trajectory is consistent and dense: USDC first-in-Japan (2025-03), tokenized-stock JV with Startale ([[SBI Holdings forms tokenized stock venture with Startale]], 2025-08), yen-stablecoin plan with Startale ([[SBI Holdings plans yen-pegged stablecoin with Startale]], 2025-12), $63m Series A into Startale ([[Startale raises $63m Series A from SBI for on-chain infrastructure]], 2026-03), JPYSC trust stablecoin (launched ~2026-06), RLUSD first-in-Japan (2026-06).
- **Why SBI acts this way:** Kitao's stated "SBI APAC Digital Economic Zone" strategy needs seamless yen↔USD convertibility onchain. SBI structurally wants to be *the* Japan on-ramp for every major stablecoin (USDC, RLUSD, now OUSD) rather than betting on one — a portfolio/aggregator play. Joining OUSD is a cheap option on the eventual winning global USD rail.
- **Startale fit:** Sota Watanabe (Astar Network founder; Soneium = Sony L2 JV, mainnet 2025-01). Startale is the technical/infra builder for JPYSC; SBI VC Trade distributes; SBI Shinsei Trust Bank is legal issuer. Open Standard gives Startale a path to connect Japanese assets to global onchain markets.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty is thin.** The genuinely new fact is just membership + PoC intent. No new terms, no numbers, no live product. The JPYSC↔OUSD "yen-dollar settlement" vision is aspirational.
- **Traction is near-zero for this specific item.** OUSD itself has weak early traction: ~$600–825M mcap but only ~554 holders (early-Oct) → institution/treasury-driven, not adoption. Ark Invest's analyst flagged OUSD's cold-start/liquidity problem and governance friction among too many stakeholders. [cryptobriefing, unchainedcrypto]
- **Who captures the margin:** the global USD settlement layer is Bridge/Stripe (OUSD issuer) + Tempo. SBI/Startale capture Japan-side distribution and the JPYSC leg. If OUSD becomes the dominant partner-USD coin, SBI is a connected distributor, not the rail owner — margin sits upstream. **What breaks the value-add:** if OUSD fails to win liquidity vs USDC/USDG, the JPYSC interop has no counterparty worth integrating.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Next step is a stated PoC; no timeline, scope, or target chain disclosed (open). Watch for whether "explore" converts to a live JPYSC↔OUSD swap/settlement path.
- **Regulatory backdrop:** supportive — US GENIUS Act accelerated enterprise stablecoin build-out; Japan's PSA trust-type regime enables JPYSC with no cap. This lowers friction for yen↔USD onchain settlement.
- **Why the market goes this way / second-order:** the race is to be the default B2B/cross-border settlement stablecoin. Counterintuitive risk — Open Standard's 140-logo breadth is also its weakness (governance friction, no single owner forcing liquidity), so a big-logo alliance can look strong yet stall. For SBI, joining many coalitions is a hedge, not a moat; its real asset is Japan regulatory-distribution primacy, which these moves reinforce regardless of which USD coin wins.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Global stablecoin supply ~$316bn as of 2026-06-12, with Tether USDT ~$187bn (~59%) and Circle USDC ~$75bn (~24%) controlling ~83% — a highly concentrated USD duopoly (per CryptoBriefing/CoinGape, via web, as of 2026-06). Structure is thus consolidated on the USD side but fragmenting at the governance layer: "consortium stablecoins" (reserve-yield shared with distribution partners) are the new wedge against the single-issuer model. OUSD (Open Standard, live 2026-10-01 on Base/Ethereum/Solana/Tempo) claims 140+→200+ partners incl. Visa, Stripe, Mastercard, Coinbase, Shopify, BlackRock, with >$1bn seed liquidity; its structural analogue is Paxos' Global Dollar Network/USDG (~2 dozen members, launched Nov-2024) (per Yahoo/thirdweb/eco.com, via web). "Why now": the US GENIUS Act (signed 2025-07-18; OCC final rule targeted ~Nov-2026, statutory date 2027-01-18) legitimised enterprise-grade USD stablecoins, while Japan's own framework (3 licensed lanes — banks, fund-transfer providers, trust banks — plus a foreign-stablecoin pathway effective 2026-06-01, 100% reserves, par redemption) opened the yen side (per fystack/techtimes, via web). The yen stablecoin market is tiny — <0.01% of global supply (per tech4impactsummit, via web) — so this is a positioning bet on future cross-border rails, not a live revenue pool.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: circulating supply / reserve float (drives the yield economics), on-chain settlement volume, and number of mint/redeem distribution partners. Players split three ways: single-issuer incumbents (Tether, Circle), consortium challengers (Open Standard/OUSD, Paxos GDN/USDG), and the bank-led Japanese stack (MUFG/SMBC/Mizuho "Project Pax", cleared from 2026-03, targeting ¥1tn ≈ $6.5bn circulation by 2028). On the yen side JPYSC (SBI Shinsei Trust Bank, trust-type, no transaction cap) competes with JPYC (fund-transfer type, launched 2025-10-27, ¥1m cap). SBI/Startale's position is **niche but first-mover in trust-type yen**: Japan's first trust-bank-backed yen stablecoin, and SBI claims first in Japan to offer USD stablecoins USDC and RLUSD. **Risk flags.**
1. **Standard fragmentation / spread-too-thin.** SBI now straddles RLUSD (Ripple), USDC (Circle), JPYSC (own), and now OUSD (Open Standard) — four competing standards. Backing every horse hedges relevance risk but dilutes the network-effect payoff; if the USD-consortium layer consolidates around USDG or stays dominated by USDT/USDC, OUSD membership yields little and SBI's multi-bet posture caps its share of any single winner's reserve economics.
2. **Announcement vs adoption gap.** The deliverable is a *PoC to explore* OUSD↔JPYSC interoperability — no live settlement, timeline, or volume commitment. OUSD's own 140→200 "partner" count is signatories, not integrations; actual mint/redeem flow (adoption) is the unproven variable underpinning the entire consortium thesis.
3. **Yen-market smallness + cross-border/FX regulatory drag.** Yen stablecoins are <0.01% of global supply; yen-dollar on-chain convertibility invites FX, AML and cross-jurisdiction (US GENIUS + Japan PSA) friction that can stall "seamless" settlement — second-order: the TAM for the specific JPYSC↔OUSD corridor may stay thin well past launch.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Signals the stablecoin contest is shifting from issuance to *interoperability and governance alliances* — whoever owns the cross-currency settlement graph (not just the token) captures the rails. Falsifiable thesis: OUSD membership is strategic optionality, not a near-term earner for SBI. Trigger that would prove it wrong: a dated, live JPYSC↔OUSD settlement corridor with real volume (vs. another PoC); watch whether the Japanese bank consortium (Project Pax) or USDG — not OUSD — becomes the default yen/consortium counterparty.
