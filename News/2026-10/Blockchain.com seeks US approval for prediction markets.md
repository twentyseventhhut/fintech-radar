# Blockchain.com seeks US approval for prediction markets

> [!info] 2026-10-09 · 1 упоминаний · 1 источника(ов) с текстом
> Каналы: Connecting the Dots in Fintech

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[Connecting the Dots in Fintech] 🇺🇸 Blockchain.com seeks U.S. approval for prediction markets, filing for two CFTC licences covering event contracts and crypto derivatives. The company wants to become a designated contract market and futures commission merchant. It already offers prediction markets to some international customers through a partnership with Polymarket.

Blockchain.com told CNBC it has applied for licenses with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission to offer event contracts and cryptocurrency derivatives in the U.S. 
The company already offers prediction markets and perpetual futures to some of its international customers. 
There is a growing list of companies seeking approvals to be futures exchanges, particularly as many look to join the prediction markets boom. 
Blockchain.com, a digital asset platform, wants to get in on the prediction market boom in the U.S. 
The company has filed for two licenses with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission to feature event contracts as well as cryptocurrency derivatives to retail and institutional customers in the U.S., Blockchain.com told CNBC.
Blockchain.com is asking the CFTC — the federal regulator for futures and derivatives — to designate it a futures exchange by receiving a designated contract market license. It also asks to be registered as a futures commission merchant, which are brokers for derivatives contracts.
"Users should be able to manage their digital assets, trade derivatives, and take positions on real-world events easily, without jumping between different apps," said Peter Smith, CEO and co-founder of Blockchain.com, in a statement. "Our DCM and FCM applications build toward that future in the U.S. through the appropriate regulatory frameworks."
Blockchain.com earlier this year began offering prediction markets through a partnership with Polymarket , as well as perpetual futures powered by Hyperliquid , to some of its customers internationally . 
Crypto-related companies have increasingly sought to participate in prediction markets , and vice versa. Digital asset exchanges Crypto.com and Gemini Space Station have their own event contract marketplaces, while Coinbase offers them primarily through a partnership with Kalshi. Meanwhile, Kalshi and Polymarket have launched perpetual futures — one of crypto's most popular trading products — for U.S. and international customers, respectively. 
Blockchain.com also joins 11 other companies that have filed this year alone to seek approval for DCM licenses . The CFTC has approved six new DCMs in 2026. 
The company is also in the midst of a move to the public markets. It confidentially filed for an initial public offering with the Securities and Exchange Commission in May, and Bloomberg reported last month that it was planning to go public this year targeting a valuation between $4 billion to $6 billion. 
 Disclosure: CNBC and Kalshi have a commercial relationship that includes customer acquisition and a minority investment.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] TL;DR.** On 2026-10-09 Blockchain.com disclosed it has filed **two applications with the CFTC** — for a **Designated Contract Market (DCM)** licence (to operate a US futures exchange) and **Futures Commission Merchant (FCM)** registration (to act as a derivatives broker) — so it can offer **event contracts (prediction markets)** and **crypto derivatives** to US retail and institutional users. The firm already runs prediction markets internationally via a **Polymarket** partnership and perpetual futures via **Hyperliquid**. The move lands while Blockchain.com is pursuing a confidential US IPO (targeting a ~$4–6B valuation, ~$500M raise, year-end 2026).

**[1] What's new vs. what's context.**
- NEW (2026-10-09): Blockchain.com's own DCM + FCM applications at the CFTC — its first direct bid to operate as a US futures exchange/broker rather than relying on partners.
- CONTEXT: It joins **11 other companies** that filed for DCM licences in 2026 alone; the CFTC has **approved six new DCMs in 2026**. Structurally identical to the May 2026 move by Bullish — see [[Bullish files CFTC applications for DCM and DCO registration]] (Bullish sought DCM + DCO/clearing; Blockchain.com seeks DCM + FCM/brokerage).

**[2] Dated timeline.**
- 2022-02: Blockchain.com Series C at >$14B valuation (peak).
- 2025-11: company signals 2026 IPO plans, names Lane Kasselman co-CEO — [[Blockchain.com plans 2026 IPO, names Lane Kasselman co-CEO]].
- 2026 (earlier): begins offering prediction markets via Polymarket and perps via Hyperliquid to international customers.
- 2026-01-29: CFTC Chair Michael Selig withdraws Biden-era proposed ban on political/sports event contracts; directs staff to draft new rules — regulatory door opens.
- 2026-05: Blockchain.com confidentially files Form S-1 for US IPO — [[Blockchain.com confidentially files for US IPO]]; same month Bullish files CFTC DCM/DCO applications — [[Bullish files CFTC applications for DCM and DCO registration]].
- 2026-06-10: CFTC issues proposed rule amending Rule 40.11, moving to a contract-specific (vs categorical-ban) approach to event contracts.
- 2026-09-29: Bloomberg/press report Blockchain.com targeting ~$500M IPO at $4–6B valuation by year-end.
- 2026-10-09: Blockchain.com discloses the two CFTC filings (this story).

**[3] Why it matters / stakes.**
- Prediction markets are booming: industry reports cite **$410B traded YTD 2026** across ~2.6B trades. A DCM licence would let Blockchain.com capture that flow directly (exchange + broker economics) instead of paying partner spreads to Polymarket.
- Competitive map is crowded: **Kalshi** (CFTC DCM, ~$22B valuation after May Series F — [[Kalshi raises $1 billion Series F at $22 billion valuation]]) and **Polymarket** (re-entered US via $112M QCEX/DCM acquisition; raised $2B from ICE at $9B — [[Polymarket raises $2B from ICE at $9B valuation]]) lead. **Coinbase** distributes Kalshi contracts; **Gemini** and **Crypto.com** run their own event-contract venues. Both Kalshi and Polymarket are now adding **perpetual futures**.
- Vertical integration play: a DCM+FCM would let Blockchain.com bundle digital-asset custody, derivatives trading and event contracts in one app (CEO Peter Smith's stated goal), strengthening the IPO narrative.
- Regulatory risk is live: CFTC event-contract rules are still proposed (Rule 40.11), appeals courts are split on whether sports contracts are CFTC swaps or state-regulated bets, and states have pushed back (e.g. [[Minnesota becomes first US state to ban prediction markets]], [[Spain blocks prediction markets Polymarket and Kalshi]]). CME has sued the CFTC over Kalshi's bitcoin perp approval.

**[4] Open questions.** Timeline/odds of CFTC approval (no announced timeline); whether Blockchain.com keeps the Polymarket/Hyperliquid partnerships or moves liquidity in-house once licensed; how the licences interact with the IPO timing; and whether the final Rule 40.11 framework survives the state-federal and CME litigation.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. "Filed" ≠ "approved." Is there ANY indication of CFTC approval or timeline? (No — CNBC explicitly notes no announced timeline; this is an application, one of 12 DCM filings in 2026.)
2. Is this materially different from the May 2026 Bullish DCM filing, or are we double-counting the same "crypto firm files for DCM" meme? (Different firm, different companion licence — FCM vs DCO — but same structural play; frame as part of a wave, not unique.)
3. Does Blockchain.com actually need its OWN DCM, or is the Polymarket/Hyperliquid partnership already serving US-adjacent demand? What's the incremental revenue case vs. partner economics?
4. The source says prediction markets are offered to "international customers" via Polymarket — but Polymarket itself re-entered the US via QCEX. Is there channel conflict if both go for the same US flow?
5. How credible is the "$410B traded YTD 2026" figure? It's an industry/law-firm aggregate — does it mix notional, double-counted both sides, or include crypto perps? Treat as directional, not audited.
6. Is the DCM bid primarily an IPO-dressing move (adding a US growth narrative ahead of a $4–6B listing) rather than a near-term product? What happens to the applications if the IPO slips?
7. Regulatory fragility: Rule 40.11 is only PROPOSED (2026-06-10). If the final rule narrows event-contract eligibility (sports/politics), does the DCM thesis shrink?
8. State-federal preemption and the CME-vs-CFTC suit over Kalshi's bitcoin perp are unresolved — could an adverse ruling strand a newly-granted DCM licence?
9. Competitive moat: Kalshi (DCM, $22B), Polymarket ($9B, ICE-backed), Coinbase, Gemini, Crypto.com all already live. Why would liquidity migrate to a late entrant mid-IPO?
10. FCM registration carries capital/segregation and AML obligations — is Blockchain.com's balance sheet (post-$14B→$4–6B valuation reset) sized for FCM capital requirements?
11. CNBC disclosure: CNBC and Kalshi have a commercial relationship (customer acquisition + minority investment). Does that color framing of the competitive landscape in the primary source?
12. Single source (CNBC), single mention, single channel. Any corroboration of the filing beyond rehashed wire pickups? (Multiple outlets echo CNBC but none appear to independently confirm filing docs — CFTC filings aren't public at application stage.)
13. "Peter Smith CEO and co-founder" — but the Nov 2025 note named Lane Kasselman co-CEO. Governance ambiguity worth flagging for any leadership attribution.
14. Does offering both event contracts AND crypto derivatives under one DCM invite tougher scrutiny (gambling-adjacency + leverage) than a single-product venue?
15. Jurisdiction risk precedent: Minnesota banned prediction markets and Spain/Indonesia blocked Polymarket/Kalshi — how durable is a US-federal DCM against state-level bans?

**Importance: 3/5** — Credible, well-corroborated regulatory filing from a notable crypto firm with a live IPO, sitting at the intersection of two hot themes (prediction markets + crypto derivatives). But it is an APPLICATION with no approval/timeline, the 12th DCM filing of 2026, and largely a me-too move behind Kalshi/Polymarket/Bullish. High signal as a trend data-point and IPO-narrative marker; low on standalone, near-term market impact.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US prediction markets / CFTC-regulated event contracts are in a hyper-growth, land-grab phase. Monthly notional volume across the sector grew from ~$1.2bn in early 2025 to >$20bn by Jan 2026, with ~800k+ unique monthly wallets (per TRM Labs, as of 2026); combined Kalshi+Polymarket volume ran ~$40bn in 2025 vs ~$9bn in 2024, +400% YoY (per TRM/press, 2026). Structure is regulatory-gated, not fragmented-by-default: access requires a CFTC Designated Contract Market (DCM) licence to list contracts and a Futures Commission Merchant (FCM) registration to broker them — exactly the two Blockchain.com is applying for. The barrier is regulatory + capital (clearing/settlement), not technology; this is why the field is a queue of applicants, not an open market. **Why now:** CFTC posture flipped — in Jan 2026 the Chairman withdrew the proposed rule that would have banned political/sports event contracts and ordered new enabling rulemaking (per CRS/ESPN/Forbes coverage, 2026). That regulatory green light is the catalyst: 17 DCM applications filed since early 2025, with the CFTC approving six new DCMs in 2026 and Blockchain.com joining 11 other 2026 DCM filers (per CNBC, 2026-10-09). Second-order effect: the "prediction markets × crypto-exchange" convergence is now a two-way street — crypto venues add event contracts, while Kalshi/Polymarket add perpetual futures.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: notional trading volume, take/fee rate, fee revenue, and share of US notional. Basis of competition = regulatory licence stack + liquidity/network effects (deepest order book wins) + distribution. Key players and recent moves: Kalshi holds ~91% of the US prediction market (per BofA/NS3.AI, 2026-04) and is the clear incumbent; Coinbase distributes Kalshi via partnership; Crypto.com and Gemini run their own event-contract marketplaces; Gemini secured a CFTC derivatives-clearing (DCO) licence on top of its DCM for a full-stack venue (2026-04-30); Payward (Kraken) bought Bitnomial for up to $550M to own a fully CFTC-licensed derivatives stack (brokerage + clearing + exchange, 2026-04-17); Polymarket is pursuing its own US CFTC exchange approval (2026-04). Blockchain.com's position: **late new entrant, catching up** — it only offers prediction markets internationally today via a Polymarket partnership and perps via Hyperliquid, i.e. it is reselling others' rails abroad and has no live US product. Its DCM+FCM filing is an attempt to move from reseller to owner-operator. Moat today: thin — brand + existing crypto custody/wallet user base it could cross-sell (`(analysis)`); it lacks Kalshi's liquidity flywheel and Gemini/Kraken's already-approved clearing licences.

**Comps & multiples.** Private; no public financials for Blockchain.com, so company-level multiples = no data. Sector reference multiples (round valuations, not market caps):
- Kalshi: $22bn ÷ ~$4bn annualised revenue = **~5.5x** EV/Revenue (round valuation, per Series F 2026-05 + Sacra/press revenue est.).
- Polymarket: ~$15bn ÷ ~$1.1bn annualised revenue = **~13.6x** (round-in-talks valuation; revenue est. per press).
- Blockchain.com itself: targeting a **$4bn–$6bn** IPO valuation (~$500M raise), down sharply from a $14bn 2021-cycle peak (per CoinDesk/PYMNTS, 2026-09) — a de-rating, revenue not disclosed so no multiple.
Internal comps in base: [[Kalshi raises $1 billion Series F at $22 billion valuation]], [[Polymarket in talks to raise at 15B valuation]], [[Gemini secures US derivatives clearing license for prediction markets]], [[Payward acquires Bitnomial for CFTC-licensed derivatives stack]], [[Blockchain.com confidentially files for US IPO]]. With only two comparable revenue figures and both private, distribution not computed — qualitative read only: the two comps span 5.5x–13.6x, with the spread reflecting Kalshi's scale/share vs Polymarket's lower revenue base. Blockchain.com's $4–6bn target looks modest vs these, consistent with its reseller-only, pre-US-product status `(analysis)`.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Binary regulatory gate / execution risk.** Both DCM and FCM applications are pending with the CFTC; in a queue of 12+ 2026 filers where only 6 were approved, approval and timing are not assured — and a licence alone does not create liquidity. Revenue impact is zero until both clear.
2. **Late entrant into a winner-take-most market.** Kalshi already holds ~91% of US notional and liquidity compounds (tighter spreads attract more flow). Blockchain.com would launch a US product after Kalshi, Coinbase/Kalshi, Gemini and Kraken/Bitnomial — a cold-start liquidity problem with no obvious edge beyond its crypto base.
3. **Dependence on others' rails abroad = no proof of own stack.** Its international offering runs on Polymarket (markets) and Hyperliquid (perps); it has not yet demonstrated it can run clearing/settlement itself, unlike Gemini (DCO) and Kraken (Bitnomial). Margin and control sit with the rails provider until it owns the stack.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a "me-too" entry, not a re-rating of the sector — it confirms that a CFTC DCM+FCM stack is becoming table-stakes for any crypto exchange with IPO ambitions, bundling spot + derivatives + events into one app (the Peter Smith "don't jump between apps" thesis). Falsifiable thesis: Blockchain.com will remain a marginal US prediction-market player (<5% notional share) through 2027 unless it buys liquidity or an already-licensed stack the way Kraken bought Bitnomial. Trigger to watch: CFTC approval (or denial) of the DCM/FCM applications and whether the IPO prices before or after a licence lands — a licence pre-IPO would be the real value event; what breaks the thesis = a distribution deal that imports an existing order book's liquidity.
