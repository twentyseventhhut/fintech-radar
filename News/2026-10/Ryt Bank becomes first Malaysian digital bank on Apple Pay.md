 Ryt Bank has become the first licensed digital bank in Malaysia to support Apple Pay , allowing cardholders to pay in stores, online and in apps.
The bank also added Google Pay support in January 2026 .
Customers can add their Ryt Card through the bank’s app by selecting the Cards tab, tapping “Add to Apple Wallet” and following the on-screen instructions.
Once they add the card to Apple Wallet on an iPhone, they have the option to add it to their other compatible Apple devices.
According to the bank, transactions require authentication through the device’s built-in security features, including Face ID, Touch ID or a passcode.
Card numbers are not shared with merchants or stored on Apple servers.
Customers who misplace their iPhone can mark it as lost through Find My to suspend their card on the device.
Other banks supporting Apple Pay in Malaysia include AmBank, CIMB, Hong Leong Bank, HSBC, Maybank, RHB and Standard Chartered.
Apple also lists Wise Payments Malaysia as a participating card issuer, with support varying by issuer and card type.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened.** On 2026-10-07 Ryt Bank became the first licensed digital bank in Malaysia to support Apple Pay for its Ryt Card (in-store, online, in-app), authenticated via Face ID / Touch ID / passcode, with card numbers tokenised and not stored on Apple servers. It had already added Google Pay in January 2026, so Ryt is now the first Malaysian digital bank to support both wallets. Incumbent banks already on Apple Pay in Malaysia include AmBank, CIMB, Hong Leong, HSBC, Maybank, RHB and Standard Chartered; Wise is also listed as a participating issuer — so this is a catch-up with incumbents, framed as a "first" only within the narrow digital-bank cohort.

**Who Ryt Bank is.** YTL Digital Bank Berhad, a JV of Malaysia's YTL Group and Singapore's Sea Limited (Shopee/SeaMoney parent). It launched August 2025 as Malaysia's self-styled first AI-native digital bank, built around Ryt AI, a conversational assistant powered by ILMU, a Malaysian LLM, handling payments, transfers and spending insights in Bahasa Malaysia, English, Manglish and later Mandarin. It is one of five BNM digital-bank licensees (conventional license under FSA 2013). Earlier infra partners in our DB: Episode Six (card issuing/ledger), Provenir (AI decisioning), Tencent Cloud (AI banking service).

**Market structure (BNM licensees, awarded 2022).** Five licensees: GXBank (Grab-led, conventional), Boost Bank (Axiata/RHB, conventional), Ryt Bank (YTL/Sea, conventional), AEON Bank (Islamic), KAF Digital Bank (Islamic). Per BNM's 2025 annual report the five together had ~2.4M customers and ~RM4.2bn deposits at end-2025; deposits PIDM-insured to RM250k. This is a crowded, sub-scale market relative to incumbents.

**Traction (external, dated).** Ryt reported 1.2M users in April 2026 (25M transactions, ~35x monthly volume growth since launch) and claimed 1.5M users at its August 2026 first anniversary — self-described as Malaysia's largest digital bank by customer count, overtaking GXBank ([[GXBank users reach 1.4 million as digital bank expands base]] reported 1.4M / 450M+ transactions in May 2026). Note GXBank's transaction count dwarfs Ryt's, implying deeper per-user engagement at GX. Ryt topped Malaysia app-download charts in Feb 2026 and ranks #1 finance app on the App Store. Figures are company-reported ("users"/downloads, not funded/active accounts) and unaudited.

**Why it matters / why not.** Apple Pay is table-stakes wallet parity, not a product breakthrough; it marginally improves card activation and tap-to-pay convenience and gives Ryt a marketing "first" versus GXBank/Boost/AEON. It does not change deposit economics, credit, or the AI differentiation that is Ryt's actual thesis. Signal value is mostly competitive-positioning in the digital-bank race.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Malaysia's five licensed digital banks (GXBank, Boost Bank, AEON Bank, KAF Digital Bank, Ryt Bank) served ~1.97m customers and held RM3.1bn deposits as at Sep-2025 (per Fintech News Malaysia, via fintechnews.my). Structure: a small, licence-gated oligopoly — Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) caps each entrant at a RM3bn asset ceiling during a 3–5yr "foundational phase," which both limits scale and keeps the field tightly bounded (regulatory barrier = the moat and the ceiling). "Why now": the Apple Pay add is a late catch-up (Ryt added Google Pay Jan-2026) — table-stakes wallet parity, not a differentiator, since AmBank, CIMB, Hong Leong, HSBC, Maybank, RHB and Standard Chartered already support Apple Pay in Malaysia. Secular driver: wallet/tap-to-pay ubiquity pulls digital banks toward spend-side engagement; headwind: "negative carry" — high-rate deposit gathering not yet matched by loan revenue (per retailnews.asia).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: customers, deposits, gross loans, path-to-breakeven. Players & basis of competition — distribution/parentage over price: GXBank (Grab + Kuok; "first" licence, top-10 card issuer); Boost Bank (Axiata + RHB); AEON Bank (first Islamic digibank); Ryt (YTL-backed, AI-first). Recent moves: Ryt debuted Aug-2025 as Malaysia's first AI-powered digital bank; crossed 1.2m users Apr-2026 (~7 months, 25m transactions, 35x monthly growth); claimed 1.5m users and "largest Malaysian digital bank by customer base" Aug-2026 (per SoyaCincau/fintechnews.my). Ryt AI: ~half of users have engaged, and those return at ~2x the rate of non-users (per The Edge Malaysia); conversational flows in Bahasa/English/Manglish, bill-snapshot payments, powered by Tencent Cloud Chat. Position: **ahead on growth/engagement, catching up on payments rails.** Moat (analysis): the AI/chat UX (intangible + switching-cost via habit) is the only real differentiator vs peers who share the same wallet rails — Apple Pay narrows, not widens, the gap.

**Comps & multiples.** All five digibanks are private, pre-profit; no public valuation/round in the note → EV/Rev, P/S, price-per-user = **no data / [UNSOURCED]**. Internal comps (per-user / growth / losses):
- [[GXBank users reach 1.4 million as digital bank expands base]] — 1.4m users, >450m transactions since launch (May-2026); H1-2026 net loss RM122.1m (vs RM119.9m H1-2025), gross loans RM1.21bn, deposits RM2.03bn, assets RM2.54bn vs RM3bn cap; targets first FY profit 2028 (per fintechnews.my).
- [[Ryt Bank crosses 1.2 million users in Malaysia]] — 1.2m users / 25m txns (Apr-2026); [[Ryt Bank debuts as Malaysia's AI-powered digital bank]]; [[Tencent Cloud powers Ryt Bank's AI banking service in Malaysia]].
- Regional pre-profit neobank comp (different geo, for scale): [[GoTyme Bank eyes $15 billion listing as customers top 21 million]] — PH, 21m customers.
Qualitative comparison only (distribution not computed — no comparable multiples). By users Ryt (~1.5m) now edges GXBank (~1.4m); by transactions GXBank (450m) dwarfs Ryt (25m) — Ryt's accounts are lighter/less monetised. Loss profile: GXBank still burning ~RM122m/half-year with rising credit allowances (RM61.9m vs RM18.6m) — a read-across risk for Ryt, which does not disclose its own losses → [UNSOURCED].

**Risk flags.**
1. **Profitability / negative carry** — no Malaysian digibank is profitable; sector estimates >3yrs to breakeven, GXBank targets 2028. User-count leadership (Ryt 1.5m) doesn't equal economics; low transactions/user (25m vs GXBank 450m) implies thin spend-side revenue despite wallet adds.
2. **Rails dependence / no payments moat** — Apple Pay + Google Pay run on third-party stacks already open to 7+ incumbents; the launch is parity, not advantage. Margin on the payment leg is captured by the network/issuer-processor layer, not Ryt.
3. **Regulatory asset ceiling** — RM3bn cap during the foundational phase constrains the balance-sheet scaling needed to turn deposits into loan income; exit requires BNM approval (GXBank's request still pending), a binary, out-of-management-control trigger.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector: a shift in the basis of competition from "who has the licence/distribution" to "who converts engagement into monetised spend + lending" — Apple Pay is defensive parity, so the AI-engagement edge is Ryt's only live differentiator (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Ryt's 2x-return AI cohort translates into rising transactions-per-user and loan uptake, it leads the pack to breakeven; **what breaks it** — if, like GXBank, transactions and loans stay thin while credit allowances climb, "largest by users" is a vanity metric and losses persist past 2028. Watch: Ryt's first disclosed loss/loan figures and whether BNM lifts the RM3bn cap.
