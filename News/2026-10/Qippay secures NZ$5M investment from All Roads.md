Qippay secures NZ$5 million investment from All Roads
 
Auckland fintech Qippay has secured a NZ$5 million investment commitment from All Roads Wholesale Fund, with the Australian investor taking a 25% stake in Qippay Group.
The funding is being received in tranches, with the transaction expected to complete this week.
Founded in 2021 by Ashley Lake-Johns and Wayne Deas, Qippay operates in New Zealand's open banking market. It builds technology that links bank payments with customer-consented financial data.
The business is based at Auckland Council's GridAKL innovation hub and employs six full-time staff. It plans to grow headcount to about 12 to 15 as it uses the investment to expand its team and develop new financial and data infrastructure.
 Growth plans 
Lake-Johns said the funding would support the next stage of the company's development as it adds staff and broadens its product base for New Zealand businesses.
"We want to build the financial infrastructure and the data infrastructure for New Zealand," said Ashley Lake-Johns, Chief Executive, Qippay.

 "We're working with businesses exploring better access to credit, smarter ways to manage working capital, less manual reconciliation and administration, and more competitive financial services for their customers," Lake-Johns said. 

Qippay's platform allows businesses to offer bank-to-bank payments as an alternative to card payments and traditional direct debits. It also uses customer-consented financial data to support operational processes and decision-making.
The deal brings in a trans-Tasman investor with experience in property-backed lending, accounting, and business advisory. All Roads is led by founder and Managing Director David Palmer, who is also set to join the Qippay Group board alongside Lake-Johns and Deas.
 Investor fit 
Lake-Johns said the company focused on finding an investor whose involvement wouldn't change control of the business.
"We looked for the right type of investor that wouldn't necessarily mean we lose control," Lake-Johns said.
That is significant for a company that describes itself as independent and Kiwi-majority owned, at a time when competition is increasing in digital payments and open banking services in New Zealand.
For All Roads, the attraction was Qippay's ability to apply its technology to adjacent areas of financial services, including lending, mortgage broking, accounting, and financial advice.

 "In respect of what Qippay does, we just see multiple synergies, and we want to help put Qippay on the map," said David Palmer, Founder and Managing Director, All Roads Wholesale Fund. 

Open banking has become a closely watched area for financial technology firms in New Zealand, as businesses seek alternatives to established payment methods and better use of customer-consented financial data. Qippay was an early entrant in the sector and has established direct connections with major banks using standardised APIs.
The company is also certified to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and accredited under New Zealand's open banking framework, credentials likely to matter for clients handling payments and financial information.
While the immediate priority is the domestic market, the new shareholding also gives Qippay a direct link to an Australian backer as it builds out its operations. Palmer's board role adds another Australian connection to the company's governance as it enters its next phase of expansion.
The investment marks a notable cross-border deal for a young New Zealand fintech scaling in a market where access to capital, bank connectivity, and regulatory standing can shape growth.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- All Roads Wholesale Fund (AU) commits **NZ$5M for a 25% stake** in Qippay Group → implied **~NZ$20M post-money** valuation (analysis; parties did not state a valuation). Money arrives **in tranches**, close "expected this week" — i.e. a commitment, not fully drawn cash (itbrief; [The Industry Spread "close still open"](https://theindustryspread.com/qippay-secures-nz5m-all-roads-stake-close-open/)).
- Use of funds: grow headcount **6 → 12–15**, build "financial and data infrastructure", explore AU. This is a tiny team seed/Series-A-scale raise, not a growth round.
- **Why structured this way / what it reveals:** A single investor taking 25% for NZ$5M, with the founder stressing he "wouldn't lose control", signals there was no competitive VC auction — this is a bilateral deal with a strategic/HNW-style backer, not a priced VC round with a syndicate. All Roads is a **property-backed first-mortgage lending fund**, not a fintech/tech VC (allroadsfund.com.au). The logic is cross-sell, not technology diligence: All Roads wants Qippay's rails/data applied to its adjacent lines (lending, mortgage broking, accounting, advice). David Palmer joins the board. So the "investor fit" framing is really "a lender bought distribution + a data pipe", and tech-VC validation is absent — a yellow flag on the quality of the capital.

## [1] Competitors / peers
NZ open-banking / pay-by-bank field (dates where known):
- **Akahu** — incumbent NZ open-finance aggregator; its tool is live inside **Westpac One** for loan applications (adoption, not announcement).
- **BlinkPay** — now owned by **BNZ**; real-time A2A, live with **Sharesies**. A bank-owned rival = structural threat to independents like Qippay.
- **Dosh**, **Wych** ([[Wych secures NZD 1.5M for open banking in New Zealand and Australia]], NZD 1.5M, 2025-10, also chasing AU CDR) — smaller peers.
- Global A2A/open-banking comps in corpus: [[vobapay integrates Mastercard Open Finance for A2A payments]], [[Lloyds and Lolly introduce Pay by Bank kiosks]], [[Quiltt raises $2.6M to simplify open banking integrations]] — same thesis (cards → bank rails) playing out in DE/UK/US.
- **Position:** parity-to-slightly-ahead on *compliance* (ISO/IEC 27001:2022, MBIE-accredited, direct bilateral APIs with all four majors — BNZ 2023, then Westpac/ASB/ANZ 2024). Behind on *distribution/scale* vs bank-owned BlinkPay and vs Akahu's Westpac deal.
- **Why the lay of the land / second order:** NZ's four banks control the rails AND increasingly own the distributors (BNZ→BlinkPay, Westpac→Akahu deal). The margin in bank-to-bank payments sits with whoever owns the checkout and the bank relationship — so an independent certified-but-small player risks being a compliant-but-commoditised pipe while banks capture the merchant. That is the central question for Qippay, not "does it have the tech".

## [2] Company history / fit
- Founded **2021** (Lake-Johns, Deas; Crunchbase also lists Dave Raethel). Based at GridAKL, 6 FTE. Built **direct bilateral APIs** with the big four (2023–2024) — real, hard-won bank connectivity, which is the genuine asset here.
- **Why it acts this way:** a 6-person firm with bank-grade credentials but no scale needs capital to survive the regulatory transition (see [4]). Taking money from a property-lending fund rather than a VC fits a company optimising for *control retention + a cross-sell customer* over valuation/validation. Expansion pitch into lending/mortgage/accounting adjacencies is exactly where All Roads operates — so the fit is commercial, not technological.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Nothing technologically new.** Bank-to-bank payments + consented data is the standard open-banking play (identical thesis to vobapay, Quiltt, Lloyds/Lolly). Qippay's only differentiators are **credentials** (ISO 27001, MBIE accreditation, no screen-scraping, direct bilaterals) — a moat vs new entrants, not vs incumbents.
- **Traction is thin / unproven:** no disclosed GMV, merchant count, or revenue in any source. "Working with businesses exploring…" is aspirational language. 6 staff. So this is pre-scale.
- **Why the value-add is fragile:** in A2A the margin accrues to whoever owns checkout distribution and the bank relationship. Qippay has the rails but a bank-owned competitor (BlinkPay/BNZ) has rails + a captive bank + an existing merchant (Sharesies). Qippay's bilateral-API moat also **erodes** as MBIE standardises access from 1 Dec 2025 (regulated framework) — standardisation commoditises the very bilateral connectivity Qippay spent 2023–24 building.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Tailwind (real, dated):** NZ regulated open banking live **1 Dec 2025** under the Customer and Product Data Act 2025 (CDR-style). **>401,000 open-banking payments / >NZ$130M in July 2026** (API Centre). Westpac reports open-banking use **up 6x since Dec**. Commerce Commission: pay-by-bank could cut the NZ$340M/yr businesses pay on Visa/Mastercard debit by **~53% (~NZ$180M)** ([newswire.co.nz](https://newswire.co.nz/2026/10/open-banking-save-new-zealand-businesses-180-million-debit-fees-westpac-sixfold/)).
- **Structural shift / risk:** Payments NZ **API Centre closes end-Sep 2026**; standards move to **MBIE** (v2.3 API standard). Good for market growth, bad for bilateral-API moats.
- **Why the market goes this way / second order:** a fast-growing, newly-regulated, standardised market is great for *volume* but *compresses differentiation* — it invites banks and well-capitalised aggregators to compete on distribution. Counterintuitive effect: the regulatory win that validates Qippay's category simultaneously dismantles its head-start. A NZ$5M tranche-paid raise from a non-tech lender is small ammunition for that fight.
- **Verdict: importance 2/5** — real market momentum and genuine credentials, but no novelty, unproven traction, sub-scale team, eroding moat, and low-quality (strategic-lender, not VC) capital at a modest implied valuation.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Qippay sits in NZ open banking / account-to-account (A2A) payments — bank-to-bank rails positioned against cards and direct debits. The regime is early and legally young: the Customer and Product Data Bill got Royal assent 29 Mar 2025, with banking designated as the first sector and the big-four banks' data-sharing obligations starting only 1 Dec 2025, Kiwibank payments from 1 Jun 2026 (per Russell McVeagh / API Centre, via search, as of 2026). Volumes are rising fast off a tiny base: Aug 2026 saw 545,681 open banking payments worth NZ$152.9M, up 36% m/m from July's ~401k / NZ$130M (per Payments NZ API Centre, via apicentre.paymentsnz.co.nz, as of Aug 2026). "Why now": the Dec-2025 bank-connectivity mandate + Qippay's existing direct bank APIs let an early entrant monetise before the regulated CDR data-sharing layer fully lands. Headwind — governance of the standards is itself in flux: Payments NZ's API Centre ceased operating end-Sep 2026 with standards transferring to MBIE (per API Centre / HGM Legal, via search), i.e. the rulebook Qippay builds on is mid-transition. TAM: no credible NZ A2A-payments market-size figure is publicly verifiable — no data; the monthly NZ$152.9M run-rate (~NZ$1.8bn annualised, back-of-envelope `(analysis)`) is the whole ecosystem, not an addressable slice.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: A2A payment count / TPV, customer consents authorised, data-request volume, take rate per transaction (Qippay discloses none of these — `[UNSOURCED]`). Basis of competition is distribution + bank connectivity + regulatory standing, not price. Players: aggregator/connectivity incumbents already in-market (Akahu is the best-known NZ open-banking aggregator) plus the same trans-Tasman set that treat NZ+AU as one market — [[Wych secures NZD 1.5M for open banking in New Zealand and Australia]] (NZD 1.5M, Oct 2025, explicitly NZ + Australian CDR). Global rails vendors (Stripe pay-by-bank, Plaid-style aggregators) loom over any scaled use case. Qippay's stated edge: founded 2021, "early entrant," direct standardised-API connections to major banks, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certified and accredited under the NZ open-banking framework (per itbrief, note source). Position: niche early mover at sub-scale — six staff, growing to 12–15. Moat `(analysis)`: regulatory accreditation + direct bank integrations are real switching/entry friction, but thin at this headcount and not unique (Akahu, Wych hold similar standing).

**Comps & multiples.** Round, not a market cap: NZ$5M for a 25% stake ⇒ post-money ≈ `NZ$5M / 0.25 = NZ$20M` (pre-money ≈ NZ$15M). No revenue/TPV disclosed, so EV/Revenue and all earnings multiples = no data. Price-per-head as a crude scale proxy: `NZ$20M / 6 FTE ≈ NZ$3.3M per employee` of implied valuation `(analysis)` — rich for a six-person pre-scale fintech, consistent with an early-stage strategic/minority round rather than a priced growth round. Internal comp: [[Wych secures NZD 1.5M for open banking in New Zealand and Australia]] (NZD 1.5M raise, same NZ/AU open-banking thesis, Oct 2025) — no valuation disclosed there, so distribution not computed; qualitative read is Qippay's raise is ~3x larger in ticket but still seed-scale. External peers (Akahu, Wych) are private with undisclosed valuations — `[UNSOURCED]`. Flag: in-line-to-rich for the stage; the valuation leans on optionality (adjacent lending/accounting/advice cross-sell cited by the investor), not current economics.

**Risk flags.**
1. Tiny domestic TAM / sub-scale. NZ is a ~5M-population market and the entire open-banking payment ecosystem is only ~NZ$153M/month; a 25%-of-a-six-person-company deal implies a very long runway before unit economics matter. Second-order: Qippay likely must win Australia (CDR) to justify the valuation, yet it is funding only 6→12–15 staff — thin for a two-country build.
2. Regulatory/standards pace and churn. The NZ regime is <1yr into bank obligations, Kiwibank's payment APIs only landed Jun 2026, and standards governance just moved Payments NZ API Centre → MBIE (end-Sep 2026). Second-order: Qippay's rails depend on a rulebook mid-handover — spec/timeline slippage hits its roadmap directly, and the cross-Tasman thesis rests on AU CDR where adoption is <10% after three years and action-initiation (payments) is explicitly "thin," largely B2B/gov, not displacing card checkout (per Australian Banking Association / PaymentBrief, via search, as of 2026).
3. Disintermediation by bigger rails + minority-investor fit. Global aggregators (Plaid, Stripe pay-by-bank) and well-funded locals (Akahu, Wych) can capture scaled A2A volume once the regulated data layer matures, squeezing an early-but-small connectivity player. The NZ$5M is a minority (25%) property-lending/advisory investor without open-banking pedigree, and the CEO explicitly optimised for "not losing control" over strategic value — second-order: capital and board help may be weighted to adjacent-services synergies rather than the capital-intensive AU expansion the valuation needs.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a small strategic minority round, not a sector re-rating — read it as a bet that an accredited NZ early mover can ride the Dec-2025 mandate into adjacent data/credit infrastructure, then port to AU CDR. Falsifiable thesis: Qippay reaches material TPV and lands a priced growth round within ~18 months. What breaks it: NZ A2A volumes keep compounding but revenue stays trapped at connectivity-vendor margins, AU CDR adoption stays stuck <10%, and a global aggregator or Akahu wins the scaled flows — leaving Qippay a sub-scale niche connector. Trigger to watch: first disclosed TPV/revenue metric, and whether the AU CDR build actually ships vs. the NZ-only "immediate priority."
