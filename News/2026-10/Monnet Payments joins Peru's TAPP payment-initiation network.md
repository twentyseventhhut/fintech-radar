## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Monnet Payments — a Lima-based LatAm payment processor (pay-ins/payouts, founded 2020 by Franco Zurita & Eduardo Luna) — announced (2026-10-07/09) that it has integrated with / joined **TAPP**, the BCRP's (Peru's Central Reserve Bank) retail payment-initiation infrastructure. Monnet reiterated its standing projection of **>US$6bn processed volume by end-2026** with **~40% annual growth** and presence in nine LatAm markets.

De-PR'd: this is an **announced integration / intent to participate**, not live retail traffic. TAPP itself is NOT yet operating — BCRP's controlled pilot starts only in **December 2026** with ~20 entities, with broader operation from 2027 (see [[Peru's central bank unveils TAPP public digital payments platform]]). So "joined TAPP" = Monnet positioned as one of the first PSPs in the pilot cohort, not processing live TAPP payments. The $6bn figure is a **company-wide, all-rails projection** (cards, QR, pay-ins/payouts across 9 countries) — the PR anchors it next to the TAPP news to borrow the halo of a landmark national rail, but essentially zero of that $6bn is TAPP volume today.

**Why framed this way:** TAPP is Peru's UPI-style public rail (built under a June-2024 BCRP–NPCI/India agreement, developed with India's Juspay + local Niubiz). Being an early PSP participant is a positioning/credibility play — it signals Monnet as an infrastructure-tier player ahead of a mandated interoperability regime, at near-zero cost since the pilot is months away. The volume number does the heavy lifting for the headline.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **TAPP cohort / PSP peers:** BCRP's pilot has ~20 entities across three groups, plus 30+ more interested. Named adjacents: **Niubiz** (teamed with India's **Juspay** to build the infra), **Tarjetas Peruanas Prepago**, and incumbent wallets **Yape** (BCP; ~20M+ users, ~54% of in-person tx) and **Plin** (bank consortium; ~13M+ users). Monnet is a PSP/initiator, not a wallet — it sits in the "fintech enabler" tier.
- **Direct A2A/payment-initiation peers in Peru:** [[Peru's OKTO launches interoperable QR account-to-account payments]] (Oct-2025, interoperable QR compatible with Yape/Plin/Bim) is the closest prior move — A2A initiation already exists privately; TAPP standardizes it publicly.
- **Regional template:** Brazil's Pix and India's UPI are the reference points; Peru is explicitly copying UPI via NPCI. Colombia's Bre-B ([[dLocal enables instant transfers via Colombia's Bre-B]]) and Redeban/Bold Peru entries are the LatAm instant-payments parallels.
- **Position:** Monnet is at **parity / slightly ahead** among PSPs for early positioning, but this is a crowded ~20-entity pilot — being "one of the first" is weak differentiation when the field is this large and the rail is a neutral public utility.

**Why the lay of the land is this way:** A central-bank public rail commoditizes the initiation layer — the margin that private A2A/QR enablers (OKTO, wallets) captured moves toward zero as the state provides interoperability for free. Second-order: PSPs can no longer win on "we connect you to the rail"; they must win on orchestration, merchant servicing, cross-border, and software on top. That is exactly where Monnet is repositioning (virtual accounts, pay-ins/payouts).

## [2] Company history / fit
Monnet's trajectory is **M&A-driven, profitable-from-year-one, no VC**: acquired Chilean pay-by-bank firm **ETpay** (Nov-2025, [[Monnet Payments acquires Chilean platform ETpay]]; grew 250→300 staff, opened a Uruguay hub), acquired Argentina's **Cobro Digital / Efectivo Digital** (Aug-2026), and launched **unified LatAm virtual accounts** (Feb-2026, [[Monnet Payments unifies LatAm payments with virtual accounts]]). Post-ETpay the group consolidated ~US$5bn processed / ~70M transactions annually, projecting 40–60% growth.

**Fit:** joining TAPP fits logically — Monnet's whole thesis is being the **multi-market collection/dispersal layer** for companies operating across LatAm. A Peru public rail is table-stakes for its home market. **Why it acts this way:** as a commodity take-rate processor facing margin compression from public rails, Monnet needs breadth (9 markets, single integration) and software/orchestration value to defend economics — hence serial acquisitions and virtual accounts. TAPP participation protects the home base rather than opening a new profit pool.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: low-to-moderate.** The genuinely new fact is Monnet being named an early TAPP PSP participant; the *capability* (A2A/payment initiation in Peru) already exists privately (OKTO, Yape↔Plin interoperability live since 2023). **Traction on TAPP specifically: zero** — the rail pilots only in Dec-2026. Monnet's real traction is company-wide ($6bn projection, ~40% growth, 700+ clients, 9 markets) but that predates and is independent of TAPP.

**Why value-add is thin here:** TAPP is a neutral public utility — it does not confer a moat. The value accrues to whoever owns merchant relationships and orchestration above the rail. On a state rail, the initiation margin compresses toward zero; Monnet's durable value is in cross-border pay-ins/payouts and reconciliation software, not in "being connected." Who captures margin: the BCRP (sets rules, [[Peru central bank sets rules for payment acquirers]]), wallets (consumer reach), and software/orchestration layers — thin, commoditized rails for pure processors.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
TAPP controlled pilot: **December 2026**, ~20 entities (Phase 3); operation from 2027. Parallel regulatory tightening: BCRP's acquirer rules (Circular 0026-2026) take effect **Jan 1, 2027** — fund segregation, fee publication, mandated QR/instant interoperability ([[Peru central bank sets rules for payment acquirers]]). Peru is building UPI-style open infrastructure at state level (NPCI/Juspay/Niubiz).

**Why the market goes this way:** BCRP is deliberately dismantling the bank-app monopoly and the Yape/Plin duopoly's closed-garden advantage to force open competition. Counterintuitive second-order effect: a free, mandated public rail **helps enablers like Monnet near-term** (distribution handed to them) but **erodes their long-run pricing power** — the same forces that made Pix compress Brazilian card/processor economics. Risk: Monnet's $6bn is a projection, not booked; pilot slippage (BCRP already moved the full-launch target from mid-2026 to end-2026/2027) is common for central-bank rails. Importance: 2/5 — a credibility/positioning press item on a real but not-yet-live national rail; no live traction, low differentiation in a 20+ entity cohort.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Peru's low-value retail payments are overwhelmingly A2A/wallet: digital wallets reach ~67.6% of low-value payments (per BIS research via paymentexpert.com, Feb 2026), led by Yape (~20M users, ~54% of in-person tx) and Plin (~13M users, ~34%) (per PaymentBrief/Wikipedia). TAPP is the BCRP's UPI-style public payment-initiation rail, built under a June-2024 agreement with India's NPCI (per larepublica.pe, Apr 2026). It extends the 2022 interoperability mandate: Phase 1 (Mar 2023) linked Yape↔Plin by alias; Phase 2 (Sep 2023) added universal QR; the current/"Phase 4" step opens initiation to fintechs and non-banks (per paymentexpert / graduateinstitute.ch). "Why now": TAPP's pilot lands end-2026 (Dec), with 20+ entities in early coordination (per larepublica.pe, elcomercio.pe) — the moment banks' app monopoly on initiation is broken, so a processor plugging in early captures the first flow. Peru e-commerce TAM ~$8bn (2024, per paymentscmi.com) — context only, not TAPP volume.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for a processor: TPV, take rate, number of connected rails/clients, per-tx margin. Monnet is a Lima-based payins/payouts processor across ~9–10 LatAm markets, ~250+ clients, ~$4bn cumulative processed (per Crunchbase/vangwe), guiding to >$6bn volume in 2026 at ~40% annual growth (per descubre.vc, this note). Basis of competition in LatAm cross-border acceptance is distribution + local rails coverage via a single integration — the same pitch as dLocal, EBANX and Kushki, who publish card/bank rates ~2.7–3.99% in Peru (per Rapyd/dLocal). TAPP is not a competitor to Monnet but a new rail Monnet resells; the real peers for the initiation layer are other Peruvian A2A entrants: [[Peru's OKTO launches interoperable QR account-to-account payments]] (Oct 2025, QR A2A on Yape/Plin/Bim), [[Monnet Payments unifies LatAm payments with virtual accounts]] (Feb 2026, local-collection virtual accounts). Recent peer context: [[Peru's central bank unveils TAPP public digital payments platform]] (May 2026). Monnet's position: catching-up incumbent-adjacent — niche strength in multi-market payout breadth, not a wallet or a licensed EMI; moat is integration switching-costs + rail coverage `(analysis)`, weak network effects of its own.

**Comps & multiples.** No round/valuation disclosed in this note → EV/Rev, P/S = no data. Monnet is private; last known corporate event is the ETPay (Chile) acquisition (per fintechile.org) — terms undisclosed `[UNSOURCED]`. Only operating comp available: processed-volume guidance — ~$6bn 2026 vs ~$4bn cumulative-to-date and a separate ~$5bn Chile-only target (per Crunchbase) suggests the "$6bn" is annual run-rate, not cumulative; flag the ambiguity. Listed peers dLocal (NASDAQ: DLO) and EBANX are an order of magnitude larger on TPV and not comparable on scale/geo — distribution not computed, qualitative comparison only. Internal comps above are product/market analogs, not valuation comps.

**Risk flags.**
1. Dependence on someone else's rails — Monnet is riding BCRP/TAPP infrastructure it neither owns nor controls; initiation economics, fees and fraud-liability split are undefined pre-pilot, and the BCRP could later let wallets/banks connect direct, disintermediating processors. (margin captured by the rail owner)
2. "Announced vs live" — Monnet has merely joined a pilot that starts in December 2026; the $6bn volume figure is self-reported guidance, not TAPP-attributable, and the note is silent on what share, if any, routes through TAPP. (PR-stage, not adoption)
3. Concentration/competition — in a Yape(54%)+Plin(34%) duopoly, a non-wallet processor's A2A flow is squeezed between two dominant closed-loops and well-funded cross-border peers (dLocal/EBANX/Kushki); pricing power is thin. (single-market, commoditized acceptance)

**What this changes (idea-lens).** TAPP is a state-led re-platforming of Peruvian payments that turns bank-app initiation from a moat into a commodity — the sector shifts value toward whoever aggregates the most rails cheapest. Falsifiable thesis `(analysis)`: if TAPP launches open initiation on schedule, 2027 should see a wave of fintech/PSP integrations (Monnet, OKTO, Kushki et al.) and compressing wallet pricing. Trigger to watch: the December 2026 pilot participant list and published TAPP fee schedule; thesis breaks if BCRP restricts initiation to licensed banks/EMIs, keeping the duopoly intact.
