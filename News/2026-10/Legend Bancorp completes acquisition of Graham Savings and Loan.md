## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** Legend Bancorp, Inc. — the Bowie, Texas holding company for Legend Bank, N.A. — has completed (closed) its acquisition of Graham Savings and Loan, SSB, a Texas community thrift headquartered in Graham and locally owned for more than 90 years. The closing was announced on/around 20–23 October 2026; as of close Graham Savings and Loan becomes an affiliate of Legend Bancorp, with the full operational merger of Graham into Legend Bank, N.A. and systems conversion planned for late April 2027 (subject to final regulatory sign-off). ([Legend Bancorp has acquired Graham Savings and Loan](https://www.legend.bank/about-us/legend-bancorp-inc-has-acquired-graham-savings-and-loan/); [Herald Democrat — "Match Made in Texas"](https://www.heralddemocrat.com/2026/10/23/news/match-made-in-texas-legend-bank-and-graham-savings-and-loan-reach-major-milestone/))

**[1] Numbers.** Graham Savings and Loan: ~$160M in assets, founded 1934, three full-service locations (Graham, Breckenridge, Possum Kingdom). Legend Bank: >$1.2B in assets, 13 full-service branches plus two loan offices across North Texas. Deal value / purchase price was not disclosed. Shareholder vote (6 Aug 2026): 99.21% of votes cast in favor. ([Legend Bancorp to buy Graham Savings in Texas bank merger — Bank Slate](https://www.thebankslate.com/2026/05/legend-bancorp-to-buy-graham-savings-in-texas-bank-merger/); [Bankers Digest](https://bankersdigest.com/graham-savings-and-loan-to-merge-with-legend-bank/))

**[2] Timeline (dated).**
- 27 May 2026 — definitive merger agreement announced ([Bank Slate](https://www.thebankslate.com/2026/05/legend-bancorp-to-buy-graham-savings-in-texas-bank-merger/)).
- 6 Aug 2026 — Graham Savings and Loan special shareholders' meeting approves merger (99.21% in favor).
- 24 Aug 2026 — Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas approves Legend's application ([Fort Worth Business Press](https://fortworthbusiness.com/banking/legend-bank-receives-federal-reserve-approval-for-acquisition-of-graham-savings-and-loan/)).
- ~20–23 Oct 2026 — acquisition officially CLOSED (this note's event).
- late April 2027 — planned systems conversion / legal merger of Graham into Legend Bank, N.A.

**[3] Why it matters / context.** This is a small, in-market consolidation typical of the fragmented US community-banking sector: a ~$1.2B North Texas bank bolting on a ~$160M local thrift to add density in Graham/Breckenridge/Possum Kingdom (Young/Stephens County area). It is a two-step structure — holding-company acquisition now, operational bank merger ~6 months later — common when the acquirer wants to run the target as a standalone affiliate during conversion. The deal is tiny in absolute terms (combined ~$1.36B) and has no fintech/tech angle beyond the acquirer's promise of "enhanced technology" to Graham customers post-conversion. It sits alongside a steady run of 2026 Texas/US community-bank tie-ups: see [[Plains State Bank to acquire Texas's Austin Colony]] (another small Texas deal), [[Pinnacle Bancorp to acquire Southern Bancshares]], [[TowneBank to acquire blueharbor bank for $154M]], and [[Arbor Bancorp to acquire Huron Valley Bancorp for $52.1M]].

**[4] Open questions.** Undisclosed purchase price (so no price/book or premium read); whether Graham's three branches are retained or rationalized after the April 2027 conversion; staff/leadership retention; any goodwill/intangibles impact on Legend's capital. No public financials for Legend Bancorp (private holding company) to gauge the deal's accretion.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US bank M&A is running at a multi-year high: 53 deals closed in Q1 2026 (largest quarterly count since Q4 2021), and the Kansas City Fed counted 127 completed community-bank mergers in 2025, the most since 2021; 2026 is tracked for 450+ closings (per Richmond Group / visbanking, via web, as of Oct 2026). Texas is the single busiest state — it led all states with 21 deals announced through late 2025 (per The Financial Brand, via web). Structure: the US community-bank/thrift tier is highly fragmented (thousands of sub-$1bn institutions) and consolidating from the bottom up — small single-state thrifts lack the scale to absorb rising compliance, technology and deposit-gathering costs, which is the core "why now" driver. A second accelerant is regulatory: average merger-approval time has reportedly fallen from ~18 months to as little as 3–6 months (per Ankura/web), lowering the execution friction that previously deterred small deals. This specific transaction is a classic bottom-of-the-pyramid tuck-in: Legend Bank, N.A. (holding co Legend Bancorp, Inc.) absorbing Graham Savings and Loan, SSB — a Graham, TX thrift founded in 1934 with ~$160M in assets (per web; note's own text only says "Texas-based"). Fed Dallas + Texas Dept. of Savings and Mortgage Lending approved; shareholders voted 99.21% in favor on 2026-08-06; close ~Oct 1 2026, systems conversion ~late Apr 2027.

**Competitive landscape.** Community-bank M&A runs on: total assets, total deposits (and deposit premium / core-deposit quality), tangible book value (TBV), and branch/geographic overlap. This is a sub-scale in-market deal — both are small Texas depositories — so "competition" is less about national rivals than about the local deposit franchise and branch density around Graham/North Texas. Legend is a privately held community bank; neither party is SEC-registered, so there is no proxy and the economics are undisclosed (de-PR: the PR emphasizes "more ways to bank / enhanced technology," which is the standard scale-and-tech rationale, not a disclosed synergy case). Recent in-base Texas peer moves: Plains State Bank → First National Bank of Lake Jackson (2026-10, target ~$218M assets, price undisclosed); Pinnacle Bancorp → Southern Bancshares/SouthStar Bank (2026-10-08, ~$1.35B target, price undisclosed); Prosperity Bancshares → Stellar Bancorp (Jan 2026, ~$2bn, Houston). Position: Legend is a serial small-market consolidator adding scale incrementally; at ~$160M of target assets this is a modest bolt-on, not transformational `(analysis)`. Moat is the usual community-bank one — sticky local core deposits and switching costs — rather than anything proprietary `(analysis)`.

External sanity range (per Mercer Capital / Dirt Cheap Banks / visbanking, via web, 2026): healthy small-bank deals ~1.2–1.8x TBV historically, with the current soft-market band ~0.9–1.2x TBV for clean banks and ~0.7–0.9x for stressed ones; ~12–18x trailing earnings for quality franchises. With Graham's TBV undisclosed no arithmetic is possible — distribution not computed, qualitative comparison only. No outlier flag can be raised without numbers.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Zero price/terms disclosure** — with no P/TBV, deposit premium or earnings multiple, the deal cannot be benchmarked; the only read is that a private buyer paid an unknown price for a ~90-year-old thrift, so franchise quality (credit book, deposit cost) is unverifiable from the outside.
2. **Integration/conversion execution risk** — systems conversion is pushed to ~late Apr 2027 with the legal merger of the thrift into Legend Bank following; a ~6-month gap between close and conversion is the window where deposit/customer attrition and operational-risk events typically surface for small acquirers.
3. **Sub-scale economics** — at ~$160M assets the target is tiny; the strategic payoff depends on cost-takeout and cross-sell that small single-branch-cluster deals often under-deliver, so accretion may be marginal `(analysis)`.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Nothing sector-moving — this is one more data point confirming the bottom-up consolidation of sub-$1bn Texas thrifts/community banks, where undisclosed private-on-private deals are becoming the norm (Legend–Graham, Plains State–Austin Colony, Pinnacle–SouthStar all terms-dark in 2026). Falsifiable thesis: Texas sub-$500M deal count keeps rising through 2027 as shortened Fed approval timelines persist; the thesis breaks if approval times re-lengthen or rate-driven deposit-cost pressure forces targets to hold out for scarce higher-TBV bids. Watch: Legend's next tuck-in and whether any of these private deals start disclosing TBV multiples.

**vs expectations / prior period.** No analyst consensus or quarterly estimates apply (private thrift acquisition by a privately held bancorp; no public equity). No data on deal multiples. Baseline is the two institutions' standalone call-report balance sheets above.

**Thesis-flags.**
- Scale / fill-in deal, not transformational: Graham adds ~13% to Legend's asset base (~$155mn on ~$1.25bn) — a classic small community-bank tuck-in extending Legend's North/West Texas footprint (Young, Stephens, Palo Pinto counties).
