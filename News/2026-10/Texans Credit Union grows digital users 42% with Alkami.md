 PLANO, Texas , Oct. 13, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Alkami Technology, Inc. (Nasdaq: ALKT ) ("Alkami"), a   digital sales and service platform provider  for financial institutions in the U.S., today announced the continued expansion of its relationship with Texans Credit Union  (Texans), a Richardson, Texas-based credit union serving more than 150,000 members. A new case study  highlights how Texans is leveraging Alkami to drive measurable growth in digital engagement, member satisfaction, deposits, and membership, while improving operational efficiency and building a more flexible foundation for continued innovation.
Since moving from its previous digital banking provider to Alkami's Digital Banking Platform , Texans has built on its initial conversion to advance a broader growth and innovation strategy. The credit union has since adopted MANTL Onboarding & Account Opening , created differentiated experiences with Alkami's software development kit  (SDK), and deployed fintech integrations through Alkami's partner ecosystem . Together, these capabilities give Texans greater control over how it evolves its digital experience, responds to member needs, and brings new capabilities to market.
"Alkami gave us more than a new Digital Banking Platform. It gave us a long-term partner and the infrastructure to create the experiences members need to manage and advance their financial lives," said Mike McWethy, executive vice president at Texans Credit Union. "Our goal isn't simply to have more digital users; it's to make the experience valuable enough that members want to keep engaging with us, and Alkami is central to how we create that engagement. By making it easier to take action, delivering relevant insights and connecting account opening directly into digital banking, we can meet members in the moment, deepen relationships, and help them do more with us. That means we can move faster, scale with the organization, and continue improving the member experience over time, supporting growth, retention, and innovation."
Texans has used Alkami's extensible Platform to tailor its digital experience around its own priorities. Using Alkami's SDK, the credit union built My Texans Hub  to make important relationship information easier for members to find and reduce avoidable support inquiries. Integrated fintech partners have added capabilities across money movement, fraud prevention, mobile deposit, direct deposit switching, and member service. Recently, Texans added MANTL Onboarding & Account Opening to connect application, decisioning, and funding with digital banking and streamline account opening across digital and branch channels.
Since beginning its digital transformation with Alkami, Texans has:
 Increased digital engagement:  Grew active digital banking users by 42% following implementation while achieving a 4.8-star App Store rating.
 Improved member satisfaction:  Increased its net promoter score from 73.6 to 89.4 following the transformation.
 Accelerated online account growth:  Doubled the number of online accounts booked in the first month after going live with MANTL.
 Transformed account opening across channels:  Reduced average account opening time to approximately 5.7 minutes across channels and made the in-branch experience up to five times faster.
 Generated early deposit momentum:  Booked more than 1,200 applications and nearly $11.5 million in deposits during the first six weeks of its in-branch MANTL experience.
"Texans Credit Union demonstrates the compounding value of treating a platform conversion as the foundation for a broader growth strategy," said Benjamin Conant, chief product officer at Alkami. "By bringing together onboarding and account opening, digital banking, an extensible SDK, and an ecosystem of integrated technology partners, Texans has greater flexibility to determine what it needs and how to deliver it. The results show how that flexibility can translate into stronger engagement, greater efficiency, and measurable growth."

# Context-enrichment: Texans Credit Union grows digital users 42% with Alkami
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Alkami (Nasdaq: ALKT) published a **vendor-authored customer case study** (PR distributed 2026-10-13) touting results at **Texans Credit Union** (Richardson, TX; ~150k members; ~$2.6B assets after its 2026-03-01 merger with Las Colinas FCU). This is **marketing collateral, not a new deal, round, or earnings event** — Texans is an existing, previously-live Alkami client that has since layered on MANTL account opening, Alkami's SDK, and partner-ecosystem fintech integrations.

Headline metrics (all **client-reported, un-audited, vendor-selected framing**):
- **+42% active digital banking users** "following implementation" — no absolute base/numerator given, no timeframe (could be multi-year ramp from a low post-conversion base); this is the classic PR trick of anchoring to a flattering base.
- **NPS 73.6 → 89.4** — self-reported, methodology/survey-base undisclosed.
- **4.8-star App Store rating** (point-in-time, no review count).
- **Doubled online accounts booked in the first month** after MANTL go-live (a one-month base — high percentage off a tiny denominator).
- **Account-opening time ~5.7 min across channels**; in-branch "up to 5× faster."
- **>1,200 applications and ~$11.5M deposits in first six weeks** of in-branch MANTL.

**Why structured this way / what it reveals:** Every figure is (a) client-supplied, (b) framed against post-conversion or first-month/six-week bases that maximize the percentage, and (c) silent on absolutes. This is a sell-side reference story timed to reinforce Alkami's **Digital Sales & Service Platform** cross-sell narrative (digital banking + MANTL onboarding + data/marketing) — i.e. land-and-expand proof, not independent evidence. No cost, contract value, or ROI is disclosed.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Alkami sits in the **U.S. community-FI digital banking platform** market (est. ~$6–8B/yr, ~9–11% CAGR) against:
- **Q2 Holdings (QTWO)** — the scale leader: ~457 installed FIs, ~27.8M registered users (Q1-26); Datos named Q2 US retail digital-banking leader 2026. Historically stronger with **banks**; the only realistic choice for banks between the two.
- **Jack Henry (JKHY)** — ~15–20% of community-bank digital via core-banking bundling (Banno); distribution advantage through core lock-in. (cf. [[Hyland joins Jack Henry FinTech Integration Network]])
- **nCino** (lending/onboarding, cloud banking — cf. [[nCino and Parlay power AI-native SBA lending nationwide]]), **Mambu / Tuum / Backbase** (more core/engagement; cf. [[Tuum Should banks build their own core]], [[Backbase launches Conversational Banking for agentic AI]]), **Bankjoy, Lumin, Narmi** (CU-focused challengers; see openbanking tracker alternatives).

Position: Alkami is **#2 by scale** to Q2, historically **CU-weighted**; its differentiation push is the unified onboarding+banking+data platform via the MANTL acquisition. **Why the landscape is this way:** switching digital-banking cores is painful and infrequent, so vendors compete on (i) winning rare conversions and (ii) cross-selling modules into the installed base. Texans is a **(ii) expansion** story — exactly where Alkami needs proof to justify its software multiple against Q2.

Alkami: IPO 2021; FY-pattern of ~20% ARR growth. **Q2 FY2026: revenue $129.8M (+15.9% YoY), ARR $512M (+21%), 313 digital-banking clients (+33 net QoQ, +12% YoY), 23.6M registered users (+13%), RPO $1.7B (~3.4× live ARR), <1% annual ARR churn.** Appointed Cassandra Hudson CFO (cf. [[Alkami appoints Cassandra Hudson as CFO]]). In **Jan 2026 Alkami reportedly tapped an adviser to explore a sale** (cf. [[Alkami taps adviser to explore sale]]) — strategically relevant backdrop.

Platform strategy: acquired **MANTL** (Fin Technologies) for ~$400M EV, **announced Feb 2025** (note: the search snippet's "2026" is the case-study date, not the deal date); launched the unified **Digital Sales & Service Platform** 2026-04-14. Texans fits as a textbook reference account for that platform thesis: convert → adopt MANTL → add SDK/partners → cite engagement lift.

**Why the company acts this way:** As a point-solution digital-banking vendor, Alkami faces a commoditization/ARPU-ceiling pressure; to defend a software multiple it must **expand ARPU per FI** (more modules, more registered-user monetization) and prove the bundle lifts outcomes — hence a steady cadence of vendor case studies (Texans here; Towpath; State Bank of Texas — cf. [[State Bank of Texas launches Alkami-powered digital banking]]; fraud via BioCatch — cf. [[Alkami customers prevent over $263M fraud with BioCatch]]). With a possible sale on the table, polished reference proof also burnishes the asset.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: essentially none.** No new product, client win, or disclosed economics — it is a reference marketing asset for an existing client. The only quasi-new substance is **operational traction evidence for MANTL in-branch** (doubled first-month openings; 1,200 apps / $11.5M deposits in 6 weeks; ~5.7-min opening) — consistent with Alkami's broader claim that **in-branch MANTL became its top growth channel in 2026** (>1M total in-branch apps; ~90k in a single month).

**Why the value-add is real or not (deeper):** The genuinely credible signal is **cross-sell/attach economics** — Texans moving from single-module (digital banking) to multi-module (banking + MANTL + SDK + partners) is exactly the ARPU-expansion that underpins Alkami's multiple; management claims 4×+ growth in FIs buying all three components since the MANTL deal. **Where it breaks:** the headline member/NPS metrics are un-auditable and base-gamed, so they prove little about **durable** value; the real margin question is whether attach revenue compounds faster than core digital-banking commoditizes. Texans is one data point, not a cohort.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect **more Alkami reference case studies** (sales-enablement cadence) and continued MANTL attach as the growth engine. The **open strategic question is the Alkami sale process** (adviser engaged Jan 2026): PE/strategic interest would value exactly this attach-ARR + low-churn (<1%) + $1.7B RPO profile. Street sentiment on ALKT: ~21% ARR growth with a **profitability miss** flagged at Q2-26 (per Investing.com on the Q2 slides) — i.e. growth is intact but margin scrutiny is rising, making bundled-ARPU proof points strategically useful.

**Why the market goes this way / second-order:** Digital-banking platforms win by **owning the FI's engagement + onboarding surface**, then monetizing registered users and modules. Counterintuitive risk: if the pending-sale chatter is real, a PE owner could prioritize margin over the land-and-expand land-grab, slowing the very cross-sell motion these case studies celebrate. For the FI market, consolidation among vendors (Alkami/MANTL; Q2's scale) narrows credible choices for community banks/CUs — reducing negotiating leverage over time.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US digital banking platform (DBP) software for community banks / credit unions — a distinct SaaS layer above the core (FIS, Fiserv, Jack Henry are the "Big Three" cores per Fed Kansas City). Addressable base is fragmented and large but finite: ~4,400 US credit unions and ~4,300 banks/thrifts ≤$55bn assets as of 31 Dec 2025 (per Jack Henry FY2026 10-K, via SEC). No clean third-party TAM figure for the DBP sub-segment surfaced — "no data" on sector dollar size; treat it as a share-of-wallet / seats game, not a greenfield. Drivers / "why now": (1) conversion cycle — FIs are ripping out legacy digital front-ends (Texans itself "moved from its previous provider"), so vendor revenue is won largely by displacement, not net-new institutions; (2) cross-sell of adjacent modules (onboarding/account opening, data & marketing) onto an installed base lifts revenue-per-user without new logos — the structurally important lever given a finite number of FIs.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: ARR, revenue-per-user (RPU / RPUs), registered users, NRR. Key players & basis of competition: Alkami (ALKT), Q2 Holdings (QTWO), nCino (NCNO, more lending/onboarding-weighted), Jack Henry Banno (cloud-native DBP, bundled with JH core — the incumbent-bundle threat), plus Backbase. Competition is on breadth/extensibility (SDK, partner ecosystem) and switching cost, not price. Recent moves: Alkami bought/integrated MANTL onboarding (Texans deployed it); Q2 enhanced account-takeover AI (2026-04, [[Q2 enhances account takeover protection with AI detection]]); MeridianLink taken private by Centerbridge for $2bn (2025-08, [[MeridianLink to be taken private by Centerbridge for $2 billion]]). Protagonist position: Alkami is a credit-union-leaning pure-play, catching up to the larger Q2 by scale; moat = switching costs (core-adjacent, multi-year conversions) + the SDK/partner ecosystem (analysis). Texans metrics (+42% active digital users, NPS 73.6→89.4, account-open ~5.7 min, ~$11.5M deposits / 1,200 apps in 6 wks) are single-client, vendor-published case-study figures — adoption signal, not disclosed cohort economics.

**Comps & multiples.** Alkami Q2 FY2026 (per SEC 8-K / Fool transcript, Aug 2026): revenue $129.8M (+15.9% YoY), ARR $511.7M (+21%), RPU $21.69 (+7%), 23.6M registered users (+13%), 54 bank clients; FY2026 guide $528–531M revenue. EV ~$1.73bn, mkt cap ~$1.46bn (late Sep 2026, per stockanalysis.com).
- **Alkami:** EV/ARR = $1.73bn / $511.7M = **3.4x**; EV/revenue (FY26E mid ~$529.5M) = $1.73bn / $529.5M = **3.3x**.
- **Q2 Holdings:** mkt cap ~$3.32bn (5 Oct 2026, eulerpool), TTM rev ~$846M, total ARR $970.8M → P/S ≈ $3.32bn / $846M = **~3.9x** (market cap, not EV — EV not verified → EV/Rev `[UNSOURCED]`); growth ~14%.
- **nCino:** EV/revenue **3.75x** current (gurufocus), mkt cap ~$2.09bn, TTM rev ~$622M (+8.6%).
Read: three comparables cluster ~3.3–3.9x revenue/ARR — Alkami is **in-line-to-slightly-cheap** despite the fastest growth of the three (+15.9% rev / +21% ARR vs Q2 ~14% / nCino ~9%). Cheapness reflects the 2026 de-rate: ALKT mkt cap fell ~33–48% YoY after its strategic review ended with no sale (Sep 2026). No EBITDA/P-E multiples computed — Alkami is GAAP-loss-making (Q2 net loss $8.9M); use revenue/ARR multiples only. Median of 3 revenue multiples ≈ 3.75x.

**Risk flags.** (1) **Failed-sale overhang** — Alkami ran a strategic review that ended with no deal (Sep 2026) and the stock de-rated ~33–48%; a pure-play that explored a sale and found no buyer at an acceptable price signals limited strategic premium and raises re-attempt / activist risk. (2) **Finite-TAM / displacement dependence** — with only ~4,400 CUs and a shrinking bank count, growth leans on RPU cross-sell and poaching rivals' installed bases; any slowdown in conversion velocity hits ARR directly. (3) **Incumbent-bundle disintermediation** — Jack Henry Banno (and Fiserv/FIS) can bundle DBP with the core they already own, pressuring a core-less pure-play on price and renewals. (4) **Case-study framing** — Texans figures are vendor-selected, single-client, post-conversion; "+42%" has no disclosed denominator/baseline, so it is a marketing proof point, not sector-generalizable economics.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** The Texans case is a cross-sell proof point (MANTL account-opening attached onto a prior DBP conversion) — directionally supports Alkami's "land the platform, then expand RPU" thesis, which is the only durable growth lever in a finite-FI market (analysis). Falsifiable: if ARR growth stays >18% and RPU keeps climbing through FY2026, the in-line-to-cheap multiple re-rates; trigger to watch — next ARR/RPU print and whether the sale process reopens. Thesis breaks if RPU growth stalls or a core incumbent wins a wave of DBP conversions on bundling.
