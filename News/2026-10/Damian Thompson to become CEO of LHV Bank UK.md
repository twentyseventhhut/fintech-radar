# Context-enrichment: Damian Thompson to become CEO of LHV Bank UK
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
LHV Bank Limited — the PRA-authorised UK banking subsidiary of Estonia's AS LHV Group — named **Damian Thompson** as CEO and executive director, **joining 4 January 2027, subject to regulatory (SMF) approval**. This is not a sudden event: it closes a ~9-month leadership gap. Founder-CEO **Erki Kilu** (led the UK build-out since Jan 2021) was recalled to Estonia from 1 April 2026 to chair/run LHV Pank; **Kris Brewster** has run LHV Bank UK on an **interim** basis since March 2026. So Thompson is the first *permanent* post-founder CEO, and the long lead time to Jan 2027 reflects notice-period + SMF vetting, not urgency.
- **Why framed around lending/Starling**: the PR anchors Thompson's "grew loans to >£5bn at Starling" (mortgages + SME; he ran Fleet Mortgages BTL from 2023, before that ~20 yrs NatWest Markets structured finance). The deliberate signal — LHV is hiring a *lending* operator, not a payments/BaaS operator, to run its "next phase of growth," which it defines as **SME lending**. That is the tell for the strategic inflection below.

## [1] Competitors / peers
UK SME/challenger-lending and BaaS peers: **Starling** (Thompson's alma mater, Engine BaaS + £5bn+ loans), **OakNorth** (SME lending flagship; recently hired ex-Seccl Tom Harris as CTO [[OakNorth appoints former Seccl exec Tom Harris as CTO]]), **ClearBank** (UK BaaS/agency banking [[ClearBank founder Andrew Smith is building the home of UK sports finance]]), **Zempler** (just named Dominic Wade CEO — same week), Allica, Griffin, Shawbrook. On the embedded-banking side LHV competes with ClearBank/Modulr for fintech client deposits. Position: LHV is **ahead on BaaS client scale** (200+ fintechs incl. Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Coinbase, TrueLayer — ~10m end-customers) but **catching up on balance-sheet SME lending**, where OakNorth/Allica are the incumbents. Hiring a Starling lending chief is an explicit attempt to close that gap.

## [2] Company history / fit
LHV Pank (Estonia) → UK branch → **PRA banking licence May 2023** (first new UK licence without restrictions in its cohort; $38m raised 2022 to fund it) → business transfer from the Estonian branch completed, then rapid balance-sheet growth. Commercial-loan book: **£289m (end-yr 1) → £700m end-2025 (+136%) → £1bn cumulative commercial output by Sept 2026**; loans & deposits "more than doubled" in 2025. Fit is logical: LHV spent its first UK chapter proving the **BaaS/payments rails** (RT1 direct SEPA Inst access [[LHV Bank joins RT1 to boost instant euro payments]]; SEPA settlement for fintechs like Solidgate [[Solidgate Treasury enables SEPA payments across Europe with LHV]]). **Why act now**: BaaS is a thin-margin, deposit-gathering, commoditising business; the licence and the fintech deposit base are the *input*, SME lending is where the **net-interest margin and software-like growth** are. Importing a proven lending operator is the structural next move once the rails and funding exist.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Novelty of the *appointment itself* is low — a standard senior hire at a mid-size bank, long pre-announced. The real signal (analysis): LHV is **pivoting its UK centre of gravity from payments-BaaS to SME lending**, and backing it with a lending specialist rather than a founder/generalist. Traction backing the pivot is *real* (hard numbers: £1bn commercial output, £700m 2025 book, 200+ live BaaS clients), not announcement-only. Value-add of Thompson specifically: he ran the exact playbook (mortgages + SME to £5bn) at Starling, so execution risk on the stated strategy is lower than a cold hire. **Who captures the margin (analysis)**: on BaaS, LHV is a low-margin rail-and-deposit utility behind Wise/Airwallex; on direct SME lending it captures the full NIM — so the strategy is about *moving up the margin stack* using fintech deposits as cheap funding. That is the substantive "why it matters," beyond the people-move headline.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect LHV UK to lean into SME/commercial lending and likely selective mortgage/specialist lending (Thompson's Fleet/BTL background) through 2027. Risks: (a) 9-month CEO vacuum + a Jan-2027 start means ~a year of interim stewardship before the strategy owner is in place — execution drift risk; (b) credit cycle — scaling an SME book into a soft UK economy is where challenger lenders (cf. past wind-downs) get burned; (c) **concentration/conflict** — LHV's BaaS clients (Wise, Airwallex) are themselves lenders/competitors, so pushing deeper into direct lending risks channel tension with the deposit base that funds it. Second-order (hypothesis): the more LHV competes with its fintech clients on lending, the more those clients may diversify banking partners — the pivot that raises margin could erode the deposit moat that makes it cheap.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** UK Banking-as-a-Service / embedded-banking rails — the infrastructure layer letting fintechs access Faster Payments/BACS/CHAPS, safeguarding and multi-currency accounts without owning a charter. UK BaaS market ~$2.48bn in 2024, projected ~$4.60bn by 2032 at ~8% CAGR; broader UK embedded finance grew ~14.9% CAGR 2021–2025 (per Verified/Persistence Market Research, as of 2025 — secondary analyst figures, not independently audited → treat as indicative). Structure: a thin tier of FCA/PRA-licensed providers — clearing/e-money names ClearBank, Modulr, Griffin — sits above a long tail of fintech resellers; barriers are regulatory (banking licence / EMI authorisation) and capital, which is precisely LHV Bank UK's edge — it holds a full PRA banking licence (granted May 2023), not just an EMI permission. "Why now": the post-shakeout cycle — Railsr (ex-Railsbank) recapitalised in 2023 and merged with Equals Money in April 2025; the market is consolidating around balance-sheet-backed, profitable rails. A licensed bank hiring a lending-heavy CEO signals a pivot from pure payments rails toward on-balance-sheet SME lending as the growth engine.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: payment volumes/transaction count, fee-income share, client count, customer balances/deposits, loan book. Key players & basis of competition: ClearBank (clearing-bank model, 279 clients, 17m accounts, £17.8bn balances, normalised revenue £121.6m +34% YoY, UK pretax profit £12.2m, 3rd straight profitable year in 2025) competes on clearing access + scale; Modulr on EMI-based embedded payments/payroll; Griffin as an API-first newer entrant. LHV Bank UK's position: catching up on rails scale but differentiated — >200 fintech clients (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, TrueLayer) on its Banking Services division; 2025 payment volumes +17% YoY to 88m transactions; deposits £1.1bn (+99%), lending £0.7bn (+136%), PAT £5m; management guides ~50% deposit/lending growth for 2026 (per LHV, Feb 2026). Moat (analysis): dual-licence group (LHV Pank EE + LHV Bank UK) + full banking licence lets it both carry deposits and lend — a balance sheet ClearBank historically lacked for lending. The Thompson hire (grew Starling's loan book to >£5bn; ex-Fleet Mortgages CEO; joins 4 Jan 2027, subject to regulatory approval) explicitly targets SME lending expansion.

**Comps & multiples.** Parent **AS LHV Group** (Nasdaq Tallinn: LHV1T) market cap ~€1.06bn / ~$1.2bn (as of Sep–Oct 2026). No clean standalone valuation for the UK subsidiary — it is consolidated → UK-only multiple = no data. ClearBank is private (no listed multiple; 2025 group normalised revenue £121.6m, UK pretax profit £12.2m) → EV/Revenue = [UNSOURCED]. Modulr/Griffin private → no data. Arithmetic not computable with ≥3 verifiable comparable figures → distribution not computed; qualitative comparison only. Scale read: ClearBank revenue (£121.6m) and balances (£17.8bn) dwarf LHV Bank UK deposits (£1.1bn) — LHV UK is a sub-scale but faster-growing (deposits +99%, loans +136% vs ClearBank revenue +34%) challenger on the rails side. Internal comps: [[ClearBank and Mambu discuss multi-rail modular core banking]], [[LHV Bank joins RT1 to boost instant euro payments]], [[Salt Edge, Tuum and LHV Bank partner on open banking]], [[Modulr begins winding-up proceedings against Paymob]].

**Risk flags.**
1. Execution/strategy-shift risk: Thompson is a lending specialist (loan book builder at Starling), yet LHV Bank UK's scale/brand sits in fintech payment rails (>200 BaaS clients). Second-order: a lending-led pivot could divert balance sheet and management attention from the Banking Services franchise that Wise/Airwallex/TrueLayer depend on — risking the rails moat while the lending book is still small (£0.7bn).
2. Regulatory-approval + timing risk: appointment is subject to PRA/FCA approval and effective only 4 Jan 2027 — a ~15-month leadership-transition overhang during a stated ~50% growth-push year (2026).
3. Concentration / disintermediation on the rails side: BaaS revenue depends on a handful of large fintech clients (Wise, Airwallex) who can multi-home or in-source rails as they obtain their own licences — margin captured upstream. Thin reported profitability (PAT only £5m in 2025) leaves little cushion if lending credit costs rise in an SME downturn.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Signals LHV positioning its UK arm as a balance-sheet SME lender, not just fintech rails — a deliberate step up the value chain where ClearBank (clearing/payments) is weaker. Falsifiable thesis: if the 2027 strategy is lending-led, expect loan-book growth to outpace payment-volume growth and new SME credit products within ~12 months of Thompson joining. Watch: 2026 full-year results (loan vs deposit mix), PRA consumer-credit permission usage (overdrafts), and whether any flagship BaaS client publicly leaves. Thesis breaks if Thompson's mandate is actually to defend/scale the Banking Services rails rather than lending.
