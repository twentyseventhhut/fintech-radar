 ВТБ запустил переводы на мобильные кошельки в шести африканских странах — Кении, Нигерии, Гане, Кот ‑ д’Ивуаре, Бенине и Камеруне. В ряде этих стран мобильные кошельки занимают до 90% рынка платежей.

 Через приложение «ВТБ Онлайн» клиенты банка могут отправлять деньги по номеру телефона на конкретные кошельки: в Кении — M ‑ Pesa и Artel; в Нигерии — Palm Pay и OPay; в Гане — Vodafone, MTN, Airtel и Tigo; в Кот ‑ д’Ивуаре и Бенине — Orange, MTN и Moov; в Камеруне — Orange и MTN. Лимиты зависят от страны и варьируются от 5 млн до 1,35 млн рублей.

 Перевод делается в разделе «Переводы по миру» или «Платежи» — «В другую страну»: клиент выбирает страну, способ «По номеру телефона», кошелек, вводит данные получателя и сумму и подтверждает операцию. Комиссия — 0,5% суммы, минимум 30 рублей. Лимиты — до 300 тыс. рублей на один перевод, до 1 млн рублей в сутки и в месяц для большинства клиентов; для клиентов сегментов «Привилегия» и private banking месячный лимит — до 5 млн рублей.

 По словам Павла Тулубьева, руководителя департамента транзакционных продуктов ВТБ, сервис дает доступ к быстрорастущему рынку мобильных финансов, где кошельки служат основным инструментом повседневных расчетов, и делает переводы быстрыми и простыми — «как сообщение в мессенджере».

 Банк отмечает, что продолжает расширять набор каналов переводов: сейчас клиенты ВТБ могут отправлять деньги в более чем 150 стран по номеру карты, телефона, счета и с выдачей наличных, а также оплачивать по QR ‑ коду, что особенно востребовано у туристов.

# Context-enrichment: VTB launches phone-number transfers to mobile wallets in six African countries
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

> **NOTE ON TAGGING (open item):** The note is mis-tagged `company/mts`. The actual operator per the source (bosfera.ru, Банковское обозрение) is **VTB** (ВТБ), Russia's #2 state-owned bank. The "Мой МТС" line at the bottom of the scraped text is unrelated boilerplate from an adjacent article. Retag to `company/vtb`.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On ~8 Oct 2026 VTB enabled, inside its retail app **VTB Online**, **outbound transfers FROM Russia** by recipient phone number to mobile wallets in six African markets: Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire, Benin, Cameroon. Named wallets: Kenya — M-Pesa, Airtel/Artel; Nigeria — PalmPay, OPay; Ghana — Vodafone, MTN, Airtel, Tigo; Côte d'Ivoire & Benin — Orange, MTN, Moov; Cameroon — Orange, MTN. **Fee: 0.5% of amount, min 30 RUB.** Per-transfer limit ≤300k RUB; daily/monthly ≤1m RUB for most (≤5m RUB for Privilege/private-banking). Quote from Pavel Tulubyev (head of transaction products) frames it as "fast as a messenger message."

**+ Why structured this way / what it reveals:** This is a **retail remittance payout product, NOT VTB building banking presence in Africa.** The direction is Russia→Africa (a Russian-app sender funding a local wallet), and the counterintuitive fact that re-centers the whole item: VTB is **SWIFT-disconnected since Mar 2022** and in **Dec 2024 surrendered the licence of its only African subsidiary, Banco VTB Africa (Angola, est. 2007)** (analysis-confirmed via web). So VTB has essentially **no correspondent-banking footprint in these six countries** — the payout leg must run over a third-party mobile-money aggregator / local acquirers, not VTB's own rails. The PR is silent on this plumbing (the single biggest "who's silent" gap). The 0.5%/min-30-RUB price is deliberately aggressive vs the ~8.5% Sub-Saharan Africa remittance average — a land-grab price, feasible only if VTB subsidizes FX or the corridor is strategically (not commercially) motivated.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Wallet-payout-to-Africa is a crowded lane, but almost no incumbent serves a **Russia→Africa** corridor:
- **TerraPay** — the dominant wallet-payout network; powers [[botim money expands remittances via TerraPay partnership]] (UAE→Africa, instant wallet payouts) and Bolivia corridors. The likely archetype for the payout leg.
- **Western Union + Sasai** — app-based Africa remittances [[Western Union and Sasai Fintech launch Africa money transfer app]] (Mar 2026).
- **PAPSS / Pesalink** — Africa-internal local-currency cross-border rails [[Pesalink and PAPSS unlock cross-border payments in local currencies in Kenya]]; different direction (intra-Africa) but same wallet endpoints.
- **Careem Pay** [[Careem Pay expands remittances to Egypt, Lebanon, Philippines]] — Gulf→Africa/Asia, the comparable "super-app remittance" play from a sanctioned-adjacent-free operator.
- Aggregators that physically could power VTB's payout: pawaPay, TerraPay, Nsano, Sendwave-type networks (web).

**+ Why the landscape is this way / second order:** Everyone above routes USD/EUR corridors via SWIFT-connected banks or card schemes. VTB **cannot** — so its real competitive moat is not price or UX but **being one of the only ways to legally move Russian retail money to African wallets at all**. The competitors don't compete for this corridor; the "competitor" is really informal hawala/crypto. Second-order: if the payout aggregator is itself exposed to OFAC/EU secondary sanctions, the whole service is fragile regardless of consumer demand.

## [2] Company history / fit
VTB has been **systematically rebuilding its cross-border retail stack since 2022** as a sanctions workaround: card-to-card transfers to 20→100+ countries (Visa/Mastercard/UnionPay, pre-cutoff legacy), then **phone-number transfers to "friendly" CIS states** — Armenia, Azerbaijan, Belarus, China, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan (web). Retail transfers in "friendly-country" currencies rose ~6x in 2024. Africa is the logical next extension of that same phone-number product.

**+ Why VTB acts this way:** Structural pressure — SWIFT exclusion + loss of Western correspondent banking destroyed VTB's traditional cross-border franchise, and it closed Banco VTB Africa rather than operate it crippled. The retail phone-number-to-wallet product is the **replacement strategy**: bypass the bank-to-bank layer entirely and plug into each country's consumer wallet rail via a local intermediary. It also aligns with Kremlin Russia-Africa policy (3rd Russia-Africa Summit, 2026, Addis Ababa) and the broader de-dollarization push (ruble/yuan bilateral corridors, the A7/A7A5 stablecoin network now itself EU-sanctioned as of Aug 2026).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty is in the corridor, not the technology.** Phone-number-to-wallet payout is years-old tech (M-Pesa since 2007; TerraPay globally). What is new: a **Russian** bank offering it to **African** wallets at retail scale and a headline 0.5% price. **Traction = zero disclosed**: no volumes, no user counts, no live-vs-pilot proof beyond "launched." Announcement, not adoption.

**+ Why the value-add is real-or-not / who captures the margin:** At 0.5% minus 30 RUB, the **wallet-payout aggregator and local acquirers almost certainly capture most/all of the economics**, and the FX spread (hidden, undisclosed) is where VTB actually makes money — the stated 0.5% is a loss-leader headline. So VTB risks being the **"front end that doesn't own the rail"**: it owns the Russian customer relationship but not the African payout infrastructure, exactly the disintermediation risk where value concentrates with whoever controls the wallet endpoint (MTN, Orange, M-Pesa) and the aggregator. The central question is therefore NOT "is 0.5% cheap" but **"can VTB keep this corridor open (settlement + sanctions) and does real Russia→Africa retail demand exist to fill it?"** — and on both, the PR is silent.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Expect VTB to extend the same phone-number product to more African/Asian "friendly" markets and lean on the Russia-Africa Summit narrative. Mobile wallets are 60–90% of payments in these markets, so the addressable rail is real and growing (West-Africa mobile-money moving toward trade finance / cross-border integration through 2030, per BISI).

**+ Why the market goes this way / counterintuitive second-order:** The structural driver is sanctions, not consumer demand — this is **geopolitically subsidized plumbing**. The counterintuitive effect: the louder the de-dollarization/sanctions-workaround framing, the **more exposed the payout aggregator becomes to secondary sanctions** (cf. EU's Aug-2026 designation of the A7 Africa / A7A5 network). So success in PR terms raises the odds the corridor is severed — capital/political concentration makes the service fragile, not durable. Real risk = settlement counterparty risk, opaque FX, thin genuine retail demand (Russian diaspora in these six countries is small), and that it is a political signal more than a profit line.

**NB — note mis-tag.** Front-matter tags this `company/mts` and attributes the launch to a Sber channel, but the primary source (bosfera.ru) and all external coverage confirm the operator is **VTB** (Russia's #2, state-controlled bank). This layer is grounded on VTB; MTS/Sber appear only as unrelated page noise scraped into the source text.

**Sector & drivers.** Cross-border remittance into African mobile-money wallets. Africa's mobile-money base crossed ~1bn registered accounts by end-2024, with ~81bn transactions worth ~$1tn processed in 2024 across 178 services (+15% YoY) — per GSMA data via AllAfrica, as of Apr-2025. Wallets are the dominant rail, not a niche: VTB's own release says they hold "up to 90%" of payment market share in some of the six target countries. Receive-side players named in the note are the incumbents — M-Pesa (Safaricom; >66m customers, 8 countries), MTN MoMo (~69.5m MAU, >200m registered wallets, ~$321bn processed in 2024), plus OPay/PalmPay (Nigeria), Orange and Moov (West/Central Africa). "Why now": the send-side driver is specifically Russian — after the Feb-2022 SWIFT cut-off, Russia–Africa trade (~$27bn; ~84% settled in rubles in 2025, per kotana.com.ru citing 2025 data) needs non-SWIFT retail rails. VTB is wiring a sanctioned send-side into Africa's deepest consumer-money network. Structure: send-side fragmented and improvised (rubles, yuan, crypto, bilateral corridors); receive-side consolidated around 3–4 wallet schemes — value and the fraud/AML liability sit with whoever holds the correspondent/aggregator relationship, which VTB does not disclose.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: transfer fee / take rate, FX spread, per-transaction and daily/monthly limits, corridor count, settlement speed. VTB's terms: 0.5% fee (min 30 RUB); per-transfer cap 300k RUB, 1m RUB/day-month for most retail, up to 5m RUB/month for Privilege/private; country limits "5m to 1.35m RUB" per the release (figure reported inconsistently — the lower bound reads like a typo in source). VTB claims >150 destination countries overall by card/phone/account/cash-out and QR. Basis of competition: price + reach + rail access under sanctions. The honest comparison is NOT to Western incumbents — Western Union (+ Sasai app, Mar-2026) and Thunes (Vodacom Tanzania cross-border M-Pesa, May-2026; pay-to-stablecoin-wallets over SWIFT, Mar-2026) run the same pay-to-wallet pattern but on SWIFT/compliant rails VTB cannot touch. VTB's real peer set is the Russia→Africa sanctions-evasion corridor: the A7 ruble-backed crypto network (Promsvyazbank-linked), which the US sanctioned in Oct-2026. VTB's position: a *regulated, in-app, retail-grade* alternative to those crypto corridors — ahead of A7 on UX/trust for Russian retail users, but structurally dependent on correspondent banks willing to touch a sanctioned entity. Moat is a distribution/regulatory artefact (state-bank balance sheet + the only compliant-looking Russian retail rail into these wallets), not a durable network effect.

**Comps & multiples.** No deal/valuation/round attached to this launch → no trading multiple to compute (no data). VTB equity multiples are not meaningfully comparable: it trades on MOEX only, is Western-sanctioned, and is a diversified bank, not a payments pure-play — EV/Rev, P/E vs fintech peers = not comparable. IR context (VTB FY2025 IFRS, per the group's own reporting, cited via TASS as the group presentation was in RUS-only PDF): net profit RUB 502.1bn (~$6.5bn), −8.9% YoY off the 2024 record; Q4-2025 RUB 121.3bn (−31.2% YoY); assets RUB 36.9tn (~$482bn, +2.3%); FY2026 target ≥RUB 600bn (source: [VTB Group FY2025 IFRS results presentation, 25-Feb-2026](https://www.vtb.ru/media-files/vtb.ru/sitepages/ir/statements/results/Presentation_IFRS_1225_RUS.pdf)). Takeaway: this retail-remittance launch is immaterial to VTB's P&L — it is a strategic / de-dollarisation corridor play, not a revenue line. Internal corpus comps (pay-to-wallet / Africa remittance):
- [[Thunes and Vodacom Tanzania launch cross-border M-Pesa payments]] — same pay-to-wallet-by-identifier pattern, compliant rails
- [[Western Union and Sasai Fintech launch Africa money transfer app]] — incumbent into African wallets
- [[Thunes launches Pay-to-Stablecoin-Wallets over SWIFT]] — SWIFT-based last-mile, contrast to VTB's off-SWIFT constraint

**Risk flags.**
1. **Correspondent / de-risking risk (primary).** A Western-sanctioned bank moving retail money into M-Pesa/MTN/Orange depends on intermediaries those schemes' compliance teams may refuse; a single receive-side partner pulling access can sever a whole corridor. VTB does not disclose who clears the last mile — the economics and OFAC exposure are exactly what the PR is silent on.
2. **Regulatory shift on the receive side.** New West African (BCEAO/UEMOA) mobile-money rules take effect 2-Nov-2026 reshaping deposit-holding and pricing in a ~$267bn West-African market (per Launch Base Africa, Oct-2026) — directly hits Côte d'Ivoire, Benin, and partly Ghana corridors just weeks after launch; terms and limits may not hold.
3. **FX / ruble concentration.** Pricing and limits are ruble-denominated; corridor value tracks RUB stability and the ruble-settlement share of Russia–Africa trade — a devaluation or a tightening of secondary sanctions on RUB cross-border flows degrades the product quickly.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a state-bank move to put a *compliant-looking retail face* on Russia's de-dollarisation push into Africa, competing less with Western Union/Thunes than with ruble-crypto schemes like A7 — i.e. keeping Russia→Africa consumer flows inside a regulated bank rather than crypto. Falsifiable thesis: if the corridor is real volume (not just optics), expect VTB to add settlement/FX partners and more African wallets within 2–3 quarters. What breaks it: a receive-side scheme publicly cutting VTB off, or OFAC/receive-side regulators sanctioning the aggregator — watch the 2-Nov-2026 West African rule change and any US follow-on to the Oct-2026 A7 designation.

> [!note] Earnings backdrop — operator is **VTB** (not MTS; note is mis-tagged `company/mts`). Source bosfera.ru press release is VTB's own. The news is a **product launch** (phone-number transfers to mobile wallets in 6 African countries, 0.5% fee), NOT an earnings print — framed here against VTB Group's latest reported results.

**Verdict (headline read).** MISS (vs prior year) · VTB Group FY2025 IFRS net profit **RUB 502.1bn, −8.9% YoY** (vs RUB 551.4bn in 2024), ROE **18.3%** (down from 22.9%) · earnings pressured by net interest income collapse under high rates, cushioned by fee income · management guides FY2026 net profit to **RUB 600–650bn**. The Africa launch is **immaterial to group P&L** but is strategically aligned with the one line that is actually growing — cross-border fee income.

**Key figures (FY2025 IFRS, with growth).**
- Net profit **RUB 502.1bn, −8.9% YoY** (2024: RUB 551.4bn). Q4 2025 alone RUB 121.3bn, −31.2% YoY.
- **Net interest income RUB 433.6bn, −11% YoY** — the core problem: net interest margin squeezed to **1.4%** for the year (though Q4 NIM recovered to 2.0% vs 0.8% a year earlier, signalling a turn).
- **Net fee & commission income RUB 307.1bn, +14.2% YoY** — explicitly driven by FX transactions and servicing cross-border payments (Q1 2025 fee income +40.6% YoY on the same drivers). This is the segment the Africa remittance corridor feeds.
- ROE **18.3%** (vs 22.9% prior year); cost-to-income **47.3%**.
- Total assets RUB 36.88trn; loan book RUB 24.5trn (+3.1%) — corporate RUB 17.2trn (+7.9%), retail RUB 7.3trn (−6.8%, deleveraging under rate pressure). NPL ratio 3.5%, reserve coverage 149.1%.
- Capital thin: total capital ratio 9.8%, Tier 1 7.7%, CET1 6.3% (as of 01-Jan-2026) — low headroom, which constrains dividend payout.

**By segment / driver.** Profit mix inverted in 2025: the rate-sensitive interest book went backwards (NII −11%, retail lending shrinking −6.8%) while **fee & commission income carried the quarter (+14.2%), specifically from FX and cross-border transaction servicing**. The new Africa mobile-wallet corridor (Kenya M-Pesa/Airtel, Nigeria OPay/PalmPay, Ghana MTN/Vodafone, Côte d'Ivoire/Benin/Cameroon Orange/MTN/Moov; 0.5% fee) is a direct extension of that growing fee engine and of VTB's stated >150-country transfer network. In scale terms it is a rounding error against RUB 307.1bn of group fee income — but it sits on the correct side of the P&L given NIM compression.

**vs expectations / prior period.** FY2025 net profit RUB 502.1bn **modestly beat the bank's own ~RUB 500bn self-set target** (RBC/Interfax) while **missing prior-year** on an absolute basis (−8.9%). No clean public sell-side consensus for a sanctioned name — beat/miss framed qualitatively vs prior year + vs management target [consensus UNSOURCED]. Momentum: the fee line's +14.2% is the standout and is accelerating (Q1 2025 fees were +40.6% YoY), whereas NII's −11% is the drag; the Q4 NIM rebound (2.0% vs 0.8%) is the forward catalyst management is leaning on.

**Guidance / forward.** FY2026 net profit guided to **RUB 600–650bn** (+20–30% over FY2025), ROE target ~20%+ (management cites 20–23% in places), driven primarily by recovering net interest margin as rates ease. Plausibility: the Q4 2025 NIM turn (0.8%→2.0%) supports the thesis, but early FY2026 prints are soft — 5M 2026 net profit ~RUB 190bn, −19% YoY — so the 20–30% full-year ramp is back-loaded and unproven. VTB declared a FY2025 dividend, but thin capital (CET1 6.3%) caps realistic payout near 25–30% (est. ~RUB 9.5–11.4/share, analyst estimate [UNSOURCED]).

**Thesis-flags.**
1. **Earnings quality shift toward fees.** Fact: fee income +14.2% is the only growing revenue line while NII fell −11%. Why: high-rate environment crushed the interest book. Why it matters: VTB is pivoting to transaction/FX/cross-border fees for growth — the Africa corridor is a textbook instance. Second-order: these flows (remittances, FX) are also more sanctions-resilient than domestic lending, strengthening the strategic rationale beyond the (tiny) direct revenue.
2. **Africa launch = strategy signal, not a needle-mover.** Fact: 0.5% fee on retail transfers to 6 African markets. Why it matters: zero measurable FY2026 P&L impact at group scale (RUB 502bn net profit, RUB 37trn assets); value is optionality on cross-border corridors and payment rails outside Western systems. De-PR: this is "announced/launched," with no disclosed volumes or take-rate economics — treat as a channel expansion, not a growth driver.
3. **NIM recovery is the real 2026 catalyst — and it's unproven.** Fact: Q4 NIM 2.0% vs 0.8% YoY underpins the RUB 600–650bn guide, but 5M 2026 profit is −19% YoY. Why it matters: the entire FY2026 beat case rests on margin normalisation, not on fee lines like remittances. Second-order: if rate cuts stall, guidance is at risk and the Africa-style fee initiatives are far too small to compensate.
4. **Thin capital caps the upside.** CET1 6.3% constrains both dividend payout and balance-sheet growth — another reason capital-light, fee-generating corridors (cross-border transfers) are strategically attractive to management.
