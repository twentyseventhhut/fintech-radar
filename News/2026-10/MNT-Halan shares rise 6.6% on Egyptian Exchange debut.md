Egypt fintech firm MNT-Halan rises 6.6% on debut after biggest IPO since 2021
Listing values Egyptian business at about $770 million
Egyptian lending and payments company MNT-Halan closed up about 6.6% on its first day of trading on Tuesday, after the country's biggest initial public offering in at least five years.
The shares were offered at 24.50 Egyptian pounds each and closed their first session at about 26.12 pounds, valuing the listed Egyptian business at nearly 42 billion Egyptian pounds ($770 million). The listing is Egypt's largest in dollar terms since 2021, according to EFG Hermes, which advised on the offering.
MNT-Halan is often described as Egypt's first fintech "unicorn," a status based on the valuation of its parent company, which also has operations outside Egypt.
The sale of 320 million shares covered 20% of the Egyptian business.
The listing comes despite an increasingly volatile regional environment since the US-Israeli war with Iran unleashed a series of widening conflicts and sent global energy prices surging.
At the weekend, Egyptian President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi asked parliament to authorise the armed forces to defend Saudi Arabia from attacks by Yemen's Houthis if Riyadh asks for support. A vote is expected next week.
Interest in MNT-Halan was a testament to "how solid the underlying foundations" of the market and company are, CEO Mounir Nakhla told Reuters after a ceremonial bell-ringing at the session's opening.
"It is a country that has a 115 million population, growing very fast, more and more tech savvy and in need of financial services through technology – and we are right here solving this problem," he said.
The Egyptian business recorded 19.8 billion pounds in revenue in 2025, up from 13.2 billion pounds the year before, according to financial statements posted by the company.
Since its inception, MNT-Halan has disbursed about 178 billion Egyptian pounds of loans, which it calculated as being worth about $6.1 billion based on average historic exchange rates, the company said in the statement announcing its IPO earlier this month.
It has about 1.9 million active customers and had a gross loan book of about 46.7 billion Egyptian pounds at the end of June, it added. MNT-Halan has also bought a bank in Pakistan and a micro-leasing company in Turkey.
(Reporting by Alexander Dziadosz; Editing by Xevi Fontdegloria)

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
HALN.CA began trading on the EGX on **Tue 20-Oct-2026** (listed 14-Sep). First session: **open EGP 24.50 (= offer) → high ~EGP 28.91 (+18%) → close ~EGP 26.12 (+6.6%)**, valuing the listed Egypt unit at **~EGP 42bn (~$770m)** vs the **~$748m clearing mark**. Egypt's biggest IPO by dollar proceeds since 2021 (per EFG Hermes, advisor).
- **The headline understates the tell: a +18% pop that gave back ~two-thirds to close +6.6%.** The marketed story is "the debut rose 6.6%" (Zawya) — but the *shape* (open at offer → +18% → fade to +6.6%) is the real signal. **Why it matters → what it reveals:** a first-day high that fails to hold on a thin ~15% public float is the classic pattern of **allocation-driven retail flipping its tiny slice** into opening strength. The ~$22m of fresh value created over the EGP 24.5 offer (close vs offer) is modest; the +6.6% is a **soft, not emphatic, aftermarket** — consistent with the book-close note's warning that 43.6x retail was a final-day spike on a 48m-share slice, not deep conviction.
- **100% secondary sale, now confirmed by the selling vehicle: MNT Investments B.V.** sold 320m existing shares (20% of the Egypt unit's capital) — **no capital reaches the company**; this is a **PE/vintage-holder exit / liquidity event**, not growth funding. The "+6.6% debut success" accrues to sellers' mark and allocated buyers, not the operating business.
- **Note the modest discrepancy across wires:** Bloomberg headlined "+18% in debut" (intraday high); Zawya/Finimize/Briefs report "+6.6%" (close). Both are the SAME session — intraday high vs close. ## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Egypt's non-bank lending runs on a devaluation backdrop: after the Mar-2024 float the EGP lost ~40%+ vs USD, so EGP-reported revenue/loan-book growth partly reflects inflation, not real volume. Egypt's regulated microfinance portfolio reached ~EGP 108bn by Q1-2026 (microfinance companies alone ~EGP 50.2bn / 1.9m clients at Mar-2026), up only ~3% YoY in nominal EGP — i.e. real contraction after ~25–30% inflation (per MSMEF chairperson, via Daily News Egypt, 18-Jul-2026). App-originated micro-lending was ~$260m of annual disbursement in 2025, forecast ~$308m in 2026 (+18%) (per Ken Research, 2026). Structure: fragmented, capital- and regulation-gated — the FRA has *frozen new consumer-finance licenses* to curb lending growth (per Egyptian Streets, 18-Oct-2026), which protects incumbent scale players like MNT-Halan. Why now: the EGX "tech IPO freeze" is thawing — MNT-Halan is Egypt's biggest IPO in dollar terms since 2021, a re-opening of a shut EM exit window despite regional war-risk (Sisi/Houthi escalation noted in the source).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: gross loan book, disbursements, active customers, net-interest/take economics, and market share by loan-book value. MNT-Halan: ~1.9m active customers, gross loan book ~EGP 46.7bn (end-Jun-2026), ~EGP 178bn ($6.1bn at historic FX) cumulative disbursed, and self-reported ~24.3% of Egypt's non-bank microfinance market by loan book + ~14% of non-bank consumer finance by 2025 disbursements (per IPO prospectus, via billionaires.africa/thndr, Oct-2026). Basis of competition: distribution (1,200+ points across 25 governorates) + super-app product breadth (micro/consumer/SME lending, payments, prepaid cards with Visa — [[MNT-Halan partners Visa to expand prepaid cards in Egypt]]) rather than price. Position: clear domestic scale leader / category-defining "first Egyptian fintech unicorn"; moat = distribution network + regulatory scale under a license freeze (network effects + a widening regulatory barrier) (analysis). Recent peer context: Egypt's FRA just approved six more non-bank fintechs ([[Egypt's FRA approves six fintechs for non-banking financial services]], Oct-2026) and Fawry/Erada entered instant microfinance ([[Fawry partners Erada Finance for instant microfinance in Egypt]], Mar-2026) — new entrants nibbling, none at Halan's book.

**Comps & multiples.** MNT-Halan listed Egypt unit priced at close (EGP 26.12, ~EGP 42bn / ~$770m): **~20x 2025 net income** and **~1.9x adjusted revenue** (per Bloomberg/Reuters, Oct-2026). Arithmetic check: 2025 net income EGP 1.98bn → EGP 42bn / 1.98bn = **21.2x P/E**; 2025 revenue EGP 19.8bn → 42 / 19.8 = **2.1x P/S** (on *total* revenue; the 1.9x is on *adjusted* revenue). Internal comps:
- [[Optasia targets ~$375M Johannesburg Stock Exchange IPO]] — Dubai-based AI micro-lender, listed JSE Nov-2025 at R19 → ~R23.5bn (~$1.25bn) market cap; 2025 revenue $265m (+76%), net income ~$40m (normalised $57.8m) → P/E ~26x, P/S ~$1.25bn/$265m = **~4.7x** (per engineeringnews/Optasia IR, 2025–26). Larger, faster (76% vs Halan's ~44% rev CAGR), multi-country, priced richer.
- [[MNT-Halan hits $1.4bn valuation in round led by Al Ahly Capital]] — the Jun-2026 *group* private round at $1.4bn (post-money, round valuation not market cap) vs the ~$770m listed Egypt-only carve-out: implies rest-of-group (Türkiye/UAE/Pakistan) ≈ $0.63bn, or the private mark was rich.
- [[MNT-Halan IPO oversubscribed 43.6x in $147M Egypt offering]] / [[MNT-Halan plans $150M IPO on Egyptian Exchange]] — same deal, terms unchanged.
Read: **in-line to slightly cheap vs Optasia** on P/S (1.9–2.1x vs ~4.7x) — justified by slower growth, single-country EGP/FX exposure, and a 100% secondary (no primary capital in). P/E ~20x is not cheap for a thin-float EM lender.

**Risk flags.**
1. **FX/macro translation risk** — book, revenue and the ~$770m valuation are EGP-denominated; a renewed EGP leg-down (the IMF-program float regime is fragile) compresses the USD value and can mask real-volume stagnation behind inflation-fattened EGP growth.
2. **Credit quality in a frozen-license, high-rate regime** — rapid ~41% loan-book CAGR into Egyptian inflation/high policy rates raises NPL risk; the prospectus-led disclosures are silent/thin on charge-off and NPL trends (de-PR: growth touted, asset quality not).
3. **Thin float + related-party/anchor-led book** — only 15% retail / 85% private placement, cornerstones (CIB, Redwheel) pre-took >1/3; 100% secondary sale means insiders sold down and no new growth capital entered — liquidity and governance/alignment risk.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a **re-rating + EM-exit re-opening** signal: the first sizeable Egyptian fintech float since 2021 clearing 16x oversubscribed sets a listed EGX benchmark for the FRA-licensed peers behind it. Falsifiable thesis: if the stock holds its +6.6% debut and the Egypt unit sustains the ~$770m mark through one post-listing results cycle, expect Fawry-adjacent and the six newly-FRA-approved fintechs to queue for EGX listings within 12–18 months. What breaks it: an EGP devaluation step or a visible NPL spike that drags HALN below the EGP 24.5 offer price, re-freezing the window (analysis).

- Net margin ~9.7% (EGP 1.98bn / EGP 20.5bn) — thin and rate-sensitive (see driver).
- Gross loan book EGP 46.7bn (~$891–947m depending on EGP spot) at end-June 2026; ~1.9m active customers. Cumulative loans disbursed since inception ~EGP 178bn (~$6.1bn at avg historic FX).
- Reported NPLs below 2.5% of portfolio (management figure).
- Market position (end-2025): 24.3% of Egypt's non-bank microfinance market by loan book; 14% of non-bank consumer-finance disbursements — largest non-bank micro-lender in Egypt.

**By segment / driver.** Profit swing is a NET-INTEREST-MARGIN story, not a volume story. FY2024 net income fell ~40% because funding costs (securitized bonds, bank lines) repriced UP faster than yields on the already-booked loan portfolio during Egypt's 2024 EGP devaluation + high-rate cycle; FY2025 recovered as new loans were written at higher yields and rates stabilized. Volume (loan book, revenue) grew throughout — the pressure point is spread, not demand. The $6.1bn cumulative-disbursement headline uses "average historic exchange rates," inflating the USD figure vs the live EGP book (~$0.9bn).

**Guidance / forward.** None given (IPO event). (analysis) Management (CEO Mounir Nakhla) framed EPS as "on its way to recovering to 2023 levels" — an implicit, unquantified guide, not a number [UNSOURCED]. Forward read hinges on two things management did NOT commit to: (1) the EGP/rate path (spread recovery is macro-dependent), and (2) asset quality as the book scales into underbanked borrowers.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. Profit quality is rate-cycle-dependent, not durable yet. Fact: net income EGP 1.98bn FY2025 still below EGP 2.27bn FY2023; EPS EGP 1.26 vs 1.40. Why: 2024 showed funding costs reprice faster than loan yields. Why it matters: the "recovery" is a reversion off a devaluation shock, not structural margin expansion. Second-order: another EGP leg-down or rate spike re-compresses NIM — the ~9.7% net margin leaves little buffer.
2. FX/EGP translation risk cuts both ways and is being used selectively. Fact: USD figures swing on which EGP rate is used ($6.1bn cumulative "historic avg" vs ~$0.9bn live book; $745m vs $770m valuation). Why it matters: foreign portfolio investors bought in EGP — returns are hostage to the pound. Second-order: EGX re-entry of foreign capital (a stated IPO rationale) reverses fast if the EGP wobbles, pressuring the aftermarket.
3. Credit quality is the single biggest unknown as the book scales. Fact: NPLs <2.5% (management), but the model lends to underbanked/mass-market Egyptians. Why it matters: reported low NPLs in a fast-growing book can mask seasoning — losses lag disbursement. Second-order: FRA (regulator) is tightening non-bank requirements; a tougher underwriting regime or a credit-cycle turn hits the thin net margin directly.
4. Valuation gap signals a group-vs-arm discount. Fact: Egyptian arm ~$745–770m implied whole-arm value vs $1.4bn June-2026 parent mark. Why it matters: the listed entity excludes Pakistan/Turkey and sold only 20%; the market is pricing the Egyptian cash-generating core, not the expansion optionality — day-1 +6.6% close (off an intraday +18%) shows demand but also profit-taking.
