Thunes Expands Real-Time Payouts to Venezuela, Strengthening Cross-Border Financial Access in Latin America
 Members of Thunes’ Direct Global Network gain faster, transparent, and direct payment capabilities to bank accounts, mobile wallets, and cash pickup locations across Venezuela. 

 SINGAPORE/CARACAS; October 20, 2026 — Thunes , the Smart Superhighway to move money around the world, today announced the expansion of its Direct Global Network into Venezuela, one of Latin America’s critical financial corridors. 
With this expansion, Members of Thunes’ Direct Global Network, including banks, Money Transfer Operators (MTOs), payment service providers and gig economy platforms, can process compliant, real-time transfers directly to millions of bank accounts via Credito Inmediato Interbancario, the local platform enabled for instant interbanking transfers. 
Venezuela represents a critical market for cross-border money movement, where diaspora remittances and international transfers serve as essential economic lifelines for approximately 35% of families, local businesses, and freelancers participating in the global digital economy . In 2025, remittances to Venezuela were estimated at around US$6 billion (7% of GDP), with about 50% originating in the US. Thunes’ entry into the market targets key needs in the region:
 Fueled Gig Economy Payouts: Empowering platforms to pay Venezuelan freelancers instantly in real time – critical given 89% live paycheck-to-paycheck or struggle with basic expenses .
 Expanded Financial Inclusion: Connecting unbanked and underbanked populations directly to local digital wallets and cash pickup points.
 Vital Remittance Lifelines: Delivering fast, predictable cross-border support to millions of families reliant on financial support. 
“Expanding our real-time payout capabilities to Venezuela marks an important step as we open instant payment reach to more countries across the world. Latin America remains a pivotal growth region for Thunes, and Venezuela’s market demands fast, transparent, and accessible cross-border financial infrastructure. By opening this corridor, we empower our Network Members to deliver life-changing financial connectivity to millions of individuals and businesses.”
 Alex Young , Chief Network Officer at Thunes 

“Venezuela represents one of Latin America’s most vital financial corridors, where cross-border transfers provide an essential lifeline for millions of families and an expanding community of digital freelancers. By extending our direct real-time payout infrastructure into Venezuela, we are removing friction points and delays, benefiting our Network Members and the end users they serve, exactly when and where it is needed most.”
 Ximena Azcuy , Head of Network – Americas at Thunes 

Thunes’ Venezuelan corridor operates on its proprietary SmartX Treasury System, for optimised liquidity management, and its Fortress Compliance Platform, supported by more than 50 global regulatory licenses, guaranteeing that every payment adheres to strict international compliance, anti-money laundering (AML), and security standards.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-10-20 (SINGAPORE/CARACAS), Thunes — the Singapore-based cross-border payments network ("Smart Superhighway") — announced the expansion of its proprietary Direct Global Network into Venezuela. Network Members (banks, MTOs, PSPs, gig-economy platforms) can now send compliant, real-time transfers to millions of Venezuelan bank accounts via **Credito Inmediato Interbancario**, the local instant-interbank rail. Thunes frames the corridor around three uses: gig-economy payouts to freelancers, financial inclusion for the unbanked/underbanked (wallets + cash pickup), and diaspora remittance lifelines. The corridor runs on Thunes' in-house SmartX Treasury System and Fortress Compliance Platform (AML/sanctions screening, 50+ regulatory licenses). ([thunes.com PR](https://www.thunes.com/news/thunes-expands-real-time-payouts-to-venezuela-strengthening-cross-border-financial-access-in-latin-america); [TNGlobal 2026-10-22](https://technode.global/2026/10/22/singapores-thunes-expands-real-time-payouts-to-venezuela/))

**[1] Why it matters / stakes.** Remittances to Venezuela reached ~US$6B in 2025 (~6–7% of GDP), ~50% originating in the US, and are an economic lifeline for ~35% of families. Crucially, ~85% of inflows historically moved through *informal/unlicensed* channels (Zelle-to-bolivar workarounds via Zinli/Reserve/Airtm, USDT, exchange houses) because sanctions and FX friction choked licensed rails. A licensed, compliant real-time bank corridor is a bid to formalize that flow — margin and volume upside for Thunes' Members, and for Thunes' take on each payout. ([The Dialogue 2026-08](https://thedialogue.org/blogs/2026/08/when-financial-infrastructure-becomes-humanitarian-infrastructure-how-remittances-can-speed-venezuelas-humanitarian-and-economic-recovery); [eldiario.com on informal channels](https://eldiario.com/2025/09/23/alternativas-que-han-surgido-enviar-remesas-venezuela/))

**[2] The unstated "why now" — sanctions thaw.** The PR tactfully omits the real enabler: the 2026 political reset. Following Maduro's removal from power (early 2026) and OFAC **General License 57** (issued ~2026-04-14), US sanctions on Banco Central de Venezuela and three state banks (Banco de Venezuela, Banco del Tesoro, Banco Digital de los Trabajadores) were lifted after ~7 years, authorizing dollar transfers, correspondent banking and remittances. That is what makes a *compliant* Thunes corridor newly bankable. GL 16B had long carved out non-commercial personal remittances, but the 2026 easing opens the licensed, at-scale door. ([Morgan Lewis 2026-01](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/01/compliance-landscape-in-venezuela-following-nicolas-maduros-removal-from-power); [Rio Times on GL 57](https://www.riotimesonline.com/us-venezuela-central-bank-sanctions-oil-revenue-2026/); [CRS sanctions overview](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF10715))

**[3] Where it sits in Thunes' LATAM / network story.** This is the latest in a steady 2025–26 LATAM and corridor build-out: [[Thunes enables instant payments via Colombia's Bre-B]] (2025-10, adjacent instant-rail integration), [[Thunes enables real-time payments to the US from worldwide network]] and [[Thunes opens New York office in US expansion after $150M Series D]] (2026-06, the inbound-US leg — relevant since ~50% of Venezuela remittances originate in the US), and [[Thunes and Coinbase partner on next-generation global payments]] (2026-10, same day, crypto/stablecoin rail). Thunes closed a Series D (~$150M headline; some sources cite up to $352M / ~$1.4B valuation, co-led by Apis Partners & Vitruvian) at ~$150M ARR run-rate; the network reaches 140 countries, 90 currencies, 220+ payment methods. ([IBS Intelligence Series D](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi-news/thunes-raises-150m-series-d-to-expand-u-s-footprint/); [Wikipedia: Thunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunes))

**[4] Competitive landscape.** Incumbent licensed rails into Venezuela are thin: Western Union (via local agent Zoom), RIA, MoneyGram, Retorna — mostly cash/agent payout. The fast-growing alternative is stablecoin/wallet rails serving LATAM diaspora: [[MAJORITY launches Latin America stablecoin transfers with Privy]], plus Zepz/Sendwave, Airtm, Zinli. Thunes' own stablecoin moves (Coinbase, Pay-to-Stablecoin-Wallets) mean it is hedging both fiat-last-mile and stablecoin flows. B2B infra rivals for the Member relationship: Nium, Airwallex, dLocal (strong in LATAM), plus SWIFT. Thunes' edge is long-tail emerging-market payout coverage; its risk is that stablecoins route around the licensed-bank corridor entirely. ([Owler competitors](https://www.owler.com/company/thunes/competitors); [frame.xyz Thunes alternatives](https://frame.xyz/insights/thunes-alternatives/); [Western Union/Zoom Venezuela](https://www.remesas.com.ve/western-union-venezuela))

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is this a *live* corridor or an announcement of intent? The PR says Members "can process" transfers — is Credito Inmediato Interbancario integration actually in production as of 2026-10-20, with named launch Members, or is it a "now available" framing without volume?
2. The PR never mentions sanctions. If the corridor depends on OFAC GL 57 / the post-Maduro thaw, how durable is it to a political reversal in Venezuela or a US policy snap-back? What is the key-man/regime risk?
3. Which US sending leg does this use? Thunes unlocked inbound-US payments in 2026-06, but ~50% of remittances *originate* in the US — does Thunes have the US-side licensing (state MTLs / MSB) to capture the origination, or only the Venezuela last mile?
4. "Millions of bank accounts via Credito Inmediato Interbancario" — is this a real, sanctioned domestic instant rail (like Colombia's Bre-B or Brazil's Pix), who operates it, and does it settle in bolivars at the official BCV rate or a parallel rate? FX conversion is where remittance economics live or die.
5. The $6B / 85%-informal figures: Thunes benefits from citing a large informal pool it claims to formalize. How much of that $6B is realistically addressable by a licensed bank-account rail vs. people who specifically want cash or USD-denominated balances?
6. Compliance claim: "Fortress Compliance Platform, 50+ licenses." Does Thunes hold a Venezuelan-specific authorization, or is it relying on local bank partners' licenses? Where does ultimate AML/sanctions liability sit if a screened-but-bad actor slips through in a high-risk jurisdiction?
7. Bolivar volatility and capital controls: Venezuela has a history of hyperinflation, FX controls and multiple exchange rates. How does SmartX Treasury hold/hedge bolivar liquidity without trapped balances? What is the settlement latency to actual usable funds for the recipient?
8. Same-day dual announcement (Venezuela + Coinbase) — is Venezuela a genuine strategic milestone or news-cycle filler bundled around the bigger Coinbase partnership? Weigh standalone importance.
9. Competitive moat: Western Union/Zoom, RIA, MoneyGram already pay out in Venezuela, and stablecoin/Zelle workarounds dominate diaspora flows. What does a Thunes bank corridor offer end-users that Zinli/Airtm/USDT don't (speed? cost? trust?)? Is this B2B-only (serving Members) with no consumer-visible differentiation?
10. Revenue materiality: Thunes is at ~$150M ARR across 140 countries. Even a successful Venezuela corridor is a single mid-size emerging market — is this a rounding error on revenue, or does it unlock a strategically disproportionate diaspora-US flow?
11. Reputational/banking-relationship risk: correspondent banks are historically skittish about Venezuela. Could taking this corridor jeopardize Thunes' banking partners or the Coinbase/Mastercard relationships?
12. Verification gap: beyond the Thunes PR and wire-service re-prints (ManilaTimes/PRNewswire/TNGlobal/Banking4.0), is there any *independent* confirmation (a Venezuelan bank, BCV, or a launch customer) that the corridor is operating?

**Importance: 3/5** — Genuinely notable: Thunes is a top-tier emerging-market payout network, and a licensed real-time corridor into a $6B, ~85%-informal remittance market is a meaningful first-mover move made possible by a rare sanctions thaw (the real, unstated story). But it is a single-country network extension, likely B2B-plumbing with no disclosed launch volumes or customers, bundled same-day with the larger Coinbase announcement, and exposed to sharp Venezuela political/FX/compliance risk. Watch-worthy signal of formalizing LATAM remittances, not a standalone market-mover.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Cross-border payout networks / LatAm remittance corridors. Global remittances ~$212bn and digital remittances ~$278bn in 2026 (per Mordor Intelligence / Fortune Business Insights secondary estimates, via market-report pages, as of 2026 — vendor TAMs, treat as directional, not audited). LatAm-Caribbean hit a record ~$161bn in 2024 with ~50% already digital (per Inter-American Dialogue, as of Apr 2025). The Venezuela corridor itself: ~US$6bn in 2025 (~7% of GDP), ~50% US-origin, a lifeline for ~35% of families (per Thunes PR / Inter-American Dialogue, 2025-26). Structure: fragmented at the consumer edge (MTOs, wallets, informal hawala-style channels) but consolidating around a few wholesale "rails/network" players (Thunes, dLocal, Wise, Nium) who sell direct bank/wallet reach to banks and MTOs. Entry barriers are regulatory (licenses — Thunes cites 50+ global licenses) and network/liquidity (last-mile integrations, FX/treasury). Why now: two drivers converge — (1) OFAC eased Venezuela sanctions through 2026 (General Licenses 52–58; GL 57, 2026-04-14, specifically authorizes collection/forwarding/processing of funds/remittances involving certain Venezuelan banks), opening a previously hard-to-bank corridor; (2) a formalization shift — since Dec 2025 >25% of US→Venezuela flows moved to licensed channels, but ~85% of funds still travel informally (per Inter-American Dialogue, 2025-26), i.e. a large pool to capture.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV/payout volume, take rate, corridor/country reach, same-day/real-time share, net revenue retention. Key players & basis of competition: wholesale payout networks compete on reach + speed + compliance, not price alone — dLocal (listed, LatAm/EM-focused), Wise, Nium, plus incumbents Western Union and MoneyGram going digital, and card/FX players (Corpay, Euronet). Recent moves (dated): dLocal + Western Union to power LatAm digital payments (2025-09-09); [[dLocal and Western Union expand remittance payouts into Cameroon]] (2026-10); Western Union selected Noah for a stablecoin network (2026-10); Thunes' own LatAm build-out — [[Thunes enables instant payments via Colombia's Bre-B]] (2025-10), [[Thunes and Banco Cathay of Costa Rica launch pay-to-wallet payments via SWIFT]] (2026-02). Protagonist position: Thunes is a credible wholesale-network peer, catching up to dLocal in LatAm but ahead on global breadth (140+ countries, 90+ currencies per PR). Moat = network effects + switching costs of direct last-mile integrations (here, Credito Inmediato Interbancario for instant interbank payout) + its Fortress Compliance platform `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Best public comp = dLocal (DLO), same wholesale-payout/EM-corridor model. dLocal market cap ~$4.37bn (2026-09-11); H1 2026 revenue $735.5m (+55% YoY), Q2 TPV $17.7bn (+92% YoY), NRR 153% (per dLocal Q2 2026 release, 2026-08-13). Annualizing H1 → ~$1.47bn TTM-proxy revenue. P/S ≈ $4.37bn / $1.47bn ≈ **3.0x** (using market cap; dLocal is broadly net-cash so EV≈mkt cap is a reasonable but unverified proxy — treat as P/S, EV/Rev clean = [UNSOURCED]). At ~55% revenue growth, ~3.0x is in-line-to-cheap for a profitable hyper-grower (growth-multiple check: multiple well below the growth rate). Thunes itself: last round Series D $150m (2025-04-28, led by Apis/Vitruvian), reportedly first crossing a ~$1bn valuation, on a ~$150m revenue run-rate and positive EBITDA (per Fintech Futures / PYMNTS, 2025) → round-valuation P/S ≈ $1.0bn / $150m ≈ **6.7x** (private round valuation, NOT market cap; richer than dLocal's public P/S — typical private-vs-public spread, not an alarm). Internal comps: [[Thunes opens New York office in US expansion after $150M Series D]], [[dLocal and Western Union expand remittance payouts into Cameroon]]. Distribution not computed (only 2 comparable figures) — qualitative comparison.

**Risk flags.** (1) Sanctions/compliance regime risk — the corridor exists only because OFAC loosened Venezuela sanctions via revocable General Licenses (52–58); these are policy-contingent and can be narrowed/revoked, which would strand the integration and the direct bank relationships (second-order: counterparty/AML exposure if a partner bank is later designated). (2) Last-mile dependence — payout relies on Credito Inmediato Interbancario, a single local interbank rail; concentration on one domestic system means operational/political single-point-of-failure risk in a volatile jurisdiction. (3) "Announced vs live/adopted" + silent economics — PR gives no take rate, no committed Member volume, and no FX-spread disclosure in a hyperinflation/multi-rate-bolivar market; monetization and FX risk on the Venezuela leg are unquantified [UNSOURCED].

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Incremental, not a re-rating: a new high-friction corridor added to an existing network — it signals that sanctions easing is reopening EM corridors to compliant wholesale rails, and the real contest is who formalizes the ~85% of still-informal Venezuela flows `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis: if formal-channel share keeps climbing (was >25% by Dec 2025), network players like Thunes/dLocal capture the shift; trigger to watch — OFAC license continuity and any Thunes/dLocal disclosure of Venezuela corridor volume. What breaks it: a sanctions snap-back or a designated partner bank.
