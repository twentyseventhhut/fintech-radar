Groww adds over 1 lakh active clients in September, Sahi clocks 11% growth
 
Groww maintained its lead in India’s stock broking market, adding over 1 lakh active clients in September 2026, while Sahi recorded the fastest growth among the top 20 brokers at 11% month-on-month.
 
 Follow Us
 Stock broking platform Groww maintained its lead in India's stock broking market in September 2026, adding over 1 lakh active clients during the month. Meanwhile, discount broking platform Sahi continued to record the fastest growth among the top 20 stock brokers, with an 11% month-on-month increase in its active client base. 
 According to data from the National Stock Exchange (NSE), the total active client base increased marginally by 0.35% to 4.61 crore in September from 4.60 crore in August, an addition of nearly 1.6 lakh clients. 
 
 Groww retained its dominant position with 1.34 crore active clients in September, an addition of 1,00,961 clients compared to August. Its market share stood at 29.16%, the highest among stock brokers in India. 
 Zerodha remained the second-largest stock broker with 67.91 lakh active clients, despite a marginal decline of 5,313 clients during the month. Its market share stood at 14.73%. 
 Angel One followed closely in third position with 67.55 lakh active clients, an addition of 36,956 clients in September. The company's market share stood at 14.65%, marginally behind Zerodha. 
 ICICI Securities retained fourth position with 21.60 lakh active clients, an increase of 4,410 clients, while Upstox remained fifth with 18.54 lakh clients after losing 10,343 clients during the month. 
 Among other major brokers, Kotak Securities had 13.92 lakh active clients, followed by HDFC Securities with 13.54 lakh. 
 Dhan recorded the third-highest net addition among the top 10 NSE brokers during September, adding 13,624 active clients to reach 11.2 lakh active clients. Meanwhile SBI Securities saw its client base decline by 22,525 to 10.21 lakh in September. 
 Paytm Money recorded one of the steepest declines among the top 20 brokers, losing 27,950 active clients, a 3.53% month-on-month drop, to reach 7.63 lakh. In contrast, INDmoney added 5,070 clients to reach 7.19 lakh in September. 
 Sahi emerged as the fastest-growing broker among the top 20 for another consecutive month, with an 11.1% increase in its active client base. The platform added 28,077 clients to reach 2.81 lakh, while its market share stood at 0.61%. 
 Choice also recorded strong growth of 2.88%, with an addition of 6,781 active clients. Meanwhile, PhonePe's Share.Market added 1,457 demat accounts in September. 
 The September data highlights the continued dominance of large discount brokers, with Groww accounting for over 63% of the industry's net client additions. The top five brokers collectively controlled more than 67% of the active client base, while smaller platforms such as Sahi continued to post faster percentage growth despite their relatively limited market share.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Monthly NSE broker-registration data drop (via entrackr, sourced from NSE active-client disclosures). September 2026 actuals:
- **Groww**: +100,961 active clients MoM → **1.34 crore (~13.4M)** total; share **29.16%** (up from ~29.04% in Aug). #1 broker.
- **Zerodha**: 67.91 lakh clients, −5,313 MoM, share 14.73% (#2).
- **Angel One**: 67.55 lakh, +36,956, share 14.65% (#3 — now within ~0.36 lakh of Zerodha).
- Industry: total active base +0.35% to **4.61 crore (~46.1M)**; net adds only ~1.6 lakh for the whole market.
- Groww took **>63% of the industry's net client additions**; top-5 control **>67%** of the active base.
- Smaller names: Sahi +28,077 (+11.1%, fastest in top-20, 2.81 lakh, 0.61% share); SBI Securities −22,525; Paytm Money −27,950 (−3.53%); INDmoney +5,070; Dhan +13,624.

→ **Why this framing**: the "100K adds / 29% share" headline flatters because the *entire market* barely grew (+1.6 lakh net). Groww's "+100K" is ~63% of ALL net adds — i.e. the market is near-stagnant and Groww is winning share in a zero-sum pool, not riding a rising tide. The denominator (46M active) is well below the Jan-2025 peak of 49.67M (see [[Groww leads stock broking with 27% share, 12.4M clients]]), so India's retail-broking active base is still contracting/flat YoY while Groww grinds up share.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Zerodha** (bootstrapped, profitable, derivatives-heavy): now *losing* active clients MoM and its share (14.73%) is being caught by Angel One (14.65%). Zerodha's refusal to do aggressive paid acquisition is visible here. See [[Zerodha launches microsavings feature on Coin platform]].
- **Angel One** (listed, performance-marketing machine): +37K, closing on #2.
- **Upstox**: −10,343, #5, fading (cf. [[Upstox, an Indian stock broker, reported ₹1,208cr ($132M) total income with 21.5%]]).
- **Paytm Money / PhonePe Share.Market / SBI Securities**: shrinking or negligible — the "super-app distribution" thesis for broking is underperforming vs the pure-play Groww/Angel model.
→ **Second-order**: the race has collapsed into Groww (mass-retail onboarding) vs Angel One (paid acquisition) at the top, with Zerodha deliberately ceding the active-count league table while (presumably) defending revenue-per-client. Active-client count ≠ revenue — Zerodha's F&O-heavy book likely still out-earns per user, so the "29% share" is a *distribution* win, not confirmed a *revenue* win.

## [2] Company history / fit
Consistent trajectory of share grind: ~27% / 12.4M (Jul-2025, [[Groww leads stock broking with 27% share, 12.4M clients]]) → 27.66% / 12.48M (Jan-2026, [[Groww adds 350,000 demat accounts in January, share at 27.7%]]) → **29.16% / 13.4M (Sep-2026)**. So ~+1.5pp share and ~+1M clients over 14 months — steady, not explosive. Post-IPO (Nov-2025, [[Groww IPO raises INR 2,984 crore, oversubscribed 17.6x]]) the company has kept executing: Q1 FY27 PAT +94% YoY to ₹735cr, revenue +66% to ₹1,501cr, EBITDA doubled (external). 
→ **Why**: Groww's model is cheap, mass onboarding + widening product surface (bonds, commodities [[Groww adds zinc, copper, and aluminium commodities]], algo [[Groww launches Groww Cloud algo-trading platform]], AI assistant [[Groww launches GR 1 AI assistant and retail bond access]], Fisdom wealth [[Groww completes $150M acquisition of wealthtech platform Fisdom]]). The share gain is the top-of-funnel; the real monetization bet is cross-sell into those adjacencies.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: essentially none.** This is a routine monthly data print in a recurring series the corpus already tracks. The only genuinely new facts are the September numbers (a new reporting month) and the symbolic crossing of **29%**. Traction is real but incremental (+0.12pp MoM). 
→ **Where the margin sits (analysis)**: active-client leadership is a vanity-adjacent metric. Groww's durable value comes from (a) derivatives/F&O activity (regulatory-pressured — SEBI's F&O curbs are the known overhang, cf. A share headline doesn't tell you whether those 100K adds are high-ARPU traders or dormant demat openings — the data literally cannot distinguish.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Groww will keep topping the monthly league table; the open question is Zerodha vs Angel One for #2. Market sentiment post-IPO is positive (stock ₹192.6 on 2026-10-12, up from ₹112 Nov-2025 low; Q1 FY27 beat, analysts flagging up to ~27% upside — external, unverified target). 
→ **Counterintuitive second-order**: a flat/shrinking total active base (46M vs 49.7M peak) means Groww's share gains are *cannibalizing rivals in a stagnant pool*. If Indian retail participation re-accelerates, laggards recover adds; if it stays flat, Groww's share can rise while absolute industry economics soften — a share win inside a soft market, not proof of sector tailwind.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** India retail broking runs on NSE *active client* count (clients who traded ≥1x in the last 12 months), not demat accounts. Total NSE active base reached ~4.61 crore (46.1M) in Sep-2026, up just 0.35% MoM / +673k over Jul–Sep (per NSE data via entrackr/whalesbook, 2026-10-08). After 2024's F&O-curb-driven slump, net adds have slowed to a trickle (~1.6 lakh in Sep), so the industry is no longer a rising-tide story — it is a **share-redistribution** game: Groww alone took ~63% of September's net adds and ~48% of the quarter's (per entrackr/CNBC-TV18, 2026-10). Structure: top-5 control >67% of active clients — a consolidated oligopoly of discount brokers (Groww, Zerodha, Angel One) that displaced bank-brokers (ICICI Sec #4, Kotak, HDFC Sec). Entry barriers are distribution/brand + a zero-brokerage-on-delivery price floor, not capital. "Why now": with user growth flat, the swing factor is net migration from shrinking incumbents (Zerodha −5.3k, Upstox −10.3k, Paytm Money −28k, SBI Sec −22.5k in Sep) toward the UI-led leader.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: NSE active clients + market share (headline), and downstream revenue mix (cash delivery vs F&O vs MTF/float). Players & basis of competition: Groww 1.34cr / **29.16%** (price + mobile-first UX + cross-sell into MF/lending/wealth); Zerodha 67.91L / 14.73% (declining, deliberately low-marketing); Angel One 67.55L / 14.65% (closing on Zerodha, +37k in Sep). Recent moves: Groww launched algo/terminal products (2025-08, 2025-11 [[Groww launches Groww Cloud algo-trading platform]]) and GR 1 AI assistant (2026-03 [[Groww launches GR 1 AI assistant and retail bond access]]); Zerodha countered on product (Kite Terminal Mode, Coin FD/insurance, 2026-02→04). Position: Groww is the clear **leader and the only large broker still compounding share** — from 27.66% in Jan ([[Groww adds 350,000 demat accounts in January, share at 27.7%]]) to 29.16% in Sep, +1.5pp YTD. Moat `(analysis)`: distribution/brand + switching costs (portfolio/MF holdings parked on-platform) + emerging cross-sell flywheel; weak network effects (broking isn't inherently networked).

**Comps & multiples.** Two listed comparables, same geo/model, very different scale:
- **Groww (NSE:GROWW, Billionbrains Garage Ventures):** ~₹192.6/share, mcap ~₹1,20,698cr (~$13.6bn) as of 2026-10-12. Q1 FY27 rev ₹1,548.67cr, PAT ₹735cr → annualized run-rate ≈ ₹6,195cr rev / ₹2,940cr PAT → **P/S ≈ 1,20,698/6,195 = 19.5x; P/E ≈ 1,20,698/2,940 = 41x** (sources quote 49.5–64x on trailing/other bases). Net margin ~47%.
- **Angel One (NSE:ANGELONE):** mcap ~₹26,990cr (~$2.8–3.0bn), FY-rev ~₹4,660cr, **P/E ~24–26x**, EV/Rev ~3.7x, net margin ~22% (per multiples.vc Aug-2026, smart-investing).
- Internal comps: Groww's own history — [[Groww posts first post-listing profit in Q3 FY26]] (₹1,216cr rev / ₹547cr PAT, mcap ₹97,595cr Jan-2026), [[Groww triples FY25 profit, raises at $7bn valuation]], [[Groww debuts as India's largest fintech IPO of 2025]]; private peers [[Stockbroking startup Dhan reports Rs408cr profit, 2.3x growth in FY25]] (₹877cr rev, ₹408cr PAT FY25) and [[Upstox, an Indian stock broker, reported ₹1,208cr ($132M) total income with 21.5%]] (flat rev, ₹215cr PAT FY25 — losing share).
- **Flag: Groww looks rich vs Angel One** — ~1.6x Angel's P/E and ~5.3x its P/S despite a *smaller* revenue base (₹6.2k vs ₹4.7k cr run-rate). The premium is defensible on growth + margin (47% vs 22% net, share still rising) but not on a 4–5x mcap gap with only ~1.3x the revenue; this is a growth/quality premium priced near the top of the range.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory / revenue-mix concentration (primary).** Groww trimmed derivatives to 53% of Q3 FY26 revenue ([[Groww trims derivatives to 53% of Q3 FY26 revenue]]) — a majority of revenue still rides on F&O, the exact segment SEBI keeps curbing. Any further F&O tightening hits the biggest broker hardest; YoY profit already fell 28% in Q3 FY26 on a tough comp.
2. **Valuation vs economics.** At ~41–49x P/E and ~19.5x P/S versus Angel One's ~24–26x / ~3.7x, Groww prices in continued share gains and cross-sell execution; flat industry user growth means mis-execution re-rates the premium away fast.
3. **Share gains off a flat pie = diminishing returns.** With net industry adds ~1.6 lakh/month and Groww already at 29%, the marginal client increasingly comes from stealing shrinking incumbents, not greenfield — a mathematically capped runway on broking alone, forcing reliance on unproven wealth/lending cross-sell (Fisdom, OBPP bonds).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` The story has shifted from "India broking TAM expansion" to "Groww consolidating a flat market" — a consolidation/share-leader thesis, not a growth-of-the-pie one. Falsifiable thesis: Groww holds >29% and converts broking dominance into higher-margin wealth/lending ARPU within 2–3 quarters, justifying the premium. **Triggers to watch:** monthly NSE active-client prints (does share keep climbing or plateau near 29–30%?), F&O-mix trend each quarter, and any new SEBI derivatives rule. **What breaks it:** a share plateau + F&O squeeze compressing both the multiple and the growth narrative at once.
