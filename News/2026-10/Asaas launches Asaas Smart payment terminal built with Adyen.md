# Asaas launches Asaas Smart payment terminal built with Adyen

Fintech brasileña Asaas lanza Asaas Smart, su nueva terminal de pagos para pymes junto a Adyen
 Asaas , Fintech de Brasil enfocada en pequeñas y medianas empresas, entra al punto de venta físico con Asaas Smart, una máquina de cobro (datáfono) creada con Adyen. Diego Contezini, CEO y cofundador de Asaas, dijo a Pipeline que el dispositivo se ofrecerá a la base de 300 mil clientes pyme. Para la nueva vertical, la fintech proyecta R$ 237 millones en TPV (volumen total de pagos) hasta finales de 2026.
Para el comerciante, es una opción de hardware que complementa Asaas Tap, recurso que convierte el smartphone del comerciante en un terminal de cobro y que, según Contezini, tiene baja adopción en lo que él llama el “Brasil con S”.
 Por qué Asaas decidió tener su propio datáfono 
La primera idea fue comprar. Según el ejecutivo, la Fintech buscó una adquirente, pero ninguna de las opciones encajaba en el ecosistema que armó. La alternativa fue construir la operación desde cero, y el resultado es el primer dispositivo físico en la historia de la compañía, fundada en 2010 por Diego y su hermano Piero.
El argumento de venta está en el software. La empresa quiere que el pago en el mostrador converse con las herramientas de gestión del pequeño negocio, hoy dispersas entre distintos proveedores y sistemas que no se hablan entre sí. 
“Es un mercado con un montón de competidores, pero ellos son buenos haciendo datáfonos y nosotros somos buenos haciendo software. Vamos a ofrecer una serie de diferenciales competitivos”, dijo Contezini a Pipeline.
La competencia no es pequeña: entre los rivales citados por el medio están BTG, el más reciente en llegar, Cielo, Itaú, PagBank y Stone. Según la proyección de la empresa, el TPV del datáfono debería duplicarse cuando cumpla su primer año en el mercado, superando el de la versión en el celular. 
“La tendencia es que el datáfono se convierta en un negocio más grande, porque logra llegar hasta el último rincón del pequeño y mediano comerciante de Brasil”, afirmó el CEO.
 Ingresos, adquisiciones e IPO en el plan 
De acuerdo con Pipeline, la entrada en adquirencia debería ayudar a Asaas a superar los R$ 1.000 millones de facturación aún en 2026 y a duplicar esa cifra, hasta R$ 2.000 millones, en 2027. En junio, la empresa hizo la mayor compra de su historia, Helena CRM , por R$ 150 millones, y el mes pasado captó R$ 300 millones en un FIDC (fondo de inversión en derechos de crédito) para financiar la operación de crédito.
Contezini espera cerrar dos adquisiciones por año a partir de 2027. La Fintech, que cuenta entre sus inversionistas a Vivo Ventures, 23S Capital, SoftBank y Bond Capital, también considera salir a bolsa a finales de la década, preparación que tiene a KPMG como socio desde que fue contratado hace dos años. “Es el momento en que vamos a necesitar más capital para internacionalizarnos. Queremos convertirnos en una empresa mundial”, dijo.
 Qué observar 
Las plataformas de gestión para pequeñas empresas que llegan al punto de venta físico parten con ventaja de distribución, pero enfrentan a adquirentes que ya compiten en tarifa y plazo de recepción de los pagos. Vale la pena seguir si la promesa de integración con factura electrónica, ERP (sistema de gestión empresarial) y anticipo de recibibles pesa más que el precio a la hora de que el comerciante cambie de terminal.
Si el cobro por celular encontró resistencia entre los comerciantes más pequeños, el ritmo de distribución del aparato físico dirá si la proyección de volumen para el primer año se sostiene, y cuánto de la meta de ingresos para 2027 depende de este canal.

_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Asaas — a Joinville-founded (2010/13) Brazilian SMB financial-software/payments platform, ~300k SME clients — launched **Asaas Smart**, its **first-ever physical card machine (datáfono/maquininha)**, built on **Adyen** as the processing/acquiring backbone. Announced ~2026-10-14/15 (LatAmFintech via Pipeline). Accepts credit/debit/voucher/multi-benefit, with Pix-QR, chip, magstripe and contactless capture. Projected R$237M TPV by end-2026; company expects that figure to **double** and overtake the phone-based Asaas Tap in the device's first full year.
- **De-PR'd reality:** this is Asaas entering **physical acquiring** (an already crowded, commoditized line), not a technology breakthrough. It is a hardware wrapper around Adyen's rails plus Asaas's own software layer. "Launch" is really a **pilot**: initial rollout is reported as ~10 selected clients on a 30-day free trial, with a monthly fee to follow — so "offered to 300k clients" is distribution potential, not adoption.
- **Why structured this way → why it matters:** Asaas first tried to *buy* an acquirer but "none fit the ecosystem," so it built from scratch on Adyen. Building on Adyen lets it skip acquirer licensing/certification and ship fast, but it means the **payments margin is shared with Adyen** — Asaas's real wedge is the *software* (integration with e-invoicing, ERP, receivables anticipation), not the take-rate. The R$237M TPV target is tiny next to Brazil's acquiring incumbents, signalling this is a **feature to deepen the SMB OS**, not a bid for acquiring share.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Brazilian acquiring is dominated by **5 players controlling ~96% of card volume**: Cielo, Rede (Itaú), PagBank/PagSeguro, Stone, Mercado Pago, with Getnet (Santander) trailing (~9% of users, falling). The media named Asaas's rivals as **BTG (newest entrant), Cielo, Itaú/Rede, PagBank and Stone**. On smart-POS + software bundling specifically, [[Getnet launches card machine with conversational generative AI]] shows incumbents already pushing software/AI onto the maquininha. Adyen itself ships terminals ([[Adyen launches S1E4 Pro and S1F4 Pro payment terminals]], [[Adyen launches two new payment terminals]]) and recently landed its AMS1 data region in Brazil.
- **Position:** Asaas is a **late, sub-scale entrant** in hardware acquiring (parity-to-behind on the device; its only edge is distribution to an existing SMB base + software integration). It is **not** competing on MDR/rate — the incumbents already fight on rate and receivables-anticipation terms.
- **Why → second order:** Incumbents win SMBs via capital, bundling (acquirer→digital account→credit) and bank relationships (Bradesco-Cielo, Itaú-Rede). Stone's model — acquiring integrated with a digital account + same-platform receivables advance — is exactly what Asaas is copying. So Asaas is entering **against players who already own the exact "software + payments + credit" bundle**; its differentiation is narrower than the PR implies.

## [2] Company history / fit
Trajectory: founded 2010/13 by Diego & Piero Contezini; $148M Series C (Bond, SoftBank, 23S, Endeavor) — first international capital; Brazil central-bank nod to operate as a finance company ([[Asaas gets Brazil central bank nod to operate as finance company]]); M&A run — Nexinvoice, Mutuus Seguros ([[Brazil's Asaas acquires insurance broker Mutuus]]), Helena CRM for ~R$150M ([[Asaas acquires Helena CRM for $29 million for WhatsApp AI sales]]); R$300M FIDC to fund credit ([[Asaas raises $55-58M in largest FIDC to date]]). Targets R$1bn revenue in 2026, R$2bn in 2027; 2 acquisitions/yr from 2027; possible IPO ~late-decade (KPMG engaged).
- **Why Asaas acts this way (structural logic):** Asaas is assembling a **full SMB financial OS** — banking, invoicing, CRM, insurance, credit — and physical acquiring was the missing piece that keeps the merchant's payment flow inside the ecosystem (and feeds receivables/credit data). Entering acquiring **before an IPO** also adds a transactional revenue line to support the R$1bn→R$2bn revenue story. The device is a **retention/cross-sell and data play**, consistent with the pre-IPO scaling of recurring + transactional revenue.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new?** For Asaas, yes — first physical hardware ever, and a new acquiring vertical. For the **market**, no — a smart-POS tied to SMB software + receivables anticipation is standard incumbent play (Stone, and Getnet's AI machine). Novelty is **company-internal**, not category-defining.
- **Traction:** essentially **zero proven** — R$237M TPV by end-2026 is a projection; live footprint is a ~10-client pilot with a free-trial-then-fee model. No clients, no GMV disclosed. Asaas's own CEO flags that the phone-based Asaas Tap saw **low adoption** among the smallest merchants — a direct warning sign that device adoption is unproven.
- **Who captures the margin → why value-add is thin:** payments economics are **split with Adyen**; on a commoditized MDR Asaas can't out-price bank-owned acquirers with cheaper capital. The *only* durable value-add is **software lock-in** (invoice/ERP/receivables integration) — if merchants switch on price, the whole thesis fails. The CEO concedes "they're good at datáfonos, we're good at software": an admission the hardware is undifferentiated.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Plans: double device TPV in year one, surpass Asaas Tap; use acquiring to help cross R$1bn (2026) and R$2bn (2027); 2 M&A/yr from 2027; IPO ~late-decade; international ambitions. Risks: (a) adoption resistance (Asaas Tap precedent); (b) margin squeeze — undifferentiated hardware + Adyen share + incumbents competing on rate/anticipation; (c) execution on a brand-new hardware/logistics/servicing operation it has never run; (d) FIDC-funded credit on receivables raises credit-cycle exposure.
- **Why the market may go this way / second-order:** Brazil acquiring is a **share-of-wallet war among software-plus-payments bundles**, not a pure-rate fight. Asaas is directionally correct to add the device, but entering a 96%-concentrated, rate-competitive market as player ~6+ means TPV comes slowly and dilutively. The counterintuitive risk: the device could **cannibalize margin** (hardware + servicing cost, shared Adyen economics) while adding little net profit — a growth-optics move for the IPO story more than a margin driver. Weight is **modest**: incremental, unproven, me-too in a crowded market.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Brazilian card acquiring processed ~R$4.2tn TPV in 2025 (per secondary market data, via dinheirodaminhaempresa.com / brazilstockguide.com, as of 2026) — a large but highly consolidated pool: the five largest acquirers (Cielo ~30%, PagBank/Rede ~26% each, StoneCo ~13%, plus Mercado Pago) concentrate ~96% of card volume (per Itaú BBA, via NeoFeed, 2026). Entry barriers are hardware economics + acquiring licence + float/credit balance-sheet, not technology; smart-POS is a mature, price-competitive category. The secular headwind is Pix: its P2B fee is BCB-capped at 0.33% (cap R$3.50) vs ~2.34% avg credit MDR and ~1.13% debit (per Flagship Advisory / BCB, via paymentreview.com, 2026), compressing blended MDR as volume migrates off cards. "Why now" for Asaas: the company is building a hardware acquiring vertical to (per note) push revenue past R$1bn in 2026 and to R$2bn in 2027, ahead of a late-decade IPO — i.e. it needs an owned revenue line, not just software, to scale toward listing.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV, net MDR/take rate, receivables-advance (anticipação) spread, and merchant CAC-to-attach. Named rivals in the note — Cielo, Itaú (Rede), PagBank, Stone, and newest entrant BTG — compete on price, settlement speed, and increasingly on bundled banking. Recent moves: PagBank launched the AI-voice "Minizinha Voz" POS (Jan 2026, [[PagBank launches Minizinha Voz, Brazil's first AI-voice POS terminal]]); Stone is explicitly pivoting from "a machine that does other things" to a digital bank, R$2.8bn credit book / ~R$10bn non-interest deposits in 2025 ([[Brazil's Stone accelerates shift from POS provider to digital bank]]). Asaas's position: a late, niche entrant in hardware (first physical device in its 16-year history) but with a genuine distribution edge — 300k existing SME software clients — and a software-first moat (switching costs via integrated invoicing/ERP/receivables), per Contezini. On raw acquiring it is behind incumbents on scale and pricing; its bet is that management-software integration outweighs price (analysis). Note the modest ambition disclosed: only R$237M terminal TPV by end-2026 — a rounding error vs Stone/PagBank MSMB TPV of ~R$500–520bn each.

**Comps & multiples.** Public LatAm acquiring comps trade cheaply, reflecting the Pix overhang:
- StoneCo (NASDAQ: STNE): EV ~$4.37bn / FY25 revenue R$14.15bn (~$2.65bn) ≈ EV/Rev ~1.6x; mkt cap ~$2.2bn (per stockanalysis.com / SEC 20-F FY25, Oct 2026). FY25 revenue +17.5% YoY.
- PagBank/PagSeguro (NYSE: PAGS): mkt cap ~$2.5bn, EV ~$1.8bn, ~6.8x normalized earnings, ~1.0x book (per simplywall.st / finance.yahoo, Oct 2026). EV/Rev not cleanly computed — mixed bank/payments P&L, so "no data" on a comparable revenue multiple.
- Adyen (platform partner, not a Brazilian acquiring comp) — see IR grounding below.

**Adyen latest print (backdrop).** Q1 2026 Business Update (released 2026-05-06): net revenue €620.8mn, up 16% YoY (+20% constant-currency); processed volume (TPV) €382.0bn, up 21% YoY. No EBITDA is disclosed in the quarterly updates (Adyen reports EBITDA semi-annually). FY 2025 (H2 2025 results, 2026-02-12): net revenue €2,364.2mn (+18% YoY, +21% cc); TPV €1,394.3bn (+21% ex a single large-volume customer, +8% incl.); EBITDA €1,245.7mn (+26% YoY), EBITDA margin 53% (vs 50% in 2024); free-cash-flow conversion 87%, capex 5% of net revenue.

**Why this matters for the Asaas deal — Platforms + POS are Adyen's fastest lines.** In Q1 2026 Adyen's Platforms segment (the embedded-finance / "payments-for-platforms" business that an arrangement like Asaas Smart sits in) posted €75.0mn net revenue, up 35% YoY (+40% cc) — the fastest of its three segments (Unified Commerce +24% YoY; Digital +9% YoY). On the hardware side, FY 2025 point-of-sale volumes were €311bn, up 34% YoY — well above group TPV growth, confirming in-person/terminal is a structural growth vector, not a legacy drag. So Asaas Smart plugs Asaas into Adyen's two best-compounding capabilities (embedded platforms + physical acquiring) rather than a mature utility.

**Guidance (Adyen, reaffirmed).** FY 2026: net revenue growth of 20–22% YoY constant-currency; EBITDA margin "broadly in line with 2025" (i.e. ~53%), with a longer-term target of >55% by 2028; capex sustained at up to 5% of net revenue. Guidance was reaffirmed at Q1 — management tone confident, citing wallet-share gains with existing merchants and resilient macro.

**Thesis-flags (for the Asaas story).**
- Platforms is Adyen's growth engine (+35% YoY) but still small (€75.0mn/quarter, ~12% of net revenue) — LatAm platform wins like Asaas (300k SME base, R$237mn projected TPV by end-2026) are incremental, not needle-movers for Adyen near-term; the strategic read is distribution/embedded land-grab, second-order being deeper SME lock-in.
