Maybank deploys AI to prevent banking disruptions
 
 ServiceNow will support technology monitoring across Malaysia, Singapore, and Indonesia under a five-year agreement. 
 Maybank has signed a five-year agreement with ServiceNow to expand its regional Resilience Operations Centre (ROC) across Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia, using artificial intelligence (AI) to identify operational risks and reduce potential banking service disruptions.
The initiative will give Maybank a consolidated view of its technology systems, allowing teams to monitor applications, infrastructure and service performance across the three markets.
Using ServiceNow's AI platform, the bank aims to detect potential problems earlier, automate operational processes and address risks before they affect customers.
The rollout will begin in Malaysia and Singapore before expanding to Indonesia over the five-year programme.
The agreement forms part of Maybank's ROAR30 transformation strategy and will support technology operations across its retail, small and medium-sized enterprise (SME) and corporate banking divisions.
“As banking becomes more digital and interconnected across the region, our customers expect us to be available, secure and dependable at every moment,” said Giorgio Migliarina, Group Chief Technology and Digital Officer at Maybank.
The system will also help the bank maintain security and operational resilience standards while meeting regulatory requirements across different markets.
Maybank will establish an internal Centre of Excellence, supported by ServiceNow University, to train employees in platform engineering, automation and AI.
The centre will also support the adoption of new technologies and improvements to banking operations.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-10-20, Malaysia's Maybank signed a five-year agreement with ServiceNow to scale its regional Resilience Operations Centre (ROC) across Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia. The ROC uses the ServiceNow AI Platform (AIOps/ITOM) to give a consolidated, real-time view of applications, infrastructure, technology assets and service dependencies — aiming to detect operational risks earlier, automate operational processes and prevent service disruptions before they reach customers. Rollout starts in Malaysia and Singapore, then extends to Indonesia over the five-year programme. Deal value undisclosed. Maybank will also stand up an internal Centre of Excellence backed by ServiceNow University to build platform-engineering, automation and AI skills in-house. ([ServiceNow newsroom](https://newsroom.servicenow.com/press-releases/details/2026/Maybank-enhances-Resilient-Operations-Centre-capabilities-with-ServiceNow-to-power-always-on-banking-across-Southeast-Asia/default.aspx), [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/10/20/maybank-inks-five-year-deal-to-expand-regional-resilience-operations-centre), [Asian Banking & Finance](https://asianbankingandfinance.net/news/maybank-deploys-ai-prevent-banking-disruptions))

**[1] Why it matters / "expand" framing.** This is the technology leg of Maybank's **ROAR30** 2026–2030 strategic plan, under which the bank announced in January 2026 it would invest ~RM10 billion over five years in tech, data and AI. The ROC already existed as a concept of "one integrated operational view"; the ServiceNow deal operationalises and regionalises it on a single AIOps platform spanning three markets and the retail, SME and corporate banking divisions. The headline word "expand" refers to broadening ROC coverage from a Malaysia-centric baseline to a tri-market footprint on ServiceNow — not a brand-new programme. Driver is regulatory: Southeast Asian regulators (BNM's RMiT, MAS, OJK) have tightened operational-resilience and outage-reporting rules, so always-on availability is both a competitive and compliance imperative.

**[2] ServiceNow's financial-services AIOps push (internal context).** ServiceNow is systematically landing large multi-year FS deals that embed its Now Assist / AI Platform into operational workflows: see [[ServiceNow and Fiserv expand AI partnership]] (Jan 2026, explicitly about "operational stability and resiliency" via Now Assist) and [[Experian launches Agent Operating System on Ascend platform]] (Jun 2026, ServiceNow as first agentic-AI integration partner). Maybank extends this pattern into a named Southeast-Asian Tier-1 bank. Peer banks are building similar AI-for-IT-ops capability — cf. [[CaixaBank creates Artificial Intelligence Office]] (uses AI to automate IT operations and cut incident-resolution time).

**[3] Competitive landscape.** The deal is a resilience/AIOps play, not a core-banking one. ServiceNow competes here with observability-first vendors — Dynatrace (causal AI, dependency graph), Datadog (telemetry + Watchdog/Bits AI), PagerDuty and New Relic. ServiceNow's differentiation is orchestration + CMDB/ITSM/ITAM and workflow automation rather than raw telemetry depth — i.e. it wins on service visibility, governance and process control, which maps to a regulated bank's need for auditable resilience. Separately, resilience is an industry-wide theme: cf. [[How Monzo uses multi-cloud infrastructure to improve resilience]] (infra-level approach) and [[ACI Worldwide on real-time payments without risk]] (payments-rail resilience).

**[4] Open angles / what to watch.** (a) No disclosed contract value or quantified outage-reduction target — success metrics are qualitative. (b) Indonesia is sequenced last, so the hardest multi-jurisdiction data-residency/regulatory integration (OJK) is deferred. (c) Giorgio Migliarina (Group Chief Technology and Digital Officer) is the named sponsor; execution risk sits on the internal CoE actually building durable in-house skills vs. vendor lock-in to ServiceNow. (d) Watch whether this becomes a reference template ServiceNow replicates with other ASEAN banks.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

**Red-team questions**

1. *Is this a new programme or a rebrand of existing work?* — Partly rebrand. The ROC and "one integrated operational view" predate this deal; the ServiceNow agreement scales/regionalises it. "Expand" is accurate but the novelty is the vendor commitment + tri-market scope, not the concept.
2. *Is the dollar value material?* — Unknown. Deal value undisclosed; sits within the ROAR30 ~RM10bn 5-year tech/data/AI envelope (not incremental to it, likely a subset). Can't size the ServiceNow spend.
3. *What concretely does "AI" do here?* — AIOps: anomaly detection, event correlation, predictive incident detection and process automation across apps/infra/dependencies. Not customer-facing GenAI. Claim of "detect problems earlier / prevent disruptions" is standard AIOps marketing, unquantified. [OPEN: no stated MTTR or availability target.]
4. *Is five years long enough / too long to judge?* — Rollout is phased (MY+SG first, ID last). Benefits accrue late; no interim milestones disclosed, so near-term impact is minimal.
5. *Regulatory driver real?* — Yes. BNM RMiT, MAS and OJK operational-resilience/outage-reporting rules make always-on availability a compliance need, strengthening the rationale beyond marketing.
6. *Vendor lock-in risk?* — High by design: single-platform consolidation on ServiceNow AI Platform. The internal CoE + ServiceNow University training deepens dependency; mitigant is in-house skill-building but that also entrenches the stack.
7. *Why ServiceNow over Dynatrace/Datadog?* — Likely because ServiceNow already provides ITSM/CMDB at the bank; orchestration + governance + auditability matter more to a regulated bank than telemetry depth. [OPEN: incumbency not confirmed in sources.]
8. *Is Maybank an outlier or following a pattern?* — Following. ServiceNow has comparable FS deals (Fiserv Jan-2026, Experian Jun-2026); peers like CaixaBank automate IT ops with AI. Incremental, not category-defining.
9. *Does this touch core banking / customer systems?* — No. It's IT operations/resilience tooling layered over existing systems; no core-banking migration implied.
10. *Any evidence of prior outages motivating this?* — Not cited in sources. Framed proactively ("prevent disruptions"), but no specific incident disclosed. [OPEN.]
11. *Execution/people risk?* — Named sponsor Giorgio Migliarina (Group CTDO). Success hinges on the CoE building durable skills and on multi-jurisdiction integration (data residency) — Indonesia deferred to last, the hardest leg.
12. *Is the source first-party?* — Yes; ServiceNow newsroom + Maybank-sourced regional press (The Star, NST, Asian Banking & Finance) corroborate consistently. Low misinformation risk.
13. *Does "expand across 3 markets" imply new geographies for Maybank?* — No — Maybank already operates in all three; this expands ROC coverage, not banking presence.
14. *Investor/market-moving?* — No. No financial disclosure, no guidance change; a strategic-IT announcement, neutral-to-mildly-positive signalling on execution of ROAR30.
15. *What would change the importance score up?* — A disclosed value, a quantified resilience target, or confirmation this is a repeatable ASEAN template for ServiceNow.

**Importance: 2/5** — A credible, well-sourced execution datapoint on (a) a Tier-1 Southeast Asian bank's ROAR30 tech transformation and (b) ServiceNow's steady land-grab in financial-services AIOps/resilience. But it is an undisclosed-value, phased, infrastructure-ops deal with no quantified targets, no core-banking impact and no market-moving content — incremental rather than trend-setting.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits in AIOps / operational-resilience tooling for banks, not core banking or payments. Size: the AIOps-for-financial-services segment is put at ~$6.36bn in 2026, projected to ~$16.12bn by 2030 at a ~26.2% CAGR (per The Business Research Company / researchandmarkets, via GlobeNewswire, 2026-07-03); the broader cross-industry AIOps market is ~$18.95bn in 2026 growing ~14.8% (per Mordor Intelligence, as of 2026) — the FS cut grows roughly 2x faster. Structure: fragmented on the vendor side — platform players (ServiceNow, Splunk/Cisco, Datadog, Dynatrace, IBM) compete with the banks' own SRE/observability stacks; value accrues at the correlation/automation layer that sits above existing monitoring. Barriers are switching costs and deep workflow integration rather than capital. "Why now": digital-channel dependence plus intensifying regulatory pressure on operational resilience (DORA in the EU, MAS/BNM frameworks in this exact Malaysia-Singapore-Indonesia footprint) pushes banks from reactive incident management to predictive detection; a severe regional bank outage is now a supervisory, not just an IT, event — second-order effect: resilience spend is defensive/non-discretionary, which underwrites the FS-AIOps CAGR above.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for the vendor it is subscription revenue growth, net-new AI ACV and net revenue retention; for the bank buyer the operational metric is MTTD/MTTR (mean time to detect/resolve) and outage-minutes avoided — none disclosed for this deal `[UNSOURCED]`. Players & basis of competition: ServiceNow (workflow + "control tower" positioning), Datadog/Dynatrace (observability-native), Splunk/Cisco (data + security), IBM (legacy enterprise); competition is on breadth of workflow integration and AI-agent depth, not price. Recent moves, dated: ServiceNow + Fiserv expanded an AI operational-resilience partnership (2026-01-30, [[ServiceNow and Fiserv expand AI partnership]]); ServiceNow became first integration partner for Experian's Agent Operating System (2026-06-04, [[Experian launches Agent Operating System on Ascend platform]]); Banorte picked Hitachi Vantara for a digital-banking platform in LatAm (2026-03-05, [[Banorte partners with Hitachi Vantara on digital banking platform]]). Protagonist position: ServiceNow is a scaled incumbent and clearly ahead in landing marquee FS resilience logos; Maybank is a fast-follower regionally — this is a buyer adopting a leader's stack, not a differentiator vs other ASEAN banks (analysis). Moat for ServiceNow = switching costs + workflow lock-in once the ROC runs on its platform across three markets and a ServiceNow-University Centre of Excellence trains staff on it.

**Comps & multiples.** This is a 5-year services/licence agreement with no disclosed contract value, so no deal multiple can be computed — "no data" on deal economics. Vendor trading comp (NYSE:NOW): EV ≈ $165.21bn, LTM revenue ≈ $14.73bn → EV/Revenue = $165.21bn / $14.73bn ≈ 11.2x (per stockanalysis.com, as of 2026-10-23); against FY2025 revenue of $13.3bn (+20.9% YoY, per FY2025 10-K / 8-K) the multiple is ~12.4x. At ~22–24% revenue growth, an ~11x EV/Revenue is in-line for a scaled rule-of-40 SaaS compounder — rich on an absolute basis but justified by the growth rate, not an outlier flag. Internal comps (base): [[ServiceNow and Fiserv expand AI partnership]], [[Experian launches Agent Operating System on Ascend platform]], [[Banorte partners with Hitachi Vantara on digital banking platform]] — all partnership/product items, none carry a valuation, so distribution not computed; qualitative only. Maybank multiples not relevant (buyer, not the asset being valued) — "no data".

**Risk flags.**
1. Announced vs live: this is a signed 5-year agreement that *begins* in Malaysia/Singapore and only later reaches Indonesia — disruption-reduction benefits are prospective; the PR gives zero before/after resilience metrics, so efficacy is unproven. Second-order: execution/change-management risk on a multi-country rollout can push value realisation years out.
2. Vendor concentration / dependence on someone else's rails: consolidating a cross-market "single pane of glass" on one vendor's AI platform deepens switching costs — a ServiceNow outage or pricing step-up at renewal becomes Maybank's problem, and resilience tooling itself turns into a single point of failure.
3. Regulatory-scope risk across three jurisdictions (BNM, MAS, OJK): differing data-localisation and operational-resilience rules complicate a shared regional platform; compliance divergence could fragment the "consolidated view" the deal is meant to deliver.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector this is another data-point that bank operational resilience is becoming a named, budgeted AIOps buying category in ASEAN, mirroring ServiceNow's earlier Fiserv/Experian FS wins — a land-and-expand platform pattern rather than one-off tooling (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: ServiceNow's FS-vertical ACV keeps compounding as resilience mandates spread; watch the 2026-10-28 earnings print and AI-ACV disclosure as the near-term trigger, and whether Maybank later publishes any outage-reduction metric — if the ROC rollout stalls or no efficacy data emerges within ~18 months, the resilience-AIOps thesis for regional banks weakens.
