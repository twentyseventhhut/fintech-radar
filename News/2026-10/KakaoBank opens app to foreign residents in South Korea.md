KakaoBank opens app to foreign residents, enabling remote account opening


 Foreign residents can now open accounts remotely, get debit cards and send remittances to 200 countries through KakaoBank’s main app.
 
KakaoBank added banking services for foreign residents in Korea to its existing app on Monday, allowing them to open checking accounts and send remittances to 200 countries in over 50 currencies.
The service gives foreign residents access to banking services available to Korean citizens, including debit card issuance, as well as features tailored to their needs, such as multilingual customer support and access to bank documents, including balance certificates and transaction histories, the internet-only bank said. Previously, services available to foreign customers were limited to mobile identification and shared accounts.
“Banks have typically developed separate apps for foreign customers, inevitably creating a service gap with apps for Korean customers over time,” said Bae Jung-ho, head of the foreign customer camp at KakaoBank, at a press conference held in central Seoul on Monday to introduce the new service.

“But we don’t separate our apps for foreign and Korean customers. Our goal is to provide foreign customers with the exact same convenient financial services and benefits that our domestic customers enjoy,” Bae added.
Just like Koreans, foreign users of KakaoBank’s banking app will be able to open an account without visiting a branch, enjoy fee waivers for transfers and ATM withdrawals and also benefit from debit card cash-back. KakaoBank said it plans to introduce savings accounts, open banking and loan comparison services in 2027.
The bank rolled out the offering as the number of foreign residents in Korea continues to grow, reaching 2.78 million at the end of last year, up 5 percent from a year earlier, according to the Ministry of Justice. Of those, 2.15 million had stayed in the country for more than 90 days.
Along with the growth, foreign residents have emerged as a key target for banks seeking new sources of revenue as their numbers grow. Shinhan Bank revamped its mobile banking app for foreign customers, SOL Global, last year, adding support for 16 languages and expanding remote banking services such as account opening and loan applications. Hana Bank has also revamped its Hana EZ app for foreign customers into an integrated financial and lifestyle platform, expanding its services this year to include overseas remittances, document issuance and utility bill payments. JB Financial Group also launched Bravo Korea, a financial service platform for foreign residents, in 2025.
"By integrating essential functions tailored for foreign customers, such as multilingual services, specialized authentication and robust security features, we are committed to making their financial lives in Korea easier and much safer,” Bae added.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] TL;DR.** On Mon 2026-10-12, KakaoBank — South Korea's largest internet-only bank (~27.6M customers) — launched a full "Foreigner Service" inside its *existing* main app (not a separate foreigner app), letting foreign residents open a checking account remotely, get a debit card, and send remittances to ~200 countries in 50+ currencies, with AI translation in English/Chinese/Vietnamese. First reported by Korea JoongAng Daily; corroborated by Korea Herald, Korea Times, KED Global, Kyunghyang Shinmun, Seoul Economic Daily (all 2026-10-12/13). Importance 3/5: incremental catch-up in a crowded niche, but strategically notable because KakaoBank refuses to fork a separate app and because it had pre-announced this for Q3 2026.

**[1] Dated timeline.**
- 2026-06 (reported 06-16): KakaoBank states it plans to roll out non-face-to-face services for foreign residents in Q3 2026 (per [[Korea's online-only banks near 60 million customers]]). → The Oct launch is a slight slip from that guidance.
- 2026-08-05: Q2/H1 2026 earnings — record net profit KRW 328.0bn (+24% YoY), fee income +92% YoY, 27.63M customers (external; Investing.com).
- 2026-10-12 (Mon): Press conference in central Seoul; service goes live. Bae Jung-ho, "head of the foreign customer camp," presents.
- 2027 (planned): KakaoBank says it will add savings accounts, open banking, loan-comparison, and expand language support to 7 languages.

**[2] What's actually new / specifics.**
- Onboarding: remote, via a chatbot conversation; ~5 minutes if no extra review. Uses foreigner-specific **selfie verification + public MyData** to pre-fill name/nationality/address/occupation (external: ID Tech, KED Global).
- Eligibility: age 19+, ~200 nationalities, needs a foreign ID document + a Korean mobile phone registered in own name.
- Remittance: ~200 countries, 50+ currencies; **zero fee** to major corridors (US, China); inbound-receive fees also fully waived.
- Fees: transfer + ATM fees waived on same terms as Korean nationals; debit-card cash-back.
- Foreigner-specific extras: multilingual support, 1:1 English consultation, and balance/transaction certificates (e.g., balance certificate needed for **visa extension**).
- Prior state: KakaoBank foreign-customer features were limited to mobile ID + shared accounts; this is its first true foreigner account product.

**[3] Why it matters / market context.** Korea's foreign-resident population hit a record 2.78M at end-2025 (+5% YoY; MoJ), ~2.15M staying 90+ days — a new revenue pool as domestic growth matures. This is a **crowded, late-mover** entry:
- Toss Bank: first internet-only bank to offer non-face-to-face foreigner account opening, since **May 2022** (external) — so KakaoBank is ~3.5 years behind on this specific feature (see [[Toss Bank posts 96.8 billion won 2025 net profit, doubling]], 14.23M customers end-2025).
- Shinhan Bank: revamped SOL Global foreigner app (16 languages) in 2025.
- Hana Bank: upgraded Hana EZ into integrated finance/lifestyle platform (remittances, document issuance, utility bills) in 2026.
- JB Financial: launched Bravo Korea for foreign residents in 2025.
KakaoBank's differentiator is the "one app for everyone" stance (Bae: "we don't separate our apps for foreign and Korean customers") plus AI translation baked into the mainline super-app — contrasting with rivals' dedicated foreigner apps. Korea's three online-only banks (KakaoBank, K Bank, Toss) together neared 60M customers (see [[Korea's online-only banks near 60 million customers]]), with the strategic shift from scale → profitability/revenue diversification. Fits KakaoBank's broader expansion drive alongside its overseas plays ([[KakaoBank prepares to launch Bank X digital bank in Thailand]]).

**[4] Open questions / what to watch.** (a) Adoption vs. incumbents who already own foreigner relationships; (b) FX/remittance economics given zero-fee corridors — margin or loss-leader for acquisition? (c) AML/eKYC risk of remote non-resident onboarding and selfie+MyData reliability for non-Korean IDs; (d) whether the 2027 roadmap (loans/open banking/savings) actually monetizes this cohort; (e) whether "same app as Koreans" actually delivers full feature parity or hidden gaps.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. Is this genuinely "new," or catch-up? Toss Bank offered non-face-to-face foreigner account opening since **May 2022**; Shinhan/Hana/JB already serve this segment. KakaoBank is a ~3.5-year late mover on the core feature — is the news really the *integration into one app*, not the capability?
2. "~5 minutes" onboarding assumes "no additional review." What fraction of foreign applicants actually trigger manual review, and does the selfie+MyData flow work for the long tail of non-Korean ID formats?
3. Zero-fee remittance to US/China and waived inbound-receive fees: is this a sustainable product or a customer-acquisition loss-leader? Who eats the FX spread?
4. Eligibility requires a Korean mobile phone registered in own name + foreign ID — how large is the *actually addressable* subset of the 2.15M long-stay residents vs. the headline 2.78M?
5. AML/CFT exposure: remote onboarding of foreign nationals is higher-risk. Has KakaoBank disclosed any regulator conditions (FSC/FSS), and could this invite scrutiny?
6. The June guidance said Q3 2026; launch slipped to mid-Oct (early Q4). Why the delay — technical, compliance, or eKYC certification for foreigners?
7. AI translation covers only English/Chinese/Vietnamese at launch (7 languages "next year"); Shinhan's SOL Global already does 16 languages. Is KakaoBank under-serving major cohorts (e.g., Uzbek, Nepali, Thai, Khmer) at launch?
8. Does "same app, same features as Koreans" hold up? Loans, savings, open banking are deferred to 2027 — so foreigners get a *deposit/remittance-only* product today. Is parity overclaimed?
9. Revenue materiality: with 27.6M customers and KRW 328bn H1 net profit, how much incremental revenue can foreign residents realistically add in 1–2 years? Risk it's strategically loud but financially immaterial.
10. Competitive response: will Toss/Shinhan/Hana cut fees or add languages in reaction, compressing the whole segment's economics?
11. Source quality: all reporting traces to a single 2026-10-12 KakaoBank press conference (Bae Jung-ho). Any independent user-side verification of the 5-min claim or zero-fee corridors?
12. Does this connect to KakaoBank's remittance/stablecoin ambitions (won-pegged stablecoin filings, Upbit) or overseas builds (Thailand Bank X, Mongolia M Bank), or is it a standalone domestic play?
13. "200 countries / 50+ currencies" — which remittance rails/partners power this, and are the zero-fee corridors FX-rate competitive vs. Wise/dedicated remittance apps foreigners already use?
14. Churn risk: foreign residents are transient; how does KakaoBank retain accounts after a user leaves Korea (and keep the account AML-compliant)?

**Importance: 3/5** — Credible, well-corroborated, strategically coherent (super-app one-app stance + large growing 2.78M-resident TAM + fits KakaoBank's expansion narrative), but it is an *incremental, late-mover* product in an already-crowded niche where Toss (2022) and others lead; no new financials, no M&A, feature parity deferred to 2027. Notable, not market-moving.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Korea digital-banking serving foreign residents is a saturated-home-market pivot, not a greenfield. Foreign residents reached 2.78m end-2025 (+5% YoY), of which 2.15m stayed >90 days (per Ministry of Justice, via Korea JoongAng Daily, as of 2026-10-13). Cumulative foreign customers at the five major commercial banks (KB, Shinhan, Hana, Woori, NH) topped 7.03m by end-June 2026 (per The Korea Times / asianews.network, 2026-10). "Why now": domestic banking is saturated (~51m population, KakaoBank alone 26.7m users), so the expat segment — growing ~5–8%/yr, ~1.69m working-age foreign residents May-2025, 1-in-3 earning >₩3m/month (per Financial News, 2025-12) — is the marginal growth pool. Remittance is the anchor use-case: 39% of foreign residents send money home; ~31% remit >₩20m/yr (per Financial News). Market TAM for outbound expat remittance as a single figure: no data (only share-of-wallet brackets are sourced).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: customers/MAU, account-open friction (time-to-onboard), remittance corridors×currencies, cost-per-transfer (fee), languages supported. Basis of competition = distribution + friction removal, not price alone. Key moves, all dated:
- KakaoBank (2026-10-12): single app for foreign + Korean customers, ~5-min chatbot onboarding, debit cards, remittance to 200 countries / 50+ currencies, AI translation (EN/CN/VN), US & China transfers free via Hanpass partnership (per Korea Herald, Seoul Economic Daily, 2026-10). Savings/open-banking/loan-comparison promised 2027.
- Shinhan: revamped SOL Global (16 languages, 2025) + SOL Global Loan up to ₩20m; Shinhan Card issued foreigner debit cards (2024).
- Hana: Hana EZ app (first global app, 2019) rebuilt into lifestyle platform; Hana Foreigner EZ Loan up to ₩10m for E7/E9 visas (Aug 2026).
- JB Financial: Bravo Korea platform (2025).
- Toss: waived foreign-resident transfer fees ([[Toss waives money transfer fees for foreign residents in Korea]], 2025-08).
Protagonist position: catching up, not leading — incumbents (Hana since 2019, Shinhan multilingual 2025) had first-mover; KakaoBank's differentiation is the single-app / low-friction UX and its 26.7m-user distribution + brand (analysis). Moat = scale/distribution + UX, not a product the peers can't copy.

**Comps & multiples.** KakaoBank (KRX:323410): 2025 net profit ₩480.3bn (+9.1%), 26.7m users, 20m Q4 MAU (per Korea Times, 2026-02); market cap ~$6.96bn as of Jul-2026 (per companiesmarketcap). P/E = $6.96bn / (₩480.3bn ≈ $0.35bn at ~₩1,370/$) ≈ 20x (analysis; FX/period-match rough — treat as indicative, not clean). In-line for a profitable, growing digital bank.
Peer comp — Toss/Viva Republica (private): targeting 2026 US IPO at >$10bn (up to $15bn), 24m MAU (per Reuters/Caproasia, 2025-07). Round/IPO-target valuation, not market cap. Price-per-user: KakaoBank ~$6.96bn/26.7m ≈ $261/user vs Toss ~$10bn/24m ≈ $417/user — Toss richer, consistent with its broader super-app (securities + bank) vs KakaoBank's bank-centric model (analysis).
Internal comps: [[Korea's online-only banks near 60 million customers]], [[Toss waives money transfer fees for foreign residents in Korea]], [[KakaoBank posts record cumulative earnings on fee and platform growth]]. EV/EBITDA, NTM multiples: [UNSOURCED] (no free clean figures).

**Risk flags.**
1. Late-mover / crowded field — Hana (2019), Shinhan (2025), JB (2025), Toss (fee-waiver 2025) already courting the same 2.78m pool; KakaoBank is racing into a contested, thin-margin segment where differentiation decays as rivals copy UX. Second-order: foreign-resident banking may become a loss-leader land-grab, not a profit center.
2. Remittance economics opacity — free US/China transfers ride a third-party rail (Hanpass); KakaoBank captures the customer but likely cedes FX/transfer margin to the partner. Dependence on someone else's rails + unclear who bears fraud/compliance liability on cross-border flows.
3. Regulatory/KYC on remote foreign-resident onboarding (selfie/5-min flow) — AML, visa-status verification and sanctions screening across 200 corridors raise execution + compliance risk; a single corridor failure or regulator pushback could force re-papering.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is a feature-parity catch-up move that normalizes "one app, no separate foreign app" as the Korean standard — expect the gap to be competed away, so the winner is whoever couples distribution scale with the cheapest/widest remittance stack (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: KakaoBank's foreign-resident base stays a rounding error on 26.7m total users unless the promised 2027 savings/loans attach meaningfully. Trigger to watch: 2027 disclosure of foreign-customer count + whether KakaoBank in-sources remittance rails (vertical integration) or stays on Hanpass; thesis breaks if foreign-resident ARPU or attach visibly lifts non-interest income.
