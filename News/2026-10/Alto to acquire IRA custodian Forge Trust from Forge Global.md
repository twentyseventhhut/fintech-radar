# Context-enrichment: Alto to acquire IRA custodian Forge Trust from Forge Global
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On ~2026-10-06 Alto (AltoIRA, Nashville self-directed-IRA platform for alternative assets) agreed to acquire **Forge Trust Co.** (and parent Forge Services Inc.) — a South Dakota-chartered IRA/retirement custodian with ~40 years of operating lineage — **from Schwab, via Forge Global**. Terms are **undisclosed** (no price, no financing). Only disclosed condition: approval by the **South Dakota Division of Banking**; no closing date given.

- **De-PR'd correction on the seller.** The note's digest text says the seller is "Forge Global, a Charles Schwab subsidiary." That is *correct for 2026* but needs the missing fact to make sense: **Charles Schwab closed its ~$660M ($45.00/share cash) acquisition of Forge Global on 2026-03-02** (FRGE delisted from NYSE). So the real decision-maker/economic seller is **Schwab**, and "from Forge Global" is a legally-true but PR-softened framing. This is a **carve-out/divestiture by Schwab**, not a Forge Global business decision. (The phrasing would have been *false* before March 2026, when Forge was still independent and NYSE: FRGE.)
- **Why structured this way (the core red-team).** Schwab bought Forge Global for the *private-markets secondary marketplace* (pre-IPO share trading), not for retail self-directed-IRA custody. Post-merger overlap review → Schwab keeps the marketplace, sheds the SDIRA custodian. So this is Schwab tidying up an acquired asset it didn't want — immaterial to Schwab, meaningful to Alto.
- **Headline PR claim to discount:** "creating the category-defining platform" with **$20B+ AUC, 60,000+ self-directed IRA accounts, 3M+ Custody-as-a-Service accounts**. That combined figure *conflates* true alt-IRA AUC with a large, likely-low-margin Custody-as-a-Service book inherited via Forge Trust. The headline scale is real in gross terms but mixes qualities of assets (analysis).
- **Lineage fact that reframes the "why":** Forge Trust = the former **IRA Services Trust Co.** (founded 2008, Sioux Falls SD), which Forge Global acquired **2019-03-28** and renamed. So Alto is buying a 15+-year-old custody book, not a greenfield unit.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Self-directed-IRA / alt-asset retirement custodians, with dates:
- **Equity Trust Co.** (Ohio) — largest independent SDIRA custodian, decades old, broad alts.
- **Inspira Financial** (ex-Millennium Trust; rebrand announced 2023-08, effective early 2024; PayFlex merged in). Rolled up **NuView Trust (Dec 2023, ~$2.2B / 12k accts)** and became successor custodian for **Quest Trust (Sept 2024, ~$3B / ~20k accts)**; **>8M clients, $62B+ AUC (July 2026)** across all lines. The consolidation blueprint Alto is now copying.
- **STRATA Trust, Kingdom Trust, Pacific Premier Trust** — established alt custodians.
- **Fintech / crypto-native SDIRA:** Rocket Dollar, iTrustCapital, BitIRA, Bitcoin IRA.
- **Direct corpus peer:** [[IRA Financial expands investment access for self-directed clients]] (2026-05, also Sioux Falls SD) — same thesis (one account for alts + traditional), via Interactive Brokers rather than owning custody.
- **Position:** Pre-deal Alto was mid-size (~$2B AUC). Post-deal (~$20B+ incl. the inherited book) it jumps into the **top tier of private-market-focused** retirement custodians — still well below Inspira's $62B all-lines, but concentrated in alternatives.
- **Why the lay of the land is this way (2nd order).** SDIRA custody is a scale/charter game: revenue is per-account/flat-fee-ish, so AUC and account count drive economics. That structurally rewards roll-ups (Inspira) and makes an acquired 15-year book (Forge Trust) more valuable to a scaled consolidator than to a cost-cutting mega-broker (Schwab). Hence the trade makes sense for both sides.

## [2] Company history / fit
Alto: founded **2015** (Eric Satz), platform launched 2018. Funding: **Series A $17M (Apr 2021, Unusual Ventures; strategics incl. Coinbase Ventures, Franklin Templeton, NY Life Ventures, Carta, Stone Ridge)**; **Series B $40M (Jan 2022, Advance Venture Partners)**; **~$73M total across 8 rounds**. Earlier disclosed scale ~$2B AUC / 30,000+ investors.
- **Crucial fit correction:** Alto **already owns its own custodian** — **Alto Trust Co.** (New Mexico-chartered passive IRA custodian) plus **Alto Securities** (FINRA/SIPC broker-dealer). So this is **NOT first-time vertical integration / "renting custody"** — the common framing is wrong. It is a **scale + charter + book acquisition** by a platform that already does custody in-house.
- **Why Alto acts this way (structural logic).** With only ~$73M of lifetime VC and ~$2B AUC, organic growth to top-tier scale is slow; buying a ~15-year, multi-billion-AUC SD-chartered book (available cheap because Schwab doesn't want it) is a fast way to ~10x AUC, acquire a second trust charter, and pick up a 3M-account CaaS distribution surface. The question this raises → how a ~$73M-VC company finances an ~$18B-AUC acquisition (see challenges; financing undisclosed and material).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **What's genuinely here:** A real consolidation that ~10x's Alto's AUC and confirms Schwab's post-Forge strategy. Traction is in *inherited* numbers (an existing custody book + CaaS accounts), not new product adoption — so weight it as M&A scale, not innovation.
- **Who captures the margin (2nd order).** SDIRA custody is a thin, per-account fee business; the inherited **3M+ Custody-as-a-Service accounts** may be low-margin/pass-through (analysis). The durable value is the *alts* AUC where Alto can cross-sell its private-market/crypto access, not the CaaS plumbing. If the "$20B" is mostly CaaS, the quality of the scale is lower than the headline implies.
- **Standalone Forge Trust figures (~$18.4B AUC / ~2.7M accounts) are UNVERIFIED** — they appear only in a secondary aggregator, not in the primary Alto/Schwab releases. Treat as open; the combined-minus-Alto math is roughly consistent but the split is not primary-sourced.
- **Attrition risk:** custodian ownership transfers can trigger account churn; no retention terms disclosed. Only Forge Trust COO **Liz Alexander** is named as "joining Alto."

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Backdrop:** Deal rides the 2025–26 "private markets into retirement accounts / 401(k)" policy push (see corpus thread: [[Robinhood CEO calls retail's private-markets exclusion a tragedy]], [[Revolut forms team to bring private markets to retail investors]], [[Citi launches tokenized shares of private companies]]). Alto is positioning to be the retirement-account rail for that flow.
- **Next steps:** SD Division of Banking approval; integration of two state charters (NM Alto Trust + SD Forge Trust); account migration.
- **Risks / why it could underwhelm (2nd order):** (a) undisclosed price + financing for a sub-$100M-VC buyer is the real question mark; (b) combined-AUC headline conflates alt-IRA with low-margin CaaS; (c) SDIRA custody carries well-known prohibited-transaction/fraud-victim compliance exposure — scaling it ~10x multiplies operational risk, i.e. capital/scale concentration can make the franchise *more* fragile, not safer. Policy durability of the alts-in-retirement trend is itself a risk.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Self-directed IRA (SDIRA) custody sits inside the ~$19.2tn total US IRA market, with SDIRAs holding a sub-5% share across ~1M+ accounts at regulated custodians (per bullioniteassetgroup.com, as of 2026 — secondary/unverified, treat as order-of-magnitude). The bull case is the broad "private markets into retirement accounts" shift: Schwab itself cited private-wealth allocation to alternatives growing from $4tn to $13tn by 2032 (per Schwab/Businesswire, 2025-11, [[Charles Schwab to acquire Forge Global]]); PE has reportedly overtaken real estate as the most common SDIRA alt (~71% of SDIRA investors, per bullionite, unverified). **Why now:** companies stay private longer; large names (Anthropic/OpenAI) already trade on thick unofficial secondaries (per TBPN, 2026-04, [[AngelList acquires Carry while Lettuce takes its retirement platform]]); and the structure is a chain of consolidation — Schwab bought Forge Global for $660M (2025-11), closed in March 2026, and is now flipping the Forge Trust custody unit to Alto seven months later. Barriers are regulatory (state trust-charter + banking approval) and operational (custody-as-a-service plumbing), not capital-intensive — which is exactly why a Series B company can be the buyer.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: assets under custody/administration (AUC/AUA), number of custodial accounts, and fee take on custody + transactions. Key players: Equity Trust (~$81bn AUC, ~500k accounts, per irafinancial/bullionite), Pacific Premier Trust (~$18bn, ~31k accounts), IRA Financial (~$5bn), plus niche alt platforms. Alto's own niche adjacency: iTrustCapital (crypto IRA, $1.3bn post-money at its 2022-01 Series A, $125M led by Left Lane; >$14bn lifetime transaction volume by 2024-25, per CoinDesk/company), and AngelList/Carry/Lettuce moving on the same "private markets in retirement" thesis (2026). **Position:** pre-deal, Alto was a sub-scale niche player — custodian for ~$2bn AUC, ~30k investors (per Alto, 2026-06), and had just sold its CryptoIRA book (~$600M AUM) to Public for a reported $65M (2025-11, per Fortune/FintechFutures). Forge Trust brings ~2.7M+ custody-as-a-service accounts and ~$18.4bn IRA assets (per altstreet/company), vaulting the combined platform to >$20bn AUC, >60k private-market SDIRA accounts and >3M custody-as-a-service accounts (per Alto/Pulse2/wealthmanagement.com). Moat: regulatory charter + incumbent custody rails and partner relationships (analysis) — this is a buy-the-infrastructure move, not organic scaling.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal terms NOT disclosed — no purchase price, so no EV/AUC or EV/account multiple can be computed. "No data" on this transaction's multiple. Reference points for triangulation only:
- Schwab/Forge Global: $660M for the whole of Forge (marketplace + data + Forge Trust), 2025-11 ([[Charles Schwab to acquire Forge Global]]); Forge Trust is the carved-out custody sliver, so its value is a fraction of that $660M (analysis).
- Public/Alto CryptoIRA: ~$65M for ~$600M AUM ⇒ ~10.8% of AUC (`$65M / $600M = 0.108`), 2025-11 — but that was a growthy crypto book, not low-margin trust custody; not a clean comp.
- iTrustCapital: $1.3bn post-money (2022-01) was a frothy crypto-cycle private round, not a custody-AUC multiple — exclude as a valuation comp.
Distribution not computed (<3 comparable, like-for-like custody deals + undisclosed target price); qualitative only. For scale context, Alto's pre-deal ~$2bn AUC vs ~$20bn combined implies Forge Trust is ~9x Alto's prior book — the acquirer is much smaller than what it is buying.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Reverse-merger / digestion risk (biggest).** A Series B company (~$73–75M ever raised, per Pitchbook/Tracxn) absorbing a ~$18.4bn-asset, 2.7M-account, 40-year-old trust operation is buying something ~9x its own book. Integration, compliance and custody-operations execution risk is the central question the undisclosed deal structure hides — funding source (debt? new equity? earn-out?) is silent.
2. **Regulatory gate.** Closing needs South Dakota Division of Banking approval of a change of control of a state-chartered trust company; not pro forma, and a known failure mode for undercapitalized buyers of regulated custodians.
3. **Low-margin, run-off-prone base.** Legacy trust-custody accounts are fee-sensitive and sticky-but-low-revenue; much of the 2.7M "custody-as-a-service" base may be dormant/legacy, so headline account counts overstate monetizable value (analysis). Migration churn risk mirrors the Alto→Public crypto transition.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Consolidation, not a re-rating: Schwab is pruning a non-core custody unit it inherited only months earlier, and an alt-focused challenger is rolling up the plumbing to become the "category-defining" SDIRA-for-private-markets platform (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Alto can cross-sell private-market/VC/private-credit products into Forge Trust's installed base, AUC monetization rises; it breaks if the deal is funded with expensive debt/dilution and the acquired accounts churn or stay dormant. **Watch:** deal financing disclosure, South Dakota approval timing, and whether net private-market AUC (not raw account count) actually grows post-close.
