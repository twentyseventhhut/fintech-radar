# Context-enrichment: Yapily appoints Daniel Morgan as Head of Policy
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On/around **2026-10-13** Yapily — the London open-banking API infrastructure provider (founded 2017 by Stefano Vaccino; see [[Yapily brings bank account verification to Google Cloud in Europe]]) — announced it has appointed **Daniel (Dan) Morgan** as **Head of Policy**, tasked with leading policy and regulatory strategy across the UK and Europe, "supporting its open banking initiatives and product development." The item describes him as "previously Vice Chair of Blockchain for Europe."

**De-PR'd reality.** This is a single executive hire, not a product, deal or capability. No contract value, no headcount, no strategic commitment attaches to it. It is the kind of announcement that matters to a narrow audience (regulators, trade bodies, competitors) and almost no one else. The role itself is not new at Yapily — the company has run a public-policy function since at least **Nov 2021**, when it appointed **Maria Palmieri** as Head of Public Policy (Open Banking Expo, 3 Nov 2021). So this is a *succession/refresh* of an existing seat, framed as a fresh appointment.

**Why framed this way / what it reveals.** A policy-lead hire is a *tell*, not an event: it signals where a company thinks its near-term value is gated. Yapily's bottleneck is not technology (account verification, Pay-by-Bank and VRP are commoditised — [[Yapily brings bank account verification to Google Cloud in Europe]]) but **regulation and scheme governance**. The UK open-banking agenda has just shifted from "will it happen" to "who sets the rules and the price" — commercial VRP went live under UKPI on 2 Jun 2026, the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 is now law, and the FCA's Open Finance roadmap landed in Apr 2026. Hiring a senior policy voice is Yapily buying a seat at that table. (analysis)

**Identity caveat — do not over-claim.** I could not locate a primary source (Yapily blog / release) confirming this specific appointment; web retrieval for "Yapily Head of Policy" surfaces the 2021 Palmieri hire, not Morgan. Separately, the well-documented public **Dan Morgan** with a Ripple / Blockchain-for-Europe lineage has been **Policy Lead, Europe at Plaid** (a direct Yapily competitor) since ~2020. If that is the same person, this is a **competitor poach** — a more interesting signal than the PR frame. But the name is common and the "Vice Chair of Blockchain for Europe" descriptor is not independently confirmed, so treat the individual's exact pedigree as **open / unverified**.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Policy/regulatory-affairs is a contested arena among open-banking infra players precisely because the rules are being written now. The named participants funding **UKPI** (the industry-owned commercial-VRP scheme) include **GoCardless, TrueLayer, Yapily, Token.io, Moneyhub, Plaid, Mastercard Open Banking Services**, plus banks (Nationwide, NatWest) — see [[Connecting the Dots on variable recurring payments and open banking]]. Every one of these competes on scheme influence as much as on API quality.
- **Plaid** (US-scale, $8bn tender valuation Feb 2026) and **Tink** (Visa-owned) are the balance-sheet-heavy incumbents; Plaid has run a European policy function for years.
- **TrueLayer** (payments/A2A focus; acquired in3 2026) and **GoCardless** (Direct Debit + OB) are the closest UK-VRP-era peers.
- Yapily's position on the *policy* axis: **parity-to-catching-up**. It is a core UKPI funder and claims cVRP-pioneer / cVRP Working Group membership (its own product page, [[Connecting the Dots on variable recurring payments and open banking]]), so it already has standing; a senior hire is about *keeping* that voice, not acquiring it from zero.

**Why the landscape is this way (second order).** When a payment rail moves from bilateral bank deals to a single multilateral scheme (UKPI's MLA, a capped pence-per-transaction price fixed ~5 years), **the locus of competition shifts from sales to scheme design** — who sits on working groups, who shapes the price cap and the Wave-2 e-commerce timetable. For a sub-scale independent (~$13M est. revenue vs Plaid's ~$430M ARR), policy influence is one of the few arenas where it can punch above its balance sheet. That is the strategic logic of the hire. (analysis)

## [2] Company history / fit
Yapily's trajectory: founded 2017; ~$70M total funding incl. a $51M Series B (Sapphire Ventures, 2021 era); now claims profitability, 2,000+ bank connections across 19 UK/EU markets, and enterprise logos (Google, Adyen, Ant Group, Intuit QuickBooks, Pleo, Allica Bank) — all vendor self-claims, detailed and caveated in [[Yapily brings bank account verification to Google Cloud in Europe]]. On the policy track specifically: Maria Palmieri as Head of Public Policy (2021), prior exec hires from OBIE (Selene Brett), and standing as a UKPI funder and self-described cVRP pioneer.

**Why Yapily acts this way.** Its revenue mix is heavily exposed to **UK/EU regulated open-banking rails**, where commercial outcomes depend on scheme economics it does not fully control (the UKPI price cap, PSD3/FIDA, DUAA smart-data schemes). A company this exposed to regulation *must* staff policy senior and keep continuity — hence refreshing the seat. The appointment fits logically; it is simply low-magnitude.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Novelty: essentially none.** It is a personnel change into a pre-existing role. There is no new product, no new client, no disclosed strategy shift. "Traction" is not applicable — the only measurable outcome would be future regulatory wins (a favourable Wave-2 cVRP timetable, open-finance scheme design under DUAA), none of which exist yet.

**Where value-add could be real (1 level deeper).** If the hire is genuinely a senior figure poached from a competitor (the unconfirmed Plaid/Ripple angle), the value is **relationship and intelligence transfer** — knowing how the rival frames its FCA/HMT submissions — plus credibility in the UKPI/FCA process as Wave-2 and the first open-finance smart-data scheme (FCA/HMT discussion paper expected Q4 2026) are negotiated. If it is simply a backfill of Palmieri's seat, the value-add is continuity, not uplift. Either way, policy influence does **not** capture margin directly; it only *protects the right to compete* on rails whose price is capped and whose customer relationship (e.g. Google Cloud) sits with someone else. (analysis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Regulatory backdrop (the reason this hire exists):
- **Data (Use and Access) Act 2025** now law — enables sector **smart-data schemes** (energy, telecoms, transport, retail); FCA/HMT discussion paper on the **first open-finance scheme expected Q4 2026**; government targets 5+ smart-data schemes by 2030, 20+ by 2035 (DBT Smart Data Strategy).
- **FCA Open Finance roadmap** ("Our vision for a smart data future", 14 Apr 2026) maps open finance to 2030.
- **Commercial VRP / UKPI** live since **2 Jun 2026** — first new UK payment scheme since Faster Payments (2008); Wave 1 (utilities, government, charities, regulated FS) live, **Wave 2 (e-commerce) expected H2 2026**; FCA/PSR declined (Jan 2026) to prioritise a Competition Act probe of the centralised pricing, giving interim comfort. See [[Connecting the Dots on variable recurring payments and open banking]].

**Why the market goes this way / second-order.** The UK has deliberately moved open banking into a *scheme-governed, price-capped* regime and is extending the model to open finance via DUAA. That is good for rail *volume* but compresses rail *margins* — the pence-per-transaction cap is fixed ~5 years. **Counterintuitive effect:** regulation that legitimises the sector simultaneously commoditises the independents, raising consolidation pressure (Visa/Tink, Mastercard/Aiia pattern). A senior policy hire is a rational defensive move, but it does not change Yapily's structural question, which remains distribution and margin, not advocacy. Sentiment on the item itself: neutral-to-low; routine people-move.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** UK/EU open-banking API infrastructure — the rails that let licensed TPPs pull account data (AIS) and initiate account-to-account payments (PIS) under PSD2/the UK CMA order. Size: per Fortune Business Insights (via their market report, as of 2025), the global open-banking market was ~$35.3bn in 2025, projected ~$42.1bn in 2026, Europe ~31% share — treat as a broad secondary estimate, not a clean TAM. Usage in the UK (per OBL/industry statistics, via thepaymentsassociation.org and axis-intelligence, as of mid-2026): >19m active user connections and >40m open-banking payments/month; VRPs grew ~98% in 2025 and now ~13–16% of open-banking payment volume. Structure: fragmented at the TPP/aggregator layer sitting on top of concentrated bank rails — value is created in the connectivity/reliability/compliance middleware; barriers are regulatory authorisation, bank-API integration breadth, and switching costs once embedded. Why now (the real reason a Head of Policy hire matters): the UK's commercial model for open-banking payments is being set *right now*. Per PSR/UKPI (via regreportingdesk and openbankingtracker, as of 2026), the commercial VRP (cVRP) scheme went live 2 June 2026 with a single rulebook and shared commercial model (initial Wave-2 pricing proposals ~3–11p/txn, recommended 6–8p, trending toward ad valorem); per an FCA/PSR joint statement of 20 Jan 2026 regulators declined (at this stage) to open a Competition Act probe into UKPI's centralised access-fee pricing. On the EU side, FiDA (Financial Data Access) would extend open banking beyond payments. These are policy/regulatory outcomes, not product launches — which is why the protagonist is staffing policy.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: API coverage (banks/markets connected), payment success/conversion rate, PIS volume & VRP mandate base, and — increasingly — per-transaction economics under the new commercial model. Key players (UK/EU): TrueLayer, Tink (owned by Visa since the $2.2bn 2021 acquisition, per press), GoCardless (bank-debit + open-banking, acquired Nordigen), Plaid (US-led, expanding in UK/EU), plus Salt Edge, Bud, Banked (acquired by NAB, per [[National Australia Bank acquires open banking fintech Banked]]). Basis of competition: coverage + reliability + compliance, shifting toward commercial-model positioning and higher-value use cases (cVRP, verification). Recent moves: TrueLayer raised $27m led by CDP Venture Capital (Oct 2026, per Finovate) and has been acquisitive — [[TrueLayer to acquire Nordic open-banking rival Zimpler]], [[TrueLayer acquires Dutch consumer-credit fintech in3]]. Protagonist's position: mid-scale niche specialist, deliberately staying out of M&A — CEO Stefano Vaccino: "We prefer to remain on the sidelines... focus on organic and sustainable growth" (Tech.eu, 2 Oct 2026). Yapily names 2027–2028 (e-commerce cVRP + FiDA) as pivotal. Moat `(analysis)`: compliance/regulatory posture + embedded enterprise integrations (Google Cloud bank-account verification — [[Yapily brings bank account verification to Google Cloud in Europe]]; VRP — [[Connecting the Dots on variable recurring payments and open banking]]), not scale or network effects.

**Comps & multiples.** Yapily is private; no market cap → P/S and EV multiples are "no data" on a market basis. Round valuation only (NOT market cap):
- **Yapily:** last round Series B $51m (2021, Sapphire Ventures); FY2025 revenue £16.7m, up from £6.7m (~2.5x YoY), swung to a ~£355k profit from a £16.2m loss, 102 staff (Tech.eu, 2 Oct 2026). Secondary mark ~$40.3m (getlatka, 2025) is unverified and likely stale — flag `[UNSOURCED]` as a true valuation.
- **TrueLayer:** ~$700–750m round valuation (Oct 2024 $50m round at $700m; Tracxn cites $750m, 2026) — a private round mark, not a market multiple.
- **Plaid:** ~$13.4bn (Series D, Apr 2021) — stale; a down-round/secondary context has been reported in press, treat as dated.
- **Tink:** acquired by Visa for $2.2bn (2021) — one-off strategic M&A price, not a live multiple.
EV/Revenue arithmetic is NOT computable here — the only pair with both legs public (Yapily $700?m vs £16.7m) mixes a non-existent valuation with revenue, so no multiple. Distribution not computed, qualitative comparison: Yapily is an order of magnitude smaller than TrueLayer/Plaid/Visa-Tink on both revenue and raised capital — a profitable, capital-light niche player rather than a scale aggregator.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Commercial-model / pricing capture.** The cVRP economics are being set by UKPI/PSR (6–8p or ad valorem). If pricing lands too low to fund TPPs or too high to beat cards, the margin on Yapily's core PIS flow is dictated by a scheme Yapily doesn't control — second-order: its unit economics are a policy output, hence the Head of Policy hire is defensive, not optional.
2. **Dependence on others' rails + consolidation squeeze.** Yapily sits on bank APIs it doesn't own, and well-capitalised, acquisitive peers (TrueLayer, Visa/Tink, NAB/Banked) are consolidating coverage. Staying "on the sidelines" organically risks being out-scaled on bank/market breadth — the KPI that most drives enterprise selection.
3. **Regulatory-timeline execution risk.** Yapily's own thesis pins upside on 2027–2028 (e-commerce cVRP + FiDA). Both are regulator-paced and have slipped before; revenue durability before then rests on a thin ~£355k profit — little buffer if timelines drift.

**What this changes (idea-lens) `(analysis)`.** A Head-of-Policy hire at a mid-scale TPP is a signal that the UK/EU open-banking game is moving from "build connectivity" to "win the rulebook" — whoever shapes cVRP pricing and FiDA scope captures the economics. Falsifiable thesis: Yapily can stay independent and profitable through the 2027–2028 cVRP-e-commerce/FiDA inflection *without* a Series C. Trigger/what breaks it: a new raise (contradicting the stated stance), an acquisition offer accepted, or FY2026 revenue growth decelerating sharply below the 2.5x pace while peers out-consolidate. NOTE: the note states Morgan was "Vice Chair of Blockchain for Europe"; public sources instead surface Maria Palmieri as a prior Yapily policy head (2021) and a "Dan Morgan" as a former Plaid Europe policy lead — the specific appointment could not be independently confirmed, so treat the bio as per-the-note, unverified.
