SUMMIT, N.J. , Oct. 14, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Simplicity Group ("Simplicity"), a holistic financial planning firm combining the best of accumulation and protection strategies and solutions, today announced the acquisition of MAIA Wealth, a prominent Denver-based independent financial advisory firm. As part of the transaction, key members of the MAIA Wealth team will transition to operate under the new banner of Simplicity Wealth Advisors: Denver.
The strategic acquisition reinforces Simplicity's commitment to expanding its wealth management platform and delivering comprehensive wealth planning, investment management, and retirement solution strategies across the Rocky Mountain region.
"We are thrilled to welcome the talented professionals from MAIA Wealth to the Simplicity family," said Bruce Donaldson, Partner and CEO of Simplicity. "Their dedication to client-first advisory services and deep roots in the Denver community align perfectly with our mission. By integrating their regional expertise with Simplicity's vast national resources, institutional infrastructure, and technology, we are positioning Simplicity Wealth Advisors: Denver for exceptional growth and success."
Through this partnership, advisors transitioning to Simplicity Wealth Advisors: Denver will gain access to Simplicity's extensive platform, including advanced marketing, wealth technology, business succession planning, and expanded insurance and investment solutions designed to support high-net-worth individuals, families, and business owners.
"Joining forces with Simplicity represents an exciting new chapter for our firm and our clients," said Scott Marek. "As Simplicity Wealth Advisors: Denver, our team gains the scale, resources, and operational backing of a leading national firm while maintaining the personalized, high-touch relationships our clients rely on."
Chandler te Velde added, "Partnering with Simplicity enables us to significantly enhance our operational infrastructure and client resources. We are excited about the new tools and broader investment offerings this collaboration brings to our team and, most importantly, to the families we serve."
Desmond Dimond added, "This transition to Simplicity allows us to elevate our service offering and deliver even greater value to our clients. Gaining access to a broader suite of wealth management tools and protection strategies ensures we can meet our clients' evolving financial needs at every stage of their lives."

## Контекст

### [0] What happened (one-paragraph brief)
On 2026-10-14 Simplicity Group ("Simplicity"), a Summit, NJ-based holistic financial-planning and insurance/wealth consolidator (~$14B AUM), announced the acquisition of **MAIA Wealth**, a Denver-based independent RIA with roughly $500M–$1B AUM (press framing "nearly $1 billion"), ~43 employees, 5 offices and ~5,265 accounts. Terms were not disclosed (no price, AUM or headcount figures released). The MAIA team — co-founders **Scott Marek** and **Chandler te Velde**, plus **Desmond Dimond** — rebrands as **Simplicity Wealth Advisors: Denver**, extending Simplicity's wealth platform into the Rocky Mountain region. This is one of a back-to-back pair of October deals: a day later Simplicity also bought Chicago's Zen Wealth Management (see comp below).

### [1] Who are the players
- **Simplicity Group** — fast-growing US financial-services partnership founded 2016, combining wealth accumulation + insurance/protection products. Majority-owned by PE firms **SkyKnight Capital** and **Dragoneer Investment Group** (invested 2024). CEO **Bruce Donaldson**. Has completed ~70 acquisitions since 2016 (~63 in the last five years) — a serial roll-up / aggregator model offering acquired practices national marketing, wealthtech, succession planning and expanded insurance/investment solutions.
- **MAIA Wealth** — Denver RIA at 1401 17th St, independently owned/operated by Marek, te Velde and Dimond. Research-driven investment management plus tax, estate and risk planning for individuals, families and business owners.

### [2] Why it matters
- Deepens Simplicity's wealth-management footprint in a new geography (Denver / Rocky Mountains) and adds an HNW-oriented RIA book (~$0.5–1B AUM).
- Reinforces the industry's dominant 2026 theme: **PE-fuelled RIA consolidation at record pace**. Q1 2026 alone saw record deal activity ($1.67T transacted AUM per ECHELON; DeVoe counted 93 deals vs 75 a year earlier), with private equity involved in ~72% of Q1 deals. ECHELON projects ~475 deals in 2026, a new record. Simplicity is a textbook PE-backed aggregator riding that wave.
- The "rebrand under acquirer banner + retain founding advisors as partners" playbook (here Simplicity Wealth Advisors: Denver) is the standard retention/integration template.

### [3] Prior art / internal comps
- [[Simplicity Group acquires Chicago's Zen Wealth Management]] — SAME ACQUIRER, different target, announced ~1 day later (2026-10-15); confirms a back-to-back Denver-RIA + Chicago-tax-shop buying spree. Primary comp.
- [[Bill Harris launches digital RIA Evergreen Wealth]] — contrasting entry model (greenfield digital RIA build vs. roll-up acquisition) in the same US wealth-RIA space.
- [[Jump to acquire advisor transcription firm Mobile Assistant]] — adjacent US advisor-tech tuck-in, illustrating the broader wealth-advisory M&A environment.

### [4] Open questions / what to watch
- Undisclosed terms → true AUM and price multiple unknown; "nearly $1B" is marketing, SEC ADV range is $500M–$1B.
- Retention: will Marek/te Velde/Dimond and the ~43 staff stay post-earnout? Aggregator integrations often see advisor attrition.
- Pace: how many more Simplicity deals before year-end, and whether SkyKnight/Dragoneer push toward a recap given maturing PE cycles.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** PE-backed aggregator tuck-in in US RIA / wealth-management consolidation: Denver RIA MAIA Wealth (~$670M regulatory AUM, RIA since Apr-2018, per SEC IAPD/AdvisorFinder, via web) folds into Simplicity Group, rebranding as "Simplicity Wealth Advisors: Denver." The sector is in a record consolidation year: RIA M&A ran at a record pace in 2026 — Q1 alone ~142 deals with ~$1.67T in assets changing hands (+107% YoY), average ~$1.8bn AUM/deal (highest since 2021), with H1 ~262 deals vs 220 a year earlier and full-year pace toward ~500 (per Echelon / DeVoe, via InvestmentNews/WealthManagement, as of 2026). Structure: **fragmented at the small end** (thousands of sub-$1bn RIAs) but rapidly consolidating, with **consolidators ~50% of H1 deals** and **PE involved in >90% of significant RIA acquisitions** (per Echelon, via web). **Why now:** aging advisor principals need succession liquidity + weak organic growth push firms to sell, while PE capital and roll-up economics pull serial buyers in. **Why-ladder:** a ~$670M founder-led RIA can't build estate/tax/insurance scale solo → selling to an aggregator buys instant infrastructure + liquidity → Simplicity captures AUM-linked fees at low incremental cost and cross-sells its core annuity/insurance protection products → second-order: end-client relationship still travels with the advisor, so retention is the real asset bought.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: AUM, organic (net-new-asset) growth, adjusted EBITDA margin, advisor/client retention. Simplicity is an **insurance/annuity-distribution-rooted aggregator**, majority-owned by **SkyKnight Capital + Dragoneer** since Aug-2024, after **Lee Equity + Twin Bridge** (Oct-2020) and **Aquiline** before that — i.e. 15+ insurance-distribution subsidiaries plus a growing wealth arm (per Weil/Willkie/Mergr, via web). Peers in the PE-backed roll-up lane: Mariner, Mercer Advisors, Focus Partners, Carson, Merit, Wealthspire, Cerity Partners (Warburg), Allworth (Integrum). Basis of competition: deal terms + platform resources + cross-sell breadth, not price to end-clients. Recent Simplicity moves: a near-identical sibling tuck-in one day later — [[Simplicity Group acquires Chicago's Zen Wealth Management]] (2026-10-15, rebranded "Simplicity Tax Advisors") — a two-deals-in-two-days cadence. Position: **active serial acquirer; mid-tier in RIA aggregation but a leader in insurance/annuity distribution** `(analysis)`. Moat: distribution scale + product breadth (insurance + wealth) enabling cross-sell and switching costs for affiliated advisors; weak moat over the end-client relationship.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal terms **not disclosed** (no price, no MAIA revenue/EBITDA) — deal-specific multiple = **no data**; arithmetic cannot be shown (EV unknown). MAIA's ~$670M AUM places it in the **$500M–$1B band**, where 2024–Q2-2026 RIA deals priced around **~10.0x adjusted EBITDA** (range ~9–12x quality-adjusted; 2025 median deal ~11.6x; sub-$100M ~5.75x, $1bn+ ~13.5x — per adastraequity / ctacquisitions, via web). So MAIA is a materially **larger, mid-market RIA than the sub-scale Zen sibling**, likely priced mid-range rather than at the low end `(analysis)` — but valuation is un-assessable without disclosure. Internal comps (same wave; terms mostly undisclosed, so qualitative): [[Simplicity Group acquires Chicago's Zen Wealth Management]] (same buyer, same week), [[Waverly Advisors acquires Oregon's MCS Family Wealth Advisors]] (regional RIA tuck-in), [[Annex Wealth to acquire Milwaukee adviser Marietta Investment Partners]] (~$765M AUM tuck-in — comparable scale), [[CohnReznick to acquire Aldrich group, including $8B Aldrich Wealth]] (accounting-firm-eats-wealth variant). **In-line vs sector** on structure; valuation un-assessable → flag as a transparency gap, not a multiple outlier.

**Risk flags.**
- **Advisor/client retention (execution).** RIA value is the relationship, and MAIA is founder-led (Marek, Dimond, te Velde). If principals leave post-earnout, the ~$670M book and client relationships can walk, hollowing the rationale. Second-order: earn-outs are lengthening on harder retention metrics precisely because buyers price this risk.
- **Leverage / roll-up fragility.** Simplicity has flipped sponsors twice (Aquiline → Lee Equity → SkyKnight/Dragoneer); each flip layers leverage + exit urgency onto a stack of tuck-ins. A rate or market drawdown compresses AUM-linked fees and refinancing capacity simultaneously; if organic growth stalls, the model needs ever-more deals to show growth — a treadmill.
- **Cross-sell / suitability conflict.** Simplicity's economics lean on annuity/insurance product distribution; bolting a fiduciary RIA onto a product-distribution engine raises a best-interest question (are MAIA's clients steered toward in-house protection products?). Second-order: regulatory/reputational exposure if cross-sell is aggressive.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Two Simplicity tuck-ins in two days — a mid-market RIA (MAIA, ~$670M) plus a small tax shop (Zen) — signals an **accelerating serial roll-up spanning both wealth and tax verticals**, consistent with a record-volume/flat-multiple consolidation wave where sponsors win on *count of deals*, not premium platform buys. Falsifiable thesis: if the model works, Simplicity keeps buying $250M–$1B RIAs through 2026 and eventually discloses aggregate AUM / advisor-count and organic cross-sell growth; it breaks if a cluster of acquired founders exit at earn-out end, or if rates/markets force a debt-driven pause. Trigger to watch: next tuck-in cadence and any disclosed platform-level AUM milestone.
