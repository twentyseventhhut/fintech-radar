 Структуры сети фитнес-клубов DDX приобрели здание в центре Москвы 
Структура девелоперской компании Vesper продала здание на 6 тыс. кв. м в жилом комплексе Lucky в центре Москвы, обнаружил “ Коммерсант ” в сервисе Asseto. Там сказано, что новым владельцем здания, где расположен термальный комплекс и фитнес-клуб DDX, стало ООО «Проект Лаки». По данным СПАРК, ООО «Проект Лаки» принадлежит закрытому паевому инвестфонду «СГ-Инвестиции» под управлением ООО УК «Промфинактив» (с долей в 30%), фонду «Биге» под управлением АО «Контрада капитал» (22,62%), Татьяне Ситниковой (30%), Андрею Суздальцеву (10,38%), Ольге Зиновьевой (4%) и Оксане Астаховой (3%). Фонд «Биге», в свою очередь, также владеет долями в ООО «Илон», которое управляет сетью фитнес-клубов DDX, среди учредителей которого Иван Ситников, а также основатели Росевробанка (в конце 2018 года объединился с Совкомбанком) Илья Бродский и Андрей Суздальцев. Господин Суздальцев также владеет долями в ГК «Росевросити», которая строит жилые комплексы.
По оценке старшего директора департамента оценки и консалтинга CMWP Антона Калистратова, рыночная стоимость проданного здания может составлять 1,2–1,8 млрд руб. Опрошенные изданием эксперты говорят, что ранее у структур, связанных с DDX, не было подобных сделок по приобретению недвижимости в собственность. Сеть развивается преимущественно в торгцентрах на арендуемых площадях, уточняет управляющий партнер FitnessData Максим Боровиков.
Для фитнес-операторов такие сделки скорее исключение, подтверждает директор по стратегическому развитию «Магазина Магазинов» Вячеслав Кацегоров. По итогам сентября 2026 года в России действовало около 10,7 тыс. фитнес-объектов, следует из подсчетов Национального фитнес-сообщества. Около 89% из них, или примерно 9,5 тыс., работают на арендованных площадях, уточняют в ассоциации.
Большинство фитнес-сетей предпочитают аренду из-за низкого порога входа и гибкости масштабирования, отмечает управляющий партнер RedStone Индира Шафикова. Аренда сегодня формирует до 35% расходов фитнес-объекта и остается одной из крупнейших статей затрат, говорит глава Национального фитнес-сообщества Елена Силина.
В отдельных случаях фитнес-оператору целесообразнее приобрести площадку и адаптировать ее под свои требования, чем инвестировать сопоставимые средства в арендованное помещение, говорит директор департамента торговой недвижимости NF Group Евгения Хакбердиева. Например, когда обустройство объектов требует более существенных вложений в инженерные системы и неотделимые улучшения, в частности в клубы с бассейнами.
Для новых собственников это не обычный проект, а венчурная инвестиция, так как само здание соединило в себе четыре формата: DDX NERO, термы, бани и бар-ресторан, отмечает управляющий партнер «Ванчугов и партнеры» Алексей Ванчугов. Крупные институциональные инвесторы редко вкладываются в здания с фитнес-клубами, резюмирует Евгения Хакбердиева.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)

A structure tied to Russia's largest low-cost fitness chain **DDX Fitness** bought the ~6,000 sqm building housing a DDX NERO club + thermal/bath/bar-restaurant complex inside the **Lucky residential complex** (2nd Zvenigorodskaya St., central Moscow). Seller: a structure of developer **Vesper**. Buyer: **OOO "Proekt Lucky"** — owned by closed-end unit investment fund "SG-Investitsii" (UK Promfinaktiv, 30%), fund "Bige" (Kontrada Capital, 22.62%), and four individuals (Sitnikova 30%, Suzdaltsev 10.38%, Zinovieva 4%, Astakhova 3%). Fund "Bige" also holds stakes in **OOO "Ilon"**, the DDX operator. Spotted by Kommersant via the Asseto service; disclosed via SPARK. No price announced by the parties — CMWP's Anton Kalistratov *estimates* 1.2–1.8bn RUB. No official deal date/terms released.

**Why structured this way / what it reveals:** This is an owner-buys-own-premises transaction, not a third-party M&A. The overlapping shareholder (Andrey Suzdaltsev — co-founder of DDX via Rosevrobank, also in developer GK "Rosevrocity"; and Bige-in-Ilon) means the real economics are an internal real-estate consolidation among related parties, not an arm's-length acquisition. The absence of a disclosed price is normal for a related-party buyout and makes the 1.2–1.8bn estimate the only (external, wide-range) anchor. Framed as a "venture investment" (Vanchugov) because the asset bundles four formats (fitness, thermae, banya, bar) — i.e. it is NOT a standard fitness-club purchase and buyers are betting on a mixed-use hospitality yield, not just gym membership.

## [1] Competitors / peers (Russian commercial-real-estate / fitness)

Fitness operators almost never own freehold: ~89% (≈9,500 of ~10,700 objects, Sep 2026, National Fitness Community) operate on leased space. DDX itself grew on leased mall space (FitnessData's Borovikov: no prior property-acquisition deals). Peer context in the corpus — closed-end/institutional funds buying Moscow commercial real estate: [[VIM Sberezheniy fund buys Poklonka Place business quarter]] (institutional ZPIF buying a business quarter), [[VTB to sell former Otkritie office to Wildberries-Russ]] (large Moscow office transfer), [[RZD's fourth Riga Station auction fails without bids]] (illiquid Moscow commercial asset). Fitness-as-fintech adjacency: [[Финтехно Russian fitness clubs shift to SBP subscription billing]] — DDX is the named driver of the recurring-billing (SBP subscription) shift.

**Why the lay of the land is this way:** Leasing dominates because of low entry cost and scaling flexibility (RedStone's Shafikova); rent is up to 35% of a club's cost base (NFS's Silina). Buying only pays when the fit-out is capital-heavy (pools, engineering, non-removable improvements) — exactly the NERO + thermae bundle here (NF Group's Khakberdieva). Second-order: by owning the premises the DDX-linked owners capture the real-estate upside themselves instead of paying a landlord, but they also take on illiquid, concentrated single-asset risk that institutional investors avoid (Khakberdieva: large institutions rarely invest in buildings with fitness clubs).

## [2] Company history / fit

DDX Fitness = largest Russian chain by club count: ~152 clubs/54 cities earlier in 2026, ~164 clubs/59 cities by June 2026, plan 200+; >900k members. 2025 revenue 17.6bn RUB (+83% YoY). Founders via Rosevrobank (merged into Sovcombank end-2018): Ilya Brodsky, Andrey Suzdaltsev; CEO/founder Ivan Sitnikov. **Gazprombank** (via "AAA Capital Management") took an ~11.1% stake in 2024. In March 2026 the operator OOO "Ilon" placed its debut bond (1bn RUB, upsized to 1.25bn; coupon ~17.7%, YTM ~19.21%, ACRA BBB+; arrangers Alfa, Gazprombank, Sovcombank, Sber). Fit: logical — a scaling, now bond-funded operator consolidating a flagship capital-intensive mixed-use asset into related-party ownership.

**Why the company acts this way:** DDX's model is asset-light leasing for speed; freehold is reserved for the exceptional, fit-out-heavy flagship where leasing a comparable spec would cost nearly as much. The related-party fund structure (Suzdaltsev/Bige on both sides) suggests this is also a balance-sheet/real-estate play by the founders' broader group (Rosevrocity develops residential) — monetizing/retaining a trophy asset rather than a pure gym-expansion move.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction

Genuinely unusual: a rare freehold purchase by a chain that is otherwise 100% lessee. Traction is real on the operating side (revenue +83%, 160+ clubs, debut bond oversubscribed), but the *novelty of THIS deal* is narrow — it is one flagship building, a related-party consolidation, no disclosed price, and it does not change DDX's core lease-based expansion model.

**Why value-add is / isn't real:** The margin capture here is real-estate (owner keeps rent + any appreciation) plus the mixed-use hospitality yield — but it is a one-off, not a strategic pivot to a property portfolio. Who captures the margin: the founders' related funds, not DDX members or the operating company's creditors directly. What breaks it: single-asset illiquidity and the fact that "institutions rarely buy fitness buildings" means a thin resale market.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment

Expect this to stay an exception, not a trend: experts (Boroviko, Katsegorov) frame freehold buys as rare for fitness. DDX's forward story is club expansion (200+) and capital-markets presence (bonds), not real-estate accumulation. Regulatory/market backdrop: high Russian rates make bond-funded leasing expensive, marginally strengthening the "own vs rent" case for capital-heavy sites. 

**Fintech relevance (candid):** Thin/indirect. DDX touches fintech via (a) its SBP recurring-subscription billing leadership ([[Финтехно Russian fitness clubs shift to SBP subscription billing]]) and (b) its debut bond / Gazprombank ownership. But THIS item is a commercial-real-estate related-party buyout with no payments, lending, or fintech-product angle. It is tagged real-estate-fintech only loosely.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a Russian consumer-discretionary / commercial-real-estate (CRE) event, not a fintech deal: a DDX-linked fund structure bought the ~6,000 m² building (DDX NERO + thermal spa + banya + bar-restaurant) it occupies in the Lucky complex, central Moscow, from a Vesper structure. CMWP estimates market value at RUB 1.2–1.8bn (per Anton Kalistratov, CMWP, via mergers.ru, 2026-10-09). Sector size: the Russian gyms/health-&-fitness market booked ~$1.1bn revenue in 2025, 3.2% CAGR 2020–25, +5.2% in 2025 (per MarketLine/Research&Markets secondary citations, as of 2025). Russia had ~10,700 fitness objects as of Sept 2026, of which ~89% (~9,500) operate on leased space (National Fitness Community, via mergers.ru). Structure: highly fragmented and almost entirely asset-light/lease-based — owning the box is the exception, not the model. "Why now": the deal is driven less by a fitness-sector cycle than by a related-party real-estate play — the buyer fund "Bige" (Kontrada Capital) also holds stakes in OOO "Ilon", which runs DDX; co-founders tie back to the former Rosevrobank (merged into Sovcombank, 2018) and to Rosevrocity, a residential developer. Read as an insider/own-the-dirt transaction, not an arm's-length institutional CRE acquisition.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for fitness: club count, membership/yield per club, and — critically — rent as a share of opex (up to ~35%, per Elena Silina, National Fitness Community). Key players named around the market: World Class (premium), and DDX itself (mid-market, mall-based). Basis of competition: location/footfall and membership price, not property ownership. Protagonist position: DDX develops "primarily in shopping centres on leased space" (Maxim Borovikov, FitnessData, via mergers.ru) — so owning this asset is an outlier for the chain, justified (per NF Group's Evgenia Khakberdieva) only where fit-out needs heavy capex in engineering/non-removable improvements (e.g. pools/thermal), making "buy-and-adapt" cheaper than funding improvements on a leased box. Moat: none at the sector level; the edge here is captive-asset control of a bespoke multi-format site `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** No listed company, no round, no revenue/EBITDA disclosed — trading multiples **not applicable**. Internal comps: semsearch is down (OpenRouter 402); grep over `News/` surfaced no Russian fitness or Moscow-CRE precedents — the corpus is Western fintech, so **no in-base comparables**. Only usable arithmetic is implied price/m²: RUB 1.2–1.8bn ÷ 6,000 m² = **~RUB 200k–300k/m²**. Context (web, as of 2026): completed Moscow office sells at ~RUB 548k/m²; central-district (CAO) premium product runs RUB 600k–1,500k/m². The implied ~200–300k/m² sits **well below** prime-office pricing — consistent with a leisure/mixed-use asset (less liquid, narrower buyer pool) rather than a premium office, i.e. **in-line to cheap** for its category, not a rich print. Treat CMWP's RUB 1.2–1.8bn as a valuer's range, not a confirmed transaction price (price not disclosed) — `[UNSOURCED]` actual consideration.

**Risk flags.**
- **Asset illiquidity / narrow buyer pool.** NF Group's Khakberdieva states large institutional investors rarely buy fitness-anchored buildings; a single-tenant, four-format leisure box is hard to re-let or exit — any distress forces a thin market and a wide bid-ask.
- **Related-party / concentration risk.** Owner fund "Bige" also owns the operator (OOO "Ilon"/DDX); the building's value is effectively collateralised by one captive tenant it controls — valuation and rent are circular, not market-tested, so an outside appraisal (and the RUB 1.2–1.8bn range) may not clear in an arm's-length sale.
- **Discretionary-demand + rates headwind.** Fitness is discretionary; CBR held the key rate at 14% (Sept 2026, down from 15% early-2026 but still restrictive), and Russian consumer spending has been soft/choppy (retail −1.9% in Jan 2026, only "moderate" growth in H2). High carry cost on a RUB 1–2bn illiquid asset against cyclical membership revenue compresses the margin of safety.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` One-off, not a sector signal: this is an owner-operator internalising real estate for a capex-heavy wet/thermal format, not the start of a buy-vs-lease shift — 89% of the market stays on leases for a reason (low entry cost, scaling flexibility). Falsifiable thesis: if asset-light leasing remains dominant, expect **no** follow-on freehold acquisitions by DDX or peers; the trigger that would break it is a sustained fall in Moscow CRE prices or rates making freehold cheaper than rent at ~35% of opex — not visible today.
