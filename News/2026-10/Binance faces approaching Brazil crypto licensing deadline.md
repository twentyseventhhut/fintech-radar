# Binance files for Brazil crypto license ahead of Friday deadline

Latest NewsPublished Oct 27, 2026

The exchange is delisting 22 crypto assets for Brazilian users and hopes talks with the central bank will allow it to restore some services.

Copy link Copied to clipboard Copy link

Binance has applied for a cryptocurrency service provider license in Brazil, seeking to maintain its operations in one of Latin America’s largest crypto markets ahead of a regulatory deadline on Friday, Oct. 30.

The exchange submitted its application to the Central Bank of Brazil on Friday, Oct. 23, through its local entity, BBrasil, according to a statement shared with Cointelegraph.

The company said the filing would allow Brazilian users to continue accessing crypto assets, stablecoin trading, payments and investment services.

Binance has also announced changes for Brazilian users effective Tuesday, including restrictions on crypto lending, margin trading and other services. The exchange is also delisting 22 crypto assets in the country.

“We support a regulated market that considers the characteristics of virtual assets, so that access to technology and innovation continues to advance, but we hope that the ongoing dialogue with the Central Bank will allow us to offer, in the near future, other products that were discontinued to comply with current regulations,” said Thiago Sarandy, Binance’s general manager in the country.

Brazil requires existing crypto service providers to submit authorization applications by Oct. 30 to continue operating under transitional arrangements. Filing an application does not constitute regulatory approval.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** Binance filed for a crypto-asset service provider license with the Central Bank of Brazil (BCB) on 23 October 2026, through its local entity BBrasil, ahead of the 30 October 2026 statutory deadline. The headline framing ("approaching deadline") is already overtaken by events: as of 27 October the exchange has *applied*, not merely been "facing" the cutoff. Alongside the filing, Binance imposed service restrictions on Brazilian users effective 27 October (curbing crypto lending, margin trading and other products) and is delisting 22 crypto assets for Brazil. GM Thiago Sarandy framed the cuts as temporary and tied to "ongoing dialogue" with the BCB. Filing an application does NOT equal approval — Binance keeps operating only under transitional arrangements while the BCB reviews. (Source: cointelegraph.com in-note; corroborated by crypto.news, cryptonomist, coinfomania.)

**[1] The regulatory regime.** The deadline stems from BCB Resolutions 519, 520 and 521, published 10 November 2025, effective 2 February 2026. Existing VASPs got a 270-day transition window ending 30 October 2026 to file for authorization. Resolution 520 defines three VASP categories — intermediary, custodian, broker. Requirements: client-asset segregation, independent AML/sanctions assurance report from a CVM-registered auditor, cybersecurity controls, and tiered minimum capital (~R$37.2M / roughly US$2M–6.7M depending on category). Crucially the regime is user-based, not incorporation-based: any firm serving Brazilian users is in scope. From ~6 November 2026, licensed Brazilian banks and payment institutions are barred from transacting with unauthorized providers — the real enforcement teeth. Firms that miss the window must cease virtual-asset operations or transfer clients to a licensed entity. (Sources: fcm.law, riotimesonline, gokhshtein, cryptonews.net/CertiK.)

**[2] Why it matters for Binance specifically.** Brazil is one of LatAm's largest crypto markets (CertiK cites a ~US$319B market). Binance has a fraught enforcement history here: a 2023 Federal Police / CVM investigation over allegedly offering crypto derivatives despite a 2020 CVM stop order, and a congressional CPI that recommended indicting CZ and local executives for fraud, unlicensed securities trading, money laundering and tax evasion (Binance denied offering derivatives in Brazil). Against that backdrop, a clean BCB authorization would materially normalize Binance's largest LatAm footprint; a rejection or protracted review would be a serious market-access setback. The pre-emptive delisting of 22 assets and the shutdown of lending/margin signal Binance is trimming the exact product lines that drew prior scrutiny to improve approval odds.

**[3] Competitive / comparative frame.** Mercado Bitcoin — Brazil's largest domestic exchange — is best positioned: it was the first LatAm platform to secure a BCB payment-institution license (June 2023, e-money issuer) and recently took a US$20M Tether investment, so it enters the VASP regime with regulatory and capital runway. Foxbit is another established local incumbent. Among foreign players, Coinbase already launched DEX trading in Brazil (Nov 2025) explicitly under the new licensing/reporting rules, and Ripple applied for a Brazil VASP license alongside expanded payments (Mar 2026). Binance filing just a week before the deadline makes it a comparative *laggard* among majors — consistent with its history of late/reactive compliance. See related DB notes: [[Brazil's central bank bans crypto settlement in cross-border payments]], [[Coinbase debuts DEX trading in Brazil]], [[Ripple deepens Brazil commitment with expanded payments offering]], [[Binance secures full FSRA licenses in Abu Dhabi]], [[Binance and Pomelo partner on crypto cards in Brazil]].

**[4] What to watch.** (a) Whether the BCB accepts the BBrasil filing and on what timeline — application != approval. (b) Whether the delistings/product cuts are reversed after dialogue, as Sarandy hopes, or become permanent. (c) Spillover from the unresolved criminal/CPI matters into the licensing decision. (d) The 6 November banking-cutoff: any unauthorized rival losing bank rails is near-term share up for grabs. (e) Binance's broader 2025–26 compliance arc globally (Abu Dhabi/Dubai VASP wins) suggesting a pivot to regulated-market posture.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector context — Brazil/LatAm crypto exchange regulation.** Brazil is Latin America's largest crypto market (~43.7% of regional crypto value in Q2 2026; the market is sized at ~$318.8B per BCB framing). Under Central Bank of Brazil (BCB) Resolutions 519/520/521 — effective 2 February 2026 — virtual asset service providers (VASPs) fall under formal licensing and supervision for the first time. The 30 October 2026 deadline sits 270 days after the effective date: existing providers that file an authorization application by that date may keep operating under transitional arrangements during review. Filing is NOT approval — the BCB still assesses whether each applicant meets requirements, a process that can run well past the filing date.

**Binance's move.** Binance filed with the BCB on 23 October 2026 via its local entity BBrasil, backed by a CertiK technical certification covering ~900 compliance items. To comply ahead of the deadline it restricted eight products from 27 October (crypto lending, margin trading, mining services, token launch programs, among others) and delisted 22 assets for Brazilian users (including Verge/XVG, Ethena USDe/USDE, Secret/SCRT). Core spot, stablecoin trading, payments and investment services continue; management (GM Thiago Sarandy) signaled hope that ongoing dialogue with the BCB will let it restore discontinued products later.

**Market share / positioning.** Binance is the primary exchange for 50%+ of LatAm crypto users and, after the post-US-election rally, its regional share rose back above 50% for the first time since June 2022 — partly as Bitso's share fell to ~17% while Mercado Bitcoin held ~12%. Mercado Bitcoin remains the leading home-grown Brazilian exchange. Since 2022 local players had been eroding Binance's lead before this 2026 rebound. Globally Binance remains #1 CEX at ~38–39% spot volume share (CoinGecko), though gradually ceding ground to rivals like MEXC and Bitget.

**In-base comps.** Peers navigating the same Brazil VASP regime: Ripple deepened its Brazil payments push with a VASP license application (2026-03); Coinbase launched DEX trading in Brazil explicitly citing the new BCB licensing/reporting rules (2025-11); Crypto.com secured a conditional VASP approval. In-base research note "Binance leads centralized exchange volume with 38.3% share" (2026-02, CoinGecko) corroborates its global dominance baseline.

**IR grounding (Binance filed materials).** Binance's 2025 End-of-Year Report ("State of the Blockchain: 2025 Year In Review") reports 300M+ registered users and ~$145T all-products trading volume for 2025; the 2024 report had logged 250M+ ("quarter of a billion") users with +47% YoY registered-user growth — framing Brazil as one of the largest single-country pools within that global base. The MGX $2B investment (Mar 2025) press materials cite "over 260 million registered users and surpassing $100 trillion in cumulative trading volume," underscoring the scale at risk if a major market like Brazil were lost. Sources: 2025 EOY Report https://drive.google.com/file/d/1qozZQu7OAX51_X8zLtP9jtQD-6r4G2yt/view ; MGX release https://drive.google.com/file/d/1MGpAXrUtHUricZfcZOUOAPDTff9dKNS3/view

**Risk flags.**
- **Regulatory-approval risk:** filing ≠ license; BCB review outcome is open-ended and could still curtail or bar operations.
- **Product/revenue drag:** forced delisting of 22 assets + suspension of lending/margin/mining/token-launch removes higher-margin activity and may push users to competitors or offshore.
- **Competitive share erosion:** restrictions hand an opening to compliant locals (Mercado Bitcoin) and licensed entrants (Coinbase, Crypto.com, Ripple) in Binance's single-largest LatAm market.
- **Precedent/contagion:** a strict Brazil regime (and any adverse ruling) sets a template other LatAm regulators may copy, pressuring Binance's 50%+ regional franchise.
- **Political/legal uncertainty:** reporting flags political uncertainty around the framework; transitional-arrangement terms could tighten.

## Earnings Review

<!-- enrichment:earnings_review -->
Binance is a private company and does not disclose Brazil-specific revenue, so this is a regulatory/licensing story rather than an earnings print. The scale figures below are Binance's own filed materials where noted, with the Brazil-specific share/volume numbers coming from third-party estimates (flagged as such).

From Binance's own disclosures: its 2025 Year In Review ("State of the Blockchain," published 2026-01-07) reports Fiat & P2P volume up 38% year-on-year in 2025, and states Binance "consistently handled between one-third and nearly half" of trading volume across 32 global exchanges studied by independent researchers. Its 2024 Year In Review cited 1,000+ payment methods, 125+ fiat currencies, and 28% more Fiat & P2P users year-on-year. Per the March 2025 MGX investment disclosure, Binance has 260M+ registered users globally and $100T+ cumulative trading volume. None of these company figures break out Brazil or LatAm.

Brazil-specific scale (third-party estimates, not Binance-filed): Brazil is Latin America's largest crypto market, reportedly ~77% of regional activity (May 2025). Binance has historically been the dominant exchange there — press estimates put its Brazil transaction share near 79% in early 2024, easing to just above 50% in 2025 as Mercado Bitcoin (~12%) and Bitso (~17%) gained ground. Kaiko-type data cited in press put Binance's LATAM-denominated volume at ~$8.4B for Jan-May 2025, its second-strongest four-month stretch since 2022. Binance became the first exchange licensed as a broker in Brazil in January 2025.

Commercial read on the licensing outcome: Brazil's Central Bank (Resolutions 519/520, Normative Instruction 704) requires all VASPs to file their first-phase authorization application by October 30, 2026. Binance filed through its local entity BBrasil on October 23, 2026 — ahead of the deadline — but filing is not approval; its longer-term standing depends on Central Bank review. Ahead of the deadline Binance has already delisted 22 tokens and, from October 27, restricted eight services for Brazilian residents (Loans, Binance Pool, Cloud Mining, Margin, Launchpool, Megadrop, HODLer Airdrops, Alpha 2.0). Commercially, authorization would protect Binance's single largest LatAm revenue base and its first-mover licensed status; a failure or forced restructuring would cede share in the region's biggest market to local rivals Mercado Bitcoin and Bitso, who are already licensed/compliant and gaining ground.
