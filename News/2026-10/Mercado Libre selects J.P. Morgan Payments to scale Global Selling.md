Key takeaways 
J.P. Morgan Payments is supporting leading Latin America e-commerce marketplace’s Global Selling program, which enables U.S. businesses to sell across Latin America.
Mercado Libre is leveraging Embedded Payments from J.P. Morgan Payments for its U.S. sellers.
The solution was designed to improve the cross-border payment experience for Mercado Libre’s sellers by simplifying cross-border payment management and improving operational efficiency.
Cross-border marketplace flows can introduce complexity across onboarding, funding movement, and seller pay-outs—especially when programs expand quickly across geographies. For marketplaces, embedded payments can streamline complex multi-party cross-border transactions, simplify payment operations, and improve the seller experience.
 Mercado Libre , the leading e-commerce platform based in Latin America, selected J.P. Morgan Payments to support the continued growth of its Global Selling program, which enables U.S.-based businesses to sell across Argentina, Brazil, Mexico, Chile, Colombia and Uruguay. By leveraging our Embedded Payments solution, Mercado Libre can streamline cross-border payments and accelerate onboarding to J.P. Morgan Payments deposit services for eligible U.S. sellers as the program continues to scale.
“J.P. Morgan Payments brought the global scale, security and infrastructure we needed to support cross-border fund flows between Latin America and the U.S.,” said Gregorio Bekes, Financial Planning & Treasury Senior Vice President at Mercado Libre. “By creating an integrated experience for onboarding and cross-border payment management within our platform, this collaboration is helping us streamline payment operations and support the continued growth of our Global Selling program.”
Our Embedded Payments solution helps accelerate sellers’ setup for payments and financial services and makes it easier for sellers to receive and manage funds across borders. In an initial pilot, Mercado Libre onboarded U.S. sellers to J.P. Morgan Payments using our embedded Know Your Customer (KYC) and Know Your Business (KYB) capabilities, reducing friction in the financial-services onboarding process and helping speed time to activation. Mercado Libre is also leveraging our ability to receive bulk cross-border payments through ACH payouts infrastructure, helping move marketplace funds from Latin America to U.S. sellers.
"We’re excited to support Mercado Libre’s well-established platform to simplify their client and customer experience with the expertise, security and resilience we are known for,” said Ryan Schmiedl, Global Head of Embedded Finance & Solutions at J.P. Morgan Payments. “Marketplace sellers can keep their whole experience within Mercado Libre’s platform, from opening a bank account to receiving and moving funds from sales proceeds, for a frictionless end-to-end solution.”

# Context-enrichment: Mercado Libre selects J.P. Morgan Payments to scale Global Selling
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

**FRESHNESS: fresh.** No prior corpus note covers this MELI–JPM tie-up. Adjacent corpus: [[MercadoLibre bets on AI and digital banking]], [[MercadoLibre's Argentina loan delinquency rate rises sharply]], [[MercadoLibre founder Galperin steps down as CEO]], [[J.P. Morgan report on Latin America's cross-border payments growth]], [[Mirakl and J.P. Morgan Payments to power enterprise agentic commerce]]. This is a distinct new partnership, not a reprint.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- MELI picked **J.P. Morgan Payments' Embedded Payments** solution to support its **Global Selling** program — the reverse-logistics flow where **U.S.-based sellers** list on MELI marketplaces across Argentina, Brazil, Mexico, Chile, Colombia and Uruguay. The job JPM does: onboard those U.S. sellers to **J.P. Morgan deposit accounts**, run **embedded KYC/KYB**, and push **bulk cross-border payouts via ACH** to move sale proceeds from LatAm back to the U.S. sellers.
- **Live vs announced:** only an **initial pilot** is confirmed live (MELI "onboarded U.S. sellers... in an initial pilot"). No seller count, no volume, no pricing, no go-live date for full scale. Everything else is "as the program continues to scale" — i.e. intent, not traction.
- **Why structured this way (→ why it matters):** This is embedded *deposit + payout* plumbing, not a MELI-wide payments switch. JPM is NOT touching Mercado Pago (MELI's own $101B/quarter payments engine). It is bolted onto one narrow, outbound corridor — **paying foreign (U.S.) sellers**. MELI keeps its own fintech rails for everything LatAm-facing and buyer-facing; it rents JPM only for the piece it structurally can't do cheaply: being a **U.S.-domiciled bank that can open U.S. accounts and run U.S. ACH + KYB on American businesses**. The PR's "global scale, security, infrastructure" framing hides how small the surface area is.
- Timed to **Money20/20 (Las Vegas, Oct 18–21, 2026)** — a joint conference session (MELI, BILL, Walmart, JPM) is the real launch vehicle. Classic conference-week client-story PR.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- Direct rails for cross-border marketplace **seller payouts**: **Payoneer** (Mass Payouts, 190+ countries, built exactly for paying marketplace sellers), **Stripe Connect** (135+ currencies but cross-border payout reach is gated to US/UK/EEA/Canada/Switzerland), **Adyen for Platforms**, **Trolley / Tipalti** (mass disbursement specialists), **PayPal/Hyperwallet**. JPM Embedded Payments is the bank-balance-sheet entrant; it also partners **Thunes (Xpedite Remit)** for last-mile reach.
- MELI's position: it's the **buyer** of this capability, not a rail competitor. For JPM, this is a marquel logo in its push (per Tearsheet) to be "marketplace infrastructure" alongside its **Mirakl** ([[Mirakl and J.P. Morgan Payments to power enterprise agentic commerce]]) and **BILL/Walmart/Unum** embedded-finance client roster.
- **Why the land lies this way (→ 2nd order):** The win for JPM is the **deposit account**, not the payout fee. Payoneer/Trolley move money but don't hold it; JPM onboards the U.S. seller onto a **J.P. Morgan deposit account**, capturing balances and the full banking relationship. The real margin isn't the ACH rail (commoditized, cheap) — it's **owning the seller's U.S. bank account** and cross-sell. That's why a bank, not a fintech, wins this logo.

## [2] Company history / fit
- MELI is LatAm's e-commerce + fintech flywheel: **Q2 2026 revenue >$10B**, Mercado Pago **TPV $101B (+56% YoY)**, 88M fintech MAU, AUM $23B. It runs full digital banks and is pushing AI ([[MercadoLibre bets on AI and digital banking]]).
- **Cross-border is a growth priority:** FX-neutral cross-border GMV +60% YoY, China fulfillment volume +170% QoQ. Global Selling (foreign sellers into LatAm) is the mirror of that. Paying those foreign sellers reliably is the bottleneck.
- **Why MELI acts this way:** Mercado Pago is optimized for *LatAm* money movement and is a competitive moat MELI will never outsource. But a U.S. seller needs a *U.S.* bank account and U.S.-side compliance — MELI is not a U.S. bank and building that is slow, capital-heavy, and off-strategy. Renting JPM is the cheap, fast way to unblock Global Selling without diluting its own fintech stack. Fits logically; it is deliberately narrow.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty: low.** Embedded KYC/KYB + ACH payouts + deposit onboarding is a productized JPM offering already sold to BILL, Unum, Mirakl. Nothing technically new; this is a **new logo**, not a new capability.
- **Traction: thin.** "Initial pilot," zero disclosed numbers (sellers, volume, activation time). "Reducing friction / speed time to activation" are unquantified PR claims.
- **Why value-add is real but bounded:** For MELI it genuinely removes a hard build (U.S. banking/compliance). For JPM the durable value is **deposits + relationship**, not the transaction fee. BUT the margin JPM captures is capped because it only touches the *outbound-to-U.S.* leg; MELI keeps the lucrative LatAm buyer/seller economics in Mercado Pago. JPM is the plumber, not the platform. (analysis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Watch for a disclosed **seller count / payout volume** to tell pilot from scale; absent that, treat as announcement-stage. Expansion likely tracks MELI's cross-border GMV (+60% YoY) and China-corridor surge.
- Backdrop: JPM is systematically assembling a marketplace/embedded-finance franchise (Mirakl agentic commerce, Thunes reach, LatAm cross-border report). This logo reinforces that narrative more than it moves either company's P&L.
- **Risk / 2nd order:** Low strategic risk, low differentiation. The counterintuitive read: **MELI deepening dependence on a U.S. megabank for a growth corridor** is a mild strategic concession — but a cheap one, since it's confined to a non-core leg. Importance is modest precisely because both sides kept it narrow.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** LatAm cross-border e-commerce payments sits at the intersection of two of the region's fastest-growing flows: marketplace GMV and USD↔local-currency settlement. Per the J.P. Morgan "Payments Without Borders" report (2025-10, see [[J.P. Morgan report on Latin America's cross-border payments growth]]), the region's adoption of real-time rails is accelerating — Pix alone reached 150M+ users. Structurally the value chain is fragmenting at the edges but consolidating at the rails layer: marketplaces own demand and seller relationships, while cross-border funding movement, FX and ACH payouts increasingly concentrate in a few global banks and processors able to hold banking licenses across geographies — a capital/regulatory barrier few can clear. Why now: as MELI scales Global Selling into six markets (AR, BR, MX, CL, CO, UY), the complexity of multi-party cross-border payouts (onboarding, KYC/KYB, funding, seller pay-outs) outgrows in-house treasury — pulling it toward an embedded-finance provider. Second-order effect: the marketplace keeps the seller UX while renting the regulated plumbing.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: GMV, TPV, take rate, fintech MAU, cross-border payout latency/cost. MELI is the clear LatAm leader — Q2 2026 net revenue & financial income $10.2bn (+50% YoY), GMV ~$22bn (+36% FX-neutral), TPV $101bn (+56% YoY FX-neutral), Mercado Pago MAU 88M (+30% YoY), AUM $23bn (+68% YoY) (MELI Q2 2026 press release, 2026-08). Basis of competition on the bank side is network/scale and regulatory reach, not price: J.P. Morgan Payments posted record FY2025 revenue $19.4bn (+5% YoY), ~$10tn processed daily across 160+ countries / 120+ currencies (JPM Payments Q4 2025 highlights). Protagonist's position: MELI is *ahead* in LatAm consumer/seller distribution; this deal is MELI the buyer, not the competitor — it is outsourcing the US-side regulated banking/ACH layer where it has no license footprint. Moat for MELI = network effects + switching costs across its ecosystem (ecosystemic users +37% YoY); JPM's moat = scale + banking-license intangibles. Note the deal is partly *announced* vs *live*: the piece describes an "initial pilot" for KYC/KYB onboarding — production scale across all six markets is not yet evidenced.

**Comps & multiples.** MELI: EV ~$100.44bn, market cap ~$92.98bn (as of 2026-10-16, stockanalysis.com); TTM revenue ~$35.18bn (to 2026-06-30). EV/Sales = $100.44bn / $35.18bn = **2.85x** — in-line-to-cheap for a ~46% TTM revenue grower; the multiple is well-justified by growth (growth-multiple correlation), not rich. Peer revenue multiples (external, 2026-10): Nubank (NU) ~3.3x EV/Sales on ~$21bn revenue, P/E ~17.9x; StoneCo (STNE) market cap ~$2.18bn and PagSeguro (PAGS) ~$2.46bn, P/E ~6-7x — the acquirer-heavy Brazilian names trade at steep discounts on margin/competitive pressure. EV/EBITDA and P/E for MELI = no data here (would need verified EBITDA/EPS). JPM is a diversified G-SIB; a standalone Payments-segment multiple is not meaningful/`[UNSOURCED]`. Internal comps: [[J.P. Morgan report on Latin America's cross-border payments growth]], [[MercadoLibre bets on AI and digital banking]], [[MercadoLibre's Argentina loan delinquency rate rises sharply]]. Distribution not computed on <3 clean comparable multiples; qualitative read: MELI in-line vs Nubank, premium vs legacy acquirers — consistent with its growth lead.

**Risk flags.**
1. *Dependence on someone else's rails.* By routing US-seller onboarding, bulk cross-border receipts and ACH payouts through J.P. Morgan Payments, MELI cedes a regulated layer of the Global Selling stack; a repricing, service change, or bank risk-appetite shift on cross-border/correspondent exposure could squeeze a flow MELI can't easily re-insource without a US banking footprint.
2. *Announced ≠ live; execution/geo-scale risk.* The source cites only an "initial pilot" for KYC/KYB; scaling embedded onboarding + bulk ACH across six jurisdictions with divergent FX and sanctions regimes is non-trivial — slippage would blunt the Global Selling growth narrative.
3. *LatAm credit/FX concentration backdrop.* MELI's own fintech arm is already showing stress (see [[MercadoLibre's Argentina loan delinquency rate rises sharply]]); AR/BR macro and currency volatility raise cross-border settlement and consumer-credit risk even as the payments partnership de-risks the US leg.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is incremental validation of the "marketplaces rent regulated cross-border rails from global banks" thesis rather than a re-rating — JPM Payments wins a marquee LatAm logo, deepening its embedded-finance land-grab against fintech processors (Stripe, Adyen, dLocal) in cross-border marketplace flows (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if MELI publicly reports Global Selling seller counts / US-seller payout volumes on the J.P. Morgan rails within 2-3 quarters, the partnership is live and material; if it stays absent from MELI's 10-Q/earnings commentary, treat it as a pilot-stage PR win (what would make the thesis wrong). Trigger to watch: Money20/20 2026 (Oct 18-21) session commentary + Q3/Q4 2026 MELI disclosures.
