 iwoca launches M&A marketplace as data reveals SME succession gap 
October 15, 2026
 iwoca has launched manda, an M&A platform connecting small businesses looking to sell with serious buyers, acting as a market maker that handles matching, business reports, and financial due diligence all in one place 
 The launch comes as new iwoca data reveals an impending succession crisis, with only 21% of SME owners holding a formal exit plan and 37% altering plans due to April 2026 Inheritance Tax changes 
 manda has already completed its first transaction, facilitating the sale of roofing contractor Total Roof Control (TRC) Midlands Ltd 
 iwoca today launches manda , a dedicated M&A platform designed to connect small businesses looking to sell with suitable buyers.
Unlike traditional corporate finance brokers that only represent one side of a deal, manda acts as a market maker, doing the whole job in one place. For SME owners looking to sell, manda matches them with serious buyers including search funds, family offices, and private equity firms. For buyers, manda draws on iwoca’s data on more than 100,000 SMEs to put the right opportunities in front of them, and uses bank feeds, filed accounts, VAT returns and payroll to carry out due diligence and advise on deal structuring. By bringing the whole process together, manda can make the process more accessible and affordable for SME owners by only charging a fee on completion of the deal.
 iwoca data reveals a growing SME succession gap 
The launch comes as new data from small and medium-sized business owners shows that a succession crisis is growing for the UK’s 5.7m SMEs. New polling from iwoca shows that only one in five (21%) SME owners have a formal, documented succession or exit plan, while a quarter (25%) have given it no thought at all.
Even owners closest to retirement lack a clear strategy. Nearly three-quarters (73%) of 55–64 year-olds say they have nothing more than a rough idea of what will happen to their business when they step back, and less than a third (29%) of SME bosses aged 65–74 have a formal plan in place. 
This comes despite heavy reliance on a business sale to fund post-work life. Half (50%) of SME owners say they will rely on their business to fund their retirement, but nearly three in 10 (29%) do not know what their business would be worth if sold today.
Government tax changes are also fuelling concerns among business owners about succession. More than a third (37%) report that they have changed their plans following the April 2026 Inheritance Tax changes, with 15% now more likely to sell and 8% more likely to close down entirely.
The data also reveals a strong preference for domestic continuity: an overseas buyer is the least preferred option for 30% of SME bosses, while selling to a larger UK company (22%) or the existing management team (20%) are the leading choices.
 manda’s first deal completed 
manda’s public launch follows the completion of its first deal, with the sale of roofing contractor TRC (Midlands) Ltd to TrueNorth Capital Group.
manda matched TRC against TrueNorth’s acquisition criteria, conducted the financial due diligence, and brought the parties together. iwoca managed the transaction from initial seller sourcing to completion.
 Christoph Rieche, CEO and co-founder of iwoca, said: “Our analysis shows the majority of small business owners have no formal succession plan for the business they’ve built. Often the hardest part isn’t finding a buyer, but finding the right one at the right time - someone who understands the business and will take it forward. manda solves that by matching sellers with buyers who actually fit what they're looking for. For businesses across the country, this platform is going to transform how deals are done, and how companies think about succession planning.”
 Bradley Lay, TrueNorth Capital Group: “TRC (Midlands) is exactly the type of business we want to bring into TrueNorth. It has an exceptional heritage, longstanding customer relationships and a reputation that has been built over generations.Our responsibility is to protect the business legacy while also providing the investment, infrastructure and support required for its next stage of growth.The roofing and external envelope market is a major part of our strategy, and TRC gives us a fantastic platform from which to continue building our capabilities in this area.”
 Trevor Horne, Managing Director of TRC (Midlands) Ltd: "We'd built the business over many years, investing time and money into its creation and growth, so we wanted a buyer who would take it forward properly. The whole process was smooth and we had the right partners around us throughout, which meant we could focus on handing the business over in good shape rather than managing the logistics of the sale."

# Context-enrichment: iwoca launches manda, an M&A marketplace for small businesses
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

**Freshness: FRESH.** Genuinely new product (a market-making M&A platform + first completed deal), not a re-report. Prior iwoca notes cover a different event stream — funding facilities, partnerships ([[Zempler Bank partners with iwoca for SME lending]], [[iwoca partners with Teya to distribute SME loans in UK]]) and the [[Iwoca explores £1bn-plus sale, eyes bank and payments buyers]] auction. manda is a first-time launch, so not a duplicate.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- iwoca launched **manda** (15 Oct 2026), an M&A platform matching SME **sellers** with **buyers** (search funds, family offices, PE). It positions as a two-sided **"market maker"** vs single-side brokers, bundling matching, business reports and financial due diligence, and charges a **success fee on completion only** (no upfront).
- Due diligence is powered by iwoca's own data moat: **bank feeds, filed accounts, VAT returns, payroll** across **100k+ SMEs** it has lent to. This is the real differentiator — not the "marketplace" framing (marketplaces for business sales are decades old), but the ability to pre-qualify and underwrite sellers from first-party financial data it already holds.
- **One** deal completed at launch: roofing contractor **TRC (Midlands) Ltd** → **TrueNorth Capital Group**. So traction = 1 transaction; everything else is capability claim.
- **Why framed this way:** the launch is wrapped in a self-commissioned "succession crisis" survey (only 21% of SME owners have a formal exit plan; 37% changed plans after April 2026 IHT changes). This is classic lead-gen framing — manufacture urgency, then offer the funnel. The survey is the marketing; the product is a lead-conversion engine sitting on top of iwoca's existing SME relationships. **(analysis)**
- **Not disclosed:** fee level/%, number of live listings, buyer-side pipeline size, whether manda is a separate P&L or a feature of the lending business, regulatory status (M&A advisory/corporate finance can touch FCA perimeter).

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Business-for-sale marketplaces (listing model):** BizBuySell (~40–45k US listings, 3M+ monthly visits, US-only), BizQuest, BusinessesForSale.com, Daltons Business, DealStream — classified-ad model, buyer/seller self-serve, no underwriting. **manda's delta:** it does the deal (matching + DD + structuring), not just the listing.
- **Online-business specialists:** Flippa (600k+ buyers, $500M+ transacted, AI matching), Empire Flippers, Acquire.com — digital/e-commerce niche, not UK high-street SMEs like roofing contractors.
- **AI M&A-advisory entrants:** [[Iconic raises $6 million for AI-powered M&A advisory]] — same thesis (tech-enabled lower-mid-market M&A) but advisory-first, no lending data moat.
- **Traditional brokers / corporate finance:** single-side representation, upfront retainers. manda undercuts on price (completion-only fee) and on data.
- **Position:** iwoca is **behind on marketplace scale/liquidity** (1 deal vs tens of thousands of listings elsewhere) but **ahead on underwriting** — no listing site has bank-feed-level data on 100k sellers. **Why it matters:** in M&A the bottleneck is not finding listings, it's trust + verified financials + a matched counterparty. iwoca attacks exactly that gap. The open question is liquidity: a market maker with one deal is a thin market. **(analysis)**

## [2] Company history / fit
- iwoca (founded 2011, Rieche + Dear) is one of Europe's largest SME lenders: **£1.5bn lent in 2025** (up from £952m in 2024), **£6bn+ cumulative**, **~96k–100k SMEs financed**, **revenue £366m (+56%)**, **profit £85.1m**. Distribution via 50+ embedded partners (Starling, Tide, Monzo, Xero) plus [[Zempler Bank partners with iwoca for SME lending]] and [[iwoca partners with Teya to distribute SME loans in UK]].
- **Why manda now:** iwoca explicitly flagged in its 2025 results that "more SME solutions in **insurance and M&A** [are] to be announced soon," alongside a Business Credit Card (May 2026), iwocaPay and Credit Compass. **Structural driver:** lending is a commodity, capital-intensive, cyclical take-rate business valued on a modest multiple. To justify the **£1bn+ auction** it is running (Qatalyst mandate, per [[Iwoca explores £1bn-plus sale, eyes bank and payments buyers]]), iwoca needs a **platform/software narrative** — multiple fee lines off the same 100k-SME relationship graph. manda converts an existing data asset into a new, capital-light, success-fee revenue stream with no balance-sheet exposure. **(analysis)**
- **Second-order:** launching manda mid-sale-process is itself a signal — it dresses up the equity story for bidders (banks/payments firms) as "SME super-app", not "a loan book."

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Real novelty:** not the marketplace (old), but **underwriting the seller from first-party banking/accounting data** and acting two-sided on a completion-only fee. That genuinely lowers friction and adverse selection vs listing sites and brokers.
- **Traction is thin:** **one** completed deal (TRC → TrueNorth). No listing count, no GMV, no take-rate. Treat as a **launch, not adoption**.
- **Where the margin sits:** value accrues to whoever **owns the verified-financials + counterparty-match layer**. iwoca's edge is defensible only while its data is proprietary and sellers trust it to broker (potential conflict: iwoca both lends to and brokers the sale of the same SME). If pure-play AI-M&A entrants (Iconic) or Open Banking commoditize the data, manda's moat compresses to brand + liquidity. **The central question shifts** from "is this a nice new feature" to "can iwoca build a liquid two-sided market before the sale closes and attention moves on." **(analysis / hypothesis)**

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Market backdrop is genuinely large:** UK "silver tsunami" — ~852k companies with avg director age 60+, ~£186bn enterprise value without an internal succession path, ~58k mid-sized owner sales within 5 years; search-fund model expanding. The TAM for an SME M&A funnel is real, not invented, even if iwoca's survey is self-serving.
- **Risks:** (1) liquidity cold-start — a market maker needs both sides at scale; (2) conflict of interest (lender + broker to same SME); (3) regulatory perimeter for corporate-finance advice; (4) distraction risk during a live £1bn sale; (5) execution — M&A is high-touch, hard to scale like a loan API.
- **Counterintuitive second-order:** the £1bn sale process may **cap** manda's ambition — a bank/payments acquirer may buy iwoca for the loan book + embedded distribution and quietly shelve a sub-scale M&A venture. So manda may be worth more as **narrative for the auction** than as a standalone business. **(hypothesis)**

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** manda sits in UK SME M&A / business-brokerage, adjacent to iwoca's core SME-lending franchise. Market backdrop: the UK has ~5.7m private-sector businesses, 99.8% SMEs (per ONS, as of start-2025, via iwoca & exitradar.co.uk). The driver is a succession/exit gap: iwoca's own polling says only 21% of SME owners hold a formal exit plan and 25% have given it no thought (per iwoca PR, 2026-10-15); third parties put the failure-to-sell rate at ~80% of owners who try to exit (per exitradar.co.uk). "Why now": (1) a demographic "silver tsunami" of retiring owner-operators, and (2) the April 2026 Inheritance Tax changes — iwoca says 37% of owners altered plans post-change, 15% now more likely to sell (per iwoca PR). Structure: the brokerage layer is highly fragmented — listing portals (BusinessesForSale, Rightbiz, Daltons) plus thousands of regional brokers and sell-side-only corporate-finance advisers; value concentrates in matching + diligence, which is where manda targets (analysis). TAM in £ terms — no data (no verifiable figure for UK sub-£-mid-market deal fees).

**Competitive landscape.** KPIs the model runs on: deal GMV/transaction value, take rate (fee on completion), match-to-close conversion, and liquidity (buyer/seller density) — none disclosed by manda yet [UNSOURCED]. Players & basis of competition: (a) listing portals — BusinessesForSale.com (f.1995, London), Rightbiz (~23.7k live listings per valius.global), Daltons — compete on inventory/traffic, charge listing fees, do NOT handle diligence; (b) regional brokers / corporate-finance advisers — compete on relationships, typically sell-side only; (c) search funds, family offices and PE as the demand side manda aggregates. Recent moves: iwoca is actively hiring a "manda" team (Sell-Side Transaction Manager, M&A Product Builder, per Ashby job posts, 2026) — signals a real build-out, not just a PR launch. Protagonist's position: niche new entrant with one structural edge — it is a two-sided market maker (both buy- and sell-side) fed by proprietary data on 100,000+ SMEs plus bank-feed/VAT/payroll underwriting reused from the lending book (per iwoca PR). Moat = data/distribution reuse (intangibles + an existing SME funnel), not network effects yet — liquidity is unproven, one completed deal (TRC Midlands → TrueNorth Capital) (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** No deal valuation attaches to manda itself (internal launch), so no multiple to compute for the product. For the parent as scale context: iwoca FY2025 revenue £366m (+56% YoY), £1.5bn lent, cumulative >£6bn; a mooted sale valued it at £1bn+ (Qatalyst mandate) → implied price/sales ≈ £1.0bn / £366m = 2.7x (per alternativecreditinvestor.com, financialit, fintechfutures) — a lending multiple, not a marketplace multiple, so not directly comparable to manda. Internal comps: [[Iwoca explores £1bn-plus sale, eyes bank and payments buyers]] (the £1bn sale thesis), [[Zempler Bank partners with iwoca for SME lending]] and [[iwoca partners with Teya to distribute SME loans in UK]] (distribution reach). The `industry/m-and-a` corpus tag surfaces only unrelated Russian corporate deals — not comparable; excluded. External brokerage comps: pure-play UK listing portals are private with undisclosed economics → multiples = no data. Flag: in-line/qualitative only — distribution not computed (fewer than 3 comparable figures).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Conflict of interest / diligence objectivity.** manda is both the lender (via iwoca's book) and the deal's diligence provider and market maker — "doing the whole job in one place" means the party vetting the accounts can also be the party financing the buyer. Why: compromises the independence buyers/sellers expect from advisers and invites FCA/regulatory and reputational scrutiny on conflicted deals.
2. **Liquidity / cold-start risk.** One completed deal at launch; a two-sided marketplace is worthless without buyer AND seller density. Why: without liquidity the "market maker" is just another sell-side broker, and the data-reuse edge doesn't offset a thin order book (second-order: fee-on-completion-only means no revenue until deals actually close).
3. **Success-fee concentration & cyclicality.** Charging only on completion ties revenue to an unproven close rate in a market where ~80% of owners fail to sell; IHT-driven supply can also reverse if policy changes. Why: lumpy, policy-sensitive revenue layered onto a credit book already exploring a sale — execution bandwidth risk.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** manda is a bet that an SME lender's proprietary data + funnel can disintermediate fragmented brokers in the succession wave — an adjacency/land-grab, not a re-rating (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if manda posts >20-30 completed deals within ~12 months, the data-as-distribution moat is real and other SME lenders (Allica, Funding Circle) will copy it; if deal flow stays in single digits, it's a PR-led vanity vertical. Watch: deal-count cadence, whether it survives/transfers in iwoca's own £1bn+ sale, and any FCA guidance on conflicted market-maker roles.
