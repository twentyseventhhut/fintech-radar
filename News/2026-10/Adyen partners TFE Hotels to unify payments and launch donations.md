Adyen Partners with TFE Hotels to Drive Global Unified Commerce and Launch Charitable Giving for UNICEF at Checkout
Through a new global partnership with Adyen, TFE Hotels consolidates its on-property, upgrade payments onto a single platform, enabling faster property setups, consistent authorization rates, and seamless round-up charity donations at checkout.
Download the image above to use for your publication.
Adyen, the global financial technology platform of choice for leading businesses, today announced a global partnership with Australian international hotel group TFE Hotels to consolidate its payment operations and power seamless checkin and checkout experiences across its global portfolio. As part of the partnership, TFE Hotels will also activate Adyen Giving across 35 participating Adina properties, allowing guests to make charity donations at the front desk on check-out to support UNICEF.
As the flagship brand of TFE Hotels, Adina Hotels represents the group's heritage, growth and ongoing evolution.  What began in Australia has expanded internationally, with Adina delivering a trusted apartment hotel experience to guests in destinations across Australia, New Zealand and Europe.
Before partnering with Adyen, TFE Hotels managed a complex payment ecosystem across its global portfolio. Multiple vendor relationships made it difficult to introduce new payment methods and expand into new markets, while disconnected systems required significant manual processing.
In addition legacy platforms offered limited integration flexibility, slowing property onboarding and payment response times. The existing setup also prevented TFE Hotels from offering guests the option to make charitable donations at checkout.
By leveraging Adyen’s Unified Commerce platform under a single contract, TFE Hotels has unified its hotel accommodation, food and beverage operations, gift cards, and guest experience app and on-property check-out terminals into a single global system. The transition has delivered rapid speed to market – enabling new properties to go live in as little as 4 weeks – while maintaining a consistent authorization rate of 94%.
“Working with Adyen has allowed us to simplify our global payments ecosystem, creating a more seamless and reliable experience for both our teams and our guests,” said Katia Giurtalis, Global Marketing Officer, TFE Hotels.
“Through the integration of Adyen Giving, we’re also making it easier for guests to support UNICEF’s important work around the world, with the opportunity to contribute directly at our hotel front desks.”
Giving went live across 35 Adina properties in Australia, Austria, Denmark, Germany and Switzerland on 19 October and will be rolled out in other TFE properties in the coming months.
UNICEF operates in more than 190 countries to provide children in crisis with health, education and protection. UNICEF Australia Director of Growth & Engagement, Libby Hodgson said, “Every donation made by Adina guests through this simple process with Adyen Giving will be a meaningful contribution to child survival and wellbeing. UNICEF applauds this initiative by Adyen Giving and Adina to support children in locations where the need is greatest”. 
"Hospitality brands today need the flexibility to deliver smooth, personalized guest experiences while maintaining operational efficiency behind the scenes. Our partnership with TFE Hotels demonstrates how a single platform overview of all payments unlocks business agility, higher authorization rates, and deeper guest insights. Crucially, through Adyen Giving, we are also empowering TFE Hotels to turn everyday payment moments into meaningful corporate social responsibility actions," said Hayley Fisher, Managing Director, Australia and New Zealand at Adyen.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Hospitality unified-commerce payments sit at the intersection of a ~$5.8tn global hospitality TAM (per The Business Research Company, 2026 est., +6.4% CAGR to 2030) and the hospitality POS-software layer, variously sized ~$8–20bn and growing ~11% CAGR (per researchandmarkets / 360iResearch, 2026). The deal economics live in the latter. Structure: payments-for-hospitality is fragmented — PMS/POS vendors, legacy acquirers and gateways each own a slice, exactly the "multiple vendor relationships / disconnected systems" TFE describes. Value accrues to whoever consolidates acquiring + on-property + F&B + app + terminals under one contract (switching costs, data gravity). "Why now": hotel groups are collapsing multi-vendor stacks for faster property onboarding (TFE: go-live in 4 weeks vs legacy) and to add guest-facing features (round-up charity, loyalty) that siloed stacks block — a secular shift from best-of-breed to single-platform.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: processed/payment volume, take rate (bps), authorization rate, EBITDA margin, net-revenue growth. Authorization rate is the hospitality differentiator Adyen leads on (94% cited for TFE). Key players and basis of competition: Adyen and Stripe (platform/single-integration, product depth), Shift4 (hospitality incumbent — SkyTab terminals, deep PMS/POS integrations, "one platform, one integration" unified-commerce launch explicitly head-to-head with Adyen/Stripe, per popularfintech 2026), plus Fiserv/Clover, Toast, Lightspeed at the SMB/restaurant end. Recent Adyen hospitality/unified-commerce moves: Prommt/Oracle OPERA hotel partnership (2025-08), Starbucks 940+ EU stores migrated in 7 weeks (2026-02), Atlas Singapore F&B (2025-08), Cathay Pacific (2026-03). Protagonist's position: ahead on enterprise unified-commerce (single global contract, high auth rates), catching up vs Shift4 on US hospitality density where Shift4's hardware+PMS install base is entrenched (analysis). Moat: switching costs + scale (one platform spanning on-property, F&B, gift cards, app, terminals) and a single-global-acquirer network effect.

**Comps & multiples.** Adyen (ADYEN:AMS), as of Sep 2026: market cap ~$31bn, net revenue ~$3.1bn → EV/Revenue ≈ `$31bn / $3.1bn ≈ 5.7x` (EV≈mkt cap, net cash); EV/EBITDA ≈ 10.8x (per multiples.vc / stockanalysis, Sep 2026). Peers:
- Shift4 (FOUR), Oct 2026: market cap ~$3.1bn, FY2026 GRLNF guide $2.48–2.53bn → EV/net-revenue ≈ `$3.1bn / $2.5bn ≈ 1.2x` on cap alone (EV higher incl. debt); EV/EBITDA ≈ 6.7x (per multiples.vc). Markedly cheaper than Adyen — reflects lower margins/US-hardware mix vs Adyen's ~49% EBITDA margin.
- Stripe (private): ~$159bn Feb-2026 tender valuation, est. net revenue ~$5.8bn → ≈ `$159bn / $5.8bn ≈ 27x` sales (round valuation, not market cap; rich even allowing for Stripe's higher ~34–36bps take rate vs Adyen's ~16bps).
- PayPal (PYPL): market cap ~$50.6bn, far slower growth (~5.7% rev vs Adyen ~18%) and ~14% margins — a low-growth comp, not a hospitality peer.
Internal comps: [[Adyen and Prommt partner on hospitality payments]], [[Adyen partners Atlas for Singapore F&B payments]], [[Starbucks taps Adyen for payments across 940+ European stores]], [[Adyen forecasts 20% annual net revenue growth after 2026]]. Flag: Adyen in-line-to-rich at 5.7x EV/Rev — above Shift4 but justified by ~20% cc growth and ~49% margins (growth-adjusted, not an automatic risk); Stripe is the outlier at ~27x.

**Risk flags.**
1. Take-rate compression — Adyen H1 2026 blended take rate fell to ~16.2bps (volume +24% but net revenue +19% / +21% cc), so these large enterprise wins (TFE, Starbucks) carry low bps; growth leans on volume, and mix toward big hospitality accounts can keep diluting the rate. Second-order: margin defense depends on add-on monetization (data, financial products), not core acquiring.
2. Disintermediation / entrenched incumbent — in US hospitality Shift4 owns the hardware+PMS layer; Adyen's platform strength is weaker where on-property terminal install base and PMS lock-in already exist, capping share-of-wallet.
3. Single-client concentration risk is low here, but the "announced vs live" gap matters: Giving went live on only 35 Adina properties (2026-10-19); the broader "global portfolio" and other TFE brands are "coming months" — economics/volume of the full rollout are undisclosed (PR is silent on ticket size and bps).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Incremental, not a re-rating: another enterprise unified-commerce logo reinforcing Adyen's single-platform land-and-expand thesis in hospitality, and a template (payments + Adyen Giving CSR hook) to differentiate on feature breadth rather than price (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Adyen converts the "coming months" TFE expansion into full-portfolio volume while holding ~49% EBITDA margin, the hospitality vertical becomes a durable growth engine; it breaks if take-rate compression accelerates faster than volume or if Shift4's unified-commerce push wins international hotel groups. Watch: Adyen's next business update unified-commerce segment growth (was +24% YoY / +28% cc in Q1 2026) and whether auth-rate (94%) stays a repeatable selling point.
