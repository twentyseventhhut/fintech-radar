Mollie strengthens Pay by Bank to power the next generation of European payments
The renewed Pay by Bank gives businesses a faster, more efficient way to accept payments, with fewer steps between checkout and paid.
The renewed Pay by Bank gives businesses a faster, more efficient way to accept payments, with fewer steps between checkout and paid.
 October 2, 2026 
Mollie has launched a renewed version of Pay by Bank, built on the technology of GoCardless, which Mollie acquired earlier this year. Pay by Bank lets consumers pay directly from their bank account, through a faster, more efficient checkout designed to boost conversion. Thanks to GoCardless's open banking technology, Mollie connects directly to the banks Dutch consumers already use.
Pay by Bank combines GoCardless's pan-European open banking connectivity – direct connections to 2,500+ banks – with Mollie's own leading checkout experience. The result is one solution that supports one-off payments, recurring payments, and invoicing from the same technical foundation. Because Mollie owns that connection directly, the payment completes in three steps, instead of the five typical of other open banking solutions – for businesses, fewer steps means fewer chances for a customer to drop off before they pay. Pay by Bank is the first major product to come from bringing GoCardless and Mollie together since the acquisition earlier this year.
"Businesses deserve a payment infrastructure that simply works," says Koen Köppen, Mollie CEO. "Reliable, invisible, and fully embedded within their own environment. Rather than launching a new brand, we prefer to build the infrastructure that businesses can rely on. We believe this is the ideal way forward."
Mollie Pay by Bank also offers guaranteed payments, instant confirmation, and transparent pricing. By securely capturing the customer's IBAN at the moment of payment, it can also be used for more than just a one-off transaction – including a direct route to recurring revenue through Direct Debit.
"Bringing GoCardless and Mollie together was never about adding a logo to our portfolio," Koen says. "It was about building the foundation for exactly this kind of product. Mollie Pay by Bank is the first proof of that vision, and it won't be the last."
Alongside Pay by Bank, Mollie is introducing a public Issuing Bank Performance Page, giving insight into how reliably banks perform within open banking connections. "Open banking only works if every link in the chain performs well," Koen says. "If we ask consumers and businesses to trust this way of paying, transparency is the very least we can offer."
Pay by Bank is available from today for businesses and consumers in the Netherlands. Expansion to the rest of Europe is planned throughout 2027. 
"We're starting in the Netherlands because this is where we can immediately prove, at scale, that it works," Koen adds. "We're bringing more than a new product. We're building the infrastructure the next generation of European payments will run on."

# Context-enrichment: Mollie relaunches Pay by Bank on GoCardless technology
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On **2 Oct 2026** Mollie launched a "renewed" **Pay by Bank** in the **Netherlands only** (EU rollout "planned throughout 2027"), built on the open-banking rails of **GoCardless**, which Mollie acquired (deal announced 11 Dec 2025, ~€1.05bn; **closed 3 Sep 2026**). Claimed specs: direct connections to **2,500+ banks**, payment completes in **3 steps instead of "the 5 typical of other open-banking solutions"**, one stack for one-off + recurring + invoicing, IBAN captured at payment for a Direct-Debit path, plus guaranteed payments and instant confirmation. Also shipped a public **Issuing Bank Performance Page** (bank reliability transparency).

**+ Why framed this way / what it reveals.** The PR frame ("the next generation of European payments", "first proof of the vision") inflates what is largely a **re-platforming/rebrand**, not a new category. Pay-by-bank already existed: GoCardless's **Instant Bank Pay** launched **2021** and GoCardless itself renamed it "Pay by Bank"; GoCardless then did **Recurring Pay by Bank** on the new UKPI scheme (first live recurring open-banking txn Mar 2026 for Jellyfish Energy; scheme live 2 Jun 2026) — see [[GoCardless launches Recurring Pay by Bank on UKPI rails]] and [[Sage and GoCardless add Pay by Bank for small businesses]]. The **genuine deltas**: (a) Mollie now **owns the rail directly** (post-acquisition) rather than reselling a third party, which is what underpins the embedded 3-step flow; (b) unified one-off/recurring/invoicing on one foundation; (c) the bank-performance transparency page. "3 vs 5 steps" is Mollie's own characterisation, **not an independent benchmark**, and **no conversion/pricing data was published**. Several outlets headlined "UK and NL", but Mollie's own release says NL — the UK capability already existed inside GoCardless, so the "UK launch" overstates novelty (flag).

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **iDEAL (EPI/Wero)** — the elephant in the room: iDEAL holds **~71% of Dutch online orders (2025)** and is itself an A2A/bank-transfer method; iDEAL is rebranding to **iDEAL | Wero** from 2026. Mollie already sits inside this scheme ([[Mollie joins EPI Company to accelerate Wero]], [[Mollie guide explains what Wero is and how it works]]).
- **Trustly** — EU A2A volume leader: FY2025 processed **SEK 1,059bn (+17.6%), first year >$100bn TPV**, ~$101M revenue, 9,000+ merchants, 12,000 banks, 30+ markets; passed 120M users (Apr 2026) ([[Trustly issues a bond for 4 billion]]).
- **TrueLayer** — 2025 revenue £28m (+39%), ~$29bn volume added; acquired **Zimpler (closed 3 Mar 2026)**, raised $27M led by CDP (Oct 2026) ([[TrueLayer to acquire Nordic open-banking rival Zimpler]]).
- **Volt** — $60M Series B, Worldpay/FIS + Shopify tie-ups.
- **Tink (Visa)** / **Plaid (~$8bn val, Feb 2026)** — aggregation layers; vobapay/Mastercard Open Finance in A2A ([[vobapay integrates Mastercard Open Finance for A2A payments]]).

**+ Why the lay of the land / second order.** Consolidation is the dominant theme (Visa–Tink, TrueLayer–Zimpler, now Mollie–GoCardless): open-banking point players have **weak standalone unit economics** and are being absorbed into full-stack PSPs or card networks. Mollie's edge is not the rail (Trustly/TrueLayer are bigger) but **distribution** — 350k+ merchants already on Mollie checkout. The competitive position in NL specifically is **catching up to parity with iDEAL**, not leapfrogging; a Dutch industry insider (Jongmans, VBIN) reportedly said Mollie's product "looks more like iDEAL than Wero itself does".

## [2] Company history / fit
Mollie's trajectory: NL PSP (last valued ~$6.5bn), aggressive 2025-26 EEA land-grab — Czechia/Hungary/Slovenia, Slovakia, Romania, then **€350M to complete all 30 EEA markets** ([[Mollie commits €350 million to complete 30-country EEA expansion]], [[Mollie launches in Slovakia to expand European e-commerce reach]]). Revenue momentum: **29% net revenue growth to €147M in 2025** ([[Mollie posts 29% net revenue growth to €147 million]]). GoCardless acquisition ([[Mollie to acquire GoCardless for about €1.05B]], frontrunner since Aug 2025: [[Mollie emerges as frontrunner to acquire GoCardless]]) added bank-payment rails + ~95k customers → combined ~350k businesses, ~€3.5bn group.

**+ Why Mollie acts this way.** A commodity card-acquiring take-rate needs a **moat and a software multiple**; owning A2A rails (vs reselling) lets Mollie keep the open-banking margin, add recurring/Direct-Debit revenue, and position for **agentic commerce** ([[Mollie prepares for ChatGPT agentic commerce in Europe]]). Launching in NL first is a "prove-at-home-scale" move, but also the market where the incremental consumer benefit is smallest (iDEAL already dominant) — the real pitch is **merchant cost/margin + unified recurring**, not consumer UX.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Net-new vs the press frame: **little**. The product concept (A2A pay-by-bank, recurring, invoicing) predates this by years in both GoCardless and iDEAL. **Zero traction numbers** disclosed at launch (no merchants live, no conversion uplift, no GMV). The one structurally interesting item is **vertical integration** — Mollie owning the connection removes a redirect/third-party hop, which is a plausible (unquantified) conversion lever and lets Mollie capture the full open-banking margin instead of sharing it.

**+ Why value-add is/ isn't real (deeper).** Who captures the margin: by owning the rail Mollie keeps the spread cards would have taken (Visa/MC ~84% of UK retail, £1.5bn fees — the A2A pitch). BUT the **Instant Payments Regulation fee-parity rule (sending mandate live Oct 2025)** is what actually makes A2A cheap — that's a regulatory tailwind Mollie rides, not a Mollie innovation. Downside: **no chargeback/dispute protection** (Dutch Payment Association's Arjan Bol: a bank transfer can't be disputed afterward), which caps consumer trust vs cards and vs Wero's planned 2028 buyer protection. "Guaranteed payments" and "transparent pricing" are asserted with **no published prices** (flag) — so the "cheaper than iDEAL/Wero" claim is unsubstantiated.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
EU expansion through 2027; more products promised ("won't be the last"). Regulatory backdrop is favourable: **Instant Payments Regulation** (receive since Jan 2025, send since Oct 2025, fee parity, €100k cap removed) and **PSD3/PSR** (texts agreed Apr 2026, OJ/applicability ~2028) mandate better bank APIs and ban open-banking obstacles. A2A e-commerce forecast ~$757bn by 2026 (Juniper-style forecast, not actuals).

**+ Why the market goes this way / second order.** Fee-parity + mandated instant rails structurally erode the card interchange pool, so PSPs that **own A2A rails + merchant distribution** (Mollie, Adyen-likes) are advantaged over pure aggregators. Counterintuitive risk: in the Netherlands the open-banking "win" is already banked by iDEAL/Wero, so Mollie's NL relaunch may cannibalise iDEAL volume it already processes rather than create new volume — the **real test is the 2027 pan-EU rollout** in markets without an entrenched domestic A2A scheme (DE, Southern Europe), where Pay by Bank could genuinely displace cards.

## FRESHNESS / DUPLICATE VERDICT
**FRESH.** This is a distinct NEW event: the **first product of the merged Mollie+GoCardless** entity (acquisition closed Sep 2026), a NL consumer launch dated 2 Oct 2026, with new branding/terms. It is NOT a reprint of the GoCardless UK launches: [[GoCardless launches Recurring Pay by Bank on UKPI rails]] (UK, Jun 2026, UKPI scheme) and [[Sage and GoCardless add Pay by Bank for small businesses]] (UK, Jul 2026, Sage integration) cover GoCardless's own UK product under different rails/geography. Related-but-distinct, so no `duplicate_of`.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** European A2A / open-banking payments surpassed ~€380bn ($415bn) in annual transaction volume, with A2A now ~13% of all EU checkouts and Europe ~26% of global A2A payment revenue (per secondary-market citations via openbankingexpo / mordorintelligence / payspacemagazine, as of 2026 — treat the TAM figures as analyst estimates, not audited). "Why now": PSD3 + the Payment Services Regulation cleared COREPER endorsement on 2026-04-22, moving the EU open-banking overhaul toward formal publication — the regulatory tailwind that makes A2A at checkout a credible card alternative. Structure: fragmented at the connectivity layer (many open-banking aggregators: TrueLayer, Trustly, Volt, Brite, Tink/Visa, Yapily) but value is consolidating toward players that own BOTH bank connectivity AND the merchant checkout. Barriers: pan-European bank-connection coverage (capital + engineering), PSD2/PSD3 licensing, and merchant distribution/network effects.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: TPV, conversion/completion rate (steps-to-pay), take rate, recurring-vs-one-off mix. Basis of competition = conversion (fewer redirect steps), coverage (banks connected), and bundling with acquiring. Named / adjacent peers and recent moves: TrueLayer — ~$150bn pay-by-bank/yr, raised $27m (2026-10, CDP-led), down-round to ~$700m in 2024 from a $1bn peak; Trustly — $100bn+ TPV in 2025, net revenue $239m in 2024 (+32%), adj. EBITDA $73m; Volt and Brite (Brite got a UK FCA licence, 2026). Protagonist's position: Mollie/GoCardless is differentiated as a vertically-integrated acquirer+A2A stack — GoCardless brings direct connections to 2,500+ banks, Mollie brings checkout; the claimed 3-steps-vs-5 is a conversion moat IF it holds in production (analysis). Moat = owning the bank connection end-to-end + an existing merchant base (combined group 350,000+ businesses) → switching costs + distribution. But it is a Netherlands-only launch (one country, iDEAL's home turf), so the "pan-European" claim is a roadmap (2027), not yet live.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal anchor: Mollie acquired GoCardless for ~€1.05–1.1bn (announced 2025-12-11; completion mid-2026). GoCardless FY25 Group turnover = £160.9m (+22% YoY), TPV = £79.2bn (doubled, heavily Nuapay-aided; underlying +~⅓). Acquisition multiple ≈ €1.05bn / ~€185m (£160.9m at ~1.15 FX) ≈ **5.7x** EV/Revenue — rich on trailing revenue but defensible given 22% growth and the first adj. **Risk flags.**
1. **Single-market, single-rail launch (NL-only).** Pay by Bank is live only in the Netherlands — iDEAL's home market, where A2A is already near-ubiquitous — so this proves little about greenfield A2A adoption elsewhere; the pan-European value depends entirely on the 2027 rollout executing on time. Second-order: if NL "proof point" doesn't travel, the GoCardless connectivity thesis underwrites a €1bn deal on a single-country demo.
2. **Economics undisclosed / de-PR gap.** PR touts "transparent pricing," "guaranteed payments" and conversion gains but discloses no take rate, no fraud-liability allocation, and no actual conversion uplift numbers vs cards. "3 steps vs 5" is a design claim, not measured completion data.
3. **Integration + cost execution.** GoCardless cut 90 jobs (2026-04) with £7.1m extra employee + £5.6m restructuring costs tied partly to the Nuapay deal, and only just reached quarterly profitability — folding it into Mollie while funding a €350m EEA expansion is concurrent execution risk; much of the TPV "doubling" was acquired (Nuapay), not organic.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is consolidation, not re-rating: Europe's A2A layer is moving from standalone connectivity vendors (TrueLayer, Trustly) toward acquirers that own the full card-plus-bank stack, pressuring connectivity-only players' pricing (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Mollie reports a measurable conversion uplift and launches ≥2 non-NL markets on schedule in 2027, the GoCardless premium is justified; trigger to watch = the 2027 cross-border rollout cadence and any disclosed take rate / conversion metric. What breaks it: rollout slips to 2028 or stays NL-centric → the deal was paid for roadmap, not product.
