An Open Letter to Global Investors Looking at Latam
The window is open. Here is what you need to know before you land.
We are entering the part of the year where tech weeks are happening every week in a different city. Agendas are packed, as new events hit the calendar, it is tough to prioritize.
It is also an opportunity for foreign investors to visit Latam for a day or two. Whether it is to participate in an event, schedule a board meeting during a week that will allow for networking, or the now classic Mexican GP and/or Dia de Muertos trip to CDMX.
 As a CDMX native, I f-ing hate the Mexican GP week btw, traffic is caotic, everything is packed and events are generally scheduled all over town. But that is the price of being in the spotlight for a few days I guess. Anyways, I digress. 
Given the likely visits of foreign investors to Latam in the upcoming days and weeks, I felt it is a good opportunity to highlight to these investors why they should double down in Latam. If you’ve been around this newsletter long enough, you know I have highlighted the need for capital in the region. Let’s give it a shot.
Where We Are 
 Latin America raised roughly $16B in venture capital in 2021. By 2023, that number collapsed more than 70%. In 2026, the region is in a clear recovery trajectory. Current estimates put the region at roughly $6B in venture funding this year. That number is far from the boom of 2021, and that is probably a good thing. 
 The 2021 cycle produced inflated valuations, misaligned expectations, and a difficult vintage that LPs are still waiting to see resolved. The current market is more disciplined. Selected global investors are cherry-picking verticals and founders. Entry prices are still very competitive. The math works in a way it did not five years ago. 
A look at the major markets tells a nuanced story. Brazil has been the dominant force in the region for quite some time. Mexico’s growth has accelerated dramatically in the last 12 to 18 months, driven by a wave of large late-stage fintech rounds. Colombia punches above its weight, likely the strongest performance per capita in the region. Argentina has extraordinary engineering talent, but macro volatility has suppressed institutional investment.
 The unicorn count stands at 40+ as of 2026. Many have seen valuation corrections from their 2021 peaks. New unicorns have been scarce. But the headline metric misses what matters most: the region has produced companies that are performing at the highest level globally. 
Nubank is NYSE-listed, with a market cap of roughly $75B, serves more than 140M customers in Latam and recently announced its expansion to the US. Mercado Libre is NASDAQ-listed, with a market cap of roughly $95B, serving more than 200M customers across its e-commerce and fintech ecosystem in Latin America. These are not regional success stories. These are global ones.
 Recent rounds in the region have included the likes of a16z, Benchmark, Founders Fund, General Catalyst and Sequoia, to name a few. The best global funds are already here. The question is whether you are.

The Trends Worth Understanding 
AI-Native Fintech 
 Fintech has been the key driver of growth, deals, and unicorns in Latam for the better part of a decade. Per an analysis I performed last year, roughly 2 out of every 3 unicorns in the region have a significant fintech component or are in a fintech-adjacent industry . 
 The next chapter is AI-native fintech. Latam’s 400M+ underbanked adults are ideal for AI-powered credit scoring, insurance underwriting, and digital wallets, to name a few. AI enables the use of alternative data and machine learning to build credit models from scratch, in markets where traditional credit bureau data is thin or nonexistent. These emerging structural advantages are tough to capture for incumbents, and make the market ripe for disruption. 
Healthtech, Finally 
 Public healthcare systems across Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia are failing. Digital health, telemedicine, and AI diagnostics are finally starting to see real traction. Leona Health (backed by a16z and General Catalyst), Telepatia (backed by a16z), Certuma (backed by 8VC), Clivi (backed by Foundation Capital), show the momentum is real, and should be escalating in coming years. This is a category that has been slow to develop in the region. The conditions are now aligned. 
Vertical AI: The Artist FKA Vertical Saas 
 Latam businesses, from the largest enterprises to the 80M+ SMBs, keep running key operational processes in spreadsheets. In the last couple of years, we’ve seen an emergence of B2B Saas plays dominate the market. Vertical AI is replacing vertical Saas, with the advantage that AI allows for new and expanded use cases. It is now possible to serve SMBs at scale or develop enterprise solutions that weren’t feasible a couple of years ago. Enter, the Brazilian legaltech startup that became the region’s first Vertical AI unicorn, offers the clearest template of what this category can produce. 
Why This Is the Right Moment 
 The conditions that make a market attractive to venture capital rarely align all at once. In Latam right now, they do. 
 Latam’s GDP is projected to grow faster than the US and Europe through 2030: 670M+ people, 500M+ mobile internet users, and a middle class that is digitizing faster than Southeast Asia did at a comparable stage 
 400M+ unbanked and underbanked adults who are being served for the first time by digital-first companies 
 Strong technical talent, especially in Argentina and Brazil: world-class engineers at a fraction of the cost of Silicon Valley, and a growing cohort of second-time founders who have been through a full cycle 
 Nubank and Meli have proved the model works at global scale. 
 AI hits differently here: the region is building without legacy infrastructure in the way, which means AI-native companies can skip entire generations of technology that incumbents elsewhere are still unwinding 
 B2B solutions selling to Latam corporates in USD with local peso cost structures have a natural hedge built in: dollar revenue, peso costs, structural margin advantage from day one 
 Most of what is on this list is not going away. These are structural tailwinds, and they compound. 
What We Need to Be Honest About 
 No market pitch is complete without the other side of the ledger. These are real constraints. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. 
 Macro volatility in key markets suppresses institutional interest, additionally political cycles in Brazil and Mexico create regulatory uncertainty that can shift the operating environment quickly 
 Exit routes are limited: threshold for IPO is huge, strategic buyers are hard to find, and secondaries market barely exists 
 Local fund ecosystem is thin: many regional funds are between vehicles or past their close dates, which means less co-investment infrastructure and thinner syndication options 
 Distribution of returns is extreme: a small number of deals will generate most of the upside, and picking the right ones from outside the region is genuinely hard without local presence 
 Inflation history in the region is long, LPs have been burned before and the memory does not fade quickly 
 2021 vintage still looming, LPs are disappointed and unlikely to see liquidity in the near future 
 None of this is disqualifying. Every emerging market worth entering looks like this before the breakout. The question is timing, conviction, and knowing which risks you are actually underwriting.

The Bottom Line 
 The recent status quo in Latam is more overall capital, less deals. Selected global investors are cherry-picking verticals and founders, and will likely see fantastic returns as entry price points are still very competitive. The time is now, key players are being aggressive, and as some markets are struggling to find funding from local funds, the opportunity is there to be captured. 
You are going to be in the region in the next few weeks. The meetings are already on the calendar. The question worth asking, beyond the board meetings and the networking dinners, is whether you are looking at Latam as a market to monitor or as one to participate in.
 The window is open. It will not stay open at these prices forever.
