## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 15 October 2026, the National Bank of Ethiopia (NBE) formally joined the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System (PAPSS), the cross-border payments infrastructure operated under the African Export-Import Bank (Afreximbank). The announcement came at a joint press conference in Addis Ababa (on the sidelines of the Ethiopia Finance Forum) by NBE Governor Eyob Tekalign and PAPSS CEO Mike Ogbalu III, who simultaneously announced that Ethiopia will co-host the 2nd edition of the PAPSS "COWRY" conference on 26–27 November 2026 at the Ethiopian Skylight Hotel. The "120 million people connected" figure in the aggregated text is Ethiopia's population — i.e. the addressable market now reachable, not a transaction count.

**[1] What PAPSS is.** PAPSS is a centralised financial-market infrastructure launched by Afreximbank and the African Union/AfCFTA Secretariat, commercially live since 2022 (West Africa first). It enables instant cross-border payments settled in local African currencies, so parties avoid routing through a third currency (typically USD) and correspondent-banking chains. Settlement is netted at the central-bank level; the aim is to cut cost/time of intra-African payments and support the AfCFTA single-market agenda. Related rails already in the vault: the [[PAPSS and Interstellar unveil African Currency Marketplace]] (an FX marketplace layer to source scarce currency pairs) and country integrations like [[Pesalink and PAPSS unlock cross-border payments in local currencies in Kenya]].

**[2] Scale / where Ethiopia fits.** Per Afreximbank/PAPSS figures current to 2026, PAPSS now spans 30+ African countries across all five regions, ~24 national/regional central banks, 200+ commercial banks and PSPs, and ~16 switches — up sharply from the "18 countries / 150+ banks / 14 switches" level cited when [[Bank of Algeria joins Pan-African Payment and Settlement System]] in Aug 2025, and the "17th country" milestone at [[Morocco joins Pan-African Payment and Settlement System]] (Jul 2025). Growth accelerated in 2026 with regional blocs like BEAC/CEMAC and DRC joining. Ethiopia — Africa's second-most-populous country (~120M) and a large, previously closed economy — is a materially significant addition to the addressable network, not just an incremental member.

**[3] Ethiopia context — why now.** The move dovetails with Ethiopia's FX liberalisation. In July 2024 the NBE floated the birr, ending ~50 years of a state-fixed rate (IMF/World Bank-backed Home-Grown Economic Reform Program); the birr depreciated ~120% vs USD through early 2025 and the NBE booked large revaluation losses (~445bn birr / ~$2.85bn FY to Jun-2025). A February 2026 amendment directive (FXD/04/2026) deepened the regime — forward contracts, broader retention, delegated remittance approvals. A liberalised, more market-driven FX regime is a precondition for local-currency cross-border settlement to work, which is likely why NBE could now join. Related African-payments momentum in the vault: [[Onafriq and PAPSS to launch cross-border payments from Ghana]], [[Transfers to mobile wallets launch across six African countries]], [[Daya raises $2.4M pre-seed for African stablecoin cross-border payments]].

**[4] What to watch.** (a) Live vs announced — joining ≠ live transaction volume; prior country integrations (e.g. Algeria, Morocco) show onboarding of commercial banks/switches lags the central-bank signing. (b) PAPSS's own 2027 roadmap to link to China and India payment systems, extending beyond Africa. (c) Whether Ethiopia's still-fragile birr/parallel-market gap and hard-currency scarcity let local-currency settlement actually displace USD flows. (d) Watch the Nov 2026 COWRY conference for concrete settlement-corridor and interoperability commitments.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
1. Is PAPSS actually *live* for Ethiopian institutions, or has only the central bank signed on? (Prior members show commercial-bank/switch onboarding lags the CB signing by months.)
2. The "120 million people connected" line — is that Ethiopia's population (addressable market) rather than any live-user or transaction figure? (It is population; do not read it as adoption.)
3. How many countries/central banks does PAPSS actually have now — the note implies "continental," but is it 30+ countries / ~24 CBs, and are all operationally transacting vs merely signed?
4. Does Ethiopia's FX regime genuinely support local-currency settlement, given the birr's ~120% depreciation since the 2024 float and a persistent parallel-market gap?
5. Will hard-currency scarcity in Ethiopia blunt the value — if counterparties still ultimately want USD, does netting in birr actually help?
6. Is PAPSS's local-currency netting adopted at scale anywhere, or is transaction volume still small relative to the headline country count?
7. What volume has PAPSS actually cleared to date? The announcements emphasise network breadth, not throughput — is there a dollar figure?
8. Is the COWRY 2026 co-hosting the real "news" (prestige/politics) with the membership itself largely symbolic for now?
9. Afreximbank's own financial stress/ratings pressure in 2025–26 — does the operator's balance sheet pose any risk to PAPSS as settlement infrastructure?
10. Does joining conflict with existing bilateral/correspondent arrangements or SWIFT reliance that Ethiopian banks already use?
11. Regulatory/AML angle — does local-currency, central-bank-netted settlement reduce visibility that correspondent banking historically provided?
12. Competition — how does PAPSS compare with mobile-wallet interoperability rails ([[Transfers to mobile wallets launch across six African countries]]) and stablecoin players ([[Daya raises $2.4M pre-seed for African stablecoin cross-border payments]]) for African cross-border flows?
13. PAPSS's 2027 China/India extension — is intra-African settlement maturing, or is the project already pivoting scope before African adoption is proven?
14. Is the Reuters-dated "2026-10-15" consistent with the announcement, and is Eyob Tekalign the confirmed NBE Governor at that date?
15. Does Ethiopia joining change anything for diaspora remittances, or is PAPSS purely B2B/interbank for now?

Importance: 3/5 — Ethiopia is Africa's 2nd-largest country (~120M) and a formerly closed, newly FX-liberalised economy, so its addition is a genuinely meaningful expansion of PAPSS's addressable base and a marker of the post-2024-float reform trajectory. But it is a central-bank *signing*, not proven live volume; PAPSS's continental reach is already broad (30+ countries), so this is one more member rather than a step-change, and real impact hinges on commercial-bank onboarding, birr stability, and actual settlement throughput that remain unproven.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Regional-settlement infrastructure for intra-African cross-border payments. Scale: per PAPSS/Afreximbank (via techcabal, 2026-09-10), the network now connects ~30 African countries, 24 central banks and ~200 financial institutions over 16+ payment switches; ~10 countries joined in 2026 alone. Transaction volumes rose ~1,000% and transaction values ~120% between comparable 2025 and 2026 periods (per Afreximbank PR, 2026). Backdrop: total intra-African trade was ~$213.8bn in 2025, +5.47% y/y (per African Trade Report 2026, Afreximbank). Structure: this is central-bank-grade financial market infrastructure (settlement entity = Afreximbank), not a competitive private market — a single continental utility built under the African Union/AfCFTA umbrella, so "share" is measured in country/bank onboarding, not price competition. Why now: the entry mechanism is de-dollarisation — PAPSS lets parties settle in local currencies and bypass USD-correspondent routing, which reportedly adds 2–5% per transaction on the "USD detour" (per Indepth Research / allbusiness.africa, 2026). Ethiopia adds a market of ~120m people and hosts PAPSS COWRY 2 on 2026-11-26/27.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: participating countries/central banks, connected financial institutions and switches, transaction volume vs value growth, and per-transaction cost savings. PAPSS is not really "competing" head-to-head — its rivals are (a) legacy SWIFT + USD/EUR correspondent banking (incumbent, deeply embedded) and (b) dollar-stablecoins (USDT/USDC ~43% of Sub-Saharan crypto volume, per techpoint/mexc 2026) as a bottom-up de-dollarisation alternative. Recent moves: Morocco (Bank Al-Maghrib, 2025-07, 17th country), Bank of Algeria (2025-08, 18th), Pesalink/Kenya local-currency corridor (2026-02), and now National Bank of Ethiopia (announced 2026-10-15 in Addis Ababa by Governor Eyob Tekalign and CEO Mike Ogbalu III). Protagonist position: PAPSS is the clear category leader for sovereign/central-bank African rails (niche of one at the infrastructure layer), with a moat from network effects (each central bank/switch added raises corridor value) and institutional backing (Afreximbank, AU, AfCFTA) — but this is a central-bank commitment, not yet proof of live SME/retail volume. Ethiopia's position: a new node, not yet a corridor with demonstrated throughput.

**Comps & multiples.** PAPSS is a not-for-profit central-bank-owned utility — no equity, valuation or trading multiple exists; EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E = not applicable (no listed security, no disclosed P&L). Distribution not computed; qualitative comparison only. Internal comps (same PAPSS-onboarding event type in base): [[Morocco joins Pan-African Payment and Settlement System]] (2025-07, 17th country), [[Bank of Algeria joins Pan-African Payment and Settlement System]] (2025-08, 18th; the "connects 18 countries, 150+ banks, 14 switches" baseline), [[Pesalink and PAPSS unlock cross-border payments in local currencies in Kenya]] (2026-02, corridor go-live), [[Onafriq and PAPSS to launch cross-border payments from Ghana]] (2025-06). The 2025-08 "18 countries / 150+ banks / 14 switches" vs the 2026 "~30 / ~200 / 16+" figures let one sanity-check the growth claim: country count up ~65% in ~14 months, consistent with the "~10 joined in 2026" statement. Claimed cost savings of ~27% for end users up to 92–95% per transaction, and ~$5bn/yr continental savings, are PAPSS self-cited [UNSOURCED] independently — treat as marketing, not verified economics.

**Risk flags.**
1. Announced ≠ adopted. Central-bank membership is a connection, not live volume; multiple 2026 sources flag a persistent gap between PAPSS capability and actual SME/retail usage (awareness, onboarding). The ~1,000% volume growth is off a tiny base and Nigeria-concentrated (~1,100% of it), so headline growth overstates breadth.
2. FX-liquidity / settlement risk. Local-currency settlement only works if Afreximbank/participants can source counter-currency liquidity; for a thin, non-convertible birr with Ethiopia's FX controls, corridor usability is unproven and could stall at the "connected but unused" stage.
3. Disintermediation by stablecoins. Dollar-stablecoins already carry ~43% of SSA crypto volume and solve the same USD-detour pain bottom-up; if retail/SMEs adopt USDT faster than PAPSS onboards banks, PAPSS wins the central banks but loses the actual payment flow — the opposite of its de-dollarisation goal.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector, Ethiopia (~120m people, a large unbanked market under tight FX controls) is a high-signal test of whether PAPSS converts sovereign sign-ups into real corridors rather than press releases (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: within ~12 months, Ethiopia will show disclosed PAPSS transaction value in at least one named corridor (e.g. Ethiopia–Kenya/Nigeria); trigger to watch = COWRY 2 (2026-11-26/27) and NBE/Afreximbank corridor-volume disclosures. What breaks the thesis: Ethiopia remains "connected" with no published throughput by end-2027, confirming the capability-vs-usage gap (analysis).
