Zip US Extends Cashflow Flexibility Beyond Point of Purchase with Zip® Cash
Tuesday, 13 October 2026
 NEW YORK - October 13, 2026 - Zip Co Limited (ASX: ZIP), the digital financial services company today announced plans to launch Zip® Cash*, a same-day cash loan product that extends Zip’s installment-payment experience beyond point of purchase checkout, giving eligible customers additional flexibility to everyday expenses.
Zip® Cash is built to address a common reality for millions of Americans: income and everyday expenses do not always arrive on the same schedule. Zip® Cash is an extension of Zip’s long-established and trusted Pay-in-Z products with the same consumer-first philosophy: a simple experience, transparent terms, no hard credit inquiry, and no compounding fees.
More than 100,000 Zip customers have already joined the Zip® Cash waitlist, including over 40,000 in its first 72 hours. “It gives me the peace of mind to know that the basic essentials will be taken care of,” said a Zip customer. Other customers cited fuel, essentials, and unexpectedly high bills as moments when a modest short-term buffer could help them navigate the month without judgment.
“Customers already trust us to provide flexibility at the point of purchase with our Pay-in-Z installment options, but not every expense occurs at checkout,” says Joe Heck, U.S. CEO of Zip. “Zip® Cash extends that value proposition by giving eligible customers access to funds for a broader range of everyday expenses or even a buffer between paycheck and bills. A timing gap shouldn’t have to derail someone’s plans or force them into a complicated financial product with unclear costs.”
Zip® Cash plans to initially launch to select customers in eligible states during the 2026 holiday season, with broader availability expected to follow. Eligible customers may be able to access loans of up to $500 with applicable repayment terms with funds deposited into their connected bank account as soon as the same business day. Each loan could include a flat origination fee, clearly disclosed before the customer accepts the loan. Customers would also have the option to request a change to their repayment date.
Consistent with Zip’s responsible lending approach, customers with a missed payment will not be eligible for another Zip platform loan until their outstanding payment is resolved. Unlike some industry practices that break up a cash loan into multiple fee-generating parts or rely on tips, Zip® Cash is designed to be customer-first: one loan with transparent fees.
“Zip® Cash builds on Zip’s existing Pay-in-2 platform, demonstrating the compounding value of a durable, extensible platform,” Heck said. “Similar to our other products, Zip® Cash is designed to be customer-first: one transparent loan and no hidden or junk fees. We believe we can serve customers’ short-term cashflow needs responsibly while giving them more control over their financial timeline.”
*Loans through Zip are originated by WebBank. All loans are subject to credit approval.
 About Zip 
Zip Co Limited (ACN 139 546 428) (ASX: ZIP) is a digital financial services company, offering innovative and people-centred products. Operating in Australia and the United States, Zip offers access to point-of-sale credit and digital payment services, connecting millions of customers with its global network of tens of thousands of merchants.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What it is.** Zip Co (ASX: ZIP) — the Australian BNPL group formerly known as Quadpay/Zip Co in the US — announced on 13 Oct 2026 plans to launch **Zip Cash**, a same-day **cash-advance / short-term closed-end loan** that moves beyond point-of-sale checkout. Eligible US customers can access loans **up to $500**, funded into a connected bank account **same business day**, with a **flat origination fee** (disclosed upfront), no hard credit inquiry, no compounding fees, and an option to reschedule the repayment date. Loans are originated by **WebBank** (bank-partner model). **>100,000 customers** joined the waitlist, incl. **40,000 in the first 72 hours**; initial launch to select customers in eligible states in the **2026 holiday season**, broader rollout to follow.

**Why it matters / strategy.** This is a deliberate pivot from pure BNPL-at-checkout into **everyday-expense liquidity** (fuel, bills, essentials) — the same ground as cash-advance apps. It leverages Zip's existing Pay-in-2/Pay-in-4 platform and US risk/credit infrastructure, framed by US CEO Joe Heck as "compounding value of a durable, extensible platform." The US is Zip's growth engine: in FY26 (full year to June 2026) Zip posted record group cash EBITDA ~A$269m (+58%), its US arm delivered a 2nd straight year of 40%+ top-line growth with US cash earnings +51% to ~US$155m and a 25% US operating margin; net bad debts fell to 1.67% of TTV in Q4. Zip had also flagged a potential Nasdaq listing ([[Zip targets Nasdaq listing to fuel US BNPL growth]]), so US product expansion and a deeper US TAM are strategically load-bearing.

**Regulatory angle (key).** Zip Cash lands in a **deregulated federal window**: the CFPB in 2025 withdrew its BNPL interpretive rule (Reg Z / "card issuer"), said it would not prioritize enforcement of its small-dollar lending rule, and reversed course on EWA — stating most *employer-partnered* earned-wage-access advances are **not** loans under TILA. Zip Cash, by contrast, is a **true closed-end loan with a flat fee**, so it is more squarely a credit product (bank-originated via WebBank sidesteps state usury caps/licensing via rate-export). The real risk vector is **state-level enforcement** (usury, disclosure, UDAP), which is widely expected to rise in 2026 even as the CFPB steps back. Consumer advocates (NCLC) argue fee-based short-term cash products are de-facto payday loans regardless of CFPB framing.

**Competitive set.** The Fed's 2026 BNPL review names six major US players — Affirm, Afterpay (Block), Klarna, PayPal, Sezzle, **Zip** — collectively ~$156.7bn US BNPL credit in 2025. On the cash-advance side Zip Cash competes with **Dave, Earnin, Cash App Borrow (Block), Gerald, Brigit, MoneyLion** and bank/retailer entrants (OnePay/Walmart). Zip explicitly differentiates against "tip"-based or multi-fee models ("Unlike some industry practices that break up a cash loan into multiple fee-generating parts or rely on tips") — a direct jab at Earnin/Dave-style tipping. Zip's edge is a pre-qualified installed base and checkout data; its disadvantage is a $500 cap and holiday-only initial footprint.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. What is the *effective APR* on a $500 same-day loan with a flat origination fee? Zip discloses the fee but not the implied APR — a $15–25 fee on a short repayment term can imply triple-digit APR, which would undercut the "customer-first / no junk fees" framing.
2. Is this closed-end loan actually "beyond" payday lending, or just payday lending with better UX? NCLC-style critique: a fee-bearing short-term cash advance repaid on a fixed date is functionally a small-dollar loan.
3. How durable is the **WebBank** rate-export (bank-partner) model under rising state enforcement? Which states count as "eligible," and are any excluded precisely because of usury/licensing exposure?
4. Is the "100,000 waitlist / 40,000 in 72h" figure a real demand signal or a marketing funnel (email capture ≠ underwritten, funded borrowers)? What's the expected approval rate given "subject to credit approval"?
5. What are the loss economics? US net bad debts were 1.67% of TTV for *BNPL at checkout* with merchant-anchored data — unsecured cash with no purchase anchor typically defaults higher. Is the 1.67% comp misleading here?
6. Does Zip Cash cannibalize or complement its Pay-in-2/4 BNPL revenue (merchant-funded) by shifting to consumer-fee revenue?
7. Why "holiday season 2026" only, to "select customers"? Is this a genuine launch or a capped pilot dressed as a launch (fresh-but-small)?
8. Competitive moat vs Dave/Earnin/Cash App Borrow/Gerald/MoneyLion — all with larger cash-advance user bases. Is "installed BNPL base" a real advantage or just a cheaper CAC?
9. Regulatory reversal risk: the favorable CFPB stance (withdrawn BNPL rule, deprioritized small-dollar enforcement, EWA-not-a-loan) is administration-dependent and could flip; Zip Cash is NOT an employer-partnered EWA, so the EWA safe-harbor doesn't even apply to it.
10. Funding/balance-sheet: unsecured same-day cash requires warehouse/securitization funding — is this on Zip's balance sheet, and how does it affect the US operating margin (25%) and cash earnings trajectory?
11. Does extending into non-purchase cash lending change Zip's risk profile for the mooted Nasdaq listing / investor narrative (higher-risk credit vs payments)?
12. "No hard credit inquiry" + "no compounding fees" — what reporting to bureaus occurs, and does non-reporting create adverse-selection / stacking risk across multiple cash-advance apps?
13. Is there a materiality gap between the ASX announcement (share-price mover, +3%) and actual near-term P&L contribution ($500 cap, pilot states, one quarter)?
14. Could the flat-fee-with-reschedule-option design trip state small-dollar disclosure rules even if CFPB is silent, creating 50-state compliance drag?

**Importance: 3/5** — A strategically meaningful product pivot for a top-6 US BNPL player (Zip) into the crowded cash-advance / small-dollar market, with a genuine regulatory-arbitrage angle (closed-end flat-fee loan via WebBank in a deregulated CFPB window, exposed to rising state enforcement). Caps its importance: it is still a *pilot* ($500 cap, select customers, eligible states, holiday-only), economics/APR are undisclosed, and it is incremental to Zip's already-known US growth story rather than a surprise. Clearly newsworthy and fresh (no prior coverage of Zip Cash), but not a market-defining event.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** US small-dollar "paycheck-gap" credit — cash advance / earned-wage-access (EWA) / BNPL-adjacent lending. No clean verifiable TAM from a free source → size = "no data"; proxy for demand/scale: Dave's ExtraCash originations hit US$2.3bn in Q2'26 alone (+27% YoY), per [Dave Q2'26 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001841408/000119312526335119/dave-ex99_1.htm). Structure: fragmented, many venture/neobank entrants (Dave, EarnIn, MoneyLion, Brigit, Chime) competing on speed, ticket size and fee framing; low product differentiation, so distribution/existing customer base is the real barrier, not the credit product itself (analysis). Why now: regulatory tailwind — on 23 Dec 2025 the CFPB issued an advisory opinion that "covered" EWA falls OUTSIDE TILA/Reg Z credit, reverting to the 2020 stance ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/blogs/2026/01/cfpb-says-some-earned-wage-access-products-not-considered-credit), [paymentsdive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/some-ewa-products-arent-loans-cfpb-consumer-protection-paycheck/808587/)). Crucially, the carve-out covers only no-recourse, next-payroll-deduction, no-fee-as-finance-charge products; Zip Cash is a **WebBank-originated loan with a flat origination fee** (per the note) → it is credit, not "covered EWA," so it does NOT sit in the safe harbor (analysis).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: originations volume, net bad debts / past-due rate, revenue per advance, ticket size. Players & basis of competition — Dave (avg advance ~$212, 28-day past-due 2.12%), EarnIn (advances up to $750), MoneyLion (bundles advances + investing/credit), Brigit, Chime; basis = speed-to-cash + fee transparency + cross-sell, per [Investing.com Dave Q2'26 call](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-dave-tops-growth-again-in-q2-2026-but-shares-sink-93CH-4839622). Recent moves: Zip Cash announced 13 Oct 2026, up to $500, same-day, 100k+ waitlist (40k in first 72h), holiday-season launch in select states. Zip's position: late entrant to US cash advance but with a large installed base — US TTV US$8.6bn (+42.5%) and 4.65m active US customers in FY26 ([Investing.com FY26](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/zip-fy26-presentation-record-profit-surges-58-us-growth-accelerates-93CH-4868539)). Moat = that existing US customer base + WebBank rail, not product novelty (analysis); catching up on standalone cash advance, ahead on distribution.

External peer (closest pure cash-advance comp): **Dave (NASDAQ:DAVE)** — mkt cap US$4.61bn as of 12 Oct 2026; FY26 revenue guide US$710–720m ([Dave 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001841408/000119312526206471/dave-ex99_1.htm), [macrotrends](https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/DAVE/dave/market-cap)). Zip's own US-segment multiple not computed here (group-level ASX listing; standalone Zip Cash P&L not disclosed → no data). Distribution of ≥3 clean comps not available (EarnIn/Brigit private, MoneyLion figure unsourced) → qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Regulatory — this product is likely CREDIT, not carved-out EWA.** Flat origination fee + WebBank origination + bank-account deposit means Zip Cash probably sits under TILA/Reg Z and state usury/small-dollar rules, i.e. outside the Dec-2025 CFPB EWA safe harbor; a $500 loan with a flat fee can imply a very high effective APR, inviting state AG / CFPB attention (second-order: forced fee redesign or state-by-state licensing drag). The "no compounding fees / no hard credit inquiry" framing is PR, not a regulatory exemption.
2. **Credit / bad-debt risk on a new unsecured product.** Standalone cash advances skew to thin-file, cash-strained users; Zip group net bad debts already rose to 1.77% of TTV in FY26 (US 1.7%, top of its 1.5–2.0% target) per [Investing.com FY26](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/zip-fy26-slides-cash-earnings-surge-58-us-growth-accelerates-93CH-4868534). A $500 non-purchase loan has no merchant/basket signal, so loss rates could run above the BNPL book (second-order: margin compression if losses exceed the flat fee).
3. **Dependence on WebBank rail + late-mover crowding.** Origination sits with a partner bank (concentration/key-supplier risk if the sponsor relationship changes), and Zip enters a crowded field where Dave/EarnIn/MoneyLion already own the behavior — waitlist ≠ funded, repeat usage (de-PR: company is silent on effective APR, loss assumptions and unit economics).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector: a scaled BNPL player cross-selling standalone cash advance validates the "super-app for subprime cashflow" convergence — BNPL, EWA and payday-replacement collapsing into one wallet (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: Zip Cash meaningfully lifts US revenue-per-customer without pushing US bad debts above 2.0% of TTV. Trigger/catalyst to watch: 1H-FY27 disclosure (Feb 2027) of US bad-debt rate + any Zip Cash volume/APR detail, and any CFPB/state regulatory characterization of flat-fee small-dollar loans. Thesis breaks if US net bad debts breach 2.0% or a regulator classifies the fee as a finance charge forcing a product redesign.
