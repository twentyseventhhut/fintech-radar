## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened (2026-10-21/22).** Ramp acquired **Vesto**, a New York-based corporate **treasury management** platform (founded 2022 by Benjamin/Philipp Döpfner), for an **undisclosed sum** (FintechFutures, 2026-10-21; Ramp blog). Vesto's tech is folded into a revamped Ramp **Banking** tab that brings external bank accounts and Ramp Checking/Investment accounts into one view, with transaction categorization, balance insights, and cross-account/cross-entity liquidity forecasting. Ramp claims direct connections to enterprise banking platforms (BofA CashPro, J.P. Morgan Access, CitiDirect, Wells Fargo Vantage) plus 12,000+ institutions via aggregation. New Banking view is live now; Vesto founder joins Ramp. Verified **fresh** — genuinely new M&A event, not a reprint.

**[1] Who/what is Vesto.** Pre-acquisition Vesto helped (~100+ companies, per Ramp) startups "put idle cash to work" — automated treasury/investment policies tuned to runway, liquidity horizon and risk, plus a consolidated multi-bank cash dashboard. It raised only a **$2.8M seed** (Nov 2022, led by Contrary Capital; Susa Ventures, SV Angel) per TechCrunch/Forbes. So this is a small **acqui-hire + product tuck-in**, not a materially priced deal — the value to Ramp is the multi-bank cash-visibility/forecasting layer and the team, not scale.

**[2] Why it matters / strategic context.** This is the operational build-out of the "Smart Treasury" thesis Ramp signaled in June via its [[Moment and Ramp partner on institutional cash management]] partnership (fixed-income yield on excess cash). Vesto supplies the *visibility + forecasting* half (see where cash sits across banks); Moment supplied the *yield* half (invest the excess). Ramp's framing — "control center for your business... see, report on, forecast, and move money across every account" — is an explicit push from spend-management into **treasury/business banking**, the battleground mapped in [[Connecting the Dots how business banking is splitting in two]] (Mercury = banking-first, Ramp = spend-first, both converging). It lands in a dense Ramp news cluster: a ~$1.85bn raise at **$60bn** pre-money ([[Ramp raises $1.85bn at $60bn pre-money valuation]], 2026-10-09), an **OCC national trust bank charter** application for crypto custody ([[Ramp applies for OCC charter to offer crypto custody]], 2026-10-19), and $2bn+ annualized revenue / 85,000 customers / $5bn+ customer balances.

**[3] Competitive landscape.** Treasury/cash-management is crowded: **Mercury** (banking-first, Command cash-flow tool, OCC charter approved, $1bn+ ARR), **Brex** (now Capital One-owned since the $5.15bn acquisition — cards + banking + spend), **Mesh** (spend/AP), and pure-play **Trovata** (AI-native multibank cash visibility + ML forecasting, mid-market TMS after its 2025 ATOM deal). Ramp's edge is distribution — it bolts cash visibility onto the spend platform 85,000 businesses already use, so cross-account control sits next to card/bill-pay data. Weakness: Vesto is tiny (100 companies, $2.8M ever raised), and enterprise TMS incumbents (Kyriba) plus Trovata are deeper on forecasting/bank connectivity.

**[4] What to watch.** (a) Whether the "direct connections" to CashPro/J.P. Morgan Access/CitiDirect/Vantage are true direct integrations or aggregator-mediated (materially changes enterprise credibility). (b) Whether the Moment yield engine and Vesto visibility layer actually unify into automated cash sweeping. (c) Any disclosed price or retention terms (none yet). (d) How the OCC trust charter + treasury push interacts regulatorily — Ramp still "is not a bank." (e) Adoption of the new Banking tab among the 85k base.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Vesto sits at the overlap of corporate spend management (where Ramp lives) and corporate treasury / cash-management software. The treasury-software segment is mid-sized and growing single digits: cloud corporate-treasury software ~$2.82bn in 2026 → ~$5.47bn by 2034 at ~8.6% CAGR (per IntelMarketResearch); the broader treasury-management-software market is put at ~$5–7bn in 2025-26 growing ~5-9% (per Coherent/VerifiedMarketResearch/BusinessResearchInsights) — low-growth as a standalone category, which is exactly why it is being absorbed into faster-growing spend/banking platforms rather than bought as a point solution. Structure: fragmented on the long tail (Vesto itself only served "over a hundred companies" after three years) but anchored by entrenched enterprise TMS incumbents (Kyriba, GTreasury, bank portals like BofA CashPro / J.P. Morgan Access / CitiDirect / Wells Vantage — all now connected by Ramp). Barriers are integration breadth (12,000+ institution connections) and switching costs once a finance team's cash-position view lives in one system. "Why now": the thesis is consolidation of the office-of-the-CFO stack — cards + bill pay + banking + now treasury/cash visibility in one pane — amplified by Ramp's own OCC trust-bank charter application (Oct 2026) that pushes it from spend software toward holding/moving the cash itself.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for the acquirer, annualized revenue, customer count and net revenue retention; for a treasury tool, # of bank connections, assets/cash under visibility, and forecast accuracy (none disclosed for Vesto — `[UNSOURCED]`). Basis of competition is product breadth + distribution, not price. Key players: Ramp (85,000+ businesses per the release), Brex (now a Capital One subsidiary since Apr 2026), Mercury (banking-led, crossed $1bn annualized revenue — [[Mercury crosses $1 billion in annualized revenue]]), plus TMS incumbents and the banks' own cash-management portals. Recent peer moves — all point the same way (banking ⇄ spend convergence): Capital One closed the [[Capital One to acquire Brex for $5.15 billion]] deal (Apr 2026); Mercury launched a cash-flow tool ([[Mercury launches Mercury Command cash flow tool]], Jun 2026) and partnered with Moment ([[Moment and Ramp partner on institutional cash management]], Jun 2026); Ramp itself has been bolting on adjacencies ([[Ramp acquires Juno to expand business travel offering]], [[Ramp acquires B2B payments firm Billhop for Europe expansion]], both Mar 2026). Ramp's position: ahead / consolidating — it is buying distribution-free capability (Vesto's ~100 customers are negligible vs Ramp's 85k base) to deepen the moat via switching costs and data gravity, not to buy a customer book `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal price undisclosed (both FintechFutures and the Ramp blog confirm "undisclosed") — so no transaction multiple can be computed; "no data". On the acquirer re-rating, external comps for the spend/banking cohort:
- **Ramp:** $1.85bn raised at **$60bn** post-money (Oct 15 2026, led by Dragoneer), on ~**$2bn** annualized revenue → ~`$60bn / $2bn = 30x` revenue (round valuation, not market cap; per PRNewswire/TechCrunch). NB — ir_latest.json lags here, showing only the Sep-2025 "$1bn annualized revenue" PR and earlier $22.5bn/$32bn/$44bn marks; the $60bn/$2bn figures are from Oct-2026 web sources.
- **Brex:** Capital One deal **$5.15bn** (Jan 2026, closed Apr 2026) — a ~58% markdown from Brex's 2022 $12.3bn peak (per Airwallex/Ramp/Asian Banker). Revenue not publicly broken out → EV/Rev "no data"; the headline read is distressed vs Ramp's up-round.
- **Mercury:** **$5.2bn** (Series D, May 2026 — [[Mercury raises $200 million at $5.2 billion valuation]]) on ~$0.7bn run-rate exiting 2025 → ~7x (per Hustle Fund/Forge); subsequently crossed $1bn annualized (Oct 2026, corpus).
- **Navan:** ~**$6.7bn** IPO (Oct 2025 — [[Navan raises ~$920M in IPO at $6.2B valuation]]) on ~$900m FY27e revenue → ~7x (per TechCrunch/Multiples.vc).
Flag: Ramp's ~30x revenue is a rich outlier vs the ~7x cohort (Mercury, Navan) and vs the distressed Brex mark — but it is the only hyper-grower here (revenue roughly doubled 2025→2026, ~$1.0bn→$2.0bn), so the premium is growth-justified rather than an automatic red flag `(analysis)`. Distribution not formally computed — only 2 clean revenue multiples (Mercury, Navan), qualitative comparison.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Thin acquired asset / integration execution.** Vesto had only ~100 customers over three years; value is the tech + team, not a book — so the payoff depends entirely on Ramp shipping and adopting the cash-forecasting view across 85k accounts, and on keeping founder Benjamin Döpfner. Announced ("new Banking view available today") ≠ broad adoption.
2. **Moving up the stack into balance-sheet/regulatory risk.** Combined with the OCC trust-bank charter bid ([[Ramp applies for OCC charter to offer crypto custody]]), Ramp is shifting from software margins toward holding/moving cash — inviting bank-grade regulatory, compliance and fraud-liability exposure that the PR is silent on.
3. **Valuation sensitivity.** At ~30x revenue the equity has no cushion if growth decelerates from its current ~2x pace; a single soft quarter re-rates hard against a ~7x peer set.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This is consolidation, not a new entry: standalone treasury software is being swallowed by the spend/banking super-apps, squeezing point-solution TMS vendors and the long tail of connect-your-banks startups `(analysis)`. Falsifiable thesis — within ~2-3 quarters Ramp materially monetizes cash (yield on balances / treasury fees) now that it has both visibility (Vesto) and, pending, a charter; watch the next funding/revenue update and OCC decision as the trigger. Thesis breaks if the Vesto view stays a free visibility add-on with no cash-economics attached, or if the charter is denied.
