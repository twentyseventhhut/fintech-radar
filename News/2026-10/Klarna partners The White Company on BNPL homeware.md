**London, October 12, 2026** – Klarna, the everyday finance network, today announced a partnership with British home and lifestyle retailer The White Company, enabling shoppers to spread the cost of homeware.

Home is where people spend the most and think the longest. A bed can be one of the biggest purchases of the year, so the way you pay for it deserves the same thought. The White Company's customers can now choose Klarna’s flexible options on everything from a set of towels to a full bedroom.

“Home buying is exactly where transparency and flexibility matters most,” said Klarna Country Manager UK & Ireland Marc O'Fathaigh. “The White Company’s customers deserve a quality checkout with flexible payment options that don’t disappear when the basket gets bigger."

Klarna is a natural fit for wide-ranging retailers like The White Company because there's an option at every price point: Pay in Full for everyday spend, Pay Later for mid-sized purchases on short repayment terms, and Fair Financing for the biggest buys.

Klarna is available now at The White Company’s online store, including Pay in Full, Pay in 3, and Pay in 30 days.

**About Klarna**  
Klarna is the everyday finance network. With over 120 million global active Klarna users and 3.8 million transactions per day, Klarna’s AI-powered payments and commerce network is empowering people to pay smarter with a mission to be available everywhere for everything. Consumers can pay with Klarna online, in-store and through Apple Pay & Google Pay. Over 1.2 million retailers trust Klarna’s innovative solutions to drive growth and loyalty, including Apple, Uber, H&M, Saks, Sephora, Macy’s, Ikea, Expedia Group, Nike and Airbnb. Klarna is listed on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE: KLAR). For more information, visit Klarna.com.

**About The White Company**  
Founded by Chrissie Rucker OBE, over 30 years ago, The White Company has become one of the UK’s fastest-growing multi-channel retailers and a leading lifestyle brand, with over 70 stores across the UK, including high-profile concessions in Harrods and Selfridges. July 2018 saw the opening of the first European flagship store outside of the UK on Dublin’s Grafton Street, while international expansion now includes a presence in the US – in Nordstrom stores nationwide – and in the UAE, with two stores in the prestigious Dubai Mall: a standalone store and a dedicated space within Bloomingdale’s Home. The quintessentially British brand is now synonymous with transforming houses into homes, creating impeccably stylish pieces for the home, wardrobe and everyday life – designed to be loved and last. What started as a range of white essentials for the linen cupboard has gradually grown into the ultimate luxury lifestyle destination. As of 2024, The White Company is a Certified B Corporation, meaning it meets high standards of social and environmental impact.

# Context-enrichment: Klarna partners The White Company on BNPL homeware
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On **12 Oct 2026** Klarna (NYSE: KLAR) announced it is live at UK premium home/lifestyle retailer **The White Company's online store** with its standard checkout stack: **Pay in Full, Pay in 3, and Pay in 30 days**. That is it — a merchant integration going live on one retailer's e-commerce site. No exclusivity, no economics (merchant discount rate / take), no GMV target, no in-store rollout disclosed. "Fair Financing" (longer-term instalment credit) is name-checked in the PR as available "for the biggest buys" but the live, named products are only Pay in 3 / Pay in 30 / Pay in Full.
- **Why framed this way:** The PR leans hard on a "home is the biggest, most-considered purchase" narrative ("a bed… deserves the same thought"). That narrative does real work: it reframes BNPL — politically toxic as "debt for fast fashion" — as responsible financing for durable, high-ticket goods. The quote from UK&I Country Manager Marc O'Fathaigh ("options that don't disappear when the basket gets bigger") is a direct pitch at homeware's **higher average order value**, where Pay in 3 is stickier than on a £20 impulse buy. (analysis)
- **Why it matters / second order:** This lands **three months after UK BNPL became FCA-regulated (15 Jul 2026)** — every DPC agreement now needs a proportionate affordability/creditworthiness check, standardised disclosures, Financial Ombudsman access, and S75-style protection on £100–£30,000 transactions. A homeware merchant (high AOV, affluent customer) is a comparatively low-risk, reputation-safe win in the new regime. The "flexibility/transparency" framing is as much regulatory positioning as marketing. (analysis)

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Clearpay (Afterpay)** — the other UK BNPL leader; ~28% UK share, over-indexes in fashion/beauty/footwear <£150. Weaker in homeware, which is exactly Klarna's over-indexed vertical (homewares, electronics, travel). This deal plays to Klarna's strength, not a contested beachhead.
- **PayPal Pay in 3** — embedded across SME/marketplace checkouts via existing PayPal rails; the default "already there" rival at any online checkout.
- **Zilch** — UK homegrown BNPL, pre-IPO ([[Zilch invites banks to pitch ahead of IPO]]), smaller merchant footprint.
- **Position:** Klarna is the UK's largest BNPL by active users and merchant count (blended ~65% Klarna / 35% Clearpay where both run). Adding one premium homeware brand is **parity-maintaining, incremental distribution** — not a competitive breakthrough.
- **Why the landscape is this way / second order:** BNPL's UK moat is merchant coverage + app-led engagement, not product differentiation (Pay in 3 is commoditised). So the game is logo accumulation. The margin question: in a commoditised, now-regulated product, who captures value — the network (Klarna) or the merchant acquirer? Klarna's answer is scale + its own app funnel, but each incremental mid-size merchant adds little. (analysis)

## [2] Company history / fit
Klarna in 2026 is running a relentless **UK merchant land-grab**, with The White Company one of a cluster in the same weeks: [[Klarna goes live on M&S.com and the M&S app]] (UK, 15 Oct), [[Klarna deepens Wayfair partnership with 0% APR financing]] (US homeware, 7 Oct), plus [[Ecommpay adds Klarna BNPL payment options]] and [[TAP expands Klarna installment payments to four markets]]. Klarna passed [[Klarna passes one million merchants milestone]] (now claims >1.2M retailers, 120M users, 3.8M txns/day) and earlier launched a UK debit card / wallet ([[Klarna launches debit card and digital wallet in UK]], [[Klarna signs Qatar Airways and launches UK debit card]]).
- **Why Klarna acts this way / structural pressure:** Post-IPO ([[Klarna CEO calls US fastest-growing market after $17 billion IPO]]) and after a brutal share drop ([[Klarna loses senior executives after 66% share-price drop]]), Klarna must show a credible path from a commoditised, cyclical BNPL take-rate to an "everyday finance network" with durable, higher-multiple revenue (card, savings, merchant graph). High-density merchant coverage in its home-advantage verticals (homeware) is the raw material for that narrative; each UK logo is a brick, not a building. (analysis)
- **The White Company fit:** Founded by Chrissie Rucker >30 yrs ago, ~62 UK stores + 12 concessions (Harrods, Selfridges), Ireland/US (Nordstrom)/UAE presence, **B Corp since Feb 2024**. FY to Aug 2025: turnover **£290M (-3/-4%)**, underlying EBITDA **£34.1M (-16%)**, underlying profit **£18.4M (-32%)** — a soft, transitional year (new distribution centre, suppressed online trade). An affluent, high-AOV, reputation-clean merchant; a natural Klarna "Fair Financing" target.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Is it new?** Only as a merchant go-live date. The products (Pay in 3 / Pay in 30 / Pay in Full) are Klarna's standard, years-old stack. **Nothing technologically or structurally novel.**
- **Traction?** Zero disclosed — no GMV, conversion, AOV uplift, or exclusivity. This is an **announcement, not adoption data**. The only hard adoption fact is "live now on the online store."
- **Value-add, deeper:** For The White Company the logic is conversion/AOV on big-ticket homeware (where instalments lift basket size) and a payments partner that matches its B-Corp/"responsible" positioning under the new FCA regime. For Klarna, incremental volume in its strongest vertical. **Who captures the margin:** in a commoditised Pay-in-3 world, the durable value sits with whoever owns the consumer relationship (Klarna's app/card/network) rather than the per-transaction take; a single mid-size merchant barely moves that. (analysis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Regulatory backdrop (the real story):** UK BNPL fully under FCA from **15 Jul 2026** — authorisation/TPR, mandatory affordability checks on every agreement, standardised disclosures, Ombudsman access, S75 protection £100–£30,000. Compliance cost rises for all; favours scaled, well-capitalised players (Klarna, Clearpay, PayPal) and a reputation-safe merchant mix (premium homeware over sub-prime fast fashion). Expect more "responsible, high-AOV" merchant signings framed around transparency.
- **Market:** UK BNPL projected to keep growing (double-digit %; multiple $B-scale forecasts) — a rising tide, not a Klarna-specific catalyst.
- **Sentiment / second order:** Each individual signing is low-signal for KLAR; the aggregate UK density is what underpins the "everyday finance network" multiple thesis. **Counterintuitive risk:** the FCA regime that lets Klarna look responsible also commoditises the category and raises fixed compliance cost, squeezing smaller rivals and compressing differentiation — good for Klarna's share, but it pushes the real battleground away from "Pay in 3" toward card/deposits/network ownership, where the margin actually lives. (analysis)

**Sector & drivers.** UK BNPL is a large, fast-growing but now fully-regulated market. Estimates vary by source: one Q2-2026 databook puts 2026 UK BNPL spend at ~$46.2bn growing ~14.9% p.a. (GlobeNewswire/ResearchAndMarkets, 2026-10-12); a parallel series puts it at ~$48.6bn in 2026 en route to ~$106bn by 2031 (GlobeNewswire, 2026-02-03) — treat these as secondary analyst estimates, not audited. For scale context, US BNPL originations were ~$160bn in 2025 (Federal Reserve, [[BNPL providers originated nearly $160 billion of US credit in 2025]]). The decisive "why now": from 15 July 2026 the FCA regulates BNPL ("deferred payment credit") for the first time — every agreement now requires a proportionate affordability/creditworthiness check regardless of ticket, Consumer Duty applies, Section 75-style protection kicks in on £100–£30,000 purchases, and firms must be FCA-authorised or under the Temporary Permissions Regime (FCA; GOV.UK, 2026-07-15). Structure: fintech-led and moderately concentrated — Klarna, Clearpay (Block/Afterpay) and PayPal dominate, with homegrown Zilch scaling fast; analysts explicitly expect regulation to "drive consolidation" (GlobeNewswire, 2026-10-12). Entry barriers just rose sharply (capital, credit underwriting, FCA authorisation).

Basis of competition in premium multi-channel retail: merchant distribution (checkout real-estate) and a product ladder across price points. **Verdict (headline read).** BEAT on the print, but guidance sends a mixed-to-negative signal. Revenue $1,042m (+27% YoY; vs public consensus ~$993m, i.e. a beat of ~$49m / ~+4.8%) · GAAP EPS $0.01 vs consensus ~$(0.05) loss · return to GAAP net profit of $9m · drivers: revenue-per-consumer +24% and transaction-margin leverage · FY adjusted operating income guidance effectively raised in profit terms, but FY GMV/revenue guidance CUT on soft German consumer + FX. Beat above the high end of guidance for a second straight quarter, yet shares fell premarket on the lowered top-line outlook (analysis).

**Key figures (with growth).**
- Total revenue: $1,042m, +27% YoY (+25% like-for-like / FX-neutral).
- GMV: $36.6bn, +18% YoY (+15% like-for-like).
- Transaction margin dollars (TMD): $446m, +42% YoY (+39% like-for-like); 1.22% of GMV (1.14% adjusted for a one-off sale); ~42.8% of revenue.
- Take rate (revenue / GMV): ~2.85% ($1,042m / $36,600m) (analysis), up from ~2.64% a year ago — monetization outpacing volume.
- Provision for credit (consumer) losses: $192m, +11% YoY; 0.52% of GMV vs 0.56% a year earlier — losses improving despite +18% GMV.
- Adjusted operating income: $91m, up $62m YoY (~8.7% margin).
- Reported operating income: $27m (2.6% of revenue), swinging from a ~$(46)m loss YoY.
- Net income: $9m vs $(53)m loss in Q2'25; GAAP EPS $0.01 vs $(0.14).
- Active consumers: 120m, +8% YoY; revenue per active consumer +24% (engagement/monetization, not user count, is the growth engine).
- Merchants: ~1.2m (1,208k), +54% YoY — fastest-growing headline metric.

**By segment / driver.** Growth is monetization-led: revenue +27% on GMV +18% implies take-rate expansion, with revenue per active consumer +24% doing more than the +8% consumer count. TMD +42% far outran GMV (+18%), lifting TMD-to-revenue to ~42.8% — operating leverage plus benign credit (provisions as % of GMV fell). The 1.14% adjusted TMD/GMV (ex one-off sale) is the cleaner run-rate. Merchant count +54% signals a distribution land-grab (the White Company deal is one instance) ahead of the volume it will later monetize.

**vs expectations / prior period.** Revenue beat public consensus ~$993m by ~$49m / +4.8% (consensus as of pre-print, Aug 2026; free aggregators — Investing.com / Yahoo). EPS $0.01 vs ~$(0.05) expected, a ~$0.06 beat. YoY momentum strong across the board; the swing from a net loss a year ago to net profit is the key inflection. No prior Klarna earnings note exists in the corpus to wikilink (irdb semantic search is DOWN / 402; grep of News/ found no prior Klarna earnings note).

**Guidance / forward.** Mixed. FY2026 adjusted operating income guided to $280–300m (6.9–7.2% of revenue) — more than 4x the $65m delivered in all of 2025, i.e. a profit-side raise. BUT FY2026 GMV cut to $149–151bn (from >$155bn prior) and FY revenue cut to $4.08–4.16bn (from >$4.34bn), blamed on softer German consumer spending and FX headwinds. Q3'26 (management's "investment quarter"): GMV $35–36bn, revenue $940–980m, TMD $340–360m, adjusted operating income only $5–15m (front-loaded PSP-launch marketing). Management tone: confident on profitability, cautious/defensive on European (German) consumer demand.

**Thesis-flags.**
1. Profitability inflection is real (net income positive; adj op income guided >4x FY25) → the "can Klarna earn money post-IPO" question is answered yes → de-risks the equity story; second-order: validates a monetization-led model that merchant wins like The White Company feed.
2. Top-line guidance cut on German consumer weakness + FX → demand softness in a core European market caps GMV optics even as margins improve → second-order: the stock was punished despite the beat, i.e. investors now weight growth durability over the profit beat.
3. Credit quality improving (losses 0.52% vs 0.56% of GMV; provisions +11% vs GMV +18%) → underwriting discipline holding as the book grows → supports the margin thesis; watch for reversal if the same European consumer stress cited in the guidance cut deepens.
