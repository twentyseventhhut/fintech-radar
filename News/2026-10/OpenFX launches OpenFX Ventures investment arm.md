October 8, 2026 
Introducing OpenFX Ventures
Karan Shah
,
Chief of Staff
Today we're launching OpenFX Ventures, our investment arm for the next generation of fintech founders.
The first generation of digital financial companies: Revolut, Nubank, Stripe, Alipay, and Robinhood created roughly half a trillion dollars of enterprise value over the last decade. They have largely been regional champions, and are only now starting to flex their global expansion. Historically, there were steep mountains to climb.
The onset of agentic payments will intensify the need for modern money movement rails, just as tokenized cash and securities is transforming capital markets with 24x7 settlement infrastructure. Software that transacts on behalf of individuals and businesses has no patience for three-day settlement windows, nine-to-five trading hours, and bank holidays. In the coming years, programmable rails will be what drives the market forward.
 Going global 
We are already seeing this innovation in the domestic payment space. Many countries across the globe have sophisticated RTP systems that settle in seconds, 24 hours a day. These systems are powered by APIs that grant service providers that plug into them the kind of transparency and speed that is required for modern business.
Cross-border payments simply have not kept up. The average transaction has to pass through multiple correspondents, each holding pre-funded balances, each taking fees and time. It’s a slow, opaque system that when something breaks, nobody is quite sure where the money is.
Stablecoins are doing for money what the shipping container did for freight: providing one standardized unit that can move across the network without needing to be unpacked at each port. But stablecoins aren’t enough to solve the problem, between these rails and applications that run on them is a layer of middleware that needs to be built.
That is where going global gets expensive. It takes years of work on licenses, local banking partners, liquidity, and automation to bring this middleware online. APAC requires separate licensing processes across Singapore, Hong Kong and India. The UAE has several regulators that differ depending on where you operate and what you do. Each market also has its own service providers, built to local standards.
This is the problem OpenFX was built to solve, and as we’ve worked on solving it, we’ve found places that we believe are worthy of investment.
 Where we're investing 
OpenFX builds horizontal infrastructure, across many corridors at once. The founders we want to partner with are tackling a different kind of problem, focused on a single challenge or market, a single type of customer where they can go deep.
We're particularly keen to partner with founders building in four areas:
Next-gen applications
AI is rewriting the interface, and blockchains are rewriting the infrastructure underneath it. Together, we think they make possible a new class of fintech applications that will move many more consumers and businesses onto modern payment systems.
Routing and clearing infrastructure
A lot of clearing infrastructure still has to be built: across spot FX, local-currency stablecoins, and further out for forwards, futures, swaps, and options. It won't be built on a single design. Some flows need real-time settlement, others are better served by slower, batched net settlement.
Onchain credit
Stablecoins have made moving money faster, but the credit underneath hasn't kept pace. We're interested in credit that originates onchain and is repaid from payment flows visible in real time, rather than repackaged off-chain loans.
AI-native payment operations
Identity checks, compliance, RFI case management, and transaction reconciliation drive a large share of cost per transaction in payments, and much of that work is still people reading documents and matching spreadsheets. We think AI will change that cost structure more than any rail upgrade.
 What we bring 
 Capital. We write supporting checks alongside a lead investor, for startups at stages ranging from Seed through Series B
 Connectivity. Access to the many markets we operate in across the US, Europe, LatAm, and APAC, so a new corridor doesn't mean starting from zero.
 Capabilities. Preferential pricing on our current products, early access to what we build next, and a direct line to our product and engineering teams when you integrate.
 About OpenFX 
OpenFX builds infrastructure to move, convert, and settle money across borders on stablecoin rails. In under two years it has grown to more than $80 billion in annualized payment volume, serving over 100 fintechs across the US, Europe, LatAm, and APAC. OpenFX is backed by Accel, Lightspeed Faction, Northzone, and Pantera, among others.
 Get in touch 
If you're building in any of these areas, or you think we've drawn the map wrong, we'd like to hear from you at ventures@openfx.com .

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
### [0] What happened
On 2026-10-08 OpenFX launched **OpenFX Ventures**, its investment arm backing the "next generation of fintech founders" in cross-border payments. OpenFX writes *supporting* checks alongside a lead investor at **Seed through Series B**, targeting four areas: (1) next-gen AI+blockchain applications, (2) routing & clearing infrastructure (spot FX, local-currency stablecoins, later forwards/futures/swaps/options), (3) onchain credit repaid from real-time payment flows, and (4) AI-native payment operations (KYC, compliance, RFI, reconciliation). Thesis: stablecoins alone cannot fix cross-border payments — a **middleware layer** between rails and applications still has to be built.
Source: <https://www.openfx.com/blog/introducing-openfx-ventures>, <https://globalfintechseries.com/banking/digital-payments/openfx-launches-openfx-ventures-to-partner-with-the-next-generation-of-cross-border-payments-companies/>

### [1] Who / the company
OpenFX builds infrastructure to move, convert and settle money across borders on **stablecoin rails**. Founded 2024 by FalconX co-founder Prabhakar Reddy. Per the launch post, in under two years it reached **>$80B annualized payment volume** serving **>100 fintechs** across US, Europe, LatAm and APAC. Backed by Accel, Lightspeed Faction, Northzone and Pantera (Series A syndicate also named Atomico and M13). The legal entity is Red Envelope Delta, Inc. (NMLS ID 2680829).
Source: <https://www.openfx.com/blog/introducing-openfx-ventures>, <https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/03/31/forex-startup-openfx-raises-usd94-million-to-expand-stablecoin-powered-cross-border-payments>

### [2] Internal context (prior notes)
- [[OpenFX announces $94m Series A for blockchain FX venue]] (2026-04) — the $94M Series A that scaled the venue; at that point volume was ~$45B annualized, now >$80B, so Ventures follows a doubling of run-rate.
- [[OpenFX report stablecoins still just 1% of global payment flows]] (2026-03) — OpenFX's own thesis that stablecoins remain only ~1% of global payment flows despite $27.6T 2024 volume; the "middleware gap" framing in the Ventures post is the direct continuation of this research.
- [[OpenFX acquires Embed for EU and UK payments licences]] (2026-06) — regulatory/footprint buildout (EU/UK licences) that underpins the "Connectivity" OpenFX offers portfolio companies.
- [[BVNK gets strategic investment from Citi Ventures]] (2025-10) — comparable: strategic capital flowing into stablecoin payments infrastructure; here OpenFX moves from *recipient* to *deployer* of strategic capital.

### [3] External context / trend
Strategic (CVC) capital into digital-assets and stablecoin infrastructure was a defining feature of H1'26: payments investment jumped to **$44.2B in H1'26** vs $20.2B for all of 2025, and VC into stablecoin-related companies exceeded **$1.5B** this year (vs <$50M in 2019). Crypto/infra platforms launching or deploying venture arms (e.g. Citi Ventures into BVNK) is now a recognised pattern. OpenFX Ventures fits this: an operating payments-infra company turning its corridor network, licences and product access into a strategic-investing edge, favouring infrastructure over speculative early-stage bets.
Source: <https://kpmg.com/xx/en/what-we-do/industries/financial-services/pulse-of-fintech.html>, <https://www.fintechfutures.com/venture-capital-funding/key-fintech-funding-trends-in-2026>, <https://ffnews.com/news/introducing-openfx-ventures-3a65476c>

### [4] Why it matters
OpenFX converts operational scale (>$80B volume, multi-region licences) into a strategic-investing flywheel: by funding middleware, routing/clearing, onchain credit and AI-ops startups at Seed–Series B, it can shape the ecosystem that plugs into its own rails — a classic infra-platform CVC land-grab. No disclosed fund size, check size, or standalone capital pool, which limits how material this is financially; it reads more as ecosystem strategy than a large dedicated fund.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a strategic-CVC launch, not a funding round for OpenFX — so no new valuation attaches to the event itself. The backdrop is stablecoin-rail cross-border payments. Sizing is wide and methodology-dependent: McKinsey/Artemis put B2B stablecoin flows at ~$226bn annualized, +733% YoY (per McKinsey & Artemis, via stablecoininsider.org, Feb 2026); FXC Intelligence frames the addressable cross-border pool at upwards of $17.9tn with stablecoins today under ~1% of global cross-border volume (via crypto.news, 2026). Treat the big TAM as a ceiling, not current revenue. Structure: fragmented and early — value is migrating from correspondent-banking fees toward a "middleware" layer (licensing, local banking partners, liquidity, clearing) that sits between stablecoin rails and apps; barriers are regulatory/licensing-heavy and multi-jurisdictional (APAC per-market licensing: Singapore/Hong Kong/India; UAE multi-regulator), which is itself the moat OpenFX claims. Drivers / "why now": (1) agentic + 24x7 tokenized settlement makes 3-day/9-to-5 bank rails untenable (analysis, from the company's thesis); (2) CVC as a strategic weapon — in 2025 ~22% of global VC flowed through corporate arms, up from ~15% three years prior (per spectup.com, 2026). A CVC lets OpenFX buy optionality on the middleware stack it needs built around its rails.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: annualized payment volume (TPV), number of corridors/trading pairs, settlement speed, fintech customers served. OpenFX disclosed: >$80bn annualized volume and 100+ fintechs served (note, 2026-10-08) — roughly a 2x step-up from the ~$45bn cited at its Series A (per Unchained/CoinDesk, Mar 2026), itself up from ~$4bn a year prior; >98% of txns settle under 60 min across 40+ pairs (Mar 2026 press). Players & basis of competition: incumbent cross-border rails (correspondent banking, Convera [[Convera appoints four senior leaders including COO and CTO]]), stablecoin-native challengers (Noah [[Noah closes $38M seed after extra $16M raise]], non-USD FX infra IMMIX [[IMMIX secures 3.12M euros for stablecoin FX infrastructure]], Rain, Yellow Card [[Yellow Card launches Global Accounts for cross-border payments]]), and the big strategic CVCs OpenFX now competes with for deals (Visa Ventures, Mastercard Start Path, PayPal Ventures, per fintechjournal/spectup, 2026). The CVC's edge is distribution: a check comes with corridor access and preferential pricing on OpenFX rails. Position: OpenFX is a scaled challenger (ahead on volume vs. seed-stage stablecoin FX peers), using CVC to deepen a distribution/network moat (analysis) — not a pure-financial investor, so returns are secondary to pulling the ecosystem onto its rails.

**Comps & multiples.** The event has no deal economics. For reference, OpenFX's own last round: $94m Series A at ~$500m post-money (per kucoin.com/Unchained, Mar 2026) on ~$45bn annualized TPV ⇒ valuation/TPV ≈ $0.5bn / $45bn = ~0.011x (round valuation, not market cap; and TPV is gross flow, not revenue — so this is a crude scale ratio, NOT an EV/Revenue). Revenue/take-rate undisclosed ⇒ EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E = no data. Internal comps (same-month stablecoin-FX/cross-border): [[IMMIX secures 3.12M euros for stablecoin FX infrastructure]] (~€3.12m, pre-revenue infra, Oct 2026); [[Noah closes $38M seed after extra $16M raise]] ($38m seed, enterprise cross-border-on-stablecoin, Oct 2026). Both are far earlier-stage/smaller than OpenFX; distribution not computed (<3 comparable valuation figures) → qualitative only: OpenFX sits well above its stablecoin-FX peer set on scale but below card-network CVC parents it now co-invests alongside.

**Risk flags.**
- Disintermediation / middleware capture — OpenFX's own thesis ("stablecoins aren't enough; a middleware layer must be built") is double-edged: if that layer standardizes (or card networks / Stripe-Bridge build it), OpenFX's rails become commoditized plumbing and margin shifts to whoever owns the app/clearing layer.
- Regulatory / multi-jurisdiction execution — the licensing maze it cites as a moat (APAC per-market, UAE multi-regulator) is also concentrated execution/compliance risk; a CVC can't de-risk its own operating licenses.
- CVC distraction & adverse selection — a sub-scale company (NMLS-registered MSB, ~2 yrs old, writing supporting checks alongside leads) spreading into Seed–Series B investing risks diluting focus and seeing only deals its larger CVC rivals passed on; no fund size disclosed (who's silent on capital committed).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** A scaled stablecoin-rail player standing up a CVC signals the sector is shifting from "build the rail" to "own the ecosystem around the rail" — an early consolidation/platform-lock move, not a re-rating (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: OpenFX Ventures is a distribution flywheel, not a returns vehicle — watch for portfolio companies that integrate OpenFX rails as a condition/benefit of the check. It breaks if the arm makes purely financial bets unconnected to its corridors, or if a card network / Stripe-class player ships the competing middleware stack first. Trigger to watch: first announced OpenFX Ventures deals and whether they bundle rail integration.
