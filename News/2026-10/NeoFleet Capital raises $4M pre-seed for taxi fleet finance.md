NeoFleet Capital raises $4M to scale professional taxi fleets and build infrastructure for autonomous mobility 
Press Release
  NeoFleet Capital , a global investment and technology platform financing professional taxi fleets, has raised $4 million in pre-seed funding. Mark Loughran , former President and CFO of the global ride-hailing operator inDrive , backed the round alongside other private investors and has joined NeoFleet Capital as President and co-founder.
 “We address a fundamental problem in many high-growth mobility markets: people need rides, drivers want to work, and capital is the missing piece to put more cars on the road,” explained Mark Loughran, President and co-founder of NeoFleet Capital. 
The company has developed a Fleet Management Franchise model that combines vehicle financing, fleet management technology, operations and maintenance, and insurance solutions. NeoFleet estimates the global mobility fleet asset class at over $500 billion today.  
Professional fleet operators currently manage an estimated 20–30% of the world’s taxis, according to NeoFleet Capital. The shift toward autonomous vehicles could drive that share close to 100%, as the absence of a driver does not remove the need to finance, insure, clean, charge, maintain, repair, reposition, and maximize the uptime of each vehicle.
 Goldman Sachs Research forecasts that the global commercial robotaxi market alone could reach roughly $415 billion by 2035 with a fleet of around six million vehicles. NeoFleet believes this growth will also create a much larger institutional asset class around mobility infrastructure. 
 "A vehicle generates income for a driver every day. This helps move them from poverty towards middle-income earnings, increases access to mobility for passengers, and ultimately supports the wider economy," added Loughran. "At the same time, enormous pools of institutional capital are looking for attractive, asset-backed returns but have no scalable way of reaching these fragmented markets. NeoFleet is building the infrastructure that bridges that gap."  
The proceeds of the $4 million round will be used to enter additional high-growth mobility markets across Africa, Latin America, and Asia. By the end of 2027, NeoFleet aims to support approximately 5,000 vehicles across its network, representing an estimated $75–100 million in vehicle value. That expansion is intended to establish the operating, financing, and technology infrastructure required for NeoFleet’s longer-term ambition: becoming a major global capital and fleet-management platform for both human-driven and autonomous mobility.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened (one line).** NeoFleet Capital, a Limassol (Cyprus)-based platform financing professional taxi fleets in emerging markets, raised a **$4M pre-seed** round (announced ~2026-10-08). Investment split roughly 50/50 between **DMTech VC** (~$2M; a fintech / asset-backed-finance fund) and **Mark Loughran** (~$2M), ex-President/CFO of ride-hailing operator inDrive, who joined as President and co-founder. Founder/CEO is **Igor Shiyanov** (ex-inDrive, ex-BCG). No formally named "lead."

**[1] What NeoFleet does / the model.** NeoFleet runs a **"Fleet Management Franchise"** model that bundles four things into one stack: (a) **vehicle financing** (co-financing cars with local fleet operators), (b) fleet-management technology, (c) operations & maintenance, and (d) insurance. Economically it treats **professional taxi fleets as an institutional, asset-backed asset class** — connecting fragmented emerging-market fleet operators to pools of institutional capital that want asset-backed yield but lack a scalable channel to reach these markets. Revenue/returns derive from each vehicle's daily ride income.

**[2] Scale & geography (calibration — this is early-stage).** Currently live in **Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire and Peru**; proceeds fund expansion across Africa, Latin America and Asia. Reported footprint ~**650 vehicles (~$10M purchase value)** today, targeting **1,000 vehicles by end-2026** and **~5,000 by end-2027** (~$75–100M vehicle value). Company cites a global mobility-fleet asset class **>$500B**, and leans on Goldman Sachs Research's **~$415B / ~6M-vehicle robotaxi market by 2035** as the long-run autonomy thesis (no driver still needs financing, insurance, charging, maintenance, repositioning).

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Vehicle/fleet financing for emerging-market ride-hailing drivers. Global automotive-finance TAM ~$315–330bn in 2025, projected ~7–7.6% CAGR to ~$620–670bn by 2035 (per SNS Insider / The Business Research Co. / Precedence, via marketresearch aggregators, as of 2026 — spread across firms; treat as order-of-magnitude, not precise). Fintech/NBFC lenders are the faster-growing slice (~8% CAGR, per Precedence, as of 2026). NeoFleet's own TAM claim — "global mobility fleet asset class >$500bn" — is a self-serving PR framing; de-PR'd it overlaps the automotive-finance figure above, so not an independent data point. Structure: highly fragmented and under-penetrated in Africa/LatAm/Asia, where drivers lack credit history and formal banking rails — that gap is both the opportunity and the risk (thin-file underwriting). Barriers to entry are capital/balance-sheet and local operations (maintenance, insurance, repossession), NOT technology. Why now: (1) ride-hailing growth outpacing formal auto-lending reach in emerging markets; (2) the autonomous-fleet narrative (Goldman Sachs Research cited by NeoFleet forecasts ~$415bn robotaxi market / ~6m vehicles by 2035) is being used to re-frame driver-financing as a future "institutional mobility-infrastructure asset class" — this is thesis, not current revenue.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: fleet size (vehicles financed), vehicle value / book, utilization/uptime, default & repossession rates, net interest margin on drive-to-own loans. NeoFleet position: sub-scale new entrant — ~650 vehicles, ~$10m purchase value as of Oct-2026, targeting 1,000 by end-2026 and 5,000 (~$75–100m vehicle value) by end-2027. Operates Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire, Peru. Differentiator claimed is a "Fleet Management Franchise" bundle (financing + fleet-mgmt tech + ops/maintenance + insurance) plus ex-inDrive operator pedigree (Mark Loughran, former inDrive President/CFO, now co-founder). Basis of competition = distribution + local ops + funding cost, not product.
Key players & recent moves:
- **Moove** — the scaled incumbent. Raised $250m Series C at a **$2.1bn** valuation (Mubadala-led, Aug-2026), up from ~$750m in 2024; pivoting to autonomous-fleet ops (Waymo Phoenix/Miami) and a reported ~$1.2bn debt raise. Already past the controversy stage NeoFleet hasn't reached — see [[Moove rejects claims it worsened driver vehicle financing in South Africa]].
- **GoCab** — [[GoCab raises $45M to scale electric mobility in Africa]] (Feb-2026, debt+equity, EV drive-to-own for drivers/couriers in Africa) — closest in-base direct peer by model and geography, ~11x NeoFleet's raise.
- **Naran** — UAE-based, raised ~$10m (Aug-2026) for rent-to-own vehicle financing in Colombia, Peru, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire — **near-identical geography to NeoFleet**, better funded, direct head-to-head (web; not in base).
- **Finmid / Bolt** — [[Finmid raises €17M extension, strikes Bolt financing deal]] (Oct-2026) — embedded-lending rail giving Bolt fleet operators up to €400k/4yr for vehicles; a platform-rail alternative to NeoFleet's balance-sheet model.
Moat: `(analysis)` none proven yet — at 650 vehicles the ops/franchise bundle is unvalidated; local ops and funding cost are the only durable edges and NeoFleet has neither at scale.

**Comps & multiples.** Private early-stage — no revenue/EBITDA disclosed, so trading multiples not computable; comparison is round-size/implied-asset basis.
- NeoFleet: $4m pre-seed; ~$10m vehicle value today → implied ~0.4x raise/assets (`$4m / $10m`), i.e. equity is seed/infra capital, NOT loan funding (fleet purchases need separate debt — unshown).
- Moove: $250m / $2.1bn post-money valuation, scaled book — `[[Moove rejects claims it worsened driver vehicle financing in South Africa]]`; ~525x NeoFleet's raise, different league.
- GoCab: $45m debt+equity — `[[GoCab raises $45M to scale electric mobility in Africa]]`.
- Naran: ~$10m debt+equity (web).
Peer post-money / P/S multiples for the private names = **no data** (undisclosed). Qualitative read: NeoFleet is the smallest, earliest and least-funded entrant in a field already containing a $2.1bn incumbent and two better-capitalized direct rivals in the exact same countries — distribution not computed, qualitative only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Funding-cost / balance-sheet gap.** Financing fleets at scale is a capital business; $4m equity funds infra, not vehicles. To hit 5,000 vehicles (~$75–100m) NeoFleet needs debt/term-funding it hasn't disclosed — in emerging markets at high local rates. Who provides it, and at what cost, is silent (second-order: NIM could be squeezed before scale).
2. **Driver-default & social/regulatory risk.** Drive-to-own lending to low-income drivers has already produced a reputational/regulatory backlash for the category leader (Moove, South Africa). NeoFleet's thin-file borrowers in Senegal/Côte d'Ivoire/Peru carry FX, repossession and conduct risk; a scandal pre-scale would be existential.
3. **Autonomous-fleet thesis is a narrative, not a cash flow.** The robotaxi/$500bn-asset-class framing (same deck as Moove's) is used to justify a $4m pre-seed with 650 cars; execution risk is that today's business is a sub-scale, capital-starved EM auto-lender, and the autonomy optionality is years out and contested by far larger players.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Emerging-market fleet finance is consolidating into a two-tier market: one scaled, well-funded leader (Moove, $2.1bn) and a crowd of sub-$50m challengers (GoCab, Naran, NeoFleet) chasing the same African/LatAm corridors — NeoFleet is entering a race already crowded in its exact geographies. Falsifiable thesis: NeoFleet either secures a large debt facility (the real unlock) within ~12 months or stalls at low-thousands of vehicles. Watch/trigger: announcement of a warehouse/term-debt line and disclosed default rates — absence of either by mid-2027 would confirm it never scaled past the pre-seed franchise pilot.
