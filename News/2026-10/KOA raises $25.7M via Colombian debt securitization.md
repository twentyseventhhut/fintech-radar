## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What KOA is.** KOA is a Colombian 100% digital *compañía de financiamiento* (financing company) supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC), which granted its operating license in June 2025 ([[Colombia's KOA granted financing entity operating license]]). Its lending franchise comes from the January 2026 merger with ExcelCredit, a 13+ year specialist in *libranza* (payroll/pension-deduction) credit to pensioners and state employees ([[Koa merges with ExcelCredit to consolidate fintech position]]). KOA's focus is financial inclusion for older/underserved borrowers (pensioners), with loan repayment discounted directly at source from pension/payroll — structurally low-default collateral.

**The deal (debt, not equity).** On ~2026-10-07 KOA closed a COP 85,000M (~$25.6–25.7M) *titularización* (securitization) on the Bolsa de Valores de Colombia (BVC). This is receivables-backed funding, not a VC/equity round: KOA packages future libranza loan cash flows into tradable notes and gets upfront cash to redeploy into new lending.

**Structure (verified via Valora Analitik / La República / LatamFintech).**
- Tranching: Class A note COP 75,000M sold to the investor market; Class B (subordinated/first-loss) retained by KOA — a standard credit-enhancement waterfall.
- Rating: AAA (national scale), stable outlook, from S&P Global Ratings (national ratings).
- Pricing: base allotment at ~15% effective annual rate; demand ~1.88x bid-to-cover (oversubscribed).
- Arranger/structurer: Titularice (a non-mortgage asset securitization company).
- Significance: KOA's *first* securitization as an SFC-supervised financing company, and the *fourth* payroll/libranza securitization across KOA/ExcelCredit's history — i.e. a repeat, proven funding channel, not a one-off.

**Why it matters (market context).** LatAm (and specifically Colombian) fintech lenders are increasingly tapping *local capital markets / structured debt* rather than offshore VC to fund loan books — a sign of maturation and a response to tighter venture funding. Direct Colombian comps in the DB:
- [[Welli raises $75M structured debt in Colombia]] — Oct 2025, CIM-funded structured debt for medical-procedure financing.
- [[Colombian fintech Addi raises $85M Series D]] — Jul 2026; Addi has stacked >$680M in debt facilities (Victory Park, J.P. Morgan warehouse) alongside equity.
- [[Zippi raises ~$15.9M via securitized structure in Brazil]] — Oct 2025, COP-analog BRL 85M securitization via Itaú/Verde — same playbook in Brazil.
- Kala (not yet in DB): COP ~55.9bn (~$63M) securitization, first under a ~$286.6M programme, also lending to pensioners/workers — KOA's closest structural peer.
Backdrop: ~37% of Colombians rely on informal 'gota a gota' loans ([[This Week in Fintech 37% of Colombians use informal 'gota a gota' loans]]) — the inclusion gap KOA's libranza model targets.

**Caveats / what's unconfirmed.** FX: sources quote COP 85,000M; the $25.6M (Dealroom) vs $25.7M (TWIF) gap is just an FX-rounding artifact — same deal. No new equity, no new investor names beyond market buyers of Class A; "investors" = BVC fixed-income market, not a named fund. KOA separately projects >COP 400,000M of *CDT Digital* (digital term-deposit) captation in 2026 — a parallel funding leg, not part of this securitization.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
Red-team questions (skeptical; several already answered by external sources, flagged inline):

1. Is this debt or equity? **Debt** — a receivables securitization, not a funding round. Headlines calling it a "raise" risk conflating it with VC; the equity story is separate (merger + CDT deposits).
2. $25.6M vs $25.7M — contradiction? No: both = COP 85,000M; pure FX rounding. Not two deals.
3. Who actually bought the paper? Only the Class A (COP 75,000M) went to the public BVC fixed-income market; KOA retained Class B. "Investors" are anonymous market buyers, not a named strategic/fund. Don't imply a marquee backer.
4. Is AAA meaningful? It's a **national-scale** S&P rating (relative to Colombian issuers), NOT global AAA. Easy to over-read.
5. Is 15% a high cost of funds? In COP terms with Colombian policy rates elevated, ~15% is plausible/market; without the benchmark rate it's hard to judge the spread. Unverified vs Colombia's policy rate at issuance.
6. Is "first securitization as supervised company" but "fourth libranza securitization" contradictory? No — the prior three predate SFC supervision (ExcelCredit-era). Confirms a repeat channel, not a debut.
7. How concentrated is the collateral? Libranza = payroll/pension-deduction loans; low default by design but exposed to public-pension/payroll counterparties and any regulatory change to libranza discounting rules. Concentration/regulatory tail risk unquantified.
8. Does $25.7M move the needle for KOA's loan book size? Unknown — no disclosed total AUM/loan book for KOA post-merger. Can't size impact.
9. Is Titularice independent / arm's length? Arranger is a specialist securitization co; no disclosed conflict, but relationship/fee terms not public.
10. Bid-to-cover 1.88x — strong or routine? Oversubscribed (good), but for a small, AAA, state-cashflow-backed COP deal, healthy demand is expected. Not evidence of exceptional appetite.
11. Is this newsworthy globally or only locally? Primarily a local-capital-markets maturation signal; limited cross-border read-through beyond "LatAm lenders funding via domestic structured debt."
12. Duplicate of the Jan-2026 merger or Jun-2025 license notes? No — distinct event (Oct 2026 funding), though it depends on both. Fresh.
13. Any sign of distress (why securitize now vs raise equity)? Securitization is cheaper/non-dilutive for a licensed lender with provable receivables — a sign of *maturity*, not distress. But absent financials, can't rule out a funding-cost squeeze.
14. Is the source quality adequate? Primary Colombian trade press (Valora Analitik, La República, LatamFintech) + Dealroom corroborate structure; the note's own body cites only TWIF + Dealroom with no full text pulled.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** KOA is a Colombian consumer lender in the libranza (payroll-deduction) credit niche, skewed to pensioners/retirees and the formally-underbanked — a segment where repayment is deducted at source, structurally lowering default risk. Colombia's alternative-lending market is ~US$315.7m (2025), growing ~14.8% CAGR 2020–25 toward ~US$363.8m in 2026 and ~US$529.6m by 2029 (per Research&Markets/GlobeNewswire "Colombia Alternative Lending Business Report 2026", as of 2026-02). Only ~12% of Colombians hold a credit card and >30m adults lack formal credit access (per Nu Colombia via Forbes.co, 2025-10) — a large, chronically-underserved base and the core "why now": fintechs are racing to formalize thin-file/excluded segments. Structurally, lending is fragmented across ~400 fintechs plus bank incumbents (Bancolombia, Banco de Bogotá); the binding constraint is not demand but *funding the loan book* — equity is dilutive, deposits are slow to build, so local-market debt is the swing variable. KOA is NOT a pure app: it is a regulated Compañía de Financiamiento, licensed by the Superintendencia Financiera in June 2025, formed from the 2023 entity + a 2024 merger with ExcelCredit (a decade-old libranza lender), and it takes retail deposits via Fogafín-backed digital CDTs (per latamfintech.co / Portafolio, 2025). That regulated, deposit-plus-securitization funding stack is its real differentiator vs VC-funded app lenders.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs for a book-lender of this type: loan-book size / AUM, net interest margin, cost of funds, NPL/default rate, and funding diversification (equity vs warehouse vs securitization vs deposits). KOA's disclosed hook is payroll-deduction credit for retirees from ~1.45% N.M.V. (per koa.co) — but it discloses no loan-book size, NPL, or NIM `[UNSOURCED]`. Basis of competition in libranza is distribution (access to pension/payroll agreements) and cost of funds, not UX. Direct peer set: in-base [[Welli raises $75M structured debt in Colombia]] (Colombia, medical-procedure lending, structured debt, 2025-10) and [[Nu Colombia launches credit products for thin-file customers]] (same inclusion thesis, deeper pockets, 2025-10). The tightest analog is [[Agibank raises $410M via third receivables fund]] (Brazil, FIDC III, payroll loans to retirees/pensioners, same 2026-10 week) — identical segment and funding mechanism at 16x the scale. Also in-base: [[Zippi raises ~$15.9M via securitized structure in Brazil]] and [[Kanastra raises $30M Series B for Brazil private credit]]. Position: niche incumbent-plus — ExcelCredit lineage gives real underwriting history in libranza (moat = switching costs / distribution agreements + a regulatory licence that app-only rivals lack), but it is sub-scale and capital-markets-dependent `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** This is a *debt* raise, not an equity round — there is no valuation, revenue, or EBITDA disclosed, so trading multiples (EV/Rev, EV/EBITDA, P/E) = **no data / not computable**; distribution not computed, qualitative comparison only. Funding-size comparison across in-base securitization/receivables deals for the inclusion-lending segment:
- KOA — ~US$25.7m, debt securitization, Bolsa de Valores de Colombia (2026-10).
- [[Agibank raises $410M via third receivables fund]] — ~US$410m, FIDC III (R$2.1bn), retiree/pensioner payroll loans (2026-10). ~16x KOA.
- [[Welli raises $75M structured debt in Colombia]] — US$75m structured debt from CIM, Colombian healthcare lending (2025-10). ~2.9x KOA.
- [[Zippi raises ~$15.9M via securitized structure in Brazil]] — ~US$15.9m (R$85m), micro-entrepreneur credit (2025-10). ~0.6x KOA.
KOA's raise sits at the small end — mid-way between Zippi and Welli, far below Agibank. Reference points: RCI Colombia raised ~US$53m in bonds on the BVC (2024-04), and Credicorp Capital's private-credit fund deployed ~US$107.6m across 10 firms by 2025-03 — i.e. a US$25.7m BVC securitization is a modest but credible local-market ticket, not an outlier. No "rich/cheap" call possible absent valuation.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Funding-model dependence / rollover risk.** Securitization funds the book but is episodic and priced to local rates; if Colombian rates or spreads widen, or investor appetite for a sub-scale issuer cools, the next tranche may not clear — growth is gated by capital-markets access, not demand. Second-order: a stalled funding pipeline forces either equity dilution or a lending slowdown.
2. **Segment / concentration concentration.** Heavy skew to pensioner/retiree libranza concentrates exposure to (a) regulatory caps on payroll deductions and interest/fee rates (Colombia enforces usury-rate ceilings; see IBA on digital-lending rate rules) and (b) one demographic's pension-flow durability. A cap cut or pension-policy change hits margin directly.
3. **Scale + cost-of-funds disadvantage vs incumbents and Nu.** At ~US$25.7m per raise KOA is orders of magnitude below Bancolombia or Nu Colombia; a well-capitalized neobank entering the same inclusion segment can undercut on price while KOA pays a securitization premium for funding. Disintermediation risk if deep-pocketed rivals target libranza.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is a second LatAm data point in the same week (with Agibank) that payroll-loan / inclusion lenders are funding growth through *local* securitization rather than offshore VC — a maturing of Colombian/LatAm private-credit rails, not a re-rating of KOA itself. Falsifiable thesis: if KOA returns to the BVC with a larger second tranche within ~12 months at comparable or tighter spreads, the local-securitization funding route is proven for sub-scale regulated fintechs; what breaks it: no follow-on issuance, a widening of Colombian credit spreads, or an NPL spike in the libranza book forcing a pivot back to equity. Watch next: tranche 2 size/spread and any disclosed loan-book/NPL figure.
