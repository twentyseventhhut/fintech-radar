# Ant International sweeps idle cash into HSBC tokenized fund

> [!info] 2026-10-23 · 1 упоминаний · 1 источника(ов) с текстом

[Connecting the Dots in Fintech] 🌐 Ant International uses HSBC tokenized deposits to sweep idle cash into a tokenized fund. The move swept surplus treasury cash into a tokenized money market fund from Franklin Templeton via WhaleRTP. It is Ant's second such deal in four months.

Ant International has used HSBC’s tokenized deposits to sweep excess corporate treasury funds into a Franklin Templeton tokenized money market fund ( MMF ). The subscription was orchestrated through WhaleRTP, Ant’s blockchain-based treasury platform. The news emerged through LinkedIn posts rather than a formal announcement.
The attraction for a treasurer is timing. Conventional MMFs have dealing cut offs in the early afternoon and settle at best the same day, so any cash that might be needed overnight or first thing stays in a bank account earning little. A company such as Ant, which moves money in 17 currencies around the clock, accumulates idle balances across time zones for exactly that reason. If both the subscription and the redemption can happen at any hour, surplus cash can earn a fund yield until the moment it is needed, with the tokenized deposit providing the instant rail in and out.
This is the second time Ant has done this in four months. In June it started using digital share classes of an Amundi MMF issued on Ethereum, an arrangement kept within the Credit Agricole group, which owns Amundi and whose CACEIS arm acts as tokenization agent. The HSBC version lacks that vertical integration. The bank’s tokenized deposits run on a permissioned ledger, Franklin’s Benji platform issues fund tokens on public blockchains, and WhaleRTP, which already connects to more than 20 banks, appears to be the orchestration hub that joins the two. HSBC described it as a first transaction, so this is a pilot rather than a live product.
“The solution helps clients to optimise liquidity by investing surplus funds when available and accessing them when needed, with the potential for around-the-clock transactions enabled by the benefits of tokenisation,” said Martijn Stoker, HSBC’s Global Head of GPS Liquidity. Which raises the question we flagged in June. Why would a bank build tools that help clients move cash out of its deposits?

## Контекст

### [0] What happened (one-paragraph)
Ant International used HSBC's Tokenised Deposit Service (TDS) to sweep surplus corporate treasury cash into a Franklin Templeton tokenized money market fund (the Benji / FOBXX franchise), with the subscription orchestrated through **WhaleRTP**, Ant's blockchain-based treasury platform. HSBC called it a "first" transaction, so this is a pilot rather than a live product; the news surfaced via LinkedIn posts, not a formal announcement (Ledger Insights, 2026-10-23). The attraction is timing: conventional MMFs have early-afternoon dealing cut-offs and same-day-at-best settlement, so overnight cash sits idle in a bank account. Ant, which moves money in 17 currencies around the clock, accumulates idle balances across time zones; if both subscription and redemption can happen at any hour via a tokenized deposit as the instant rail, surplus cash can earn a fund yield right up until it is needed. HSBC's Martijn Stoker (Global Head of GPS Liquidity) framed it as helping clients "optimise liquidity … with the potential for around-the-clock transactions." This is Ant's **second** such tokenized-MMF deal in four months.

### [1] Why it matters / significance
This pushes tokenization one layer up the stack — from tokenized deposits (a 24/7 payment/settlement rail, which Ant/HSBC already launched in 2025) to a tokenized *investment* product, closing the loop so idle treasury cash earns yield on-chain instead of sitting dormant. It is a concrete, revenue-relevant RWA use case rather than a PoC: a real corporate treasurer (Ant, a sophisticated reference client) moving real surplus cash. It also raises the structural tension HSBC itself flagged — why would a deposit-taking bank build tools that help clients move cash *out* of its deposits? The answer is fee/relationship capture and defending against stablecoins and non-bank tokenized-MMF rails: the bank keeps ownership of the "in/out" plumbing even as the cash earns a fund yield elsewhere.

### [2] Key players / entities
- **Ant International** — Alipay+/WorldFirst parent; blockchain for internal treasury since 2019; proprietary **Whale** platform (WhaleRTP connects 20+ banks) is the orchestration hub. See [[Amundi, CACEIS and Ant International advance blockchain tokenisation]], [[Standard Chartered rolls out tokenized deposits on Ant's Whale]].
- **HSBC** — provides the permissioned-ledger **Tokenised Deposit Service**; Ant was its first TDS client in HK (Sept 2025) and first MEA client (Oct 2026). See [[HSBC launches tokenized deposit service with Ant International]], [[Ant International and HSBC expand tokenized treasury to MEA]].
- **Franklin Templeton** — issues the fund tokens via its **Benji** platform (FOBXX / Franklin OnChain U.S. Government Money Fund; ~$760M AUM, ~3.66% 30-day APY as of early Oct 2026) on public blockchains.
- Note the architecture gap vs the June deal: **no vertical integration** here (HSBC's permissioned ledger + Franklin's public-chain Benji tokens + Whale as the bridge), unlike the Amundi deal which stayed inside Crédit Agricole (Amundi fund + CACEIS as tokenization agent).

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
### Red-team / challenge

1. **Is this a live product or a pilot?** Open/answered-partial. HSBC described it as a "first transaction"; it surfaced via LinkedIn, not a formal press release. Treat as a pilot/single-transaction milestone, not a GA product.

2. **Is this a duplicate of the June Amundi deal?** No. Different fund (Franklin Templeton Benji vs Amundi MMF–Short Term), different bank/rail (HSBC TDS vs Crédit Agricole/CACEIS), different architecture (no vertical integration here). The note itself frames it as the "second such deal in four months" — a distinct event.

3. **Is it a duplicate of the MEA/OCBC tokenized-deposit items?** No. Those are tokenized *deposit* payment rails; THIS adds a tokenized *MMF investment* leg (yield on idle cash). Different event.

4. **How big was the sweep?** Unknown — no amount disclosed. Weakens the "significance" claim; could be a token demonstration transaction.

5. **Who issued the formal claim?** Weak sourcing: the news came from LinkedIn posts; only HSBC's Stoker is quoted on record. Franklin Templeton's role is inferred from the Benji platform; confirm Benji/FOBXX specifically vs a bespoke share class.

6. **Does the bank cannibalise its own deposits?** The note raises but doesn't answer this. HSBC's incentive is plausibly fee/relationship capture + defence vs stablecoins, but unproven.

7. **What chain do the Benji tokens settle on, and how does Whale bridge a permissioned ledger to a public-chain token?** Open — reconciliation/atomic-settlement/DvP risk is material and undisclosed.

8. **Is 24/7 redemption actually guaranteed?** Claimed benefit ("around-the-clock transactions"), but MMF intraday liquidity depends on the fund's own mechanics and the tokenization agent; "potential for" is hedged language.

9. **Is the yield advantage real after fees?** Benji/FOBXX ~3.66% 30-day APY, 0.20% mgmt fee — plausible pickup vs an idle bank balance, but net benefit depends on how much cash and for how short a window.

10. **Regulatory status?** Tokenized MMFs/deposits sit in evolving regimes (HK Project Ensemble, MAS Guardian). No new license/approval claimed here; don't overstate regulatory endorsement.

11. **Is Ant a representative client or a captive reference?** Ant is a sophisticated, repeat, arguably captive pilot partner (first client across HSBC TDS, StanChart, OCBC, Amundi). Adoption signal is weaker than if an unrelated corporate had done it.

12. **Competitive framing accurate?** Yes, directionally: BlackRock BUIDL, JPMorgan Kinexys MONY/JLTXX, Fnality, Ondo are credible competitors in tokenized-MMF/cash-management; the corporate-treasury 24/7 use case is genuinely contested.

13. **Any double-counting of "firsts"?** HSBC has many "first client" claims with Ant (HK TDS, MEA, now MMF). Each is scoped (first MMF *via* TDS) but the pattern risks announcement inflation.

14. **Does the headline overclaim?** "Sweeps idle cash into HSBC tokenized fund" is slightly imprecise — the *fund* is Franklin Templeton's; HSBC provides the tokenized *deposit* rail. Minor but worth flagging for the digest.

Importance: 3/5 — A genuinely meaningful step (tokenized cash management moving from deposits to on-chain yield, a real RWA treasury use case with named players), and part of an accelerating tokenized-MMF race. Docked from 4 for weak sourcing (LinkedIn, no amount), pilot-not-product status, and a captive reference client (Ant) rather than independent adoption.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This sits at the intersection of two fast-growing on-chain cash rails: tokenized money-market funds (MMFs) and tokenized bank deposits, both aimed at 24/7 corporate treasury. Tokenized U.S. Treasury/MMF products grew from under $1bn in early 2024 to over $15bn by April 2026 (per secondary trackers, via stablecoininsider.org/eco.com, as of 2026); the broader tokenized-RWA pool crossed ~$22bn by May 2026. The protagonist asset here — Franklin Templeton's BENJI (the "tokenized Benji MMF") — held ~$828M AUM, a distant follower to BlackRock's BUIDL (~$2.5–2.9bn, ~40% share) and Circle/USYC (~$3bn) as of early–mid 2026 (secondary data, unverified against filings → treat as `[UNSOURCED]` for precision). Structure: the fund side is concentrating around 2–3 issuers (BlackRock, Circle, Franklin) while the deposit-rail side is a land-grab of banks (JPMorgan Kinexys, Citi Token Services, HSBC TDS, OCBC, plus a US Clearing House consortium targeting H1-2027). Why now: regulatory clarity post-stablecoin legislation + banks racing to put deposits on-chain *before* stablecoins disintermediate corporate cash (per pymnts/Forbes, 2026). The thin barrier is orchestration — connecting permissioned bank ledgers to public-chain fund tokens — which is exactly the gap WhaleRTP fills.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: for the deposit rail — daily tokenized-transfer volume, bank count / currency count on the network, settlement window (T+0 vs intraday); for the fund — AUM, yield, subscription/redemption cut-off hours. Key players: (a) orchestration/treasury platforms — Ant's **WhaleRTP** (20+ banks, 17+ currencies, 24/7), the real moat here; (b) deposit-token issuers — JPMorgan Kinexys (>$7bn/day, >$7tn since inception), Citi Token Services (live), HSBC TDS (pilot); (c) fund issuers — Franklin (BENJI), BlackRock (BUIDL), Amundi. Basis of competition is distribution + network reach, not price. Recent moves: Amundi/CACEIS launched EUR+USD tokenized MMF share classes on Ethereum *specifically for Ant* (2026-06-15, [[Ant Group files 'AntCoin' trademark in Hong Kong]] context); Ant–HSBC piloted AED tokenized-deposit transfers in the UAE (2025); JPMorgan put a deposit token on Base (2026). Ant's position: **ahead** as the neutral orchestration hub — it is the party that both Credit Agricole/Amundi and now HSBC/Franklin plugged into, which is the defensible node `(analysis)`. The banks each own only their own deposit silo; Ant owns the interconnect.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Deposit cannibalization / bank-incentive conflict** — the note itself flags it: why would HSBC build a tool that sweeps cash *out* of its own deposits into a Franklin fund? If the economics don't work for the bank, the rail stays a perpetual "pilot" and never goes live (HSBC called this a "first transaction"). Second-order: treasurers get the feature only as long as banks tolerate deposit flight.
2. **Pilot ≠ product** — this is Ant's *second* such deal in four months but still a one-off LinkedIn-surfaced pilot, not a live, repeatable product. Execution risk that neither the Amundi nor the HSBC/Franklin arrangement scales beyond Ant itself.
3. **Disintermediation of the orchestrator** — WhaleRTP's value depends on banks and fund issuers *not* connecting directly or via a shared network (e.g. the TCH consortium, H1-2027). If a neutral industry utility emerges, Ant's interconnect moat erodes; conversely stablecoins could bypass the whole bank-deposit layer.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` Signals that corporate treasury is becoming the first real at-scale use case for tokenization — not retail crypto but idle multi-currency cash earning fund yield around the clock. Falsifiable thesis: Ant/WhaleRTP becomes the de-facto neutral switch between bank deposit tokens and tokenized MMFs across Asia-Europe-ME corridors. Trigger to watch: a *third-party* corporate (not Ant itself) running live, recurring sweeps on WhaleRTP, and HSBC/Franklin moving from "first transaction" to a published product. What breaks it: the US bank consortium's shared deposit network or a stablecoin rail standardizing the interconnect before Ant locks in partners.
