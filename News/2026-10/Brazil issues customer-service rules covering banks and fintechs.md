# Brazil issues customer-service rules covering banks and fintechs

> [!info] 2026-10-21 · 1 упоминаний · 0 источника(ов) с текстом

## Агрегированный текст (из дайджестов)

[This Week in Fintech] 🇧🇷Brazil’s federal government issued new customer-service rules that, for the first time, explicitly cover banks and fintechs, including a requirement to offer human support, starting November 2. Firms get just 20 days to adapt.

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** Brazil's federal executive issued **Decree No. 13,135/2026** (signed 2026-10-09, published in the *Diário Oficial da União* 2026-10-13), overhauling the country's customer-service (SAC — *Serviço de Atendimento ao Consumidor*) rules and, for the first time, **explicitly extending them to banks, payment institutions and credit/"fintech" providers that deliver financial, payment or credit services by digital means** and are subject to Central Bank (BCB) regulation/supervision. The decree takes effect **2026-11-02 — just 20 days after publication**, giving firms a record-short window to adapt. Core mandates: **human support available 8:00–20:00 every day** across phone and digital channels; at least one electronic channel operating 24h; AI/bots permitted **only if the consumer can switch to a human at any time**; phone wait ≤20 seconds for 80% of calls (others called back within 300s); no forcing the customer to repeat the request or restart; immediate cancellation allowed at any point; automated menus capped at five levels.

**[1] Who issued / who enforces (important nuance).** This is a **consumer-protection decree from the federal government under the Consumer Protection Code (CDC)** — *not* a BCB prudential rule. Enforcement sits with **Senacon (Secretaria Nacional do Consumidor, Ministério da Justiça e Segurança Pública)** and the state Procons, "without prejudice" to sectoral regulators (BCB, Anatel). So digital banks now face a **second, consumer-law compliance axis** on top of BCB supervision. The decree does not itself set fine amounts; sanctions flow from CDC penalties applied by Senacon/Procons.

**[2] Who's affected.** Brazil's large neobank/payments cohort — **Nubank, Mercado Pago, PicPay, Inter, C6, Banco BV**, plus card issuers and digital-credit lenders. These players built lean, chat/AI-first support models precisely to avoid costly call-center overhead; the human-support-on-demand and 8am–8pm live-agent mandate directly raises their cost base and reverses part of the automation thesis. Incumbents (Itaú, Bradesco, Banco do Brasil) already run large SAC operations and are less disrupted.

**[3] Why it matters / context.** Brazil has spent 2026 tightening the regulatory perimeter around its fintech boom: Congress is advancing bills reshaping bank/fintech rules and BCB autonomy ([[Brazil's Congress advances bills reshaping bank and fintech rules]]); BCB has moved on crypto classification, Pix device limits and deposit-insurance. This decree adds a **consumer-protection front** amid a political backlash — Reuters recently reported voters "feel burned" by the fintech revolution ([[Reuters Brazilian voters feel burned by fintech revolution]]), and this year's Banco Master failure and fintech-fraud incidents have hardened sentiment. The SAC overhaul is best read as the government responding to consumer complaints about unreachable, bot-only digital-bank support.

**[4] Watch next.** (a) Whether digital banks seek a transition/grace period or challenge the 20-day runway; (b) hiring/cost signals from Nubank et al. on call-center staffing; (c) first Senacon/Procon enforcement actions after 2026-11-02; (d) interaction with ongoing Congressional fintech bills and any BCB-side service-quality rules.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team Q&A**

1. **Did the Central Bank issue this?** No — common misread. It is a **federal decree under consumer-protection law (CDC)**, signed by the executive, enforced by Senacon/Procons. BCB-supervised entities are merely brought *into scope*. (resolved)
2. **Which decree / number?** Decreto **No. 13,135/2026**, signed 2026-10-09, published 2026-10-13. (resolved, multiple sources incl. legisweb, Ministry of Justice)
3. **Effective date and the "20 days"?** In force **2026-11-02** — 20 calendar days after the 2026-10-13 publication. The "20 days to adapt" in the digest text = this vacatio legis, not a special fintech window. (resolved)
4. **Is this genuinely the first time banks/fintechs are covered?** The SAC framework (prior Decree 11.034/2022, orig. 6.523/2008) historically targeted telecom/utilities/regulated services; sources state the new decree **expressly includes** digital financial/payment/credit providers, "eliminating previous uncertainties." So it's an explicit extension/clarification rather than a brand-new concept. (resolved; nuance flagged)
5. **Does it mandate a fully human call center?** No — AI/bots remain allowed; the requirement is **human available on demand** 8am–8pm and a 24h electronic channel. (resolved)
6. **Who actually bears the cost?** Digital-only players (Nubank, Mercado Pago, PicPay, Inter) with lean/bot-first support; incumbents already comply. (reasoned, not firm-confirmed per company)
7. **Are specific fines defined?** No — the decree sets no fine amounts; sanctions come via CDC enforcement by Senacon/Procons. Magnitude of risk is therefore uncertain. (resolved → open on actual penalty scale)
8. **Have any firms publicly reacted / pushed back?** Not found as of 2026-10-21 — no named Nubank/Mercado Pago statement or legal challenge yet. (OPEN)
9. **Could the 20-day runway slip?** Possible via judicial challenge or a regulatory transition note, but none confirmed. (OPEN)
10. **Is this a duplicate of an existing note?** No. Internal notes cover Congressional bills ([[Brazil's Congress advances bills reshaping bank and fintech rules]]) and BCB prudential/crypto/Pix actions — none is this consumer-service SAC decree. **Fresh.** (resolved)
11. **Is the lone source reliable?** Finsiders is a credible Brazilian fintech outlet; corroborated by Baker McKenzie, Trench Rossi, the Ministry of Justice site and legisweb — high confidence on the facts. (resolved)
12. **Scope precision — all fintechs or only some?** Applies to providers of financial/payment/credit services by digital means under BCB regulation/supervision, plus the bank/account-holder and card-issuer/cardholder relationships. Pure non-financial fintechs (e.g. infra/B2B) may fall outside. (resolved)
13. **Does it override BCB service rules?** No — it layers on "without prejudice" to sectoral regulators; dual compliance. (resolved)
14. **Material financial impact quantifiable?** No public cost estimate found; qualitatively raises opex for digital banks. (OPEN)
15. **Any AI-specific angle worth flagging?** Yes — it is one of the first Brazilian rules to explicitly regulate AI in customer service (bot allowed but human-escape mandatory), a notable precedent. (resolved)

**Importance: 3/5** — A concrete, dated, near-term (Nov 2) regulatory change touching every major Brazilian neobank/payments player and adding a new consumer-law compliance axis, which matters given the political backlash against fintechs. Capped below 4 because it is a consumer-service/operational rule (not prudential or systemic), carries no defined fines, and affects cost base rather than business models or licensing.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a consumer-protection (SAC) regulation landing on Brazil's banking + fintech sector — one of the world's deepest fintech markets. Brazil digital banking & fintech-apps market ~$28.0bn in 2025, CAGR ~12.4% to ~$63.5bn by 2032 (per Ken Research, via Yahoo/secondary citation, as of 2025) — sizes the pool of firms now in scope, not the decree itself. Concrete instrument: Decree No. 13,135 (9 Oct 2026, Official Gazette 13 Oct), in force 2 Nov 2026, revoking Decree 11,034/2022; for the FIRST time it explicitly covers financial, payment and credit services delivered through digital means (per Trench Rossi / Baker McKenzie Connect On Tech, as of Oct 2026). Core mandates: human agent available 8am–8pm every day even when AI/automation is used; phone wait ≤20s for ≥80% of calls (≤300s otherwise); max five service levels; immediate cancellation at any point of the journey. Structure: the obligation is horizontal (applies to incumbents and digital-only alike), so it does NOT create a moat — it raises a fixed cost floor. "Why now": the headline driver is the compression of the adaptation window from 180 days (old rule) to 20 days — a deliberate political signal, not a gradual phase-in (per Finsiders / Mattos Filho, Oct 2026).

**Competitive landscape.** The KPIs that matter here are operational, not valuation: cost-to-serve per active user, contact-rate (contacts per customer/month), and share of contacts resolved by bot vs human. Who bears the cost asymmetrically: digital-only neobanks built on near-zero-marginal-cost, bot-first, no-call-centre models — Nubank (~118m Brazil customers, Q2 2026), PicPay (66m registered, Nasdaq IPO Jan 2026), Mercado Pago (>60m regional MAU), Inter, C6, PagBank. A human-availability floor (12h/day, 7 days) is structurally more disruptive to these pure-digital unit economics than to incumbents (Itaú, Bradesco, Santander, Banco do Brasil) that already run large staffed call centres. Enforcement sits with SENACON plus state Procons — a credible, already-active sanctioning apparatus. Protagonist position: n/a — regulatory item, no single company; the "protagonist" is effectively every digital-first player whose support stack was never built to a human-SLA.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/deal in the item → multiples not applicable (no data). Internal comps — the recent Brazil regulatory-tightening cluster, which this extends: [[Pix installment rules to be released by Brazil Central Bank]], [[Brazil removes fixed cap on contactless Pix payments]], [[Brazil tightens crypto self-custody wallet reporting rules]], [[Binance to tighten Brazilian crypto transfer rules Nov 1]]. Adjacent capital-side pressure (not in-note, web): BCB has separately raised the payment-institution capital floor from R$1m to R$9.2m, with ~679 of ~1,751 mapped institutions short (per streamline/secondary, Oct 2026) — SAC compliance stacks on top of that. Distribution not computed; qualitative only.

**Risk flags.**
1. Timeline risk — 20 days vs the prior 180 is near-impossible for firms that must stand up or re-contract staffed human support (hiring, telephony, SLA monitoring) from a bot-only base; high probability of day-one non-compliance and reactive Procon/SENACON actions. Second-order: a rush to outsourced BPO call-centre capacity, re-introducing the fixed opex neobanks were built to avoid.
2. Asymmetric cost incidence — the rule is model-agnostic but cost is not: it disproportionately taxes the thin-margin, high-volume digital-only model, partially eroding the structural cost advantage over incumbents (a mild re-leveling, not reversal).
3. AI-channel constraint — mandated human fallback "at any time" caps how far bot/AI deflection can go on consumer support, limiting a lever these firms were counting on for scale economics; watch for creative "human-in-the-loop" interpretations that invite enforcement disputes.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Marginal negative for Brazilian neobank/payments unit economics and a mild competitive re-leveling toward incumbents on service cost — not a thesis-breaker given 12%+ sector growth, but a new recurring opex line. Falsifiable thesis: if large digital-only players miss the 2 Nov date and absorb visible Procon/SENACON sanctions in Nov–Dec 2026, expect a wave of support-BPO contracting and possibly an industry push (Abipag/Febraban) for a phase-in extension — the trigger to watch. What would make it wrong: regulators grant a de-facto grace period on enforcement, rendering the 20-day shock cosmetic.
