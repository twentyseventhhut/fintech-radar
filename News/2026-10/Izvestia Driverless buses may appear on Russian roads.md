# Izvestia: Driverless buses may appear on Russian roads

Минтранс России работает над расширением сферы использования беспилотного транспорта. Об этом 22 октября на форуме автоиндустрии «Для тех, кто рулит» сообщил директор департамента госполитики в области автомобильного и городского пассажирского транспорта Минтранса РФ Андрей Енин, передает корреспондент «Известий».
«Мы планируем начать эксперимент по эксплуатации беспилотных автобусов в Москве , Санкт-Петербурге и на Дальнем Востоке. Сейчас совместно с Минэкономразвития разрабатываем для них экспериментальный правовой режим», — сообщил Енин.
Он также рассказал, что министерство намерено запустить международные перевозки беспилотными грузовиками. Сейчас такие грузовики работают на трассах М-11 «Нева», ЦКАД, М-4 «Дон» и М-12 «Восток».
Кроме того, в Москве и Санкт-Петербурге уже эксплуатируются беспилотные трамваи, в ряде регионов страны есть беспилотное такси, а в Москве проходят тесты беспилотные поезда метро, напомнил представитель Минтранса России.
Накануне в Минтрансе сообщили, что автономные грузовые автомобили могут появиться на российских трассах в 2028 году .
Президент России Владимир Путин 31 марта дал старт движению беспилотных грузовиков по трассе М-12 «Восток». Как отмечал президент, к 2028 году парк грузовых беспилотных машин будет насчитывать 992 автомобиля, а к концу 2030 года превысит 4 тыс. единиц .

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
### [0] What happened (one-paragraph fact base)

On 22 Oct 2026, at the "Для тех, кто рулит" auto-industry forum, Andrey Enin (director of the department for state policy on road and urban passenger transport at Russia's Ministry of Transport, Mintrans) said the ministry plans to launch an **experiment operating driverless buses in Moscow, St. Petersburg and the Far East**, and is jointly developing an **experimental legal regime (ЭПР / EPR)** for them with the Ministry of Economic Development (Minekonomrazvития). He added Mintrans intends to start **international freight runs with driverless trucks** (already operating on the M-11 "Neva", ЦКАД/Central Ring Road, M-4 "Don", M-12 "Vostok"), noted driverless trams already run in Moscow and St. Petersburg, robotaxi operates in several regions, and driverless metro trains are being tested in Moscow. This is a **proposal/announcement, not a live deployment** — the driverless-bus pilot has not yet started.

### [1] Why it matters / stakes

- Caps a multi-year Russian push to move autonomous transport from a sandbox into general regulation. The underlying driver is the planned **federal law on highly automated vehicles (ВАТС/HAV)**, expected to pass in 2026–2027 and take effect ~Q3 2027, after which driverless vehicles could in principle operate on all Russian roads.
- The **EPR (экспериментальный правовой режим)** is the legal sandbox that lets operators test ahead of universal rules — the same instrument used for the M-11/ЦКАД driverless-truck program.
- **Fintech relevance is adjacent but real on the insurance/liability axis**: draft HAV rules require every driverless vehicle to carry **ОСАГО (compulsory motor liability insurance)**, ЭРА-ГЛОНАСС, and a data recorder. The owner bears first-line liability (with regress against manufacturer/developer for defects). Insurers are reportedly proposing a **two-tier model**: a base policy for fast victim payout plus a manufacturer/developer liability layer (up to ~RUB 500M), with insurer access to data-recorder telemetry as the mechanism. Passenger-transport angle also touches **fare collection/payments** for driverless buses, but that is not addressed in this news item.

### [2] Prior context / timeline (dated)

- **2018–2019**: Russia opens initial public-road testing of driverless cars (Yandex, KamAZ).
- **2023**: EPR launched for driverless trucks on M-11 "Neva" and ЦКАД.
- **31 Mar 2026** (prior spring): President Putin formally starts driverless-truck movement on M-12 "Vostok"; targets ~992 freight autonomous vehicles by 2028, >4,000 by end-2030.
- **10 Aug 2026**: Transport Minister Andrey Nikitin tells a Gossovet presidium meeting that pilots of driverless buses are proposed for Moscow, Moscow Oblast, St. Petersburg, Tatarstan and the Far East; HAV federal law expected to pass 2026, in force Q3 2027. EPR for HAV operation reportedly extended to ~autumn 2028.
- **~Apr 2026**: 67 driverless trucks on M-11/ЦКАД, targeted to reach 100 by year-end; ~90 autonomous KamAZ/Navio trucks, >9.5M km cumulative mileage.
- **22 Oct 2026** (this item): Mintrans reiterates/extends the plan — buses pilot + international driverless-truck freight; frames it as broad EPR expansion.

### [3] Key players / entities

- **Mintrans RF** (Andrey Enin; Minister Andrey Nikitin) — policy owner. **Minekonomrazvития** — co-author of the EPR.
- **KamAZ / Navio** — autonomous-truck hardware already on M-11/ЦКАД; KamAZ also confirmed autonomous-bus development.
- **Yandex** — robotaxi/self-driving heritage and now building precise geopositioning infrastructure for autonomous transport in Russia ([[Yandex builds precise geopositioning network for autonomous transport]]).
- Insurers (via ВСС/industry) — shaping ОСАГО + two-tier liability cover.

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
### Red-team questions

1. **Proposal vs. deployment** — This is a stated *plan* ("мы планируем начать эксперимент") plus a *draft* EPR, not an operating bus pilot. Has a single driverless bus actually carried passengers under this regime yet? (No evidence it has.)
2. **No start date** — The item gives no launch date for the bus pilot. The 10 Aug 2026 framing was already "proposed"; what has concretely advanced between Aug and Oct beyond being restated at a forum?
3. **Single source, single mention** — Only iz.ru, n_mentions=1. Is this a genuine policy update or a rehash of the 10 Aug Gossovet statement repackaged as forum news?
4. **Regulatory dependency risk** — The whole path hinges on the ВАТС federal law (expected 2026, in force ~Q3 2027). Russian autonomous-vehicle legislation has slipped repeatedly since ~2021; is the 2027 date credible?
5. **Buses ≠ highways** — Truck success on M-11/ЦКАД is controlled-access, geofenced highway driving. Urban bus routes (Moscow, SPb) are far harder (pedestrians, dense traffic). Does highway mileage (>9.5M km) actually de-risk city buses? (KamAZ itself reportedly cautioned highway record does not transfer to city streets.)
6. **Fintech relevance** — This sits in a fintech corpus. The only real fintech hooks are (a) ОСАГО/liability insurance redesign and (b) eventual fare-payment systems for autonomous buses — neither is the subject of this item. Is it fintech-core or adjacent? (Adjacent.)
7. **Liability unresolved** — Who pays in a fatal driverless-bus crash: operator, Mintrans, manufacturer? Draft puts first-line on owner with regress to maker; is that settled law or still a draft proposal?
8. **Insurance capacity** — A two-tier model with up to RUB 500M manufacturer cover assumes insurers will underwrite novel autonomy risk with scarce actuarial data. Will the Russian insurance market actually price this?
9. **Sanctions / tech-supply** — Autonomous stacks need high-end compute/sensors (lidar, chips). Under sanctions, can Russia scale domestic autonomous buses, or is this aspirational?
10. **Driver displacement / political** — Replacing bus drivers has labour and political sensitivity. Is passenger and union acceptance a gating factor the announcement ignores?
11. **Entity precision** — Enin is a department director, not the minister; the minister (Nikitin) made the broader Gossovet statement. Don't over-attribute weight to a forum comment.
12. **Geography scope** — "Far East" is vague; which city? Moscow/SPb/Tatarstan/Moscow Oblast appeared in August — does the Oct item narrow or broaden the list? (It names Moscow, SPb, Far East — a *subset* of August's list.)
13. **Metric double-check** — Fleet targets (992 by 2028, >4,000 by 2030) are *truck* targets from the Putin March statement, not bus targets. Ensure no conflation of truck numbers with the bus pilot.
14. **Competitive read** — Globally (Baidu Apollo Go, Waymo) robo-buses/shuttles remain limited and geofenced. Is Russia's timeline realistic vs. the global state of the art?
15. **Payments angle** — If driverless buses launch, fare collection shifts fully to cashless/contactless/QR — a potential transit-payments story. Is that a future watch-item worth a follow-up note rather than part of this one?

**Importance: 2/5** — A credible but incremental policy announcement (plan + draft EPR), single-source, no deployment or start date, and only *adjacent* to fintech (insurance/liability and future fare-payment rails). Worth tracking as the ВАТС law and the ОСАГО/liability model mature, but it does not move a fintech thesis today.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** This is a Russian autonomous-transport *policy* item (Mintrans + Minekonomrazvития drafting an experimental legal regime, ЭПР, for driverless buses in Moscow, St. Petersburg and the Far East), not a fintech deal — direct fintech linkage is weak (see below). Market facts: Russia's unmanned-truck programme targets a fleet of 992 vehicles by 2028 and >4,000 by end-2030 (per President Putin, cited in the note; secondary via iz.ru, as of 2026-07) [en.iz.ru]. ~90 unmanned trucks were in operation as of mid-2026 (per Mintrans, via TAdviser) — so the 2028 target implies roughly an order-of-magnitude scale-up from a tiny base. No credible Russia AV-market TAM/CAGR surfaced from a free source → "no data". Structure: state-led, not market-led — demand, routes (M-11, ЦКАД, M-4, M-12) and legality are all set by the ЭПР sandbox regime; entry barrier is regulatory access, not capital or network effects. "Why now": Mintrans wants to move from the proven freight corridor (launched on M-12 "Восток" 31 Mar) to passenger buses and cross-border freight — a regime-expansion step, announced, not live.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector runs on non-financial KPIs here (fleet count, disengagement rate, route-km under ЭПР) rather than payments/SaaS KPIs — a signal of how far this sits from core fintech. Key domestic AV makers: **Yandex**, **KAMAZ**, **NAVIO** — all three ran the M-12 launch convoy (per iz.ru/www1.ru, Mar–Apr 2026). KAMAZ confirmed it is developing an autonomous *passenger bus* (per iz.ru/TechTimes, Aug 2026) — directly relevant to this bus-ЭПР item; basis of competition is engineering/state-contract access, not price. Moscow separately is adding five LiDAR-equipped driverless e-buses (per Tribune) — "announced", de-PR: pilot scale, not fleet deployment. Protagonist = the *regulator* (Mintrans), so "position" is N/A; among vendors, Yandex and KAMAZ look ahead on freight, bus autonomy is earlier-stage for all (analysis).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Weak fintech relevance (classification risk).** This is transport policy; the only fintech-adjacent hook is AV insurance/liability — Russian rules already require ≥RUB 10m liability cover per autonomous vehicle and place accident liability on the owner (per Minek draft rules, via TAdviser/Lexology). Second-order: a 4,000+ AV fleet by 2030 is a new commercial-auto/liability insurance pool, but no insurer, premium or product is named here — thesis is speculative, not sourced.
2. **Announced ≠ live.** Buses are at the "drafting the ЭПР / plan to start an experiment" stage; the freight law project was only slated for Q1-2026 submission (per TAdviser). Timeline slippage is the base case for state AV programmes.
3. **State-demand concentration.** Routes, permissions and (implicitly) procurement run through Mintrans/ЭПР — vendor economics depend on one buyer-regulator, a concentration/execution risk with no competitive pricing signal.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For fintech this is a watch-item, not a catalyst: the falsifiable thesis is that passenger-AV expansion eventually creates a specialised AV-liability/telematics-insurance line and mobility-financing demand for fleets (analysis). Trigger to watch: an insurer or leasing/financing product explicitly priced against the ЭПР bus fleet, or the Q1-type federal AV law actually passing. Absent that, there is no fintech signal to act on.
