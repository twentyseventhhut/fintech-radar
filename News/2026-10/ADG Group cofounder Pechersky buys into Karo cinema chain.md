 Сооснователь ADG Group Григорий Печерский вошел в капитал киносети «Каро» 
Основатель, совладелец и управляющий партнер девелопера ADG Group Григорий Печерский через цепочку компаний стал единственным владельцем головной компании киносети «Каро» – ООО «Каро фильм менеджмент», по данным ЕГРЮЛ. Также 100% этого юрлица находится в залоге у ООО «СПР финанс», которая полностью принадлежит Печерскому. Ранее собственником «Каро фильм менеджмент» был консорциум инвесторов, в который входили бизнесмен Пол Хет, фонд UFG Private Equity и Российский фонд прямых инвестиций, напоминют " Ведомости ".
Киносеть «Каро» – одна из крупнейших в стране, в ее управлении на сегодня находится 26 кинотеатров в Москве, Екатеринбурге, Краснодаре, Калининграде и т. д. По итогам 2025 г. выручка ООО «Каро фильм менеджмент» составила 4,7 млрд руб., чистая прибыль – 380 млн руб., по данным «СПАРК-Интерфакс».
ADG Group занималась реконструкцией старых московских кинотеатров в районные торгово-досуговые центры «Место встречи» (ее партнером по этому проекту был гендиректор «Первого канала» Константин Эрнст). Изначально в портфель компании входило 39 таких объектов: еще в 2014 г. структура девелопера – компания «Эдисонэнерго» – выкупила их у мэрии Москвы за 9,6 млрд руб. В 2020 г. ADG Group и киносеть «Каро» объявили о партнерстве: они собирались запустить в столице 29 новых кинотеатров до конца 2022 г. Компании открыли лишь часть заявленных площадок, а в 2023 г. на фоне кризиса кинопроката в ADG Group заявили, что планируют сместить акцент в развитии локаций на фитнес-центры.
Кроме того, девелопер позднее перепродал некоторые кинотеатры из своего портфеля. К примеру, новым владельцем «Варшавы» рядом со ст. м. «Войковская» на Ленинградском шоссе стала фирма «Нова бахча», принадлежащая близкому к ГК «Киевская площадь» бизнесмену Илюше Юсупову. Еще семь объектов были выкуплены у ADG Group мэрией Москвы для размещения городских сервисов. В этом году 26 районных центров «Место встречи» были внесены в ЗПИФ под управлением Parus Asset Management. Сейчас кинотеатры «Каро» работают только в четырех центрах «Место встречи». Еще в пяти функционируют кинотеатры сети «Москино» столичного правительства.

# Context-enrichment: ADG Group cofounder Pechersky buys into Karo cinema chain
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Grigory Pechersky — founder, co-owner and managing partner of Moscow real-estate/retail developer ADG Group — became, via a chain of companies, the **sole (100%) owner of Karo's holding entity, OOO "Karo Film Management"**, per the EGRUL corporate registry (reported by Vedomosti/Mergers.ru, 2026-10-05/06). The previous owners were a consortium: businessman Paul Heth (Karo's long-time CEO), UFG Private Equity, and the Russian Direct Investment Fund (RDIF).

De-PR'd caveats — this is the opposite of a PR event; there is essentially **no PR**:
- **No disclosed price, no disclosed stake-transfer percentage, no disclosed consideration.** The facts are inferred from a registry filing, not announced by the parties. Neither ADG nor Karo commented on terms or valuation. Treat "deal value" as **unknown**.
- **The structure is the real tell:** 100% of Karo Film Management is **pledged (в залоге) to OOO "SPR Finance", which Pechersky himself fully owns.** A buyer pledging the acquired asset to his own financing vehicle strongly suggests the purchase was **debt-financed through a related-party structure** (likely a loan from SPR Finance, or SPR Finance borrowing externally and on-lending). → Why structured this way: it keeps the acquisition leveraged and ring-fenced inside Pechersky's perimeter rather than on ADG Group's balance sheet, and lets him control Karo without visibly committing equity. It also means the "ownership" is encumbered — if the financing sours, control could revert to the pledgee. (analysis)
- **ADG Group itself is not formally the buyer** — Pechersky as an individual/through personal vehicles is. This matters because ADG's own project portfolio has been shrinking (see [2]). The deal is a personal/related-party consolidation, not a corporate ADG expansion.

## [1] Competitors / peers
Russian cinema exhibition is a consolidated, post-shock oligopoly:
- **Cinema Park / Formula Kino** (controlled interests historically tied to A. Mamut / later restructured) — long the #1 chain by screens.
- **Kinomax, Mori Cinema, Premier Zal / Luxor** — regional and mid-tier chains.
- Historically Karo was #2 after Cinema Park (2012 consortium-era: ~31 theatres / ~197 screens). Today Karo lists **26 cinemas** — a contraction consistent with the whole sector shrinking after Hollywood's 2022 withdrawal.
- → Why the landscape looks this way: after the 2022 exit of Disney/WB/Universal/Sony/Paramount, screen counts and attendance fell; weaker operators closed, and distressed/undervalued chains changed hands at depressed valuations. Karo at **₽4.7bn revenue / ₽380m net profit (2025)** is a modest, thin-margin asset (~8% net margin) — not a trophy, which is consistent with a quiet registry transfer rather than a competitive auction. (analysis)
- No VTB involvement here (earlier 2024-era reports floated VTB interest in Karo); the actual acquirer is Pechersky, not a bank.

## [2] Company history / fit
- ADG Group + Karo is **not a new relationship — it is the culmination of one**. In **2020** ADG and Karo announced a partnership to open **29 new cinemas** inside ADG's "Mesto Vstrechi" (Meeting Place) district retail-leisure centres — the 39 old Moscow cinemas ADG's vehicle "Edisonenergo" bought from the city for **₽9.6bn in 2014** (ADG's partner on the concept was Konstantin Ernst, CEO of Channel One).
- That plan underdelivered: only part of the venues opened, and by **2023** ADG pivoted the locations toward **fitness centres** amid the cinema-distribution crisis. ADG later resold assets (e.g. "Varshava" cinema to Ilyusha Yusupov, close to GK Kievskaya Ploshchad; 7 objects bought back by Moscow city). In 2026 the **26 "Mesto Vstrechi" centres were folded into a ZPIF (closed-end fund) managed by Parus Asset Management.** Karo cinemas now operate in only **4** of those centres (Moskino operates in 5).
- → Why Pechersky acts this way (analysis): ADG's development/retail core has been largely monetised/de-risked into a fund (Parus). Taking personal control of Karo looks like Pechersky backing the **operating tenant/content side** of the ecosystem he helped build — vertical consolidation of the cinema operator that anchors leisure footfall in his retail format. It is a logical, low-drama fit, but it is a **bet on a structurally shrunken market**, financed with leverage.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty is low.** This is a **change of control of an existing, operating chain**, not a new product, round, or capability. The ADG–Karo link dates to 2020.
- **Traction = the actual operating numbers, not the announcement:** 26 cinemas, ₽4.7bn revenue, ₽380m net profit (2025, SPARK-Interfax). Profitable but small. Russian box office ~$0.55bn (2025), with domestic films now 77–85% of the box office and ~88% of state-funded 2025 films unprofitable — the content pipeline is thin and risky.
- → Where the value is / who captures the margin (analysis): In exhibition, margin is squeezed between (a) weak, state-dependent domestic content supply and (b) real-estate occupancy cost. Pechersky uniquely controls one side of that — the **real estate** ("Mesto Vstrechi" / the relationship with the Parus ZPIF). So the real value-add is **landlord-operator alignment**: he can set rents/terms to keep screens viable where a third-party operator couldn't. That is a defensible, if modest, synergy. What breaks it: a further collapse in film supply (end of Hollywood "pre-screening" workarounds, expected to stop within months) removes the last blockbuster draw and pressures attendance. (hypothesis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- Expect **quiet operational integration** with ADG's retail-leisure footprint rather than expansion; possible rebalancing of screens vs. fitness/other uses continues.
- Market backdrop is the dominant risk: Hollywood majors remain absent (4+ years), the grey "pre-screening" of pirated US films is reportedly being wound down within 2–3 months, and domestic output is largely unprofitable. Statista projects modest ~3.45%/yr box-office growth to 2029 off a low base.
- → Second-order (analysis): leverage + pledged collateral in a shrinking market makes the position **fragile, not safe** — a soft box-office year pressures debt service. Conversely, if the state continues propping domestic film funding and footfall at mixed-use centres holds, Pechersky captures a cheaply-acquired, landlord-aligned operator. Net: a defensive, idiosyncratic consolidation play, low systemic/fintech relevance.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russian cinema exhibition — a leisure/real-estate-adjacent vertical reshaped by the 2022 Hollywood exit. The majors (Disney, WB, Universal, Sony, Paramount) had historically supplied up to ~75% of Russian box office before their 2022 withdrawal; that content void collapsed attendance and forced theatre closures and layoffs (per Patrick von Sychowski / Forbes, as of 2025). The market has since partly rebuilt on domestic product: Russian films reached ~77% of box office by 2024 (per industry coverage, 2025). Market size is modest and the dollar figure is distorted by FX — ~US$0.55bn projected for 2025 (per Statista outlook, 2025); Russian/CIS gross was ~$456m in 2025, +20.2% YoY (per Box Office Mojo). TASS cited total box office rising to ~54bn RUB over five years (TASS, undated in snapshot — treat the precise figure as `[UNSOURCED]`). "Why now": the deal closes a messy 2020–2023 ADG/Karo real-estate-plus-cinema experiment (see note [0]) and consolidates ownership under a single real-estate operator just as box office claws back off a low base.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: screens/theatres, admissions (tickets sold), average ticket price, and occupancy per screen — box-office GBO is the headline. Structure is fragmented at the screen level but top-heavy at the chain level. Key players by screens (as of Jan 2024, per Statista): the merged **Cinema Park + Formula Kino** chain leads with ~641 screens / ~10.5% of Russian screens; **Cinema Center** ~4.8%; **Kinomax** ~4.4% / 259 screens. Karo is a long-standing top-tier operator (historically Russia's #2 behind Cinema Park, ~197 screens / 31 theatres pre-2022 per Hollywood Reporter / Celluloid Junkie) but the note reports it now runs only **26 cinemas** (Moscow, Yekaterinburg, Krasnodar, Kaliningrad etc.) — i.e. a shrunken footprint. Basis of competition: location/catchment (malls, district centres), screen quality/premium formats, and content-programming skill in a Hollywood-free market. Protagonist position: **niche/mid-scale, below the Cinema Park leader** `(analysis)`. Prior owners were a consortium of Paul Heth + UFG Private Equity + RDIF (note; UFG historically ~60% per web). Pechersky's edge is the ADG "Mesto Vstrechi" district-centre real estate itself — but the note is explicit that this synergy has decayed: Karo now operates in only 4 of the 26 Mesto Vstrechi centres (which were folded into a Parus Asset Management ZPIF this year), with Moscow's Moskino in 5 others. Moat: weak — mostly real-estate adjacency, not a defensible content or network advantage `(analysis)`.

**Comps & multiples.** No transaction price was disclosed — Pechersky took 100% of OOO "Karo film management" via a chain of companies, with the stake pledged to his own OOO "SPR Finance" (note). So deal EV/Revenue and deal P/E = **no data**. From Karo's own 2025 financials (SPARK-Interfax, via note): revenue 4.7bn RUB, net profit 380mn RUB → net margin = 380 / 4,700 = **8.1%**, a thin-but-positive margin typical of a high-fixed-cost exhibition business. External listed-peer multiples: Russian cinema operators are private; Cinema Park/Formula Kino, Kinomax and Cinema Center are not listed with free verifiable financials → trading comps = `[UNSOURCED]`. Internal comparables: semsearch was down (OpenRouter 402); grep over `News/` surfaced no Russian cinema/media-exhibition M&A precedents in-base (closest adjacent Russian deals are unrelated — e.g. [[MTS sells 49.9% stake in its tower company]], [[Ekto Prime fund becomes owner of Top Lubricants plant]] — not comparable on model/geo, excluded). **Distribution not computed; qualitative comparison only.** Flag: a profitable 4.7bn-RUB chain changing hands with 100% pledged as collateral is more consistent with a **leveraged/debt-backed buyout than a cash re-rating** `(analysis)`.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Content-supply dependence (structural).** With Hollywood majors absent and ~88% of state-funded 2025 Russian films reportedly unprofitable / only ~5 of 41 recouping costs (per KG Progress, via web), the entire chain's throughput rides on a thin, hit-or-miss domestic slate — a demand ceiling no amount of real-estate quality can lift.
2. **Shrunken footprint + lost real-estate synergy.** Karo fell from ~31 theatres to 26, and the ADG "Mesto Vstrechi" synergy that justified the 2020 partnership has mostly unwound (only 4 of 26 centres, the rest in a Parus ZPIF, some to Moskino) — the strategic rationale for a real-estate developer owning the chain is now largely gone.
3. **Leverage/collateral concentration.** 100% of the holding company is pledged to Pechersky's SPR Finance (note) — opaque related-party financing; a weak box-office cycle pressures debt service on a business already at ~8% net margin, with single-person ownership concentrating all execution and financing risk.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** This reads as **distressed/opportunistic consolidation by a real-estate insider, not a sector re-rating** `(analysis)`: a developer picking up a devalued anchor tenant he already partners with, likely as a real-estate play as much as a cinema one. Falsifiable thesis: if Pechersky re-expands Karo screens inside ADG/Parus district centres within 12–18 months, it's a genuine exhibition bet; if screen count keeps falling and locations convert to fitness/retail (as ADG already signalled in 2023, per note), the acquisition was a real-estate/control move and the "cinema chain" framing is misleading. Trigger to watch: disclosed price or debt terms, and 2026 screen-count / box-office share.
