Sebi weighs allowing mutual fund distributors to become investment advisers
This is a Mint Premium article gifted to you. Subscribe to enjoy similar stories.
Mumbai: The Securities and Exchange Board of India (Sebi) is weighing whether individual mutual fund distributors (MFDs) should also be allowed to hold a registered investment advisor (RIA) licence, as it reviews the regulatory framework governing distribution and investment advice, according to two people familiar with the matter.
“The working group is discussing whether individual MFDs should also be allowed to hold an investment adviser (IA) licence," said one of the two people on condition of anonymity.
It is one of several issues considered by the working group set up by the capital markets regulator earlier this year to streamline norms governing MFDs and RIAs. The group's recommendation would allow individual MFDs to hold dual licences, similar to those held by institutions and other non-individual entities, the person added.
The group was first mentioned by Sebi chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey in March.
The review comes against the backdrop of a longstanding overlap between mutual fund distribution and investment advice. MFDs, who sell mutual fund products to investors and earn commissions from fund houses, also provide incidental advice on other financial products. However, the scope of such advice has not been clearly defined.
An RIA, on the other hand, provides investment advice to clients for a fee and is permitted to advise on financial products beyond mutual funds.
The recommendation, if implemented, would more clearly codify the investment advice given by MFDs. The norms could also help individual MFDs expand their businesses into advisory services.
India has 1,048 RIAs and more than 100,000 MFDs.
 Mint 's queries emailed to Sebi remained unanswered.
Pros and cons
Individual MFDs argue that selling mutual funds without giving an investor advice on their other portfolio is often not possible. The concern is particularly relevant when investors have multiple financial products.
An equity MFD may advise on equity products, while an insurance MFD may advise on insurance products; however, the investor may still have to deal with different intermediaries for different portfolio needs.
“If an equity MFD is only allowed to give advice on equity products, while an insurance MFD gives advice on insurance products. An investor needs one advisor or MFD who can recommend a product based on a thorough analysis of the portfolio, combined with advice on how to generate better overall returns," said the second of the two persons.
While allowing individual MFDs to become investment advisors could address the overlap, it would also create challenges, including potential conflicts of interest, dual compliance requirements and higher costs.
“An (R)IA and an MFD would effectively be wearing two different hats. As an (R)IA, they are expected to provide advice without a commission-driven incentive. As an MFD , for the advice they give, they earn commissions from the products they sell,” said a senior RIA on condition of anonymity.
“This could create an incentive to recommend a product that generates a higher commission, even when another product or a direct-plan alternative may be more appropriate for the client. Over time, an individual having both the licences could blur the distinction between fiduciary advice and commission-based distribution,” the person added.
Then comes the compliance costs.
Kartik Sankaran, an MFD with platform Happyness Factory, which manages assets worth more than ₹ 7,000 crore, said, “If an individual MFD has to gain an RIA licence to give a comprehensive financial plan, it would only increase their costs and compliance. Small MFDs would now need to have a compliance officer, additional documents and record-keeping, which will only add costs.”
To be sure, such compliance costs would only be levied on distributors that also choose to enter advisory.
Another RIA, also on condition of anonymity, said that having a dual licence doesn't solve the underlying problem. “An individual may struggle to manage two businesses, two sets of compliance requirements and two different incentives without mistakes or conflicts,” the RIA added.
Sebi executive director Manoj Kumar said the working group's recommendations have been submitted to Sebi, and final guidelines on the matter will be issued soon, Cafemutual reported on 4 October.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
SEBI's MFD-review working group is **discussing** (not yet rule) whether **individual** mutual fund distributors (MFDs) should be allowed to also hold a Registered Investment Adviser (RIA) licence — i.e. dual registration, the same flexibility institutions/non-individual entities already have. Per LiveMint (2 anonymous sources). Status: the 12-member group (headed by ex-SEBI ED B. Rajendran) has **submitted its recommendations to SEBI**; ED Manoj Kumar said final guidelines "will be issued soon" (Cafemutual, 4 Oct 2026). So this is **"recommendation submitted, guidelines pending"** — not a notified rule, not a consultation paper out for public comment.
- **Why this framing matters:** the headline reads like liberalization, but the group's near-term deliverable is narrower — **guidelines on the scope of "incidental advice"** MFDs may give (where distribution guidance ends and regulated advice begins). The dual-licence idea is one option inside a broader harmonization exercise; the nomenclature piece (letting MFDs call themselves "adviser"/"wealth manager") is explicitly flagged as **early-stage and slower**. → The real near-term change is likely a clarified advice perimeter, with dual-licensing as the headline-grabbing but less-certain element. (analysis)

## [1] Competitors / peers (regulatory peers & the structural divide)
This reverses direction on a **long-standing separation**: SEBI (Investment Advisers) (Amendment) Regulations 2020 (gazetted 3 Jul 2020, effective **1 Oct 2020**) hard-separated advice and distribution at the client level — an individual IA cannot provide distribution services, and the family/group cannot straddle advice+distribution for the same client, explicitly "to avoid conflict of interest." The current proposal walks part of that back for individuals.
- Scale asymmetry is the whole story: **~100,000+ MFDs vs 1,048 RIAs**. The fee-only RIA model never scaled in India; distribution/commission dominates. → Allowing MFDs a dual licence is less about empowering advice than about **regularizing the advice MFDs already give informally** and giving the tiny RIA channel a path to grow via the large MFD base. (analysis)
- Compare global fiduciary debates (e.g. US DOL fiduciary rule, UK RDR which **banned** commissions for advice in 2013): India is moving the opposite way — toward **coexistence of commission + fee** in one person, not separation. That is the counterintuitive bit.

## [2] Company history / fit (SEBI's own trajectory)
Direct continuation of a documented SEBI thread: [[SEBI forms working group to review mutual fund distributor framework]] (2026-03-22) created this exact group (first flagged by chair Tuhin Kanta Pandey in March). Broader arc: [[SEBI reviews mutual fund regulations as AUM hits INR 72 lakh crore]] (2025-06), [[SEBI proposes mutual fund changes to cut costs and boost transparency]] (2025-11), [[SEBI and AMFIexplored reducing KYC and depository costs to improve mutual fund]] (2026-01). SEBI is in a multi-year "ease + rationalize the MF distribution stack" posture while AUM balloons. The dual-licence proposal fits: streamline the MFD/RIA framework rather than police the overlap.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Novelty is **incremental and conditional**: nothing is live; it is a submitted recommendation. The genuinely new fact vs the March note is that the group has **reported out** and a specific option (individual dual licence) + imminent incidental-advice guidelines are on the table.
- **Who captures value / the core tension:** the proposal collides with its own 2020 rationale. An individual wearing both hats earns **commissions as MFD** and **fees as RIA** — the exact conflict the 2020 segregation was built to kill. A quoted senior RIA: dual-hatting "could blur the distinction between fiduciary advice and commission-based distribution" and incentivize recommending higher-commission products over cheaper direct plans. (source)
- **Traction reality for small MFDs:** dual licence raises cost/compliance (compliance officer, records) — one MFD (Happyness Factory, >₹7,000cr) says it "only adds costs." SEBI's mitigation: costs fall only on MFDs who *choose* advisory. → Likely outcome: **larger MFDs/platforms** opt in, small MFDs stay distribution-only; concentration, not democratization. (analysis)

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Watch for SEBI's **incidental-advice guidelines (expected "soon")** as the first concrete output; the dual-licence rule and nomenclature changes trail behind (nomenclature explicitly "early stage"). Industry split: MFDs want status quo / minimal cost burden; RIAs warn on conflict-of-interest; SEBI wants to codify a grey zone 100k+ MFDs already operate in. Second-order: codifying "incidental advice" could **legitimize advice-giving by distributors without fiduciary duty**, which may entrench commission economics rather than shift India toward fee-only — the opposite of the 2020 intent. (hypothesis)

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** India's wealth-management market was ~$154bn (2024) to ~$171bn (2025) by AUM, projected to a ~10.6–11% CAGR through 2030–34 (per imarcgroup/Ken Research, via secondary citations, as of 2026). Deloitte estimates wealth AUM climbing from ~$1.1tn (FY24) to ~$2.3tn (FY29) (per Deloitte, via web, as of 2026). The structural fact this news turns on is the asymmetry of two parallel registries: >100,000 MFDs (commission-paid distributors) vs only ~1,048 RIAs (fee-only fiduciaries) per the note — an ~95:1 ratio. The RIA count is not just small but *shrinking*: ~1,500 in 2020 → ~941–967 in 2025 (per The Ken / business-standard, via web). SEBI itself flagged this decline publicly in March 2026 (per newsonair). "Why now": SEBI's chairman named a working group in March 2026 to review the MFD/RIA framework; per Cafemutual (4 Oct 2026, cited in the note) the group has already submitted recommendations and "final guidelines will be issued soon" — so this is late-stage regulatory drafting, not a fresh consultation.

**Competitive landscape.** The sector's economics run on three levers: (1) distribution commission (trail, paid by AMCs to MFDs), (2) fee-for-advice (paid directly by client to RIA), and (3) compliance load (an RIA needs a compliance officer, record-keeping, audit — a fixed cost that crushes small operators). The two "players" here are regulatory archetypes, not firms: the mass MFD channel (bank/distributor-led; banks hold ~41.7% of the provider market per imarc) vs the tiny fiduciary RIA channel. Basis of competition between the models is the commission-vs-fee incentive, and the gap between them is being filled by *unregulated* finfluencers — the explicit problem SEBI is trying to close (hence the parallel March-2026 "SEBI partners Google to verify trading apps, track finfluencers" thread in-base). Recent regulatory moves easing the RIA side: Dec-2024 SEBI amendment dropped experience/net-worth requirements (deposit-based instead); Aug-2025 proposal to drop degree restrictions for advisers [[SEBI proposes removing degree restrictions for advisers]]. None of these has reversed the RIA decline — which is why SEBI is now reaching for the much larger MFD base as the supply of advice.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/deal/metrics in this item (regulatory) — trading multiples N/A, "no data". Internal regulatory-precedent comps from the base (same regulator, same MFD/RIA framework): [[SEBI forms working group to review mutual fund distributor framework]] (2026-03 — the direct precursor; this note is the outcome of that group), [[SEBI proposes removing degree restrictions for advisers]] (2025-08 — widening adviser entry), [[SEBI reviews mutual fund regulations as AUM hits INR 72 lakh crore]] (2025-06), [[SEBI proposes mutual fund changes to cut costs and boost transparency]] (2025-11). Market-sizing figures above carry low confidence: multiple vendor reports disagree ($154bn vs $171bn base; $286bn vs $436bn targets) and all are secondary/promotional — treat as order-of-magnitude, not precise.

**Risk flags.**
- **Conflict of interest / mis-selling (primary).** A single individual holding both licences wears two hats: fee-advice (meant to be commission-free) and commission distribution. The note's own RIA sources warn this blurs fiduciary advice with commission-driven selling and could bias recommendations toward higher-commission products over direct-plan alternatives — i.e. the reform could *import* the distribution conflict into the advisory channel rather than fix it.
- **Compliance cost → it won't scale to small MFDs.** Dual licence means compliance officer, extra documentation, record-keeping (per Happyness Factory's MFD in the note). Small MFDs — the bulk of the >100,000 — are unlikely to opt in, so the reform may add fiduciary-grade advice only at the top end and leave the long tail (and the finfluencer gap) untouched.
- **Execution / unfinished rule.** Status is "recommendations submitted, guidelines soon" — scope, fee caps, and separation-of-hats safeguards are undefined. The economic impact flips entirely on those details; until published, this is direction-of-travel, not a live regime.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` If implemented, the supply of *regulated* advice in India could expand off the ~100k MFD base rather than the stagnant ~1k RIA base — a structural re-plumbing of advice distribution, favouring fintech/platform players (Happyness Factory-type MFD platforms, and bond/MF platforms like Groww) that can absorb compliance centrally and onboard distributors into dual-licence tooling. Falsifiable thesis: the reform only works if SEBI pairs dual licensing with a hard conflict-of-interest firewall (segregated fee vs commission disclosure); if the final guidelines lack that, watch for the same RIA voices to argue it entrenches mis-selling — the trigger is the "final guidelines" SEBI said are imminent.
