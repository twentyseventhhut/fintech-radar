 NEW YORK - OCTOBER 27, 2026 — zerohash, the leading onchain infrastructure provider, today announced that Open USD (“OUSD”) is now live. The capability is available today to all partners zerohash powers across all products, meaningfully expanding distribution of the stablecoin introduced by Open Standard. OUSD is supported across Base, Ethereum, Solana and Tempo.
This integration reflects zerohash’s role as a distribution layer for onchain money. zerohash now offers OUSD as a rail for the partners it powers, which today include industry leaders including Marqeta and Visa Direct.
The announcement extends zerohash's role in providing leading institutions and enterprises with access to a broad range of chains, assets and instruments without taking on the regulatory or operational complexity themselves. For partners, zerohash also provides the interoperability layer between OUSD and other assets and chains. zerohash partners can now:
Leverage zerohash’s interoperability layer to move onchain money seamlessly across chains and assets. zerohash provides the interoperability layer between OUSD and more than 15 stablecoins across approximately 25 chains.
Seamlessly embed support for OUSD across treasury, account funding, payouts and payins use cases.
“We integrated OUSD because it’s a stablecoin built for payments by the networks that already move the world’s money,” said Mark Daly, Chief Business Officer at zerohash. “For our partners, that means they can seamlessly enable OUSD through their existing zerohash integration, without building additional infrastructure, and with full interoperability across the other stablecoins and chains that we support.”

## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-10-27 Zerohash — a regulated onchain-infrastructure provider (crypto, stablecoins, tokenized assets; regulated entities across 51 U.S. jurisdictions plus EU/LatAm/Australia/NZ/Bermuda) — announced that **Open USD ("OUSD")** is now live across its entire network. Every partner Zerohash already powers can enable OUSD through their existing integration, with no new infrastructure, across treasury, account-funding, payouts and payins. OUSD runs on Base, Ethereum, Solana and Tempo. Flagship partners named: **Marqeta** (card issuing) and **Visa Direct** (global payouts). Zerohash positions itself as a *distribution layer for onchain money*, and provides an interoperability layer between OUSD and >15 other stablecoins across ~25 chains. Source: <https://zerohash.com/press/zerohash-brings-open-usd-ousd-stablecoin-live-across-its-network>.

**[1] What is OUSD / Open Standard.** OUSD is a fully-reserved USD stablecoin governed by **Open Standard**, an independent consortium (140+, later 200+ firms) announced 2026-06-30 and live 2026-09-30 with >$1B committed liquidity. Founding/consortium members span TradFi and crypto: Visa, Mastercard, Stripe, American Express, Coinbase, BlackRock, BNY, Standard Chartered, Google Cloud, IBM, DoorDash, Fireblocks, Solana, Polygon. The token is *issued by Bridge (a Stripe company) on behalf of Open Standard*; CEO is Zach Abrams (Bridge co-founder). Its distinguishing economics: reserve income is **shared across participating institutions** rather than retained by one issuer, and any participant can mint/redeem without volume caps — a direct attack on the Circle/Tether "issuer-keeps-all" float model. See [[Visa, Stripe, 140 firms launch Open USD stablecoin]], [[SBI Holdings and Startale join Open Standard OUSD stablecoin alliance]], [[Stripe challenges Circle with bank-backed Open USD stablecoin]].

**[2] Why Zerohash matters here (distribution, not issuance).** Zerohash does not issue OUSD; it *distributes* it. Its pitch is that institutions get access to many chains/assets/instruments "without taking on the regulatory or operational complexity themselves." This is the same embeddable-rail model seen across prior Zerohash notes: [[Zerohash integrates Monad for real-time USDC payments]] (Feb 2026), [[Tastytrade and Zero Hash enable stablecoin account funding]] (Jul 2025), [[Fintech Wrap Up about Zero Hash]]. The company raised **$104M** in Sep 2025 (Interactive Brokers-led; Morgan Stanley, SoFi, Apollo — [[Zerohash raises $104M from Morgan Stanley, SoFi, Apollo]]), won a **Dutch EMI license** ([[Zerohash receives EMI license from Dutch central bank]]) and launched **Zero Hash Trust Company** ([[Zero Hash Trust Company approved to launch]]), and expanded its Amsterdam EU HQ ([[Zerohash expands European headquarters in Amsterdam]]). Existing clients include Stripe, Shift4, Interactive Brokers.

**[3] Competitive / market context.** Stablecoin-infra peers: **Bridge** (Stripe-owned, $1.1B; Open Issuance + Stripe's 5M+ merchant distribution — and also the OUSD *issuer*), **Paxos** (bank/trust-chartered white-label issuance), **Brale** (branded issuance for fintechs), **Fireblocks** (custody), Crossmint (wallets). Zerohash's edge is the breadth of its regulated stack (trade + custody + tokenization) and multi-chain interoperability rather than issuing its own coin. On the asset side, OUSD is still tiny: ~$0.70B circulating vs ~$74.3B USDC as of early Oct 2026 (USDC settled ~79% of ~$38T adjusted onchain volume in H1 2026). But OUSD's launch rattled Circle (CRCL stock fell 15–18%). Zerohash adding OUSD to its already-distributed network is a meaningful *distribution* accelerant for an otherwise sub-scale coin.

**[4] So what / watch.** This is incremental distribution plumbing, not a new product or raise — but it is strategically aligned: Zerohash becomes a neutral rail that can route OUSD *or* USDC/USDT (>15 coins) for the same partners, hedging whichever stablecoin wins. Watch: (a) whether Marqeta/Visa Direct volumes in OUSD actually materialize vs USDC; (b) whether Zerohash captures interchange/FX/settlement economics from OUSD reserve-sharing; (c) NY DFS / jurisdictional approval caveats flagged in the release (OUSD support "may be subject to regulatory approval… including in New York"). Related rails news: [[Visa Direct moves stablecoin payouts to production with Inyo Global]], [[Marqeta and Coinbase collaborate on stablecoin-backed card payments]].

## Челлендж / ред-тим

<!-- enrichment:challenge -->
**Red-team questions**

1. "Live across its network" — is OUSD truly enabled for *all* partners today, or opt-in per partner? The release says "available today to all partners… across all products," but the NY-DFS disclosure implies jurisdictional gating. Which is it in practice?
2. Are Marqeta and Visa Direct actually *transacting* in OUSD, or merely *technically able* to embed it? The press language ("can now embed") suggests capability, not live volume.
3. Zerohash issues nothing here — this is distribution of someone else's coin. How much economic upside accrues to Zerohash vs Open Standard / Bridge (the issuer)?
4. Does Zerohash get a cut of OUSD's shared reserve income, or only settlement/interop fees? Unstated.
5. "Interoperability across >15 stablecoins and ~25 chains" — is that a new capability or pre-existing? If pre-existing, OUSD is just one more SKU on an existing rail, lowering novelty.
6. Conflict/overlap: Bridge (OUSD issuer) is a direct Zerohash competitor in stablecoin infra. Does distributing OUSD strengthen a rival's coin while commoditizing Zerohash?
7. OUSD is ~$0.70B vs USDC ~$74B. Is partner demand real, or is this a "list everything" move with minimal flows?
8. Does adding OUSD cannibalize Zerohash's existing USDC/USDT volumes, or is it net-new?
9. Visa Direct already moved stablecoin payouts to production (Inyo Global) — is this OUSD step redundant with existing USDC rails on the same platform?
10. Regulatory: OUSD support "may be subject to regulatory approval… including in New York." Which partners/geographies are actually blocked at launch?
11. Press release is self-published (zerohash.com) — any independent confirmation of partner go-live from Marqeta/Visa? (Secondary coverage exists but mostly re-reports the release.)
12. Tempo chain support is listed — Tempo is Stripe/Paradigm-backed and nascent; how much does chain breadth matter vs it being marketing surface area?
13. Does OUSD's consortium governance create any obligation/exposure for Zerohash, or is it purely a technical integration?
14. Timing: OUSD launched 2026-09-30; Zerohash integration ~4 weeks later. Is this a planned consortium rollout (Zerohash was always going to list it) rather than a competitive win?

**Importance: 2/5** — Incremental distribution plumbing: a regulated infra provider adds one more (currently sub-scale, ~$0.70B) stablecoin to an existing multi-coin/multi-chain rail, no raise, no new product, no disclosed economics. Strategically coherent (Zerohash stays coin-neutral and gains OUSD-consortium alignment via Marqeta/Visa Direct) and worth noting as an OUSD-adoption signal, but low standalone materiality versus the OUSD launch itself and Zerohash's funding/licensing milestones.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Stablecoin settlement infrastructure / embedded crypto B2B rails. Size/growth: genuine B2B stablecoin payment flows reached ~$226bn annualised, up ~733% YoY (per McKinsey/Artemis Analytics, via blindpay/stablecoininsider, as of Feb 2026); B2B is ~58–60% of real stablecoin payment volume. Total stablecoin payments ran ~$401–527bn Jan–Aug 2026 (+42–63% YoY), though much of on-chain volume is trading/internal transfers, not commerce (de-PR caveat). Structure: a thin, fast-consolidating "distribution/interop layer" sitting between issuers (Circle, Tether, now Open Standard) and the enterprises/fintechs that want to move onchain money; value accrues to whoever owns regulated rails + breadth of chains/assets (barriers = money-transmitter licensing in 51 US jurisdictions + NYDFS virtual-currency authorisation, plus network/integration lock-in). Why now: the Open USD go-live (Sep 30, 2026) backed by a 140+ firm consortium (Visa, Mastercard, Stripe, Coinbase, BlackRock) with no mint/redeem fees and reserve-interest shared with users (per CoinDesk/Unchained, 2026) is a direct assault on Circle/Tether economics — Circle (CRCL) reportedly dropped ~15% on the launch news. Zerohash's role is to distribute OUSD, not issue it.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: settlement/payment volume (TPV), number of chains/assets/stablecoins supported, regulated-jurisdiction coverage, partner count. Key players: distribution/interop infra — Zerohash, Bridge (Stripe-owned), BVNK (being acquired by Mastercard), StraitsX, Anchorage Digital (bank-focused settlement), Aryze; issuers — Circle (USDC), Tether, Open Standard (OUSD). Basis of competition: regulatory footprint + breadth of chains/assets + distribution reach (embedding into Marqeta, Visa Direct), not price. Recent moves: Anchorage launched bank stablecoin settlement (Feb 2026); TransferMate chose BVNK for real-time settlement (Jun 2026); Mastercard agreed to buy BVNK for up to $1.8bn (Oct 2026, after Coinbase's ~$2bn talks collapsed Nov 2025). Protagonist position: ahead among neutral, multi-issuer distribution layers — Zerohash claims interoperability across 15+ stablecoins and ~25 chains and cites Marqeta and Visa Direct as live partners; adding OUSD on day-one of broad distribution positions it as a neutral switchboard rather than taking a single-issuer bet (analysis). Moat: regulatory licensing scale + switching costs of an embedded API integration (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** Zerohash last priced at ~$1.0bn post-money on a $104M Series D-2 led by Interactive Brokers, Sep 2025 (round valuation, not market cap; total raised ~$275M) — per CoinDesk/Sacra. No public revenue disclosed → EV/Revenue = no data / `[UNSOURCED]`. Private peer anchors: BVNK — Mastercard deal up to $1.8bn (incl. ~$300M contingent) vs a $750M Series B valuation (Dec 2024), on ~$12bn volume (May 2025); Bridge — Stripe acquisition ~$1.1bn on ~$5bn payment run-rate → price/volume ≈ `$1.1bn / $5bn ≈ 0.22x` (analysis, run-rate not revenue). Distribution not computed (mixed metrics, <3 comparable revenue multiples) → qualitative only: Zerohash's $1.0bn looks in-line-to-modest vs BVNK's $1.8bn given BVNK's larger disclosed volume, but Zerohash's metrics are undisclosed so comparison is directional. Internal comps: [[Anchorage Digital launches stablecoin settlement for banks]], [[StraitsX unveils institutional stablecoin settlement infrastructure]], [[TransferMate partners with BVNK for real-time stablecoin settlement]], [[Aryze raises €3 million pre-Series A for stablecoin infrastructure]].

**Risk flags.**
1. Issuer/consortium disintermediation — OUSD is backed by Visa, Mastercard, Stripe and Coinbase, who each own or could build their own distribution rails; as a neutral distributor Zerohash risks being bypassed if the consortium members route OUSD through in-house stacks (second-order: margin captured upstream at the issuer/network layer).
2. Regulatory/jurisdiction concentration — enablement of OUSD is explicitly "subject to regulatory approval in certain jurisdictions, including New York" (per PR); Zerohash's edge is its licence stack, but any NYDFS or state MTL setback directly gates the product and no FDIC/SIPC backstop exists.
3. Commoditisation of the interop layer — competing on breadth of chains/stablecoins invites a race where incumbents (Mastercard-via-BVNK, Stripe-via-Bridge) replicate coverage; without disclosed take-rate/volume it's unclear Zerohash's economics are defensible vs well-capitalised network owners (second-order: multiple compression on the next round).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** OUSD's consortium model (fee-free, reserve-yield shared with users) is a structural attack on Circle/Tether issuance economics, shifting value toward distribution and interop — the layer Zerohash occupies (analysis). Watch: whether Marqeta/Visa Direct OUSD volume actually migrates to Zerohash rails (trigger: disclosed OUSD TPV or a follow-on funding round repricing Zerohash above $1bn). Thesis breaks if Visa/Mastercard/Stripe route OUSD through their own captive infra (Bridge/BVNK), leaving Zerohash a secondary rail.
