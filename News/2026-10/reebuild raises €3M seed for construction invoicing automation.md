reebuild Raises €3M to Build AI-Native Back-Office Automation for Construction
 • reebuild uses AI to automate the commercial back office of construction companies and property developers, from invoice review to cost management. • Customers process document workflows 7x faster, and customers report that procurement costs can be reduced by up to 20%. • The funding accelerates expansion into additional European countries and new AI-native modules that automate contractors' workflows from calculation to accounting, while seamlessly integrating into existing IT landscapes. 
‍ Vienna, October 13, 2026. reebuild , the AI-native back-office automation platform for construction and real estate, has raised a €3 million seed round. The round is led by European investor Fortino Ventures, with participation from Rise PropTech and CuriosityVC. The funding will support expansion into additional European countries and new AI-native modules that automate the commercial workflow of contractors from calculation to accounting. 
reebuild is a vertical AI platform that automates invoice review, cost management and material tracking for construction companies and property developers. Every incoming document – whether PDF, photo, scan or e-invoice – is read line by line by AI, structured and matched automatically. Today, reebuild processes more than €530 million in invoice volume for over 120 companies across more than 8,200 construction sites and projects, and more than 345,000 documents have passed through the platform. Its customers include Pittel+Brausewetter, 3SI Immogroup, Rödl & Herdegen and Kratzert Gruppe. The software is developed in Vienna, hosted in Germany and GDPR-compliant.
 ‍ In construction and real estate, much of the cost administration and control – especially between departments in day-to-day operations – is still managed manually in spreadsheets and filed in paper folders. Invoices are matched by hand against contract terms or delivery notes. Approvals take too long and lack transparency, and early-payment discounts expire unused. Project costs sit in outdated tables, so strategic decisions are made on stale information. With rising risks in financing, energy and materials, margins can shrink quickly and turn into real financial losses. 
On top of that, a generational shift is underway in many companies: as the next generation takes charge, the expectation grows that cost tracking and transparency should be just as digital as they already are in everyday private life. Demand for software that meets that expectation is growing accordingly.
 ‍ “We know the problems of this industry firsthand, and we build reebuild together with our customers, extremely close to the day-to-day reality on site,” says Alexander Kornell, CEO and founder of reebuild. “The software many construction companies and developers still work with today has barely evolved in decades and has fallen behind rising expectations. We're convinced that we can shake up this market with a team that combines deep construction and technology know-how. Not with another tool, but with a solution that truly understands how construction sites and projects are handled on the commercial side.”
That claim is rooted in the team's own experience: Alexander Kornell (CEO) and Matteo Cimadom (CRO) have deep roots in construction through their family businesses. Together with Luca Papay (CTO) and Nicolaus Lederer (COO), they bring years of hands-on experience in construction and real estate, as well as in AI research and development.
 ‍ reebuild's AI reads every incoming document – PDF, photo, scan or e-invoice – line by line and stores it as structured data. Delivery notes, invoices and order confirmations are matched automatically, invoice corrections are recalculated automatically, and the software learns from the decisions of managing directors and commercial managers, taking more and more repetitive work off their hands. On site, a foreman photographs a delivery note with the app, and it is automatically embedded in the system. 
AI agents prepare the work, take over repetitive tasks, and recommend next actions, while the team keeps the final say: approvals run through workflows, so people decide and the software does the time-consuming and error-prone reading, matching and calculating.
 ‍ For contractors, the effect is directly reflected in profit margins. Less time spent on administration and better decisions on current project data add up to measurable savings across the organization: handling an invoice takes around three minutes instead of over twenty, the path from receipt to approval shrinks from five days to a day and a half, and customer feedback reports that contractors can reduce procurement costs by up to 20%. In an industry where margins are typically in the single digits, that is the difference that counts. 
Building a growth company in construction means tackling the fundamental problems, not just the symptoms around them – and building a solution that is not just good but superior for the customer. That is how reebuild grows from customer to customer: by working closely with the people who use the product every day, more than 2,350 of them across over 120 customers, and by making every account more valuable over time. 
 ‍ reebuild's vision reaches far beyond workflow automation: the goal is to reduce administrative overhead in construction to zero. Built on the structured data behind every document, reebuild is developing the agentic back office for contractors, in which AI agents independently handle routine tasks like invoicing, on-site documentation and project estimation – and over time the entire commercial workflow of a contractor, from calculation to accounting. Teams in construction get their time back for what counts: revenue-generating work instead of day-to-day administration.
 ‍ The funding goes into three areas. Geographically, reebuild is established in Austria and Germany and is now expanding into further European countries, where development partnerships already provide first proof points beyond its home markets. On the product side, reebuild is building AI-native modules that fully automate contractors' processes from calculation to accounting. And in Vienna, reebuild is building out its team, which today comprises around 25 people, with new colleagues planned over the coming months.
 ‍ Wouter Goossens, Partner at Fortino Ventures, says, “We believe now is the time for construction companies to digitize and automate their back-office processes with SaaS and AI. reebuild is the right team to execute on this, with the founding team having their family roots in the construction sector.” 
“We’re really happy with how Alexander and the team have developed and excited to keep supporting them on the journey ahead,” says Andrei Podlesnyi, Investment Manager at Hansmann Family Office. "Over the past years, reebuild has proven that staying close to customers is what drives both exceptional growth and outstanding retention."

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
Vienna-based **reebuild** (founded 2022) raised a **€3M ($3.4M) seed**, announced **Oct 13, 2026**, **led by Fortino Ventures** (Ghent/Amsterdam/Munich B2B-AI fund, 3rd fund), with **Rise PropTech** (Liège ConTech/PropTech seed/A) and **CuriosityVC** (Amsterdam, early-stage AI SaaS), plus existing backer **Hansmann Family Office** (Vienna). Product: a vertical-AI "back office" for construction/real-estate GCs and developers — line-by-line OCR/extraction of invoices, delivery notes, order confirmations (PDF/photo/scan/e-invoice), auto-matching against POs/contracts, cost management and material tracking, human-in-the-loop approval workflows. Use of funds: expand beyond Austria/Germany into more European countries, build "calculation→accounting" AI modules, grow the ~25-person Vienna team.

Reported traction (vendor-stated, not independently audited): **>€530M invoice volume processed**, **>120 customers**, **>8,200 sites/projects**, **>345,000 documents**, ~2,350 users. Named customers: Pittel+Brausewetter, 3SI Immogroup, Rödl & Herdegen, Kratzert Gruppe. Efficiency claims: invoice handling ~3 min vs >20; receipt→approval 1.5 days vs 5; "up to 20%" procurement-cost reduction (**explicitly "customer feedback"/self-reported, not a measured study**).

**+ Why framed this way:** This is a small, early seed dressed in "AI operating system / agentic back office" language. The anchor metric — €530M **invoice volume** — is a flattering throughput number, not revenue; at a seed this size real ARR is almost certainly low-single-digit €M (undisclosed — the silence is the tell). The "AI-native" and "agents" framing is doing heavy lifting over what is, at core, document-ingestion + matching + approval routing, a mature AP-automation category. "Up to 20%" savings is the weakest kind of claim (ceiling + anecdotal).

## [1] Competitors / peers
Construction-specific AP/back-office: **Agave** (US, "AI financial operations for contractors," line-item extraction + job-cost coding + ERP sync, shipped AP automation late-2025), **GetBuilt** (US construction AP/payments), **Procore** (invoice mgmt tied to budgets/commitments/change orders). DACH-adjacent ConTech: **Cosuno** (Berlin, digital tendering), **Capmo** (Munich, site mgmt), **PlanRadar** (Vienna, docs/defects) — adjacent, not direct on commercial AP. Horizontal AP/spend that can move down-market into construction: BILL, Ramp, Stampli, AvidXchange, and in Europe **Basware** (which just acquired **Trustpair** [[Basware completes acquisition of Trustpair]]) and **Sovos/Flowie** [[Sovos acquires Flowie for agentic finance orchestration]]. Invoice-verification peers this very month: [[Iridium Credit raises $3M to verify every invoice]] (US, same $3M ticket, AI invoice-vs-document verification).

**+ Why the landscape looks this way / second order:** The defensible wedge in construction AP is **line-item job-cost coding** — the one thing horizontal tools (BILL/Ramp) do poorly — so reebuild's DACH + construction-domain focus is a genuine, if narrow, moat vs horizontal players. But against construction-native incumbents (Agave in the US, Procore globally, Cosuno/Capmo/PlanRadar in DACH) reebuild is **catching up, not ahead**: the capabilities (OCR, PO matching, approval routing, ERP integration) are table stakes by 2026. Its edge is regional/domain intimacy (founders with construction family businesses), not technology differentiation.

## [2] Company history / fit
Founded 2022 in Vienna by Alexander Kornell (CEO) and Matteo Cimadom (CRO) — both from construction family businesses — with Luca Papay (CTO) and Nicolaus Lederer (COO). Three+ years to a €3M seed with >120 customers implies steady, capital-efficient, customer-close growth rather than blitzscale (consistent with investor quotes emphasizing "retention" and "staying close to customers"). Software built in Vienna, hosted in Germany, GDPR-compliant — deliberately positioned for the regulated, data-sensitive DACH SME market.

**+ Why it acts this way:** Construction is a low-margin (single-digit), fragmented, software-laggard industry; the structural logic is to own the **structured data** behind every document, then upsell "agentic" modules (calculation→accounting) that raise ACV per account. The pitch is a land-and-expand data play, not a payments/take-rate play — reebuild touches invoices but is **not** (yet) in the money-movement flow, which caps its B2B-payments relevance despite the tag.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
Not technologically novel — AI document extraction + matching + approval workflow is a crowded 2025–26 category (see the whole spend/AP-automation cluster: [[BILL launches AI agents to automate accounts payable]], [[Ramp adds AI agents to Bill Pay platform]], [[Light raises $30M Series A for AI accounting, US expansion]]). The real, defensible value-add is **vertical specificity for construction in DACH**: e-invoice/delivery-note matching at line-item level, on-site photo capture, and job-level cost transparency — things horizontal tools miss. Traction is **real but early and self-reported**: >120 customers and €530M throughput are solid proof points for a 2022 seed, but revenue, churn, gross margin and the "20% savings" claim are undisclosed/anecdotal.

**+ Where the margin sits:** reebuild captures value as **SaaS per-seat/per-volume software**, not as a payment take-rate — so its economics ride on retention and ACV expansion via new modules, which is why investors stress retention. The risk: as e-invoicing becomes mandatory across the EU (ViDA), structured invoice data becomes commoditized infrastructure, and horizontal AP platforms + ERPs (SAP, Datev in DACH) can bolt on construction coding, squeezing a point-solution. The moat is data + domain trust, and it is thin.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Near-term: European geographic expansion (dev partnerships cited as first proof points outside Austria/Germany), "calculation→accounting" agentic modules, hiring past ~25. Sentiment is constructive but modest — this is one of several small-ticket ConTech/AP seeds, not a category-defining round. Tailwinds: EU e-invoicing mandates (ViDA, Germany's phased B2B e-invoice rollout from 2025–28) force digitization and directly expand reebuild's TAM; generational handover in family construction firms raises demand for digital tooling (reebuild's own framing, plausible).

**+ Why the market goes this way / counterintuitive:** The e-invoicing mandate is a **double-edged** driver: it pulls reebuild's customers toward digitization (demand up), but it also standardizes the very data layer reebuild extracts, lowering the barrier for ERPs and horizontal players to enter — so the mandate that creates the opportunity also invites the competition that could commoditize it. At seed stage with €3M, reebuild's survival depends on reaching defensible ACV/retention before a well-capitalized incumbent (Agave-style or an SAP/Datev module) targets DACH construction AP.
