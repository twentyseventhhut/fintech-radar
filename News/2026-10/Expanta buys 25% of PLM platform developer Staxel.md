Холдинг разработчиков промышленного ПО «Экспанта» (входит в ГК «Ультиматек») приобрел 25% ООО «СиЭс Технологии» — разработчика российской PLM-платформы «Стаксель», рассказали " Коммерсанту " в компании. Сделка предполагает доведение доли до 50% в два транша, говорят в компании, добавляя, что средневзвешенная оценка по двум траншам составит 300 млн руб.
«Стаксель» — модульная платформа для управления жизненным циклом продукта, инженерными данными и процессами разработки, включена в реестр российского ПО.
«Сделка ориентирована на средний сегмент рынка управления жизненным циклом изделий. Для „Экспанты“ сделка замыкает продуктовый контур. Холдинг уже развивает решения для производственного планирования (Adeptik APS), финансового планирования и расчета себестоимости («Алгоритм1»), управления ремонтами («Датабриз»). «Стаксель» добавляет недостающее звено — инженерные данные, связывая проектирование с производством и финансовым результатом», — говорят в «Экспанте».

# Context-enrichment: Expanta buys 25% of PLM platform developer Staxel
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 2/5._

Internal retrieval: semsearch index down (OpenRouter 402). Grep fallback over `News/` finds NO prior notes on Expanta, Staxel/CS Technology, Ultimatek, or Adeptik (the only hit for each tag is this note itself). The "PLM" grep hits are false positives (the acronym in unrelated EU/fintech notes). So no internal duplicate — freshness = fresh. This is a small Russian industrial-software (not strictly fintech) M&A tuck-in; it sits at the edge of the corpus's remit.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- **The deal**: Expanta (industrial-software holding inside GK Ultimatek) bought **25% of OOO "SiEs Tekhnologii" (CS Technology)**, developer of the **Staxel** PLM platform. The company frames it as a staged plan to reach **50% in two tranches**, with a **weighted-average valuation across both tranches of RUB 300m** (~USD 3.6m at ~83 RUB/USD). Source: Kommersant via Mergers.ru.
- **De-PR'd**: only 25% is actually bought today; the 50% is an *intention* across future tranches, not a signed full acquisition. "Weighted-average valuation 300m across two tranches" is a soft number — it is neither the price paid for 25% now, nor a clean post-money; it is a blended figure that conveniently anchors the headline without disclosing the cash outlay for the first tranche. **No disclosed cash, no revenue/ARR of Staxel, no client count, no profit** — classic early-stage domestic-M&A opacity.
- **Why structured as a 25%→50% two-tranche creep, not an outright buy** (analysis): staged minority entry lets Expanta (a) de-risk against unproven mid-market traction, (b) keep the founding team incentivized/locked via an earn-in to the second tranche, and (c) defer the real valuation until Staxel's revenue under the holding is visible. It also means the headline "bought a PLM developer" overstates control — Expanta is buying optionality, not yet a subsidiary.
- Staxel is **in the Russian software registry** (reg. 19.05.2023, #17742); the dev team has built it since ~2019 (company active since 2013). Modular PLM for product lifecycle, engineering data and development processes. ([[Expanta buys 25% of PLM platform developer Staxel]] is this note.)

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Domestic PLM incumbents** (post-2022 import-substitution beneficiaries): **Askon (KOMPAS-3D / LOTSMAN:PLM)** and **Top Systems (T-FLEX PLM/DOCs)** are the entrenched leaders; also **1C**, **Digital Alliance**, **Programsoyuz** in the "Development" quadrant of the 2024 import-substitution matrix. Western vendors — **Dassault (SolidWorks/CATIA/ENOVIA), Siemens (NX/Teamcenter), PTC (Creo/Windchill), Autodesk** — suspended sales/renewals in 2022, creating the vacuum.
- **Position**: Staxel is a *small challenger* explicitly targeting the **mid-market** ("средний сегмент"), i.e. deliberately NOT competing head-on with Askon/Top Systems for the large OAK/defense/heavy-industry Teamcenter-replacement deals. That is a sober positioning — the big enterprise PLM-replacement budgets (e.g. OAK's phased Teamcenter import-substitution) are already being contested by the majors with deeper engineering stacks.
- **Why the lay of the land is this way** (analysis): the majors won the enterprise tier because PLM replacement is deep, multi-year integration work gated on CAD/engineering-data compatibility. The mid-market is under-served precisely because it is less lucrative per deal — so Staxel's open niche is real but structurally lower-margin and slower to consolidate. Second-order: Expanta isn't buying a market leader, it's buying a *puzzle piece* to complete its own stack (see [2]).

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Expanta** = Ultimatek's roll-up of Russian industrial software, serving oil & gas, petrochemicals, mining, machine-building. It is assembling a **vertical "contour"**: **Adeptik (APS/MES, production planning)** entered the holding in July 2025; **Algoritm1 (financial planning / cost)**; **Databriz (maintenance management)**. Staxel adds the **missing link — engineering/PLM data** — connecting design → production planning → financial result.
- **Why Expanta acts this way** (analysis): a holding of point products has limited pricing power; a *connected* design-to-cost suite can be sold as a platform with cross-sell and higher stickiness — a classic "integration premium" roll-up logic. PLM is the upstream data spine the rest of the stack feeds on, so without it the "contour" was incomplete. Buying 25% (not building) is a buy-vs-build time-to-market choice in a market where the import-substitution window is open NOW.
- **Fit check**: logically coherent. But execution risk is integration — stitching four separately-built Russian products into one data model is exactly where roll-ups usually underdeliver (data standards, release cadence, team retention). The two-tranche structure hints Expanta itself wants proof before fully committing.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Novelty**: low. This is a **consolidation/tuck-in**, not a new product or technology. Staxel itself predates the deal (registry 2023, dev since 2019). The only new thing is ownership + the stated intent to integrate.
- **Traction**: **undisclosed and unverifiable** — no revenue, no client list, no deployments cited. Expanta's own target (2–3% of the PLM segment in 4 years = RUB 360–540m annual revenue) implies Staxel's *current* PLM revenue is well below that; it is an aspiration, not a run-rate.
- **Who captures the margin** (analysis): value in this stack sits with whoever owns the engineering-data spine and the integration layer, which is why Expanta wants PLM. But at 25% today, Expanta captures only a minority of a small, pre-scale asset. The multiple here is governed by import-substitution tailwind, not by demonstrated unit economics — if the mandated-localization push slows (or a major like Askon moves down into mid-market), the thesis weakens quickly.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Market size (Strategy Partners)**: RU PLM ≈ **RUB 18bn in 2024** out of a ~RUB 50bn engineering-software market; domestic-solutions share targeted at **93% by 2030** (policy-driven). Engineering SW grew ~+20% YoY in 2024. So the TAM is real and policy-backed, but PLM is a *fraction* of it and the domestic share already climbing means late-movers face narrowing whitespace.
- **Plans**: lift stake to 50% (second tranche), integrate Staxel into the Adeptik/Algoritm1/Databriz contour, chase 2–3% share.
- **Risks / counterintuitive second-order** (analysis): (a) the import-substitution tailwind is a *one-time* share grab — once Western vendors are displaced, growth reverts to GDP/industrial-capex pace, and consolidation pressure favors the majors, not sub-scale challengers; (b) a RUB 300m blended valuation is tiny — this signals Staxel is early, so the "replaces an exiting Western PLM" narrative is aspirational; (c) policy dependence cuts both ways: mandated localization helps now but caps optionality (no export, ruble-only, defense-adjacent scrutiny).
- **Sentiment**: neutral-to-mildly-positive domestic trade press (Kommersant/TAdviser/CNews); no analyst price targets (private). This is routine import-substitution consolidation.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Russian PLM (product-lifecycle management) software, mid-market segment. Per Strategy Partners (via Mergers.ru/Kommersant, as of 2024): the RU PLM market was ~18bn RUB inside a ~50bn RUB engineering-software market, with the domestic-share target set at 93% by 2030; the broader engineering-software segment is projected to grow ~16%/yr through 2030 (per Strategy Partners, via tadviser/d-russia, 2024). Structure: consolidating out of a sanctions-driven import-substitution wave — Siemens Teamcenter and Dassault were deeply entrenched before 2022, and PLM is one of the harder Western stacks to replicate domestically (per upFront.eZine / tadviser, 2024-25). Entry barriers are high: deep CAD/engineering-data integration, regulatory tailwind (RU software registry inclusion, state funding), and switching costs once a vendor owns the engineering-data layer. Why now: import-substitution mandates + the "registry of Russian software" gate make a credible full-stack domestic suite (design → production → finance) a land-grab window.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: annual software revenue, registry inclusion, installed workstations replaced (import-substitution volume), module breadth across the PLC. Key players (per Habr/tadviser, FY2024 revenue): Askon/KOMPAS-3D ~6.6bn RUB (+18% YoY), Nanosoft/nanoCAD ~4.56bn, Top Systems/T-FLEX PLM ~4.1bn; top-8 CAD/PLM vendors ~15bn RUB combined (+22% YoY). Also named as PLM "Development"-quadrant vendors: 1C, Digital Alliance, Programsoyuz (per d-russia "Matrix of Import Substitution 2024"). Basis of competition: product breadth + registry status + replacing specific Western seats, not price. Protagonist's position: Staxel (OOO "CS Technology" / CS Group) is a niche mid-market entrant, well behind Askon/Top Systems on scale; Expanta's angle is distribution/breadth, not standalone PLM share. Moat `(analysis)`: Expanta is assembling a vertically integrated industrial suite — Adeptik APS (production planning), Algoritm1 (cost/financial planning), Databriz (maintenance) — and Staxel closes the "engineering data" gap, linking design→production→P&L. The moat is switching costs across a bundled stack, not PLM product leadership.

**Comps & multiples.** Deal: Expanta (GK Ultimatek) buys 25% of Staxel now, to 50% over two tranches, weighted-avg value ~300m RUB. The two numbers needed for a clean revenue multiple (Staxel's current revenue) are not disclosed → EV/Revenue = **no data**. Forward frame from the buyer's own target: Expanta expects 2–3% of the PLM segment within four years = 360–540m RUB annual revenue (per company, via Mergers.ru); against the ~300m RUB implied 100% valuation that is <1x forward revenue `(analysis)` — but it is a 4-year aspiration, not a filing, so treat as management guidance, not a verified multiple `[UNSOURCED]` on actuals. External peers for scale context only (private, no market cap): Askon 6.6bn / Top Systems 4.1bn RUB revenue — Staxel is an order of magnitude smaller. Internal comps (RU industrial/enterprise-software consolidation, same-month base): [[MTS bundles enterprise solutions under MTS CorpTech brand]] (bundling enterprise solutions under one brand — same "assemble a suite" playbook), [[Kommersant Positive Technologies enters the data market]] (RU software vendor expanding adjacency), [[RusGazDobycha transfers 49% in oilfield-equipment supplier GMS]] (RU industrial-asset M&A, staged/minority ownership). Distribution not computed — <3 comparable valuation figures; qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.** (1) Valuation/disclosure gap: a 25%→50% staged buy at a ~300m RUB weighted average with no disclosed target revenue means the implied multiple rests entirely on the buyer's 4-year 2–3%-share aspiration — if share lands below plan the entry price looks rich. (2) Competitive scale: Askon (6.6bn) and Top Systems/T-FLEX PLM (4.1bn) already own mid-to-large deployments and the engineering-data layer; Staxel must win seats against entrenched domestic leaders, not just departing Western vendors — execution/ramp risk. (3) Policy dependence: the entire thesis leans on the import-substitution mandate and continued registry preference/state funding; any easing of that tailwind (or a leader like Askon moving down into mid-market) compresses the opening.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is consolidation, not a re-rating: Expanta is buying the missing "engineering data" node to sell an integrated design-to-finance industrial suite, mirroring the broader RU enterprise-software bundling trend ([[MTS bundles enterprise solutions under MTS CorpTech brand]]). Falsifiable thesis: if the second tranche closes to 50% and Expanta reports PLM revenue approaching the 360–540m RUB / 2–3%-share path within four years, the suite strategy is working; trigger to watch — tranche-2 close terms and any disclosed Staxel revenue. What breaks it: Askon/Top Systems extending down-market into the mid-segment Expanta is targeting.
