## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**What happened.** On 2026-10-23, Banca d'Italia Vice Director General (Deputy Governor) **Chiara Scotti** told the Institut International d'Études Bancaires that banks should start investing now in digital-asset technology — tokenization and DLT — "despite uncertainty" over how the ecosystem will develop. Her core argument: postponing investment saves cost in the short term but "hinders the learning process" and risks banks missing future business opportunities and losing influence over the future financial system. She counseled small-scale experiments and gradual commitment rather than betting on a single technology — "flexibility and gradual commitment can balance caution with innovation." (Reuters, Ansa, Cointelegraph, The Block, 2026-10-23.)

**Who / what exactly.** Scotti framed tokenization — converting bank deposits/assets into transferable on-chain tokens — as a **strategic challenge** that goes beyond payment specialists and touches enterprise strategy and the future market structure. She argued the **two-tier monetary system** (public anchor + private intermediation) is "not obsolete" but is evolving, and that banks "must act if they are to preserve their role as issuers of private money" (i.e. deposits).

**EU policy context.** Scotti pointed to public-sector initiatives already underway: the **digital euro** (ECB retail CBDC, extending the public monetary anchor to retail digital payments) and the ECB wholesale-DLT tracks **Pontes** (bridging DLT platforms to TARGET central-bank money) and **Appia** (longer-term wholesale/cross-border settlement). She also urged the EU to assess whether **SEPA** could be extended into a **tokenized SEPA** scheme — leveraging Europe's existing scale, shared standards and interoperability — framed as getting ahead of private stablecoins before they "take over." This sits against the **MiCA** backdrop (in force; EURØP and other euro stablecoins now regulated under it).

**Internal links.**
- EU euro-stablecoin / MiCA momentum: [[European firms launch Eurøpe Consortium for onchain finance]] (MiCA-regulated EURØP stablecoin) — the private-sector side of the same "who issues euro money on-chain" contest Scotti is reacting to.
- Wholesale-CBDC tokenized settlement precedent: [[MAS plans to settle tokenised MAS Bills with wholesale CBDC]] — the model Pontes/Appia echo in Europe.
- Bank-facing regulated crypto rails: [[SaaScada and Zumo give UK banks regulated crypto route]] and tokenized funds [[Sygnum brings Fidelity tokenized liquidity fund to Singapore]] — the "small-scale experiments" lenders can start with.
- Bank stablecoin/payments moves: [[Citi details stablecoin checkout with Coinbase]].

**Timeline.** 2026-05-04 Scotti speech "Digital money and the architecture of trust" (BIS/Bankitalia) → 2026-10-23 IIÉB remarks urging investment + tokenized SEPA → ongoing ECB digital-euro preparation and Pontes/Appia wholesale pilots.

**Why it matters.** A euro-area central bank publicly nudging commercial banks to spend on tokenization — and floating tokenized SEPA — is a notable shift from "wait and see," signaling EU competitiveness anxiety vs. US stablecoin growth. But it is a speech/exhortation, not a rule or mandate; no money, timeline, or supervisory requirement is attached.

3. Date integrity: speech dated 2026-10-23; note dated 2026-10-27 (digest ingest). No conflict.
4. "Tokenized SEPA" — is this a Bankitalia proposal, an ECB decision, or Scotti's personal "area for reflection"? It is Scotti urging the **EU to evaluate** it; not an approved scheme. Avoid implying a live program.
5. Pontes/Appia — are these real ECB initiatives? Yes, ECB wholesale-DLT settlement tracks announced 2026; Scotti cites them as public-sector adaptation. Verify we didn't conflate them with the retail digital euro.
6. Does "digital asset" mean crypto/Bitcoin, or deposit tokenization/DLT? Scotti's emphasis is **tokenization of deposits/assets and DLT**, not speculative crypto holdings — framing should not suggest banks buy Bitcoin.
7. Contradiction risk: Scotti says two-tier system is "not obsolete" yet urges heavy digital investment — is that inconsistent? No; she argues banks must digitize to **preserve** their private-money role within the two-tier model.
8. Novelty vs. prior statements: Scotti's 2026-05 "Digital money and the architecture of trust" already covered this — is 2026-10-23 incremental? Yes, it sharpens the "invest now or lose influence" and tokenized-SEPA angle; fresh enough but part of a continuum.
9. Is this duplicated by any existing vault note? No note covers the 2026-10-23 Scotti remarks specifically; related notes are private-sector (Eurøpe Consortium) or non-EU (MAS). Verdict: fresh.
10. Competitiveness framing — is "EU vs US stablecoins" the note's spin or Scotti's? It is supported (tokenize SEPA "before stablecoins take over" per spaziocrypto/The Block) but keep it attributed.
11. Who actually benefits? Tokenization vendors, DLT infra (Fireblocks-type), custodians — but note [[Fireblocks to cut about 7% of workforce]] shows sector cost pressure; adoption is early.
12. Measurable impact? None yet — no bank commitments, spend figures, or pilots announced in response. Pure signaling.
13. Source quality: Reuters primary wire; Italian outlets (Ansa, Teleborsa) and crypto trades (Cointelegraph, The Block) corroborate. Strong.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector context.** Scotti's push lands in a European digital-asset market defined by regulatory structure first, adoption second. MiCA has given banks a single 27-state rulebook since 2024, but issuance has concentrated in stablecoins rather than the tokenised deposits Scotti is flagging. A ~37-bank consortium (BNP Paribas, BBVA, ING, UniCredit, SEB and others) is building a MiCA-compliant euro stablecoin on Fireblocks infrastructure, targeting an H2-2026 launch contingent on De Nederlandsche Bank approving the Qivalis EMI. The ECB's digital euro remains in its preparation phase with no realistic launch before 2028-2029. So the "invest now" message is explicitly aimed at the gap: dollar-backed stablecoins dominate on-chain settlement today, and the EU's own retail CBDC is years out.

**What's actually novel here.** This is a regulator (not a vendor or bank) telling lenders that *delay* is the risk — that sitting out tokenisation cedes influence over future market structure. That framing matters because the standard bank objection is "the tech and standards aren't settled, so wait." Scotti counters with a staged-investment thesis: small experiments, bets that stay useful across multiple technology scenarios, learning-by-doing. It is advocacy, not a mandate or supervisory requirement — no capital, licensing or deadline changes attached.

**In-base comps / where EU banks are moving.** The vault contains several 2026-10 data points consistent with the thesis: SaaScada + Zumo giving UK banks a regulated crypto route; Sygnum bringing Fidelity's tokenised liquidity fund to Singapore; S&P launching a Vault Risk Assessment framework (vault deposits at $10B, up from $1.5B two years prior — rapid growth but off a small base); plus a wave of stablecoin-rail plays (Citi/Coinbase checkout, Euronet/Fireblocks card issuing, SAP/Circle). The pattern: European and global banks are building, but predominantly via stablecoins and third-party infrastructure rather than native deposit tokens — exactly the lag Scotti is implicitly warning about. European Commission's own April-2026 read was that tokenised deposits remain far less developed than stablecoins and best suited to treasury/settlement use cases for now.

**Competitive gap.** US route is stablecoin-led and faster-moving; Asia (Singapore via MAS, Sygnum) is active in tokenised funds. Europe's advantage is a comprehensive rulebook; its handicap is speed and the overhang of a not-yet-live digital euro plus potential dual MiCA+PSD2 licensing (from March 2026) that can double compliance cost for EMT custody/transfer. Scotti's intervention reads as central-bank acknowledgement that rulebook comprehensiveness alone does not close the adoption gap.

**Risk flags.**
- **Advocacy, not policy — low market impact.** A deputy governor speech carries no supervisory teeth; no capital/licensing/timeline change. Signalling value only; easy to over-read as regulatory greenlight.
- **Uncertainty cuts both ways.** Scotti herself concedes the technology path (which rail, which standard) is unsettled; "invest across scenarios" is hard to operationalise and small experiments rarely move P&L — ROI on early tokenisation bets is speculative.
- **Tokenised deposits still immature.** EC's own 2026 assessment: deposit tokens lag stablecoins, confined to treasury/settlement; no proven retail or cross-border product-market fit yet.
- **Digital-euro / stablecoin tension.** Banks investing in private tokenisation could be partly pre-empted or crowded out by an ECB digital euro (post-2028) and by the bank-consortium euro stablecoin; strategy risk in picking the wrong horse.
- **Compliance cost drag.** Potential dual MiCA + PSD2 licensing for EMT services from March 2026 raises the cost of exactly the activity being encouraged — an unaddressed friction in the "invest now" call.
