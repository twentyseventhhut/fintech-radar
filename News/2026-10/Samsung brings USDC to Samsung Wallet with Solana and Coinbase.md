NEW YORK, Oct. 07, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- Bastion , a stablecoin infrastructure provider for global enterprises and financial institutions, today announced that it has been selected by Samsung as the licensed stablecoin custodian and an infrastructure partner for new built-in stablecoin capabilities inside Samsung Wallet, as part of a service delivered with other partners. The feature will be available the last week of October 2026 across 82 million eligible U.S. Galaxy devices.
Bastion provides regulated custody, stablecoin account operations, and compliance infrastructure for Samsung Wallet’s stablecoin feature, and remittances to bank accounts in more than 60 countries. The feature combines Samsung’s customer experience with Bastion’s infrastructure, operations and regulatory license oversight to support compliance and ease of use. At launch, Galaxy users will be able to hold and send stablecoins directly in Samsung Wallet without installing a separate crypto app or managing private keys.
"The crypto industry has spent years talking about reaching the mainstream, and this is what it looks like. Samsung is putting stablecoins in front of millions of U.S. Galaxy users," said Nassim Eddequiouaq, CEO of Bastion. "Most of them will never think about custody, compliance, blockchains, or cross-border remittance rails, and that's exactly the point. Financial infrastructure works best when people don't have to see it. We built Bastion to handle that complexity so the experience feels simple and safe."
Bastion is the licensed custodian and money transmission partner for Samsung’s stablecoin wallet functionality within a multi-partner service. Through its APIs, Bastion provides the following services.
 Custody and accounts: Bastion provides stablecoin custody for users of Samsung Wallet’s stablecoin feature, keeps the ledger for each user's balance and executes USDC transfers on blockchain networks.
 Identity and compliance: Bastion verifies and screens the identity of each user of the stablecoin service. Bastion also screens blockchain addresses and monitors transactions for fraud, money laundering, and sanctions exposure.
 Cross-border remittances: Users can send money from Samsung Wallet to a bank account in more than 60 countries, delivered in local currency. Bastion converts the USDC and routes each payout through its network of local regulated partners.
 Transaction authorization: Bastion provides transaction authorization for the stablecoin service by verifying the device signature on each transfer request. Transfers can only be initiated from a registered device and must be authorized by the user's biometric authentication.
Bastion is partnering with Coinbase as its official USDC sub-custodian for Samsung Wallet via Coinbase Prime Vault. Operated together with Bastion and Coinbase, USDC will be the first supported stablecoin in Samsung Wallet. Solana and Sui will provide blockchain network support. Future capabilities will include the ability to pay with stablecoins online or by tapping a Galaxy device in store, followed by expansion into additional markets.
"Samsung Wallet is about making useful experiences feel simple and intuitive," said Woncheol Chai, EVP and Head of the Digital Wallet Team at the Mobile eXperience (MX) Business at Samsung Electronics. "Stablecoins have the potential to make moving money around the world faster and easier, and we want Galaxy users to be able to take advantage of that without having to navigate the complexity of traditional crypto tools. Bastion brings regulated custody, stablecoin account operations, cross-border remittances, and compliance capabilities that help us deliver this experience with confidence."
The news closely follows Bastion’s conditional approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) for a national trust bank charter, advancing regulatory oversight beyond current licenses. Its operating entity, Bastion Platforms US, LLC, is a money services business registered with FinCEN and holds state money transmitter licenses.

_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 4/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On 7 Oct 2026 Samsung, the Solana Foundation, Bastion and Coinbase announced that **Samsung Wallet / Samsung Pay will natively support USDC transfers**, launching **"the last week of October 2026"** across **82M eligible U.S. Galaxy devices** (U.S. residents 18+, Samsung Account, Android 13+). Users can hold/send USDC without a separate crypto app or managing private keys, with integrated fiat on/off-ramps and **cross-border remittance to bank accounts in >60 countries** in local currency.

De-PR'd: this is **announced, not live** — the launch date is still ~3 weeks out and no transaction volumes, fees, FX spreads or merchant metrics exist. The three press releases each flatter a different partner's role; the real stack is a **multi-partner division of labor**:
- **Bastion** = licensed stablecoin custodian + money-transmission partner. Keeps the per-user ledger, executes on-chain USDC transfers, runs KYC/AML/sanctions screening, device-signature + biometric transaction authorization, and routes the >60-country payouts via local regulated partners. This is the regulated spine of the product.
- **Coinbase** = USDC **sub-custodian** via Coinbase Prime Vault (holds the actual USDC); USDC is the **default/first** supported stablecoin.
- **Solana (and Sui)** = blockchain rails behind the scenes.
- **Samsung** = distribution + UX (82M devices).

**Why structured this way → what it reveals:** Samsung is NOT becoming a crypto company — it is renting a fully regulated stack and keeping only the wallet UX. The chain (Solana) is deliberately invisible. The load-bearing choice is **Bastion**, which holds NY trust charter + conditional OCC national-trust-bank approval (18 Sep 2026) + FinCEN MSB + state MTLs. Critically, **Bastion is a Samsung Next and Coinbase Ventures portfolio company** (founded 2023 by ex-a16z-crypto execs) — Samsung is deploying its own invested infrastructure, which explains the partner choice more than a competitive bake-off would.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Revolut** already does Solana send/receive/pay with USDC/USDT/SOL + staking ([[Revolut integrates Solana for payments, transfers and staking]], Dec 2025) — but Revolut is a neobank app, not a pre-installed OEM wallet.
- **PayPal (PYUSD) and Western Union** are cited as live enterprise users of Solana stablecoin rails.
- **Apple Pay / Google Pay**: no native in-wallet stablecoin send/remittance as of this date — Samsung is **first among the big three OEM wallets** to embed stablecoin remittance at the OS-wallet layer.
- **Coinbase + Samsung themselves** previously shipped Coinbase One access inside Samsung Wallet ([[Samsung taps Coinbase to bring crypto to 75 million Galaxy users]], Oct 2025) and Samsung Pay as a Coinbase funding method ([[Coinbase adds Samsung Pay for crypto payments in North America]], Jul 2025) — both were **trading/exchange** plays, not payments.

**Why the landscape looks like this → second order:** Stablecoin remittance is where the three big OEM wallets diverge. Apple/Google route card rails and are cautious on crypto-in-OS; Samsung, lacking a card-network moat, uses crypto remittance as **differentiation at the top of the Android funnel**. Position: **ahead on OEM-wallet remittance distribution, parity-or-behind on actual capability** (Revolut/PayPal ship more features today). The moat is distribution (82M devices), not technology.

## [2] Company history / fit
Samsung Wallet's 2025–26 arc: Coinbase One embed (Oct 2025), Samsung Pay as Coinbase on-ramp (Jul 2025), and a parallel **won-stablecoin push** via the Dunamu stake ([[Samsung affiliates buy Dunamu stake amid won stablecoin push]], May 2026). This USDC launch is the logical escalation: from *access to crypto* → *native dollar money-movement*. Fits cleanly.

**Why Samsung acts this way:** Samsung Wallet is a loyalty/stickiness layer around Galaxy hardware with no take-rate of its own (unlike Apple's card deals). Embedded USD remittance adds a utility that (a) raises wallet engagement and (b) positions Galaxy against Apple on a mainstream-useful feature — structural pressure to add software value where it has no payments-network economics.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
**Genuinely new vs the 2025-10 note:** that was Coinbase One (premium exchange access). This is **native USDC hold/send + cross-border remittance inside the OS wallet** — a different product and a distinct integration. Novelty is real at the **distribution layer** (82M pre-installed devices, no app download, biometric auth, invisible chain).

**But traction = zero today** — not live, no volumes, no fee disclosure. Solana's $5.25T 2026 stablecoin volume and ~20% YoY supply growth are *network* stats, not *Samsung* adoption.

**Who captures the margin (1 level deeper):** The economics are silent. Likely revenue sits in **FX conversion spread** on the >60-country off-ramps (Bastion's local partners) and on/off-ramp fees — NOT in the on-chain transfer. So the value accrues to **Bastion (regulated money-movement) and the local payout partners**, with Samsung taking a distribution/UX cut. Samsung risks being the "shop window" while Bastion/Coinbase own the regulated rails and economics — the same disintermediation risk flagged generally for distribution-only wallet plays. USDC being default also hands **Circle** a large captive distribution channel for free.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
Roadmap: in-store tap-to-pay and online stablecoin checkout with Galaxy, then more markets "subject to local regulatory requirements." Regulatory backdrop is the tailwind: the **GENIUS Act** (signed 18 Jul 2025; OCC/Fed implementing rules through 2026, issuer-only restrictions phasing in by ~2028) gives a compliant U.S. framework for payment stablecoins — the reason a mainstream OEM can ship this now. Bastion's OCC conditional charter is the practical enabler.

**Why the market goes this way → counterintuitive second order:** mainstream stablecoin adoption will likely come through **invisible embedding in trusted consumer surfaces** (OEM wallets), not standalone crypto apps. But the counterintuitive risk: distribution at 82M devices does **not** guarantee demand (AMBCrypto's framing — "will demand follow?"). Remittance is a habit-driven, corridor-specific market dominated by Wise/Western Union/bank apps; a pre-installed button does not automatically beat an incumbent in a specific corridor. Downside triggers: thin FX/fee take-rate, slow corridor coverage, and that USDC-only + U.S.-only limits the actual remittance use case at launch.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Consumer-wallet stablecoins. Total stablecoin market cap ~$308bn (Aug 2026), off the ~$322bn May 2026 peak; Citi/US Treasury-cited projections see ~$420bn by year-end (per KuCoin/BitcoinFoundation roundups, as of Aug 2026). The use case here is cross-border remittances — a market variously sized at ~$579bn (Juniper) to ~$880bn (Research&Markets) for 2026, where the average send still costs ~6.49% (via einvoicegenerator, 2025–26). "Why now": the structural pitch is disintermediating a 5–10% fee, multi-day correspondent-banking stack with near-instant blockchain settlement. Structure — the stablecoin value chain is unbundling into issuance (Circle/USDC, PayPal/PYUSD, Tether), rails/chains (Solana, Ethereum, Tron), and regulated custody/MTL + on/off-ramp infrastructure (Bastion, Coinbase Prime). Samsung occupies none of those layers: it supplies pure distribution — 82M US Galaxy devices — and rents everything else. Entry barrier it clears is the one nobody else has: a pre-installed wallet on the device.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: stablecoin supply/circulation, on-chain settled volume, active wallets, take rate on conversion/FX, and distribution reach (installed base). Key players and basis of competition = distribution, not product:
- **PayPal (PYUSD)** — 400M+ users, Venmo 60M+ US; PYUSD mkt cap peaked ~$4.2bn Mar 2026, contracted to ~$2.8bn by mid-Jul 2026 (via CryptoBriefing/eco.com). Expanded to 70 markets Mar 2026. See [[PayPal expands dollar stablecoin to seventy markets]], [[PayPal's PYUSD stablecoin reaches $1B market cap via Spark]].
- **Stripe** — stablecoin cards to 100+ countries, see [[Stripe to expand stablecoin cards to 100+ countries]].
- **Circle (USDC issuer)** — the asset Samsung defaults to. Q2 2026: $73.3bn USDC in circulation (+19% YoY), quarterly on-chain volume $14.8tn (+151%), revenue $701m (via stablecoininsider, Q2 2026). USDC ~23% stablecoin share, #2 behind USDT.
- **Apple Pay / Google Wallet** — no native stablecoin feature surfaced in 2026 search; Samsung is first mover among the big-three device wallets on native stablecoin send (analysis).

Samsung's position: niche-but-structurally-advantaged distributor — it ships the default wallet to 82M devices, so customer-acquisition cost is near-zero, but it owns no economics layer. Moat = installed-base distribution + brand trust (switching cost of a pre-loaded wallet); it does NOT own issuance, custody, FX or chain, so margin sits with Circle/Bastion/Coinbase/Solana.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/metric attaches to Samsung's wallet unit, and Samsung Electronics is a conglomerate where this is immaterial — company-level multiples would be meaningless here, so **not computed**. For the ecosystem that captures the economics: Circle (NYSE: CRCL) — the only pure-play with public figures — on $701m Q2 revenue and ~$73.3bn circulation, price-per-dollar-of-circulation and EV/Revenue are derivable only with a live share count/EV, which is not in a free source → EV/Revenue, P/S = **[UNSOURCED]**. PYUSD market cap ($2.8bn, mid-Jul 2026) is a float figure, not a valuation multiple. Internal comps as data points, not trading multiples: [[SAP-backed Tereina launches payments inside SAP software with Circle]] (Circle-as-infrastructure distribution deal), [[Stripe to expand stablecoin cards to 100+ countries]], [[Solana Foundation launches Solana DvP settlement program]]. Distribution not computed — qualitative comparison only; the deal is a distribution/partnership, not a priced transaction.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Announced ≠ adopted.** Launch is "last week of October 2026" and the headline number — 82M eligible Galaxy devices — is addressable reach, not users. Nothing is disclosed on activation, transfer volume, or who earns the FX/conversion take. PYUSD is the precedent: broad distribution (400M+ PayPal users) yet PYUSD float still shrank ~33% from peak in four months. Reach does not equal flow.
2. **Samsung captures no economics; it rents the entire stack.** Issuance (Circle), licensed custody + MTL + remittance payout (Bastion), USDC sub-custody (Coinbase Prime), chain (Solana/Sui). Samsung is a distribution front-end whose partners hold the licenses and the take rate — disintermediation risk runs against Samsung, not for it. Second-order: if partners can plug the same stack into any wallet, Samsung's distribution edge is replicable.
3. **Chain concentration on Solana (with reliability tail-risk).** Solana is only ~5% of total stablecoin supply (~$17.3bn ATH Sep 2026) vs Ethereum ~48% and Tron ~31%; real-economy payment flows still skew 60–80% to Tron. Routing a mainstream consumer remittance product onto a thinner chain concentrates exposure to Solana's throughput/outage history and to USDC single-issuer risk (Solana is ~55–57% USDC). Sui is named as secondary but unproven for this flow.
4. **Regulatory gating.** US-only at launch "subject to local regulatory requirements" for expansion; Bastion's OCC national-trust-bank charter is only *conditional* (Sep 2026). Cross-border payout to 60+ countries multiplies the compliance surface the moment it scales beyond the US.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** If it converts, this is the template for stablecoins going mainstream via pre-installed device wallets rather than standalone crypto apps — the OS/device layer becomes a stablecoin distribution channel, pressuring Apple/Google to answer and re-rating distribution-rich names. Falsifiable thesis (analysis): watch for Samsung to disclose *active users or transfer volume* within 1–2 quarters of the late-Oct launch, and for Solana's stablecoin payment share to climb off ~5%. Trigger that breaks the thesis: a quiet, metric-free rollout (à la many wallet-crypto features) + PYUSD-style float stagnation = distribution without adoption. (analysis)
