# Context-enrichment: EU Council agrees to send only largest crypto firms to ESMA
_Analytical notes (not a post). Importance: 3/5._

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
On **2026-10-09** the Council of the EU agreed the main elements of its **negotiating position** (general approach) on the Commission's crypto-supervision reform, bundled into the broader Savings-and-Investments-Union (SIU) package (Consilium press release 2026-10-09). The headline: ESMA would get **direct supervision only of the "most significant" cross-border CASPs**, not all of them — a **two-tier model** where ~everyone else stays under national regulators.

De-PR'd reality and why the framing matters:
- This is a **Council stance, not law**. It is the Council's input into the ordinary legislative procedure. The Council must formally adopt the position; the **European Parliament must still adopt its own position**; only then do **trilogues** begin. So "agrees to send" overstates finality → nothing changes for firms yet, and MiCA requirements are unchanged (analysis).
- The Council **deliberately narrowed** the Commission's original July-2025 SIU proposal, which wanted **all** MiCA CASPs under ESMA. EU finance ministers rejected centralisation-of-everything and carved out a "significant" subset. Commissioner Maria Luís Albuquerque said the Commission **"deeply regrets the proposed compromise"** — i.e. the Council watered it down. The headline "EU agrees to centralise" obscures that this is a **retreat from centralisation**, not a leap toward it.
- **Threshold ambiguity (key red-team point):** The Council's confirmed text does **NOT** fix the exact "significance" thresholds (Tokenpost, Consilium). Figures circulating in trade press — e.g. **≥15M active EU users, or meeting ~3 of 5 criteria (>1M active users, >€1bn crypto AUC, >€4bn annual client transactions)** and an estimate of only **~10–15 of ~360 CASPs** qualifying (Ledger Insights, Tokenpost) — are **reported/estimated, not locked into the Council framework**. Treat the numbers as directional, the thresholds as the live battleground for trilogue.
- Supporting machinery: a **two-year transition** during which **joint ESMA + national-authority teams** transfer supervisory responsibility (ESMA retains final supervisory decisions), plus a push for a **full-time ESMA executive board** (chair + 5 independent members). This is why the model is "built this way": national regulators keep most firms and staff the transition — a political compromise to buy national buy-in (analysis).

## [1] Competitors / peers — the regulatory-architecture landscape
Not a company story; the "competitors" are supervisory models and the political camps:
- **Commission (max-centralisation)** vs **Council/finance ministers (minimal-centralisation, top ~10–15 only)**. The Council won the first round; the Parliament's position is the next variable.
- **National-regulator hawks:** France's **AMF**, Italy's **Consob**, Austria's **FMA** pushed hardest for ESMA direct supervision of the biggest CASPs, arguing early MiCA application exposed **regulatory arbitrage** via passporting. AMF's chair called blocking passported firms France's **"atomic weapon."** Paradoxically, these national regulators are *pro*-centralisation for the big fish — because the alternative (light-touch member states passporting big exchanges EU-wide) threatens their markets.
- **US analogue:** the SEC-style single federal supervisor is the implicit model ("EU wants ESMA to oversee crypto like the SEC"). The Council's two-tier outcome is closer to a **hybrid** than a true SEC — second-order: fragmentation risk persists for the ~345 firms left national (analysis).
- Position of this item vs the field: it is the **supervision-architecture** decision that sits one layer above the conduct actions ([[ESMA gives crypto exchanges three months to drop unauthorized stablecoins]], [[ESMA questions Binance over reverse-solicitation exemption]]) — those are ESMA *using* current coordination powers; this is about *who holds the powers* going forward.

## [2] Fit / trajectory
This is the predictable next step in a year-long arc visible in the corpus:
- 2025-10: [[EU eyes crypto oversight under ESMA to unify supervision]] (ECB-backed trial balloon) and [[EU plans to move crypto and exchange oversight to ESMA]] (crypto + exchanges + clearing to ESMA). The Commission's maximalist proposal.
- 2026 enforcement wave under national + ESMA coordination: France's AMF June-30 licence deadline, Binance's Greece/EU access fight, Tether/USDT delistings. These exposed the **arbitrage problem** that motivates centralisation.
- 2026-10-09: Council agrees the *scaled-back* architecture. Logical fit: political reality (member states guarding supervisory turf + fee income) forced the carve-out. Structural pressure: the SIU's goal is deeper capital markets, but 27 supervisors = friction → pressure to centralise, countered by sovereignty concerns → compromise on "significant only" (analysis).

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new vs the 2025 notes:** yes — the 2025 items were Commission *plans/trial balloons*; this is the **Council's formal negotiating stance** with a defined *shape* (two-tier, transition, exec board). It is a concrete legislative milestone, not a re-report. So **fresh**, not a duplicate.
- **But traction is low in practice:** zero firms move yet; thresholds undefined; Parliament + trilogue unresolved; ~2-yr transition even after adoption. Value-add is **signalling** — direction of travel (big cross-border CASPs → ESMA) is now the Council consensus, narrowing the range of outcomes. The real prize — exact thresholds — is still open, and that is where lobbying by large exchanges (who may *prefer* a single ESMA supervisor over 27 national ones, i.e. one-stop passporting) will concentrate (hypothesis).
- Who captures value: large cross-border CASPs get regulatory certainty + a single supervisor (a plus for scale players); small national firms keep their home regulator. Second-order: centralising only the top ~10–15 could **entrench incumbents** (ESMA relationship as a moat) while leaving the long tail fragmented.

## [4] What's next / sentiment
- **Process:** Council formally adopts → Parliament adopts its position → trilogue → final text. Thresholds and scope will be fought in trilogue; Parliament could push back toward or away from centralisation. Timeline: months to >1 year, then a 2-year transition.
- **Sentiment:** Commission disappointed (wanted more); national hawks (AMF/Consob/FMA) partially satisfied; industry reaction mixed — big players may welcome one supervisor, smaller players wary of cost. Watch France's "atomic weapon" rhetoric: if trilogue dilutes further, unilateral national blocking of passported firms becomes a live threat → the counterintuitive second-order risk is that **failing to centralise enough re-fragments the single market via national ring-fencing** (analysis).
- **Risk to the item's weight:** it is a stance, not a rule; easily superseded by Parliament's position. Importance is moderate, not high.

## FRESHNESS / DUPLICATE VERDICT
**FRESH.** This is the Council's **supervision-architecture** stance (who supervises whom). It is a DIFFERENT event from [[ESMA gives crypto exchanges three months to drop unauthorized stablecoins]] (2026-10-12), which is ESMA using *existing* powers to force conduct (drop non-MiCA stablecoins by 2027-01-08) — a stablecoin-compliance enforcement action, not a change to the supervisory map. No prior corpus note covers *this* Council general-approach decision; the 2025 notes ([[EU eyes crypto oversight under ESMA to unify supervision]], [[EU plans to move crypto and exchange oversight to ESMA]]) are the *Commission's* earlier maximalist plan, which this Council stance explicitly narrows. Not a re-run. **No duplicate_of.**

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** On 2026-10-09 the EU Council (Ecofin) agreed its negotiating position on the Market Integration and Supervision Package — the supervisory centrepiece of the Savings and Investments Union — handing ESMA *direct* supervision only of the largest cross-border crypto-asset service providers (CASPs), not all of them as the Commission's Dec-2025 text proposed (per Council press release 2026-10-09; eunews.it, 2026-10-09). This caps a regulatory arc visible in-base since late 2025: the Commission floated central ESMA oversight (see [[EU eyes crypto oversight under ESMA to unify supervision]], [[EU plans to move crypto and exchange oversight to ESMA]]), the ECB "fully supported" it in Apr-2026, and the Council has now scaled it back to a two-tier model. Structure of the CASP market: fragmented and still consolidating under MiCA — ~360–386 CASPs authorised across 27 EEA states as of Sep–Oct 2026 (ESMA register via outrun.at / casptracker.eu, 2026-09/10), ~60% crypto-native vs ~40% TradFi, Germany leading (~96), then France (~36) and Netherlands (~29). Barriers are regulatory (MiCA authorisation) + the passport: one national licence sells into all 27 markets, which both lowers entry cost and created the supervisory arbitrage this reform targets. Why now: MiCA has been fully applicable to CASPs only since end-2024, so the supervisory model is being retrofitted after ~18 months of uneven national application (ESMA's Jul-2025 peer review found Malta only "partially met expectations" on a CASP authorisation).

**Competitive landscape.** This is a regulation item, not a company deal — no single protagonist. Sector KPIs that now carry regulatory weight: active EU users, crypto assets under custody, annual client transaction value (these are the significance tests). The affected cohort is the largest pan-EU exchanges/custodians that forum-shopped their MiCA licence: Coinbase (via Luxembourg — see [[Coinbase secures MiCA licence to serve EU crypto customers]]), OKX and Gemini (via Malta), Kraken (Ireland + Cypriot MiFID), Binance (seeking Greece — [[Binance applies for MiCA license in Greece]], [[Binance faces loss of EU access over Greece MiCA licence]]), plus recent entrants Ripple (Luxembourg), Nuvei, StoneX, Crossmint. Basis of competition: distribution/passport reach and licensing speed, not price. Position: the "significant" names move to a single Paris supervisor (levelling the national-regulator leniency some exploited); the long tail of ~350 smaller CASPs stays with national competent authorities. Moat shift (analysis): a firm's regulatory-jurisdiction choice stops being a durable advantage once ESMA supervises the top tier directly.

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/market-cap in the item — this is a supervisory-perimeter change, so trading multiples are "no data" / not applicable. The relevant quantitative anchor is the *perimeter sizing*, shown as arithmetic: significance tests = ≥15m active EU users, OR meeting 3 of 5 criteria (incl. >1m active users, >€1bn crypto assets under custody, >€4bn annual client transactions) (Council position via thecurrencyanalytics.com / stobox.io, 2026-10). Officials estimate only ~10–15 of ~360 CASPs qualify = roughly 3–4% of the register by count (`10/360 ≈ 2.8%` to `15/360 ≈ 4.2%`); the rest (~96%+ by count, though far less by volume) remain nationally supervised. Internal comps for the licensing backdrop: [[Crossmint secures MiCA authorization to expand across EU]], [[Nuvei secures EU CASP licence under MiCA]], [[StoneX adds crypto offering under MiCA license]], [[Ripple receives preliminary MiCA CASP licence in Luxembourg]], [[MiCA transitional window for crypto service providers closes 1 July]].

**Risk flags.**
- **Dilution / enforcement gap (regulation).** Council narrowed the Commission's "all CASPs" to only ~10–15 giants, and the ECB/Commission publicly criticised the curb (eunews.it, 2026-10-09); the ~96%-by-count tail stays with national authorities — the exact forum-shopping and uneven-application problem ESMA flagged (Malta peer review) is only partially closed, so the arbitrage second-order effect persists for mid-size firms.
- **Compliance-cost step-up for the top tier (concentration).** The 10–15 significant CASPs face dual/relocated supervision, ESMA fees and a possible resourcing lag (ECB warned ESMA needs far more staff/funding) — second-order: higher fixed regulatory cost favours the largest incumbents and could push the next tier to stay just under the thresholds.
- **Legislative execution risk (process).** This is only the Council's *negotiating position*; trilogue with Parliament and Commission is still to come and the thresholds/perimeter can shift, so firms cannot yet plan supervisory structure with certainty — plus a parallel wind-down of non-compliant stablecoins adds moving parts (coinalertnews, 2026-10-12).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Direction of travel is unmistakably toward EU-level supervision of systemic crypto venues, but the Council just signalled that national capitals (Luxembourg/Ireland/Malta) retain real veto power — so the likely 2027 outcome is a compromise two-tier regime, not full centralisation (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: the final trilogue text keeps the "significant CASP" perimeter at a small double-digit number rather than reverting to the Commission's all-CASP scope. Trigger/what breaks it: Parliament siding with the Commission/ECB to broaden the perimeter back out, or the significance thresholds being lowered enough to pull in several dozen more firms. Watch: trilogue timeline and whether the €1bn custody / €4bn transaction thresholds move.
