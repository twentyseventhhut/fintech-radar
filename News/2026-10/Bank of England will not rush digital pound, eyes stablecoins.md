## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] TL;DR.** On 2026-10-12, at the IMF annual meetings (Bangkok), BoE Deputy Governor Sarah Breeden said there is "no rush" to launch a retail digital pound ("Britcoin"), arguing that commercial **stablecoins** and **tokenised bank deposits** may deliver the same tokenisation/innovation benefits. The Bank and HM Treasury are still due to decide whether to proceed with a retail CBDC **before the end of 2026**. The channel item's claim that the BoE "expects three sterling stablecoins, each with £40B in demand" conflates the **£40B per-issuer issuance cap** from the June 2026 draft rules with a demand forecast — see verdict below.

**[1] Why it matters.** This is a directional shift for one of the G7's most-watched CBDC programmes: the UK is tilting toward a **private-sector-led** digital-money stack (regulated sterling stablecoins + tokenised deposits + renewed RTGS) rather than a state retail CBDC. It raises the bar for a "Britcoin" business case, reframes the BoE as the "banker to the stablecoin," and signals that disintermediation/commercial-bank-protection concerns are driving design.

**[2] Context / background (dated timeline).**
- 2025-11: BoE publishes an update on the digital pound; emphasises a retail CBDC "must not undermine commercial banks."
- 2026-04: Renewed RTGS (RT2) goes live, built with capability for near-24×7 operation and to support tokenised settlement.
- 2026-06-22: BoE publishes policy statement + draft Code of Practice for **systemic sterling stablecoins** — scraps individual/firm holding limits, sets a **£40B per-issuer issuance cap** (applies only to "systemic" coins); backing ≈30% unremunerated deposits at the Bank + up to 70% short-dated (<6m) UK gilts; consultation open to 2026-09-22; final Code by year-end; live operation possibly from 2027.
- 2026-05-18: BoE consults on **extending RTGS/CHAPS hours** toward near-24×7 (CHAPS open 01:30 from Sept 2027; weekend/bank-holiday settlement not before 2029).
- 2026-10-12: Breeden's "no rush" remarks (this story). Retail CBDC go/no-go decision still expected before end-2026.

**[3] Internal connections (prior notes).**
- [[Bank of England sets five commitments on tokenised markets]] — same institution's private-tokenisation posture.
- [[UK names six banks for first digital gilt issuance]] — the gilt-tokenisation leg of the same UK digital-money agenda.
- [[FCA opens applications for UK cryptoasset regime]] — parallel FCA conduct track to the BoE's prudential/systemic track.
- [[BNY launches tokenized deposits]] and [[Lloyds completes UK's first tokenized-deposit gilt purchase]] — the "tokenised deposits" alternative Breeden points to.
- [[ECB advances to next phase of digital euro project]] — contrast: the ECB is pressing ahead with a retail CBDC while the BoE hesitates.

**[4] Open questions / what to watch.**
- Will HM Treasury + BoE issue a formal go/no-go on the retail digital pound before end-2026, or defer again?
- Does "no rush" effectively kill retail Britcoin, or just sequence it behind stablecoins/tokenised deposits?
- Will the £40B systemic cap actually attract issuers, or (per critics) be "too cautious, three years late"?
- How does near-24×7 RTGS interplay with stablecoin/tokenised-deposit settlement in practice?

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Retail digital-money policy is splitting three ways — retail CBDC, regulated private stablecoins, and tokenized bank deposits — and the BoE's "no rush" stance (Deputy Governor Sarah Breeden, IMF Bangkok meetings, Oct 2026) is an explicit bet that the latter two can fill the gap the digital pound was meant to plug (per Bloomberg / Cryptopolitan, 2026-10-12). Structure: the UK has moved first on a *systemic stablecoin* regime — in June 2026 the BoE dropped the earlier £10k–£20k individual holding caps and replaced them with a temporary ~£40bn issuance guardrail per systemic coin, with final Code of Practice targeted for end-2026 and issuers operating under it in 2027 (The Block / Cointelegraph, 2026-06-22). Backing rules remain strict (earlier draft: ≥40% unremunerated reserves at the BoE, ≤60% short-term gilts). Why now: (1) competitive pressure to keep sterling payments from migrating to USD-pegged coins (dollarization-by-stablecoin fear); (2) the design/decision window on the digital pound closes end-2026, forcing a public positioning; (3) RTGS modernization gives the BoE a public-rails alternative. Contrast with the ECB, whose digital euro is a *public-first* strategy — trilogue legislation targeted end-2026, pilot H2-2027, possible first issuance 2029, holding limits (~€500–€3,000) still under negotiation (ECB; KuCoin, 2026). So the UK is leaning private-sector-led while the EU builds a central-bank base layer — an ideological split on who issues retail digital money.

**Competitive landscape.** The "players" here are jurisdictions/central banks, and the KPIs are adoption-readiness (timeline), holding/issuance limits, and whether private issuers get a regulated on-ramp. UK (BoE): private-led, stablecoin regime first, CBDC deferred — the BoE "expects three sterling stablecoins each with ~£40bn demand" (this is the Bank's own forward estimate, not realized demand — treat as guidance `(analysis)`). EU (ECB): public-led digital euro on a 2027-pilot / 2029-issuance track, legislation not yet adopted. Others moving on central-bank money on-chain: ECB's three models for central bank money on-chain [[ECB outlines three models for central bank money on-chain]], Singapore's MAS settling tokenized bills with wholesale CBDC [[MAS plans to settle tokenised MAS Bills with wholesale CBDC]], and live retail CBDC rollout in Russia (220k digital-ruble accounts in month one) [[Russians open 220,000 digital ruble accounts in first month]]. BoE position: *ahead on the regulated-stablecoin track, deliberately behind on retail CBDC* — a hedge, not a lead. Moat: regulatory authority + the RTGS/CHAPS rails it controls (switching cost for any UK payment system is the central-bank settlement layer).

**Comps & multiples.** No valuation/round/deal in this item — it is central-bank policy, not a company. Trading multiples **not applicable / no data**. Internal comps (policy/precedent, not financial): [[Bank of England sets five commitments on tokenised markets]] (same institution, tokenization stance), [[ECB outlines three models for central bank money on-chain]] (the public-first contrast), [[MAS plans to settle tokenised MAS Bills with wholesale CBDC]] and [[Russians open 220,000 digital ruble accounts in first month]] (wholesale-CBDC and live-retail-CBDC comparators), [[UK names six banks for first digital gilt issuance]] (UK tokenization adjacency). Distribution not computed — qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **De-PR / overstated settlement claim.** The channel text says the RTGS upgrade "will enable instant settlement 24/7." BoE's own plan is only *near* 24×7 and phased: early-morning CHAPS from Sept 2027, weekend/bank-holiday settlement "not before 2029," final shape (22×7 vs 23.5×7) still consulted (BoE CP, 2026-05-18). "24/7 now" is not supported — this is guidance for ~end-of-decade.
2. **Private-issuer concentration / dollarization.** Betting on ~3 sterling stablecoins with a per-coin £40bn guardrail concentrates systemic payment risk in a handful of private balance sheets; if sterling coins don't launch at scale, UK payments could still drift to USD-pegged incumbents — the exact outcome the policy is meant to prevent.
3. **Optionality cost / strategic lag.** Deferring the digital pound while the ECB builds a public base layer leaves the UK dependent on private execution; if stablecoins + tokenized deposits underdeliver, restarting a CBDC is multi-year — a one-way-door timing risk.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** `(analysis)` This is the clearest signal yet of a jurisdictional fork: UK = regulated-private-money model, EU = public-CBDC model — watch whether sterling stablecoin issuers actually file under the end-2026 Code (the falsifiable trigger). Thesis: if ≥2 systemic sterling stablecoins reach material circulation under the 2027 regime, the digital pound is effectively shelved; the thesis breaks if the BoE restarts a retail-CBDC decision in 2027, signaling private rails fell short.
