# Nu Mexico introduces Score Nu credit score feature

Mexico City, October 19, 2026. Credit users generally understand the importance of paying on time, but they rarely know the other factors that affect their credit history or how to strengthen it. To close this knowledge gap, Nu Mexico introduces Score Nu, a feature within its app designed to show—monthly and simply—the evolution of its users' financial habits, their variations, and suggestions to boost them.
The lack of knowledge on this subject is measurable. According to a survey conducted among the institution's customers in October, only 30% understand which habits impact their evaluation, while 36% have never checked their credit history issued by Credit Reporting Agencies (SIC). However, the main obstacle is not fear, but a lack of guidance: among those who have never checked their status, 34% do not know how to do so, and barely 1% avoid it out of fear.
“The data shows that while there is a lack of clear information, there is great interest in adopting habits that strengthen financial futures. In fact, 74% of our surveyed customers consider it useful to receive a monthly summary of their activity. With this launch, we aim to help every user understand what happens behind their credit history through a monthly snapshot that offers practical recommendations to move forward,” stated Diego Sierra, Director of Product at Nu Mexico.
How does Score Nu work?
Each month, the tool analyzes the user's real financial activity and assigns a score ranging from 0 to 1,000, accompanied by a level from Very Low to Very High. To calculate this result, the feature integrates internal and external information sources. On one hand, it analyzes length of account history, usage frequency, and payment punctuality for the Nu Credit Card, as well as expenses and savings in the Nu Debit Account; on the other hand, it incorporates data issued by Credit Reporting Agencies, which is updated every six months.
Score Nu displays the customer's progress compared to the previous period and allows users to break down each habit to identify which factors caused variations. Additionally, the app generates personalized suggestions, such as scheduling automatic credit card payments so users do not have to memorize due dates or risk forgetting them.
Access is automatic and requires no prior registration. To check Score Nu, simply follow these steps:
Open the home screen of the app.
Select the profile icon.
Choose the Score Nu option.
With this integration, the company reinforces its commitment to reducing complexity in financial services and providing money management tools to its more than 16 million customers.

## [0] Что произошло

On October 19, 2026, **Nu México** launched **Score Nu**, a free in-app feature that gives customers a monthly credit-health score from **0 to 1,000** (with a "Very Low" to "Very High" band), plus a breakdown of the habits driving it and personalized tips to improve. It is available to customers who hold a Nu Debit Account and Nu Credit Card with at least three months of active use; access is automatic with no registration (home screen → profile icon → Score Nu). The score blends **internal signals** (Nu Credit Card account-history length, usage frequency, payment punctuality; spending/savings in the Nu Debit Account) with **external bureau data** from Mexico's SIC (Sociedades de Información Crediticia), refreshed every six months.

Nu framed the launch around a customer survey (October 2026): only **30%** know which habits affect their evaluation, **36%** have never checked their bureau history, and among non-checkers **34%** don't know how and only **1%** avoid it out of fear — i.e. the gap is guidance, not fear. **74%** said a monthly activity summary would be useful. Diego Sierra, Director of Product at Nu México, is quoted. Nu cites **>16 million** Mexican customers.

## [1] Почему это важно

This is a **retention/engagement and underwriting-funnel play dressed as financial education**, shipped at the exact moment Nu México needs it most. Nu became Mexico's **largest digital bank** when its full banking license went live (operations started **Aug 6, 2026**), moving deposits from the SOFIPO ~US$12.6k guarantee to the bank-level IPAB guarantee (~US$201k) and unlocking payroll, deposits and a bigger lending book. Nu is already Mexico's **third-largest credit-card issuer** and reaches ~16.5% of the adult population, with Mexican ARPAC (~$12.3) monetizing faster than Brazil did at the same stage. A proprietary score (a) keeps users opening the app monthly, (b) nudges the exact behaviors — on-time payment, card usage, saving — that lower Nu's own default risk and feed its AI underwriting (see [[Nubank's AI underwriting expands credit access; renames Palmeiras stadium]]), and (c) advances the "financial inclusion / demystify credit" narrative that underpins Nu's regional expansion (see [[Nu Colombia launches credit products for thin-file customers]], [[Nu Mexico surpasses 13 million customers]]).

## [2] Контекст и связи

- **Direct product precedent:** Nubank launched **NuScore in Brazil on April 8, 2025** (in testing since Sept 2024), same 0–1,000 scale, same five bands, same "internal behavior + bureau data" blend. Score Nu is the **Mexico port of NuScore**, localized to SIC data. → <https://nu.com/en/newsroom/company/nubank-launches-nuscore-in-brazil>
- **Mexico banking-license backdrop:** CNBV operations authorization notified July 9, 2026; full-bank operations from Aug 6, 2026. → <https://nu.com/en/newsroom/company/nu-mexico-receives-authorization-to-begin-operations-as-a-bank> · <https://finovate.com/nu-receives-full-banking-license-in-mexico/>
- **Mexico credit-bureau landscape:** two SICs — Buró de Crédito (Mi Score, 356–848, revamped Oct 2025) and Círculo de Crédito (FICO, 300–850), both CNBV-regulated and obligated to give one free annual report; Círculo de Crédito's app already surfaces a score on its home screen. Nu's 0–1,000 scale is its **own index**, not a bureau score. → <https://www.digitt.com/blog/que-es-un-buen-score-crediticio>
- **Related Nu moves:** teen cards, NuCel, AI underwriting ([[Nubank's AI underwriting expands credit access; renames Palmeiras stadium]]), thin-file credit in Colombia ([[Nu Colombia launches credit products for thin-file customers]]).

## [3] Открытые вопросы / чего не хватает

- No disclosed adoption/eligibility numbers: how many of the 16M customers actually qualify (debit + credit card + 3 months)?
- The Nu 0–1,000 score is **proprietary and opaque** — unclear how it maps to (or diverges from) the SIC bureau scores lenders actually use; risk of a "score that only matters at Nu."
- Bureau data refreshed only every **6 months** — the "monthly" score is mostly Nu-internal behavior between refreshes.
- No independent coverage beyond PR syndication (TyN, Mobile Time, Banca21, Roastbrief largely echo the Nu release).
- Unclear whether Score Nu feeds a formal consumer-facing credit-building product (e.g., secured-card style) as in Colombia.

## [4] Если один вывод

Score Nu is Nu's Brazil-proven **NuScore** rolled into Mexico right as it becomes the country's largest digital bank — a low-cost engagement + underwriting flywheel wrapped in financial-education messaging, not a new credit product.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Mexico digital-banking / consumer-credit, structurally under-penetrated: per the source survey, 36% of Nu's own customers have never checked their SIC bureau history and only 30% know which habits move it (nu.com, 2026-10-19) — a greenfield for thin-file/first-time credit. Mexico runs two CNBV-regulated SICs — Buró de Crédito (1996) and Círculo de Crédito (2005), which skew to regional SOFOMes/microfinance outside major cities (per Círculo de Crédito / creditolab, as of 2026). The neobank layer is highly **consolidated**: ~3 players control >82% of market volume, with Nubank, Klar and Stori the leading challengers (per legalparadox/Miranda Intelligence, 2026). "Why now": Nu's full banking license went live Aug 6, 2026 (per note [1]/[2]) — a score feature that drives monthly app opens and nudges on-time payment feeds the newly-expanded lending book precisely when deposit guarantees and underwriting scale matter most. Value in this chain accrues to whoever owns the primary banking relationship + proprietary behavioral data; barriers are capital (banking license) and distribution/trust, not technology.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: active customers, ARPAC, loan-to-deposit ratio, deposit base, credit-card penetration. Players & basis of competition (distribution + trust + product breadth, not price):
- **Nu México** — ~15.8M customers Q2'26, 16M by July 2026 (Nu Q2'26 IR); ~3rd-largest card issuer, ~16.5% of adults (note [1]). Score Nu is a localized port of Brazil's NuScore (launched 2025-04-08, note [2]).
- **Klar** — 4.7M users; $800M valuation after $190M Series C (Jul 2025, [[Klar raises $190M Series C led by General Atlantic]]); 7 consecutive profitable months Sep'25–Mar'26 (per GL Insight / dexteragent, 2026).
- **Stori** — ~3.5M card holders; first full-year profitable challenger; eyeing IPO by 2027 ([[Mexico fintech Stori eyes IPO by 2027]]).
- **Mercado Pago** — MELI Mexico fintech revenue +75.7% YoY to ~$781M in Q1'26 (per Miranda Intelligence, 2026); directly attacking Nubank ([[Mercado Pago targets Nubank in new advertisement]]).
Protagonist's position: **ahead on scale** (largest digital bank by customers), catching up only on profitability vs Klar/Stori. Moat: proprietary behavioral + bureau data feeding AI underwriting + switching costs from primary-account ownership (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** Public comp — **Nu Holdings (NU)**: Q2'26 gross revenue ~$5.9bn, +39% YoY; record net income >$1bn; total deposits $45.3bn (+18% YoY), of which Mexico $5.7bn; group ARPAC ~$17; Mexico loan-to-deposit only 35% (Nu Q2'26 IR, SEC 6-K). The under-levered 35% L/D ratio quantifies the headroom Score Nu is meant to help fill. Private peer — **Klar** last round $800M post-money on 4.7M users = **~$170 price-per-user** (round valuation, not market cap; $800M / 4.7M). Nu Holdings trades on growth, not earnings multiples; EV/Revenue and P/E omitted (requires live market-cap pull) → [UNSOURCED]. Internal comps: [[Nu Mexico surpasses 13 million customers]], [[Nu Mexico to invest $2.5bn in national market push]], [[Nu Mexico investments to reach $4.2 billion by 2030]], [[Nubank's AI underwriting expands credit access; renames Palmeiras stadium]]. Distribution not computed (one public + one private, different stages) — qualitative only.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Proprietary-index credibility / conflict of interest.** Score Nu is Nu's own 0–1,000 index, NOT a bureau score, blending internal behavior with SIC data refreshed only every 6 months. A lender scoring users on data that also drives its own card/loan funnel invites a steering/fairness question and can mislead users who assume it equals their official Buró/Círculo standing — regulatory (CNBV/CONDUSEF) and reputational exposure if the index diverges from bureau reality.
2. **Engagement feature ≠ profitability.** Nu México is still margin-thin while Klar and Stori are already profitable; Score Nu lifts engagement/underwriting quality but does not by itself close the profitability gap, and the $2.5bn–$4.2bn investment runway keeps Mexico a cost center near-term.
3. **Competitive/disintermediation.** Círculo de Crédito already surfaces a native score in-app, and Mercado Pago (fintech rev +75.7% YoY) is converting faster on revenue — a free score feature is easily copied and does not, alone, defend the primary-relationship moat.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Not a re-rating event; it is a **retention + underwriting-data play** that signals the Mexico competition is shifting from land-grab (customer count) to depth-of-relationship and credit monetization now that Nu holds a full bank license (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Score Nu works, watch Nu México's loan-to-deposit ratio climb off 35% and Mexican ARPAC converge toward Brazil over the next 2–4 quarters (Nu IR disclosures the trigger); thesis breaks if Mexico deposits/ARPAC stall or Klar/Stori hold their profitability edge while Nu's lending book stays sub-scale.
