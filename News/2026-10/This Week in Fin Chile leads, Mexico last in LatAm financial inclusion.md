## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
### [0] What happened
This Week in Fintech's one-liner — "Chile leads Latin America's financial inclusion ranking, and Mexico comes last" — points to the **2026 edition of Credicorp's Índice de Inclusión Financiera (IIF)**, published mid-October 2026 by Banco de Ideas, Credicorp's research center (based on survey work with Ipsos). It is the index's sixth annual edition. The study covers eight countries — Argentina, Bolivia, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Mexico, Panama and Peru — and scores each 0–100 across three dimensions: **Access, Use, and Perceived Quality**. (Note: the single URL attached to this note is a Cointelegraph/TradingView piece on stablecoin remittance costs and is unrelated to the ranking — treat it as a mis-attached link, not the source.)

### [1] The 2026 numbers
Full 2026 country ranking (points / 100):
- **Chile 59.2 (1st)**
- Argentina 56.9
- Panama 54.3
- Ecuador 51.5
- Colombia 50.1
- Peru 47.7
- Bolivia 47.5
- **Mexico 46.7 (8th / last)**

Regionally, the aggregate hit **50.1 points, up from 48.5 in 2025** — the first time LatAm crosses the 50-point line since measurement began. Chile's lead is driven by **perceived quality (64.1, +~7 pts YoY)**, while its access (55.9) and use (57.6) are roughly flat. Across the region, **perceived quality (≈60.9) is the strongest dimension and Use remains the weakest/most critical**.

### [2] What's driving the move
The headline driver is **digital wallets** ("billeteras digitales"). Wallet ownership has roughly **quadrupled since 2021 to ~43% of the regional population**, and intensive use for payments/services rose from ~7% to ~22%. Wallets are now framed as the on-ramp to the formal system (e.g., Peru: 69% have a mobile wallet, 51% of users pay daily, yet 40% still use no formal financial product — illustrating the access-vs-use gap). Colombia posted the largest YoY advance.

### [3] Why Mexico is last / why it matters
Despite being LatAm's second-largest economy and a crowded neobank market (Nu, [[Mexico's Plata raises $250M at $3.1B valuation|Plata]], Klar, etc.), Mexico ranks **last at 46.7**, below smaller economies — reflecting persistently low *use* of formal products rather than a lack of fintech supply. This is the inclusion-depth problem fintech keeps chasing: account/wallet ownership rises faster than active, everyday formal use. Related DB signals on the Mexico gap and thin-file inclusion plays: [[Credit-scoring metric boosts financial inclusion in Mexico]], [[Nu Colombia launches credit products for thin-file customers]], [[RedotPay adds crypto-to-MXN payouts via Circle in Mexico]]. Caveats: the IIF is a **perception-heavy survey index** (quality is self-reported) and is published by Credicorp, a Peru-based financial group — not a neutral multilateral — so scores are directional, not an official regulator statistic. It is also not directly comparable to World Bank Findex (different method/universe).

## Челлендж / ред-тим

### Red-team questions
1. **Source attribution:** The note's only linked URL is a Cointelegraph stablecoin-remittances article — NOT the Credicorp IIF. Did the aggregator cross-wire the source? Any downstream use must cite Credicorp/Banco de Ideas directly, not this URL.
2. **Which index exactly?** Multiple "financial inclusion" rankings exist (World Bank Findex, GSMA, etc.). Confirmed this is **Credicorp's IIF 2026 (6th edition)** — do not conflate with Findex; scores and methods differ and are not comparable.
3. **Perception vs. hard data:** One of three dimensions is *perceived quality* (self-reported survey), the region's highest-scoring axis. Does Chile "lead" on real access/use or mostly on sentiment? Its lead is largely quality-driven (64.1) while access/use are flat — worth flagging as a soft signal.
4. **Sponsor bias:** Published by Credicorp, a Peru-based commercial financial group, not a neutral multilateral. Treat as directional/industry research.
5. **Mexico-last nuance:** 46.7 vs Bolivia 47.5 / Peru 47.7 is a narrow spread at the bottom — "last" is within a tight cluster, not a dramatic outlier. Avoid overstating the gap.
6. **Scale/exact scores:** Verified all eight scores from two independent Spanish-language outlets (Infobae, Revista Mercado, Río Times). Chile 59.2, Mexico 46.7, region 50.1 (up from 48.5). If any figure is reused, keep to these.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Theme = LatAm financial inclusion / digital-banking penetration, not a single company. Per Credicorp's 2026 Financial Inclusion Index (8 countries, access/use/quality dimensions, household surveys), the region crossed 50/100 for the first time; ranking: Chile 59.2, Argentina 56.9, Panama 54.3, Ecuador 51.5, Colombia 50.1, Peru 47.7, Bolivia 47.5, Mexico 46.7 (last) — Chile +2.5pts vs 2025 (per Credicorp, via revistamercado.do / swissinfo, 2026-10-20). Primary driver: digital-wallet adoption jumped from 11% (2021) to 49% (2026) regionally (per Credicorp, via ticosland/PPRO). Structure: inclusion is bimodal — high access/ownership but shallow *use*; cash still dominates payments. Why now: wallet rails (Pix-analogues, Mercado Pago) plus neobank credit onboarding are converting nominal accounts into active usage. (analysis) The index measures *people*, not valuations — so the "sector" here is the addressable underbanked base that neobanks monetize.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: account-ownership %, active-usage %, digital-wallet penetration, customers/ARPAC for the neobanks capturing it. Players: Nubank (Brazil 112M+ customers ≈ >60% of adults early-2026; Mexico ~15M ≈ ~15% of adults, became a licensed commercial bank 2026-08-06), Mercado Pago (72M users, $11B credit wallet per [[Mercado Pago hits 72 million users, $11B credit wallet]]), Plata ([[Plata reaches $1B annualized revenue in Mexico]]), Ualá, plus incumbents. Basis of competition: distribution + zero-fee onboarding + credit to the thin-file (secured cards). Recent moves: McKinsey est. LatAm neobank customer base +45% in 2025, concentrated in Brazil/Mexico (per gfmag/Euromoney). Protagonist's position: the ranking is a *landscape readout*, not a company — Chile leads on depth-of-use; Mexico trails despite ~80% product ownership (ENIF 2024) because 66% of payments stay in cash and ~60% of adults lack digital financial services (per facephi/crowdfundinsider, 2026). The "moat" being contested is the active-usage layer, not account sign-ups. (analysis)

**Comps & multiples.** No deal/valuation in the source — it's a research-report headline, so trading multiples = no data for the theme itself. Monetization proxies from the names capturing inclusion: Plata hit $1B annualized revenue in Mexico ([[Plata reaches $1B annualized revenue in Mexico]]); earlier round [[Mexico's Plata raises $250M at $3.1B valuation]] → ~3.1x on $1B annualized revenue run-rate (`$3.1bn / $1.0bn = 3.1x` P/S on the last private round vs current run-rate — mixes a round valuation with a later revenue figure, so directional only, not a clean comp). Internal comps: [[Mercado Pago hits 72 million users, $11B credit wallet]], [[Nubank applies for US national bank charter with OCC]]. **Risk flags.**
1. Ownership ≠ usage: a 50+ index headline can overstate real inclusion — Mexico shows ~80% product ownership yet 66% of payments in cash, so revenue the TAM implies may not convert (over-reading the index = mis-sized opportunity).
2. Mexico-specific drag for anyone banking on it: cash dominance, trust deficit (~37% cite distrust), stalled CoDi/DiMo rails, gender gap — structural, slow to unwind; neobank growth there is swimming against the national average.
3. Measurement/methodology dependence: Credicorp is a self-interested publisher (owns BCP/Yape wallet) and the index is survey-based — treat the ranking as a signal, not ground truth (publisher-as-participant conflict).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) The re-rating story is *usage over access*: the winners are whoever converts dormant accounts into daily wallet/credit activity (Nubank, Mercado Pago in Brazil; still open in Mexico). Falsifiable thesis: Mexico's inclusion score rises materially next cycle only if CoDi revitalization + Nu's new bank license drive active usage — watch 2027 Credicorp index + Nu Mexico active-customer/ARPAC disclosure; if Mexico stays last despite Nu's banking license, the bottleneck is demand (cash preference/trust), not supply, and the LatAm-ex-Brazil neobank TAM is smaller than bulls assume.
