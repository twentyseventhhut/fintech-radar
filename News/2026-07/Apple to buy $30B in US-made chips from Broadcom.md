## Контекст

<!-- enrichment:context -->
**[0] What happened.** On 2026-07-08 Apple announced a multiyear agreement worth **more than $30 billion** with **Broadcom (AVGO)** to design and produce chips in the United States, covering **more than 15 billion U.S.-made chips through at least 2031**. The FT flash ("Apple to buy $30B of US-made chips from Broadcom") reflects this deal. It is the **largest single commitment Apple has made under its American Manufacturing Program (AMP)**. Broadcom filed updated long-term agreements with the SEC on 2026-07-06 confirming the multi-year custom-chip contracts through 2031. ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/07/apple-to-increase-spend-with-broadcom-to-produce-billions-more-us-chips/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/08/apple-commits-30-billion-to-broadcom-for-us-chipmaking-push.html), [MacRumors 6-K](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/07/06/apple-and-broadcom-extend-deal/))

**[1] What chips / terms.** The scope is **wireless connectivity + RF silicon**, not logic/SoC: custom **ASIC silicon**, **FBAR radio-frequency filters**, and next-gen wireless connectivity (Wi-Fi/Bluetooth/cellular RF) for future Apple devices. Production hub is **Broadcom's Fort Collins, Colorado facility**, which gets a **$1.5B upgrade/modernization capex**. Term runs through at least 2031. Deal value ">$30B" is a purchase/design commitment, not an equity outlay. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/08/apple-to-produce-made-in-america-wireless-chips-with-broadcom/), [Manufacturing Dive](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/apple-30b-produce-more-us-semiconductors-broadcom-amp/824735/))

**[2] Program context.** Sits inside Apple's **American Manufacturing Program**, launched Aug-2025 when Apple raised its total US commitment to **$600B over four years** (a pledge aligned with the Trump administration's onshoring push). AMP launch partners include Broadcom, TSMC, Samsung, Texas Instruments, GlobalFoundries, Amkor, Corning, Applied Materials, Coherent, GlobalWafers. Complements Apple's separate **TSMC Arizona (Phoenix)** ramp — Apple on track to buy **>100M advanced chips from TSMC Arizona** in 2026; TSMC's US investment plan totals ~$165B. ([Apple Newsroom Aug-2025](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/08/apple-increases-us-commitment-to-600-billion-usd-announces-ambitious-program/), [AZ Tech Council](https://www.aztechcouncil.org/apple-chips-tsmc-fab/))

**[3] Relationship history.** Apple has been a top Broadcom customer since ~2010 (wireless connectivity hardware). The two signed a **2023 multibillion-dollar agreement** for Broadcom to develop/make **5G RF components in the US** — this 2026 deal extends and scales that relationship. ([Alpha Spread](https://www.alphaspread.com/market-news/corporate-moves/apple-signs-30-billion-broadcom-deal-to-make-us-made-wireless-chips), [Yahoo/Barron's](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/broadcom-lands-30-billion-chip-173002657.html))

1. **Is $30B new money or a rebadge of existing spend?** Partly framing. Apple has bought Broadcom wireless/RF for years and signed a 2023 US-RF deal; ">$30B through 2031" bundles ongoing procurement. Answered: it is a *commitment ceiling*, not incremental capex by Apple.
2. **Is this a fab / does Apple make chips?** No. Apple designs; Broadcom manufactures at Fort Collins. Apple's outlay is a purchase/design commitment, not equity or a fab build. Answered.
3. **What chips exactly?** Wireless connectivity + RF: custom ASICs, FBAR filters, Wi-Fi/BT/cellular RF — NOT the A-series/M-series logic SoC (that's TSMC). Answered.
4. **Where is production?** Fort Collins, CO ($1.5B upgrade). Answered.
5. **Duration / cadence?** Through at least 2031; annual spend unspecified — ">$30B" is cumulative, so ~$4–5B/yr if linear. Open: no year-by-year breakdown disclosed.
6. **Who put in the $1.5B?** Broadcom (its capex), not Apple. Answered.
7. **Political framing risk.** Deal is explicitly tied to Apple's $600B AMP pledge to the Trump administration; announcement is partly a policy/optics event. Treat headline number skeptically as a political-commitment figure. Answered.
8. **Does it reduce Apple's China/Taiwan exposure?** Marginally — only wireless/RF, and only relative to prior Broadcom sourcing; leading-edge logic still TSMC (Arizona + Taiwan). Answered.
9. **Material to AVGO revenue?** Positive but spread to 2031; Apple already a top-3 customer. Framed as win-win (Barron's/Yahoo). Open: incremental vs baseline AVGO Apple revenue not quantified.

12. **Yield / FBAR US-manufacturing risk?** FBAR filters are US-mature for Broadcom (Fort Collins is an existing plant), so execution risk is lower than a greenfield fab. Open: capacity ramp timeline vs Apple product cycles undisclosed.
13. **Does it change iPhone BOM cost / prices?** No direct signal; unrelated to the memory-cost-driven price hikes in [[AI memory-chip demand pushes Apple to raise iPhone prices]]. Answered.
14. **FT number vs sources.** FT says "$30B"; Apple/CNBC/Bloomberg say "more than $30B / could exceed." Consistent, FT rounded down. Answered.

**Importance: 4/5** — Largest-ever AMP commitment by Apple ($30B+, 15B chips, to 2031), SEC-confirmed, marquee names (AAPL/AVGO), and a clean read-through on US semiconductor onshoring and Broadcom's design-services franchise. Capped below 5 because it's an extension of an existing relationship, wireless/RF-only (not leading-edge logic), the dollar figure is a multi-year cumulative political-pledge number, and near-term financial impact on either company is modest and spread out.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Semiconductor supply chain / US onshoring. Apple committed >$30bn over a multiyear term (through 2031) to buy Broadcom chips made in the US, targeting >15bn US-made chips; Broadcom adds a $1.5bn expansion of its Fort Collins, Colorado fab for RF components (FBAR filters, wireless connectivity) — Apple's largest single US manufacturing commitment to date, part of its ~$600bn four-year US pledge and its American Manufacturing Program (per Apple newsroom / CNBC / Bloomberg, 2026-07-08). Structure: the leading-edge logic/foundry layer is highly consolidated (TSMC dominant; Apple's A/M-series SoCs are TSMC-fabbed), while RF front-end / connectivity is an oligopoly (Broadcom, Qorvo, Skyworks). "Why now": US policy pressure (CHIPS-era onshoring + tariff/administration leverage) is pushing device OEMs to re-domicile parts of the supply chain — this deal is a policy-driven capex reallocation, not a demand/technology shift. Second-order: onshoring RF filters raises unit cost vs Asian supply, a cost Apple's ~49% gross margin can absorb but smaller OEMs cannot.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: fab capex, capacity utilization, AI/custom-silicon (ASIC) revenue growth, customer concentration. Key players: TSMC (leading-edge logic foundry, Apple's SoC partner), Broadcom (RF front-end + custom AI ASICs), Qorvo / Skyworks (RF peers), plus custom-ASIC rivals emerging around hyperscalers. Basis of competition: process leadership, IP (FBAR/BAW filter patents), and design-win lock-in. Recent moves: OpenAI + Broadcom unveiled a custom inference chip ("Jalapeno") in 2026-06 (see internal comps) — Broadcom's ASIC franchise is its growth engine (AI revenue +106% FY2025 per FourWeekMBA/10-K). Apple's positioning: a buyer onshoring supply, not a chip vendor; it keeps designing in-house silicon while outsourcing fabrication. Broadcom's position: entrenched — Apple has for years relied on Broadcom RF filters, "the one chip Apple can't easily copy" (analysis; Broadcom's BAW/FBAR IP is the moat, switching costs high). Moat (analysis): Broadcom = IP + design-win lock-in; Apple = scale + vertical integration.

**Comps & multiples.** Protagonists: **Apple (AAPL)** — Q2 FY2026 (qtr ended 2026-03-28) revenue $111.2bn (+17% YoY), Services record $31.0bn, gross margin 49.3%, net income $29.6bn (per Apple 10-Q; SEC EDGAR). **Broadcom (AVGO)** — FY2025 revenue $63.9bn (Semiconductor Solutions $36.9bn, Infrastructure Software $27.0bn); AI revenue +106% YoY (per FourWeekMBA reading of 10-K). Customer concentration: Broadcom's top-5 end customers ≈40% of FY2025 net revenue (10-Q); Apple is estimated ≈20% of Broadcom annual revenue (analyst/press estimate, not a named 10-K line — treat as `(analysis)`). Trading multiples: forward P/E, EV/Rev, EV/EBITDA for AAPL/AVGO — **`[UNSOURCED]`** (no free market-cap/consensus snapshot pulled; not computed to avoid a stale multiple). Internal comps: [[OpenAI unveils first custom inference chip Jalapeno with Broadcom]], [[OpenAI and Broadcom unveil AI inference chip]] (Broadcom custom-silicon design wins), [[Apple seeks memory chips from sanctioned supplier, FT reports]] (Apple supply-chain fragility), [[ByteDance develops custom CPUs to power AI infrastructure]] (in-house silicon trend), [[Qualcomm in talks to buy AI software startup Modular]] (semi M&A). Distribution not computed — qualitative comparison only.

**Risk flags.**
- **Customer concentration (Broadcom side).** Apple ≈20% of Broadcom revenue; deepening the relationship raises Broadcom's dependence on a single buyer whose own component roadmap (Apple has repeatedly sought to in-source modems/RF) is the tail risk — second-order: if Apple ever internalizes RF filters, Broadcom loses a fifth of revenue.
- **Cost / margin.** US-fabbed RF filters carry a structural cost premium vs Asian supply; the deal is partly policy-motivated capex, so ROIC on the $1.5bn Fort Collins expansion depends on committed Apple volumes holding through 2031 — execution/utilization risk if iPhone units soften.
- **Policy dependence.** The commitment sits inside a politically-negotiated $600bn US pledge; a change in administration priorities or tariff regime could strand onshoring economics (announced ≠ locked-in demand).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector: reinforces the RF front-end oligopoly and validates Broadcom's non-AI franchise as a durable annuity alongside its AI-ASIC growth `(analysis)`. Watch: whether other OEMs (Samsung, hyperscalers) follow with onshoring pledges — a consolidation/re-rating catalyst for US-fab-exposed suppliers. Falsifiable thesis: this is capex-reallocation, not new demand — if Broadcom's FY2026 RF/wireless segment revenue does NOT step up despite the "$30bn" headline, the deal was optics over incremental economics; a Broadcom guide that folds this into existing wireless run-rate would confirm that.

> Context only — this is a supply-chain/onshoring story (Apple–Broadcom $30bn US-chip deal, 2026-07-08), not an earnings report. The block below attaches Apple's latest reported quarter (Q2 FY2026, ended 2026-03-28; reported 2026-04-30) for the chip-cost / supply-chain angle.

**Verdict (headline read).** BEAT · Q2 FY2026 revenue $111.2bn (+16.6% YoY; vs public consensus ~$109.7bn, +$1.5bn / +1.4%) · diluted EPS $2.01 (+22% YoY; vs consensus ~$1.94–1.95, +~3.6%) · driven by iPhone 17 cycle (+22%) and record Services · gross margin 49.3%. Eighth straight EPS beat. But management guided Q3 gross margin DOWN to 46.5–47.5% on ~150–200bp of memory-chip cost headwind — the direct read-through to the "buy US-made chips" story.

**Key figures (with growth).**
- Revenue $111.2bn, +16.6% YoY — a March-quarter record.
- Gross margin 49.3% (above the high end of prior guidance; +~110bp QoQ). Products margin lifted by iPhone mix; Services remains the structural margin anchor.
- Diluted EPS $2.01, +22% YoY.
- Net income $29.6bn.
- Dividend raised 4% to $0.27; ~$100bn buyback re-authorized.

**By segment / driver.**
- iPhone $56.99bn, +21.7% YoY — March-quarter record, the top-line driver (iPhone 17 family).
- Services $30.98bn, +~16% YoY — all-time record; highest-margin segment, the margin ballast.
- Mac $8.4bn · iPad $6.91bn · Wearables/Home/Accessories $7.9bn.
- CFO Kevan Parekh flagged that iPhone and Mac shipments were constrained by memory shortages — a supply/cost signal, not a demand miss.

**vs expectations / prior period.** Beat on both lines: revenue +$1.5bn (+1.4%) and EPS +~$0.06 (+3.6%) vs public consensus (StockAnalysis/Yahoo/Zacks aggregation, as of the 2026-04-30 print). Mac, iPad, Wearables and Services each came in ahead of Street; the one soft spot was iPhone vs its own (high) consensus bar per one aggregator, even as iPhone set a record and beat overall — a "beat the total, high bar on the hero product" pattern. Momentum accelerated vs the prior quarter and comfortably beat the year-ago period.

**Guidance / forward.** Q3 FY2026: revenue growth 14–17% (reaffirmed strong top line), but gross margin guided to 46.5–47.5% — a step DOWN from the 49.3% just posted, explicitly attributed to ~150–200bp of memory-cost headwind, partly offset by Services mix. Management framed rising input/component costs (memory, plus tariff drag cited at "billions per quarter") as the near-term pressure. Tone: confident on demand and Services, candid on cost/supply headwinds.

**Thesis-flags (tie to onshoring / supply cost).**
1. Margin is now a COST story, not a demand story. Fact: GM guided down 150–200bp on memory costs → why: input inflation + component scarcity is the marginal swing factor → why it matters: at ~49% GM, every ~100bp is ~$1bn+/quarter → 2nd-order: this is the economic motive for the $30bn Broadcom US-chip deal and the broader American Manufacturing Program (Houston Mac mini, TSMC Arizona 100M+ chips) — onshoring is being pursued to de-risk supply and blunt tariff/cost drag, not for margin accretion in the near term (US-made silicon is likely cost-neutral-to-higher initially).
2. Tariff exposure is already in the P&L. Management has quantified tariff drag in the billions per quarter; the Broadcom/AMP commitments are the mitigation lever — flag: watch whether FY2027 guidance shows the onshoring spend converting into a smaller cost headwind or just shifting it.
3. iPhone concentration risk cuts both ways. iPhone +22% carried the beat, but iPhone is also the product most dependent on advanced processors — the exact chips the Broadcom deal targets. Supply diversification away from single-source Taiwan (TSMC) directly de-risks the segment that drives >50% of revenue.
4. Services is the offset. Record $31bn Services (+16%) at structurally higher margin is what lets Apple absorb hardware cost inflation while still guiding double-digit growth — the buffer that makes an expensive US supply-chain pivot affordable.
