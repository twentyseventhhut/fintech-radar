Waze adds new AI-powered features and customization updates
Waze, the Google-owned navigation app, is getting new AI-powered features and customization updates , including the ability to report road updates conversationally and get personalized navigation. 
Some of the new features are powered by Google’s Gemini AI assistant, which reflects the tech giant’s broader push to integrate Gemini across its products while also better positioning Waze to compete with rival services such as Apple Maps.
The app now suggests routes based on both a user’s trip history and its understanding of a city’s traffic patterns. For example, if a user prefers driving on highways over local streets, they will see those suggested first. If users don’t want personalized routes, they can choose alternate routes or turn off personalization altogether in their settings. Personalized navigation is rolling out now globally on Android and iOS.
Users can now also use Gemini to find their destination through a quick chat when they know what they need but aren’t sure exactly where to go. Users can tap the search voice icon to ask questions like “Find me a coffee shop that’s open right now,” “Find me parking close to Grand Mall,” or “Find me a gas station nearby with the lowest prices.” Waze will then respond with a list of options.
The ability to find your destination with Gemini capabilities is rolling out now to the Waze beta community globally on Android and iOS.
Additionally, Waze is introducing a new Motorcycle mode that uses AI to account for two-wheeler-specific shortcuts and road restrictions to help riders find the best route and receive more accurate ETAs. It also shows hazards that could be tricky for riders, like potholes, speed bumps, raised crosswalks, shoulder endings, and narrow bridges. 
Motorcycle mode is rolling out now in Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, Malaysia, Mexico, Peru, and the Philippines on Android and iOS, with more countries on the way, Waze says.
While Waze already lets users report traffic incidents, such as slowdowns, using natural speech, it now also allows them to suggest map updates the same way, including reporting road closures or outdated addresses.
For example, you could say “The road is closed here,” and Waze will send these details to local map editors. The option to report road updates conversationally is rolling out now globally on Android and iOS.
There’s also a new “less chatty” mode that features fewer interruptions on your drive when you want to focus on your music or a podcast. When you toggle on the setting, Waze minimizes the number of voice prompts and keeps them short. You’ll still be alerted to hazards and turns, but you’ll get them less frequently. Less chatty mode is rolling out now globally on Android and iOS.

**[0] What happened.** On 2026-07-13 Google announced a batch of AI and customization updates for **Waze**, its crowdsourced navigation app, several of them powered by **Gemini**. The rollout (from the official Waze/Google blog and TechCrunch) bundles: (a) **conversational map reporting** — users can suggest map updates in natural speech, e.g. "The road is closed here," forwarded to local map editors; (b) **Gemini destination search** — voice queries like "Find me a coffee shop that's open right now" or "gas station nearby with the lowest prices," returning a shortlist (beta-community only); (c) **personalized navigation** — routes ranked by trip history + a city's traffic patterns, with an opt-out; (d) **Motorcycle mode** — AI-tuned two-wheeler routing/ETAs and rider-specific hazards; and (e) a **"less chatty" mode** that reduces voice prompts. Most features roll out globally now on Android/iOS; Motorcycle mode is limited to Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, Malaysia, Mexico, Peru, Philippines; Gemini destination search is beta-gated. ## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Consumer navigation/mapping is a mature, winner-take-most category dominated by Alphabet: Google Maps has surpassed ~2bn users in 2026, with Waze a second, community-driven property (~150mn MAU across ~185 countries per secondary trackers, via expandedramblings/worldpopulationreview — not company-confirmed, treat as estimate) folded into Google's "Geo" division alongside Maps and Earth since 2022. Apple Maps is the main external rival on iOS. Structure: heavily consolidated, with deep moats — real-time traffic/hazard data network effects, map/POI corpus, and OS-level distribution (Android default, CarPlay/Android Auto). "Why now": the driver is not navigation demand but Gemini distribution — Google is pushing its Gemini assistant across its whole app portfolio (Search, Maps, Finance, hardware, and now Waze) to raise per-app engagement and defend query volume as generative AI reshapes discovery. This Waze update (conversational reporting, personalized routing, Gemini destination search, motorcycle mode, "less chatty" mode) is a feature refresh, not a market event.

**Competitive landscape.** The category runs on engagement/retention (DAU/MAU, session time), data density (real-time reports, map freshness), and — for monetization — ad load and local-commerce intent. Basis of competition: data/network effects and OS distribution, not price (both apps are free). Recent Google moves, all 2026: Google Maps added Gemini "Ask Maps" + Immersive Navigation (Mar 2026, per MacRumors); Gemini extended to Google Maps on CarPlay (May 2026); this Waze update (Jul 13 2026). Notably, Apple rebuilt Siri on Google's Gemini models (WWDC, Jun 2026, per androidheadlines/qz) — so the framing of Waze "competing with Apple Maps" is partly hollow: Google supplies the AI brain to its supposed rival's assistant. Protagonist position: Waze is a niche-but-sticky complement to Maps (secondary sources cite ~269 min/month in-app time vs ~12 min for Apple Maps — unverified, `[UNSOURCED]` at company level), not a standalone competitive threat. Moat: network effects on crowdsourced hazard/traffic data + Google's distribution — `(analysis)`.

**Risk flags.**
1. Announced vs adopted — most features are "rolling out now" or beta-only (Gemini destination search is beta-community; motorcycle mode limited to 7 LATAM/SEA markets); engagement uplift is unproven, so this is optionality, not realized value. Why: PR cadence outruns live adoption.
2. Cannibalization/overlap inside Geo — Waze and Maps now carry near-identical Gemini features; incremental value of a separate Waze app narrows as Maps absorbs the same AI. Why: two apps, one roadmap → duplicated spend, blurred differentiation.
3. AI-search disruption to the ad engine — the same generative shift Google is riding in-app can compress query-driven local-ad monetization if answers replace listings; relevant because ads are ~86% of Google Services revenue (IR). Why: second-order margin risk to the parent's core.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** For the sector: incremental, not a re-rating — it deepens Alphabet's already-dominant Geo moat and normalizes "assistant-in-every-app" as table stakes, raising the AI bar for Apple Maps. Falsifiable thesis `(analysis)`: if Google starts disclosing Waze/Maps Gemini engagement or new local-commerce ad formats (e.g., Gemini destination search monetized like the Affirm/Klarna AI-shopping tie-ins), the value is real; absent that, treat these updates as retention hygiene. Trigger to watch: any Alphabet earnings mention of Maps/Waze AI monetization, or Apple shipping a genuinely competitive (non-Gemini) Maps assistant.
