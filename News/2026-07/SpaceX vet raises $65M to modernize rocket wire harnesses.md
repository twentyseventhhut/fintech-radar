A SpaceX vet raised $65M to pull wire harnesses out of the Cold War era
When Senra CEO Jordan Black was a SpaceX engineer, he took on the job of scaling up the company’s wire harnesses to support production of Starship, the company’s next-generation rocket.
Wire harnesses are what they sound like: the internal electrical cabling that runs through a rocket ship, car, plane, or tractor and becomes increasingly important the smarter those vehicles get. They’re bespoke, put together by technicians who are, functionally, experienced craftspeople.
“I traveled all over the world to go visit wire harness companies,” Black told TechCrunch last month. “It really hasn’t changed since the Cold War era of wooden tables [and] manual processes.”
Black and co-founder Benjamin Shanahan started Senra in 2023 to offer a more modern solution to vehicle manufacturers. Today, the startup is announcing a $65 million Series B round, co-led by Lowercarbon and Interlagos with participation from General Catalyst, Sequoia Capital, Andreessen Horowitz, and Founders Fund, among others.
Senra isn’t looking to take humans out of the handmaking process — at least not while robots find manipulating wires a challenge and relevant training data remains scarce. Instead, it’s turning to software tools and other forms of automation to modernize aspects of the traditional manual work.
The company is benefiting from the surge of money into U.S. manufacturing, particularly the defense industrial base. While Black couldn’t disclose customers, he said they include builders of “anything from submarines and maritime vehicles, to defense vehicle systems on land, to launch vehicles, to satellites.”
If it doesn’t sound immediately important, consider a recent wire harness disaster. In 2023, Boeing discovered that its Starliner spacecraft’s wiring was held together with flammable tape, forcing an expensive delay while the entire wiring system was redone.
Black points to that experience as a reason to raise the standards for wire harnessing, using automated systems to track materials and engineering changes. “Having it all in the same software is probably the most important thing, because it’s all the little inputs that happen that can make a catastrophic change down the road,” he said.
Senra uses Amp, a proprietary software platform, to standardize the inputs throughout the wiring process and produce a digital twin to guide its technicians, who are trained by the company in what Black says is the only federally certified wire harness training program. The company is also, as it scales, finding ways to automate more of the process.
“It goes back to the Elon principle of, ‘automation is last,’” Black told TechCrunch. “We’re working on it now, but a lot of it the standardization and the foundation building that made SpaceX be able to scale something like rockets, which you could only build one a year if you were lucky, and now they do hundreds a year.”
Senra — which, by the way, is “harness” spelled backwards, minus the “h” and “s,” because Black says the company takes the “horsesh*t” out of harnesses — produces 1,000 each month across two different factories and plans to increase production to 10,000 a month in 2027.

## [0] What exactly happened (de-PR'd)
- **Fact:** Senra Systems (US, California; "Senra" = "harness" reversed minus h/s) announced a **$65M Series B on 2026-07-15**, co-led by **Lowercarbon Capital** and **Interlagos**, with **General Catalyst, Sequoia, a16z, Founders Fund, 8VC, Dylan Field, CIV, The Friedkin Group, Jaws Estates, Sozo, Alumni Ventures** participating. Total raised now **$112M+** (prior: **$25M Series A**, led by Dylan Field & CIV, announced ~2024). Founded 2023 by ex-SpaceX engineers **Jordan Black (CEO)** and **Benjamin Shanahan**; recently hired **Ken Venner (ex-SpaceX CIO)** as CTPO.
- **What was actually done:** It makes **wire harnesses** — the bespoke internal electrical cabling for rockets, missiles, satellites, submarines, defense vehicles. The "innovation" is **process/software**, not a new physical product: a proprietary platform (**Amp**) that unifies quoting/engineering/supply-chain/production and produces a **digital twin** to guide human technicians. **Explicitly still manual assembly** ("automation is last" — Musk principle); robots can't yet manipulate wires and training data is scarce.
- **→ Why framed this way:** The "$65M / out of the Cold War era" narrative anchors to a **legacy, unglamorous, fragmented craft industry** (wooden tables, manual processes) to make an incremental software-on-top-of-manual-labor story sound transformational. The de-PR'd reality: this is a **contract manufacturer with a software layer and a services model (assembly-as-a-service)**, riding the defense-industrial-base capital wave — not a robotics/automation breakthrough. Amp is real differentiation, but the value is **workflow standardization + traceability**, not lights-out manufacturing.
- **Vagueness flags:** No **valuation** disclosed; no **revenue**; no named customers ("8 Fortune 500" at Series A, unverifiable). Capacity claims (1,000 harnesses/month now → 10,000/month in 2027, third factory) are **plans/projections**, not shipped results.

## [1] Competitors / peers
- **Legacy A&D harness suppliers:** NAI Group, Kauffman Engineering, Opti (PR), Salt Lake Cable & Harness, Phoenix Dynamics (HUBER+SUHNER) — established, AS9100/ITAR-certified, manual, low-volume/high-mix. Senra's wedge = software + speed vs their "months-long manual workflows."
- **Defense/reindustrialization hard-tech peers (the real comp set by stage/thesis):** **Hadrian** (Factories-as-a-Service, machined parts, ~$1.6B valuation), **Anduril** ($5B Series H, ~$61B val), **Layup Parts** (composite manufacturing). Senra is a **component-level onshoring play** vs Hadrian's broader machined-parts FaaS and Anduril's platform/autonomy.
- **Position:** Early-stage, sub-scale (1,000 units/mo), **catching up** on scale vs incumbents but **ahead** on software/traceability. **→ Second-order:** the moat question is whether **Amp** is a durable software moat or a nice-to-have wrapper around what is still a labor-arbitrage/onshoring contract manufacturer. If the latter, margins commoditize toward legacy harness-shop economics.

## [2] Company history / fit
- **Trajectory:** 2023 founded → ~2024 $25M Series A → 2026-07 $65M Series B ($112M total) → 2 CA factories, third being scouted (+~80k sq ft). Hiring ex-SpaceX CIO signals intent to **productize SpaceX's own manufacturing-scaling playbook**.
- **→ Why they act this way:** The founders lived the **Starship harness-scaling** pain at SpaceX; the pitch is "we did this inside SpaceX, now we sell it to the whole industrial base." Structural pressure: harnesses are a **supply-chain bottleneck and single point of catastrophic failure** (cited: Boeing Starliner's flammable-tape wiring rework, 2023) → buyers will pay for traceability/reliability. The **assembly-as-a-service + software** structure is chosen to earn a software/services multiple on top of a commodity craft, not just a manufacturing markup.

## [3] Novelty / value-add / traction
- **Genuinely new:** applying **digital-twin + integrated-workflow software (Amp)** and a **federally-certified training program** to a craft industry that "hasn't changed since the Cold War." That is a real, if incremental, delta.
- **What's NOT new:** the physical output (harnesses) and the fundamentally **manual assembly** — automation is aspirational ("working on it now").
- **Traction:** capacity numbers are **forward projections** (10,000/mo in 2027 is 10x current); no revenue, no named customers, no disclosed valuation. **→ Who captures the margin:** value here is **onshoring + traceability + speed** for defense primes; the durable margin depends on (a) whether Amp locks in customers (switching costs) vs (b) primes multi-sourcing and squeezing a contract manufacturer. Absent lights-out automation, this stays **labor-intensive** → gross margins likely closer to advanced manufacturing than to software. The multiple rests on the **defense-capital narrative**, which can compress fast if the funding wave cools.

## [4] What's next / market sentiment
- **Plans:** third factory, 10x capacity by 2027, more automation over time, deeper Amp platform.
- **Backdrop/sentiment (bullish):** record **defense-tech VC — >$14.6B in 2026 vs $9.6B in 2025**; explicit **US reindustrialization** push (Anduril $5B/$61B; Hadrian/T. Rowe Price $1.6B). Senra is a clean pick-and-shovel bet on that wave.
- **Risks / → counterintuitive second-order:** (1) **Capital concentration into defense hard-tech makes a sub-scale component supplier fragile, not safe** — it must scale 10x against incumbents while the narrative holds. (2) **Execution risk:** manual scaling is hard; the 10x claim is unproven. (3) **Customer concentration** in defense = program/budget cyclicality and long qualification cycles. (4) **Software-moat risk:** if Amp is not sticky, this reverts to commodity harness-shop margins.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Senra sits in the aerospace/defense wire-harness & cable-assembly niche within the broader US industrial-base reshoring wave. Market size: the global aerospace wiring-harness market is put at ~$6.6–7.2bn for 2025–2026 with a ~7% CAGR to ~$13.3bn by 2035 (per multiple secondary market-research vendors, e.g. Spherical Insights / MarketsandMarkets, as of 2026 — note: vendor estimates vary widely, from a narrow ~$1.44bn "aerospace wire harnesses" figure to ~$6.7bn "aerospace wiring harness"; treat as directional, not audited). Structure: highly fragmented and labor-intensive — harnesses are bespoke, hand-built by certified technicians ("craftspeople"), with the process largely unchanged "since the Cold War era of wooden tables and manual processes" (Black, via TechCrunch). Value in the chain sits in certification, engineering-change control and defense-grade traceability, not raw copper. Barriers: ITAR/federal certification, security clearances, program qualification cycles — regulatory more than capital. Why now (analysis): the defense-tech funding surge — global defense/dual-use VC exceeded ~$15bn in 2025 and is pacing toward ~$18bn in 2026 (per Crunchbase/citybiz, 2026) — plus Pentagon industrial-base policy actively courting VCs, is pulling capital into the "picks-and-shovels" supply chain (harnesses, machined parts) that primes like Anduril and traditional OEMs depend on.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: units/month capacity, factory sq-ft, on-time/defect (quality-escape) rate, and certification scope; Senra's disclosed metrics are ~1,000 harnesses/month across two factories today, targeting 10,000/month in 2027, with a new ~80,000 sq-ft plant and a third facility planned (per TechCrunch/Axios, 2026-07-15). Basis of competition: quality/traceability and delivery, not price. Key players: (1) incumbent interconnect giants — Amphenol, TE Connectivity, Carlisle Interconnect, Collins Aerospace, Nexans — who dominate high-reliability mil/aero interconnects; (2) specialist harness/cable shops (Epec, PIC Wire, A.E. Petsche); (3) the new "software-defined manufacturing" cohort Senra is explicitly modeled on — Hadrian (AI-driven aerospace machining, ~$1.6bn valuation Jan-2026 per finsmes/Robot Report; reported-but-denied talks at ~$7.5bn) and Anduril's vertically-integrated supply chain (Anduril itself at ~$61bn, per CNBC 2026-05). Protagonist position (analysis): niche/early — Senra differentiates on its Amp software platform (digital twin + input standardization) and a claimed only-federally-certified harness training program, i.e. a process/data moat rather than a scale moat. It is a supplier-to-primes, not a prime, so it is catching up on volume vs entrenched harness incumbents but ahead on software tooling. Boeing Starliner's 2023 flammable-tape wiring rework is cited as the demand rationale (de-PR: this is Senra's own framing).

**Comps & multiples.** No public revenue disclosed for Senra → no valuation-based multiple computable. Round facts: $65M Series B; total funding now >$112M (per finsmes/citybiz, 2026-07-15); post-money not disclosed → EV/Revenue = no data. Internal corpus comps: none — the fintech news base has no space/defense-manufacturing hardware deals (semsearch 'notes' table unbuilt; grep of `industry/infrastructure` and defense/aerospace terms across News/2026-* returned only fintech/crypto items). External public reference points (directional only — these are $16–23bn-revenue diversified interconnect leaders, NOT scale-comparable to a sub-$10M-revenue harness startup): Amphenol ~$165.4bn market cap on ~$23.1bn TTM revenue (as of 2025-12-31) → P/S ≈ `$165.4bn / $23.1bn ≈ 7.2x` (per Amphenol filings via Yahoo/Nasdaq); TE Connectivity ~$17.3bn FY25 revenue, ~20% operating margin (per Yahoo, 2026). Private-manufacturing comp: Hadrian at ~$1.6bn valuation (Jan-2026). Verdict: no in-line/rich/cheap call possible — comparability fails (scale, disclosure). `[UNSOURCED]`: Senra revenue, margins, post-money, EV.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Capital intensity & execution on a 10x scale-up** — going from ~1,000 to 10,000 harnesses/month in ~18 months plus a third factory is a hardware/CapEx bet; harnesses remain hand-built (Senra explicitly keeps humans in the loop, "automation is last"), so scaling means hiring/training certified technicians, a labor-throughput constraint that a $65M round may not fully fund. Second-order: if demand slips, fixed factory footprint becomes a margin drag.
2. **Customer concentration in the defense industrial base** — customers undisclosed but span "submarines, defense vehicles, launch vehicles, satellites"; revenue is likely concentrated in a few programs/primes. Defense procurement is cyclical and budget/politics-dependent — a program cancellation or a prime insourcing harnesses (as Anduril vertically integrates) directly hits the top line.
3. **Disintermediation / crowded well-funded field** — the "software-defined manufacturing" thesis is capital-magnet but contested; incumbents (Amphenol/TE) have decades of qualification and scale, and primes may build in-house. Senra's moat rests on the Amp platform + certification, which are defensible but unproven at scale (analysis).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Senra is a "supply-chain enabler" play riding the defense-reshoring re-rating — the same thesis that took Hadrian and Anduril up-market applied to the least-automated corner of the stack. Falsifiable thesis: if Senra hits ~10,000 harnesses/month in 2027 with named prime contracts and stable quality-escape rates, it becomes an acquisition target for a prime or an interconnect major. Trigger/catalyst to watch: third-factory groundbreaking, first disclosed anchor customer/program, or a follow-on at a disclosed post-money (which would finally allow a real multiple). What breaks it: a defense-budget pullback, a quality escape on a flight program, or a prime (Anduril-style) insourcing harness production.
