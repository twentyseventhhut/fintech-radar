 Foundational protein design startup Mandrake Bio has raised around Rs 16 crore in a pre-seed funding round co-led by Activate and Antler, with participation from Spectrum Impact and DeVC. The round also saw investment from angel investors, including Vijay Chandru, Paras Chopra, Sanjiv Rangrass, and Vatsal Du. 
 The proceeds will be used to advance the company’s AI based protein design platform, expand its research team across AI and biophysics, and scale wet lab validation of its gene editing enzymes for agricultural and medical applications, Mandrake Bio said in a press release. 
 Founded in March 2025 by Tanay Lohia and Kutubuddin Molla, Mandrake Bio develops programmable gene editing enzymes using generative AI, biophysics, and wet lab validation. The startup aims to design compact and precise enzymes for applications across agriculture and medicine. 
 The company says its technology can help reduce the time required to develop improved crop varieties through gene editing, while also supporting the development of therapies for genetic diseases. 
 Unlike conventional approaches that rely on naturally occurring gene editing systems such as CRISPR Cas9, Mandrake Bio designs custom gene editing enzymes from scratch using generative AI and structural biology. It plans to license these enzymes to seed companies and therapeutic developers.

**[0] What happened (dated).** On 2026-07-18 (StrictlyVC), Mandrake Bio — a ~one-year-old, Bengaluru (India) startup — announced a pre-seed round to develop **programmable, AI-designed gene-editing enzymes**. Reported size: **~₹16 crore**, cited as **$1.7M** by StrictlyVC and as **$1.9M** by Dealroom (FX/rounding discrepancy — treat as "~₹16 Cr / ~$1.7–1.9M"). Round **co-led by Activate and Antler**, with **Spectrum Impact** and **DeVC** participating, plus angels (Vijay Chandru, Paras Chopra, Sanjiv Rangrass, Vatsal Dusad). Geography confirmed: **India (Bengaluru)** — the `region/india` tag is CORRECT.

**[1] Company / product.** Founded **March 2025** by **Tanay Lohia** and **Dr. Kutubuddin Molla**. It is a "foundational protein design" company that designs **custom gene-editing enzymes from scratch** using generative AI + structural biology/biophysics + wet-lab validation — explicitly NOT repurposing naturally-occurring CRISPR-Cas9. Design flow starts from a brief (target cell type, delivery route, precision, compactness) and generates enzyme candidates to fit. Business model: **license enzymes to seed/agriculture companies and therapeutic developers** (dual-use: crops + genetic-disease therapy). Pre-revenue; plans first lab validation of enzymes over ~2 months, which is meant to unlock a larger round. Notable proof point: won the **GEM × Adaptyv RBX1 protein-design competition (ICLR, Rio 2026)** — reportedly the only "Strong"-rated submission among >12,000 designs.

**[2] Why it matters / stakes.** Bets on the "de-novo/AI-first" gene-editing thesis — designing editors to spec rather than mining nature — which promises more compact, precise, deliverable enzymes and freedom-to-operate outside CRISPR-Cas9 IP thickets. Dual agriculture + therapeutics market widens the addressable base but also spreads a tiny pre-seed team thin.

**[3] Internal context (prior notes).** Related AI-bio / deep-tech funding in the vault: [[Lycia Therapeutics raises round for protein-degrading therapeutics]] (US biotech, $75M Series D, protein-degrading therapeutics — a much later-stage AI-adjacent bio play) and [[Neolab Mirendil exits stealth with $200M seed at $1B valuation]] (AI-native deep-tech seed, illustrating how much larger US frontier-tech pre-seed/seed rounds run vs an Indian ₹16 Cr biotech pre-seed). **[4] External landscape / competitors (dated).**
- **Profluent** (Berkeley, founded 2022) — closest analog: AI-designed CRISPR editors; released **OpenCRISPR-1** (Nature, Jul 2025), raised **$106M** (Nov 2025, co-led by Altimeter, Bezos Expeditions; ~$150M+ total). Far better funded and further along.

**Red-team questions**

1. Round size is inconsistent across sources — StrictlyVC says **$1.7M**, Dealroom says **$1.9M**, Indian press says **~₹16 Cr**. Which is authoritative, and does the FX gap hide undisclosed angel tranches?
2. This is a **~$1.7–1.9M pre-seed** — vanishingly small vs Profluent's $150M+. Is Mandrake competitive at all, or effectively an academic project with a company wrapper?
3. **Zero validated product**: they only plan first wet-lab enzyme validation over the next ~2 months. Is there ANY experimental proof the AI-designed enzymes actually edit DNA in vivo?
4. The **ICLR/Adaptyv RBX1 "Strong" design win** is a benchmark/competition result — does it translate to functional, deliverable, safe gene editors, or is it in-silico bragging?
5. **Dual market (agriculture + therapeutics)** from a <20-person pre-seed team: is this focus or fatal spread? Regulatory paths (crop gene-editing vs human therapeutics) are wildly different.
6. **Freedom-to-operate / IP**: de-novo enzymes may dodge CRISPR-Cas9 patents, but can a tiny Indian startup actually defend novel enzyme IP against Profluent/Broad/others?
7. **Delivery problem**: compact enzymes help, but gene-editing's hardest bottleneck is delivery, not the editor. Does Mandrake address delivery at all?
8. **Fintech/tech-investor relevance**: this is an early-stage synthetic-biology / agri-bio company. Why is it in a FINTECH pipeline at all beyond "AI + funding round"? Relevance to the digest's audience is thin.
9. Investors **Activate, Antler, Spectrum Impact, DeVC** — are these deep-tech/bio specialists or generalist accelerators writing small checks? Signal value of the syndicate?
10. **Founder track record**: Tanay Lohia and Dr. Kutubuddin Molla — do they have prior gene-editing/protein-design credentials, or is this first-time founders in a capital-intensive science field?
11. **Time-to-revenue**: licensing enzymes to seed companies/therapeutic developers takes years of validation. What is the realistic revenue horizon, and does ₹16 Cr even fund the next 12–18 months?
12. **Geography risk**: India's wet-lab/biotech infrastructure and gene-editing regulatory clarity vs US/EU — does location constrain execution or offer cost advantage?
13. Is there any **duplicate/overlap** with prior vault notes? (Checked: none — first gene-editing note; [[Lycia Therapeutics raises round for protein-degrading therapeutics]] and [[Neolab Mirendil exits stealth with $200M seed at $1B valuation]] are adjacent but distinct.)
14. **Hype vs substance**: "AI-designed gene editors from scratch" is a crowded 2025–26 narrative (Profluent already did OpenCRISPR-1 in Nature). What is genuinely differentiated here beyond marketing?
15. Single-source ingest (StrictlyVC blurb) — is coverage otherwise thin/promotional (much of the Indian press reads press-release-driven)?

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Mandrake sits at the intersection of AI-designed proteins and gene-editing enzymes ("foundational protein design"), selling designed enzymes to seed companies and therapy developers rather than running its own drug pipeline. Sector size: the CRISPR-based gene-editing market is put at ~$5.5bn in 2026 growing to ~$18.9bn by 2035 (14.8% CAGR) per towardshealthcare, and $8.3bn→$28.8bn by 2035 per BioSpace/one report; gene-editing *therapeutics* revenue is far smaller (~$0.36bn 2026 → $0.65bn 2031, 12.5% CAGR per GlobeNewswire) — i.e. the value today is in tools/enabling IP, not approved drugs. Estimates vary widely by firm; treat as order-of-magnitude, not precise TAM. Structure: two-sided — a research-tool/IP-licensing layer (Mandrake's model) sitting upstream of both pharma and ag; barriers are proprietary data (protein datasets), AI/biophysics talent, and wet-lab validation, not capital-at-scale. "Why now" (India ag angle): India in 2022 exempted SDN-1/SDN-2 genome-edited plants with no foreign DNA from GEAC/GM biosafety review, and in 2025 advanced gene-edited rice lines to multi-location trials with genome-editing budget allocations rising sharply (Rs 406 lakh in 2020-21 → ~Rs 25,035 lakh projected 2025-26, per ISAAA/Nature/govt) — a structural tailwind for a Bengaluru enzyme-licensing startup targeting seed companies (analysis).

**Competitive landscape.** Sector "KPIs" for a pre-revenue tools startup: designed-enzyme performance (compactness/precision vs Cas9), number of licensing partners, and wet-lab validation hit-rate — none disclosed by Mandrake (`[UNSOURCED]`). Basis of competition = platform/data + IP, not price or distribution. Key players by proximity to Mandrake's exact model (AI-designed editors → licensing): **Profluent** (built OpenCRISPR-1, first CRISPR designed from scratch by AI; licenses to pharma/ag incl. Corteva, Revvity); **Mammoth Biosciences** (ultracompact Cas14/Casɸ enzymes, licenses foundational CRISPR IP — e.g. VedaBio deal Sep-2025). Adjacent AI-protein-design tool peers: **Cradle**, **Basecamp Research**. Recent moves: Profluent raised $106m (Nov-2025, Bezos Expeditions/Altimeter), ~$150m total, near-$1bn valuation; Cradle $73m Series B (Nov-2024); Basecamp Research $60m Series B. Mandrake's position: earliest-stage, most capital-light entrant (niche) — same thesis as Profluent (design compact/precise editors from scratch, license them) but ~100x behind on funding and data scale; moat today is founder/talent + intangible IP, unproven (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** No revenue/EBITDA at pre-seed → trading multiples "no data"; the meaningful comparison is round size / stage / capital-to-milestone, not EV/Revenue.
- Mandrake Bio (India, pre-seed, Jul-2026): ~$1.7m / Rs 16 cr, co-led Activate + Antler; post-money not disclosed (`[UNSOURCED]`).
- Profluent (Nov-2025): $106m round, ~$150m total, valuation ~$1bn (round valuation, private). Direct model comp (AI-designed CRISPR → licensing).
- Mammoth Biosciences: ~$303m raised, ~$1.1bn valuation at 2021 Series D (round valuation, private). Compact-enzyme licensing comp.
- Cradle: >$100m total ($73m Series B, Nov-2024) — AI protein-engineering tools comp.
- In-base: only loosely comparable biotech round is [[Lycia Therapeutics raises round for protein-degrading therapeutics]] ($75m Series D, protein-degrader *therapeutics* — different model, not gene-editing tools); no true gene-editing/AI-protein comp exists in the corpus. Read: Mandrake's raise is ~2 orders of magnitude below the AI-protein-design cohort — consistent with a March-2025 founding and a pre-seed, i.e. in-line for stage, not cheap/rich (nothing to re-rate yet).

**Risk flags.**
1. **Capital gap vs commercial-stage peers.** $1.7m against Profluent's ~$150m and the $60-106m rounds the AI-protein cohort now commands — designing, validating and licensing enzymes is data- and wet-lab-intensive; runway to a licensable, validated enzyme likely exceeds a pre-seed, so near-term dilution/further raises are near-certain and execution-dependent (analysis).
2. **IP / freedom-to-operate.** Pitch is "custom enzymes from scratch, not natural Cas9" — partly a design-around of the contested CRISPR-Cas9 patent estate (Broad vs UC Berkeley); novel AI-designed enzymes still face uncertain patentability and possible overlap with incumbents' claims, which directly threatens the licensing revenue model (analysis).
3. **Long, dual-track validation timeline.** Two go-to-markets (ag seed traits and human therapeutics) with very different regulatory clocks — ag is faster in India post SDN-1/2 exemption, but therapeutic editors carry multi-year clinical/regulatory risk; splitting a tiny team across both raises focus risk (analysis).

**What this changes (idea-lens).** Data point that the "AI-designed gene-editing enzyme, license-not-own" model (Profluent/Mammoth) is now reaching emerging markets at seed stage, with India's ag deregulation as the near-term wedge (analysis). Falsifiable thesis: if Mandrake lands a named seed-company or pharma licensing partner within ~18 months, the model travels; watch for (trigger) a first commercial license/co-development deal or a priced seed round. What breaks it: no validated enzyme + no licensing partner before cash runs out → absorbed into the tools cohort or shelved.
