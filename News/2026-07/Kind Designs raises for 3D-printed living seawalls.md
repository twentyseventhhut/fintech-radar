For decades, seawalls have served one purpose: keep the water out. Along Florida’s coastline, where stronger storms, rising seas, and aging infrastructure are forcing cities to rethink how they protect the shore, one Miami startup believes they can do much more.
That vision has attracted another wave of investor confidence.
Miami-based Kind Designs announced this week that it closed a $10 million Pre-Series A funding round after originally seeking just $5 million. Investor demand nearly reached $20 million, prompting the company to double the size of the raise. The round values the company at $70 million and brings its total funding to $21.5 million.
The investor list includes returning backers Mark Cuban and former Washington, D.C., mayor Adrian Fenty, alongside new investor and NBA forward Kyle Kuzma.
The funding comes as Kind Designs moves well beyond its early days serving homeowners. The company generated $1 million in revenue during 2025 but says it has already secured $10 million in contracted revenue and built an active pipeline worth $175 million. That growth has been fueled by projects spanning municipalities, luxury hotels, and the U.S. military, including a recently awarded $2 million contract with the U.S. Navy.
Using large-scale 3D printing and ecological engineering, Kind Designs develops Living Seawalls, artificial reefs, and other shoreline systems designed to protect coastlines while encouraging marine life.
“The seawall industry is a multi-hundred-billion-dollar market that has never had a true technology company,” founder and CEO Anya Freeman shared in the company’s announcement. “We’re building one of America’s most important tech companies to put resiliency on the map as the next great industrial category.”

That vision resonated with Cuban, who has now invested in multiple funding rounds.
“Most climate founders pitch you on saving the planet. Anya pitched me on an opportunity to disrupt a multi-billion-dollar coastal infrastructure market in a way that’s faster, cheaper, and also better for the environment,” Cuban said. 
“Kind Designs continues to cement itself as a leading innovator in coastal resiliency and is a perfect reminder that you can both ‘do well’ and ‘do good,’” he continued. “This is why I wrote another check into the company.”
Kind Designs’ recent projects include installations at Fontainebleau, Pagani, and the Ritz-Carlton in Miami Beach, alongside municipal work in Miami Shores and Longboat Key. The company has also landed projects in New York City, Charleston, and Nassau, while expanding into California.
The startup is also benefiting from policy changes. Miami-Dade County recently updated its seawall codes to provide expedited permitting and environmental mitigation exemptions for Kind Designs’ systems, while Florida has directed the Department of Environmental Protection to develop incentives supporting Living Seawall adoption statewide.
The new funding will help the company expand into new markets, increase production capacity, hire additional engineers, and support larger municipal and federal projects.
“We’re done building at the pace permission allows,” Freeman said. “The technology is proven. The demand is here. Now it’s about scaling a new generation of coastal infrastructure that protects America while making this country more beautiful.”

**[0] What happened (verified).** Miami-based Kind Designs (founded ~2021, CEO Anya Freeman) closed an oversubscribed ~$10M funding round to scale its 3D-printed "Living Seawalls." The round was labelled "Pre-Series A" in most trade coverage (finsmes, VentureBeat/BusinessWire, VoxelMatters); the internal StrictlyVC blurb called it a "Series A." It values the company at **$70M** and brings total funding to **~$21.5M**. The company originally sought $5M; demand reached nearly $20M, prompting a doubling of the raise. New investor: NBA forward Kyle Kuzma; returning backers Mark Cuban and ex-DC mayor Adrian Fenty. (Sources below.)

**[1] Company & technology.** Kind Designs uses large-scale 3D printing plus ecological/artificial-reef engineering to produce Living Seawalls, artificial reefs, and living shorelines that mimic mangrove-root structure — protecting coastlines while creating marine habitat, collecting water-quality data, and sequestering carbon. It claims to be the first to apply artificial-reef science (e.g., ≥3-inch "caves" for marine life) to a structural seawall product.

**[2] Traction / business model.** Municipal, luxury-hospitality, and federal/defense contracts. Reported figures: ~$1M revenue in 2025, ~$10M contracted revenue, and a ~$175M pipeline. Named projects: Fontainebleau, Pagani, Ritz-Carlton Miami Beach; municipal work in Miami Shores and Longboat Key; expansion to NYC, Charleston, Nassau, and California. Recently awarded a ~$2M U.S. Navy contract. Business model = selling/installing engineered coastal-infrastructure units to homeowners, municipalities, hotels, and government.

**[3] Regulatory tailwind.** Miami-Dade County updated seawall codes (July 2025) to give expedited permitting (reportedly ~2 years → ~90 days) and environmental-mitigation exemptions for Living Seawalls; Florida SB 302 (signed by Gov. DeSantis) directs the state DEP to develop statewide adoption incentives. This regulatory capture-of-favor is a genuine moat/tailwind but also a single-jurisdiction concentration risk.

**[4] Competitive landscape.** The eco-engineered / "living" seawall space is not empty: **ECOncrete** (commercial, port/coastal infrastructure, San Francisco & Port of Vigo projects); the Sydney **Living Seawalls** program (SIMS/UNSW/Macquarie + **Reef Design Lab**, 35+ sites/5 continents, 2018 Volvo panel, 2025 Eureka Prize). Kind Designs' differentiation claim = full 3D-printing at structural scale + US coastal/defense go-to-market + regulatory positioning, rather than academic/CSR panel retrofits. 3. "$10M contracted revenue" and "$175M pipeline" are company-supplied and unaudited. What's the conversion history from pipeline to booked revenue? No third-party validation.
4. How much of the round is celebrity/marketing signal (Cuban, Fenty, Kuzma) vs. institutional diligence? Star-studded cap tables can inflate valuations without deep operational conviction.
5. Concentration risk: revenue leans on Florida/Miami-Dade regulatory tailwinds (SB 302, county code changes). What happens outside jurisdictions that mandate/incentivize Living Seawalls?
6. Durability/performance data: are there independent, multi-year structural and ecological performance studies on Kind Designs' printed seawalls, or only vendor claims + academic analogs (Sydney/ECOncrete)?
7. Unit economics of large-scale concrete 3D printing: is it actually "faster, cheaper" than traditional seawalls at scale, or only at small/premium (hotel/homeowner) volumes?

10. Capex intensity: tripling production via robot inventory needs working capital; $10M may be thin for the stated municipal/federal ambitions. Runway?
11. Climate-adaptation policy dependence: federal/state coastal-resilience funding is politically cyclical. Sensitivity to budget shifts?
12. Is any of the "pipeline" LOIs vs. signed contracts? The distinction is frequently blurred in startup PR.

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Sector & drivers.** Kind Designs sits in coastal-protection infrastructure / nature-based (eco-engineering) solutions — a hard-infrastructure market adjacent to marine construction. Sizing (all secondary analyst estimates, verify before quoting): the marine construction market was ~$61.7bn in 2024, projected ~$83.7bn by 2030 at ~5.2% CAGR (per Grand View Research); a narrower "coastal energy defense infrastructure" segment (seawalls/levees/breakwaters) is put at ~$12.5bn in 2025 → ~$22.3bn by 2034 at ~6.7% CAGR (per IntelMarketResearch, as of 2025). The World Bank estimates global coastal-adaptation investment ~$300bn/year by 2030 (via secondary citation). Founder's "multi-hundred-billion-dollar market" claim is PR framing — the sourceable seawall/coastal-defense TAM is low-tens-of-billions, not hundreds; treat the larger figure as unverified. Structure: highly fragmented, project-based, dominated by local marine/civil contractors and precast-concrete suppliers, procured municipality-by-municipality; barriers are permitting, capital intensity and local relationships rather than technology. Why now: (a) climate-adaptation funding crossed into asset-class territory — up ~64% in 2025, within global climate-tech VC of ~$40.5bn (+8% YoY, first rise after two down years) per SVB/industry data; (b) policy tailwind — Miami-Dade updated seawall codes to give Kind expedited permitting + environmental-mitigation exemptions, and Florida directed DEP to build Living Seawall incentives, i.e. the moat here is partly regulatory, not only technical.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: contracted backlog / pipeline coverage, revenue per installed linear foot, gross margin on print-vs-pour, permitting cycle time. Named/adjacent players: Reef Design Lab (Australia; original Sydney Institute of Marine Science "Living Seawalls" design, Earthshot 2021 finalist — retrofit habitat panels, not full structural seawalls), ECOncrete (bio-enhanced concrete for marine structures), Reef Arabia (3D-printed reef units); traditional incumbents are precast-concrete seawall/riprap contractors. Basis of competition: price and installed durability vs ecological/permitting benefit — Kind's differentiation is a full structural 3D-printed seawall (not just a bolt-on panel) plus the regulatory fast-track. Recent Kind moves (2026): $10m round closed (announced Jul 16 2026), $2m U.S. Navy contract, projects at Fontainebleau/Pagani/Ritz-Carlton Miami Beach, municipal work (Miami Shores, Longboat Key), expansion to NYC, Charleston, Nassau, California. Position: niche category-creator/leader in the "structural 3D-printed living seawall" sub-niche (analysis) — ahead on regulatory positioning in Florida, but small in absolute scale (2025 revenue ~$1m). Moat = intangibles (Miami-Dade code approval, brand, celebrity-investor distribution) + early switching/spec-in advantage; durability moat is unproven (see risks). Proprietary unit economics (margin, CAC, install cost/ft) — `[UNSOURCED]`, not disclosed.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Project/contract-based, lumpy revenue with heavy concentration.** ~$1m 2025 revenue against a $70m valuation, dependent on a handful of large municipal/federal/luxury contracts (incl. one $2m Navy deal); loss or slippage of a few projects materially dents growth — second-order: valuation leans on the $175m pipeline converting, which is unweighted and unproven.
2. **Permitting & regulatory dependence.** The economics lean on Miami-Dade's expedited-permitting / mitigation-exemption code and pending Florida DEP incentives; this advantage is geographically concentrated and reversible with a policy/administration change, and does not travel automatically to NYC/California/Bahamas — each new market resets the permitting clock.
3. **Unproven long-run durability & ecological performance at scale.** Traditional seawalls are specified for 50–70 year lives; a 4-year-old company's 3D-printed structural walls have no multi-decade field record. Any early failure, storm-load underperformance, or weaker-than-claimed habitat outcome would undercut both the spec-in moat and the "technology is proven" PR claim — a real liability given municipal/federal buyers. Also note the round is de-PR'd as "Pre-Series A"/"Series A" inconsistently in sources, and celebrity backers (Cuban, Fenty, Kuzma) add distribution but not diligence signal.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) This is a category-creation bet — turning a fragmented, contractor-led seawall market into a productized, spec-driven tech category, with regulation as the wedge. Falsifiable thesis: if Kind converts even ~20–30% of the $175m pipeline into recognized revenue over the next 12–24 months while holding the Florida permitting advantage, it validates "resiliency as an industrial category" and should pull copycats/incumbent-precast responses and follow-on adaptation capital. Watch: pipeline-to-contracted conversion rate, replication of the Miami-Dade code fast-track in a second state, and first durability data from installed walls. Thesis breaks if new-market permitting stalls or an early structural/durability issue surfaces.
