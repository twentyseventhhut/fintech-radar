## [0] TL;DR

Tyred, a London-based mobility-tech startup, raised **£2.5M** (seed) to build a "connected ownership platform for cycling" — bundling maintenance, repairs, insurance, warranty management and finance into one digital experience, underpinned by proprietary IoT sensors embedded in bikes that feed predictive-AI models (wear-and-tear / breakdown prediction). Round led by **Raw Ventures** and **Ada Ventures**, with angels including **Anton Buzdalin** (former CEO of SBER Eapteka; earlier lead of London proptech **Dwelly**). Announced ~2026-07-17. Small early-stage round — **modest importance**.

> [!warning] Note-title discrepancy
> The note title/story summary calls this "Tyred" and the Russian source (Русский венчур) describes it as **bicycle** repair/servicing ("велосипедов") — NOT car tyres/automotive. All English sources confirm: this is a **cycling / micromobility** ownership platform, not a tyre-retail or automotive-fintech play. The "Tyred" brand is a pun; treat prior "tyre marketplace" framing as incorrect.

## [1] What actually happened

- **Amount / stage:** £2.5M, described as seed / early-stage (Startup Magazine, Soapbox, Finsmes).
- **Lead investors:** Raw Ventures and Ada Ventures (both UK VCs). Confirmed by rawventures.com and multiple outlets.
- **Angels:** Anton Buzdalin among a group of angel investors. Note the framing nuance: the Russian channel called Buzdalin the "lead investor," but English/investor sources credit **Raw Ventures + Ada Ventures as leads** and list Buzdalin as an **angel** participant. Treat "Buzdalin leads the round" as likely overstated.
- **Founders:** Lancelot Hoare (Co-Founder & CEO), Daniel Bursztynski (Co-Founder & CTO), Nikita Baranov (Co-Founder & COO).
- **Use of funds:** product development, UK expansion, foundations for European growth; both consumer and commercial-fleet segments.

## [2] Business model & traction

- **Model:** Vertical "ownership OS" for bikes — recurring maintenance/servicing + repairs, plus attached financial products (insurance, warranty, finance). IoT-hardware + data moat feeding predictive maintenance ("predicts your bike will break down before it does").
- **Real traction (differentiator vs. typical seed):** Already runs one of London's largest specialist bike-maintenance operations — serviced **>100,000 bikes** in the past year, and maintains ~**50,000 Lime bikes** across London (maintenance + battery-swapping for the shared-mobility operator Lime). This is an unusually operational, revenue-bearing seed company rather than pre-product.
- **Geography:** London / UK, with stated European ambitions.

## [3] Who is Buzdalin (the Russian-venture angle)

- **Anton Buzdalin** — background at **SBER Eapteka** (Russian online pharmacy; served as CEO), plus A5 Group, Parusinvest, lats.com; HSE grad; now London-based, Co-Founder of ZINIT.
- As an angel he has led/backed early-stage London startups — most notably the pre-seed of proptech **Dwelly** (AI-first lettings & property management). Tyred is his second notable UK angel bet.
- Russian-venture relevance = this is why a Russian channel covered a UK cycling seed round: it tracks the London activity of a prominent ex-Russian-tech operator. (See the concurrent Eapteka ownership churn: [[Wildberries RVB to acquire Russian online pharmacy Eapteka]] / [[RWB buys majority stake in Eapteka pharmacy service]] — Buzdalin's former asset is itself changing hands.)

## Market Research

<!-- enrichment:market_research -->
**Correction to the note text.** The aggregated Russian digest is misleading. Tyred is a **bicycle** ownership platform (the name is a "tyre/tired" pun — NOT automotive/tyre). Buzdalin ("Eapteka founder") was an **angel co-investor**, not the lead; the round was led by Raw Ventures and Ada Ventures. The company is run by CEO Lancelot Hoare, CTO Daniel Bursztynski, COO Nikita Baranov; founded 2022, started as a mobile bike-repair service, now building a "connected ownership platform" bundling maintenance, repairs, insurance, warranty and finance, plus IoT sensors on bikes feeding predictive-AI failure models. It claims ~100,000 bikes serviced in the past year and maintains ~50,000 Lime bikes across London (per company/press, via TFN & FinSMES, 2026-07).

**Sector & drivers.** This sits at the intersection of UK cycle repair/aftercare + embedded micromobility insurance/finance — a small, fragmented vertical.
- Size: UK cycle-repair market ~£100m; bike parts & accessories ~£750m; total bicycle retail revenue ~£1.6bn but declining at ~-3.2% CAGR through 2025-26 (per IBISWorld/Bicycle Association, via web, 2025). So the new-bike market is shrinking while the aftercare pocket Tyred targets is the resilient slice.
- Growth driver ("why now"): with the post-COVID bike boom faded, riders keep existing bikes longer — services volume/value rose ~+7%/+5% and repair grew while sales fell (Bicycle Association, 2025). >40% of current/potential cyclists say they'd take a subscription bundling bike+insurance+tracking+servicing (Mintel, 2025) — the exact wrapper Tyred sells. (analysis) Fleet contracts (Lime) are the real near-term revenue: recurring B2B maintenance, less exposed to fickle consumer demand.
- Structure: highly fragmented — thousands of independent local repair shops, no dominant aftercare brand; barriers are low on repair (labour) but higher on the IoT+data+insurance stack Tyred is trying to build.

**Competitive landscape.** Sector KPIs: bikes-under-management / fleet count, service revenue per bike, attach rate of insurance/finance, and (for the insurance layer) loss ratio. None disclosed by Tyred beyond ~100k bikes serviced and ~50k Lime bikes `[UNSOURCED]` on economics.
- **Embedded insurance:** [[London-based Laka raises]] — Laka (London) is the clearest peer on the embedded-finance angle: embedded micromobility insurance via retailers/manufacturers (Decathlon, Brompton, Gazelle), "collective" premium model, raised to ~£14.1m Series B incl. a £6.5m HSBC debt facility and Achmea investment (2025). Laka owns the insurance layer Tyred wants to bundle — potential partner OR competitor.
- **Hardware/DTC e-bike:** Cowboy (€5m, 2025), VanMoof (collapsed 2023) — cautionary tales on capital-intensive bike hardware; Tyred is asset-light services by contrast (analysis).
- **Fleet ops:** Lime (Tyred's customer, ~50k bikes) is going public and runs its own ops — customer-concentration risk for Tyred if Lime in-sources.
- **Basis of competition:** distribution (fleet + retailer deals) and data/IoT, not price. Tyred's position: niche/early, differentiated on predictive-maintenance IoT + one-stop bundle; moat is unproven — early network/data effects at best (analysis).

**Comps & multiples.** No public multiples computable — Tyred is a pre-scale private seed-stage company and discloses no revenue/GMV. Round: £2.5m (seed; type = round, not valuation — post-money not disclosed → "no data"). Internal comps from the base: [[Anda raises $3.4 million seed for mobility fintech platform]] (2025-11, $3.4m seed, mobility-fintech, embedded-finance) — same stage/size band, validating the "mobility × embedded finance" seed thesis. External comp Laka is 1–2 rounds ahead (~£14m raised) and insurance-only. Distribution not computed (fewer than 3 comparable disclosed figures); qualitative: Tyred's ticket is in-line with early mobility-fintech seeds.

**Risk flags.**
1. **Customer concentration on Lime.** ~50k of the bikes under management are one client (Lime). If Lime in-sources maintenance or picks another vendor, a large revenue slice disappears — and Lime, going public, has scale/incentive to internalise (second-order: valuation built on a churnable B2B anchor).
2. **Shrinking underlying market + fragmentation.** UK bike retail is contracting (~-3.2% CAGR) and repair is a low-margin, labour-intensive, hyper-fragmented ~£100m pocket; asset-light aggregation is hard to defend and margins are thin unless the insurance/finance attach actually monetises.
3. **Unproven embedded-insurance economics + hardware/regulatory dependency.** The insurance/warranty layer (the fintech thesis) implies loss-ratio and capital/underwriting exposure Tyred hasn't shown it can run (Laka took multiple rounds + a bank debt facility to get there); IoT hardware on bikes adds cost and a hardware-failure/data-quality dependency. Marked `[UNSOURCED]` on unit economics — nothing disclosed.

**What this changes (idea-lens).** (analysis) Signal that the mobility aftercare vertical is being reframed from fragmented repair labour into a data + embedded-finance play — a new entry, not a re-rating. Falsifiable thesis: the moat is B2B fleet lock-in (Lime-type contracts) plus insurance attach, NOT consumer subscriptions. Watch for: (a) whether Tyred lands a second large fleet contract beyond Lime (validates), or (b) Lime/an operator in-sourcing maintenance or Laka moving into servicing (breaks the thesis).
